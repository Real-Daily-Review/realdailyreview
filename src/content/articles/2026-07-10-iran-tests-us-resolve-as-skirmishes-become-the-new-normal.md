# Iran's Brinkmanship Tests U.S. Resolve—But Colorado Shouldn't Foot the Bill for Another Middle East Quagmire

Tehran is playing a dangerous game of regional poker, and Washington needs to respond with strength—not another open-ended military commitment that drains American resources and Colorado defense dollars.

According to *National Review*, Iran's strategic position is weaker than its rhetoric suggests, yet the regime continues probing American resolve through regional proxies and military posturing. The *Washington Examiner* reports that low-level U.S.-Iran skirmishes are becoming the "new norm," with neither side seeking full-scale war but Tehran determined to test American patience through calculated provocations.

Republicans and defense hawks argue that a credible deterrent—backed by clear red lines and swift consequences—is essential to prevent escalation. However, conservatives also warn against the temptation to treat every Iranian provocation as justification for expanded military presence or spending.

**The Colorado angle:** Any sustained military buildup in the Middle East inevitably means federal defense spending that could otherwise reduce the national debt or fund tax relief for Colorado families and businesses. Colorado hosts significant defense and aerospace contractors; while some benefit from military spending, taxpayers bear the cost.

**Watch next:** Whether the Biden or Trump administration commits to genuine deterrence without mission creep—or whether Iran's testing becomes an excuse for another generational entanglement.
