# Denver's Crumbling Water Infrastructure Leaves Residents High and Dry

A 69-year-old water main burst on South Holly Street in Denver's Virginia Village neighborhood this weekend, forcing eight homes to lose water service and shutting down the street for days—a stark reminder that Colorado's aging municipal infrastructure is failing under the weight of neglect and mismanagement.

Denver Water crews worked to repair the broken main between East Evans and East Iliff avenues, with the street expected to remain closed through Sunday night (9News Denver). The incident highlights a broader problem conservatives have long warned about: government agencies routinely defer maintenance while demanding higher taxes and fees from ratepayers.

**The conservative case:** Denver Water has raised rates repeatedly over the past decade, yet basic infrastructure—some pipes dating to the 1950s—continues to deteriorate. Rather than prioritize core services like water delivery, city officials have diverted resources toward pet projects and bureaucratic expansion. Taxpayers are footing the bill for both the rate hikes *and* the emergency repairs that should have been prevented through competent long-term planning.

Democrats and city leaders argue aging infrastructure requires sustained investment, but they've failed to demonstrate fiscal discipline or accountability for how existing revenue is spent.

**Watch for:** Whether Denver Water's next rate request includes transparency on infrastructure spending priorities—or if ratepayers get hit again without answers.
