# House Backs Trump Immigration Enforcement as Iran Deal Talks Drag On

The Republican-controlled House is moving to fund President Trump's immigration enforcement actions for the remainder of his term, signaling GOP confidence in border security as a governing priority. The funding follows nearly $140 billion Congress allocated to ICE and Customs and Border Protection last year (9News Denver).

Meanwhile, Trump continues projecting optimism about an Iran nuclear deal, claiming resolution is imminent—a posture he's maintained despite unresolved uranium enrichment disputes and a ceasefire that nearly collapsed (Fox News, Just The News). Trump told reporters a deal could materialize "in two or three days," though negotiations remain fragile.

**The Colorado angle:** Immigration enforcement funding directly affects Colorado's labor markets, agriculture, and law enforcement cooperation. Republicans argue sustained ICE and CBP resources are essential to enforcing immigration law and protecting border security—a contrast to Democratic calls for restraint on deportations.

On Iran, conservatives remain skeptical of any deal that doesn't permanently halt uranium enrichment and includes sunset clauses. They argue the Biden-era JCPOA template proved inadequate and warn against repeating those mistakes.

**Watch next:** Whether House Democrats block or delay immigration funding, and whether Trump's Iran optimism translates into concrete agreements or another round of failed negotiations.
