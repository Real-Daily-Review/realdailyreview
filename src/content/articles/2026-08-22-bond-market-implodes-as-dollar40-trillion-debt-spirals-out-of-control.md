# Treasury Crisis Looms as Congress Ignores $40 Trillion Debt Bomb

The bond market is sending Washington a message Congress refuses to hear: unsustainable spending has consequences. With the 10-year Treasury yield holding above 4.7% and the 30-year climbing toward 5.3%—its highest level since before the Great Recession—investors are demanding higher returns to hold U.S. debt, a direct tax on borrowing costs for Colorado homebuyers, businesses, and anyone seeking a mortgage or car loan.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent cannot fix what Congress created. According to the Washington Examiner, the bond market is "well and truly imploding" as the federal government drowns in red ink. Just The News reports that Vice President Vance claims Bessent has a "very discreet plan" to reduce the $40 trillion national debt—but details remain scarce.

The spending problem is bipartisan but asymmetrical: a 2024 Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget analysis found Trump's first-term policies added $8.4 trillion to the debt over 10 years, while Biden approved $4.3 trillion in spending in his first three years and five months. Republicans argue Congress must cut spending and rein in entitlements; Democrats resist meaningful cuts, preferring tax hikes on the wealthy and corporations.

For Colorado taxpayers and retirees on fixed incomes, rising Treasury yields mean inflation pressures persist and purchasing power erodes. Watch whether the new administration actually proposes—and Congress actually passes—real spending cuts or merely talks around the problem.
