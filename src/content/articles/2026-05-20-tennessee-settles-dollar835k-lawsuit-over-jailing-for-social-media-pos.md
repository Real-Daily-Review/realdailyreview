# Tennessee Settles Lawsuit Over Jailing for Social Media Post

A Tennessee man who spent 37 days in jail over a Facebook post about conservative activist Charlie Kirk will receive $835,000 from state officials, according to CBS News and PBS NewsHour.

The 61-year-old retired police officer was arrested on a felony charge related to comments he made on social media. Authorities dropped the charge in October, according to PBS NewsHour.

The settlement resolves a lawsuit challenging the legality of his detention. The case raises questions about the boundaries between protected speech and criminal threats on social media platforms, particularly regarding posts about public figures.

Tennessee officials did not admit wrongdoing in agreeing to the settlement, which compensates the man for his time in custody and legal costs. The case drew attention from free speech advocates who argued the arrest represented an overreach in prosecuting online speech.

The settlement comes as courts nationwide continue to grapple with how to distinguish between constitutionally protected political commentary and genuine threats in the social media era.

Watch for any policy changes Tennessee may implement regarding how law enforcement evaluates social media posts before making arrests.
