# Denver Heat Wave Poses Risk to Power Grid and Vulnerable Residents

Colorado faces a dangerous weekend as record-breaking heat threatens the state's power infrastructure and vulnerable populations. Denver braces for temperatures near 100 degrees, with the National Weather Service warning of potential heat alerts (9News Denver). While weather events are inevitable, conservatives should scrutinize whether Colorado's aggressive push toward renewable energy has left the grid fragile during peak demand.

The heat wave underscores a critical vulnerability: as Democrats and environmental regulators have accelerated coal plant retirements and pushed aggressive decarbonization timelines, Colorado's baseload generation capacity has thinned. Peak summer demand—exactly when Coloradans need reliable air conditioning—strains a grid increasingly dependent on intermittent wind and solar sources.

Republicans and energy advocates have long warned that premature fossil fuel phase-outs without adequate backup capacity invite blackouts and price spikes. Colorado's grid operator and utilities will face a real-time test this weekend of whether the state's energy transition planning adequately accounts for extreme-weather scenarios.

Beyond the grid question, the heat poses genuine public health risks for elderly residents and those without air conditioning—a legitimate government concern focused on emergency response and vulnerable-population support.

**Watch whether Colorado regulators and Democratic lawmakers acknowledge grid stress this weekend and whether they reconsider the timeline for retiring reliable generation capacity.**
