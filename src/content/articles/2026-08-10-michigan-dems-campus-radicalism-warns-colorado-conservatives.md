# Michigan Democrat's Campus Radicalism Signals Troubling Trend for Colorado Progressives

Michigan's Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed rose through a campus political machine designed to push far-left activism into mainstream politics—a blueprint Colorado's progressive establishment is quietly replicating.

El-Sayed, a physician and former Michigan health official, built his 2024 Senate campaign on a foundation of student activists trained in resistance to immigration enforcement and corporate divestment campaigns. According to the Washington Examiner, the University of Michigan's student government—dominated by El-Sayed's allies in the "Human Rights Party"—functions as a pipeline funneling campus radicals into Democratic politics.

The Daily Wire reports El-Sayed has also attacked American football as breeding "toxic masculinity," preferring European soccer as more "egalitarian." These aren't fringe positions in progressive circles; they reflect the cultural radicalism now mainstream in Democratic campaigns.

**Why Colorado should care:** University of Colorado and Colorado State campuses host similar activist networks. If El-Sayed's model succeeds in Michigan, expect Colorado Democrats to accelerate campus-to-politics recruitment, pushing anti-energy, open-borders, and anti-American cultural positions into state races.

Republicans argue this represents ideological capture of higher education—institutions taxpayers fund should educate, not indoctrinate. Conservatives should monitor whether Colorado Democratic candidates in 2026 emerge from this same activist pipeline.
