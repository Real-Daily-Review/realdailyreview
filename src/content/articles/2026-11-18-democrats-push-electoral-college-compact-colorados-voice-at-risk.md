# Colorado Must Guard Against Electoral College Abolition Scheme

Democrats are quietly advancing the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact—a backdoor assault on the Electoral College that would hand presidential power to coastal urban centers and leave Colorado's voice irrelevant.

The compact, which has already secured commitments from states totaling 209 electoral votes (National Review), aims to reach the 270-vote threshold needed to effectively abolish the Electoral College without a constitutional amendment. Once activated, participating states would award all their electoral votes to the national popular vote winner, regardless of how their own voters cast ballots.

**Why this matters to Colorado:** The state's 10 electoral votes—hard-won through population growth and conservative turnout—would become meaningless. A Colorado voter's preference could be overridden by Democratic supermajorities in California and New York. Rural Colorado interests in energy, agriculture, and water rights would be drowned out by urban progressive agendas.

Republicans and conservatives argue the Electoral College is a constitutional safeguard ensuring smaller states and rural America maintain meaningful representation. "The left knows where its power centers reside," Complete Colorado notes, and the compact exploits that advantage.

Democrats counter that the popular vote is more democratic, though they conveniently ignore that the Founders designed the Electoral College precisely to prevent tyranny of the majority.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado's legislature resists pressure to join the compact—a test of whether state lawmakers prioritize Colorado's electoral leverage or progressive ideology.
