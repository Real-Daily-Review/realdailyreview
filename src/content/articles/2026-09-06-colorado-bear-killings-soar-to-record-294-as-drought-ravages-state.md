# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfires Drive Wildlife Crisis

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push starving animals into unprecedented human contact across the state (Colorado Sun). The surge reflects a cascading environmental crisis: reduced natural food sources force bears into residential areas, triggering dangerous encounters that leave officials with few options beyond lethal removal.

Conservatives and property owners argue the state's land-management failures—decades of fire suppression, water misallocation, and regulatory gridlock—have created the conditions for this wildlife disaster. Overgrown forests and depleted watersheds leave bears with no choice but to raid garbage, livestock, and homes. Rather than reflexively killing record numbers of animals, critics say Colorado should prioritize forest thinning, controlled burns, and water infrastructure to restore habitat and reduce human-wildlife conflict at the source.

Wildlife managers face an impossible bind: protect public safety or preserve bears. But the root problem traces to government mismanagement of Colorado's forests and water—issues that demand serious policy reform, not just body counts.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado lawmakers will fund aggressive forest restoration and water projects, or continue managing the symptoms of ecological decline.
