# Trump Escalates Trade War With Canada—Colorado Manufacturers in the Crosshairs

President Trump is turning up the heat on Canada with sweeping tariff threats, declaring he doesn't want "Canadian anything"—a hardline stance that could squeeze Colorado manufacturers and energy producers who rely on cross-border supply chains.

Trump has escalated rhetoric against Canadian leadership, calling them the "worst" he's dealt with, according to the *Washington Examiner*. In posts flagged by *Just The News*, Trump vowed to block Canadian cars and parts, claiming decades of unfair trade practices. National Review notes the trade war is intensifying, though critics call the approach "absurd."

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's manufacturing sector—from automotive suppliers to aerospace—depends heavily on integrated North American supply chains. Tariffs on Canadian parts could raise production costs for Colorado-based companies and potentially trigger retaliatory measures against U.S. exports. Energy producers shipping oil and gas across the border also face uncertainty.

**Republicans argue** Trump's tariff threats force Canada to renegotiate unfair trade terms that have disadvantaged American workers. **Critics counter** that broad tariffs risk inflation, job losses in border-dependent industries, and retaliation that harms Colorado exporters.

Colorado taxpayers and business owners should watch whether Trump follows through with concrete tariff rates—and whether Canadian retaliation targets Colorado energy or agricultural exports.
