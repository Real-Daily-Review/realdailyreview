# EPA Guts Obama-Era Power Plant Emissions Rule, Saves Industry $300 Billion

The Trump administration's EPA has repealed a sweeping Obama-era regulation that imposed strict greenhouse gas limits on coal and natural gas power plants—a move that could reshape Colorado's energy landscape and lower electricity costs for consumers and businesses across the state.

The agency estimates the repeal eliminates roughly $300 billion in compliance costs for the power industry (9News Denver, Just The News). The EPA framed the action as removing regulatory barriers to "unleash" American energy production—a direct rebuke of the previous administration's climate-focused approach.

For Colorado, this matters. The state's energy sector, including coal operations in northwest Colorado and natural gas producers, faced significant compliance burdens under the old rule. Lower regulatory costs could translate to cheaper power bills for Colorado households and manufacturers, though environmental groups will argue the tradeoff isn't worth it.

Democrats and climate activists counter that eliminating emissions limits accelerates climate change and removes protections for public health. They argue the rule was essential to meeting climate commitments. Republicans counter that the rule was economically destructive, killed jobs, and that American energy independence and affordable power matter more than unilateral emissions restrictions that don't bind China or India.

Watch for Colorado's Public Utilities Commission to weigh how utilities pass savings—or don't—to ratepayers.
