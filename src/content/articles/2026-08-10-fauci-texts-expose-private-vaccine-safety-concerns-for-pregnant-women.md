# Fauci Texts Reveal Private Vaccine Safety Doubts—Questions Linger on Public Messaging

Newly released text messages show Dr. Anthony Fauci privately discussed potential miscarriage risks from fever after mRNA COVID vaccines in pregnant women, raising fresh questions about what health officials knew versus what they publicly communicated.

The text chain, released by Senate Republicans including Sens. Ron Johnson and Rand Paul, documents conversations between Fauci, former CDC Director Rochelle Walensky, and former Surgeon General Vivek Murthy regarding theoretical complications in first-trimester pregnancies (Fox News, Just The News).

**The conservative concern:** Republicans argue the private discussions contradict the uniform public messaging that vaccines were universally safe for all pregnant women without qualification. If officials were internally debating safety thresholds, critics say the public deserved transparent discussion of those concerns rather than blanket reassurance.

Democrats and health officials counter that discussing theoretical risks in private doesn't mean those risks materialized or warranted changing guidance—and that vaccine safety for pregnant women was ultimately supported by broader data.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado women of childbearing age who received vaccines during pregnancy deserve clarity on what officials knew and when. The state's health department should address whether Colorado's vaccine guidance reflected the full scope of internal discussions.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand a state-level review of vaccine communication protocols and whether federal health agencies release additional internal documents.
