# Colorado Small Businesses Face Uphill Battle Against AI-Driven Holiday Shopping

Colorado's small retailers are getting squeezed this holiday season—not by inflation or supply chains, but by artificial intelligence reshaping how Americans shop. According to the Colorado Sun, local merchants are largely sitting out the traditional Black Friday-to-Cyber Monday sprint, unable to compete with the algorithmic precision and scale of online giants deploying AI to capture holiday dollars.

The challenge is structural. While Colorado hosts more than 70 holiday markets this year offering face-to-face shopping experiences, the broader retail landscape has tilted decisively toward digital. CPR News reports robust interest in holiday light displays and festive experiences, but these attractions don't translate into sustained sales for independent retailers competing against AI-optimized pricing, personalized recommendations, and logistics networks that dwarf local capacity.

**The conservative angle:** This underscores why Colorado's small-business community needs regulatory relief, not more red tape. While Democrats often tout "supporting local business" through mandates and taxes, the real help comes from lower compliance costs, reduced regulatory burden, and tax policy that lets entrepreneurs reinvest profits into competitive tools—including technology adoption—rather than feeding government coffers.

Holiday markets remain a bright spot, offering Coloradans a chance to support makers and artists directly. But without addressing the structural cost disadvantages facing independent retailers, these seasonal boosts won't reverse the long-term trend.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado policymakers will pursue tax incentives for small-business tech adoption or instead reach for the progressive playbook of new regulations and mandates.
