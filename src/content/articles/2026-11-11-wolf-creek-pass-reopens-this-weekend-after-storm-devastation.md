# Wolf Creek Pass Reopening Signals Colorado's Resilience After Devastating Storm Damage

Colorado's critical mountain corridor is fighting back. Wolf Creek Pass on U.S. 160 in southern Colorado could reopen by this weekend after back-to-back storms left the highway buried under up to 8 feet of rock, mud, and debris—with guardrails literally "hanging in thin air," according to the Colorado Sun.

The damage underscores why mountain infrastructure matters to Colorado's economy. Wolf Creek Pass is a vital artery connecting the San Juan region to the Front Range and beyond. Extended closures disrupt commerce, strand travelers, and strain local economies dependent on highway access and tourism.

State and federal crews have mobilized rapidly to clear the roadway and stabilize the damaged guardrails. The speed of the repair effort reflects Colorado's experience managing alpine weather crises—a necessity in a state where winter storms routinely test infrastructure and taxpayer-funded maintenance budgets.

Conservatives should note the broader lesson: Colorado's transportation network requires sustained investment and efficient management, not bloated bureaucracy. The state's ability to respond quickly here shows what focused, no-nonsense infrastructure work looks like.
