# Trump Administration Begins 'Great Triumphal Arch' Project at Arlington

The Trump administration is moving forward with excavation for a 250-foot gilded arch at Arlington National Cemetery's Memorial Circle, a project that has drawn criticism from preservationists concerned about disrupting historic Washington sightlines.

Interior Secretary Doug Burgum announced Thursday that work will begin within two weeks, ending what he called a "very long wait (over 125 years!)" for the monument. The administration frames the arch as a tribute to American achievement and presidential legacy.

Critics—including historic preservation groups—argue the structure would mar the sacred character of Arlington and alter the carefully maintained visual landscape around the nation's most hallowed ground. Opponents question whether a gilded monument of that scale belongs in a military cemetery dedicated to fallen service members rather than presidential glorification.

Conservatives backing the project counter that honoring American greatness and presidential accomplishment is appropriate, and that the administration has the authority to proceed. The debate reflects broader tensions over how Washington's monuments should evolve and what narratives they should tell.
