## Colorado Health Care Costs Keep Climbing — And Government Is Part of the Problem

State subsidies were supposed to make Colorado health care more affordable. Instead, conservatives argue they've done what government intervention almost always does: propped up a broken pricing structure and shielded the industry from the market competition that actually drives costs down. Colorado's health care sector is the state's largest employer, yet costs continue rising. Republicans say the fix isn't more spending — it's getting government out of the way and letting Coloradans shop for real value.

## Denver Braces for Record Heat, Pinpoint Weather Alert Days

After a stormy Friday evening along the I-25 corridor, Denver and the Front Range are looking at potential record high temperatures this weekend, with heat advisories in effect. Colorado taxpayers don't need a lecture from government officials about this one — they need reliable power grids and affordable energy. That's a reminder of why responsible, affordable energy development matters: when the heat hits, Coloradans need the lights and air conditioning on, not rolling blackouts courtesy of an over-regulated grid.

## Paramount-Warner Merger Stalled by California AG's Lawsuit

California Attorney General Rob Bonta has managed to delay Paramount Skydance's acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery by up to 10 months while courts examine the merger's legality. Conservatives say this is a textbook example of a Democratic state attorney general using litigation as a policy weapon to interfere with private business decisions. Colorado companies and investors should take note: the regulatory and legal climate in blue states doesn't stay contained — it sets precedents that ripple across the country.

Republicans broadly support a posture of strength against Iran, but conservatives in Congress are pressing for clearer communication about objectives and endgame. Colorado's military families — anchored around Fort Carson, Buckley, and Peterson — have skin in this game, and they deserve straight answers from Washington.
