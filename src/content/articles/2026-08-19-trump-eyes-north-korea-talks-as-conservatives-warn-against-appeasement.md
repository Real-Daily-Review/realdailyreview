# Trump Signals Diplomatic Opening With North Korea—But Conservatives Warn Against Naive Engagement

The Trump administration is signaling a potential meeting with Kim Jong-un, even as it scales back joint military exercises with South Korea (Just The News). The move reflects a broader diplomatic posture toward Pyongyang that conservatives view with deep skepticism.

**The conservative case for caution is straightforward:** North Korea remains "firmly within the China and Russia axis," according to National Review, making any bilateral talks a potential strategic gift to America's adversaries. The Washington Examiner's analysis argues that Kim Jong-un fears genuine peace with the South—not because he wants reconciliation, but because a unified, prosperous Korea would threaten his regime's survival. Scaling back military readiness with Seoul, conservatives warn, weakens deterrence without extracting meaningful concessions.

Republicans argue that dialogue without preconditions—denuclearization, human rights reforms, or verifiable commitments—amounts to legitimizing a brutal dictatorship. The administration's reported willingness to downgrade joint exercises signals weakness to both North Korea and our South Korean ally.

**Colorado's stake:** Any shift in U.S. military posture in the Pacific affects defense contractors and military families across the state.

Watch whether the Trump administration secures concrete North Korean commitments before further reducing military readiness—or whether this becomes another example of diplomatic theater that leaves America's position weaker.
