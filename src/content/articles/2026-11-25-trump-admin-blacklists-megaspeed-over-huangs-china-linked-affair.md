# Trump Admin Moves Against Nvidia-Linked Firm Amid Huang Scandal

The Trump administration has blacklisted Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company entangled in Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's extramarital affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence operative, adding it to the restricted entity list alongside six other firms this week (The Daily Wire). Huang's involvement with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive, triggered his resignation before his reinstatement as CEO—raising fresh questions about foreign influence in America's critical semiconductor sector.

The move signals the administration's hardline stance on national security vulnerabilities within tech leadership. Conservatives argue that personal compromises involving foreign nationals create counterintelligence risks, particularly in industries essential to U.S. defense and economic competitiveness. The blacklisting reflects Trump's stated priority: securing America's chip supply chain from foreign entanglement.

When asked about a potential pardon for Huang, Trump said it remains "always an option" but likely won't be "necessary" (Daily Caller). The administration has emphasized its focus on the *business* of chips over personal scandals—though the Megaspeed listing suggests security concerns trump executive convenience.

For Colorado: Nvidia's dominance in AI and data-center chips affects state tech employment and venture capital flows. Any disruption to Nvidia's leadership or operations could ripple through Colorado's growing tech sector.
