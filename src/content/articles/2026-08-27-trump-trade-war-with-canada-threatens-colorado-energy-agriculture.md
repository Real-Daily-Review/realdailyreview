# U.S.-Canada Trade Tensions Threaten Colorado Energy, Agriculture

The Trump administration's escalating trade dispute with Canada risks disrupting Colorado's cross-border energy and agricultural markets, as U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer warned Canada against retaliatory measures (Just The News). Greer disputed Canadian claims that last-minute U.S. demands derailed negotiations, signaling the administration won't tolerate trade retaliation.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue tough negotiating postures protect American workers and domestic industries. However, National Review warns the confrontation threatens a "close friendship" with a "resource-rich and strategically located" neighbor whose cooperation matters for North American stability and commerce.

**Colorado's stake:** Colorado's energy sector—oil, gas, and renewables—depends heavily on Canadian trade flows and cross-border investment. Agricultural exporters in the state also rely on Canadian markets. Prolonged tariff tensions could raise costs for Colorado businesses and consumers while destabilizing supply chains that took years to build.

The broader concern: while conservatives support leveraging trade policy to win better deals, National Review's skepticism reflects worry that this particular dispute may inflict more damage on American interests—especially in resource-dependent states like Colorado—than it extracts in concessions.
