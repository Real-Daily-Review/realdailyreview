# Tech Outages Not Foreign Attack, But America's Crumbling Infrastructure Exposed

America's recent technology outages weren't a coordinated foreign assault—but they've exposed something conservatives have long warned about: government and private sector reliance on aging, fragile digital infrastructure that can't handle modernization without breaking down.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told *ABC News This Week* and CNN's *State of the Union* that the incidents "were not coordinated and they were not state-sponsored." Instead, Clayton attributed the disruptions to upgrades to outdated U.S. digital systems, framing it as part of "a period of technological step change."

The distinction matters. While no hostile nation was behind the outages, the underlying problem—crumbling infrastructure struggling under the weight of necessary upgrades—underscores a real vulnerability. Conservatives argue this is precisely why government should get out of the way and let private enterprise lead infrastructure modernization, rather than relying on bureaucratic timelines and regulatory gridlock that slow critical updates.

For Colorado, tech outages ripple through energy grids, water systems, and financial markets. State officials should prioritize removing regulatory barriers that slow infrastructure upgrades, not add new compliance burdens that delay critical work.

Watch whether Congress uses this incident to justify more government control over tech infrastructure—or finally deregulates the sector enough to let companies move fast.
