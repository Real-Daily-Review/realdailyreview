# Fed Chair Warsh Signals Potential Shift in Central Bank's Market Role

Kevin Warsh's appointment as Federal Reserve chair could reshape how the central bank intervenes in financial markets, according to CNBC. Warsh may guide the Fed toward a smaller day-to-day role while establishing clearer rules governing when and how it should act.

Wall Street is already pricing in expectations for a rate increase by year-end, the Financial Times reports, as markets factor in intensifying inflation pressures amid geopolitical tensions including the Iran conflict. The shift reflects investor confidence in a new policy direction under Warsh's leadership.

The changes would represent what CNBC characterizes as a "regime change" in the Fed's operational approach—moving away from the expansive market management of recent years toward more defined boundaries on central bank intervention.

Warsh, who previously served as a Federal Reserve governor, brings a different philosophy to the role. His tenure will test whether markets can function with less Fed involvement and whether clearer intervention rules can coexist with inflation control.

Watch for Warsh's first policy meeting and any formal statements clarifying the Fed's new framework for market operations.
