# FAA Ground Stop Grounds Colorado Travel, Raises Questions About System Reliability

A nationwide FAA ground stop that lasted more than four hours Friday halted all U.S. flight departures—the longest such shutdown in 25 years—leaving hundreds of flights delayed or canceled and stranding travelers across the country, including at Denver International Airport.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin attributed the stoppage to a telecommunications outage, though details remain sparse. The FAA said U.S. air travel should normalize by Sunday, two days after the disruption.

For Colorado travelers and businesses relying on DIA—a major hub for regional and national connectivity—the chaos underscores a critical vulnerability: the nation's aviation infrastructure depends on aging, fragile systems that can fail catastrophically with minimal warning. When government-run air traffic control networks go down, there's no market alternative to absorb the shock.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that critical infrastructure modernization requires private-sector efficiency and competition, not bureaucratic band-aids. The FAA's inability to prevent or quickly resolve a telecom failure raises hard questions about whether centralized government control of air traffic is the best model for 21st-century travel demands.

Coloradans should watch whether Congress demands a full audit of FAA infrastructure vulnerabilities and whether policymakers seriously consider competitive alternatives to monopoly government control.
