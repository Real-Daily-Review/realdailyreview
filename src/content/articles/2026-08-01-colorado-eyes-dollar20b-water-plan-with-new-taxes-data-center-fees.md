# Colorado Water Funding Plan: New Taxes and Fees on the Table

A state task force is pushing multiple new revenue streams—data center fees, a special trust mechanism, and a so-called "sod tax"—to raise roughly $20 billion for Colorado water infrastructure, according to the Colorado Sun. The proposal reflects Democratic frustration that severance tax revenue from energy production is too volatile to fund long-term water projects reliably.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives will likely resist. Colorado's energy sector already faces regulatory headwinds; layering new fees on data centers and water-related activities amounts to backdoor tax hikes on businesses and consumers. The "sod tax" framing itself signals progressive overreach—taxing landscaping choices to engineer behavior is exactly the kind of government micromanagement conservatives oppose.

Colorado's water challenges are real and urgent, especially as population grows and drought persists. But the conservative argument is straightforward: before inventing new taxes, lawmakers should audit existing spending, eliminate waste, and consider whether current severance tax allocations are being used efficiently. Tying water funding to volatile energy revenue is a real problem—the answer, however, isn't a tax-and-fee expansion that burdens businesses and homeowners.

Watch for legislative pushback this session on how these funds would actually be spent and whether any sunset provisions or spending caps are included.
