# The Real Burrito Problem Isn't the Price—It's What Government Did to Your Wallet

Coloradans aren't losing sleep over burrito sticker shock; they're furious about the inflation eating their paychecks. A recent *Washington Examiner* piece cuts through the noise: the real culprit isn't ingredient costs—it's service inflation, labor mandates, and the regulatory squeeze on small businesses.

Conservative commentators, including Matt Walsh of the Daily Wire, have correctly identified the root cause: "insane" grocery prices tied to reckless government spending and foreign policy overreach. But the burrito debate itself misses the forest for the trees. Colorado's restaurant owners face rising labor costs, energy expenses, and compliance burdens that force menu prices skyward. When you mandate higher wages without corresponding productivity gains, when you regulate energy use and drive up utility bills, when you tax business revenue—someone pays. Usually the customer.

Democrats and progressive activists frame this as a "living wage" victory. Republicans counter that artificial wage floors and regulatory expansion destroy small-business margins and kill jobs, especially in rural Colorado communities where restaurants operate on thin margins.

The real story: Colorado taxpayers and business owners are caught between inflation they didn't cause and government policies that made it worse.

**Watch for:** How Colorado's 2024 ballot measures on labor mandates and business regulation shake out—they'll determine whether Main Street survives the next economic downturn.
