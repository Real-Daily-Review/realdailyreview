# Colorado's Top Races Pit Election Security Against Progressive Governance

Colorado voters face a choice between candidates who fundamentally disagree on how much government should intervene in elections, immigration enforcement, and consumer regulation heading into November.

The secretary of state's race centers on election integrity. Both James Wiley and Amanda Gonzalez say they want to depoliticize the office (Denver Post), but their approaches diverge sharply—a sign of deeper disagreement over mail-ballot security and how to handle election skepticism. Republicans argue the office has become too politicized; Democrats counter that election security requires robust government oversight.

In the 5th Congressional District, Jeff Crank and Jessica Killin clash over the economy, healthcare, and immigration—core issues where conservatives favor lower taxes and border enforcement while progressives push expanded government programs and sanctuary policies (Colorado Sun).

The attorney general's race between Michael Allen and Jena Griswold reflects the Trump administration divide. Griswold, the incumbent Democrat, has positioned herself as a check on federal overreach; Allen, the Republican, argues progressive attorneys general have weaponized the office against business and energy development (Colorado Sun).
