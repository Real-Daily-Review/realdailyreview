# CNBC's 'Good Life' Rankings Expose Progressive Bias in Economic Coverage

CNBC's latest "best places to live" rankings reveal how mainstream business media now conflates Democratic policy priorities with actual quality of life—a troubling shift for Colorado voters trying to separate ideology from economic fact.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, CNBC's methodology for ranking states appears to prioritize abortion access, government-subsidized childcare, and voting without photo ID alongside traditional metrics like job growth and cost of living. The outlet notes the rankings align suspiciously with Democratic Party platform positions rather than measures of genuine human flourishing or economic opportunity.

Conservatives argue this represents a fundamental corruption of business journalism. When CNBC packages progressive social policies as essential components of "the good life," it abandons pretense of objectivity and becomes a cheerleader for left-wing governance—not a neutral analyzer of economic conditions.

For Colorado specifically, this matters: The state's competitive advantage has historically rested on low taxes, business-friendly regulation, and individual liberty—not government mandates on childcare or abortion policy. If major business media outlets are now ranking states based on ideological alignment rather than fiscal health, Colorado taxpayers and entrepreneurs should know their economic interests are being systematically misrepresented in national coverage.

Watch whether other major business publications follow CNBC's lead in weaponizing "quality of life" rankings as a proxy for progressive policy advocacy.
