# Trump Orders Warning Signs at Smithsonian Over 'Inaccurate' History Displays

President Trump has ordered warning signs posted outside the Smithsonian's American History museum, escalating his long-running critique of how federal institutions present the nation's past.

The executive order directs signs to be placed on federal sidewalks outside the museum, alerting visitors that exhibits contain what the administration views as inaccurate depictions of American history. According to *Just The News*, the signs would redirect visitors to alternative resources where American history is presented more accurately—though the order did not specify which locations or materials qualify.

This move reflects Trump's broader push against what conservatives call "revisionist" history in government-funded institutions. Republicans argue that major museums have adopted progressive narratives that downplay American achievements and overemphasize historical grievances, effectively using taxpayer dollars to fund ideological messaging rather than objective education.

Democrats and museum advocates typically counter that modern historical scholarship reflects a more complete and honest accounting of America's past, including previously marginalized perspectives.

The Smithsonian, a federally funded institution, has faced sustained conservative criticism over its exhibits and curatorial choices. Colorado taxpayers help fund these institutions through federal taxes—making the question of what version of American history gets displayed on the public dime a legitimate policy debate.

Watch whether Congress moves to defund or restructure federal museum operations if the administration escalates this cultural fight.
