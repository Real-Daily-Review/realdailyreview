# Colorado Voters Oust Judges, Pass Tax Hikes, Tighten Mail Ballot Rules

Colorado voters delivered a mixed message this November: they ousted three judges in retention elections, approved what conservatives warn are three major tax hikes, and backed stricter voter ID requirements for mail ballots—a rare win for election integrity advocates in a state moving toward all-mail voting.

According to Complete Colorado, over 120 judges faced retention votes statewide, and three failed to survive the cut. A fourth resigned ahead of the election. The judicial housecleaning reflects voter frustration with the bench, though specifics on which judges or their records remain unclear from available reporting.

On taxes, Colorado voters approved the "Big Three" tax increases (Complete Colorado). Conservatives argue that new revenue does not substitute for difficult choices, meaningful oversight, or fiscal restraint—a warning that state government will simply spend more without addressing underlying budget discipline.

The bright spot for conservatives: Initiative 362 passed, adding voter authentication requirements to Colorado's all-mail voting system. Election integrity advocates have long criticized Colorado's mail-ballot expansion as vulnerable to fraud; the new ID rules represent a rare legislative victory on that front.

Separately, Complete Colorado flagged concerns that Colorado's election rules themselves are "substantially undemocratic and extremely stupid," citing confusion in the governor's race.

**Watch next:** whether Colorado's Democratic legislature attempts to roll back Initiative 362's voter ID safeguards or whether the tax hikes trigger a taxpayer backlash in 2027.
