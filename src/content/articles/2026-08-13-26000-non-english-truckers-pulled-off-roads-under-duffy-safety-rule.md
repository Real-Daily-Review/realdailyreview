# Federal Crackdown on Non-English-Speaking Truckers Pulls 26,000 Off Roads

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's common-sense rule—requiring commercial truck drivers to read English highway signs—has already yanked 26,000 unsafe operators off American interstates, and conservatives say it's about time.

Since the August 7 announcement, the federal government has enforced a basic safety standard that should never have required a formal crackdown: if you cannot read English road signs, you cannot safely pilot an 80,000-pound vehicle at highway speeds. The rule targets a genuine public safety hazard. Trucks that size, piloted by drivers unable to comprehend critical signage, pose catastrophic risks to other motorists—especially on Colorado's mountain passes and busy I-25 corridor where heavy commercial traffic is constant.

Republicans argue this represents responsible government action: protecting citizens without imposing new taxes or regulations on law-abiding businesses. The rule simply enforces a baseline competency standard that any rational person would expect already existed.

Critics on the left have remained largely silent on this particular measure, though immigration advocates have historically opposed English-proficiency requirements as exclusionary. The Biden-Harris administration did not pursue such enforcement before Duffy took office.

Colorado taxpayers and families should watch whether the administration maintains this standard or caves to pressure from trucking lobbies seeking cheaper labor regardless of safety costs.
