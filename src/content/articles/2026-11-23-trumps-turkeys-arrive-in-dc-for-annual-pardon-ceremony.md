# Trump's Thanksgiving Turkeys Arrive in D.C. for Annual Pardon Spectacle

The annual presidential turkey pardon—a quirky Washington tradition that costs taxpayers money while real Americans struggle with inflation—kicked off this week as two West Virginia farm birds arrived at the Willard InterContinental in luxury style (Fox News).

The white turkeys strutted a red carpet before photographers and hotel guests ahead of Tuesday's pardon ceremony, where Americans will vote on their names. The birds made their "grand entrance" in the kind of pomp typically reserved for state dinners (New York Post).

**The conservative take:** While the turkey pardon is harmless tradition, it symbolizes Washington's disconnect from kitchen-table concerns. Coloradans facing grocery price hikes, energy costs, and property tax increases might reasonably ask why federal resources go toward staging elaborate ceremonies for fowl when government should be cutting waste, not celebrating it.

The pardon itself dates to the Eisenhower era and has become a media event. Republicans have long argued the federal government should focus on core constitutional duties—not theatrical gestures that reinforce the bloated bureaucracy's self-importance.
