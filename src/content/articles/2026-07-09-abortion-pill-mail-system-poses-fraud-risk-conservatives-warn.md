# Federal Abortion-Pill Rules Leave Colorado Women Vulnerable to Fraud, Say Conservatives

The Biden administration's lax rules on mail-order abortion drugs have created a dangerous loophole: women can obtain chemical abortions in days without ever seeing a doctor, opening the door to identity theft and cross-state pill trafficking, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

Cases like that of Rosalie Markezich—whose name was allegedly used to obtain and mail abortion pills across state lines without her knowledge—illustrate the real-world risks of the current system. Conservatives argue the federal government has prioritized speed and anonymity over basic safety protocols and fraud prevention.

Colorado voters should pay attention: the state's mail-in voting system and lax verification standards create similar vulnerabilities. If federal agencies can't secure the abortion-pill supply chain, what confidence should Coloradans have in election security or medical privacy?

Republicans are calling on incoming officials, including Todd Blanche at the Justice Department, to tighten federal oversight of mail-order abortion drugs and require in-person medical evaluation. Democrats have resisted such restrictions, framing them as barriers to access rather than safeguards.

Watch whether Colorado's state health department adopts stronger verification requirements for mail-order pharmaceuticals—or continues following federal permissiveness.
