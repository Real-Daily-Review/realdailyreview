# Trump's Iran Deal Collapse Sparks Conservative Reckonings

President Trump's sudden reversal on Iran negotiations has drawn sharp criticism from both the right and center-left, exposing fractures within Republican ranks over foreign policy credibility.

National Review argues that Trump "abandoned his previous positions in favor of the Iranian position on all the key issues of contention," calling it a "prompt and utter humiliation." Meanwhile, Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin contends Trump is now "lying" about the agreement's terms, citing Vice President JD Vance's "equally ridiculous whoppers" as evidence of a broader pattern of misinformation.

**The conservative split is real.** Establishment Republicans at National Review—traditionally hawkish on Iran—see Trump's capitulation as a betrayal of hardline principles. Yet Trump's base and populist wing argue the deal avoids costly military entanglement. Neither camp is satisfied.

**For Colorado:** This matters. Colorado hosts significant defense contracting and energy interests tied to Middle East policy. A weakened Iran position could reshape oil markets and defense spending that supports Colorado jobs and the state budget.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether this Iran reversal signals broader shifts in Trump's foreign policy doctrine—and whether Republican unity on national security can survive another election cycle.
