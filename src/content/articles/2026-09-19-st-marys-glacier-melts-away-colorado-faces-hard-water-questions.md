# St. Mary's Glacier Gone: Colorado Icon Melts Away

Colorado just lost one of its most recognizable natural landmarks—St. Mary's Glacier in Clear Creek County has completely melted, leaving visitors staring at bare rock where ice once stood.

The disappearance follows an abnormally hot summer compounded by a record snow drought, according to CPR News. Hikers expecting to reach the glacier are now finding only what one observer called "just a big rock pile" (CPR News).

The glacier's collapse raises hard questions about Colorado's water future—a critical concern for ranchers, farmers, and energy producers who depend on reliable snowpack and runoff. While some will blame climate change, conservatives note that Colorado's water challenges stem primarily from population growth, competing demands, and aging infrastructure rather than weather alone. The state's water managers should focus on smart storage solutions, not restrictions on development or energy production.

Democrats and environmental groups will likely seize on St. Mary's disappearance to push for stricter climate regulations and carbon limits. Republicans counter that Colorado's economy—including agriculture and responsible energy development—depends on pragmatic water policy, not ideological mandates that harm jobs.

Watch whether state lawmakers use this as cover for new environmental regulations or water-use restrictions that could squeeze Colorado's agricultural and energy sectors.
