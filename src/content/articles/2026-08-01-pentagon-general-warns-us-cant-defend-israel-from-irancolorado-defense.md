# Colorado Conservatives Should Demand Answers on Military Readiness as Iran Threat Looms

Colorado's defense contractors and military families deserve to know: does America have the firepower to deter Iranian aggression? A top U.S. general reportedly told Pentagon leadership this week that the U.S. lacks sufficient military capacity to defend Israel from Iranian missile attacks (Washington Examiner), raising hard questions about force posture, readiness, and whether years of defense budget constraints have left America overextended.

The warning comes as tensions spike in the Middle East. The U.S. has long relied on naval destroyers in the eastern Mediterranean to stabilize the region and protect Israeli security, but apparently that's no longer enough—at least according to military brass.

Republicans and defense hawks argue this exposes the folly of underfunding the military while expanding social spending. Conservatives say the Biden-Harris administration's pivot toward green energy mandates and regulatory bloat has diverted resources and focus from core national security. Democrats counter that military spending remains robust and that diplomacy, not hardware alone, prevents conflict.

For Colorado—home to NORAD, Fort Carson, and dozens of defense suppliers—this matters directly. A hollowed-out military means fewer contracts, fewer jobs, and fewer resources for troops stationed here.

Watch for congressional testimony and Pentagon budget justifications in coming weeks; Colorado's defense industry and military community should demand clarity on readiness gaps.
