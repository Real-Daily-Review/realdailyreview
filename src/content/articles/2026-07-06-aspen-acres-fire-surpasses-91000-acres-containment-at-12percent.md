# Massive Aspen Acres Fire Strains Colorado Resources as Containment Lags

Colorado's wildfire season is turning catastrophic: the Aspen Acres fire southwest of Pueblo has torched more than 91,000 acres with containment stuck at just 12 percent, forcing mandatory evacuations across Fremont County and consuming over 1,300 personnel in firefighting efforts (Colorado Sun, CPR News).

The blaze is moving fast. The nearby Willow fire near Leadville grew 900 acres overnight, while strong winds continue pushing the Aspen Acres inferno across the landscape. The fire's intensity has already claimed major community events—the Ouray 100 race was canceled due to the Gold Mountain fire (Colorado Sun).

**The conservative concern:** Colorado's wildfire response depends heavily on federal land management decisions. Years of deferred forest maintenance, environmental litigation blocking fuel-reduction projects, and regulatory gridlock have left Colorado's forests tinderbox-dry. Republicans and land-use conservatives argue that overly restrictive environmental rules and federal bureaucracy have prevented the aggressive thinning and prescribed burns that could reduce catastrophic fire risk.

The state's firefighting resources, while substantial, are finite. As fires multiply, costs skyrocket—ultimately borne by Colorado taxpayers through state emergency budgets and federal disaster declarations that add to the national debt.

**Watch:** Whether state and federal officials will finally streamline environmental reviews to allow faster forest management and fuel reduction on public lands.
