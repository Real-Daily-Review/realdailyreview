# America Marks 25 Years Since 9/11—But Still Debates the Cost of Security

A quarter-century after the September 11 terrorist attacks, Americans are reflecting on how the nation has changed—and conservatives are asking hard questions about whether post-9/11 security expansions have delivered real protection or simply grown government power at the expense of liberty.

Congress designated September 11 as both Patriot Day and a National Day of Service and Remembrance, though it remains a regular workday rather than a federal holiday (9News Denver). The attacks fundamentally reshaped American life: technology, media, security protocols, and the republic itself have undergone "dramatic turns" in the past 25 years (Just The News).

For Colorado conservatives, the 9/11 anniversary raises uncomfortable questions. The post-attack era spawned the Department of Homeland Security, the TSA, expanded surveillance programs, and federal spending that shows no signs of slowing. Republicans have long argued that while security matters, many of these initiatives sacrificed constitutional protections—particularly Fourth Amendment privacy rights—without proportional security gains.

Meanwhile, progressives defend the security apparatus as necessary, though even some on the left now question surveillance overreach.
