# Trump Pivots to China While NATO Takes a Backseat

President Trump is signaling that great-power competition with Beijing, not European alliance management, will define his second-term foreign policy—a shift that could reshape defense spending and military priorities affecting Colorado's aerospace and defense contractors.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, Trump's upcoming meeting with China's Xi Jinping later this month has already eclipsed his earlier NATO summit in Turkey in terms of administration focus. The Trump administration's latest National Security Strategy reflects this reorientation toward the Indo-Pacific theater and direct U.S.-China rivalry.

Conservatives argue this recalibration is overdue. They contend that Washington has spent decades managing European security commitments while China built economic and military dominance—a strategic imbalance Trump aims to correct. Republicans say prioritizing China competition means demanding NATO allies spend more on their own defense rather than relying on American taxpayer-funded umbrella coverage.

The shift carries real implications for Colorado. Defense contractors in the Denver metro area and along the Front Range—from Lockheed Martin to Ball Aerospace—could see increased demand for advanced systems targeting Pacific operations, from satellite reconnaissance to hypersonic weapons development.
