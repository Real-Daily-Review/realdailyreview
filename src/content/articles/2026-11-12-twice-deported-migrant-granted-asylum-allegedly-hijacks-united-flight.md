# Twice-Deported Migrant Granted Asylum, Then Allegedly Tries to Hijack United Flight

A twice-deported Honduran national granted asylum after claiming transgender persecution has exposed serious vulnerabilities in America's immigration screening system—and raised hard questions about how the government vets asylum claims.

Rony Alexander Gomez-Alberto, according to the Department of Homeland Security, was granted asylum after claiming he feared persecution in Honduras because he is transgender. Despite two prior deportations, he was released into the country. On Monday night, he allegedly boarded United Flight 2174 from Newark to New Orleans, claimed to have a bomb, and attempted to storm the cockpit.

DHS now says Gomez-Alberto was "gaming" the asylum system (Washington Examiner, Daily Wire). The incident underscores what conservatives have long argued: the Biden-Harris administration's asylum policies lack rigorous vetting, creating security risks while rewarding fraud.

Republicans point to this case as evidence that the current asylum framework is broken—that bad actors exploit sympathetic claims to gain entry, then pose threats to public safety. The fact that a twice-deported individual was readmitted and granted asylum status raises urgent questions about background checks, deportation enforcement, and the credibility of asylum adjudication.

Colorado travelers and airline workers deserve assurance that the government can distinguish legitimate asylum seekers from security threats. Watch whether Congress demands accountability from DHS on asylum fraud detection and deportation follow-through.
