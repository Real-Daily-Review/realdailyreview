# Small Plane Crashes Into Beijing's Tallest Tower; China Clamps Down on Coverage

A small aircraft slammed into CITIC Tower, Beijing's 108-story flagship skyscraper, on Friday, triggering evacuations across the Chinese capital's business district and immediate censorship of reporting by Beijing authorities.

The light sport aircraft—described by Reuters as roughly "the size of a car"—struck the tower and fell to street level below (The Daily Wire). Multiple outlets with ground reporters confirmed the incident, though Chinese state control quickly moved to suppress coverage, according to Breitbart.

The crash underscores a familiar pattern: when accidents or incidents threaten the Communist Party's narrative, Beijing's censorship apparatus kicks into overdrive. American media outlets including 9News Denver and The Daily Wire reported the story freely; Chinese citizens faced the usual information blackout.

For Colorado conservatives watching Beijing's heavy-handed response, the contrast is stark. Here, even controversial incidents generate open debate and competing coverage. In China, the state decides what citizens see—a reminder of why First Amendment protections and a free press matter, and why American skepticism of government overreach has deep roots.

Watch for whether Chinese authorities release any official account of the crash or continue the information lockdown.
