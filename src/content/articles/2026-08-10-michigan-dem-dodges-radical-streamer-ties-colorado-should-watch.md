# Michigan Democrat Dodges Questions on Radical Streamer Ties—and Colorado Should Take Note

Abdul El-Sayed, Michigan's Democratic Senate nominee, brushed off criticism of his repeated campaign appearances alongside far-left Twitch streamer Hasan Piker, who has made inflammatory comments suggesting America deserved the 9/11 terrorist attacks. When pressed on NBC's "Meet the Press" to disavow Piker's remarks, El-Sayed deflected rather than clearly condemning them (The Daily Wire).

Republicans argue this reveals troubling judgment. Peter Laffin at the Washington Examiner countered claims that questioning El-Sayed's association is "Islamophobic," noting that legitimate concerns about a candidate's choice of allies transcend identity politics. The streamer's extreme rhetoric—and El-Sayed's apparent comfort campaigning alongside him—raises red flags about where the Democratic Party's center of gravity has shifted.

Colorado conservatives should recognize the pattern: Democratic candidates nationwide are increasingly comfortable with radical voices on the far left. From open-borders rhetoric to anti-American sentiment, these fringe figures are no longer fringe—they're on the campaign trail. When Colorado Democrats court similar activists and ideologues, voters deserve straight answers about whether they endorse or merely tolerate such views.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic Senate and gubernatorial candidates face similar scrutiny, and how they respond when pressed on their own radical allies.
