# HHS Opens Vaccine Injury Clinic as RFK Jr. Pushes Long-Overdue Accountability

The federal government is finally taking seriously what millions of Americans have long suspected: vaccine injury complaints deserve rigorous investigation, not dismissal. The Department of Health and Human Services this week launched a new NIH clinic dedicated to studying reported vaccine injuries and established protocols requiring electronic health records to capture vaccine-related adverse events (Just The News).

The move follows RFK Jr.'s push for a comprehensive research initiative into vaccine safety concerns—a position that drew ridicule from the establishment media for years before gaining traction in Washington. Conservatives have argued that legitimate vaccine safety questions were stonewalled by federal health bureaucrats more interested in protecting pharmaceutical interests than investigating citizen complaints.

The clinic represents a modest but meaningful shift: the government will now systematically document and investigate health problems reported after vaccination rather than dismissing them outright. Whether this translates into genuine transparency or becomes another box-checking exercise remains to be seen.

For Colorado families and workers who faced vaccine mandates—including those who lost jobs over refusal—this acknowledgment of injury investigation comes too late. Taxpayers should watch whether HHS actually funds this effort adequately and whether findings are made public without political interference.
