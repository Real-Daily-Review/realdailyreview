# Iran Threatens Strait of Hormuz Blockade—Colorado Energy Markets Should Brace for Impact

Iran's lawmakers are considering a sweeping ban on U.S. and Israeli ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz, plus imposing cargo fees up to 7% on commercial vessels from other nations, according to *Just The News* and *Breitbart*. The move is framed as compensation for alleged war damages.

This is a direct threat to global oil markets—and Colorado's energy economy. The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly one-third of seaborne traded oil worldwide. Any Iranian blockade would spike crude prices, ripple through refining operations, and hit Colorado's oil and gas sector hard. Higher energy costs also mean higher heating bills for Colorado families and increased transportation costs for businesses statewide.

Republicans and energy advocates warn that Iran's aggression reflects the weakness of Biden-Harris foreign policy and the administration's reluctance to enforce deterrence in the Middle East. Conservatives argue that appeasement and sanctions relief under the Iran nuclear deal emboldened Tehran to escalate threats against U.S. interests.

Democrats would likely counter that military escalation risks broader regional conflict, though the Biden administration has maintained a hardline posture on Iran's nuclear program.
