# Trump's Iran Deal Draws Mixed Conservative Support as Americans Remain Divided

President Trump's preliminary agreement with Iran is splitting the right: while some conservatives praise the pragmatic diplomacy, others worry the deal doesn't go far enough to constrain Tehran's nuclear ambitions and regional aggression.

A Reagan Institute survey shows Americans themselves are fractured—39% favor negotiated settlement while 36% back regime change, according to Fox News. Yet Breitbart reports a separate Quantus poll found "strong majority approval" among U.S. voters for the deal itself, suggesting messaging and framing heavily influence public opinion on Iran policy.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei endorsed the agreement Thursday after receiving assurances the deal protects Tehran's interests—a detail that should concern conservatives skeptical of Iranian intentions. The Daily Wire notes Trump is operating as a "pragmatist," though some right-leaning analysts question whether negotiations truly address the fundamental threat Iran poses to American interests and regional stability.

For Colorado, this matters: energy markets, military installations near Colorado Springs, and defense contractors across the state could face ripple effects depending on how the agreement reshapes Middle East tensions and oil markets.

Conservatives should watch whether the final agreement includes robust verification mechanisms and whether Iran actually honors its commitments—or whether this becomes another capitulation to hostile regimes.
