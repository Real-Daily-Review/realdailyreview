# Colorado Capitol Drowning in Bill Overload While Taxpayers Foot the Party Primary Bill

Colorado legislators are introducing so many bills that the Capitol can barely function—and taxpayers are footing the bill for party primaries they didn't choose to support.

Complete Colorado argues the state needs hard limits on bill introductions to restore legislative focus and accountability. The sheer volume of legislation creates chaos, dilutes debate, and lets bad ideas slip through in the noise.

Meanwhile, Colorado forces all taxpayers to finance primary elections for political parties, regardless of whether they belong to or support those parties. Complete Colorado contends this amounts to government-mandated political subsidy—a clear violation of individual liberty and fiscal responsibility. Voters who are unaffiliated or belong to minor parties have no say in how their tax dollars fund Democratic and Republican primaries.

On elections themselves, conservatives argue Colorado needs independent audits to restore voter confidence. Complete Colorado's position: skepticism about election integrity isn't silenced by dismissing concerns—it's addressed by making the system more transparent and trustworthy. Current audit structures lack the independence needed to convince voters the process is sound.

These three issues—legislative bloat, forced party funding, and election transparency—reflect a broader problem: Colorado government has grown too large, too expensive, and too insulated from taxpayer scrutiny.

Watch whether the 2027 legislative session caps bill introductions and whether any serious effort emerges to end taxpayer-funded party primaries.
