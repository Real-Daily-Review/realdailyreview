# America's 250th Should Celebrate Liberty, Not Self-Loathing

As America marks its 250th anniversary this summer, conservatives are pushing back against what they see as a dangerous drift toward national self-flagellation in official commemorations.

The National Review argues that framing America's founding around oppression and systemic guilt misses the actual story—including Spain's crucial, often-overlooked support for the Patriots during the Revolution. Rather than centering shame, conservatives say the nation should celebrate the principles of individual liberty and limited government that made American independence possible.

The Washington Examiner emphasizes that anniversary events from the National Mall to Philadelphia should reflect on founding principles while highlighting faith and free expression as cornerstones of American identity. Conservatives contend that 250 years of constitutional governance—however imperfect—deserve honest celebration, not apology tours disguised as reflection.

For Colorado, this debate matters. State education boards and local governments will decide how to mark the milestone. Conservatives worry that progressive activists will use the occasion to push revisionist curricula that teach children to resent rather than understand American achievement—a pattern already visible in Colorado schools.
