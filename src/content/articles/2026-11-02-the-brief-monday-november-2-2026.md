### Monday, November 2, 2026

The final sprint before Tuesday's midterms is here, and the stakes for Colorado conservatives couldn't be higher — Senate control, the future of Trump's agenda, and the question of whether Republicans squandered a winnable map. Here's what you need to know this morning.

## Trump Hits the Trail in Iowa as Midterms Loom

President Trump reversed course and returned to the campaign trail Sunday, holding a rally in Dubuque, Iowa, just 48 hours before polls open. After canceling in-person appearances earlier in the week, Trump's late push signals the White House knows turnout is everything. For Colorado conservatives, the message is simple: what happens Tuesday in swing states determines whether Trump's agenda — border security, energy production, and tax relief — has a legislative engine behind it or gets strangled in a Democrat-controlled Senate.

## Republican Candidate Quality Could Cost the Senate

A veteran Democratic operative is warning — or perhaps gloating — that weak Republican candidates in Texas, Mississippi, Kansas, and Ohio could hand Democrats full Senate control by Wednesday morning. Republicans argue the party's nominee quality in key races has been uneven, and conservatives should be clear-eyed about it. A Democrat-run Senate means committee chairmanships, judicial blockades, and a green light for every regulatory overreach that Colorado's energy workers, ranchers, and small businesses have been fighting off for years.

## DOJ's 'Decoy Dan' Probe and the Precedent Jack Smith Set

Former FEC Chairman Hans von Spakovsky argues that the Biden DOJ's relentless prosecution of Donald Trump opened the door to the Justice Department's current criminal probe into Alaska's "Decoy Dan" Senate race scheme. Conservatives have long warned that weaponizing federal law enforcement against political opponents is a two-way street — and that the left's appetite for using the DOJ as a political cudgel would eventually come back around. Colorado Republicans watching their own state's election integrity battles should take note: the precedent matters.

---

## Ilhan Omar Controversy Isn't Going Away

President Trump is calling for Rep. Ilhan Omar to resign over alleged immigration fraud, but as conservatives and legal observers note, the Constitution grants that power solely to the House — not the executive. The saga underscores a broader question Republicans are pressing heading into Tuesday: accountability in Congress, and whether Democratic leadership will ever hold its own members to the same standard they demanded of Republicans. Colorado voters who've watched their own delegation dodge accountability questions know exactly how that story usually ends.

## Federal Judge Tosses Trump's $10 Billion WSJ Lawsuit — With Prejudice

A federal judge dismissed President Trump's $10 billion lawsuit against the Wall Street Journal with prejudice, meaning it cannot be refiled. Trump's team says he will "continue to press forward." Whatever one thinks of the underlying dispute, the ruling is a reminder that the courts remain an unpredictable arena — and that conservatives who've cheered judicial restraint should apply that standard consistently, regardless of who's doing the suing.

---

## What to Watch

All eyes turn to Tuesday's polls — and Colorado conservatives should be watching Senate race results in Ohio and Texas particularly closely, as those outcomes will determine whether Republicans hold the majority or hand Democrats the gavel. Watch turnout numbers in Colorado's competitive congressional districts, where ground game and late momentum could tip the balance. And keep an eye on any last-minute legal challenges to ballot integrity: with control of Congress on the line, nothing should be taken for granted before the final count is in.
