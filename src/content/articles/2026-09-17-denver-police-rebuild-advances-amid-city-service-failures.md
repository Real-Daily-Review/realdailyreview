# Denver's Police Station Rebuild Stalled While City Struggles With Basic Services

Denver is moving forward with a police station rebuild on Colfax Avenue, but the project highlights a deeper problem: the city can't seem to manage either public safety infrastructure or basic government services competently.

The rebuild plan includes housing on a nearby former pharmacy site, but Colfax leaders say the parcel is too small to attract developers—a familiar refrain in Denver's perpetually stalled development pipeline (9News Denver). Meanwhile, residents in the Vesty Park housing complex on Denver's west side have gone six months without mail service after USPS suspended operations at the facility (KDVR Fox31). 

The contrast is telling. Denver's progressive leadership has prioritized housing mandates and mixed-use development schemes while failing to ensure that residents receive mail—a core federal service. Conservatives argue this reflects misplaced priorities: the city pursues ambitious zoning experiments and density targets while neglecting the unglamorous work of maintaining functional neighborhoods and supporting law enforcement facilities.

The police station rebuild itself faces questions about cost and timeline in a city already grappling with budget pressures and service failures. Taxpayers should watch whether Denver's city council addresses the mail crisis and completes the police facility on budget, or whether this becomes another example of municipal dysfunction.
