# Colorado Stargazers Get Prime Viewing Window for Perseid Meteor Shower

Colorado's clear summer skies are about to deliver one of nature's best free shows—and taxpayers won't foot the bill for it.

The Perseid meteor shower peaks Wednesday night into Thursday morning, with as many as 50 to 100 meteors per hour visible under ideal conditions, according to NASA. The Northern Hemisphere is positioned for especially promising viewing, and Colorado's high elevation and typically clear summer nights make the state an ideal vantage point.

The timing is fortuitous: the meteor shower's peak coincides with a total solar eclipse visible across Spain, Iceland, and Greenland, plus a rare six-planet parade. Stargazers should note that the best time to view the Perseid fireballs comes after totality ends.

For Colorado observers, the key is getting away from light pollution. Head to higher elevations or rural areas away from Denver's sprawl—the same instinct that drives Coloradans to escape government-heavy urban centers applies here. Bring binoculars or a telescope if you have them, though the naked eye works fine for meteor showers.

No permits required. No environmental impact studies. Just clear skies and a celestial light show that's been running for millennia.

Watch for peak viewing conditions Wednesday night; cloud cover forecasts will be critical for Front Range and Western Slope observers.
