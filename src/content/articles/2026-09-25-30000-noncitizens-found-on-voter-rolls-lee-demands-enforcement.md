# Noncitizen Voting Errors Expose Election Security Gaps, Lee Warns

Colorado election officials should face hard questions about whether noncitizens have infiltrated voter rolls here, after a report found 30,000 noncitizens mistakenly added to rolls across 11 states since 2000 (Fox News, New York Post).

Sen. Mike Lee (R-Utah) blasted the recurring failures, saying that "even though we have laws that prohibit things like noncitizen voting, those laws have been rendered difficult and essentially impossible to enforce" (New York Post). Lee, who introduced the SAVE America Act earlier this year, argues the problem reflects systemic enforcement breakdowns—not just isolated clerical errors.

**The conservative case:** Republicans contend that lax voter roll maintenance and weak verification procedures undermine election integrity. If 30,000 noncitizens slipped through in just 11 states over two decades, the actual national number could be far higher. Lee's legislation aims to tighten citizenship verification at the ballot box.

**Democrats' position:** Election officials typically frame these discoveries as proof the system works—errors are caught and corrected. They argue noncitizen voting is vanishingly rare and that stricter ID requirements burden legitimate voters.

Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold (D) has resisted voter ID expansion, citing accessibility concerns. Taxpayers and election integrity advocates should demand a full audit of Colorado's voter rolls and clear answers on citizenship verification protocols before 2026.
