# Appeals Court Blocks Release of Palestinian Green Card Holder Detained Over Pro-Palestinian Activism

A federal appeals court has reversed a lower court's order to release Mohsen Mahdawi, a Columbia University student and Palestinian green card holder detained by the Trump administration, finding the lower court lacked jurisdiction to intervene in the case.

The 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled Tuesday that Mahdawi's claims should have been resolved through proper immigration channels rather than federal court. The decision marks a significant win for immigration enforcement advocates who argue that national security and immigration law must take precedence over activist sympathies.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and immigration hawks contend that green card holders retain obligations to U.S. law and that pro-Palestinian activism—particularly on campuses—warrants scrutiny when tied to potential security concerns. The appeals court's deference to executive immigration authority reflects the proper constitutional balance, they argue.

**Democrats counter** that the ruling threatens due process rights and sets a troubling precedent for detaining individuals based on political speech and activism.

**Colorado angle:** The case underscores ongoing tensions over campus activism and immigration enforcement that directly affect Colorado universities, particularly CU Boulder and the University of Colorado system, where pro-Palestinian demonstrations have intensified.
