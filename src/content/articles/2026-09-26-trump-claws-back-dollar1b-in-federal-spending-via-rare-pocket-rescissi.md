# Trump Claws Back $1B in Unspent Federal Spending—Second 'Pocket Rescission' in 50 Years

Trump administration just deployed a rarely used power to yank nearly $1 billion in previously approved federal spending, targeting DEI programs, immigration initiatives, and foreign aid—a move that has Republicans cheering and Democrats crying foul over process.

The president issued what's only the second "pocket rescission" in five decades (9News Denver), a legal maneuver allowing him to request Congress rescind funding, then withhold it for 45 days while lawmakers decide whether to approve, reject, or ignore the move (Just The News). The $810 million cut targets immigration and refugee programs, foreign debt-relief initiatives, and business grants (Washington Examiner).

**Republicans' case:** Conservatives argue this is exactly the kind of fiscal discipline Washington needs. Trump is using existing legal authority to eliminate wasteful spending Congress approved but never spent—a straightforward exercise in executive restraint.

**Democrats' complaint:** Sen. Susan Collins, the Republican chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, complained the action came "without warning or consultation" (9News), signaling even some GOP lawmakers want a seat at the table.

**Colorado angle:** Federal spending cuts ripple through state budgets and contractor networks; watch whether Colorado nonprofits or businesses receiving federal grants face disruption.

Conservatives should monitor whether Congress actually blocks any rescissions or rubber-stamps Trump's cuts.
