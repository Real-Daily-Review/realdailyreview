# Wyndham Clark Wins US Open Despite Sports Psychologist's Mid-Tournament Exit

Colorado golfer Wyndham Clark won the US Open at Oakmont, but not before his sports psychologist abandoned him mid-tournament—a stunning lapse in mental-health support when Clark needed it most.

Clark, who faced public criticism last year after smashing a locker following a missed cut, appeared headed for another collapse before steadying himself to avoid a record meltdown and secure the championship (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The Daily Caller flagged the bizarre decision by his psychologist to leave Clark without support during the tournament, raising questions about the professional's judgment and commitment.

Clark's comeback matters beyond golf: it's a Colorado success story of grit and resilience in the face of adversity—exactly the kind of character-driven narrative that resonates with Coloradans who value toughness and self-reliance over excuses.

The incident also underscores a broader conservative skepticism about reliance on specialized mental-health consultants. While sports psychology has its place, Clark's victory suggests that old-fashioned determination and personal accountability still trump outsourced emotional coaching.
