# Trump's Iran Bluff Backfiring as Negotiations Stall

President Trump's repeated threats of military action against Iran without follow-through are undermining American negotiating power and prolonging a costly standoff, according to conservative analysts.

The Washington Examiner editorial board argues that Trump's pattern of promising "major strikes" only to back off has stripped away U.S. leverage. By constantly threatening without executing, the administration has made Iran *less* likely to negotiate in good faith—the opposite of intended effect. National Review notes that Iran, emboldened by these empty threats, shows little willingness to accept any diplomatic settlement.

The core conservative critique: credibility matters in foreign policy. When a president repeatedly warns of military action and then doesn't follow through, adversaries learn to ignore the warnings. Iran has calculated that American threats are hollow, removing the teeth from diplomacy.

For Colorado, this matters. Extended Middle East conflicts drive energy prices higher—bad news for families filling up at the pump and businesses managing fuel costs. Prolonged military entanglement also diverts resources from domestic priorities conservatives prioritize: border security, infrastructure, and economic growth.

The real test comes next: whether Trump recalibrates his Iran strategy with credible consequences, or whether the bluffing continues to erode American standing in the region.
