# Tennessee's Botched Execution Exposes the Real Problem: We've Lost Focus on Victims

Tennessee's failed execution of death row inmate Christa Pike—who laughed, sang, and reportedly slept through two rounds of lethal injection—has reignited the capital punishment debate, but conservatives say the real scandal is how America has become obsessed with the perpetrator instead of the victim.

Pike murdered a 19-year-old girl in 1995. Yet the botched procedure has dominated headlines, with Pike's bizarre behavior during the execution drawing national attention and sympathy. Former Knoxville Police Chief Phil Keith pushed back hard, telling The Daily Wire that the public's fixation on Pike represents a dangerous cultural shift toward "glorifying the offender" while the victim's family is forgotten.

President Trump, campaigning in Texas, cut through the noise: executing someone shouldn't be "very difficult," he told Rep. Ronny Jackson.

Conservatives argue the real issue isn't whether executions are uncomfortable for murderers—it's whether we've lost sight of justice for victims and their families. The endless legal challenges, procedural delays, and media spectacle surrounding capital cases have made swift justice nearly impossible, leaving families in limbo for decades.

For Colorado, which abolished capital punishment in 2020, this serves as a reminder: the death penalty debate often obscures a harder question about whether our justice system prioritizes victims at all.
