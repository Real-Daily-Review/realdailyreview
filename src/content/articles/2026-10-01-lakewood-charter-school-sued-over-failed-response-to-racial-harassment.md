# Lakewood Charter School Faces Lawsuit Over Racial Harassment, Mishandled Response

A Black student is suing Addenbrooke Classical Academy in Lakewood after school leaders allegedly failed to stop racial harassment and, according to the complaint, encouraged him to respond with violence instead of addressing the problem. The lawsuit names the school, principal, and former executive director, claiming they did not investigate or prevent harassment that included a "slave auction" written in a school bathroom (Colorado Sun, CPR News).

The case highlights a critical failure: when students engage in racist conduct, school administrators have a duty to investigate and stop it—not tell a victim to "hit, punch or slug" bullies. This approach exposes the school to liability and leaves a student without protection.

Charter schools operate with public funding and should be held to the same accountability standards as traditional public schools. Parents deserve assurance that their children—regardless of race—will be safe and that administrators will handle serious incidents professionally and promptly. If Addenbrooke failed to document complaints, investigate thoroughly, or take corrective action, that's a governance failure that Colorado's charter school authorizers need to examine.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether this case prompts meaningful accountability measures and whether the school's authorizer reviews its oversight practices.
