# Republicans Hold Line on Iran, Reject Symbolic War Rebuke

Senate Republicans narrowly defeated a symbolic measure Thursday to end the war in Iran, voting 51-48 largely along party lines in what the *Washington Examiner* calls the first unity test for the GOP since the election. The vote signals Republicans remain skeptical of constraining presidential war powers, even as the party absorbed losses in the Senate and elsewhere.

Meanwhile, Rep. Nick Begich held Alaska's lone House seat after ranked-choice tabulation—a rare Republican victory in an otherwise Democratic wave, according to *Fox News*. Begich's win offers conservatives a modest morale boost after a difficult cycle.

For Colorado, these developments matter. Senate Republicans' stance on executive war authority and foreign policy will shape how Colorado's delegation votes on defense spending, military installations, and veterans' benefits. Colorado hosts significant military infrastructure and a large veteran population; how Republicans navigate presidential power and military commitments directly affects state interests.

Conservatives argue that preserving presidential flexibility on national security prevents Congress from hamstringing the commander-in-chief during crises. Democrats counter that symbolic rebukes assert congressional war powers and constrain executive overreach.
