# Appeals Court Orders Release of Biden Memoir Tapes in Classified Documents Case

A federal appeals court has rejected former President Joe Biden's attempt to suppress audio recordings and transcripts from his memoir work—materials that Special Counsel Robert Hur examined during his investigation into Biden's handling of classified documents.

The D.C. Circuit Court ruled Monday that Biden failed to demonstrate he would likely succeed on the merits of his request to block release (Just The News). The court found the public has a strong interest in evaluating Hur's decision not to charge the former president, according to the Washington Examiner.

The tapes, held by the Justice Department, have been the subject of a lengthy legal battle. Biden's legal team sought emergency intervention to keep the recordings and transcripts sealed, but the appeals court majority rejected that bid.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans and watchdog groups have long contended that transparency around the classified documents probe is essential to public trust. The court's decision underscores that even a former president cannot use executive privilege to indefinitely suppress materials relevant to a criminal investigation—a principle conservatives say should apply equally regardless of party affiliation.

**What's next:** Coloradans should monitor whether these materials reveal any pattern of mishandling classified information that might inform ongoing debates over presidential accountability and the rule of law.
