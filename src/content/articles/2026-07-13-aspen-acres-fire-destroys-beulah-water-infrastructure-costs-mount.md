# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has left the small community of Beulah without reliable water service—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis now threatens the utilities and infrastructure that keep towns functioning. According to the Colorado Sun, water managers are scrambling to restore clean water supplies after the blaze damaged critical systems serving the roughly 800-person community. CPR News reports many homes were lost as residents fought to protect what remained.

This isn't just a housing crisis. When wildfires destroy water infrastructure, the cascading costs fall on local taxpayers and state agencies already stretched thin. Beulah's water teams face a long reconstruction road, and the question looms: who pays? State Democrats have pushed billions in new spending on fire prevention and climate programs, yet Colorado communities still lack hardened infrastructure and adequate firefighting resources on the ground.

Republicans and conservatives argue that before greenlight­ing more regulatory schemes and climate spending, the state must prioritize basic resilience—hardening water systems, improving forest management on state and federal lands, and ensuring local fire departments have real funding and equipment.

The Colorado Sun notes most other active fires have spared water systems so far—but that luck won't hold forever. Watch whether state lawmakers demand accountability on how existing fire-prevention dollars were spent before approving new ones.
