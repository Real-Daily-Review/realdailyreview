# Michigan Democrat's 9/11 Posts Expose Left's Double Standard on Extremism

Michigan Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed faces mounting scrutiny over deleted social media posts from around the 9/11 anniversary, with conservatives arguing the mainstream media is running interference for a progressive candidate it favors.

According to the Washington Examiner, CBS's Bari Weiss documented how El-Sayed's controversial posts were scrubbed from the internet, yet the left-leaning media establishment has largely downplayed the controversy rather than demand answers. Republicans argue this represents a glaring double standard: similar rhetoric from Republican candidates would trigger wall-to-wall condemnation and calls for withdrawal from the race.

The Daily Wire reports that Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA)—hardly a conservative voice—challenged El-Sayed to campaign alongside far-left streamer Hasan Piker on the 25th anniversary of 9/11, offering $100,000 to make the point. Fetterman's stunt underscores how even Democrats recognize the toxicity of El-Sayed's associations and past statements.

Conservatives argue the episode reveals how progressives enforce a selective moral code: extremist rhetoric is acceptable when it advances the left's agenda, but unacceptable when it becomes a political liability. The media's muted response, they contend, proves the press functions as a Democratic operative rather than an impartial watchdog.
