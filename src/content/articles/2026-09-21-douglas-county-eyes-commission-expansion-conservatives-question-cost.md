# Douglas County Voters Face Push to Expand County Commission—and Bigger Government Payroll

Douglas County residents will decide whether to grow their county commission, a move that would increase government overhead as the region booms. Boulder County is pursuing the same expansion, with commissioners arguing that population growth justifies more seats and staff (Denver Post).

**The conservative case against expansion is straightforward:** More commissioners mean higher salaries, benefits, and administrative costs—ultimately paid by Douglas County taxpayers already facing rising property values and assessments. Proponents like Boulder County Commissioner Ashley Stolzmann claim growth demands "more representation," but Republicans counter that efficient government doesn't automatically scale with population. Existing commissioners can represent larger constituencies without multiplying the bureaucracy.

Douglas County has grown substantially, and the commission expansion pitch will likely frame this as necessary modernization. But conservatives should ask: Where's the evidence that current commissioners can't handle the workload? What's the actual cost to taxpayers? Will this commission bloat lead to more regulations on development, land use, and business?

This ballot measure tests whether Colorado voters will reflexively expand government when presented with growth arguments—or demand proof that bigger government actually serves them better.

**Watch whether Douglas County Republicans organize opposition based on cost-benefit analysis and taxpayer impact.**
