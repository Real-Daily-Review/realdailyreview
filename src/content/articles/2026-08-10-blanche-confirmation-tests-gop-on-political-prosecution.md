# Blanche Confirmation Tests GOP on Prosecutorial Independence

Todd Blanche's confirmation as Attorney General now forces Republicans to prove they won't weaponize the Justice Department against political opponents—even as Democrats warn the Trump administration will do exactly that.

The narrow confirmation hinged on Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), whose decisive vote reflected GOP anxiety about the optics. Cassidy called it a "gradation of gray" rather than a clear-cut decision, signaling discomfort with the political minefield ahead (Washington Examiner). The real test arrives immediately: Blanche must decide whether to prosecute the contempt-of-Congress case against Anthony Fauci, whom House Republicans voted to hold in contempt.

Democrats are already framing any prosecution as "weaponized" justice—the same accusation they leveled at Trump's first term (Fox News). Republicans counter that Fauci faces legitimate accountability for his role in pandemic policy decisions and alleged misleading testimony to Congress.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters: the Blanche confirmation signals whether the Trump administration will actually challenge the institutional left's two-tiered justice system or merely talk about it. A Fauci prosecution would demonstrate resolve; a pass would confirm GOP fears about institutional capture.

Watch whether Blanche's DOJ pursues the Fauci case or shelves it—that decision will define his tenure and test Republican credibility on equal justice.
