# South Carolina GOP Senate Race Tightens as Trump-Backed Nordone Faces Primary Challenge

President Trump's endorsement of Sen. Darline Graham Nordone isn't enough to lock down South Carolina's Republican primary for Lindsey Graham's U.S. Senate seat ahead of Tuesday's special election, according to polling cited by the Washington Examiner and Just The News.

Nordone, who was appointed by South Carolina's governor to fill her late brother's unexpired term, faces a competitive field that includes challengers Norman, Fry, and Sanford. Despite Trump's backing—a typically decisive advantage in GOP primaries—the race remains tight, suggesting the Trump brand's power has limits even among Republican voters.

The contest reflects broader tensions within the GOP over who best represents the party's future direction. Nordone's appointment rather than electoral victory may be working against her with primary voters who prefer candidates who've earned their nomination through grassroots support.

For Colorado Republicans, the South Carolina race offers a cautionary tale: endorsements alone don't guarantee primary success, and appointed candidates face skepticism from the base. As Colorado heads into its own election cycles, GOP candidates will need more than establishment backing to energize voters.
