# Colorado's Renewables 'Milestone' Masks the Real Story: Coal Collapse, Not Green Success

Colorado's celebrated shift toward renewable energy generation owes far more to the decline of coal than to any genuine expansion of wind and solar capacity, according to analysis from Complete Colorado. While state officials trumpet hitting a "majority renewables" threshold—bolstered by a new 120-megawatt solar farm on school trust land in Pueblo County (Colorado Sun)—the narrative glosses over an uncomfortable truth: coal generation has simply cratered, creating the statistical illusion of green progress.

Conservatives argue this distinction matters enormously. Colorado's energy transition wasn't driven by market demand or technological breakthroughs; it was engineered by state policy favoring renewables while strangling coal operations. The result: lost jobs in coal communities, higher electricity costs for ratepayers, and grid reliability concerns that regulators have largely downplayed.

The Mirasol solar development, which the state Land Board approved for school trust land, exemplifies how Colorado's progressive energy agenda prioritizes environmental mandates over fiscal stewardship. School trust lands exist to fund education—not to serve as testing grounds for renewable-energy experiments that may ultimately burden Colorado's power grid and wallet.
