# U.S. Fires on Iranian Tankers as Fragile Ceasefire Strains

U.S. forces fired on and disabled two Iranian oil tankers Friday in the Strait of Hormuz after exchanging fire with Iranian forces, according to NPR and CBS News, as a month-old ceasefire between the countries shows signs of fraying.

The U.S. military said the tankers were attempting to violate an American blockade of Iranian oil exports. The strikes came as the United Arab Emirates reported a separate Iranian missile and drone attack, according to NPR.

The incident complicates ongoing diplomatic efforts. Secretary of State Rubio was in Rome urging European allies to help reopen the strait, according to PBS NewsHour, while the U.S. maintains the ceasefire remains officially in effect—a characterization PBS described as "tenuous."

The blockade has already disrupted global energy markets. Pakistan cancelled plans to buy liquefied natural gas on the spot market, expecting Gulf shipments to resume once the strait reopens, according to the Financial Times.

The tanker strikes raise questions about whether military enforcement of the blockade will undermine negotiations to end the broader conflict between the U.S. and Iran.
