# Flawed Surveillance Tech Leaves Colorado Woman Fighting False Theft Accusation

A Colorado woman is suing Flock Safety and local police after license plate camera data wrongly tagged her as a package thief—a cautionary tale about government reliance on unvetted surveillance technology.

Chrisanna Elser filed suit a year after police showed up at her door with accusations based on Flock's automated system. The case highlights a growing conservative concern: law enforcement increasingly outsources investigative work to private surveillance vendors without adequate safeguards, leaving innocent citizens to prove their innocence rather than police proving guilt.

According to 9News Denver, Elser's lawsuit names Flock Safety, the police department, and the officer involved. The case underscores how algorithmic errors—compounded by police failure to verify data before acting—can violate due process and damage reputations.

Republicans and civil libertarians have long warned that surveillance infrastructure, even when well-intentioned, creates liability when accuracy isn't guaranteed. The burden falls on taxpayers and innocent residents to fight back in court. Colorado law enforcement agencies must establish clear protocols: verify camera data independently before confronting suspects, and hold vendors accountable for false positives.
