# Colorado Education Co-op Faces Dissolution After Failed Christian School Experiment

A Colorado education cooperative is heading toward the chopping block after its controversial venture into publicly funded Christian education found no buyers—a cautionary tale for government-run school experiments gone wrong.

The Eastern Region Board of Cooperative Educational Services (ERBOCES) opened what it billed as a "public Christian school," but the initiative flopped spectacularly. According to CPR News, one board member declared the lack of interest "a collective, statewide referendum on ERBOCES," signaling the group's dissolution is warranted.

The failure underscores a broader conservative argument: when government runs schools—whether traditional or experimental—accountability suffers and taxpayer dollars get wasted on failed initiatives. Republicans and school-choice advocates point to this as evidence that education dollars should follow students, not bureaucracies.

National Review notes that Education Savings Accounts (ESAs)—which let parents control education spending directly—offer a market-based alternative to government-managed systems. Unlike tax-credit scholarships, ESAs align with broader tax reform and genuine parental choice.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state lawmakers learn the right lesson: that dissolving failed government education ventures is progress, but the real solution is empowering families to choose schools that work—not waiting for the next experiment to fail.
