# Iran Closes Strait of Hormuz After US Strikes; Energy Markets Brace for Shock

Iran's regime announced Wednesday it is fully closing the Strait of Hormuz to all vessel traffic—including oil tankers—following U.S. military strikes on multiple targets, escalating a confrontation that threatens global energy supplies and Colorado's oil and gas economy.

The closure, if enforced, would choke one of the world's most critical shipping lanes. According to the Washington Examiner, Iran declared the strait "closed to the traffic of any type of vessel" due to what Tehran claims is regional "insecurity." National Review notes the Iranian regime has proved "more resilient and resourceful than anticipated," complicating Trump administration strategy.

**The Colorado angle:** A sustained Hormuz closure would spike global oil prices, affecting Colorado's energy sector—from production economics to downstream refining and petrochemical operations. Higher energy costs ripple through the state's economy and household budgets.

**What conservatives argue:** Republicans contend that weakness invites aggression; a credible deterrent posture prevents escalation. The Biden-era approach of appeasement and the Iran nuclear deal's collapse created space for Tehran's adventurism. Strength, not negotiation from a position of weakness, restores stability.

Democrats and the State Department typically emphasize diplomatic off-ramps and de-escalation, though current circumstances leave little room for that framing.

Watch whether Iran actually enforces the blockade and how U.S. naval assets respond—the next 72 hours will signal whether this is brinkmanship or genuine economic warfare.
