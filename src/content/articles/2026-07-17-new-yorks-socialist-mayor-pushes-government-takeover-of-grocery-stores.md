# New York Mayor's Government-Takeover Agenda Shows Why Colorado Must Guard Against Socialist Overreach

New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani's push for government-run grocery stores—and now talk of state-controlled airlines—is a cautionary tale for Colorado taxpayers watching progressive activists expand government's footprint nationwide.

Mamdani, a self-described socialist six months into his term, is pitching New Yorkers on the idea that government should run essential services. Local bodega owners and small business operators are rightfully alarmed, according to the Washington Examiner. City entrepreneurs see the scheme as government crowding out private enterprise, while taxpayers worry about the inevitable cost explosion when bureaucrats replace market competition.

This isn't abstract ideology. Colorado has its own battles over government overreach—from regulatory expansion in energy and agriculture to tax-and-spend schemes that burden working families and small business owners. When progressive mayors and state officials talk about "public options" and government-run services, they're borrowing directly from Mamdani's playbook: use government power to displace private enterprise, jack up costs, and expand the dependency class.

Republicans argue that free markets, not government monopolies, deliver better service at lower prices. When government runs grocery stores or airlines, innovation dies, costs soar, and taxpayers foot the bill for inefficiency.

Colorado conservatives should watch whether similar "public option" proposals gain traction in Denver or Boulder—and be ready to fight them.
