# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfires Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push hungry wildlife into residential areas at unprecedented rates, forcing a grim choice between public safety and animal management.

The surge reflects a cascade of environmental pressures: prolonged drought has decimated natural food sources, while wildfires have destroyed habitat and forced bears downslope into populated zones. Residents report record human-bear contacts, leaving state wildlife officials with limited options. When bears become habituated to human food and pose safety risks, euthanasia often becomes the default response.

Conservatives and property owners argue that Colorado's land-use policies and forest management failures have worsened the crisis. Years of fire-suppression policies and resistance to active timber management have left forests overstocked and vulnerable to catastrophic burns. Additionally, some point to environmental regulations that restrict controlled burns and thinning projects—tools that could reduce wildfire severity and maintain healthier bear habitat.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that climate change and drought are the primary drivers, requiring long-term climate action rather than increased logging.

The real issue: Colorado's regulatory approach to forest management has left the state unprepared for the ecological consequences of megafires and prolonged drought. Taxpayers should watch whether state lawmakers will finally prioritize aggressive forest thinning and prescribed burns over environmental litigation.
