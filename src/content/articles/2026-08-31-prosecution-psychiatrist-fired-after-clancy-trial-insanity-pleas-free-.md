# Prosecution Psychiatrist Fired After Clancy Trial Testimony; Insanity Pleas Keep Freeing Killer Parents

A key prosecution witness in the Lindsay Clancy infanticide case has been terminated from his position at Nemours Children's Health, raising questions about witness intimidation and the reliability of expert testimony in high-profile criminal trials (Daily Wire, Daily Caller). Dr. Avram Mack, a psychiatrist who testified for prosecutors and clashed repeatedly with the defense, is now "no longer employed" by the Delaware hospital as jurors deliberate Clancy's fate.

The timing is troubling. Meanwhile, another mother—Dimone Fleming of New York City—walked free on an insanity plea after fatally stabbing her two young sons (Daily Wire). The pattern exposes a dangerous trend: courts increasingly accepting mental-health defenses that effectively excuse infanticide, while witnesses who challenge such narratives face professional consequences.

Conservatives argue this reflects a broader failure of the criminal justice system to hold violent offenders accountable, particularly when progressive judges and defense attorneys weaponize psychiatric testimony to circumvent justice. When expert witnesses face termination for testifying against defendants, the integrity of trials collapses—and dangerous individuals return to society.

Colorado families should watch whether similar insanity-plea patterns emerge in state courts, and whether our own expert witnesses face retaliation for inconvenient testimony.
