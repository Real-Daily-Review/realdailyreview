# Colorado Cities' Sanctuary Policies Create Constitutional Clash Over Immigration Enforcement

Denver and Boulder's sanctuary city policies are colliding with federal immigration authority—and the Constitution may have the final say.

The Washington Examiner reports that while conservatives oppose sanctuary cities on principle, the Constitution's Naturalization Clause grants Congress plenary power over immigration law. Article I, Section 8 explicitly vests Congress with authority to "establish an uniform Rule of Naturalization throughout the United States," and the Supreme Court has long upheld this federal dominion.

Here's the Colorado angle: Denver's refusal to cooperate with federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detainers puts city taxpayers at odds with federal law. When local police decline to hold undocumented immigrants for federal pickup, they're effectively nullifying Congress's immigration statutes—a power cities don't constitutionally possess.

Republicans argue that sanctuary policies undermine the rule of law and shift enforcement costs to states and counties. Conservatives counter that cities cannot simply opt out of federal law, no matter how progressive their politics.

The legal tension is real: cities claim local control; the Constitution says immigration is a federal matter. Colorado taxpayers should watch whether federal courts force Denver and Boulder to comply with ICE detainers—or whether progressive cities continue defying Washington's authority.
