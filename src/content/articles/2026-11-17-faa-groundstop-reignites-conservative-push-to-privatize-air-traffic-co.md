# FAA's Latest Failure Reignites Push to Privatize Air Traffic Control

A nationwide four-hour groundstop that disrupted at least 18,000 flights over the weekend has exposed what conservatives have long argued: the federal government cannot competently manage critical infrastructure.

The FAA's communications breakdown—serious enough to warrant a safety halt—demonstrates the agency's inability to modernize and operate efficiently. *National Review* argues the solution is clear: privatize air traffic control. Conservatives contend that a government monopoly on this essential service leaves no accountability when systems fail and no market pressure to innovate.

Republicans and free-market advocates say the FAA's repeated failures justify removing air traffic control from Washington's political dysfunction. *Washington Examiner* editors note the disruption "should be a wake-up call for American aviation," arguing that critical infrastructure shouldn't be held hostage to budget fights and bureaucratic gridlock.

While the FAA deserves credit for prioritizing safety by grounding flights, conservatives argue that's precisely why private operators—answerable to liability and competition—could deliver both safety *and* reliability without the government's track record of delays and modernization failures.

For Colorado travelers and businesses dependent on Denver International Airport, repeated FAA failures mean lost productivity and economic drag. Watch whether Congress moves to separate air traffic control from the FAA's political entanglements.
