# Turkey Arms Deal Raises Red Flags for U.S. Security

The Biden administration's consideration of F-35 fighter jet sales to Turkey has sparked sharp pushback from defense hawks who argue the NATO ally cannot be trusted with advanced American military technology.

National Review warns that selling F-35s to Turkey would "needlessly jeopardize American security and service personnel," citing Ankara's unreliable track record and strategic ambiguity in Middle Eastern conflicts. The Washington Examiner goes further, questioning whether Turkey qualifies as a genuine U.S. ally at all, noting the country's past flirtation with joining conflicts against American interests and Israel.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and defense experts argue Turkey's NATO membership doesn't guarantee loyalty. The country has purchased Russian air defense systems, maintained ties to hostile regimes, and demonstrated willingness to pivot away from Western interests when politically convenient. Arming Ankara with cutting-edge fighter jets risks compromising classified American technology and emboldening a partner whose geopolitical alignment remains questionable.

**The administration's position:** The White House frames the sale as strengthening NATO cohesion and deterring Russian aggression in the region—a rationale conservatives reject as naive given Turkey's demonstrated unreliability.

**What to watch:** Whether Congress blocks the sale through defense appropriations riders or whether the administration proceeds despite bipartisan skepticism.
