# DC Gym Shooting Reveals Dangerous Pattern of Grievance-Fueled Violence

A Washington, D.C. gunman's rampage—which killed building manager James Stuart and wounded an innocent mother walking her dog—exposes how personal grievances can metastasize into deadly violence when left unchecked by law enforcement and mental health systems.

According to the Daily Caller, shooter Matt Lambert referenced Luigi Mangione, the UnitedHealthcare CEO killer, during a pre-attack interview with a journalist, claiming his "manifesto is an engineering report." The New York Post reports that Susan Hamerski was seriously injured while simply walking a dog during Lambert's attack at a gym facility.

Lambert's case mirrors the Mangione incident: both men nursed documented complaints—Lambert over apartment mold and HOA disputes—before turning to violence. The pattern is troubling: grievance + ideological radicalization + access to firearms = tragedy.

**The conservative concern:** While Second Amendment rights are sacrosanct, this case underscores the failure of threat assessment and mental health intervention. Lambert's documented complaints and public statements preceded his attack. Where were the warning systems? Why didn't authorities act on observable red flags?

Colorado law enforcement and mental health agencies should review protocols for identifying and intervening with individuals displaying similar patterns of escalating grievance and violent rhetoric before they reach the trigger-pull stage.

Watch whether federal authorities use this incident to push new gun restrictions rather than address the actual breakdown in threat assessment and mental health response.
