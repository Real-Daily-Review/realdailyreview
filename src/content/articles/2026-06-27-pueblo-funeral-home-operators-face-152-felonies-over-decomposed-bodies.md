# Colorado Funeral Home Horror: 152 Felonies After Bodies Left to Decompose

A former Pueblo County coroner and his brother face 152 felony charges each after state inspectors discovered decomposed bodies, cremains, bones, and human tissue at their funeral home during a routine inspection Thursday, according to the Colorado Sun.

Brian and Christopher Cotter operated Davis Mortuary in Pueblo. The charges stem from what authorities describe as gross mishandling of human remains—a failure that raises serious questions about regulatory oversight of Colorado funeral homes and whether state inspection protocols are adequate to catch such egregious violations before families are left devastated.

The Cotters' case represents a catastrophic breach of trust. Families entrust funeral homes with their loved ones' remains during their most vulnerable moments. When a state-licensed operation allows bodies to decompose on premises, it exposes a critical gap: either inspectors weren't doing their jobs, or the funeral industry lacks sufficient accountability mechanisms.

Conservatives and taxpayers should demand answers on two fronts: Why did state regulators miss this? And what reforms are needed to ensure Colorado's funeral licensing board actually protects consumers without creating burdensome new regulations that punish legitimate operators?

Watch for the state's response and whether the Funeral Service Board tightens inspection frequency and enforcement.
