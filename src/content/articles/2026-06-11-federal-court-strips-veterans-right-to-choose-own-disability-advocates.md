# Federal Court Strips Veterans' Right to Choose Their Own Disability Advocates

A federal court has ruled that veterans cannot independently select their own representatives to help navigate disability benefits claims—a decision that conservatives say exemplifies government overreach at the expense of those who served.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the ruling restricts veterans' freedom to choose advocates of their choice, forcing them instead into a system controlled by federal bureaucracy. The decision has drawn sharp criticism from those who argue it undermines individual liberty and delays critical support for disabled service members.

Republicans and conservative veterans' advocates argue the ruling prioritizes government control over veteran autonomy. They contend that veterans—who sacrificed for the nation—should retain the fundamental right to select representatives they trust, whether private advocates, VSOs, or attorneys. The restriction, they say, creates unnecessary barriers to benefits that veterans have already earned through their service.

The ruling also raises concerns about Colorado's veteran population, which includes hundreds of thousands of former service members who depend on disability benefits for healthcare, housing, and living expenses. Conservatives warn the decision could slow claims processing and limit access to experienced private advocates who often expedite approvals.

Watch for potential legislative pushback from Republican lawmakers seeking to restore veterans' choice in representation.
