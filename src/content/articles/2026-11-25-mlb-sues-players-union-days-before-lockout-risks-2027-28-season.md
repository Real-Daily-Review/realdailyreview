# MLB Escalates Labor War With Preemptive Lawsuit Against Players' Union

Major League Baseball has filed suit against its players' association days before labor contracts expire, risking the 2027-28 season in what amounts to a high-stakes legal gambit to prevent union dissolution (Washington Examiner). The 38-page complaint, filed in U.S. District Court in Manhattan, names the players' association and eight union executives, including player Tarik Skubal, as defendants (New York Post).

MLB's move is a calculated preemptive strike—a tactic designed to box in the union before it can dissolve itself as a negotiating weapon. The strategy mirrors past labor disputes where unions have threatened to disband to avoid lockout injunctions.

**The conservative take:** While labor disputes are typically outside Colorado's direct purview, this case illustrates how litigation increasingly replaces genuine negotiation in American labor markets. Both sides—management and union—use courts and legal threats rather than compromise. The real losers are fans and the sport itself.

For Colorado baseball fans and the state's tourism economy (which benefits from spring training and regular-season games), a lockout means lost revenue and disrupted schedules. The dispute also underscores how union power, even in professional sports, can prioritize litigation over the interests of workers and consumers alike.

**Watch for:** Whether federal courts intervene to prevent a lockout or allow the dispute to play out, and whether the 2027-28 season gets canceled.
