# Colorado's First Winter Weather Advisory Signals End to Unseasonable Fall Heat

Colorado is bracing for its first significant winter weather event of the season, with the National Weather Service issuing a Winter Weather Advisory as rain and snow move across the state through Thursday.

The advisory marks a sharp turn from Colorado's record-breaking fall temperatures. According to CPR News, the state has endured unseasonably warm conditions, but cooler air is finally pushing in this week. 9News reports that snow is already falling at Colorado ski areas, while mountain travel faces impacts from the incoming system.

Denver and lower elevations will see rain, while the high country can expect accumulating snow. The timing is typical for early October mountain weather but comes after weeks of anomalously warm conditions across the Front Range and Western Slope.

For Colorado's outdoor recreation economy and ski industry, the early snow is welcome news—resorts can begin their seasons sooner rather than later. Water managers will also monitor snowfall as a potential contributor to Colorado's ongoing drought concerns, though a single storm provides limited relief to long-term water supply challenges.

Coloradans should watch whether this pattern shift holds or if warm weather reasserts itself, which would affect everything from winter tourism to reservoir management heading into 2025.
