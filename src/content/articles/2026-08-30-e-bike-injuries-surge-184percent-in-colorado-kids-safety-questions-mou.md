# E-Bike Surge Leaves Colorado Kids Injured—and Parents Asking Who's Liable

Colorado is facing a sharp rise in serious e-bike injuries among children, with Children's Hospital Colorado reporting a 184% increase in traumatic cases from 2024 to 2025 (Colorado Sun). The trend has alarmed doctors and law enforcement across the state, raising questions about safety standards, parental responsibility, and whether government mandates or liability rules need tightening.

The injuries come as e-bikes and e-scooters have proliferated in Colorado communities, particularly in urban areas. While advocates tout them as eco-friendly transportation, conservatives and safety officials are asking harder questions: Are manufacturers adequately warning users about speed and handling risks? Should parents bear clearer liability for unsupervised riders? Do local governments need stricter regulations on where these devices operate?

Democrats and progressive cities have largely embraced e-bikes as part of climate and mobility agendas, resisting restrictions. Republicans and safety advocates counter that market-driven safety standards and parental accountability—not government subsidies or bike-lane expansions—should drive policy.

A separate incident in Fort Collins underscored the dangers: a pedestrian was struck and killed by a pickup truck during the Tour de Fat event; police said the driver did not target pedestrians (9News Denver).
