# Twenty-Five Years After 9/11: A Generation Learns What We Nearly Forgot

A quarter-century after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students born years after the towers fell are grappling with a national trauma that has become history rather than lived memory—and conservatives worry the lessons of that day are slipping away.

CPR News reports that younger Coloradans are encountering 9/11 through education and reflection, not experience. That matters. The attacks exposed vulnerabilities in American security and intelligence that demanded serious, sustained vigilance. Yet as the immediacy fades, so does the clarity about the threats that demanded our response.

National Review warns that while "the terror is past," ideological threats persist: Sharia supremacism continues to advance globally, even as Americans grow complacent. The Washington Examiner underscores the point by recalling how British intelligence prevented a potential mass-casualty attack just five years after 9/11—a plot that could have dwarfed the original tragedy had it succeeded.

Conservatives argue that generational amnesia about terrorism's reality leaves America vulnerable. Counterterrorism requires sustained commitment, not nostalgia. The question isn't whether we remember 9/11—it's whether we maintain the vigilance that prevents the next one.

Watch whether Colorado schools and national security officials prioritize genuine counterterrorism education or retreat into sanitized historical commemoration.
