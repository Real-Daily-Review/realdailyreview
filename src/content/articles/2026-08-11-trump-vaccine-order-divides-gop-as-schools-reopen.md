# Trump's Vaccine Order Sparks GOP Infighting as Schools Reopen

President Trump has signed an executive order directing a revamp of childhood vaccine recommendations, proposing separate shots across different visits rather than combination vaccines—a move that immediately divided Republicans and drew fire from medical professionals as measles cases resurface nationwide.

The order directs the health department to improve vaccine research and alter administration protocols. Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-La.) quickly pushed back, arguing the president lacks "expertise to make these changes" (Just The News). Medical establishment figures have similarly questioned the scientific basis for altering proven vaccine schedules.

**The conservative case for caution:** Republicans skeptical of the order argue that vaccine policy should rest on rigorous science, not executive mandate. Conservatives also worry about federal overreach into medical decisions—even when the executive branch is conservative-led. The principle of limiting government power applies across administrations.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** As schools reopen statewide, any disruption to vaccine protocols could affect enrollment requirements and public health responses. Colorado parents and school administrators will face confusion if federal guidance conflicts with state health department standards.

Conservatives should watch whether this order survives legal challenge and whether it creates a template for future health-policy executive orders that bypass Congress and the regulatory process.
