# Stubborn 3.4% Inflation Haunts Trump Heading Into Midterms

Colorado families hitting the grocery store and gas pump know the score: prices aren't falling fast enough, and voters are noticing. The Commerce Department's August inflation report shows the year-over-year rate stuck at 3.4%—unchanged from July—a figure analysts are calling "stubbornly high" (Washington Examiner).

For President Trump, who campaigned on controlling inflation, the stalled progress is a political liability heading into the midterm elections. Voters care less about whether inflation is *slowing* and more about whether their paychecks stretch further at the checkout line. In Colorado, where energy costs and housing affordability already strain household budgets, persistent 3.4% inflation compounds the squeeze on working families and retirees on fixed incomes.

Republicans argue that Biden-era spending and regulatory overreach—including aggressive green-energy mandates that have driven up energy costs—fueled inflation in the first place. They contend that pro-growth policies, energy independence, and fiscal restraint are the path to genuine price relief.

Democrats counter that inflation is a global phenomenon tied to post-pandemic supply chains and energy markets, not domestic policy.
