# GOP Blindsided by New Details on Trump Butler Shooter's Family Warnings

Republican lawmakers are demanding answers after learning that Thomas Matthew Crooks' father had actively tried to block his son's gun access months before the July assassination attempt on Trump in Butler, Pennsylvania—and that federal authorities failed to share this critical intelligence with the Secret Service task force investigating the threat.

According to Fox News and Just The News, Matthew Crooks sought ways to prevent his son from accessing firearms as early as May 2024, roughly two months before the shooting. Yet the task force was apparently unaware that federal authorities had even interviewed Crooks' family members, raising serious questions about inter-agency coordination and threat assessment protocols.

Republicans argue this represents a catastrophic intelligence failure. If the father's documented concerns about his son's access to guns reached federal investigators but not the protective detail, it suggests systemic breakdown in how threat information flows between agencies—a gap that could have cost a former president his life.

The revelations underscore what conservatives have long maintained: the problem isn't gun access itself, but government's failure to connect existing dots and act on warning signs already in the system.

Watch for congressional hearings demanding full transparency on what federal agencies knew, when they knew it, and why the Secret Service was kept in the dark.
