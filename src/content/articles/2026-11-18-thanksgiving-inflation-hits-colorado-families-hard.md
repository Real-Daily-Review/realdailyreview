# Colorado Families Facing Thanksgiving Sticker Shock as Inflation Squeezes Holiday Budgets

Inflation is hitting Colorado dinner tables hard this Thanksgiving, with fewer Americans willing to foot the bill for holiday meals amid persistent price pressures on groceries and essentials.

According to survey data cited by 9News Denver, financial strain is forcing families to scale back or skip hosting Thanksgiving altogether—a telling sign of how Biden-era inflation continues to erode household purchasing power across the country, including Colorado.

Republicans and conservatives argue that unchecked government spending and the Federal Reserve's monetary expansion have driven inflation that disproportionately hurts working families and fixed-income earners. Democrats counter that supply-chain disruptions and corporate profit-taking bear primary responsibility, not fiscal policy.

For Colorado families already grappling with rising energy costs, property taxes, and housing affordability, the holiday price surge adds insult to injury. Grocers and retailers are offering deals to lure cost-conscious shoppers, but the underlying problem remains: middle-class Coloradans have less discretionary income than they did two years ago.

Conservatives argue that runaway government spending—not market forces—created the conditions for sustained inflation, and that policymakers should prioritize fiscal restraint and sound money over further stimulus.
