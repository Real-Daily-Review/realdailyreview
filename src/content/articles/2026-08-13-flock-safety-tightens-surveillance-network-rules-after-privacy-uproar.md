# Flock Safety Tightens Reins on License Plate Reader Network After Privacy Backlash

Colorado law enforcement agencies relying on Flock Safety's sprawling automatic license plate reader network face new restrictions after the company announced policy changes Thursday amid mounting concerns that the system enables mass surveillance without adequate constitutional safeguards.

Flock CEO Garrett Langley unveiled the modifications following criticism from privacy advocates and civil libertarians who argue the technology—deployed across 120,000+ cameras in 49 states via contracts with over 6,000 municipalities—creates a surveillance infrastructure ripe for abuse (Washington Examiner). The company operates extensively in Colorado, where police departments have adopted the readers for traffic enforcement and criminal investigations.

Conservatives and privacy advocates argue that warrantless access to real-time license plate data threatens Fourth Amendment protections and enables government tracking of citizens' movements without probable cause. Republicans counter that while law enforcement tools are necessary, Flock's system requires stronger guardrails: mandatory warrant requirements, audit trails, and limits on data retention (9News Denver).

Flock's new protocols aim to impose stricter protocols on police access, though critics remain skeptical that voluntary corporate compliance substitutes for statutory protections. Colorado taxpayers should monitor whether state legislators demand legislative safeguards—not corporate promises—to govern how local police deploy this technology.
