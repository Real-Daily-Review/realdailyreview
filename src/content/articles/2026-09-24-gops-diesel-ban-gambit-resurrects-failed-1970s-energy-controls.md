# GOP's Diesel Export Ban Gambit: A Losing Play on Energy Policy

Some farm-state Republicans are pushing a federal ban on diesel exports as a midterm messaging strategy—a move that would resurrect the failed energy controls of the 1970s and risk catastrophic disruption to global supply chains, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The pitch is simple: restrict American energy exports to lower domestic prices and win rural voters. The problem is far more complex. Energy export bans don't work. They create artificial scarcity, invite retaliation, and destabilize markets—exactly what happened when Nixon-era price controls tanked the economy and triggered gas lines nationwide.

For Colorado, this matters. The state's energy sector—oil, natural gas, and refining operations—depends on open markets and export capacity. Diesel export restrictions would chill investment in Colorado's energy infrastructure, suppress prices for producers (bad for state tax revenue and jobs), and ultimately fail to meaningfully lower pump prices for farmers or anyone else.

Republicans chasing this message are abandoning free-market principle for short-term political gain. It won't work. Voters see through gimmicks, and farmers understand that artificial price controls backfire.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican delegation—particularly those representing energy-producing regions—will resist this protectionist pressure or fold to farm-lobby demands.
