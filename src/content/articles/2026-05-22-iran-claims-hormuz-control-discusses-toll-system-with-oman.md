# Iran Claims Control of Strait of Hormuz as Toll Talks Advance

Iran has published a map asserting "armed forces oversight" across more than 22,000 square kilometers of the Strait of Hormuz, escalating its territorial claims over one of the world's most critical shipping lanes, according to BBC World.

The move coincides with separate discussions between Iran and Oman about establishing a toll system for vessels transiting the waterway, The Hill reports. President Trump has publicly opposed such fees.

Amid these developments, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio signaled "good signs" that an agreement to end the Middle East conflict may be within reach, according to CNBC. However, the U.S. and Iran remain at odds over uranium enrichment and the proposed Hormuz tolls.

The Trump administration is exploring alternative energy solutions to reduce dependence on the strait. Louisiana Gov. Jeff Landry, serving as Trump's special envoy to Greenland, suggested Friday that the Danish territory's "untapped resources" could help ease energy prices and relieve pressure from potential Hormuz disruptions, The Hill reports.

The Strait of Hormuz handles a significant portion of global oil transit, making control and access fees a high-stakes issue for international commerce and energy markets.

Watch for whether Iran and Oman finalize a toll agreement and how the U.S. responds to both the territorial claims and any fee structure.
