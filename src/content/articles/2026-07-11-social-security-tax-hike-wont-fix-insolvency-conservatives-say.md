# Social Security Tax Hike Proposal Won't Solve the Real Problem, Conservatives Warn

Colorado retirees and workers should be wary of Democratic proposals to eliminate the payroll tax ceiling—a scheme that would hit high earners and small-business owners without fixing Social Security's underlying insolvency.

The Moreno–Warren plan, championed by progressives, would remove the current cap on earnings subject to payroll taxes, generating what proponents claim is new revenue. But conservatives argue the math doesn't work. According to National Review, eliminating the ceiling would produce "a fizz of new revenues" followed by higher benefit costs and fewer taxes available to meet them—a classic case of government spending growth outpacing revenue gains.

Republicans counter that the real fix requires addressing benefit structures and retirement ages, not simply taxing more income. Colorado's business community and high-earning professionals would face a direct hit: the tax hike would apply to wages above the current threshold, effectively raising labor costs for employers and reducing take-home pay for workers.

Meanwhile, Social Security payments continue flowing. The Washington Examiner reports the second round of July payments—capped at $5,181 per recipient—went out to retirees born between the 11th and 20th of the month on July 15.

Coloradans should watch whether Democrats push this tax increase through Congress before the 2026 midterms.
