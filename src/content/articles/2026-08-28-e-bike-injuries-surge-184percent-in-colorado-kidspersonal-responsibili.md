# E-Bike Surge Leaves Colorado Kids Injured—and Parents Liable

Colorado doctors are sounding the alarm as e-bike injuries among children skyrocket, raising hard questions about parental responsibility and government's role in regulating these devices.

Children's Hospital Colorado reported a 184% increase in traumatic e-bike injuries from 2024 to 2025 (Colorado Sun), with injuries nearly doubling across the state (CPR News). The surge reflects e-bikes' explosive popularity—but also a troubling gap between device capability and rider maturity.

Here's the conservative concern: as government considers regulating e-bikes and e-scooters, the real issue isn't bureaucratic oversight—it's parental judgment and personal accountability. These devices are powerful machines, not toys. Parents who let kids ride them without proper helmets, training, or supervision bear responsibility for the consequences, not taxpayers funding emergency rooms.

Colorado's medical establishment is right to document the trend. But the answer isn't more rules from Denver or federal agencies. It's parents making smart choices, manufacturers providing clear safety guidance, and kids understanding risk. Government mandates on helmet laws, age restrictions, or speed limits typically fail to change behavior—they just create compliance theater while shifting liability away from families.

Watch whether Colorado legislators push for e-bike regulations that would burden manufacturers and riders with new restrictions rather than focusing on personal responsibility and parental oversight.
