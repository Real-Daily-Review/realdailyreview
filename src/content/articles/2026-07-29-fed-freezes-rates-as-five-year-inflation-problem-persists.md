# Federal Reserve Stuck in Neutral as Inflation Refuses to Budge

The Federal Reserve is keeping interest rates frozen despite inflation that has stubbornly remained above its 2% target for over five years—a policy paralysis that threatens Colorado savers, retirees, and anyone holding cash.

According to 9News Denver, Fed policymakers are "losing patience" with persistent inflation, yet they're holding the line on rates at least through the fall meeting, per Just The News reporting. The central bank's hesitation reflects deeper uncertainty: officials want more economic data before making moves that could either cool inflation or trigger a recession.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters. Prolonged low rates punish savers and retirees living on fixed income—many of them Colorado's seniors—while inflating asset prices and widening wealth gaps. Meanwhile, the Fed's five-year failure to contain inflation above target suggests the damage from years of government spending and monetary accommodation runs deeper than rate tweaks can fix.

Republicans have long argued that the real problem isn't the Fed's rate decisions—it's Washington's reckless fiscal spending. Democrats counter that the Fed must balance inflation control against employment concerns, though that balance has clearly tilted toward inaction.

**Watch whether the Fed finally moves in fall, and whether Colorado voters connect the dots between government spending, inflation, and their shrinking purchasing power.**
