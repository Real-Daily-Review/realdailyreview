# NYC Official's Immigrant Map Snub Shows Progressive Priorities Run Deep

A New York City official's "cultural map" omitting Italian, Irish, and Jewish neighborhoods exposes how progressive identity politics selectively erases communities that don't fit the current narrative—a cautionary tale for Colorado voters watching similar ideological drift in state institutions.

Zohran Mamdani, a city official, defended the controversial graphic after Italian American groups and others called out the erasure. According to the Washington Examiner, NYC's Tourism + Conventions bureau promoted the map titled "New York City: A Cultural Map" without including Little Italy, Irish, or Jewish immigrant enclaves. Mamdani blamed the Eric Adams administration for the oversight, though the damage was done.

Conservatives argue this exemplifies how progressive bureaucracies prioritize certain identity groups over others, rewriting civic history to match ideological preferences. When government agencies curate which communities "count," they're not being neutral—they're making political choices about whose heritage matters.

Colorado taxpayers should note: state tourism boards, education departments, and cultural agencies operate under similar progressive assumptions. The question isn't whether NYC will add Little Italy back (it will, after backlash); it's whether Colorado institutions are quietly making similar erasures in how they present our state's history and communities.

Watch whether Colorado's tourism and education agencies face similar accountability for whose stories they tell—or omit.
