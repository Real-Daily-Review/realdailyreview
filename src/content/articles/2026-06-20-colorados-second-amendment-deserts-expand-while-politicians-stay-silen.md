# Colorado's Gun Rights Gap Widens as Second Amendment Access Shrinks

Colorado gun owners face a growing problem: entire regions of the state lack accessible firearms dealers, creating what conservatives call "Second Amendment deserts" that mirror food deserts but draw none of the progressive outrage.

According to Complete Colorado, politicians and progressive media remain silent on the issue despite their vocal concern about other access disparities. The problem reflects a broader squeeze on gun retailers—some driven by local regulations, others by market forces—that leaves rural and suburban Coloradans traveling hours to exercise a constitutional right.

The issue gained fresh urgency following a Supreme Court ruling this month. All nine justices agreed that blanket marijuana-use prohibitions on gun ownership violate Second Amendment protections, as National Review reported. The decision, cited in the Hunter Biden case, underscores the Court's commitment to robust Second Amendment protections—making Colorado's retail gaps even more glaring.

**The conservative argument:** If progressives genuinely care about equal access to constitutional rights, they should oppose zoning restrictions and regulations that squeeze out gun dealers. Instead, silence. Colorado taxpayers and gun owners should demand their elected officials address these barriers to lawful firearm purchases.

Watch for whether Colorado Democrats introduce legislation to remove local barriers to gun retailers—or whether they continue ignoring Second Amendment access entirely.
