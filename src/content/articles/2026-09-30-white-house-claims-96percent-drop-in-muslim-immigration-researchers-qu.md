# White House Claims 96% Drop in Muslim-Majority Immigration; Researchers Dispute Methodology

The Trump administration is touting a sharp decline in legal immigration from Muslim-majority nations as proof of effective border enforcement—but the data behind the claim is drawing scrutiny from immigration researchers.

According to Fox News and The Daily Wire, White House officials have highlighted a 96% drop in legal immigration from Muslim-majority countries since 2024, attributing the decline to stricter screening protocols and targeted travel restrictions. Republicans are celebrating the figure as validation of the administration's hardline immigration stance.

However, the Cato Institute researcher whose work informed the White House graph has pushed back on how the data is being presented and interpreted, raising questions about whether the methodology supports such sweeping claims.

The Colorado angle matters here: stricter federal immigration policy directly affects labor availability in agriculture, energy, and construction—sectors critical to Colorado's economy. Tighter screening could reduce workforce supply in industries already facing labor shortages, potentially driving up costs for ranchers and small businesses.

Conservatives argue that border security and vetting rigor protect national security and preserve American jobs. Critics counter that aggressive restrictions harm economic growth and workforce stability.
