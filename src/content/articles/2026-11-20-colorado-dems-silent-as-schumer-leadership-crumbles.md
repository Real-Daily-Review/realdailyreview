# Colorado Democrats Silent on Schumer Leadership Fight

Colorado's two Democratic senators are dodging questions about whether they'll back Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader as his grip on party leadership fractures. While at least five Senate Democrats have publicly endorsed Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen's challenge to the New York incumbent, Michael Bennet and John Hickenlooper remain noncommittal—a telling silence that suggests internal Democratic turmoil heading into the new Congress.

Schumer faces the most serious leadership threat in years. The Washington Examiner reports that Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) became the fifth Democrat to snub the current leader, backing Van Hollen instead. The rebellion signals deep dissatisfaction among Senate Democrats over strategy and direction.

Colorado voters should note that Bennet and Hickenlooper's refusal to take a stand—either way—reflects the broader Democratic Party fracture over how to respond to Republican gains. Conservatives argue this infighting demonstrates the left's inability to unite around coherent policy, particularly on energy, border security, and fiscal restraint.

Republicans see opportunity in Democratic disarray; watch whether Colorado's senators eventually break ranks or remain silent through the leadership vote—either choice reveals their political calculation heading into 2026.
