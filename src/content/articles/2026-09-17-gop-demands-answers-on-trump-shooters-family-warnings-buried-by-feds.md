# GOP Blindsided by New Details on Trump Butler Shooter's Family Warnings

Republican lawmakers are demanding answers after learning that Thomas Matthew Crooks' father had actively tried to block his son's gun access months before the July assassination attempt on Trump in Butler, Pennsylvania—a critical detail that federal authorities apparently kept from the Secret Service task force investigating the shooting.

According to Fox News and Just The News, Matthew Crooks attempted to prevent his son from accessing firearms in May 2024, roughly two months before the attack. Yet the task force was unaware that federal authorities had already interviewed Crooks' family, raising serious questions about intelligence-sharing failures between agencies.

Republicans argue the revelations expose a catastrophic breakdown in government coordination. If the father's concerns were known and shared across agencies, the shooting might have been prevented—a damning indictment of federal law enforcement's ability to connect the dots on threats to national figures.

The incident underscores a broader conservative concern: when government agencies fail at their core security mission, the Left's instinct is always to blame guns and gun owners rather than fix bureaucratic incompetence. Coloradans should watch whether this becomes a pretext for new federal gun restrictions or whether Congress actually holds agencies accountable for the failures that mattered.
