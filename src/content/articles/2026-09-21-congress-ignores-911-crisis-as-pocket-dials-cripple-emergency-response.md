# Congress Leaves 911 System Crumbling While Pocket Dials Overwhelm Dispatchers

Colorado's emergency responders are drowning in false calls while Congress dithers over funding for a system that handles 240 million calls annually nationwide, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The 911 infrastructure crisis is real and immediate. Local public safety answering points across the country are buckling under the weight of pocket dials, misdirected calls, and genuine emergencies competing for dispatcher attention. When a Colorado rancher, parent, or business owner dials 911, they deserve a system that answers—not one gasping for resources because Congress won't act.

The problem isn't mysterious: outdated infrastructure, insufficient funding, and congressional gridlock have left emergency dispatch systems operating on fumes. This isn't a partisan talking point—it's a public safety failure that affects every Coloradan, regardless of politics.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Congress must prioritize core government functions like emergency response before chasing new spending initiatives. The left's silence on this unglamorous but critical infrastructure need speaks volumes about misplaced priorities.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state legislators and local officials demand federal action—or whether they'll step up with state-level solutions to ensure their constituents' emergency calls actually get through.
