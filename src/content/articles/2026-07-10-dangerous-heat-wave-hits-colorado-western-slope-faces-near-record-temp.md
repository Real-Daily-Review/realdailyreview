# Colorado Faces Dangerous Heat Wave as Western Slope Braces for Near-Record Temperatures

Dangerous heat is bearing down on Colorado this weekend, with western Colorado facing near-record temperatures that could pose serious risks to vulnerable populations, according to CPR News. The National Weather Service is warning of extreme conditions across the state, with thunderstorms expected Thursday night before temperatures spike significantly.

Grand Junction and surrounding areas are particularly at risk. CPR News reports that officials are concerned about the 2,500 unhoused individuals in the Grand Junction area who will face exposure to dangerous heat with limited shelter options. The combination of extreme temperatures and limited resources for vulnerable populations underscores how government-dependent communities struggle when crisis hits—a reminder that private charity, faith-based organizations, and community networks often respond faster than bureaucratic systems.

Denver will see thunderstorm chances ahead of the hot weekend, according to KDVR Fox31, with conditions expected to remain volatile through the forecast period.

Meanwhile, the Colorado Black Arts Festival is proceeding with its 40th-anniversary celebration in Denver this weekend, with organizers emphasizing community resilience despite external pressures (CPR News).

Coloradans should monitor local emergency alerts and heat-safety guidance from county health departments, and watch whether government agencies adequately coordinate with private shelters and nonprofits to protect vulnerable residents during the heat event.
