# Border Chaos in Spain Shows What Happens When Nations Lose Control of Their Frontiers

Spain's tiny enclave of Ceuta descended into crisis this week when thousands of migrants breached the Morocco border, resulting in at least 18 deaths and forcing Madrid to deploy military forces to restore order (Washington Examiner, New York Post). The surge—described as a "serious humanitarian crisis"—saw burned vehicles and scenes of chaos as migrants overwhelmed frontier controls in a matter of hours.

The Ceuta breach offers a cautionary tale for American policymakers and Colorado voters watching the southern border deteriorate under Biden-Harris open-border policies. When governments signal weakness at their frontiers—whether through lax enforcement, catch-and-release practices, or sanctuary policies—smugglers and migrants respond with mass rushes that end in tragedy and disorder.

Republicans argue that sovereignty begins at the border. Secure frontiers save lives by deterring dangerous crossing attempts and disrupting smuggling networks. Democrats and open-border advocates counter that humanitarian concerns should drive policy, though they rarely address how permissive policies incentivize the very crossings that kill migrants.

Colorado conservatives should watch whether the Biden-Harris administration learns anything from Spain's failure—or whether it continues policies that invite similar chaos at our own southern frontier.
