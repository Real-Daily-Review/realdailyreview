# Senate Crypto Clarity Vote Looms as Regulatory Turf War Drags On

Congress has spent 15 months unable to answer a basic question: who regulates cryptocurrency? That gridlock comes to a head Sept. 15, when the Senate votes on cloture for the CLARITY Act—a bill designed to finally clarify which federal agencies oversee digital assets.

The Washington Examiner notes the dispute surrounding the CLARITY Act involves "legitimate" concerns, suggesting the regulatory framework itself isn't inherently partisan. The core issue: the crypto industry operates in a regulatory gray zone where the SEC, CFTC, and other agencies claim overlapping authority, strangling innovation and creating compliance chaos for Colorado-based blockchain firms and investors.

Republicans and crypto advocates argue clear rules will unlock American competitiveness in digital finance. Democrats and regulators worry about consumer protection gaps. Both sides claim the other is obstructing progress.

The procedural vote Sept. 15 will signal whether Senate leadership can break the logjam. If it fails, expect more months of regulatory uncertainty—bad news for Colorado's growing fintech sector and any state looking to attract blockchain talent and capital.

Watch whether Colorado's senators support cloture and whether the final bill actually passes before year-end.
