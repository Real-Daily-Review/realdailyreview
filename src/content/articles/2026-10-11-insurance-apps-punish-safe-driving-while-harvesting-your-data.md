# Insurance Tracking Apps Penalize Safe Driving—and Your Privacy

State Farm and other major insurers are selling usage-based insurance as a win for safe drivers, but the reality is far darker for Colorado motorists already squeezed by inflation.

The pitch is simple: download an app, let the company track your every move, and watch premiums drop. What consumers discover instead is that even defensive driving—like hard braking to avoid a collision—gets flagged as "risky behavior" and triggers rate hikes (Washington Examiner). The system punishes the very actions that prevent accidents.

This represents a troubling convergence of corporate surveillance and perverse incentives. Insurers collect granular data on location, speed, and driving patterns while algorithms make opaque decisions about your rates. Coloradans already dealing with rising costs of living now face the prospect of paying more for *avoiding* crashes.

**The conservative concern:** This is what happens when corporations and regulators collude on data collection without meaningful privacy protections or transparency. Drivers have little recourse when an algorithm decides their defensive maneuver was "unsafe." Colorado regulators should demand that insurers disclose exactly how their algorithms work and ensure consumers aren't penalized for genuinely safe behavior.

Watch whether Colorado's insurance commissioner takes action to require algorithm transparency and protect drivers from algorithmic rate discrimination.
