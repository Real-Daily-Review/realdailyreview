# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, June 22, 2026

Colorado conservatives woke up this morning to a state where activist prosecutors may be steering tax dollars into climate litigation, a landmark Supreme Court case could rein in federal overreach — and the rest of the country is demanding an end to the Iran conflict. There's plenty to watch. Let's get into it.

---

## Colorado DA Hints at AG Weiser's Hand in Climate Lawsuit

The Boulder District Attorney is raising questions about whether outgoing Attorney General Phil Weiser directed state resources toward climate litigation — the kind of lawfare that, if successful, drives up energy costs for every Colorado household and business. Conservatives say Coloradans deserve a straight answer: are taxpayer dollars funding ideological climate crusades? Colorado's energy sector — oil, gas, and coal workers on the Western Slope — has the most to lose if this litigation succeeds and regulators use the courts to do what the legislature won't.

---

## Colorado Case Could Give SCOTUS a Shot at Reining In Federal Commerce Power

A Colorado case is working its way toward the Supreme Court with the potential to challenge Washington's decades-long abuse of the Commerce Clause — the constitutional provision the federal government has stretched beyond recognition to justify nearly unlimited regulatory reach. Conservatives and constitutional originalists argue it's long past time for the Court to reassert real limits on federal power. For Colorado ranchers, small businesses, and property owners who've watched Washington micromanage their livelihoods, a favorable ruling would be a genuine win.

---

## 78% of Americans Say End the Iran Conflict

A new poll shows nearly 8 in 10 Americans believe the United States should bring the Iran conflict to a close — and a clear majority is skeptical the current peace arrangement will produce a pro-American government in Tehran. Republicans and conservatives have long argued that military commitments require clear objectives, defined endpoints, and congressional accountability. Colorado has a significant military and veteran community that bears the human cost of prolonged foreign engagements. The public's message is hard to misread.

---

## Angel Dads Mark Another Father's Day Without Their Children

DHS honored Angel Dads this Father's Day — fathers whose children were killed in crimes committed by illegal aliens and cartel members. "She should be graduating," one father said. These families paid the ultimate price for open-border policies that conservatives have argued for years put American lives at risk. Colorado has not been immune: the state has seen its own tragedies tied to illegal immigration and cartel activity. No political spin changes what these fathers live with every single day.

---

## Lynn Bartels, Colorado's "Grand Dame" of Political Reporting, Dies at 69

Lynn Bartels, longtime reporter for the Rocky Mountain News and the Denver Post, passed away at 69. Whatever one's politics, Bartels was widely respected across the aisle for her deep sourcing, relentless work ethic, and genuine love of Colorado politics. The state loses one of its most knowledgeable political observers. She covered Colorado's political landscape for decades and earned the trust of Republicans and Democrats alike — a standard that's increasingly rare in today's media environment.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Boulder DA's office and any response from Phil Weiser's camp on the climate lawsuit question — if state resources were weaponized for ideological litigation, that's a scandal Colorado voters heading into the next election cycle need to know about. Watch for any movement on the Colorado interstate commerce case as the Supreme Court's next term approaches; a strong ruling could reshape the regulatory landscape for Colorado businesses and landowners. And as the Iran conflict winds down, Colorado's congressional delegation should be pressed on what oversight they exercised — and what it cost.
