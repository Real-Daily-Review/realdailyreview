# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Wage War on Progress

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are quietly powering the modern internet, yet environmental activists and progressive regulators increasingly treat them as obstacles rather than assets. These facilities—essential infrastructure for cloud computing, AI, streaming, and digital commerce—represent a massive competitive advantage for Colorado's economy and job market, but face mounting pressure from green-energy mandates and anti-development sentiment.

Complete Colorado reports that data centers are "not the enemy of modern life" but rather "what makes it run." The facilities generate substantial tax revenue, create high-wage jobs, and position Colorado as a technology hub. Yet Democrats and environmental groups have pushed aggressive regulations on energy consumption and water use, arguing data centers strain resources.

Republicans counter that Colorado should embrace this economic engine rather than strangle it with costly restrictions. The state's competitive edge in attracting data center investment depends on reliable power, reasonable permitting, and tax certainty—precisely what progressive overregulation threatens.

Conservatives argue Colorado should double down on responsible energy development—including natural gas and nuclear—to power data centers affordably, rather than forcing expensive renewable mandates that drive operations to Texas or other states.

**Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to impose new data center regulations or energy mandates that could push this critical industry elsewhere.**
