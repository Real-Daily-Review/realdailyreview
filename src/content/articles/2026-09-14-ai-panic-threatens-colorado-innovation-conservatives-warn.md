# AI Panic Follows Predictable Pattern of Government Overreach, Conservatives Warn

Conservatives are sounding the alarm that artificial intelligence fears will trigger the same regulatory cycle that has plagued previous technological advances—with government rushing to regulate what it doesn't understand (Washington Examiner). The pattern is familiar: public anxiety spikes, politicians and special interests exploit the moment, and heavy-handed rules follow before anyone grasps the actual risks or benefits.

For Colorado, this matters. The state is home to growing tech sectors and energy companies increasingly reliant on AI optimization. Premature federal or state regulations could hamper innovation in oil and gas efficiency, water management systems, and agricultural technology—sectors critical to Colorado's economy and rural communities.

Republicans argue that instead of reflexive regulation, policymakers should let the market and industry self-governance address legitimate concerns. Rushing to legislate AI, they contend, will only entrench incumbent players and stifle the startups and entrepreneurs who drive real solutions.

The left, meanwhile, typically pushes for proactive government oversight to prevent hypothetical harms—a framework that historically expands bureaucracy and compliance costs without delivering promised safety.

Watch for whether Colorado lawmakers attempt their own AI regulations or fall in line with federal overreach.
