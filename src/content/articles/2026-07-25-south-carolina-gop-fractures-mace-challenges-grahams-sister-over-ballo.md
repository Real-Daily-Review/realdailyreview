# South Carolina GOP Civil War: Mace Challenges Graham's Sister Over Ballot Rules

South Carolina Republicans are tearing into each other over ballot eligibility as the 2026 Senate race heats up—a messy primary fight that signals deepening fractures in the GOP establishment.

Rep. Nancy Mace has questioned whether Darline Graham, sister of Sen. Lindsey Graham, meets South Carolina ballot eligibility requirements under new state voting laws, according to *Fox News*. The challenge underscores tension between Trump-aligned conservatives and the Graham wing of the party.

Meanwhile, former South Carolina Gov. Mark Sanford announced his bid for Graham's Senate seat, *Daily Caller* reports. Sanford's entry adds another layer to what's shaping up as a crowded primary—and signals that anti-establishment Republicans smell blood in the water.

The infighting reflects a broader conservative frustration with Graham's record: his past criticism of Trump, his centrist votes on judicial confirmations, and his perceived distance from the party's base on key issues. For Colorado Republicans watching South Carolina, the lesson is clear: primary challenges to establishment figures are gaining traction nationwide.

Conservatives should monitor whether Mace's ballot challenge gains traction and whether Sanford's candidacy fractures the anti-Graham vote enough to hand the seat to a Democrat.
