# Oman's Neutrality Collapses as Iran-US Tensions Explode in Strait of Hormuz

Oman's decades-long diplomatic tightrope has snapped, dragging the Gulf nation into active conflict between Washington and Tehran—and threatening global energy markets that Colorado's oil and gas sector depends on.

Long positioned as a neutral broker between the U.S. and Iran, Oman abandoned that careful balance to pursue a secret shipping deal aimed at reviving its paralyzed economy, according to the *Washington Examiner*. That gambit has backfired spectacularly. Iran claimed Sunday it struck an unmanned U.S. vessel attempting to enter the Strait of Hormuz, marking the latest escalation in renewed hostilities (*9News Denver*).

The deterioration matters for Colorado. Instability in the Strait—through which roughly one-third of global seaborne oil passes—drives energy prices upward and threatens the competitiveness of American energy producers, including Colorado's oil and gas operators. Tighter supplies and higher prices ripple through the state's economy and household budgets.

Conservatives argue the Biden-Harris administration's weakness on Iran policy—including the failed nuclear diplomacy that left Tehran emboldened—created the conditions for this escalation. A more forceful deterrent posture, they contend, would have prevented Oman's miscalculation and kept the Strait secure.

Watch whether the conflict spreads and how energy markets respond—Colorado taxpayers and workers are watching their wallets.
