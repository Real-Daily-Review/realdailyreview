# Nvidia CEO's China Ties Expose AI Security Blind Spot—And Colorado Should Pay Attention

Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO following revelations of extramarital affairs—including one with a woman U.S. intelligence assesses as a likely Chinese intelligence asset—exposes a dangerous vulnerability in America's AI supply chain, according to *National Review* and *The Daily Wire*.

The scandal raises urgent questions about how a company controlling the lion's share of advanced AI chip production has maintained such lax counterintelligence protocols. *National Review* pointedly asks: "Why, exactly, are we allowing Nvidia to sell advanced AI chips to China?" The affair involved Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, whom intelligence officials believe works for Beijing's Ministry of State Security.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts major tech operations and data centers that depend on Nvidia's chips. If critical AI infrastructure remains vulnerable to foreign intelligence penetration, Colorado's tech economy and national security interests are at risk.

Republicans argue this episode demonstrates the need for stricter export controls on advanced semiconductors and tougher vetting of foreign nationals in sensitive tech roles. The Biden-Harris administration's approach to AI oversight has been criticized by conservatives as insufficiently focused on counterintelligence threats.

Watch whether Trump's incoming administration moves to tighten Nvidia's export licensing and impose new security requirements on AI chipmakers handling sensitive defense applications.
