# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Wednesday, July 15, 2026

Colorado conservatives are waking up to a state GOP that may have handed Democrats a gift-wrapped general election, a wildfire emergency stretching across the southern Rockies, and a federal government finally getting serious about defense and foreign policy. Here's what matters today.

---

## Colorado GOP Primary Results: A Self-Inflicted Wound

Primary voters delivered what Complete Colorado is calling the Colorado GOP's "death wish" — nominating candidates who may be positioned to lose winnable races in November. Republicans argue the party must field candidates who can actually win swing districts and suburban voters, not just fire up the base. For Colorado conservatives who want to roll back the Polis agenda on taxes, energy, and crime, a weak general-election slate isn't a strategy — it's a surrender.

---

## Aspen Acres Fire Nears 100,000 Acres — And It's Getting Worse

The Aspen Acres fire in Pueblo and Custer counties has grown to nearly 99,000 acres, making it Colorado's largest active wildfire, with additional fires threatening areas near Ouray, Leadville, and Dolores. Incoming heat and wind are expected to intensify conditions. Conservatives have long argued that federal land mismanagement — choked with overgrown timber and strangled by environmental regulations blocking responsible thinning — turns Colorado's forests into tinderboxes. Ranchers, rural communities, and energy workers in the region are bearing the cost of that failure right now.

---

## Colorado Springs Sued Over April Municipal Elections

A lawsuit has been filed against Colorado Springs for holding its municipal elections in April, marking the first enforcement action since a 2025 state voting law took effect. Republicans and conservatives argue the law is yet another example of Democrats in Denver using the legislature to override local control and consolidate election timing in ways that benefit their turnout operations. Colorado Springs voters elected their own officials under their own charter — and now the state wants to dictate the calendar. Watch this one closely.

---

## The Left Targets Marco Rubio

Socialists have identified Secretary of State Marco Rubio as a primary threat to their agenda — which tells you everything you need to know about how effectively he's operating. Rubio's foreign policy posture, including President Trump's blunt statement Tuesday that he would order a ground invasion of Iran's Kharg Island if U.S. forces sufficiently degraded Iranian capabilities, signals an administration that is not bluffing on national security. Colorado's defense contractors and military families at Fort Carson and the Air Force Academy have a direct stake in whether that resolve translates into deterrence or conflict.

---

## Pentagon Doubles Down on Defense Innovation

Army Secretary Dave McCormick hosted hundreds of defense companies at the Army War College for a Defense and Innovation Summit, focused on building new partnerships to sharpen America's warfighting edge. For Colorado — home to a massive defense and aerospace sector along the Front Range — this kind of public-private engagement means real economic opportunity. Conservatives say this is exactly what government should be doing: strengthening national security while creating private-sector partnerships rather than bureaucratic boondoggles.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado GOP's post-primary path to November — if party leadership doesn't rally around electable candidates fast, Democrats will run the table on the state legislature and potentially the governor's race. On the wildfire front, watch whether federal land managers accelerate emergency resource deployment or get bogged down in process while southern Colorado burns. And in Washington, Trump's Iran posture is escalating — Colorado's military community will be among the first to feel any shift from deterrence to direct action.
