# Washington Must Rediscover Bipartisan Deal-Making—Before Colorado Families Pay the Price

Washington's partisan gridlock is strangling progress on issues that matter to Colorado families and small business owners. The *Washington Examiner* argues that leaders should look back to the Clinton-Gingrich era, when Democrats and Republicans actually negotiated budget deals that worked—rather than lurching from crisis to crisis.

The case is straightforward: a Colorado small business owner juggling payroll and a parent stretching a paycheck shouldn't have to wonder whether Washington will manufacture another debt-ceiling standoff or government shutdown. Those manufactured crises cost jobs, freeze hiring, and create uncertainty that ripples through Main Street Colorado.

Republicans have long argued that bipartisan compromise requires both sides to abandon ideological maximalism. That means Democrats must accept spending restraint and regulatory rollback; Republicans must accept that some revenue discussions are necessary. The alternative—perpetual brinkmanship—serves no one except lobbyists and bureaucrats who profit from chaos.

Colorado's energy sector, agriculture, and small-business community need predictable policy, not annual Washington theater. Conservatives should demand that their representatives in Congress prioritize actual legislation over performative opposition.

Watch whether the incoming Congress moves beyond talking points to draft real budget frameworks that both parties can defend to their voters.
