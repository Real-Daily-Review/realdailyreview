# Colorado Schools Face Funding Squeeze as Enrollment Plummets

Colorado's public school districts are staring down a fiscal cliff: enrollment is dropping, and state funding follows students out the door.

According to Complete Colorado, the state's education budget is directly tied to student headcount. As families leave Colorado's government-run schools—whether for charter alternatives, homeschooling, or relocation—districts lose the per-pupil dollars that fund classrooms, teachers, and operations. The trend signals tighter budgets ahead for already-stretched districts.

The enrollment decline reflects broader concerns among Colorado parents about school performance, curriculum choices, and educational quality. Conservatives argue that rather than throwing more taxpayer money at failing systems, the state should empower families with school choice, expand charter school access, and let education dollars follow children to schools that actually deliver results.

Meanwhile, Colorado fire departments face a separate but related crunch. KDVR reports that firefighters are handling more emergency calls while battling funding and staffing shortages. Many departments say they lack the resources to respond adequately to rising demand—a problem that hits rural Colorado particularly hard.

Both crises point to the same issue: government agencies claiming they need endless budget increases to meet basic obligations. Conservatives should demand accountability: why are enrollment-driven school budgets shrinking when per-pupil spending remains high? And why do fire departments need perpetual funding growth?

Watch for how Colorado lawmakers respond to these funding gaps—and whether they'll finally embrace competition and efficiency over blank checks.
