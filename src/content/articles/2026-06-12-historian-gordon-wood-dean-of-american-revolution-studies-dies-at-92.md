# Legendary Historian Gordon Wood, Dean of American Revolution Scholarship, Dies at 92

Gordon S. Wood, the preeminent historian of the American Revolution and a towering figure in conservative intellectual life, died in a traffic accident in East Providence, Rhode Island. He was 92.

Wood's scholarly output—spanning decades and reshaping how Americans understand the founding—stood apart for its commitment to historical truth over ideological convenience. *National Review* noted his interpretation was "both forward-looking and painstakingly committed to the truth," a standard increasingly rare in academia. The *Washington Examiner* called him the "Dean of the American Revolution," a title earned through meticulous research and a rare ability to reach beyond university walls into popular culture.

For conservatives, Wood's work mattered because he refused to let the founding be colonized by progressive revisionism. His books grounded American exceptionalism in rigorous historical analysis rather than wishful thinking or guilt-driven reinterpretation. He demonstrated that fidelity to primary sources and intellectual honesty could coexist with defending the founders' vision.

Wood will not live to see the nation's 250th anniversary in 2026—a milestone he might have marked with the same unsparing clarity he brought to every subject.

Conservatives should watch whether universities and mainstream media outlets will honestly reckon with Wood's legacy or attempt to minimize his influence on how Americans understand their own history.
