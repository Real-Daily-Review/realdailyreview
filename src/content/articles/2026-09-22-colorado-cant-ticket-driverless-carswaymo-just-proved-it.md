# Colorado Has No Law to Ticket Driverless Cars—and One Just Drove Through a Denver Farmers Market

Colorado's regulatory vacuum on autonomous vehicles just became a real-world problem. A Waymo robotaxi drove through a closed road and into a Denver farmers market during setup, and neither Denver Police nor Colorado State Patrol could cite the vehicle for the violation—because no state law exists to do so.

The incident (KDVR) exposed a critical gap: Colorado has no mechanism to ticket driverless cars for traffic violations. DPD and CSP confirmed the legal dead end, leaving taxpayers and public safety advocates asking who's accountable when a robot car breaks the rules.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that progressive cities rushing to embrace autonomous vehicle technology without proper regulatory guardrails invite chaos. This isn't theoretical anymore. While tech companies lobby for minimal oversight, Colorado residents deserve clear laws establishing liability, traffic enforcement authority, and safety standards before these vehicles proliferate on state roads.

The incident raises hard questions: If a Waymo causes an accident, who pays? If it endangers pedestrians, who's responsible? Colorado lawmakers must act before more autonomous vehicles flood Denver streets operating in a legal gray zone.

Watch whether the Colorado legislature moves to close this loophole—or lets Silicon Valley's self-driving experiment continue unregulated.
