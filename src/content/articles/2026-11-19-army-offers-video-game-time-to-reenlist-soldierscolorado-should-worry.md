# Army Bribes Soldiers With Video Game Time—While Colorado Faces Military Readiness Questions

The U.S. Army is offering four-day passes to play "Grand Theft Auto VI" as a reenlistment incentive, and 43 of 130 eligible soldiers at Fort Stewart, Georgia took the bait (Fox News, The Daily Wire). 

While the Army frames this as a creative recruitment tool for Gen Z troops, conservatives raise a legitimate concern: is this the best use of military resources and leadership focus? The Pentagon faces real readiness challenges—from recruitment shortfalls to retention crises—and gimmicks like video game weekends sidestep the harder questions about pay, career development, and unit cohesion.

Colorado's military installations, including Fort Carson and Schriever Space Force Base, depend on robust recruitment and retention pipelines. If the Army is struggling to keep soldiers committed without gaming perks, that signals deeper morale and compensation issues that won't be solved by four-day passes.

Republicans and defense hawks argue the military should invest in competitive salaries, better family support, and clear career pathways—not novelty incentives that cheapen the commitment to service. The broader question: Is the Pentagon serious about readiness, or are they chasing headlines?

Watch whether other branches adopt similar gimmicks, and whether Colorado's military leadership addresses underlying retention problems head-on.
