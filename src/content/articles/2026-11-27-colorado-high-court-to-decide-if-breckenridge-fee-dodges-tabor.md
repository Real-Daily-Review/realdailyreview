# Colorado High Court to Weigh Whether Breckenridge's Rental Fee Dodges TABOR

Colorado's Supreme Court is about to settle a fundamental question: can towns disguise tax hikes as "fees" to sidestep voter approval under the Taxpayer Bill of Rights?

The lawsuit challenges Breckenridge's fee on rental properties, arguing it functions as a tax and therefore violates TABOR, which requires voter consent for revenue increases (Complete Colorado). The distinction matters enormously for Colorado taxpayers and property owners—if towns can reclassify taxes as fees, TABOR's protections evaporate.

Conservatives and taxpayer advocates argue that Breckenridge is using semantic gamesmanship to fund government spending without the democratic check TABOR provides. The town collects revenue from a mandatory charge on a defined class of property owners, they contend, which is textbook taxation. Allowing this precedent would open the door for other Colorado municipalities to bypass voter approval on future revenue grabs.

The court's ruling will determine whether TABOR remains a meaningful restraint on government expansion or becomes a hollow promise. If Breckenridge wins, expect a cascade of similar fee schemes across Colorado—and watch for property owners and conservative groups to push for legislative clarification of what constitutes a "tax" under state law.
