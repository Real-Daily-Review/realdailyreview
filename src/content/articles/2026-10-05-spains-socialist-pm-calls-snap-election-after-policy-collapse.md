# Spain's Socialist PM Calls Snap Election After Policy Failures

Spain's Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez is calling a snap election for November 29 after his government's agenda hit a wall—a cautionary tale for progressive leaders everywhere.

Sánchez's government faced twin crises: Congress rejected two housing decrees his administration championed, and his government was reeling from backlash over a migrant crisis in Ceuta, one of Spain's North African enclaves, where tens of thousands of migrants stormed the border and ransacked the city (Just The News, Breitbart).

The collapse of Sánchez's legislative agenda mirrors a broader pattern: when governments overreach with expansive social programs and fail to secure borders, voters punish them at the ballot box. Spain's housing crisis—partly driven by government intervention and regulation—became politically toxic when Congress rejected his proposed fixes. The migrant surge exposed the costs of open-border policies that progressive leaders championed but couldn't manage.

For Colorado conservatives, the lesson is clear: ambitious government schemes to solve housing shortages through regulation and decree tend to backfire. When politicians promise to engineer solutions to complex problems, they often create new crises instead. Sánchez's snap election is a referendum on failed progressivism—and voters are about to render their verdict.
