# Colorado Marks 150 Years: A State Built on Pioneer Grit, Now Facing Progressive Headwinds

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives as the Centennial State celebrates a century and a half of frontier independence, resource development, and individual enterprise—values increasingly at odds with Denver's progressive governing class.

The state marked its 150th birthday with festivities at the Capitol, where leaders reflected on Colorado's past while charting its future (CPR News). The Colorado Sun highlighted 150 things embodying Colorado's "awesomeness," leaning heavily into the state's natural beauty and outdoor culture.

But here's what the celebrations glossed over: modern Colorado has drifted far from the self-reliant, limited-government ethos that built this state. Today's Denver Democrats have imposed aggressive gun restrictions, strangled energy development with climate mandates, and grown state government at the expense of taxpayers and landowners. The pioneer spirit that settled Colorado—one of grit, property rights, and skepticism of distant authority—has given way to regulatory overreach and green-energy ideology that threatens Colorado's working families.

The state's founders would recognize the mountains and rivers. They'd struggle to recognize the bureaucracy.

Conservatives should watch closely as Democrats use Colorado's 150-year milestone to push nostalgia while advancing policies that undermine the very independence that made the state great.
