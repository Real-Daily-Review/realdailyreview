# Colorado's Digital ID App Down Ahead of Holiday Travel—State Offers No Timeline

Colorado's myColorado digital ID app sits offline just days before peak Thanksgiving travel, leaving up to 150,000 residents unable to access state-issued digital credentials after a cybersecurity incident (Colorado Sun). The state has provided no restoration timeline, compounding travel headaches as a massive coastal storm already disrupts over 550 flights nationwide and triggers widespread power outages (Washington Examiner).

The timing is brutal: Coloradans heading to airports or crossing state lines cannot rely on the app as a backup to physical IDs. The state's silence on when service returns raises questions about government IT infrastructure resilience—a concern conservatives have long flagged regarding centralized digital systems and data security.

Republicans have consistently warned that consolidating citizen data into government-managed digital platforms creates single-point-of-failure risks and privacy vulnerabilities. This incident validates those concerns: the state collected data from 150,000 users, then failed to protect or quickly restore it during a critical travel window.

The lack of transparency about the breach's scope and recovery timeline is typical of government crisis management—reactive rather than proactive, and leaving taxpayers in the dark.

Watch whether Colorado officials acknowledge the security failures that enabled the breach and commit to decentralized alternatives that give citizens control over their own credentials.
