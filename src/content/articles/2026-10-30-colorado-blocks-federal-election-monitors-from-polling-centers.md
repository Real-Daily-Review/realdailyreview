# Colorado Stands Firm Against Federal Election Overreach—DOJ Backs Down

Colorado's Secretary of State successfully pushed back against federal election monitors attempting to insert themselves into Adams County voting centers, forcing the Justice Department to operate from outside polling locations.

The state imposed clear limits: federal observers must remain in designated areas, avoid direct voter contact, and accept accompaniment by state-appointed monitors. The DOJ rejected these reasonable restrictions and announced it would station its monitors at least 100 feet outside the buildings instead (Colorado Sun, Denver Post).

**The conservative case is straightforward.** Colorado election officials—accountable to Colorado voters—should control their own polling places. Federal bureaucrats inserting themselves into local elections, even under the guise of "monitoring," represents exactly the kind of centralized government overreach conservatives oppose. States, not Washington, manage elections. Period.

Democrats and their allies argue federal monitors ensure voting rights compliance, but Republicans counter that Colorado's election security measures are robust and that federal presence undermines state sovereignty and voter confidence in local election administration.

This outcome—the DOJ backing down rather than forcing its way inside—reflects Colorado's willingness to defend its electoral independence against federal pressure. Secretary of State Jena Griswold's office held the line.

**Watch whether the DOJ attempts similar interventions in other Colorado counties or whether this precedent emboldens other states to reassert election authority.**
