# Denver Voters Embrace Nanny-State Micromanagement Over Kitchen Choices

Denver voters are deciding whether government should dictate what diners can eat, pushing Initiated Ordinance 311—a ballot measure banning the sale of foie gras and the production methods behind it. The measure exemplifies progressive overreach: bureaucrats substituting their culinary preferences for consumer choice and market freedom.

This isn't about animal welfare alone. It's about the left's expansionist view of government power. While Denver City Council races and school mill levy overrides also appear on ballots (Denver Post), the foie gras ban reveals how progressives weaponize ballot initiatives to regulate personal consumption—the kind of micromanagement conservatives argue belongs nowhere near city hall.

Republicans and conservatives counter that such bans represent creeping authoritarianism: if government can ban a luxury food item today, what's next? Beef? Dairy? The slippery slope isn't hypothetical—it's the playbook of the progressive left, which consistently uses health, environmental, and animal-welfare arguments to justify restricting individual liberty.

Meanwhile, Colorado taxpayers face broader economic headwinds. Nationally, inflation continues outpacing wages and mortgage rates linger above 7% (Fox News), pressures that hit Colorado families hard. Yet Denver's progressive majority focuses on foie gras rather than affordability.

Watch whether Denver voters reject this nanny-state overreach—or signal appetite for more government control over everyday choices.
