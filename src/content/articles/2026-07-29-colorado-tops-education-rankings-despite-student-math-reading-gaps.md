# Colorado Tops Education Rankings While Students Struggle in Core Subjects

Colorado earned the No. 1 spot in U.S. News &amp; World Report's education rankings, but the accolade masks a troubling reality: students are lagging in reading and math proficiency. Gov. Jared Polis celebrated the ranking while candidly calling it "a low bar" (Colorado Sun).

The disconnect between national rankings and classroom performance raises hard questions about what Colorado's education system is actually prioritizing. While the state gets credit for infrastructure and spending metrics, students aren't mastering fundamentals—a conservative concern that test scores, not feel-good rankings, should drive education policy.

Meanwhile, the U.S. Education Department is investigating allegations that a Colorado high school teacher coerced same-sex students into kissing during class, part of a broader probe into classroom conduct and LGBTQ-focused activities in schools (Just The News). The department is also examining a Washington state district that displayed testosterone vials as part of a Pride Month exhibit.

These incidents underscore conservative arguments that schools are increasingly focused on ideological messaging rather than academic rigor. Parents and taxpayers should demand accountability: Colorado's ranking means nothing if kids can't read at grade level, and schools must maintain professional boundaries regardless of cause.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers push for transparency in curriculum and classroom conduct standards—or double down on the current approach.
