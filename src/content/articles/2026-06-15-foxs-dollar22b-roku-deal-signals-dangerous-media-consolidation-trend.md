# Fox's $22B Roku Acquisition: Media Consolidation Raises Competition Concerns

Fox's announced $22 billion acquisition of streaming platform Roku marks another major consolidation in Big Tech and media—a deal that should concern Colorado conservatives watching corporate power concentrate in fewer hands.

The merger, announced Monday, will combine Fox's content library and Tubi streaming service with Roku's TV platform and 100+ million global streaming households, according to 9News Denver and Just The News. Fox gains direct access to first-party user data and control over a major distribution channel—precisely the kind of vertical integration that typically reduces consumer choice and competition.

While the deal isn't a government mandate, it reflects a troubling trend: massive corporations using market dominance to eliminate rivals rather than compete on merit. Republicans and conservatives have long warned that Big Tech monopolies—whether in search, social media, or now streaming—operate without meaningful accountability. This acquisition consolidates control over both content creation and distribution, raising legitimate questions about whether independent creators and smaller platforms can compete fairly.

The deal still requires regulatory approval, though antitrust enforcement has been inconsistent under both administrations.

Coloradans should watch whether federal regulators scrutinize this deal with the same intensity they've applied to other tech consolidations—or whether political connections smooth the path.
