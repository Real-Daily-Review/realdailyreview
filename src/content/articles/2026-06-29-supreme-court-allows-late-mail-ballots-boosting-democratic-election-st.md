# Supreme Court Allows Late Mail Ballots, Raising Election Integrity Concerns for 2026

The Supreme Court has handed Democrats a significant victory by ruling that mail ballots postmarked by Election Day but arriving days later can be counted—a decision that could reshape close races heading into 2026.

In a 5-4 decision, the high court upheld Mississippi's law allowing late-arriving ballots to count, rejecting Republican arguments that federal law requires ballots to be received by Election Day itself (Washington Examiner). The ruling found that states retain authority to set their own mail-ballot deadlines, even when those deadlines extend beyond Election Day.

**The conservative concern is straightforward:** extending ballot-counting windows creates opportunities for fraud, chain-of-custody problems, and delayed election certification. Republicans argue that Election Day should mean Election Day—ballots must arrive by polls close to ensure integrity and finality. The decision also complicates election administration and auditing, particularly problematic in Colorado, where mail-ballot security and timely counting have been contentious issues among conservative voters skeptical of mail-in voting expansion.

Democrats counter that postmark-by-Election-Day rules protect voters whose ballots are delayed by mail carriers, not voter negligence.

Colorado Republicans should monitor whether state Democrats use this ruling to push for looser ballot-counting deadlines in the state legislature—a move that would further erode Election Day meaning and invite the very vulnerabilities conservatives have warned about.
