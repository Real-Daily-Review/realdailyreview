# State's Digital ID App Down After Cybersecurity Breach—Colorado Travelers Left Scrambling

Colorado's myColorado digital ID app has crashed right before Thanksgiving travel, leaving up to 150,000 residents potentially exposed after a cybersecurity incident the state has yet to fully explain (Colorado Sun). The app remains offline with no restoration timeline announced, forcing Coloradans to carry physical IDs at airports this holiday week (KDVR Fox31).

This is exactly what conservatives warned about: government rushing to digitize citizen data without adequate security safeguards. The state's push for a "digital-first" identity system prioritized convenience over protecting Coloradans' personal information—and now taxpayers are paying the price in disrupted travel plans and potential identity theft exposure.

State officials have been vague about what triggered the breach and how many records were actually compromised. Democrats and progressive tech advocates have long championed these centralized digital systems as the future of government services, but this incident exposes the real-world risks of consolidating sensitive data in government databases.

The lack of transparency about the incident's scope and cause is troubling. Coloradans deserve to know exactly what happened, who was responsible, and what safeguards failed.

Watch whether the state conducts a transparent, independent audit of the myColorado system and whether lawmakers demand accountability for the security failures that left residents vulnerable.
