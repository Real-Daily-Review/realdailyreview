# Colorado GOP Eyes Election Integrity Push as Libertarian Candidate Roils Race

Republican secretary of state candidate James Wiley is making a direct pitch to Colorado voters on election security, championing in-person voting and hand-counting ballots—a stark contrast to the state's mail-heavy system (CPR News). Wiley, a former Colorado Libertarian Party leader, is positioning himself as the conservative alternative on election administration.

The race has grown complicated. A Denver judge ordered the placement of Libertarian candidate Alex Astley on the November ballot after he initially dropped out, denying claims he'd stepped aside to help Wiley (Denver Post). The move threatens to split the anti-establishment vote and could benefit Democrats in a statewide race where election procedures remain contentious.

Meanwhile, Colorado voters will also weigh two ballot propositions touching transgender issues this fall. A coalition opposing the measures argues they would harm families (KDVR Fox31), though the specific policy details remain contested.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue Colorado's reliance on mail voting creates security vulnerabilities and lacks transparency. Hand-counting and in-person voting, they contend, restore voter confidence and election integrity—priorities that resonate with Colorado conservatives skeptical of mail-ballot expansion.

**Watch for:** Whether the Libertarian-Republican split allows a Democrat to capture the secretary of state's office, potentially cementing Colorado's mail-voting model for years.
