# Colorado AG Joins 12-State Lawsuit to Block $81B Paramount-Warner Bros Merger

Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser has joined 11 other states in suing to block Paramount's $81 billion merger with Warner Bros. Discovery, arguing the deal would "extinguish competition" in Hollywood and reduce consumer choice (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The lawsuit represents another aggressive antitrust action by state attorneys general—a trend that has accelerated across both Democratic and Republican administrations. Weiser, a Democrat, is using his office's enforcement powers to challenge what his coalition characterizes as market consolidation in the entertainment industry.

**The conservative case for scrutiny:** While free-market advocates typically oppose heavy-handed antitrust intervention, they also recognize that genuine monopolistic behavior can stifle competition and innovation. The question here is whether this merger truly eliminates meaningful consumer alternatives or whether streaming platforms, cable, and digital distribution have already fragmented the old Hollywood gatekeeping model beyond recognition.

**What matters for Colorado:** The lawsuit's outcome could affect media landscape and content availability for state residents, though the entertainment industry's national scope limits direct state-level economic impact compared to energy or regulatory decisions.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether this antitrust push becomes a template for broader, more aggressive state intervention in business combinations—a potential opening for regulatory overreach disguised as consumer protection.
