# Trump's White House Ballroom Blocked by Court—Another Executive Power Battle Heads to Supreme Court

President Trump's plan to construct a "massive" White House ballroom hit a legal wall Friday when a federal appeals court halted above-ground construction, ruling that Congress—not the president—must authorize the project (The Daily Wire). The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit issued a 2-1 decision blocking the work, with Trump immediately vowing a Supreme Court appeal (Fox News Politics).

Trump argues the ruling "jeopardizes the lives of future presidents" by constraining executive authority over White House operations (Fox News Politics). The decision, authored by judges appointed by Obama and Biden, reflects the ongoing constitutional clash over presidential power versus congressional oversight.

While this fight centers on the White House itself, it echoes broader conservative concerns about judicial overreach limiting executive function. The case will likely reach the Supreme Court, where Trump's conservative majority could reshape the balance of power between branches.

Coloradans watching this case should note the precedent it sets: if courts can block presidential decisions on federal property, similar logic could eventually constrain state and local executive authority over land use, energy development, and infrastructure—issues that directly affect Colorado's economy and energy independence.

**Watch for the Supreme Court docket this fall—the outcome could reshape executive power nationwide.**
