# Cornell President Pledges Transparency After Alleged Gang Rape; Greek Life Under Fire

Cornell University's president is finally admitting what conservatives have long warned: campus "party culture" and Greek life create environments where serious crimes go unaddressed. After an alleged gang rape at a fraternity house sparked outrage over the university's handling of the case, President Michael Kotlikoff pledged greater transparency and said the school would examine Greek life's role on campus (Washington Examiner).

The case exposes a familiar pattern: elite universities prioritize reputation management over student safety and accountability. Kotlikoff's vague promises of "transparency" ring hollow without concrete action—Colorado parents paying six figures for their children's education deserve assurance that campuses will report crimes promptly to law enforcement, not bury them in administrative processes.

New York officials have taken over the criminal investigation, a tacit admission that Cornell's internal handling failed. Republicans and conservatives argue that universities must stop treating serious felonies as "conduct violations" subject to campus tribunals. Real crimes require real police, real prosecutors, and real consequences—not diversity statements and task forces.

Watch whether Colorado's universities—CU Boulder, CSU, and others—tighten Greek life oversight and commit to mandatory law enforcement reporting for sexual assault allegations, or continue hiding behind institutional opacity.
