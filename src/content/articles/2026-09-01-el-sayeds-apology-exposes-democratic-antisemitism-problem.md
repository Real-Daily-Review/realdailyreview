# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed's carefully staged apology to the state's Jewish Democrats reveals the left's deeper problem: it can't credibly distance itself from the antisemitic fringe it has cultivated.

El-Sayed met with Michigan's Democratic Jewish Caucus to apologize for past remarks, but Republicans aren't buying it. Mike Rogers called the gesture a "sham apology" given El-Sayed's refusal to dump Hasan Piker, the streamer who has blamed Jews for violent retaliation against them and promoted anti-American ideology (Fox News Politics).

The Washington Examiner's analysis cuts deeper: despite media claims that Democrats have "distanced themselves" from Piker's latest antisemitic rant, only six Democrats have explicitly spoken out—a pittance for a party of hundreds (Washington Examiner). Conservatives argue this silence exposes the Democratic Party's tolerance for extremism when it aligns with their anti-Israel, anti-American narrative.

Colorado Democrats should answer: do they stand with El-Sayed's equivocation, or will they demand their party actually purge antisemitic voices from its coalition? This matters for Colorado's Jewish communities and any voter concerned about whether the Democratic Party still values basic decency over ideological purity.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic delegation—particularly those with significant Jewish constituents—publicly demand accountability from national party leadership.
