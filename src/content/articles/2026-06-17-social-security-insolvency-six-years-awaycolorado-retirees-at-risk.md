# Social Security Insolvency Clock Ticks Down to Six Years—Colorado Retirees Face Uncertain Future

The Social Security trust fund will be exhausted in just six years, forcing an immediate reckoning that Washington has dodged for decades. Current retirees are receiving payments capped at $5,181 monthly, but without congressional action, benefit cuts of roughly 20 percent could hit automatically when the fund runs dry, according to *National Review*.

This isn't abstract fiscal policy—it's a direct threat to Colorado's 900,000-plus seniors who depend on Social Security as their primary income source. The trust fund's exhaustion, long treated as an accounting fiction by Democrats, now has concrete implications for real retirees.

Republicans argue that meaningful reform requires raising the payroll tax cap, adjusting benefit formulas for higher earners, or gradually increasing the retirement age. Democrats have resisted these solutions, preferring to ignore the crisis or propose tax hikes on workers and employers. The left's refusal to address insolvency head-on means younger Coloradans face either massive tax increases or benefit cuts—or both.

The Biden administration and congressional Democrats have offered no credible plan. Conservatives warn that kicking this can down the road another election cycle guarantees a far more painful reckoning for Colorado families already squeezed by inflation and rising costs.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democrats—will finally propose concrete reform measures before the 2026 deadline becomes unavoidable crisis.
