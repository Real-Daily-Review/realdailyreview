# U.S. Adds 115,000 Jobs in April as Unemployment Holds Steady

The Labor Department reported Friday that the U.S. added 115,000 jobs in April, significantly outpacing economist expectations of 55,000 new positions, according to the Dow Jones consensus cited by CNBC. Unemployment remained unchanged, according to PBS NewsHour.

The stronger-than-expected payroll growth suggests underlying labor market resilience. Mohamed El-Erian, chief economic advisor at Allianz and a Wharton School professor, told PBS NewsHour the numbers offer insight into the broader economic picture.

However, CNBC reported that the jobs report contained "several red flags for the economy," signaling that the headline figure may mask underlying weaknesses not immediately apparent in the headline number.

The divergence between robust job creation and cautionary signals in the data underscores the complexity of interpreting monthly employment figures. Economists often scrutinize components beyond headline payroll growth—including wage growth, labor force participation, and industry-specific trends—to assess economic health.

Watch for May's employment data and any revisions to April figures, which could clarify whether April's strength represents sustained momentum or a temporary uptick.
