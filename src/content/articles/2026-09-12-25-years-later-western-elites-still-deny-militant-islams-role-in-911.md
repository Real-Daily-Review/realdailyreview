# Colorado Conservatives Must Reckon With 25 Years of Denial About Islamic Terrorism

A quarter-century after 9/11, the Western establishment—including much of America's political and media class—continues to downplay or deny the ideological reality of militant Islam, according to analysis from *National Review* and the *Washington Examiner*.

The facts are stark: 19 Islamic terrorists hijacked four commercial aircraft on September 11, 2001, killing nearly 3,000 Americans. Two planes destroyed the Twin Towers and three surrounding buildings in New York; one struck the Pentagon; passengers on the fourth forced it down in Pennsylvania. Yet conspiracy theories and revisionist narratives have persisted for 25 years, muddying public understanding of the threat.

Conservatives argue this denial has real consequences. When political and cultural elites refuse to name the enemy clearly—militant Islam and its theological justifications for war against the West—they handicap America's ability to defend itself and make informed policy decisions. The refusal to confront uncomfortable truths about ideology, theology, and asymmetric warfare leaves the nation vulnerable to repeat mistakes.

For Colorado, this matters: our state hosts military installations, tech hubs, and energy infrastructure that remain potential targets. Clear-eyed threat assessment, not politically correct euphemism, should drive homeland security and counterterrorism policy.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—and the Biden-Harris administration—will honestly address the ideological roots of terrorism or continue the 25-year pattern of obfuscation.
