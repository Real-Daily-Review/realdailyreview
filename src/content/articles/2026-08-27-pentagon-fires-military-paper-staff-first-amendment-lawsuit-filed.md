# Pentagon Fires Military Newspaper Staff; First Amendment Lawsuit Follows

The Pentagon's abrupt termination of three senior *Stars and Stripes* staffers—the publisher, editor-in-chief, and a reporter—over insubordination charges has triggered a federal First Amendment lawsuit, raising hard questions about government control of speech even within military ranks.

Publisher Max Lederer, Editor-in-Chief Erik Slavin, and reporter Lara Korte filed suit Thursday in U.S. District Court for D.C., arguing the firings violated their constitutional rights (Washington Examiner). The *Stars and Stripes* is congressionally funded, making the Pentagon's personnel decisions a matter of taxpayer concern and constitutional principle.

**The conservative angle:** Republicans and First Amendment advocates argue the Pentagon cannot simply silence journalists—even military ones—without due process or clear legal grounds. Government agencies firing employees for editorial decisions or perceived disloyalty sets a dangerous precedent for federal overreach. If the Pentagon can suppress speech at a congressionally funded outlet, what's to stop other agencies from doing the same to contractors, grantees, or employees?

Democrats and Pentagon officials have not yet publicly detailed their insubordination claims, but the lack of transparency fuels conservative skepticism about the real motivation behind these firings.

Coloradans should watch whether this case exposes broader patterns of federal agencies punishing dissent or editorial independence—a principle that matters far beyond the military press.
