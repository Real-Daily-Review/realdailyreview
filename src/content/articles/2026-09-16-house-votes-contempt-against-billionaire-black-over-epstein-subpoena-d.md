# Billionaire Defies Congress Over Epstein Ties; House Votes Contempt

Leon Black, the Colorado-connected financier, faces a contempt-of-Congress vote after stonewalling House Oversight investigators seeking details on his payments to convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. Rather than appear for deposition, Black sued to block the subpoenas—a move that prompted bipartisan lawmakers to hold him accountable (Just The News).

The vote reflects rare congressional agreement: both Republicans and Democrats want answers about Black's relationship with Epstein and the scope of financial transfers between them. Black's legal maneuver to dodge questioning has triggered the contempt action, signaling that even wealthy elites cannot simply ignore congressional authority without consequence.

For Colorado, the case carries weight given Black's significant business footprint in the state and his philanthropic ties to local institutions. Conservatives generally support congressional oversight when it targets potential wrongdoing and financial impropriety—particularly when a witness attempts to evade legitimate inquiry through litigation rather than testimony.

The contempt vote underscores a principle conservatives champion: no one, regardless of wealth or status, should be above the law or immune from congressional scrutiny when credible questions about misconduct arise.
