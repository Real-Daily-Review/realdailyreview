# Trump's Drug Price Reforms Deliver Real Relief—Without Government Price Controls

The Trump administration's pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) reforms are cutting prescription costs dramatically, proving that free-market competition—not government mandates—can solve healthcare affordability.

According to the Washington Examiner, some medications have plummeted from $1,000 to $149, marking the largest drug price drop in 60 years. The reforms target the opaque middleman system that has long inflated prices for Colorado patients and families nationwide.

Unlike Democrats' approach of government price-setting and regulatory mandates, these reforms work by increasing transparency and competition among PBMs—the insurers' intermediaries that negotiate drug prices. By exposing how PBMs pocket rebates and markup spreads, the administration is letting market forces drive prices down without bureaucrats deciding which drugs Americans can access.

Conservatives argue this vindicates the free-market case: when you remove hidden fees and increase transparency, prices fall naturally. No price controls. No rationing. No waiting lists. Just competition working as intended.

For Colorado families already squeezed by inflation and rising healthcare costs, lower prescription prices mean real money back in their pockets—money that stays in local economies instead of flowing to corporate middlemen.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature attempts to impose its own drug-price regulations that could undermine these market-driven gains.
