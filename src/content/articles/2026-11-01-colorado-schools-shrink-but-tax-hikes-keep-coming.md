# Colorado Schools Face Enrollment Cliff—Yet Taxes Keep Rising

Colorado's K-12 districts are shrinking, but that hasn't stopped the push for higher taxes "for the kids." According to Complete Colorado, school enrollment is declining and expected to continue falling for the foreseeable future—a reality that raises a hard question: why are taxpayers being asked to fund more when fewer students are in classrooms?

The enrollment squeeze creates a fiscal trap. As districts lose students, per-pupil spending rises unless budgets shrink proportionally. Yet Colorado Democrats and education advocates routinely pitch tax increases as necessary for quality schools, ignoring the demographic headwind. Republicans argue this is backwards: declining enrollment should trigger spending discipline and efficiency reforms, not new levies on already-burdened taxpayers.

Meanwhile, Colorado's community college system is seeing enrollment growth—but mainly among older adults seeking retraining in AI, healthcare, and other fields (CPR News). That's a different problem: workforce development, not K-12 funding.

The disconnect matters for Colorado families and business owners. Declining K-12 enrollment suggests either demographic shifts or families leaving the state—both warning signs. Yet the education establishment's answer remains predictable: spend more, tax more.

Watch for the 2027 legislative session, where Democrats will likely propose new school funding measures while enrollment data continues to deteriorate.
