# America's 250th Should Celebrate Liberty, Not Self-Flagellation

As America marks its 250th anniversary this summer, conservatives are pushing back against what they see as a relentless "America Bad" narrative dominating the national conversation.

According to National Review, rather than dwelling on oppression themes, the milestone should highlight America's genuine achievements—including often-overlooked contributions like Spain's critical support for the Patriots during the Revolution. The Washington Examiner frames the celebration as an opportunity to reflect on founding principles of individual liberty and shared heritage, emphasizing free expression and conscience as core American values.

Conservatives argue the anniversary represents a chance to reaffirm what makes America exceptional: limited constitutional government, individual rights, and economic freedom. They worry progressive activists will use the 250th to amplify grievance narratives and demand new government interventions rather than celebrate the nation's founding ideals.

For Coloradans, this debate matters. How America's founding principles are framed shapes policy debates over gun rights, property rights, energy development, and state sovereignty—all live issues in Colorado politics. If the national conversation defaults to self-criticism, it emboldens those pushing expanded government power and restrictions on individual liberty.
