# Federal Broadband Boondoggle: Colorado Loses $100M to Bankrupt Contractor

Colorado taxpayers are out more than $100 million after a federal broadband subsidy scheme collapsed into investigation and insolvency. The Colorado Broadband Office has rescinded awards to Maverix Broadband, a Parker-based internet provider that filed for bankruptcy while holding over $125 million in public funding (Colorado Sun). The company received $9 million in actual payments but appears to have lost track of $3.2 million, with limited work completed on promised rural broadband service.

This is exactly what conservatives warned about: government throwing federal dollars at private contractors with minimal oversight and predictable results. The Broadband Office's inability to prevent fraud or mismanagement before bankruptcy suggests systemic failure in how Colorado administers federal grants.

Republicans have long argued that broadband expansion works better through private investment and competitive markets, not government-directed subsidies that reward connected contractors and leave taxpayers holding the bag. Democrats counter that rural broadband requires federal intervention since private markets won't serve sparsely populated areas—but Maverix's collapse proves the subsidy model itself is broken.

Colorado's rural communities needed reliable internet service, not a lesson in government waste. Watch whether state officials demand accountability from the Broadband Office and whether similar federal programs face renewed scrutiny.
