# Boulder's Climate Lawsuit Could Cost Colorado Taxpayers Millions in Higher Energy Bills

Colorado's Attorney General Phil Weiser appears to be steering state resources into Boulder's aggressive climate litigation—a move that could drive up energy costs for families across the state, according to reporting from Complete Colorado.

The lawsuit targets energy companies over climate damages, but conservatives warn it represents an end-run around federal energy policy and democratic processes. National Review argues the case amounts to "a tax on every American," potentially inflating electricity and heating costs for Colorado households already squeezed by inflation.

Republicans contend that climate policy should be set by elected legislatures, not courtrooms. Using taxpayer-funded attorneys to pursue speculative climate damages diverts resources from core legal functions and exposes the state to countersuit liability. If Weiser's office is indeed involved, Coloradans deserve transparency about which state funds are being deployed and for what purpose.

The lawsuit also signals how progressive officials are weaponizing the legal system to impose energy restrictions without voter consent—a pattern conservatives say undermines both property rights and affordable energy access in Colorado.

Watch whether the legislature demands a full accounting of state resources committed to climate litigation and whether courts ultimately reject Boulder's attempt to bypass federal regulatory authority.
