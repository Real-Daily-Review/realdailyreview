# Vice President Vance Keeps Iran Strategy Close to the Vest—and Won't Throw Allies Under the Bus

Vice President JD Vance is taking a measured, strategic approach to the Iran standoff, refusing to telegraph U.S. intentions or timelines while conditioning any diplomatic talks on Tehran halting attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

At a White House press briefing (Washington Examiner, New York Post), Vance declined to characterize the six-month-plus conflict as a "war," noting "right now, there is no active shooting." He also refused to set a deadline for resolution, telling reporters it would be "irresponsible" to publicly outline strategy and timelines (Just The News).

The administration is leveraging economic pressure: Vance indicated China will help economically isolate Iran (New York Post), while the U.S. has launched fresh strikes on Iranian assets targeting interference with commercial shipping in a critical global chokepoint (Just The News).

On the domestic front, Vance defended his refusal to condemn Tucker Carlson over political disagreements, saying he won't "throw friends under the bus" over tactical disputes—a signal that Trump-aligned conservatives maintain solidarity despite occasional friction (Just The News).

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether this measured posture holds if Iranian strikes on shipping resume or escalate, and whether economic isolation actually forces Tehran to the negotiating table.
