# Denver's Foie Gras Ban: Another Nanny-State Ballot Measure

Denver voters face yet another micromanagement initiative—Initiated Ordinance 311—that would ban the sale of foie gras and criminalize its production method, according to Complete Colorado. The measure exemplifies progressive overreach: government dictating what consenting adults can buy and how restaurants source ingredients.

Conservatives argue this represents exactly the kind of paternalistic regulation that stifles business freedom and consumer choice. Denver already struggles with rising costs and business flight; banning a specialty product serves no public health purpose and only adds compliance burdens to restaurants already navigating inflation and labor shortages.

The Left frames the ban as animal welfare protection, claiming the force-feeding process used in foie gras production constitutes cruelty. Democrats and animal-rights activists have pushed similar bans in California and other progressive strongholds.

Republicans counter that these ballot measures distract from real governance—affordable housing, public safety, and infrastructure—while treating voters like children incapable of making their own purchasing decisions.

Meanwhile, Denver's ballot block party in Five Points (KDVR) aims to boost voter education, though conservatives should remain vigilant about which "education" messaging gets amplified in left-leaning neighborhoods.
