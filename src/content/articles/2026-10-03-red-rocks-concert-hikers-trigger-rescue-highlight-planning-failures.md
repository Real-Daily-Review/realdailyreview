# Teens' Red Rocks Hike Turns Into Costly Rescue Operation

Two teenagers' decision to hike back from a Red Rocks concert instead of taking transportation ended with a West Metro Fire Rescue operation Wednesday night—a reminder that good intentions and poor planning can burden emergency responders and taxpayers.

The teens set out on foot from the iconic Morrison venue after the show, according to reports from KDVR Fox31 Denver and 9News Denver. What "seemed like a great idea" quickly deteriorated when they lost their way in unfamiliar terrain after dark, forcing rescue crews to mobilize and locate them.

West Metro Fire Rescue confirmed the incident on Facebook, noting the hikers' miscalculation. The agency did not immediately disclose the cost of the operation or whether the teens faced citations for misuse of emergency services—a growing concern as rescue calls spike from poor outdoor decision-making.

Colorado's outdoor recreation culture is a genuine asset, but it comes with real responsibility. Hikers, especially young people unfamiliar with the terrain around Red Rocks, should plan routes in daylight, carry communication devices, and use established trails. Preventable rescues drain resources from genuine emergencies.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether West Metro Fire Rescue or Jefferson County considers cost-recovery policies for avoidable rescue calls—a practice some Colorado counties have begun implementing to discourage recklessness.
