# FAA Ground Stop Grounds Hundreds of Flights; Colorado Travelers Stranded

A nationwide FAA ground stop Friday paralyzed U.S. aviation for over four hours, halting all departures and leaving more than a million travelers scrambling—including thousands at Denver International Airport. The disruption, triggered by a telecommunications outage according to Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, forced airlines to cancel and delay hundreds of flights as passengers waited on tarmacs and in terminals (CPR News, Daily Caller).

The FAA ruled out cyberattack or AI-related causes, with FAA Administrator stating there was "zero evidence of anything nefarious" (The Daily Wire). Still, the incident exposed a critical vulnerability: America's aviation infrastructure depends on aging telecommunications systems with single points of failure. One outage crippled the entire national network for hours.

For Colorado travelers and businesses relying on DIA connectivity, the disruption underscored a hard truth conservatives have long warned about: government-run systems often lack redundancy and resilience. The FAA's inability to quickly isolate and resolve the problem raises questions about modernization priorities and whether federal aviation management is adequately equipped for 21st-century demands.

On a lighter note, three rock bands—Simple Plan, Hoobastank, and The Used—turned one stranded Southwest flight into an impromptu Warped Tour concert (9News Denver).
