# Trump Claws Back Nearly $1B in Unspent Federal Spending—Second Time in 50 Years

President Trump is using rare executive authority to rescind nearly $1 billion in congressionally approved but unspent federal funding, targeting DEI programs, immigration initiatives, and foreign aid—a power so seldom deployed that this marks only the second "pocket rescission" in five decades (Washington Examiner).

Under federal law, Trump can request the rescission and withhold the money for 45 days while Congress decides whether to approve, reject, or ignore it (Just The News). The move includes $810 million stripped from immigration and refugee programs, foreign debt-relief initiatives, and business grants.

Republicans see this as fiscal discipline. Democrats and their allies cry foul: Sen. Susan Collins, the GOP chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, complained the action came "without warning or consultation" (9News Denver)—a rare rebuke from a Republican, signaling concern about executive overreach even among Trump allies.

For Colorado, the rescission's impact depends on which programs lose funding. If refugee resettlement dollars shrink, Denver and other sanctuary cities could feel the pinch. If foreign aid or green-energy grants are among the targets, Colorado's progressive communities will howl.

**Watch whether Congress challenges the rescission or lets it stand—and whether Republican moderates join Democrats in forcing Trump's hand.**
