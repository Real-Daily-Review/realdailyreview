# Colorado's Griswold Mess Exposes Fatal Flaw in Primary System

Colorado's Democratic primary chaos has handed conservatives a powerful argument for election reform: plurality winners in crowded fields don't reflect genuine voter preference.

According to Complete Colorado, the Griswold situation demonstrates how a candidate can capture a nomination with only a small minority of votes when the opposition splits among multiple contenders. This is precisely the kind of outcome that frustrates voters across the political spectrum—and it's a vulnerability Republicans should exploit.

Conservatives argue that Colorado's current primary system allows ideologically extreme or unpopular candidates to advance by gaming a fractured field. A runoff system or ranked-choice voting could ensure nominees actually command majority support, not just a plurality. Republicans see an opening to push for structural reforms that would make Democratic nominees more accountable to broader voter consensus—and potentially more vulnerable in general elections.

Democrats, meanwhile, will likely resist changes that might disrupt their machine, even if the current system occasionally backfires on them.

The real question for Colorado taxpayers: Will GOP lawmakers seize this moment to demand primary reforms that strengthen electoral legitimacy, or will they let Democrats quietly fix their own mess behind closed doors?
