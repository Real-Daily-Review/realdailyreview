# Colorado High Court Blocks Democrat Gerrymandering Scheme

Colorado's high court has shut down a Democratic attempt to redraw state legislative districts—a rare win for Republicans in a state where the left controls the redistricting process. According to Complete Colorado, the ruling keeps Colorado out of the national gerrymandering wars, at least temporarily.

Democrats have long held the upper hand in Colorado's redistricting battles, but this court decision signals judicial pushback against partisan map-rigging. Republicans argue that gerrymandering—whether by Democrats or Republicans—undermines representative government and voter choice. The left counters that redistricting is a legitimate political tool and that Colorado's independent commission process is already fairer than most states.

For Colorado taxpayers and voters, the stakes are real: district lines determine which candidates can win, which policies get traction, and ultimately how state resources get spent. A gerrymandered map favoring Democrats means Republican-leaning areas lose legislative clout, making it harder to block tax hikes, gun restrictions, or regulatory overreach that conservative Coloradans oppose.

The ruling also matters nationally. As Complete Colorado notes, it keeps Colorado from becoming another flashpoint in the ongoing redistricting litigation that's consumed courts across the country.

Watch for Democrats' next move: they may appeal, attempt a new map, or push for changes to Colorado's redistricting rules before 2028.
