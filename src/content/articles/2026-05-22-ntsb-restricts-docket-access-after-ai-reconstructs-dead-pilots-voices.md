# AI Reconstruction of Cockpit Voices Prompts NTSB Access Restrictions

People are using artificial intelligence to reconstruct the voices of deceased pilots from crash investigation spectrograms, prompting the National Transportation Safety Board to temporarily restrict access to its public docket system.

According to TechCrunch, users leveraged AI technology on spectrogram images of cockpit recordings to recreate audio, circumventing federal law that prohibits the NTSB from disclosing actual cockpit voice recordings. Ars Technica reports the workaround effectively flouts regulations designed to protect sensitive crash investigation materials.

The NTSB's temporary docket closure represents an emergency response to the practice. The agency publishes investigation documents publicly but is legally barred from releasing raw cockpit audio—a restriction the AI reconstruction method appears designed to bypass.

The incident highlights a broader tension between transparency in safety investigations and privacy protections for those involved in accidents. While the NTSB's public docket serves accountability and safety purposes, the agency has long maintained that releasing actual voice recordings raises ethical and legal concerns.

Watch for the NTSB's next steps on permanent policies governing AI-generated reconstructions of protected investigation materials and whether Congress considers legislative updates to address this emerging loophole.
