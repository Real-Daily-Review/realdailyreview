# Rural Colorado's Lifeline at Risk: Air Ambulance System Faces Collapse Under Outdated Medicare Rates

Medicare's frozen reimbursement rates for air ambulances—unchanged for over 20 years—are strangling a critical service that rural Colorado depends on when seconds count. According to the Washington Examiner, a newly released congressional report highlights how the current reimbursement model is pushing air ambulance operators toward collapse, threatening the only emergency lifeline available across vast stretches of Colorado's mountains and plains.

Air ambulances operate as "flying ICUs," equipped to handle trauma, cardiac events, and medical emergencies in regions where ground transport would mean death. Yet operators face mounting costs—fuel, maintenance, personnel, and equipment—while Medicare payments stagnate. The gap between what the government reimburses and what these services actually cost is unsustainable.

Conservatives argue that outdated federal reimbursement schedules exemplify government's failure to adapt policy to real-world economics. When Washington sets rates and refuses to update them for two decades, it doesn't eliminate costs—it kills the services patients need. Rural Colorado communities, already underserved by federal healthcare policy, face the prospect of losing emergency air transport entirely.

The fix is straightforward: Congress must modernize Medicare reimbursement rates to reflect actual operating costs, or watch rural emergency medicine collapse.

**Watch whether Congress acts on the report's recommendations before another air ambulance operator goes under.**
