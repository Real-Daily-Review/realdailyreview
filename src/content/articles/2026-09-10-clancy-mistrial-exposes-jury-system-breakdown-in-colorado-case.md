# Colorado Mom's Mistrial Exposes Jury System Breakdown—and Jurors Who Can't Keep Quiet

A mistrial in the Lindsay Clancy case has exposed serious cracks in jury deliberation standards, with jurors openly admitting post-verdict that they changed their votes and couldn't resist running to media outlets for attention. One juror told *Breitbart* she originally believed Clancy was guilty but switched her position, saying "maybe I still believe that in a certain way"—hardly the standard of certainty required for a murder conviction. Another juror, per the *New York Post*, even mimicked the strangulation of Clancy's three children during deliberations, prompting fellow jurors to object.

Clancy's defense attorney Kevin Reddington appeared on *CBS Mornings* to reject TikTok conspiracy theories targeting her ex-husband, but the real scandal here is juror misconduct and the erosion of jury discipline. When jurors treat a capital case like a reality-TV audition, rushing to media interviews before the ink dries on the mistrial order, the entire system suffers.

Colorado taxpayers should demand accountability: how did trial judges allow this circus, and what safeguards exist to prevent future juries from treating deliberation rooms as content-creation studios?
