# Oil Flows Again After Iran Deal—Colorado Energy Workers Watch Closely

An interim peace agreement between the U.S. and Iran has opened the Strait of Hormuz to shipping traffic, with Vice President JD Vance reporting that more than 12.5 million barrels of oil passed through overnight, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The breakthrough follows months of supply disruptions that pushed global oil prices higher—a burden felt by Colorado's energy sector and consumers statewide.

Saudi vessels alone carried roughly six million barrels, while tankers from the UAE, Qatar, and China also moved through the critical chokepoint, *Just The News* reports. The resumption signals potential relief for oil markets that have been volatile due to regional tensions.

For Colorado, where oil and gas production remains economically vital despite progressive pressure to phase out fossil fuels, stable global supply and predictable prices matter. Lower oil costs could ease inflationary pressure on Colorado households and businesses—though Democrats in the statehouse continue pushing carbon taxes and renewable mandates that undercut domestic energy development.

Republicans argue the deal demonstrates the value of strong diplomacy backed by credible deterrence. Conservatives caution that any agreement with Iran requires rigorous verification and shouldn't compromise U.S. energy independence or embolden Tehran's regional ambitions.
