# Colorado Honors Fallen Firefighter Nathan Matthews

Colorado paused this week to honor firefighter Nathan Matthews, who died from injuries sustained battling the Knowles Fire—marking the fifth firefighter fatality from Colorado wildfires in 2026. A solemn procession and memorial service in Grand Junction recognized Matthews' sacrifice as the state grapples with an escalating wildfire season.

The loss underscores a hard reality: Colorado's wildfire crisis continues to claim lives despite billions in state and federal spending on fire management. While Democrats and environmental groups often blame "climate change" and push for aggressive land-use restrictions, conservatives argue the real culprit is decades of mismanagement—overgrown forests choked with dead timber, federal land policies that prevent proper thinning, and environmental litigation that blocks defensible forest management.

Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for wildfire response while watching preventable tragedies mount. The state's firefighting resources, though brave and dedicated, remain stretched thin by a landscape left to burn hot rather than managed responsibly.

Republicans and fire management experts have long advocated for active forest stewardship: mechanical thinning, controlled burns, and timber harvesting to reduce fuel loads. Yet environmental groups and progressive regulators continue blocking these proven solutions.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers will finally prioritize forest health over environmental litigation and green ideology—or whether next year brings more memorials.
