# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has left the small community of Beulah facing a dual crisis: homes lost and water systems damaged—a reminder that Colorado's wildfire season doesn't just threaten lives, it cripples the infrastructure taxpayers depend on daily.

The blaze roared through Beulah, a town of roughly 800 residents, destroying numerous homes and damaging critical water utilities that serve the community (Colorado Sun). Water managers are now scrambling to restore clean water service, a process officials acknowledge will be lengthy and costly (Colorado Sun).

The damage underscores a hard reality: as wildfires grow more frequent and intense, the burden of rebuilding essential infrastructure falls on already-stretched local budgets and state coffers. Beulah's water crisis illustrates why Colorado needs to prioritize forest management and fuel reduction—preventive measures that cost far less than post-fire recovery and infrastructure repair.

The good news: most other active fires across Colorado have spared critical water systems so far. But Beulah's experience shows the stakes are real for rural communities dependent on aging utility networks.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether state and local leaders prioritize aggressive forest management and private-sector solutions over new regulations or spending that doesn't address root causes of catastrophic fire behavior.
