# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, September 5, 2026

Colorado heads into Labor Day weekend with a sewage crisis fouling the Arkansas River, Democrats in Washington blocking a common-sense court-packing guardrail, and the nation's energy future hanging in the balance — all while your tax dollars and your freedoms remain squarely in the crosshairs.

## Arkansas River Crisis: 4.25 Million Gallons of Sewage and a Logjam to Boot

Colorado wildlife crews just finished breaking up a 100-yard logjam on the Arkansas River ahead of the holiday weekend — no explosives needed, thankfully. But the bigger story is what's already in the water: a 4.25 million gallon sewage spill that has Coloradans asking whether their cantaloupes, peppers, and recreational waterways are safe. Ranchers and farmers downstream deserve straight answers from state regulators, not bureaucratic hand-wringing. Colorado taxpayers will be watching closely to see who's accountable for this environmental failure and what it costs to fix it.

## Democrats Block Amendment to Lock SCOTUS at Nine Justices

Senate Democrats voted down a constitutional amendment that would have permanently fixed the Supreme Court at nine justices, killing the one measure that could have taken court-packing off the table for good. Republicans argue this is exactly what it looks like: the left keeping the door open to stacking the Court the moment they regain power. For Colorado conservatives, this is a five-alarm warning. An ideologically packed Supreme Court would threaten Second Amendment protections, property rights, and every legal guardrail against federal overreach that Coloradans depend on.

## Biden Judge Blocks Trump Birthright Citizenship Order

A Biden-appointed federal judge blocked President Trump's executive order on birthright citizenship, declaring it "almost certainly unconstitutional" — a ruling the White House swiftly labeled judicial activism. Conservatives say a single district court judge overriding a presidential executive order on immigration policy is precisely the kind of bench legislating that has no business in a constitutional republic. Colorado, which has seen significant pressure on public services from illegal immigration, has a direct stake in whether the federal government can enforce coherent border and citizenship policy.
