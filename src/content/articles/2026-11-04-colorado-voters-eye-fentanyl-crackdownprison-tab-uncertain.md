# Colorado Voters Weigh Fentanyl Crackdown—But Prison Costs Loom Large

Colorado voters are deciding whether to crack down hard on fentanyl dealers and users, but the real question is whether taxpayers are ready to foot the bill for more prison beds.

Proposition 132 would stiffen penalties for possessing and distributing fentanyl across the state. According to CPR News, the measure is leading in early election returns and would fundamentally reshape Colorado's approach to the opioid crisis by imposing stricter criminal consequences.

The catch: Colorado Sun reports that Prop 132 would increase state spending on prisons—a significant fiscal commitment when Colorado's budget is already strained. Conservatives face a familiar tension here. Tough-on-crime voters want dealers locked up; fiscal hawks worry about ballooning corrections costs and whether harsher penalties actually deter drug trafficking or simply warehouse offenders at taxpayer expense.

Republicans generally support stronger drug enforcement, but some question whether mandatory minimums and expanded incarceration are the most cost-effective way to combat fentanyl's grip on Colorado communities. Democrats have been quieter on the measure, though some progressives argue resources should flow to treatment and prevention instead.

The fentanyl crisis is real and deadly across Colorado—but voters should watch whether the state legislature addresses *how* Prop 132 gets funded without raiding education or transportation budgets.
