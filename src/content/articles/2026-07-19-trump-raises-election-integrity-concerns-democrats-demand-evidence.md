# Trump Claims Voting System Vulnerabilities; Democrats Demand Proof

President Trump raised concerns about election integrity in a primetime address, warning that the country's voting systems are vulnerable to being "rigged and stolen"—though he did not present new evidence of fraudulent votes cast in any election, according to CPR News.

Democrats quickly pushed back. Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) told CNN's *State of the Union* there was "no evidence" supporting the Trump administration's allegations that noncitizens voted in 2020, citing his experience as Pennsylvania's lieutenant governor during that election (Washington Examiner).

**The Colorado angle:** Election integrity remains a flashpoint in Colorado politics. Republicans have long argued that mail-in voting systems—which Colorado expanded significantly—create vulnerabilities, while Democrats and election officials maintain Colorado's processes are secure. Trump's renewed focus on voting system vulnerabilities could energize Colorado conservatives demanding stricter voter ID requirements and tighter ballot chain-of-custody protocols.

Republicans counter that Democrats' reflexive dismissal of election concerns without rigorous public audits fuels voter distrust. Conservatives argue that transparency—not accusations of bad faith—should guide election administration.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado election officials will face renewed pressure to conduct comprehensive audits of voting systems and ballot procedures ahead of the 2026 midterms.
