# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Sunday, July 26, 2026

Colorado conservatives head into a pivotal week with a thinned Republican gubernatorial field, a federal court meddling in election integrity, and a reminder that the radical left's agenda carries real-world consequences — here and abroad. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Colorado GOP Narrows Governor's Race to Two

Out of 18 Republican gubernatorial candidates, only two survived the state assembly process and advanced to the primary ballot. That's a meaningful filter — and a sign that Colorado Republicans are serious about fielding a focused, competitive ticket rather than a crowded, diluted field. Conservatives should pay close attention to which two candidates emerged and whether either has the backbone to take on Colorado's entrenched Democratic machine in November.

---

## Federal Court Blocks Trump's Mail-In Voting Order

A federal appeals court has blocked President Trump's executive order on mail-in voting rules across 23 states, handing election-integrity advocates a significant setback ahead of the midterms. Republicans argue that tightening mail-in voting rules is essential to ensuring secure, verifiable elections — and Democrats have fought those efforts at every turn. With the case potentially headed to the Supreme Court, Colorado voters — who cast nearly all ballots by mail — have a direct stake in how this fight ends.

---

## Van Plows Into Crowd at Berlin LGBT Event — At Least One Dead

At least one person is dead after a van drove into a crowd at an LGBT event in Berlin, with police shutting down the event and ordering everyone to evacuate. The attack is a sobering reminder that political and cultural gatherings — regardless of ideology — can become targets for violence. Conservatives who have long argued for robust law enforcement and serious threat assessment will be watching how German authorities respond and whether the political class draws the right lessons.

---

## Razorback Sucker Downlisted From Endangered to Threatened

Federal wildlife officials have downlisted the razorback sucker — a fish native to Colorado and other Western river systems — from endangered to threatened status. That's a genuine conservation win, and one achieved largely through water management cooperation rather than heavy-handed federal mandates. Colorado ranchers, farmers, and water users should note the distinction: species recovery is possible without shutting down agriculture or strangling Western water rights.

---

## Colorado Should Get Out of the Party-Recognition Business

A sharp piece out of Complete Colorado makes the case that Colorado government has no proper business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties — and that separating state from party is the genuinely principled move. Conservatives who believe in limited government should agree: the state apparatus shouldn't be in the business of propping up any party's infrastructure, including the GOP's. Clean lines between government and party are good for liberty, full stop.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the two Republican gubernatorial candidates who cleared the assembly — vetting starts now, and Colorado conservatives cannot afford a weak nominee against a well-funded Democratic incumbent. Watch the mail-in voting case closely; if it reaches the Supreme Court, the ruling could reshape election law in Colorado and across the country before November. And as the Berlin attack investigation unfolds, pay attention to whether national media apply the same scrutiny they would demand in any other context — or look the other way.
