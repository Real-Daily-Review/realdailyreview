# Federal Grand Jury Indicts Kentucky Woman for Vandalizing WWII Memorial

A Kentucky woman has been formally indicted on federal charges for defacing Washington, D.C.'s World War II Memorial with spray paint and bubble soap in what authorities are calling a brazen attack on a sacred American monument.

Melissa Farris, 41, of Elizabethtown, Kentucky, faces up to 20 years in prison after allegedly livestreaming herself vandalizing the memorial with pink, green, and red graffiti earlier this month (New York Post). U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro characterized the incident as a "despicable attack" on a site honoring those who sacrificed for the nation (Fox News Politics).

According to the Washington Examiner, Farris appeared to be homeless and in a questionable mental state when she committed the vandalism. The federal grand jury indicted her on two counts related to the destruction of federal property.

The case underscores a broader conservative concern: the erosion of respect for American institutions and symbols, particularly among those with untreated mental illness or ideological hostility toward the nation's heritage. While progressives often frame such incidents as isolated mental-health crises, conservatives argue they reflect a cultural breakdown in accountability and reverence for America's military legacy.
