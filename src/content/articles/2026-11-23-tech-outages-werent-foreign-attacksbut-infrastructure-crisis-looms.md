# Tech Outages Weren't Foreign Attacks—But America's Digital Infrastructure Still Needs Work

The recent wave of technology outages that disrupted American businesses and services wasn't a coordinated foreign cyberattack, according to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton. Speaking on CNN's "State of the Union," Clayton said the incidents stemmed from upgrades to the nation's outdated digital infrastructure, emphasizing the outages were "not coordinated" and "not state-sponsored" (Daily Caller).

While that's reassuring on the national security front, it exposes a harder truth: America's critical systems are aging and fragile. Clayton framed the disruptions as part of "a period of technological step change"—bureaucratic speak for "we're playing catch-up" (Breitbart).

For Colorado, this matters. The state's energy sector, water infrastructure, and financial systems depend on reliable digital networks. If federal and state governments can't maintain basic technological stability, Colorado businesses and taxpayers foot the bill when outages hit. The question conservatives should ask: Why does modernizing critical infrastructure take a crisis to get attention? And more importantly, will government-led "solutions" mean more spending, more regulation, and more bureaucratic bloat—or genuine efficiency?

Watch whether Colorado state officials push for federal infrastructure dollars that come with strings attached, or demand private-sector leadership in rebuilding America's digital backbone.
