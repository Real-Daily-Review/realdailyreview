# Feds Deploy Drones to Police July 4th Fireworks—Another Surveillance Expansion

The federal government is using Independence Day as cover to expand the surveillance state. Public safety agencies nationwide will deploy drones to spot illegal fireworks during Fourth of July celebrations, thanks to FAA rules loosened last year that fast-tracked so-called "Drone as First Responder" (DFR) initiatives by cutting bureaucratic review (The Daily Wire).

The move exemplifies how Washington uses crisis rhetoric and public safety concerns to normalize aerial monitoring. While fireworks enforcement may sound reasonable, conservatives worry the precedent opens the door to broader drone surveillance of lawful activities—a particular concern in Colorado, where land-use disputes, energy development, and property rights already face federal overreach.

Republicans argue that cutting red tape for drone deployment without robust privacy safeguards inverts the proper order: government should prove necessity *before* expanding surveillance tools, not after. The left frames DFR programs as efficient public safety measures, but skeptics note that once these systems are in place, scope creep is inevitable.

Colorado taxpayers and gun owners should watch whether state and local authorities adopt these federal drone programs and what privacy protections—if any—accompany them.
