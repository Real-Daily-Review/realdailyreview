# Non-Citizen Police Officers: A Dangerous Experiment Spreading Across America

Blue-state and swing-state legislatures are quietly dismantling citizenship requirements for law enforcement—a move that threatens public safety and national security at a moment when border chaos demands accountability, not compromise.

Connecticut's Danbury city council voted 18-2 to allow non-citizens and DACA recipients to wear badges and carry guns. Utah lawmakers followed suit, passing legislation to open police ranks to non-citizens. Democrats frame this as inclusion; Republicans call it reckless.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Police officers take oaths to uphold the Constitution and enforce federal law. Vetting procedures assume citizenship verification as baseline security. Allowing non-citizens—particularly those without full legal status—into law enforcement creates liability, conflicts of interest, and potential national security gaps. Former Rep. Jason Chaffetz (R-Utah) called the Utah bill "crazy and wrong" on social media.

**Democrats argue** the moves address police staffing shortages and signal welcome to immigrant communities. One Danbury official claimed the vote "sends a message" against ICE enforcement.

Colorado hasn't yet moved on this front, but progressive Denver and Boulder leadership could push similar measures. Conservatives should watch whether Colorado cities or the state legislature attempt comparable citizenship-waiver bills—and whether Republican lawmakers will preemptively block them.
