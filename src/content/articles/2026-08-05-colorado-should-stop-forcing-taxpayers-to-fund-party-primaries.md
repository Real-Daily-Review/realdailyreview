# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Party Primaries They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are bankrolling primary elections for political parties they may not belong to—a government overreach that deserves to end.

Complete Colorado has raised a straightforward question: why should the state compel taxpayers to finance Democratic and Republican primaries when many voters are unaffiliated or belong to neither major party? The arrangement amounts to government-mandated subsidy of private political organizations, a practice conservatives argue violates basic principles of limited government and individual liberty.

Republicans and conservatives contend that Colorado should separate state from party entirely. Funding primary elections through general tax revenue forces citizens to support partisan machinery against their will—a form of coerced political speech that contradicts free-market principles and fiscal responsibility. If parties want primaries, they should fund them directly through member dues and voluntary donations.

Democrats have not publicly challenged this framing in the available reporting, leaving the fiscal and philosophical argument largely uncontested in conservative outlets.

The issue reflects a broader tension in Colorado politics: how much should government entangle itself with party machinery? As primary season approaches, taxpayers should demand clarity on whether their dollars truly belong in partisan contests.
