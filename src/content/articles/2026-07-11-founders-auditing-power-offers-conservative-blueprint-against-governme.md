# Founders' Legacy of Auditing Power Offers Blueprint for Restraining Modern Government

America's founders understood something modern Washington has forgotten: government exists to answer to the people, not rule them. That's the core insight animating fresh conservative commentary on the Founders' example as a check on unchecked executive and bureaucratic power.

Kelley Paul's new children's book, *Good Night, Young American*, reframes the Founders as "the first civil rights heroes" (Fox News), emphasizing their commitment to individual liberty against state overreach. The Washington Examiner expands the argument: the Founders "audited power" by gathering facts, naming abuses, and insisting that legitimacy flows from consent of the governed—a principle conservatives say has eroded as federal agencies and progressive administrations bypass Congress and ignore constitutional limits.

For Colorado, this matters. State taxpayers and business owners face expanding federal regulations on energy development, water rights, and land use—all justified by distant bureaucrats claiming authority the Constitution never granted them. The Founders' model of accountability and limited government directly challenges the regulatory state that constrains Colorado's economy and energy independence.

Republicans and conservatives argue the Founders' framework demands we audit *today's* government with equal rigor: demand transparency, name abuses, and reassert constitutional limits on federal power.

Watch for how Colorado's congressional delegation applies this principle to federal overreach on energy and water policy.
