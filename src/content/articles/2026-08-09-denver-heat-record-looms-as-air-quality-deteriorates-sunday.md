# Denver Braces for Dangerous Heat Sunday; Air Quality Concerns Mount

Denver faces a potentially record-breaking heat event Sunday, with temperatures climbing into the upper 90s to 100 degrees (KDVR), threatening to shatter historical benchmarks as poor air quality compounds the danger for residents across the Front Range.

The National Weather Service forecasts near-record highs Sunday, with gusty virga showers—precipitation that evaporates before reaching ground—offering little relief (9News). The combination of extreme heat and degraded air quality creates a public health risk, particularly for vulnerable populations, outdoor workers, and those with respiratory conditions.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses, extreme weather events underscore the importance of reliable, affordable energy to power air conditioning and cooling centers. Conservatives have long argued that Colorado's aggressive push toward renewable-only electricity mandates—driven by progressive climate policies—risks grid reliability during peak demand periods like this. While Democrats frame such policies as necessary climate action, Republicans counter that premature coal plant retirements and over-reliance on intermittent wind and solar threaten the stable, affordable power Coloradans need when temperatures spike.

The state's Public Utilities Commission continues weighing utility proposals that would accelerate coal phase-outs; Sunday's heat event will test whether current grid capacity can handle the load.

Watch whether Colorado's energy regulators acknowledge grid stress during extreme weather and adjust renewable mandates accordingly—or double down on ideology over reliability.
