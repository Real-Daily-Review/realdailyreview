# China's Missile Test Exposes NATO's Spending Shuffle—And Trump Won't Let Allies Dodge the Bill

China's rare submarine ballistic missile test into the Pacific has rattled Australia and Japan just as NATO prepares to confront a harder truth: many allies are still gaming the numbers on defense spending rather than actually paying up.

President Trump is set to warn NATO countries against using "funny math" to hit the 2030 defense spending targets he's demanding (New York Post). Several prominent NATO members are struggling to meet even the current benchmarks, let alone Trump's more ambitious goals. The message is blunt: no accounting tricks, no creative accounting, no pretending.

The timing matters. China's provocative missile display underscores why NATO's European members can't keep freeloading on American security guarantees. For decades, they've underfunded their own defense while the U.S. footed the bill. Trump's warning signals he's done subsidizing that arrangement.

**The Colorado angle:** Defense spending debates directly affect Colorado's aerospace and defense contractors, military installations, and the broader economy. If NATO allies finally start paying their fair share, U.S. defense budgets could shift—potentially benefiting Colorado's robust defense sector.

Conservatives argue that real defense commitments, not creative accounting, are the only credible response to Chinese aggression. Watch whether Trump actually enforces consequences for NATO laggards or settles for more promises.
