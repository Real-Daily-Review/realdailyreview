# Trump Seizes Central Asia Opportunity; Congress Dragging Feet

The Trump administration is moving decisively to fill the geopolitical vacuum Russia left behind in Central Asia—but Congress is slow-walking the play, according to the Washington Examiner.

Russia's war in Ukraine has gutted Moscow's influence over its former Soviet sphere. The White House capitalized by displacing Russia as the broker in the Armenia-Azerbaijan conflict as of August 2025. Yet Congress has not kept pace with the administration's strategic tempo, the Examiner reports.

The core issue: Congress is hesitating on repealing Cold War-era trade restrictions (Jackson-Vanik provisions) that would unlock American economic leverage in Central Asia. Republicans argue swift action is essential to cement U.S. advantage while Moscow remains weakened and regional powers are receptive to American partnership.

**The Colorado angle:** Expanded Central Asian trade could benefit Colorado energy and agricultural exporters competing globally. Delays in congressional authorization squander market-opening momentum that Colorado businesses need.

Conservatives say Congress must stop treating geopolitical opportunity like a committee assignment. The window to establish American influence in Central Asia won't stay open indefinitely—and every month of legislative foot-dragging hands Moscow a chance to rebuild.

Watch whether Republican leadership in Congress can force a vote on Jackson-Vanik repeal before the window closes.
