# Trump Threatens Tax on Tech Giants Building AI Centers Abroad

President Trump is signaling he'll slap "a tremendous tax" on U.S. tech companies that relocate artificial intelligence data centers outside America—a protectionist move that could reshape where tech investment flows and how Colorado's energy and data infrastructure compete for major corporate projects.

The threat, announced during Trump's recent remarks (9News Denver), targets the growing trend of tech firms outsourcing data center construction to countries with cheaper labor and energy costs. Trump's position reflects his broader "America First" economic doctrine: keep high-value industrial capacity—and the jobs and tax revenue it generates—on U.S. soil.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans argue that penalizing offshoring protects American workers and ensures critical AI infrastructure remains under domestic control. They contend that without such leverage, companies will continue chasing lower costs abroad, hollowing out domestic manufacturing and energy sectors.

**The Counterargument:** Free-market conservatives worry that tariffs and punitive taxes distort markets, raise costs for consumers, and invite retaliation. Some note that heavy-handed industrial policy can backfire.

For Colorado specifically, this matters: the state has positioned itself as a data center hub, with abundant hydroelectric power and cooling advantages. A federal tax on offshore AI centers could redirect investment toward Colorado facilities—or it could trigger trade wars that harm the broader economy.

**Watch next:** Whether Trump follows through with specific tariff rates and how Colorado's tech and energy sectors respond to the new competitive landscape.
