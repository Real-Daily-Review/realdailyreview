# Trump's Press Restrictions Draw Fire From Right and Left—But for Different Reasons

Trump's attempt to restrict press access is backfiring across the political spectrum, creating an unusual alliance of critics. The Colorado Sun's Mike Littwin compares the move to Trump's Iran policy, calling it a "disaster" that undermines democratic norms. But conservatives aren't rallying to defend it either. National Review argues Trump should rescind the ban outright, contending it violates "the spirit of a free press" and that Trump would be "better served by confronting reporters directly."

The tension highlights a core conservative principle: government power—even when wielded by allies—demands scrutiny. Republicans have long championed First Amendment protections against state overreach. A press ban, regardless of provocation, hands ammunition to the left's claims about authoritarian impulses and gives hostile media outlets a legitimate grievance to exploit.

For Colorado conservatives, the lesson is straightforward: winning battles through executive fiat often costs more than it gains. Restricting access doesn't silence critics; it amplifies them. Direct confrontation, transparency, and letting facts speak for themselves remain the stronger play.

Watch whether Trump reverses course and whether Colorado Republicans distance themselves from the policy or defend it—a telling indicator of where the state GOP stands on constitutional limits.
