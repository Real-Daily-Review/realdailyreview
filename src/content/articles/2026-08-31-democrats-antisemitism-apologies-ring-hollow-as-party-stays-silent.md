# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Abdul El-Sayed's carefully choreographed apology tour with Michigan's Democratic Jewish Caucus won't solve the Left's deepening antisemitism crisis. While El-Sayed met with Jewish Democrats to walk back his association with streamer Hasan Piker—who has made antisemitic remarks and expressed sympathy for terrorists—Republicans and conservative observers say the damage control rings hollow.

Mike Rogers, Michigan's Republican Senate candidate, called El-Sayed's apology a "sham," and for good reason. According to the Washington Examiner, only six Democrats have explicitly condemned Piker's latest antisemitic rant blaming Jews for potential violence against them. That's not distancing; that's silence masquerading as leadership.

The pattern is clear: when progressive Democrats court fringe voices and radical influencers, they inherit the moral stain. El-Sayed's refusal to dump Piker entirely—drawing a line at the streamer but not severing ties—exposes the limits of Democratic accountability on antisemitism.

For Colorado Democrats, this is a warning. As the party continues drifting left and embracing increasingly radical rhetoric on Israel and Jewish identity, state party leaders should ask themselves whether they're comfortable with the company their national allies are keeping.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic establishment distances itself from similar rhetoric or follows the national party's pattern of selective outrage.
