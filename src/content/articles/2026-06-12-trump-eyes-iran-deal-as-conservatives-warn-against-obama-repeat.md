# Trump Signals Iran Deal Talks—But Conservatives Warn Against Repeating Obama's Blunder

President Trump is reportedly considering renewed negotiations with Iran over the Strait of Hormuz and its oil exports, backing away from threats to seize Tehran's Kharg Island terminal after signals of Iranian willingness to discuss terms, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The pivot raises immediate red flags among conservatives. The *Examiner's* opinion section warns Trump risks repeating the Obama administration's failed Iran nuclear deal strategy—a diplomatic gambit that critics argue only enriched and emboldened a hostile regime while delivering nothing durable in return. "The Islamic Republic has spent decades using negotiations" as cover for advancing its interests, the outlet notes, cautioning that another agreement could prove equally hollow.

**Colorado angle:** Energy markets tied to Middle Eastern oil stability directly affect Colorado's economy and gas prices at the pump. Any Iran deal that destabilizes global energy markets or weakens U.S. leverage could ripple through state energy policy and consumer costs.

Republicans argue Trump must maintain maximum pressure on Iran rather than negotiate from a weakened position. Conservatives worry that diplomatic "breakthroughs" with Tehran historically collapse once the ink dries, leaving America worse off.

**Watch:** Whether Trump pursues a formal agreement or uses negotiations as leverage to extract concessions—and how any deal affects Colorado energy markets and national security posture.
