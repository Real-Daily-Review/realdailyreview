# Douglas County Cracks Down on E-Bikes While Wildlife Overpass Shows Promise

Douglas County commissioners just handed government regulators another tool to micromanage how Coloradans use their own property—approving an emergency ordinance Tuesday to restrict electric dirt bikes and off-road scooters, citing a "rash of crashes" (Colorado Sun). The move bypassed the standard 30-day public comment period, raising questions about whether safety concerns justified the regulatory fast-track.

Meanwhile, the county is seeing real success with infrastructure that actually works: an elk recently became the first recorded animal to cross the Greenland Wildlife Overpass, a project designed to reconnect fragmented habitat and reduce wildlife-vehicle collisions (9News). The overpass demonstrates that smart engineering—not bans—can solve genuine safety problems.

The e-bike restriction reveals the progressive instinct to regulate first and ask questions later. Conservatives argue that emergency ordinances should be rare, not routine, and that property owners and recreational users deserve a voice before government restricts access. The wildlife overpass, by contrast, shows what happens when government invests in solutions that benefit both safety and conservation without trampling individual choice.

Douglas County taxpayers should watch whether commissioners use emergency powers again to expand restrictions—or whether they'll rely on proven infrastructure solutions instead.
