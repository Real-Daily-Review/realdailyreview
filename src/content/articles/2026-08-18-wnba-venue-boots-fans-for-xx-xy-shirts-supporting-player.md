# WNBA Venue Censors Fans Supporting Player Over Sex-Based Biology Shirts

Colorado's culture wars just went courtside. At State Farm Arena in Atlanta, security ordered a mother and daughter to cover up or leave—their crime: wearing "XX-XY" shirts supporting Indiana Fever guard Sophie Cunningham, who has publicly backed the brand. The WNBA later claimed it "fell short" of its values, a corporate non-apology that dodges the real issue: a publicly funded venue (or one with public partnerships) silencing fans for expressing a viewpoint on biological sex.

**The conservative argument:** This is textbook corporate censorship masquerading as inclusivity. Fans weren't being disruptive; they were wearing shirts celebrating basic biology. The fact that a major sports league felt compelled to police fan speech over sex-based messaging reveals how thoroughly progressive ideology has infiltrated even entertainment venues. If venues can eject fans for "XX-XY" shirts, what's next—ejecting fans for saying "women's sports"?

**Democrats and the WNBA counter** that the league prioritizes an inclusive environment for all fans, though specifics on what violated policy remain vague.

The incident underscores a broader Colorado concern: when corporations and venues adopt progressive speech codes, taxpayers and fans lose the freedom to express dissent. Watch whether Colorado venues adopt similar policies—and whether conservative fans push back.
