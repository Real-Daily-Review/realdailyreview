# Arizona Authorities Release Ransom Notes in Nancy Guthrie Kidnapping Case

Authorities investigating the February abduction of 84-year-old Nancy Guthrie have released two ransom notes publicly, hoping someone recognizes the handwriting or details that could crack the case. Guthrie vanished from her home outside Tucson on February 1st; the notes were sent to local Arizona news outlet 13 News on February 2nd and February 6th, according to the New York Post. Police are treating the case as an active kidnapping investigation and believe releasing the notes may prompt tips from the public.

The decision to publicize the ransom demands represents a calculated law-enforcement gamble—sometimes transparency pressures suspects or reveals identifying details witnesses recognize. Authorities have not disclosed specific demands or threats contained in the notes, citing investigative sensitivity.

The case has drawn national attention as weeks stretch into months without resolution. Guthrie's family and law enforcement continue appealing for information from anyone who may have seen suspicious activity near her property or recognized the handwriting in the notes.

Coloradans should watch for any connection to our state, as kidnapping cases often involve interstate movement and tips from neighboring regions.
