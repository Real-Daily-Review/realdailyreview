# Colorado Healthcare System Must Confront Postpartum Psychosis Gap

The Lindsay Clancy case has exposed a critical blind spot in how Colorado's healthcare industry screens for and treats postpartum psychosis—a rare but severe mental health crisis that demands immediate clinical attention.

A University of Denver social work professor is now pushing Colorado hospitals and providers to learn from the high-profile case, which drew national scrutiny to the condition (KDVR Fox31). The case also raised alarm bells elsewhere: an Illinois mother accused of killing her son cited the Clancy case as inspiration for her own alleged crime (Daily Caller), underscoring how media coverage of untreated mental illness can have dangerous ripple effects.

The core issue conservatives and healthcare advocates should focus on: Colorado's medical system needs better protocols for identifying postpartum psychosis risk factors during pregnancy and immediately postpartum, without creating a new regulatory burden or expanding government oversight of maternal care. The solution lies in professional accountability and clinical best practices—not more state mandates.

Postpartum psychosis is distinct from postpartum depression and requires emergency psychiatric intervention. Colorado OB-GYNs and midwives should voluntarily adopt screening standards and ensure rapid access to mental health specialists.

Watch whether Colorado's Department of Health and Environment uses this moment to impose new regulations on maternity care providers, or whether the medical community steps up with self-directed improvements.
