# Colorado DA Anne Kelly Turns Around Crisis-Ridden Office While National Progressive DAs Undermine Justice

Anne Kelly inherited a disaster: Alamosa County's district attorney's office was drowning in misconduct and dysfunction. Four years later, she's stabilized the operation and earned respect in rural Colorado—a stark contrast to progressive DAs in major cities who've turned crime-fighting into a punchline.

Kelly's turnaround in the San Luis Valley demonstrates what competent, no-nonsense prosecution looks like. She faced real challenges in a rural setting, learned the community's crime patterns, and rebuilt institutional credibility (CPR News). That's the kind of practical leadership Colorado counties need.

Meanwhile, progressive district attorneys elsewhere are making national headlines for the wrong reasons. Philadelphia's Larry Krasner has become a symbol of the soft-on-crime movement—so extreme that Pennsylvania's Supreme Court recently invoked extraordinary jurisdiction to override his office's handling of a convicted murderer case (The Daily Wire). When state courts have to step in because a DA won't do the job, you've got a legitimacy crisis.

The contrast matters for Colorado. Voters in rural and suburban counties are watching whether their DAs will actually prosecute crime or become ideological activists. Kelly's success shows that Coloradans don't need radical criminal-justice experiments—they need competent prosecutors who respect victims and public safety.

Watch whether Colorado's progressive prosecutors adopt Krasner-style policies or follow Kelly's example of restoring institutional trust.
