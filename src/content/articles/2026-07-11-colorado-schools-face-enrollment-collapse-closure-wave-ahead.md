# Colorado School Enrollment Collapse Looms as Parents Abandon Public System

Public school enrollment is cratering nationwide, and Colorado communities will soon face hard choices about closures and consolidations that could reshape local budgets and property values.

The Washington Examiner reports that K-12 public school attendance has fallen to 50.8 million students, triggering a wave of facility downsizing across districts. Colorado is not immune. As families flee to charter schools, homeschooling, and private alternatives—or simply have fewer children—traditional public school districts face shrinking tax bases and the prospect of closing neighborhood schools.

**The conservative argument:** Declining enrollment exposes a fundamental problem with government-run education: bloated administrative overhead and inflexible union contracts that prevent schools from adapting quickly. When enrollment drops, districts should cut spending proportionally rather than raising property taxes to prop up underutilized buildings. School choice—charters, education savings accounts, and vouchers—allows families to vote with their feet, forcing public systems to compete or downsize naturally.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** Rural and suburban districts dependent on property tax revenue will face acute pressure. Consolidations could eliminate local control and force longer bus rides for students. Meanwhile, progressive districts may resist closures in favor of tax increases, burdening Colorado homeowners already squeezed by inflation.

Watch whether Colorado school boards prioritize fiscal discipline or demand new revenue from taxpayers facing enrollment collapse.
