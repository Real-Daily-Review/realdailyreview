# Trump's Iran Deal Draws Fire From Right and Left Over Capitulation Claims

President Trump's Iran truce agreement is drawing sharp criticism from both conservative and progressive quarters—though for starkly different reasons. National Review argues the president "abandoned his previous positions in favor of the Iranian position on all the key issues of contention," calling it a "prompt and utter humiliation." Meanwhile, Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin attacks Trump and VP JD Vance for what he characterizes as spreading misinformation about the deal's terms.

Republicans and conservatives are split. National Review's right-flank critique suggests Trump gave away leverage on nuclear negotiations and sanctions relief without securing meaningful concessions. Littwin and the left, conversely, appear concerned the administration is misrepresenting the agreement to the public—citing Vance's inflammatory rhetoric as evidence of a broader pattern of dishonesty.

The deal's specifics remain contested. What's clear: Trump's signature foreign-policy gambit has unified critics across the ideological spectrum, each side accusing the other of either capitulating to Tehran or deceiving Americans about what was actually negotiated.

**Watch for:** Congressional Republicans' formal response and whether conservative media pressure forces the White House to release the full agreement text.
