# Trump Heads to NATO Summit Amid Iran Tensions, Ukraine Peace Push

President Trump arrives at the NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey, with a dual agenda: pressing allies on military support for operations against Iran and pitching himself as the dealmaker who can end the Ukraine war—even as frustration over denied base access and overflight rights threatens alliance cohesion (Fox News).

The Trump administration is leveraging renewed confidence that Putin "feels the pressure" to restart Ukraine peace negotiations, a sharp reversal from Trump's earlier dismissal of the conflict as "in the rear view mirror" (Washington Examiner). Conservatives argue this represents Trump's proven ability to negotiate from strength—a contrast to the Biden administration's open-ended commitment to Ukraine.

Republicans point to a concrete win: since Trump's first term, NATO allies have added $1.2 trillion in defense spending, dubbed the "Trump Trillion" by NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte. Conservatives credit Trump's demands for burden-sharing with forcing Europe to take its own security seriously rather than freeloading on American taxpayers.

For Colorado, the outcome matters: sustained NATO commitment affects defense contractors and military installations across the state, while any Ukraine settlement could reshape defense spending priorities.

Watch whether Trump secures the Iran operation support he's demanding—and whether European resistance signals cracks in the alliance Trump claims to have strengthened.
