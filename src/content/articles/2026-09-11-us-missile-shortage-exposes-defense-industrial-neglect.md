# U.S. Faces Critical Missile Shortage as Defense Industrial Base Struggles

America's military is running dangerously low on critical munitions after prolonged conflict with Iran, exposing years of neglect in defense procurement and production capacity.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the U.S. faces a severe shortage of essential munitions stemming from slow production rates and insufficient congressional funding for inventory replenishment. Years of underinvestment in the defense industrial base have weakened America's ability to sustain military commitments and project power globally.

The Trump administration has responded by pressuring defense contractors to accelerate production schedules and urging Congress to approve larger munitions inventories. Conservatives argue this shortage reflects decades of strategic mismanagement—prioritizing social spending over military readiness while adversaries like Iran test American resolve.

**The Colorado angle:** Defense manufacturing and aerospace remain critical to Colorado's economy. Companies like Lockheed Martin (Grand Prairie facility) and other contractors depend on stable, predictable defense spending. A robust munitions production surge could mean jobs and economic activity for the state, but only if Congress follows through with sustained funding rather than treating defense as a budget afterthought.

Republicans contend that rebuilding America's military capacity requires not just tariff policy but serious, sustained appropriations—and a cultural shift away from treating defense spending as discretionary.

Watch whether Congress will authorize the emergency funding and multi-year contracts necessary to rebuild the munitions stockpile, or whether political gridlock will leave America's defenses inadequate.
