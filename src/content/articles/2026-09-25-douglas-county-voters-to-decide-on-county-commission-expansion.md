# Douglas County Voters Face Government Expansion Pitch as County Grows

Douglas County residents will decide this fall whether to expand their county commission—a move that pits growth management against bigger government and higher costs.

Boulder County Commissioner Ashley Stolzmann argues that as counties grow, "there's more of a need for more representation" across Longmont, Boulder, and outlying areas (Denver Post). Proponents frame commission expansion as necessary to handle increased population and service demands.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives counter that adding commissioners means more government overhead, higher salaries, and expanded bureaucracy—without proof that more politicians improve service delivery. They argue Douglas County should first demonstrate that existing commissioners cannot handle growth through better efficiency and delegation, not headcount expansion.

The timing matters: Douglas County has experienced rapid suburban growth, and commissioners already juggle competing demands from Longmont, Boulder, and rural areas. Yet conservatives worry that expansion sets a precedent for perpetual government growth tied to population increases—a model that inevitably leads to higher taxes and regulatory creep.

Both counties will put the question to voters this fall (Denver Post). Coloradans should watch whether expansion campaigns rely on efficiency arguments or simply assume bigger government is the default answer to growth.
