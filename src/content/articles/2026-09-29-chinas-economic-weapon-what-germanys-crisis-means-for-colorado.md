# Colorado Conservatives Should Brace for China Trade Fallout

Colorado's energy and agriculture sectors face mounting pressure as China tightens its grip on global supply chains—and Germany's economic struggles offer a cautionary tale of what happens when Western nations grow dependent on Beijing's goodwill.

Germany's recent economic shock (National Review) underscores a hard truth: cozy trade relationships with communist regimes leave democracies vulnerable. China weaponizes commerce. Colorado ranchers, oil and gas operators, and manufacturers who rely on export markets should pay attention.

The Washington Examiner warns that even Republican administrations can fall into the trap of treating China as a legitimate peer rather than a strategic adversary with fundamentally hostile interests. That diplomatic softness translates into real costs for Colorado workers and businesses caught in retaliatory tariffs or supply-chain disruptions.

Conservatives argue the solution isn't isolation—it's strategic decoupling from sectors where China holds monopoly leverage, paired with policies that strengthen domestic energy independence and agricultural resilience. Colorado's energy sector, already battered by progressive regulation, cannot afford to be held hostage by Beijing's trade whims on top of Washington's green mandates.

Watch for how Colorado's congressional delegation responds to China policy and whether they prioritize protecting state industries over feel-good diplomatic theater.
