# Democrats Surge in Generic Ballot as 2026 Midterms Enter Final Sprint

Colorado Republicans face a tightening national environment as Democrats hit their strongest generic ballot position since 1996, needing just six House seats to reclaim the gavel (Fox News). With 100 days until the midterms, the party-in-power is mobilizing resources across competitive districts—a critical moment for Colorado's swing seats, where control of Congress could hinge on turnout and messaging discipline.

Meanwhile, Big Tech's political wallet tells its own story. OpenAI employees donated more than $296,000 to Democrats in recent filings while Republicans received nothing (Daily Caller), underscoring the Silicon Valley-to-progressive pipeline that conservatives have long criticized. That cash advantage matters in a close cycle.

On defense spending, cracks appeared in both parties' unity. Some Republicans—including Thomas Massie and Tim Burchett—voted against the NDAA, citing concerns over endless military commitments (Daily Caller), while some Democrats crossed party lines. The fractures suggest 2026 won't be a simple binary choice.

Looking ahead to 2028, Vice President JD Vance emerges as the likely Republican frontrunner (Just The News), while Democrats' field remains unsettled.
