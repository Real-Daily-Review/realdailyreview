# Colorado Springs Real Estate Crisis Deepens as City Faces Election Law Showdown

Colorado Springs' economic troubles are mounting: the state's second-largest city is watching its two tallest buildings head to foreclosure auction starting Oct. 21, according to El Paso County records cited by the *Colorado Sun*—a stark sign of commercial real estate distress in a region already grappling with sluggish growth and business uncertainty.

Meanwhile, the city faces legal pressure over its municipal election schedule. A lawsuit has been filed challenging Colorado Springs' plan to hold municipal elections in April, marking the first enforcement action under Colorado's 2025 voting law (per *Complete Colorado*). Republicans argue the state's election timing mandates are creating unnecessary compliance burdens on local governments already stretched thin.

The foreclosure crisis reflects broader concerns about Colorado's business climate. When major commercial anchors fail, it ripples through property tax bases, employment, and investor confidence—issues that hit taxpayers and job creators hardest.

On the political front, *Complete Colorado* reports that unaffiliated voter registration continues climbing statewide, leaving party-affiliated voters who skip caucuses with diminished influence in primary elections. Conservatives worry this trend fragments the electorate and hands disproportionate power to activist-dominated caucus participants.
