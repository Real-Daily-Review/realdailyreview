# Chinese Capital Infiltration of U.S. Tech Poses National Security Risk

Silicon Valley's casual tolerance of Chinese military-linked investment represents a backdoor threat to American national security that Washington has been slow to address.

The Pentagon's 2025 designation of Tencent—a major Chinese tech firm with deep stakes in American gaming and software companies—as a military-connected entity finally forced action. The Justice Department compelled Tencent to surrender board seats and director appointments at Epic Games, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Yet the muted response from tech leadership underscores how thoroughly Beijing has embedded itself in U.S. innovation infrastructure.

Republicans and national security hawks argue this exposure reflects years of regulatory negligence. While venture capital flows freely across borders, foreign adversaries exploit the gap between Silicon Valley's profit motive and America's strategic interests. The concern isn't academic: Chinese firms gain access to proprietary technology, user data, and intellectual property that strengthen Beijing's military and surveillance capabilities.

Colorado's growing tech sector and defense contractors—from aerospace to cybersecurity firms—face indirect exposure to this infiltration through supply chains and talent networks. State policymakers should scrutinize venture funding sources and foreign ownership stakes in Colorado tech ventures.

Conservatives should watch whether the Biden-Harris administration expands CFIUS (Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States) authority to close loopholes before the next administration takes office.
