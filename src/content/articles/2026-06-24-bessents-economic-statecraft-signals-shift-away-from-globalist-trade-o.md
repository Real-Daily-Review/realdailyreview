# Treasury Secretary Bessent Pivots to 'Economic Statecraft'—What It Means for Colorado

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is reframing American economic policy around "economic statecraft," signaling that Washington will deploy its financial leverage to protect national sovereignty rather than chase globalist trade orthodoxy (Breitbart). The doctrine marks a sharp break from decades of free-trade-at-all-costs consensus and aligns with the Trump administration's skepticism of arrangements that hollow out American manufacturing and energy independence.

For Colorado, this shift carries real implications. A sovereignty-first economic posture could mean stronger protections for domestic energy development—critical as the state balances oil and gas production with renewable expansion. It also suggests the administration may resist trade deals that undercut Colorado agriculture or manufacturing without reciprocal market access.

Bessent's framing—that national strength flows from *what we can build*—echoes conservative arguments that deindustrialization and energy dependence weaken America. Republicans have long argued that offshoring production to hostile nations while gutting domestic capacity is economic suicide. Democrats typically counter that free trade lowers consumer costs and that climate concerns should override energy self-sufficiency.

The real test: whether "economic statecraft" translates into policies that actually protect Colorado jobs in energy, agriculture, and manufacturing—or becomes rhetorical cover for selective corporate favors.
