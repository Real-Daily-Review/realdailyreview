## SCOTUS Takes Up Federal Prisoner Rights Lawsuit

The Supreme Court has agreed to hear *Nielsen v. Watanabe*, a case asking whether federal prisoners can sue prison officials directly for constitutional violations. The case has broad implications for the scope of federal civil liability — and conservatives will be watching closely. Republicans argue that expanding the right to sue federal officials opens the floodgates to litigation that bogs down government operations and rewards bad-faith claims. The outcome could set a precedent affecting how Colorado's federal facilities — and their staff — operate for years.

## Colorado's CD-3 Primary: Hurd vs. Hanks, One Week Out

Republican voters in Colorado's 3rd Congressional District head to the polls June 30 to choose between incumbent Rep. Jeff Hurd and challenger Ron Hanks. The race is one of the most consequential on Colorado's primary calendar — CD-3 covers the Western Slope, San Luis Valley, and rural communities that depend on agriculture, energy development, and Second Amendment protections. Conservatives in the district should know exactly where each candidate stands before Tuesday. If you haven't looked at the policy contrasts yet, now is the time.

## Biden Judge Blocked ICE Courthouse Arrests — Appeals Court Pushes Back

A Biden-appointed federal judge drew sharp criticism after blocking ICE arrests at immigration courts, but a federal appeals court has now revived the Trump administration's nationwide expedited removal policy, clearing the way for fast-track deportations of eligible illegal immigrants. Republicans argue that sanctuary-style judicial interference undermines federal law and rewards illegal entry. For Colorado — a state where Democrats have pushed sanctuary policies and limited local law enforcement cooperation with ICE — the appellate ruling is a meaningful check on the left's effort to nullify immigration enforcement from the bench.

## Trump Touts Gas Price Drop as Iran Conflict Winds Down

President Trump is pointing to a roughly 60-cent drop in gas prices following the end of hostilities with Iran, with traffic flowing again through the Strait of Hormuz. For Colorado energy workers, ranchers hauling livestock, and commuters on the Western Slope who drive long distances, fuel costs aren't an abstraction — they hit the bottom line directly. Conservatives say the price relief is a direct result of Trump's willingness to project strength abroad, a contrast to the energy price spikes that defined the Biden years. The question now: how durable is the drop?
