# Supreme Court Clears Trump's $400M White House Ballroom Project—But Questions Linger

The Supreme Court handed President Trump a narrow 5-4 victory Monday, allowing construction of the White House ballroom to resume despite warnings from a conservative justice that the project is "likely unlawful" (Fox News).

The decision replaces a temporary hold Chief Justice John Roberts had issued, letting work continue on the roughly two-thirds-complete East Wing renovation (Just The News). The National Trust for Historic Preservation had challenged the project, but the high court rejected their bid to halt it outright (New York Post).

Here's the rub: even as the court allowed construction to proceed, Roberts himself flagged serious legal concerns. A 5-4 split on the nation's highest bench—with a conservative justice expressing doubts—signals this fight isn't over. The project's estimated $400 million price tag raises legitimate questions about federal spending priorities and whether proper oversight occurred.

**For Colorado:** This case underscores how courts handle executive power and spending authority. As Washington expands federal budgets, Coloradans should care about whether checks on executive branch spending actually work.

**Watch next:** Whether lower courts revisit the "unlawful" question or if Congress demands accountability for the ballroom's final cost.
