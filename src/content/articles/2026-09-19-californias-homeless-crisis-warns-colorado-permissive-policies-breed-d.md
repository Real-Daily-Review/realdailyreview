# Colorado Should Learn From California's Homeless Policy Disaster

Progressive cities across the country are discovering that soft-on-encampment policies create public health crises, not solutions—and Colorado's own left-leaning municipalities are watching closely.

In California, Democratic-led cities are reaping what they've sown. Los Angeles city councilwoman Nithya Raman, who has opposed expanding anti-camping restrictions, now faces backlash from residents and police unions over unsafe conditions near schools and neighborhoods (New York Post). Meanwhile, Berkeley—the progressive vanguard—is locked in legal battles to remove disease-ridden encampments where homeless individuals have organized union representation, creating obstacles to basic sanitation and public safety (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** Compassion without accountability breeds chaos. When cities prioritize the "rights" of encampment residents over the safety of working families, children, and neighborhoods, they've abandoned their core duty. Raman's resistance to anti-camping rules reflects the broader Democratic playbook: expand homeless services without enforcing standards, then act shocked when disease and disorder follow.

Colorado's progressive strongholds—Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins—have embraced similar permissive policies. The result: visible encampments, crime, and deteriorating quality of life in once-livable neighborhoods. Taxpayers foot the bill for endless services while conditions worsen.

**Watch whether Colorado cities will learn from California's cautionary tale or double down on failed progressive homelessness ideology.**
