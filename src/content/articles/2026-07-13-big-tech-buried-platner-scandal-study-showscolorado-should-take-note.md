# Graham Platner's Collapse Exposes Big Tech's Political Bias

A Maine Democratic Senate candidate's implosion reveals how Silicon Valley giants systematically shield progressive politicians from scrutiny. Graham Platner's campaign imploded after years of negative coverage was largely suppressed by Apple News and Google News, according to a Media Research Center study cited by The Daily Wire. The two platforms, which collectively draw 215 million monthly hits, buried damaging stories while the candidate maintained a sanitized public image—until the facade cracked entirely.

Republicans argue this pattern demonstrates Big Tech's entrenched bias toward Democratic candidates and progressive causes. The suppression wasn't accidental; it reflects algorithmic choices made by companies claiming neutrality while operating as de facto Democratic operatives. National Review's coverage of Platner's downfall underscores a broader conservative concern: when the internet's gatekeepers decide which stories reach voters, elections cease to be fair contests of ideas.

For Colorado, this matters. As tech companies increasingly control information flow during election cycles, Colorado voters—particularly in competitive races—may never see the full picture about candidates or their records. The state's 2026 midterms could hinge on what Apple and Google decide Coloradans are allowed to know.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans demand transparency from Big Tech platforms about their news-curation algorithms before the next election cycle.
