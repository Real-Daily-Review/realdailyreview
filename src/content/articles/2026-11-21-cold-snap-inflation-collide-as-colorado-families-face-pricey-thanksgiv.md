# Colorado Families Face Perfect Storm: Brutal Cold, Inflation, and Holiday Sticker Shock

Denver is bracing for a sharp weather turn this weekend—temperatures dropping well below normal with the possibility of the season's first snow—just as Colorado families sit down to Thanksgiving dinners they'll pay significantly more for than last year.

The timing couldn't be worse. As *KDVR Fox31 Denver* reports, a major cold front is moving into the Front Range, bringing temperatures far below seasonal norms and potential early-season snow. Meanwhile, Colorado Sun cartoonist Drew Litton captured the mood bluntly: families are planning holiday gatherings "in the shadow of AI and prohibitive prices on everything."

The inflation squeeze is real. Turkeys, stuffing, and all the trimmings cost more—a direct result of years of government spending, Federal Reserve money-printing, and supply-chain disruptions that conservatives warned would follow the pandemic stimulus blitz. Democrats and their media allies spent 2021-2023 insisting inflation was "transitory"; Colorado families know better as they pay at the checkout line.

Republicans argue the Biden-Harris administration's reckless fiscal policy—combined with regulatory overreach on energy and agriculture—has made everyday life unaffordable for working Coloradans. The left counters that external factors drove prices up, but the math doesn't lie to families cutting corners on holiday meals.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans make inflation and cost-of-living relief central to 2026 messaging as voters remember this Thanksgiving's empty wallets.
