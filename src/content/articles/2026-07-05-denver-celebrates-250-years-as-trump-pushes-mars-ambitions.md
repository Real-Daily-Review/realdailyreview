# Denver Celebrates America's 250th—While Trump Pushes Space Ambitions

Denver's Fourth of July weekend is packed with festivities honoring America's 250th birthday, as the metro area rolls out events across the region (KDVR). Meanwhile, President Trump used his Independence Day address to spotlight NASA's Artemis II astronauts and reaffirm American space dominance. "We're going to go to Mars, and we're going to continue to be way ahead," Trump said, positioning the nation's space program as a cornerstone of American leadership (New York Post). Trump also displayed one of the country's earliest flags—dating to 1777—on stage during the celebration, underscoring the historical weight of the moment.

For Colorado conservatives, the space agenda matters. Federal investment in aerospace and space technology supports jobs across the state, from engineering firms in the Denver metro to defense contractors along the Front Range. A renewed commitment to Mars exploration and American space leadership aligns with Republican priorities: technological innovation, American exceptionalism, and beating China to the stars—without the bloated green-energy mandates Democrats typically attach to federal spending.

The contrast is sharp: Republicans frame space exploration as a driver of American strength and private-sector innovation; Democrats often use such programs as vehicles for climate spending and government expansion.

**Watch for:** whether Congress funds Artemis expansion without padding the bill with progressive climate riders or wasteful bureaucratic overhead.
