# Denver Mayor Pushes Housing Mandates While City Grapples With Affordability Crisis

Denver Mayor Mike Johnston is doubling down on government-managed solutions to the city's housing crunch, announcing plans for thousands of affordable housing units and new childcare licensing rules in his 2026 State of the City address. The administration is also rolling out new public dashboards tracking housing complaints and violations—another layer of regulatory oversight.

Here's the conservative concern: Denver's affordability problem stems largely from restrictive zoning, permitting delays, and rising costs imposed by city regulations themselves. Rather than loosening those constraints to let the free market build, Johnston's approach adds more government intervention—affordable housing mandates that shift costs to developers and taxpayers, plus expanded licensing bureaucracy that increases operational expenses for childcare providers.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that Denver should focus on reducing regulatory barriers, streamlining permits, and letting private developers respond to market demand. Mandates and new oversight layers only drive up costs and slow housing production.

The city's new housing complaint dashboards may improve transparency, but they're a band-aid on a wound created by government overreach. Watch whether Johnston's listening tour actually leads to deregulation—or just more spending and red tape.
