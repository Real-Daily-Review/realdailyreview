# Major Networks Refuse Trump Primetime Slot; GOP Demands FCC Review

Network gatekeeping of presidential speech time is raising serious questions about who controls the public airwaves—and whether Big Media answers to anyone.

Rep. Buddy Carter (R-GA) has asked FCC Chairman Brendan Carr to investigate whether ABC, NBC, and CBS violated their public interest obligations by refusing to air President Trump's primetime address (Fox News). The networks' decision to block the speech comes as Trump raised allegations about Chinese interference in the 2020 election, including claims that China compromised voter records—charges the Washington Examiner notes the mainstream media has largely ignored rather than investigated.

Republicans argue the networks' refusal exposes a double standard: these same broadcasters regularly air Democratic addresses and statements without hesitation. The question cuts to the heart of media bias and whether corporations holding broadcast licenses should face accountability when they suppress political speech from one side.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters. Local stations operating under FCC licenses answer to the same regulatory framework. If networks can arbitrarily block presidential addresses based on content they dislike, what other speech might be filtered? The precedent affects how information reaches Colorado voters during elections and major policy debates.

Watch whether the FCC actually investigates or whether regulatory capture leaves these networks unaccountable.
