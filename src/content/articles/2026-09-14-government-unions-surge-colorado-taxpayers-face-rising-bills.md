# Government Unions Surge in Colorado, Threatening Taxpayer Wallets

Colorado taxpayers face a mounting bill as state-level union-friendly legislation gains traction across the country, emboldening public-sector organizing that could drive up labor costs and reduce government efficiency.

According to *National Review*, new pro-union laws at the state level are fueling a resurgence in government union membership and bargaining power. The trend threatens to lock in higher compensation packages, pension obligations, and benefits that Colorado taxpayers ultimately fund through taxes and reduced services.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that government unions prioritize member benefits over public interest, creating bloated payrolls and inflexible workforces that resist accountability and performance-based management. They point to states with strong public-sector unions facing budget crises driven partly by unfunded pension liabilities—a warning sign for Colorado's own PERA system, already underfunded by billions.

Democrats counter that unions protect worker standards and prevent a race to the bottom in public employment, ensuring quality services and stable communities.

The Colorado angle is sharp: if state lawmakers follow the national trend toward union-friendly legislation, expect pressure on already-strained state budgets, potential tax hikes, and reduced flexibility in managing government workforce costs during economic downturns.

**Watch for:** Any Colorado legislative push to ease union organizing in state and local government—and the fiscal impact estimates that follow.
