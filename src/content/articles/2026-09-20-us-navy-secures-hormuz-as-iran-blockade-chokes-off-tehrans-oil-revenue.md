# U.S. Navy Secures 1 Billion Barrels Through Hormuz as Iran Blockade Holds Firm

American military strength is keeping global oil markets open while choking off Tehran's revenue—a stark reminder that peace through strength works.

U.S. Central Command announced that over one billion barrels of crude oil have transited the Strait of Hormuz in recent months under coordinated American naval protection, with CENTCOM chief Adm. Brad Cooper crediting U.S. forces for safeguarding more than 2,000 commercial ship passages (Just The News). Meanwhile, Iran has exported zero barrels under Washington's "ironclad blockade" (Breitbart), effectively neutering the regime's ability to fund proxy militias and nuclear ambitions.

The strategic win extends beyond the Persian Gulf. China has reportedly pressured Iran to rein in Houthi proxies in Yemen, who've repeatedly attacked shipping through the Bab el Mandeb Strait (Breitbart). That diplomatic leverage—backed by American naval dominance—signals that Beijing recognizes the cost of Iranian instability.

For Colorado energy workers and taxpayers, this matters: stable global oil supplies and constrained Iranian aggression reduce geopolitical risk premiums on energy prices. A weakened Iran means less funding for terror networks that threaten American interests abroad.

Watch whether the Biden-Harris administration maintains this naval posture or signals retreat—and whether Colorado's energy sector benefits from sustained market confidence.
