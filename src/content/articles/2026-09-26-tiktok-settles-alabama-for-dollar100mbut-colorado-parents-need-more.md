# TikTok Settles Alabama Suit—But Colorado Parents Still Face the Real Problem

TikTok has agreed to pay Alabama at least $100 million and implement new safety guardrails for minors, including two-hour daily limits, nighttime restrictions, and stronger age verification (9News Denver). Alabama sued the platform in 2025 over its contribution to negative mental health effects on young people (Just The News).

The settlement sounds good on paper. Conservatives have long warned that social media platforms designed to addict teenagers pose genuine risks to child development and parental authority. Stronger age checks and usage limits address real concerns about screen time and algorithmic manipulation targeting vulnerable minds.

But here's the catch: TikTok's track record of compliance is spotty, and enforcement across state lines remains murky. Colorado parents shouldn't assume this Alabama deal solves the problem in their homes. The platform's business model—engagement-driven content designed to keep kids scrolling—remains fundamentally unchanged. Parental controls only work if parents actually use them, and TikTok's incentive structure still rewards endless scrolling.

Republicans have rightly pushed back against Big Tech's unchecked influence on youth. This settlement is a step, but it's no substitute for parental vigilance and, frankly, skepticism about whether a Chinese-controlled app should have this much access to American teenagers at all.

Watch whether Colorado legislators move to strengthen state-level protections or whether they'll rely on corporate promises that have repeatedly failed.
