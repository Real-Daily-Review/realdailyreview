# Colorado Should Embrace AI Opportunity, Not Regulate It Into Oblivion

Colorado has a genuine chance to lead in artificial intelligence and data center development—but only if state lawmakers resist the urge to over-regulate a nascent industry.

The Colorado Sun's opinion section argues the state should channel AI growth into apprenticeships, positioning young workers in AI-enabled workplaces under "solid supervision" and "clear standards." That framing reflects the progressive instinct to manage emerging technology through government-designed programs.

Conservatives counter that Colorado's competitive advantage lies in *market-driven* adoption, not state-mandated training schemes. Complete Colorado notes that recent polling from Magellan Strategies shows Coloradans recognize genuine reasons to "ease off the artificial intelligence panic pedal"—suggesting voters are ready to move past alarmism and embrace the economic upside.

The stakes are real. Data centers and AI operations represent high-wage jobs, tax revenue, and energy demand that could benefit Colorado's economy and workforce. But they also require reliable, affordable power and minimal regulatory friction. If Denver and Boulder impose AI-specific rules, environmental restrictions, or labor mandates, companies will simply relocate to Texas, Arizona, or other states rolling out the welcome mat.

**Watch whether the legislature introduces AI-specific regulations or tax incentives in the 2027 session—that will signal whether Colorado is serious about competing or content to lecture from the sidelines.**
