# Secret Service in Crisis: Leaks, Threats, and Accountability Questions

A Secret Service agent has been pulled from Vice President Vance's detail over suspected leaks about internal frustrations with the VP's travel requests—the latest sign of dysfunction within an agency stretched dangerously thin (9News Denver).

The problems run deeper. A Secret Service official told *Just The News* that threats against protected officials have hit record levels, with approximately 10,000 threats logged so far in 2026—a 40% jump year-over-year. That's a staggering workload for an agency already struggling with internal discipline and morale.

Meanwhile, a bystander injured during a Secret Service shootout has filed a personal injury claim, with his attorney citing surveillance footage suggesting the agency itself fired the shot that hit his client. The lawsuit underscores a troubling pattern: the Secret Service is simultaneously overwhelmed by unprecedented threat levels and plagued by operational failures that put innocent Americans at risk.

**The Colorado angle:** As federal security threats surge, Colorado's congressional delegation and state officials hosting national events face heightened vulnerability—and questions about whether the Secret Service can actually protect them.

Conservatives should watch whether Congress demands accountability for the agency's leadership failures and whether taxpayers foot the bill for negligent security operations.
