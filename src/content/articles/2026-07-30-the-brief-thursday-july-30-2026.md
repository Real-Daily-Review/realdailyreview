# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, July 30, 2026

The Middle East is back on a war footing, Washington's confirmation machinery is grinding, and the culture war inside women's sports is getting uglier by the day — all of it with real consequences for Colorado.

## U.S. Strikes Iran Again After Jordan Missile Attack

After pausing strikes over the weekend to give peace negotiations a chance, U.S. Central Command resumed military action against Iran Wednesday evening, calling it "a powerful response to yesterday's attempted Iranian attack" following a missile strike on Jordan. President Trump had extended a diplomatic window; Iran answered with missiles. Republicans argue the administration is right to respond with force — weakness invites escalation. Colorado's military families, defense contractors along the Front Range, and energy markets all have a direct stake in how this conflict unfolds.

## Blanche AG Confirmation Hits a Speed Bump

The Senate Judiciary Committee postponed a key vote Wednesday that would have advanced Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche's nomination to permanently hold the position. No new date has been set. Conservatives say the delay is another example of Senate Democrats slow-walking a legitimate executive appointment to hamstring the Trump administration's law enforcement agenda. Colorado conservatives should watch whether this bottleneck affects DOJ priorities — including enforcement actions tied to the state's ongoing sanctuary policies and Second Amendment litigation.

## Fort Morgan Cargill Lockout Nears End

Nearly 70 days after Cargill locked out its 1,700 beef processing workers in Fort Morgan, the two sides have reached a tentative agreement. The workers have been off the floor since May 20 after contract negotiations stalled. For rural Colorado, this is straightforward economic news — Fort Morgan's meatpacking operation is a backbone employer for Morgan County and the surrounding agricultural economy. A resolution that gets workers back on the line without crippling the plant's competitiveness is the right outcome for the region.

The Seattle Storm's culture-war offensive is escalating. Conservatives say this is exactly what happens when a professional sports league decides activism trumps basic decency toward fans. Colorado parents should take note of what the WNBA now considers acceptable behavior toward young women who hold a mainstream biological position.

## Trump Admin Cuts Biden's Medicare Prescription Subsidy

The Trump administration announced it will end a Biden-era subsidy program designed to artificially suppress prescription plan costs under Medicare. The left claims the move will hurt seniors; conservatives and Republicans counter that the Biden subsidy was fiscally unsustainable — a budget gimmick that masked skyrocketing projected costs rather than fixing the underlying problem. Colorado has a large and growing senior population, and honest Medicare accounting matters more to their long-term security than a short-term subsidy that was never built to last.
