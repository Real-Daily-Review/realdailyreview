# Colorado Voters to Decide 'Right to Natural Gas' Constitutional Amendment

Colorado taxpayers and energy workers will get a direct say this November on whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state Constitution—a rare pushback against the left's relentless campaign to phase out fossil fuels.

Initiative 177, brought forward by Advance Colorado, qualified for the ballot, according to the Colorado Secretary of State's office (Denver Post). The measure would constitutionally protect access to natural gas, a critical energy source for Colorado's economy, households, and industrial base.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Colorado's natural gas industry supports thousands of jobs and generates substantial tax revenue. Progressives have pursued regulatory strangulation of fossil fuels through state rules, utility mandates, and building codes designed to eliminate natural gas hookups in new construction. Republicans and energy advocates argue voters deserve a voice in whether government can simply ban or restrict a fuel that heats homes, powers businesses, and fuels Colorado's economy.

Democrats and environmental groups oppose the measure, viewing it as an obstacle to their climate agenda and transition to renewable energy.

The amendment reflects growing frustration among Colorado conservatives and working families over one-party Democratic control of state government and its hostility toward the energy sector that built Colorado's prosperity.
