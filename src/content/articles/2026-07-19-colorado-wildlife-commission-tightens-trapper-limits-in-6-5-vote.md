# Colorado Wildlife Commission Restricts Trappers in Narrow 6-5 Vote

Colorado's wildlife commission just handed rural trappers and hunters a loss they didn't need. In a razor-thin 6-5 decision, the commission imposed new daily bag limits—capping trappers at two animals per person for 17 species—while rejecting an outright ban on fur sales (Colorado Sun).

The vote exposes the commission's drift toward urban environmentalist priorities over rural livelihoods. Trappers argue these restrictions threaten a time-honored practice and economic activity in counties where wildlife management funds conservation. The commission rejected the fur-sales ban, a small win, but the daily limits still tighten the noose on an already-pressured industry.

This follows Colorado's broader pattern: state bureaucracies increasingly reflect Denver's progressive base rather than rural Colorado's interests. Wildlife management should balance conservation with sustainable use—not treat trapping as a relic to be phased out.

Meanwhile, Complete Colorado reports the state health department faces a separate reckoning over Medicaid spending spiraling without structural reform (Complete Colorado). Bureaucracies respond to incentives, and HCPF won't improve without real accountability.
