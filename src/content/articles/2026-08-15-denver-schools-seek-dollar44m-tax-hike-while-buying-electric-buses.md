# Denver Schools Push $44M Tax Hike as Spending Priorities Raise Questions

Denver Public Schools is asking voters to approve a $44 million mill levy override in November—a direct tax increase on property owners—with $25.8 million earmarked for employee raises, according to the *Denver Post*. Meanwhile, DPS is simultaneously rolling out 25 new electric school buses ahead of the school year, *KDVR Fox31 Denver* reports.

The timing raises a conservative concern: why are Colorado taxpayers funding fleet electrification while the district claims it needs a tax hike for basic operations? Republicans argue that school districts should prioritize classroom instruction and fiscal discipline before asking voters for more money. The mill levy override—a permanent increase in property taxes—shifts the burden to homeowners and businesses already facing inflation and rising housing costs.

DPS officials frame the override as necessary for competitive teacher compensation. But conservatives counter that government agencies routinely spend on capital projects and green initiatives while pleading poverty on payroll, suggesting misaligned priorities rather than genuine budget shortfalls.

Colorado taxpayers should scrutinize DPS's full budget before November to determine whether the override reflects real need or bureaucratic mission creep.
