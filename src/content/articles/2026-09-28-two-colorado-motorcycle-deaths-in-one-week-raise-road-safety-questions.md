# Motorcyclist Deaths Highlight Colorado Road Safety Concerns

Two separate motorcycle fatalities in Colorado this week underscore persistent dangers on state roadways. A motorcyclist died Friday in a three-vehicle crash near Guanella Pass on Highway 285 and County Road 62, according to Colorado State Patrol (KDVR Fox31). Days later, another motorcyclist was killed Sunday at the intersection of Speer Boulevard and Wewatta Street in Denver (9News).

While accident investigations remain ongoing, conservatives and safety advocates have long argued that Colorado's road infrastructure requires sustained investment in maintenance and safety upgrades—not through wasteful government programs, but via efficient allocation of existing transportation budgets and user fees. Republicans consistently push back against diverting fuel-tax revenue to unrelated progressive priorities, insisting those dollars stay dedicated to road repair and safety.

Colorado's mountainous terrain and increasing traffic volumes compound risks for all motorists, particularly those on two wheels. Guanella Pass, a popular scenic route, has seen recurring safety concerns that demand attention from state transportation officials.

Taxpayers should monitor whether state lawmakers prioritize road safety funding or continue redirecting transportation revenue to pet projects unrelated to actual infrastructure needs.
