# FCC Under Trump Veers Toward Government Control of Airwaves, Alarming Conservatives

Federal Communications Commission Chairman Brendan Carr is steering the agency toward the kind of broadcast market interventionism that would make FDR blush—and it's drawing fire from Republicans who expected better from a Trump appointee.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, Carr's latest moves echo Franklin Roosevelt's heavy-handed approach to controlling the airwaves rather than Ronald Reagan's free-market philosophy. The concern is sharp: government picking winners and losers in media markets, using regulatory power as a cudgel rather than a guardrail.

Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) captured the unease bluntly: "Sometimes the FCC scares me right now."

For Colorado conservatives, this matters. Media consolidation and regulatory overreach threaten local broadcasters and the free speech ecosystem that independent outlets depend on. When federal bureaucrats start weaponizing licensing and market rules, they're not protecting competition—they're rigging it.

The irony stings: conservatives backed Trump partly to *reduce* regulatory state power, not to watch it reshape itself under new management. Carr's interventionism suggests the FCC remains a tool waiting for the next administration to wield it differently.

Watch whether Republican senators—particularly those who've already expressed alarm—demand Carr justify his market interventions or face confirmation headwinds.
