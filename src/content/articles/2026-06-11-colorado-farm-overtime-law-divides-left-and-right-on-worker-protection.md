# Colorado Farm Labor Law Sparks Conservative-Progressive Clash Over Worker Protections

Colorado's new overtime threshold for farm workers is reigniting the left-right divide over labor regulation and agricultural economics. The Colorado Sun argues the law represents a "step backward for justice," claiming legislators are abandoning farm workers after praising their contributions. Progressive advocates contend stricter overtime rules protect vulnerable workers from exploitation.

Republicans and agricultural industry groups counter that higher overtime mandates increase labor costs for Colorado farmers already squeezed by input prices, water scarcity, and market pressures. Conservatives argue that rigid wage floors, while well-intentioned, can reduce hiring and push operations toward automation—ultimately harming the workers the law claims to help. They point to similar policies in California that have accelerated farm consolidation and eliminated entry-level positions.

The stakes matter for Colorado's agricultural economy. The state's farming sector depends on seasonal labor, and cost-prohibitive regulations risk making Colorado farming less competitive against out-of-state producers with lower compliance burdens. Ranchers and crop operators worry the threshold will force difficult choices: cut hours, reduce headcount, or exit the business entirely.
