# Barney Frank, Pioneering Congressman and Financial Reformer, Dies at 86

Barney Frank, a longtime Democratic congressman who became one of the first openly gay members of Congress and architect of major post-2008 financial reforms, has died at 86, according to PBS NewsHour and the BBC.

Frank spent decades in the House representing Massachusetts, earning a reputation for acerbic wit and combative advocacy for marginalized communities. He brought unprecedented visibility to gay rights during his tenure and, according to the Financial Times, led landmark banking reforms in the wake of the financial crisis—legislation that represented the most significant overhaul of the financial system in a generation.

The congressman spent his final weeks in hospice at his home in Maine before his death, the BBC reported.

Frank's career spanned multiple decades of Democratic politics and social progress, combining legislative work on economic regulation with pioneering representation as an openly gay elected official at a time when few held such positions publicly.
