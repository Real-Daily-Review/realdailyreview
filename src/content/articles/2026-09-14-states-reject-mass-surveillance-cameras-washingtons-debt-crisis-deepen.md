# Brief: ALPR Surveillance Cameras Face Bipartisan Pushback Across States

Across the country, states are pumping the brakes on automatic license plate reader (ALPR) networks—the surveillance infrastructure that tracks every vehicle movement—and Colorado should take note.

According to the Daily Caller, both Republicans and Democrats are increasingly skeptical of mass ALPR deployment, citing privacy concerns and government overreach. For conservatives, the issue cuts to core principles: warrantless tracking of citizens' movements violates Fourth Amendment protections and expands the surveillance state without meaningful oversight or warrant requirements.

The pushback reflects growing recognition that these camera networks create permanent records of where Americans drive—data that can be weaponized by government agencies or breached by bad actors. Colorado taxpayers should question whether their state is funding similar infrastructure and what safeguards exist to prevent abuse.

Meanwhile, Washington's fiscal crisis deepens. The Washington Examiner reports that Treasury yields have hit 19-year highs as the federal government's borrowing binge strains bond markets, squeezing Americans already facing rising living costs. Republicans argue Washington must embrace fiscal responsibility—cutting wasteful spending and eliminating regulatory bloat—rather than printing money and raising taxes on working families.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado lawmakers move to restrict ALPR data collection and retention, and whether state budget hawks push back against unfunded federal mandates that drain local resources.
