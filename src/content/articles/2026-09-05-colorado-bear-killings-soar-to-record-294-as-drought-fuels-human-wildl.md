# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push hungry wildlife into residential areas at unprecedented rates, forcing state officials into a grim choice between public safety and animal preservation.

The surge reflects a hard reality: when natural habitat shrinks and food sources vanish, bears don't relocate voluntarily. They raid garbage, break into homes, and threaten families. Colorado taxpayers and homeowners are bearing the cost of what amounts to a managed crisis.

State wildlife managers face mounting pressure. Conservatives argue that robust forest management—thinning overgrown timber, reducing catastrophic wildfire risk, and maintaining healthy ecosystems—could prevent these encounters before they turn lethal. Better land stewardship upstream saves lives downstream, both human and animal.

Democrats and environmental groups often resist aggressive timber harvesting, citing habitat concerns. Yet the current outcome—record bear deaths amid drought-stricken forests—hardly vindicates a hands-off approach.

The real question: Will Colorado's leadership prioritize preventive forest management and water conservation to restore ecological balance, or continue managing the symptoms of mismanagement with euthanasia protocols?

Watch whether state lawmakers push for expanded forest thinning and drought-resilience funding in the next budget cycle.
