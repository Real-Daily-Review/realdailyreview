# Xcel Energy Wins Rate Hike Despite PUC Pushback—Colorado Families to Pay More

Colorado's largest utility will squeeze ratepayers for an extra $5 monthly starting Aug. 29, after state regulators trimmed Xcel Energy's initial ask but ultimately approved the increase anyway. The Public Utilities Commission cut the company's original proposal roughly in half following months of negotiations, according to CPR News and the Colorado Sun, but the hike still lands on Colorado families and small businesses already facing inflation pressures.

Republicans and conservative taxpayer advocates have long warned that Colorado's regulatory environment—dominated by pro-green-energy mandates and grid modernization costs—inevitably drives up utility bills. Xcel's request reflected billions in infrastructure spending tied to the state's aggressive renewable energy targets, a reality the PUC acknowledged but couldn't escape.

The utility hasn't ruled out requesting a rehearing, the Colorado Sun reports, meaning this fight may not be over. For Colorado households already managing higher property taxes and cost-of-living increases, the $60 annual bill bump adds insult to injury—especially as the state pursues energy policies that prioritize environmental mandates over affordability.

Watch whether Xcel pursues that rehearing and whether Colorado lawmakers respond with rate-relief measures or double down on green-energy spending that keeps pushing utility costs skyward.
