# Pentagon Data Breach Exposes 2.76 Million Service Members' Personal Information

The Pentagon's catastrophic mishandling of sensitive data has exposed Social Security numbers and personal information belonging to 2.76 million active and former U.S. military and civilian personnel, according to a Pentagon official cited by *Just The News* and *Fox News*. The breach also compromised records of roughly 294,000 deceased individuals.

This is precisely what happens when massive government databases lack adequate cybersecurity safeguards—a warning sign for every American whose personal data sits in federal systems. The Pentagon, entrusted with defending the nation, cannot even protect the identities of those who serve it.

Colorado military families and retirees should take immediate steps to monitor their credit and consider identity theft protection. Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, and Schriever Space Force Base all house thousands of Colorado-based personnel whose data may be at risk.

Republicans and defense hawks argue this breach underscores the urgent need for Pentagon accountability and modernized cybersecurity infrastructure—not more bloated spending on legacy systems. The Trump administration is simultaneously launching a merit-based competition to select a new military base location, with winning states evaluated on land availability, housing, schools, community support, and regulatory cooperation (*Fox News*).

Conservatives should watch whether the Pentagon faces real consequences for this failure and whether base-selection criteria favor states with business-friendly, low-regulation environments.
