# Jefferson County Schools Board Eyes Tax Hike as Enrollment Craters and Voter Trust Erodes

Jefferson County school officials are pushing for a tax increase to plug budget holes, but Colorado taxpayers are right to be skeptical—declining enrollment means the district is asking for more money while serving fewer students.

According to *Complete Colorado*, board members claim the existing budget can't address district issues because of shrinking enrollment. That's the problem in a nutshell: instead of right-sizing operations to match their shrinking student population, JeffCo leadership wants to raid taxpayer wallets.

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** When a school district loses students, it should cut costs proportionally. Asking voters to approve a tax hike while enrollment declines signals fiscal mismanagement, not necessity. Colorado families are already burdened by state and local taxes; JeffCo needs to prove it can spend existing dollars wisely before asking for more.

The board's pitch also arrives amid broader voter distrust of public education institutions—a sentiment that extends well beyond Jefferson County. Parents across Colorado are increasingly skeptical of how schools spend money and what they teach.

**Watch whether JeffCo's tax measure even makes it to the ballot, and if so, whether Colorado voters reject it outright.**
