# Kansas City Dismantles 45-Year DEI Contracting Program—A Warning for Colorado Cities

Kansas City's unanimous vote to kill its Minority and Women Business Enterprise program signals that even deep-blue cities are abandoning indefensible race-based contracting schemes—and Colorado municipalities should take note.

The program, which had run for 45 years, was so legally vulnerable that the city's own consultant told officials they couldn't defend it in court. Yet Kansas City kept it running anyway for two additional months before the city council voted 12-0 on Aug. 13 to scrap it entirely, replacing it with three tiers of race-neutral small business certification (Washington Examiner).

The lesson here is stark: government contracting programs built on racial preferences, not merit or competitive bidding, crumble under legal scrutiny. Colorado cities and counties—particularly Denver, which has pursued aggressive DEI initiatives in procurement—should recognize that these programs expose municipalities to costly litigation and ultimately fail to survive judicial review.

Conservatives argue that race-based contracting violates equal protection principles and diverts taxpayer dollars from the most qualified bidders. The left claims such programs remedy historical discrimination, but Kansas City's experience proves the legal and political case for these schemes is fundamentally weak.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic-controlled cities follow Kansas City's lead or double down on DEI contracting before federal courts force their hand.
