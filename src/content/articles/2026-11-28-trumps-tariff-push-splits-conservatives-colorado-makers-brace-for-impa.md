# Trump's Tariff Gamble Splits Right; Colorado Manufacturers Face Uncertainty

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire from his own side, with conservative outlets warning that protectionist economics could backfire on American workers and businesses—including Colorado manufacturers already navigating supply-chain headwinds.

The Washington Examiner's opinion section notes Trump's approval ratings "began to slide in April 2025" following his "Liberation Day" tariff announcement, calling the justifications "absurd" and the rollout "chaotic." National Review argues the administration should adopt a Reagan-era approach to major policy shifts: build credibility through strength before pursuing ambitious goals.

Colorado's manufacturing sector—from aerospace suppliers to equipment makers—depends on efficient supply chains and predictable trade policy. Tariffs risk raising input costs, potentially forcing price hikes that hurt competitiveness against foreign competitors and squeeze profit margins already thin in competitive markets.

Republicans remain divided on Trump's trade approach. Some argue tariffs protect American workers and leverage against China; others contend they're inflationary and economically counterproductive. Conservatives emphasize that durable policy requires clearer messaging and broader buy-in than Trump's rollout achieved.
