# Hawaii Faces Back-to-Back Storms as Tropical Storm Moke Threatens Renewed Flooding

Hawaii is bracing for a one-two punch of tropical weather that will test emergency response systems already stretched thin by Hurricane Lala's devastation. Tropical Storm Moke is advancing toward the Big Island with forecasters warning of more than a foot of rain in some areas, compounding flooding and landslide risks just as cleanup operations from Lala are underway (9News Denver, Just The News).

The timing is brutal: residents and recovery crews have little time to clear debris, restore power, or stabilize saturated terrain before the next system arrives. Heavy rainfall and strong gusts will hammer already-compromised infrastructure and communities still assessing damage from the previous storm.

For Colorado, this serves as a stark reminder of why disaster preparedness and resilient infrastructure matter—and why federal disaster spending must be targeted, efficient, and free from wasteful bureaucracy. When Americans face genuine emergencies, government's job is to get out of the way and let local responders and private relief efforts work. Too often, federal disaster aid becomes a vehicle for unrelated spending and regulatory expansion.

Conservatives should watch whether Hawaii's recovery becomes another test case for bloated federal disaster packages laden with pork, or whether lawmakers prioritize direct aid to affected residents and businesses.
