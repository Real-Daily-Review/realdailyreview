# Colorado's Real Energy Problem Isn't Data Centers—It's Regulatory Gridlock

Colorado's electricity costs are climbing, and politicians are busy blaming artificial intelligence data centers instead of fixing the actual culprit: regulatory red tape strangling energy development.

Towns across the country are freezing data center projects. Governors are piling on conditions. Senate candidates promise moratoriums. Meanwhile, according to the *Washington Examiner*, almost no one is addressing the permitting and regulatory barriers that drive up power bills in the first place.

The conservative argument is straightforward: Colorado's energy crisis stems from overregulation of power generation and transmission, not from the companies trying to build modern infrastructure. When state and local governments impose endless conditions, environmental reviews, and permitting delays on new generation capacity—whether natural gas, nuclear, or hydro—they artificially constrain supply. Basic economics: constrained supply plus steady demand equals higher prices for Colorado families and businesses.

Blaming data centers is politically convenient. Fixing the regulatory maze that prevents affordable energy development requires gutting the permitting state that environmental groups and progressive bureaucrats have spent decades constructing.

Colorado taxpayers and energy-dependent industries should watch whether state legislators will actually streamline permitting for new generation capacity, or whether they'll continue scapegoating tech companies while letting regulatory costs keep climbing.
