# Colorado Voters Reject Judges, Approve Tax Hikes in Blue Wave Election

Colorado's judicial retention system failed spectacularly this week, with voters ousting three judges and forcing a fourth to resign rather than face likely defeat (Complete Colorado). But that was the only conservative win on election night—the state approved multiple tax hikes and moved further left across the board.

According to Complete Colorado's post-election analysis, Colorado voters approved the "Big Three" tax increases despite warnings from fiscal conservatives that new revenue does not substitute for difficult choices, meaningful oversight, or fiscal restraint. The tax hikes will burden Colorado businesses and workers already facing economic headwinds.

The election also exposed Colorado's broken voting rules. Complete Colorado criticized the state's election framework as "substantially undemocratic and extremely stupid," particularly regarding the governor's race, where arcane ballot access rules created a farce rather than a genuine contest.

One bright spot: Initiative 362 added new voter authentication requirements for Colorado's all-mail voting system, tightening ballot security—a win for election integrity advocates who have long warned that mail-in voting without proper ID verification invites fraud and undermines confidence in results.

Colorado woke up "poorer, bluer and still wondering who ordered another round," as Complete Colorado put it. Conservatives should watch whether the new tax revenue actually funds promised services or simply feeds government bloat.
