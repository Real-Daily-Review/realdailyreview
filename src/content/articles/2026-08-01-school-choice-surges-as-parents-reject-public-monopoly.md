# School Choice Gains Traction as Parents Reject One-Size-Fits-All Public Education

North Carolina's near-universal eligibility for Trump's school choice initiative signals what Colorado parents increasingly understand: government monopolies on education don't serve families—they serve bureaucrats.

Nearly 93% of North Carolina's 1.7 million students qualify for the program, *Just The News* reports, reflecting overwhelming parental demand for alternatives to traditional public schools. Democrats, including former Gov. Roy Cooper (now a U.S. Senate candidate), have consistently opposed school choice expansion, arguing it diverts resources from public systems.

Republicans counter that school choice empowers parents rather than politicians, allowing families to select education models matching their children's needs and values—whether traditional public, charter, private, or homeschool options.

The economic case strengthens the argument. The *Washington Examiner* notes back-to-school supply costs have surged as much as 42% this year, straining family budgets already hammered by inflation. School choice advocates argue competition forces institutions to operate efficiently and responsibly, rather than absorbing costs through tax increases.

Colorado has limited education savings accounts and charter school options, but remains far behind states embracing robust choice. As inflation continues squeezing Colorado families and public school enrollment declines, expect renewed pressure on state lawmakers to expand parental control over education dollars—and fierce resistance from teachers' unions protecting their turf.
