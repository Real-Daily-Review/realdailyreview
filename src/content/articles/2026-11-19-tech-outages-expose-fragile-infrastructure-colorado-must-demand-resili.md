# Tech Outages Spark Prepping Frenzy—Colorado Should Demand Infrastructure Resilience

Recent cascading tech failures—from the FAA nationwide ground stop to banking and airline meltdowns—have exposed how fragile America's digital backbone really is, and Coloradans are right to take notice.

The outages, blamed on a malfunctioning router during routine maintenance (9News Denver), have triggered a wave of doomsday prepping across the country (Breitbart). President Trump told Americans to "relax" and assured the public no malicious cyberattack was coordinated against the U.S. (The Daily Wire), but that messaging rings hollow when critical infrastructure fails this spectacularly.

**The conservative case:** These failures underscore why government and private industry must prioritize redundancy, decentralization, and resilience over flashy AI expansion. We're building fragile, centralized systems dependent on single points of failure—exactly what limited-government advocates warned against. Colorado's energy grid, water systems, and aviation hubs depend on the same vulnerable infrastructure. Rather than pouring billions into unproven AI projects, policymakers should demand hardened, distributed systems with real failsafes.

The left's response—more regulation and government oversight of tech—will only add bureaucratic bloat without fixing root causes. Market competition and private-sector accountability work better.
