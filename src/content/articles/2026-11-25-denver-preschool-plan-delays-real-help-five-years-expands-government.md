# Denver's Preschool Expansion Plan: Another Government Program Chasing a Five-Year Promise

Mayor Mike Johnston's administration is launching yet another childcare initiative, this time through the Denver Preschool Program, promising expanded care for children under five—but not anytime soon. According to the Denver Post, the new program operates separately from Johnston's broader "Childcare for All" pledge and won't actually begin delivering services for the next five years.

This is classic government overreach dressed up as compassion. Conservatives argue that subsidized childcare programs expand the state's footprint into family decisions, drive up taxes on working Coloradans, and create dependency on public services. Rather than funding more government programs, taxpayers would benefit from lower taxes, deregulation of childcare licensing, and policies that let families and the private market determine care solutions.

Denver voters should ask hard questions: What's the total cost? How will it be funded—property taxes, sales taxes, or both? Why does a five-year rollout inspire confidence? And why should government, not parents and private providers, drive childcare policy?

The real test comes when Johnston's administration reveals the price tag and funding mechanism. Coloradans deserve transparency on how much this experiment will cost and whether it actually delivers results or simply grows another bureaucracy.
