# Aurora Rejects Data Center Moratorium, Opts for Targeted Rules Instead

Aurora City Council rejected a full moratorium on data center development Monday, choosing instead to impose new operational restrictions that could still hamper Colorado's tech economy.

The council approved regulations prohibiting evaporative cooling systems and requiring buffer zones around residential areas (CPR News). Councilmember Amy Wiles had pushed for a six-month pause to study water use, zoning, noise, and facility size limits—a classic progressive delay tactic that would have frozen investment and job creation while bureaucrats deliberated.

**The conservative case:** Data centers represent high-value, job-creating infrastructure for Colorado's economy. A full moratorium would have been economically destructive. However, the approved restrictions still impose compliance costs and regulatory friction that could discourage future development. Republicans and business advocates argue that targeted environmental safeguards—like water management—should be negotiated with developers rather than mandated through one-size-fits-all rules that ignore site-specific conditions.

**The left's concern:** Progressive council members worry data centers consume scarce water resources in a drought-prone state, threatening residential supply and environmental sustainability.

The vote was close, signaling deep community division over growth versus conservation—a tension defining Colorado's future. Watch whether these new rules actually get enforced fairly or become a backdoor moratorium through regulatory burden.
