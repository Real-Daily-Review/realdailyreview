# US Indicts Raúl Castro for 1996 Plane Shootdowns; Cuba, Mexico Protest

The U.S. Department of Justice has indicted former Cuban leader Raúl Castro for murder and other crimes related to the 1996 downing of two civilian aircraft, according to BBC World. The charges center on his alleged role in the incident, which killed passengers aboard the planes.

The indictment has triggered sharp pushback from Cuba and regional allies. Thousands of Cubans rallied outside the U.S. embassy in Havana to protest the charges and show support for Castro, according to Al Jazeera English. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum also objected to the prosecution, arguing the timing was inappropriate given the incident occurred three decades ago, the Washington Examiner reported.

The case underscores lingering tensions between Washington and Havana over Cold War-era incidents. Cuban-Americans hold divided views on the indictment, according to BBC reporting, reflecting broader disagreements over how to address historical grievances between the two nations.
