# Democrats' Dark Money Pledge for 2028 Debates Rings Hollow Without Real Enforcement

The Democratic National Committee is demanding that 2028 primary candidates denounce dark money and super PAC funding to qualify for debate stages—a transparently political move that ignores Democrats' own reliance on unlimited outside spending.

The DNC's "Fair Campaign Practices Pledge" requires candidates to renounce dark money from 501(c)(4) groups and super PACs across 12 planned primary debates (Fox News, Daily Wire). Republicans argue the pledge is performative theater: Democrats have benefited enormously from the same dark money channels they now claim to oppose, and the pledge contains no enforcement mechanism or penalty for violations.

**Republicans counter** that if Democrats were serious about campaign finance reform, they would support legislation—not symbolic pledges that bind only those naive enough to sign. The real issue, conservatives say, is that the left wants to restrict *conservative* dark money while preserving their own network of progressive mega-donors and union-backed groups.

For Colorado Democrats eyeing 2028, this pledge could create awkward moments if they've accepted or plan to accept outside spending from national progressive groups—a common practice in competitive Western states.

Watch whether any major Democratic candidate actually refuses dark money support, or whether the pledge becomes a forgotten formality once the primary heats up.
