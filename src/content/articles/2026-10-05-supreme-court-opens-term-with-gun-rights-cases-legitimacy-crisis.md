# Supreme Court Opens Term Amid Legitimacy Crisis—But Major Wins Loom for Gun Rights

The Supreme Court begins its new term facing record-low public approval, yet the docket includes cases that could deliver major victories for Second Amendment advocates and religious liberty defenders—exactly the kind of rulings that matter most to Colorado conservatives.

The Court will hear cases on gun rights, voter citizenship requirements, and religious liberty protections (Fox News Politics). These are precisely the constitutional battles conservatives have fought for years. But the Court's credibility problem is real: justices are publicly feuding with one another, undermining public confidence in the institution itself, according to National Review. The mudslinging between chambers damages the bench's legitimacy when it needs it most.

For Colorado specifically, gun rights cases carry outsized weight. The state has pushed increasingly restrictive firearms regulations in recent years—magazine limits, red-flag laws, and ammunition taxes—all of which conservatives argue violate Second Amendment protections. A strong Supreme Court ruling could provide legal cover to challenge Colorado's overreach.

Republicans and conservatives argue the Court must focus on constitutional interpretation, not politics. Democrats, meanwhile, have spent years attacking the Court's legitimacy after the Dobbs decision, framing judicial independence as "illegitimate" when rulings don't align with progressive preferences.

**Watch whether the Court's internal divisions—and public perception—affect how boldly justices rule on the Colorado-relevant cases ahead.**
