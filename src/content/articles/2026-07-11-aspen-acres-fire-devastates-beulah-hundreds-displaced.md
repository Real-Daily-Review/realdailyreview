# Aspen Acres Fire Devastates Beulah; Residents Face Long Recovery

The Aspen Acres fire has left the small community of Beulah in ruins, destroying homes and displacing hundreds of residents in one of Colorado's latest natural disasters. According to CPR News and KDVR Fox31 Denver, the blaze tore through the town of roughly 800 people, leaving some homes burned to ashes while others stood eerily untouched—a pattern that complicates both recovery and rebuilding efforts.

The human toll extends beyond structures. The Humane Society of the Pikes Peak Region is working to reunite displaced pets found as strays near the fire zone (KDVR). Wildlife crews also relocated a juvenile bear that wandered into Pueblo during the chaos, underscoring how natural disasters disrupt entire ecosystems.

For Colorado conservatives, the Aspen Acres fire raises critical questions about forest management, land stewardship, and whether state and federal policies have adequately addressed wildfire prevention. Years of regulatory constraints on timber harvesting and controlled burns have left Colorado's forests dangerously overgrown—a reality that fuels catastrophic blazes like this one.

Beulah residents showed resilience in fighting back, but prevention should have come first. Watch for how state lawmakers respond: will they prioritize aggressive forest management and property-rights-friendly recovery policies, or double down on regulations that hamper landowners' ability to defend their own land?
