# Colorado's Energy Future Hinges on Nuclear Power, Not Green Mandates

America's 250-year energy story proves one thing: reliable, affordable power wins. Yet Colorado's progressive policymakers keep doubling down on wind and solar mandates while ignoring nuclear's proven track record.

The Daily Caller argues that nuclear energy—not ideological renewable quotas—built American prosperity and can sustain it (Daily Caller). Conservatives point out that Colorado's aggressive clean energy standards, pushed by Democrats and environmental groups, artificially favor intermittent renewables over dispatchable nuclear baseload power. This drives up electricity costs for families and small businesses while threatening grid reliability during peak demand.

National Review warns that today's young socialists pushing anti-growth energy policies understand economics no better than their predecessors (National Review). The result: Colorado risks becoming a cautionary tale of well-intentioned climate activism that prices out working families and kills good-paying energy jobs.

Republicans argue Colorado should embrace an all-of-the-above energy strategy that includes nuclear expansion, responsible natural gas, and yes, renewables—driven by markets, not mandates. Democrats counter that aggressive decarbonization is necessary to meet climate goals, even if it costs more upfront.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's next legislature will reconsider nuclear as a baseload solution or continue sacrificing affordability on the altar of progressive energy ideology.
