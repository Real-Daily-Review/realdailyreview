# Aspen Acres Fire Scorches Nearly 94,000 Acres; Volunteers Lose Homes While Fighting Blaze

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire has consumed nearly 94,000 acres across Pueblo County, destroying homes and testing the resolve of volunteer firefighters who continued battling the blaze even after learning their own properties had burned (9News Denver). A Red Creek volunteer fire chief and his firefighter wife lost their home while he stayed behind to help neighbors, exemplifying the sacrifice rural Colorado communities depend on when state and federal wildfire resources stretch thin (9News Denver). Pueblo County resident Ned "Doc" Pittman watched his home burn during a medical appointment on June 29 (KDVR Fox31 Denver). Beulah's volunteer firefighters prevented the fire from consuming their town despite the personal devastation (9News Denver).

Forecasters offered a glimmer of hope: scattered showers could arrive Wednesday, though hot, dry, and windy conditions are expected to return by weekend (Colorado Sun). The fire underscores a hard reality for rural Colorado: when catastrophic wildfires strike, volunteer departments—chronically underfunded and understaffed—become the frontline defense. State and federal land management policies, including decades of fire suppression and inadequate forest thinning, have created tinderbox conditions across the West. Conservatives should watch whether state officials acknowledge these management failures or simply demand more federal spending without addressing root causes.
