# Noncitizens on Voter Rolls: DOJ Escalates as States Resist Cleanup

The Department of Justice is escalating its fight against lax voter roll maintenance, subpoenaing California election officials who have refused to remove up to 20,000 suspected noncitizens from the rolls (Washington Examiner). Election expert J. Christian Adams warns that states like California and Washington are deliberately blocking use of the federal SAVE database—a tool designed to verify citizenship—leaving noncitizens on voter rolls (Fox News).

This matters for Colorado. While Colorado has better election practices than West Coast states, conservatives worry that the Biden administration's reluctance to enforce voter integrity nationwide creates a template for lax standards that could spread. Republicans argue that citizenship verification is basic election hygiene; Democrats counter that such measures suppress legitimate voters.

The DOJ's subpoena came after a Clinton-appointed federal judge blocked the agency from prohibiting California from counting ballots tied to voters whose citizenship status is questioned—a stunning rebuke to election security advocates. California's refusal to use available verification tools, despite federal pressure, underscores a deeper divide: progressive states view strict voter ID and citizenship checks as barriers to participation; conservatives see them as non-negotiable safeguards against fraud.

Watch whether Colorado election officials face similar pressure to weaken citizenship verification, or whether the state's Republican-led legislature moves to strengthen SAVE database integration before the 2026 cycle.
