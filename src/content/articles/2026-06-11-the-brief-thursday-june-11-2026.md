# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, June 11, 2026

Colorado conservatives are waking up to a mix of national accountability stories and economic news that cuts straight to the heart of what's wrong — and occasionally right — with the country right now. From secret government spending to a red-hot private market, here's what matters today.

## DOJ Indicts Eight Anti-Israel Activists at University of Michigan

The Department of Justice has indicted eight anti-Israel protesters tied to the University of Michigan, charging them with alleged threats and disruptive "autonomous actions" — including targeting a local Jewish nonprofit. Republicans have long argued that campus radicalism has been allowed to fester under weak institutional leadership and selective law enforcement. Colorado's own university campuses have seen similar protest activity, and Colorado taxpayers who fund public higher education should be asking hard questions about who's running the show on their dime.

---

## UFO Whistleblower: Billions Hidden From Congress in Secret Slush Funds

UAP whistleblower David Grusch is accusing U.S. intelligence agencies of concealing billions in secret spending from congressional oversight through alleged slush funds. Whatever you think of the UFO angle, the core accusation is a serious government accountability problem. Conservatives have consistently warned that the national security bureaucracy operates with far too little transparency and far too little respect for the constitutional authority of Congress. If billions are being hidden from elected representatives, that's not a mystery story — it's a spending scandal.

## Democrat Fundraising CEO Takes the Fifth

The CEO of a major Democrat fundraising operation has refused to answer questions, invoking Fifth Amendment protections. Republicans argue this is exactly the kind of institutional rot that flows from a political machine that has operated without accountability for years. Colorado Democrats have benefited enormously from national fundraising networks, and voters here deserve to know whether the money fueling their state's leftward policy drift comes with strings — or worse, secrets — attached.

---

## Belfast Violence Revives Immigration Debate — and the Lessons Are Clear

Riots and a beheading attempt in Belfast are reigniting the American debate over illegal immigration and the consequences of porous borders. Conservatives say the lesson is simple: nations that fail to control who enters their borders eventually pay a steep price in public safety. Colorado, a state that has seen its border-adjacent communities strained by illegal crossings and where sanctuary policies remain politically contentious, has a direct stake in whether Washington finally gets serious about enforcement.

## SpaceX IPO Demand Is Four Times Available Shares

Demand for SpaceX's initial public offering is reportedly running at four times the available shares, with the offering set to raise $75 billion — three times the next-largest market debut in history, Saudi Aramco's $29.4 billion debut in 2019. This is what American free-market innovation looks like when government gets out of the way. Colorado's aerospace and defense corridor, home to thousands of workers tied to the space economy, has every reason to cheer a private sector that's outpacing anything a government program could deliver.

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the DOJ's University of Michigan indictments — if the Trump Justice Department follows through aggressively, it will set a national precedent for how campus-based political violence and intimidation are prosecuted, and Colorado's activist university culture will be watching nervously. The Democrat fundraising CEO's Fifth Amendment stand deserves far more scrutiny from Colorado's mainstream press than it will likely receive. And the SpaceX IPO demand is a signal: private capital is betting big on American innovation, and Colorado's economy should be positioned to benefit — if state government can resist the urge to regulate and tax it into submission.
