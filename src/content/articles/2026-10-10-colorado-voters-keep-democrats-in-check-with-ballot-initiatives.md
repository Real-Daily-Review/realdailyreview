# Colorado Voters Keep Democrats in Check—With Ballot Initiatives

Colorado Democrats keep winning statewide races, but voters keep using citizen initiatives to block their progressive agenda. That's the real story of Colorado politics: ticket-splitting on steroids.

Coloradans are sending a clear message: we'll elect Democratic governors and senators, but we don't trust them with a blank check. According to Complete Colorado, voters repeatedly approve ballot measures that directly counter Democratic policy priorities—a pattern that reveals deep skepticism about the left's governing instincts even among those who vote blue at the top of the ticket.

Meanwhile, Boulder DA Michael Dougherty dismissed a GOP-led congressional report on Colorado's sanctuary policies as a "political stunt" (9News Denver), but the underlying issue remains: Colorado's sanctuary laws shield illegal immigrants from federal immigration enforcement, a practice conservatives argue undermines law and order and strains local resources.

The sanctuary debate exposes a broader tension. Democrats control the statehouse and many local offices, yet Coloradans consistently use direct democracy to rein in progressive overreach—whether on taxes, regulations, or immigration enforcement. It's a voters' veto that keeps the left from running too far ahead of the state's center-right instincts.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans capitalize on this voter skepticism in the next election cycle by making the case that ticket-splitting is a symptom of Democratic governance Coloradans don't actually want.
