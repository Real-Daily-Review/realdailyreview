# Colorado's 'Hail Alley' Takes Another Beating—and Taxpayers Will Foot the Cleanup Bill

Softball-sized hail pummeled Colorado's Front Range and Eastern Plains again, leaving a trail of destruction that will drain state and federal disaster resources while farmers and homeowners face months of recovery without guaranteed aid.

The second consecutive day of severe weather hit hard: Fort Morgan endured egg-sized hail, Akron battled flooding, and Fleming experienced a tornado, according to 9News Denver. CPR News reported that residents described the violence of the storms—"It sounded like everything was just breaking"—as hail the size of softballs shattered windows, roofs, and vehicles across communities already reeling from the previous day's damage.

Colorado's "Hail Alley" corridor has become a recurring disaster zone, and conservatives should ask hard questions about preparedness and recovery spending. While disaster relief is necessary, the pattern raises concerns about how state and federal governments allocate limited resources and whether communities are adequately incentivizing resilient building standards and insurance coverage rather than relying on taxpayer bailouts.

Agricultural areas face particular losses—crop damage, livestock exposure, and infrastructure destruction will ripple through rural Colorado's economy. Energy infrastructure and transmission lines are also vulnerable to repeated severe weather events.

Watch whether state lawmakers push for federal disaster declarations that trigger open-ended spending, or whether they prioritize market-based resilience solutions and private insurance recovery.
