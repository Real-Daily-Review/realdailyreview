# I-70 Shut Down Near Glenwood Springs as Paradise Fire Spreads

Colorado's critical east-west corridor took another hit Tuesday when the Paradise Fire forced authorities to close Interstate 70 in both directions near Glenwood Springs, snarling traffic and threatening the state's economic lifeline once again.

The brush fire had burned approximately 20 acres by afternoon, according to CPR News, with no immediate word on containment or cause. The closure disrupted commerce, commuter traffic, and supply chains across the state—a recurring nightmare for Colorado businesses and taxpayers already battered by wildfire season.

I-70 near Glenwood Springs has become a chronic chokepoint. Wildfires, mudslides, and weather regularly shut the corridor, costing the state economy millions in lost productivity and stranding travelers. The Paradise Fire is the latest reminder that Colorado's infrastructure remains vulnerable to natural disaster, and state and federal land management policies—particularly regarding forest health and fuel reduction—remain inadequate.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that aggressive forest management, including controlled burns and timber harvesting on federal lands, could reduce catastrophic wildfire risk. The Biden administration's environmental restrictions have slowed such efforts, leaving Colorado's forests dangerously overgrown.

Watch for updates on containment timelines and whether state officials will finally push the federal government to accelerate forest thinning projects along critical corridors.
