# Trump Renews Attack on Obama's Iran Deal as New Negotiations Loom

Trump is hammering the Obama-era JCPOA again, calling it a catastrophic giveaway that left America weaker and Iran emboldened. As reports surface of emerging nuclear talks that some observers say echo the original agreement's flawed architecture, conservatives are sounding the alarm that the Biden-Harris administration may be walking back Trump's harder line on Tehran.

Trump withdrew from the JCPOA in 2018, calling it "very dangerous" and arguing the deal handed Iran a path to nuclear weapons while extracting minimal concessions (The Daily Wire). He has continued blasting the agreement as a strategic disaster that allowed Iran to laugh at American weakness.

**Republicans argue** the original deal was fatally compromised: it lifted sanctions without permanent nuclear restrictions, failed to address Iran's ballistic missile program, and relied on inspections Iran could obstruct. They contend Trump's "maximum pressure" campaign was the only language Tehran understood.

**Democrats counter** that abandoning the JCPOA isolated America from allies and accelerated Iran's nuclear advancement, but that framing ignores conservatives' core objection: any deal that doesn't permanently dismantle Iran's nuclear infrastructure is appeasement.

For Colorado, the stakes are national security and energy policy—Iran's regional aggression affects Middle East stability and global oil markets that impact state energy workers and consumers.

Watch whether the administration formally returns to JCPOA terms or negotiates genuinely tougher restrictions.
