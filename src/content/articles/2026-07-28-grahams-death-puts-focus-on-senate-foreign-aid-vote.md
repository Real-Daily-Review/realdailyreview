# World Leaders Honor Lindsey Graham as Senate Prepares Final Vote on Russia Sanctions

Sen. Lindsey Graham's death has drawn global attention to his hawkish foreign policy legacy—and raised questions about whether his interventionist approach still commands Senate support.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky are both traveling to Washington to attend Graham's funeral at the National Cathedral, underscoring his decades of advocacy for military aid to Israel and Ukraine (9News Denver, Fox News Politics). The timing is significant: Graham's Russia sanctions bill faces a key Senate vote Tuesday night, just hours after his funeral concludes.

Graham's death on July 11 from a heart condition leaves a void in the Senate's most vocal advocates for aggressive foreign aid and military intervention abroad. His final legislative push—the Russia sanctions measure—will test whether Republicans and Democrats remain aligned on punishing Moscow, or whether the party's skepticism of endless overseas commitments has shifted the calculus.

For Colorado taxpayers, Graham's legacy matters: his foreign aid votes consistently pushed federal spending toward Ukraine, Israel, and other allies—money that conservatives argue could address domestic priorities like border security or infrastructure.
