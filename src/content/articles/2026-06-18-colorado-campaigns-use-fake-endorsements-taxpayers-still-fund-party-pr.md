# Colorado Political Ads Exploit Famous Figures; Taxpayers Still Funding Party Primaries They Don't Support

Colorado campaigns are running deceptive political ads featuring the Pope, Barack Obama, and Ruth Bader Ginsburg—implying endorsements these figures never gave, according to 9News Denver. The ads use video and audio clips stripped of context to manufacture false support, a tactic that raises questions about campaign ethics and voter manipulation in the state.

Meanwhile, conservatives are raising a separate but related concern: Colorado taxpayers are forced to finance primary elections for political parties they don't belong to or support. Complete Colorado argues this represents an unjustifiable government subsidy of partisan machinery. Why should a libertarian-leaning voter or independent fund Democratic or Republican primaries? Republicans and limited-government advocates say the practice exemplifies how government privileges entrenched parties at taxpayer expense.

The two issues highlight how Colorado's election system tilts toward establishment players—whether through misleading messaging or mandatory party-primary funding. Voters deserve transparency about who actually endorses candidates, and taxpayers deserve the right to opt out of financing parties they reject.

Watch for whether Colorado election officials crack down on deceptive ad practices, and whether legislators take up the case for ending taxpayer-funded party primaries.
