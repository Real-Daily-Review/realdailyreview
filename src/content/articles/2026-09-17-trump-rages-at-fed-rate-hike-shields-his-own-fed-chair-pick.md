# Trump Blasts Fed Rate Hike, But Spares His Own Pick to Lead the Central Bank

President Trump erupted over the Federal Reserve's first interest rate increase in three years—raising rates to 4.00%—but notably withheld fire from Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, whom he appointed earlier this year. Speaking in North Carolina, Trump called the Fed "hostile" and declared on Truth Social that interest rates "should be 1%, or less" (Breitbart, Daily Wire). The rate hike comes after an extended period of steady rates, and Trump has consistently pushed the Fed to cut rather than raise.

For Colorado businesses and homeowners already squeezed by higher borrowing costs, Trump's position reflects a broader conservative argument: aggressive rate hikes slow economic growth, strain small enterprises, and make mortgages and business loans more expensive. Republicans argue the Fed should prioritize growth over inflation concerns, especially heading into an election cycle.

Trump's decision to spare Warsh—despite the rate decision—signals confidence in his Fed leadership pick and suggests the president expects future rate cuts under Warsh's tenure. The contrast underscores Trump's willingness to pressure the central bank while maintaining control over its direction.

Watch whether Warsh signals a pivot toward lower rates in coming months, or if Trump's criticism intensifies if the Fed holds firm.
