# Aspen Acres Fire Devastation Exposes Colorado's Wildfire Crisis—and Government's Failure to Prevent It

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire has destroyed at least 780 structures across Pueblo and Custer counties, according to aerial analysis by Vexcel Data, far exceeding initial county damage assessments still being tallied (Colorado Sun). The destruction includes homes, garages, barns, and sheds—and claimed a historic lodge that served generations of Coloradans (9News Denver).

The scale of devastation underscores a hard truth conservatives have long warned about: Colorado's forests are tinderboxes, and decades of mismanagement by federal and state agencies have made catastrophic wildfires inevitable. Overgrown forests choked with dead timber, combined with environmental regulations that block sensible logging and fuel-reduction projects, have created powder kegs across the state.

Republicans and conservatives argue that aggressive forest management—thinning, controlled burns, and timber harvesting—must replace the hands-off approach that has left Colorado vulnerable. Instead, progressive environmental policies have often blocked these proven solutions in the name of "preservation."

As damage assessments continue and rebuilding begins, Colorado taxpayers will foot much of the bill for recovery. Watch whether state and federal officials finally embrace forest management reforms or double down on the regulatory barriers that helped create this disaster.
