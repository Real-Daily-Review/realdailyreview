# Colorado Transit Systems Face Fare-Evasion Pressure as Progressive Policies Backfire

Colorado's public transit agencies are watching a cautionary tale unfold in left-leaning cities: when progressive leadership prioritizes social-justice ideology over basic law enforcement, fare evasion explodes—and taxpayers foot the bill.

Cities from New York to San Francisco have seen their subway systems hemorrhage revenue as "woke" policies effectively decriminalized turnstile-hopping. The result? Transit agencies spent millions upgrading to higher faregates that actually *work*—and fare revenue rebounded (Washington Examiner). The lesson is stark: permissive policies don't reduce crime; they enable it.

For Colorado, this matters. RTD and other regional transit systems already struggle with funding and ridership. If Denver or Boulder adopt the same soft-on-crime approach that gutted East Coast transit systems, Colorado commuters and taxpayers will pay twice: once through subsidies for the system, again through lost revenue that could fund service improvements.

Republicans and conservatives argue that enforcing basic rules—paying your fare, respecting public property—isn't oppressive; it's the foundation of functional public services. The left's insistence on treating fare evasion as a victimless act of resistance has proven disastrous everywhere it's been tried.

Watch whether Colorado transit officials learn from these failures or double down on ideology over accountability.
