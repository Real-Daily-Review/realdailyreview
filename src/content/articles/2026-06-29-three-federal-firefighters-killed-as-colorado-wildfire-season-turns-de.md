# Three Federal Firefighters Killed as Colorado Wildfire Season Turns Deadly

Colorado's wildfire season has claimed its first federal firefighter lives, a stark reminder that no amount of government land management can fully tame nature's fury.

Three federal firefighters died Saturday when the Snyder wildfire in Mesa County exploded during high winds, overwhelming survival shelters deployed by their crew (Colorado Sun). Two crew members survived with injuries. The same week, emergency evacuations were ordered for the Aspen Acres neighborhood in Custer County as the Rye Fire spread (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

The deaths underscore a hard truth conservatives have long argued: federal land management agencies face inherent limits. While the Biden administration has pushed aggressive prescribed-burn and forest-thinning programs—often at significant taxpayer expense—Colorado's terrain, drought cycles, and extreme weather events remain unpredictable and deadly. Better forest management helps, but it cannot eliminate risk.

These tragedies also highlight the real costs of federal land ownership. Nearly 37% of Colorado is federally managed, constraining state and local fire-response flexibility and creating vast tinderboxes that endanger communities and workers alike.

Conservatives should watch whether state lawmakers push back on federal land-management overreach and demand greater local control over Colorado's forests and fire response.
