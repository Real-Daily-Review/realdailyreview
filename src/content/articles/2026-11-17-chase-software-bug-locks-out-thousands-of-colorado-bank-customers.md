# Major Bank Outages Hit Chase, Other Lenders Over Software Glitch

Colorado customers of Chase and other major U.S. banks faced disrupted online access Monday after what Chase attributed to a "software bug," raising fresh questions about the fragility of critical financial infrastructure that millions of Americans depend on daily.

Thousands of customers reported difficulty accessing online accounts and checking balances, according to reports from KDVR Fox31 Denver and 9News. The Daily Wire noted that Downdetector recorded more than 2,500 user reports involving Chase alone at noon Monday, with 39% involving account balances and transactions, 32% reporting web login issues, and 16% citing other problems. Smaller spikes in complaints affected several other major U.S. banks.

Chase said transactions returned to normal after the outage, but the incident underscores a critical vulnerability: Colorado families and small businesses—already squeezed by inflation and rising costs—depend on seamless access to their own money. When financial institutions fail to maintain reliable systems, ordinary people pay the price through lost productivity and eroded confidence.

The outage also highlights why conservatives remain skeptical of expanding government mandates on banks. Regulators already impose heavy compliance burdens; adding more rules won't prevent software failures—it will only drive up costs that get passed to customers.

Watch whether regulators use this incident to justify new restrictions on banking operations or digital infrastructure.
