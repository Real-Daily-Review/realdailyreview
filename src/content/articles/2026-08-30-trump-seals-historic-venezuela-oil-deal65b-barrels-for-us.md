# Trump Secures Historic Venezuela Oil Deal—What It Means for Colorado Energy

President Trump announced Friday a landmark agreement with Venezuela granting the U.S. majority control of more than 65 billion barrels of oil reserves, a move that could reshape American energy independence and Colorado's oil and gas sector.

Trump touted the deal as "the biggest oil deal in world history," arguing it addresses U.S. energy security and could help moderate domestic gas prices (Daily Caller, Daily Wire). The agreement represents a significant geopolitical win and a direct challenge to OPEC's pricing power—a priority for Republicans who view energy dominance as critical to national security and economic strength.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil and gas industry, already under pressure from state regulations and federal land-use restrictions, could benefit from increased domestic supply and reduced reliance on foreign crude. Lower global oil prices typically support domestic producers and energy workers across the state.

Conservatives argue that expanding U.S. oil reserves strengthens economic growth, creates jobs, and reduces dependence on hostile regimes. The deal also signals Trump's willingness to use diplomatic leverage to advance American interests—a stark contrast to the Biden administration's approach to energy policy, which prioritized climate restrictions over production.
