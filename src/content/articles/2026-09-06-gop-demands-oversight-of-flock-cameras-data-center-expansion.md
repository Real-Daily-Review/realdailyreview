# Conservative Leaders Sound Alarm on Unchecked Surveillance Expansion

Republican politicians are demanding accountability over the explosive growth of data centers and Flock surveillance cameras, warning that career politicians have allowed privacy-eroding technology to expand without meaningful oversight.

Rep. Byron Donalds told the Daily Caller that privacy protection must be the priority, stating his "number one concern is the privacy of people" as data centers proliferate across the country. Louisiana congressional candidate Stephen Wagley echoed the concern on Breitbart, accusing politicians of letting "Flock cameras, data centers 'slide'" without proper scrutiny.

The tension reflects a broader conservative anxiety: while Republicans generally support free enterprise and technological innovation, they're increasingly uneasy about surveillance infrastructure that operates in regulatory gray zones. Data centers consume massive power and water resources—critical issues in water-scarce Colorado—while Flock's automatic license plate readers raise Fourth Amendment questions that civil libertarians across the political spectrum find troubling.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's energy grid and water supplies already face strain from data center expansion in the Front Range. Conservative lawmakers should demand transparency on how many Flock cameras operate statewide and what data-sharing agreements exist with state and local law enforcement.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans introduce legislation requiring legislative approval before new surveillance networks deploy in the state.
