# Trump's Tariff Gamble Divides Right; Colorado Manufacturers Watch Closely

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire from his own side—a sign that even conservatives skeptical of free-trade orthodoxy worry about unintended consequences for American workers and businesses.

The "Liberation Day" tariffs, announced in April 2025, sparked immediate backlash. *Washington Examiner* opinion writers called the move chaotic, with "absurd" justifications that sent approval ratings sliding. Critics argue Trump is repeating failed protectionist experiments rather than pursuing genuine economic reform.

The core conservative complaint: tariffs sound tough but often backfire. They raise prices for consumers, invite retaliation against U.S. exporters, and hand ammunition to Democrats claiming Republicans harm working families. *National Review* suggests Trump should instead follow Reagan's Cold War playbook—building strength from within rather than imposing blunt trade barriers.

**The Colorado angle matters.** Manufacturing hubs along the Front Range, agricultural exporters, and energy companies dependent on global supply chains face real exposure. Higher input costs ripple through construction, food production, and industrial sectors where Colorado jobs depend on predictable trade flows.

Republicans remain split: some back Trump's nationalist instincts; others warn tariffs are an expensive detour from pro-growth policy. Conservatives should watch whether Trump adjusts course or doubles down—and whether Colorado's business community breaks ranks publicly.
