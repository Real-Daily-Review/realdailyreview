# Colorado Voters Face Sticker Shock as Amendment 87 Tax Hike Hits Groceries

Colorado Democrats got what they voted for—and now they'll pay for it at checkout. Amendment 87, approved by voters, will nearly double taxes on grocery stores earning over $500,000 annually, according to Complete Colorado. Those increased operating costs won't stay on store shelves; they'll flow directly to consumers through higher prices on staples.

Republicans warned this was economic self-sabotage. The amendment targets a narrow slice of retail, but grocery margins are thin, and Colorado's already-tight supply chains mean businesses will pass costs downstream. Families already squeezed by inflation will feel the pinch hardest.

Meanwhile, Colorado Democrats are doubling down on constitutional overreach. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the 10th Circuit is hearing arguments over Colorado's pronoun law, which amended the Anti-Discrimination Act to mandate use of "chosen name and preferred pronouns" (Washington Examiner). Conservatives argue the law violates free speech and compelled speech protections under the First Amendment—a position Colorado has already lost on in multiple previous cases. Democrats seemingly haven't learned from those defeats.
