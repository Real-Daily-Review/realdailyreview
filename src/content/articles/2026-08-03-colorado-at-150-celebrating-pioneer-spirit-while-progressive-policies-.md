# Colorado at 150: A State Built on Freedom, Now Facing Progressive Headwinds

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives as the Centennial State celebrates a century and a half of pioneering spirit—but also confronts whether that legacy of individual liberty and limited government can survive the progressive regulatory tide sweeping Denver.

State leaders gathered at the Capitol on statehood day to mark the milestone (Colorado Sun, CPR News), reflecting on Colorado's founding principles: gold rush entrepreneurship, frontier self-reliance, and the kind of rugged individualism that built towns from nothing. Those values attracted generations of Coloradans who came here to escape overregulation and build their own futures.

Today's Colorado tells a different story. The state has drifted sharply left—Denver's progressive city council, statewide Democratic supermajorities, and expanding regulations on energy development, water use, and gun ownership have transformed the political landscape. The Colorado that once embodied frontier freedom now leads the nation in some of the most restrictive environmental mandates and gun control measures.

Conservatives argue the state's founders would be alarmed at how far Colorado has strayed from the principles of limited government and economic liberty that made it great. The question for the next 150 years: can Colorado reclaim its heritage, or will progressive governance continue remaking the state in the image of California?

Watch for whether Republican candidates in 2026 can message Colorado's founding values effectively enough to reclaim lost ground in suburban Denver.
