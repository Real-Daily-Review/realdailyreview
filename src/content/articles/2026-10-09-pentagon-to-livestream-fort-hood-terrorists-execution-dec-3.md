# Pentagon to Livestream Fort Hood Terrorist's Execution—Hegseth Signals Swift Justice

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has announced that the military execution of Nidal Malik Hasan, the Fort Hood shooter who killed 13 soldiers in 2009, will be livestreamed to the public on December 3. The Pentagon confirmed the decision, with Hegseth telling media outlets that Americans should witness the consequences of Hasan's attack on U.S. military personnel.

Hasan's execution by firing squad will mark the military's first execution in decades. Hegseth framed the public nature of the proceeding as a matter of accountability and deterrence, emphasizing that the gravity of murdering American soldiers demands transparency (Daily Wire, Just The News).

The move reflects the Trump administration's more assertive stance on military justice and national security. Conservatives have long argued that swift, certain punishment serves both justice and deterrence—principles Hegseth's decision underscores. The livestream approach represents a departure from typical military execution protocols, signaling the administration's commitment to public accountability for attacks on U.S. forces.
