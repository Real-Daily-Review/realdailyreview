# Moscow's Victory Day Parade Stripped of Military Hardware

Russia held a scaled-back Victory Day parade on Red Square on Saturday, with President Vladimir Putin presiding over ceremonies commemorating the Soviet defeat of Nazi Germany in World War II. The parade notably lacked the military hardware the Kremlin typically displays to project power internationally, according to BBC World's Russia editor.

Putin used the occasion to voice confidence in a Russian victory in Ukraine, according to PBS NewsHour. The event proceeded under heavy security measures.

The absence of tanks, missiles, and other weaponry marked a stark departure from previous years' parades, which have traditionally featured extensive displays of Russia's military capabilities. The scaled-down nature of this year's commemoration reflects constraints on Russia's ability to stage its customary show of force, though the Kremlin proceeded with the symbolic event.

Victory Day, observed annually on May 9, holds deep historical and political significance in Russia. The parade typically serves as a platform for the government to demonstrate military strength and rally nationalist sentiment.

Watch for how Russia's military posture and parade displays evolve in coming years as the Ukraine conflict continues.
