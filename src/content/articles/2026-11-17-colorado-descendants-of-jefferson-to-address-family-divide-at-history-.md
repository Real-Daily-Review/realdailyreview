# Colorado Descendants of Thomas Jefferson to Address Family Divide at History Museum

Two branches of Thomas Jefferson's family—including former Colorado state representative Sal Pace—will speak Wednesday at the Colorado History Museum about reconciliation efforts between descendants separated by historical divisions (Colorado Sun). The event comes as national debate intensifies over how Americans interpret the Founding Fathers' legacies.

The discussion arrives amid broader cultural disputes over Jefferson's historical record. Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis recently weighed in on the topic, offering what he framed as historical context when Muslim politicians referenced Jefferson's personal Quran (Breitbart). DeSantis argued the Founding Fathers faced realities 240 years ago that modern commentators often overlook.

Conservatives argue that understanding the Founders requires grappling with the full historical context—including the constraints and threats they faced—rather than selectively invoking their symbols for contemporary political purposes. The left counters that Jefferson's intellectual contributions to American liberty can be separated from his personal failings and that his writings belong to all Americans regardless of faith.

For Colorado, the event underscores how state institutions are engaging with foundational American questions about history, identity, and national unity. Taxpayers should watch whether Colorado's publicly funded museums present these debates with intellectual honesty or lean toward progressive historical revisionism.
