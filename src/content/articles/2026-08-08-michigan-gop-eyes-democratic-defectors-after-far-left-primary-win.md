# Michigan Republican Sees Opening as Democrats Nominate Far-Left Candidate

Michigan Republicans smell blood in the water after Democrats nominated a far-left primary winner, and GOP Senate nominee Mike Rogers is making a direct play for moderate Democratic voters who feel abandoned by their party's leftward lurch.

Rogers released a video Friday targeting the roughly 728,000 Michigan Democrats who voted for Rep. Haley Stevens (D-MI) over primary winner Abdul El-Sayed, according to *The Daily Wire*. Republicans argue El-Sayed's "divisive" victory signals how thoroughly progressive activists have captured the Democratic Party machinery, leaving centrist voters without a home—and making them potential swing voters in November.

*Fox News Politics* reports Rogers is explicitly messaging that "there is still hope" for Democrats uncomfortable with their nominee's ideological positioning. The play reflects a broader Republican strategy: when the other side nominates someone too far outside the mainstream, peel off the moderates.

For Colorado conservatives watching Senate races nationally, this Michigan dynamic offers a cautionary tale about primary capture. When activist bases dominate nomination contests, they often hand general-election ammunition to the other side. The question now is whether Rogers can convert Democratic defection into actual crossover votes—or whether party loyalty ultimately holds.

Watch whether El-Sayed's general-election positioning softens, or whether he doubles down on the platform that won the primary.
