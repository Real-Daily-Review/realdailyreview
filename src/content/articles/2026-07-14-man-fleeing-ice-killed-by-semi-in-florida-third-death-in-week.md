# Man Fleeing ICE Struck and Killed by Semi in Florida

Colorado's open-borders left should reckon with the real-world consequences of sanctuary policies that encourage illegal immigrants to flee law enforcement.

A man was struck and killed by a tractor-trailer in St. Augustine, Florida, early this week while fleeing from Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Homeland Security Investigations officers during a traffic stop, according to police (Washington Examiner). The incident marks the third death in roughly a week involving encounters with ICE agents—following separate shootings in Texas and Maine (9News Denver).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that sanctuary jurisdictions and rhetoric demonizing immigration enforcement create dangerous situations where illegal immigrants panic and flee into traffic rather than comply with lawful stops. When politicians and activists frame ICE operations as inherently unjust, they incentivize exactly this kind of reckless behavior. Colorado cities like Denver and Boulder have adopted sanctuary policies that limit cooperation with federal immigration authorities—a stance that, critics say, prioritizes ideology over public safety and rule of law.

**Democrats counter** that aggressive immigration enforcement traumatizes vulnerable communities and that the real problem is an immigration system in need of reform.

The pattern of deaths during ICE encounters raises hard questions about enforcement tactics—but also about whether sanctuary policies are actually protecting anyone, or simply creating chaos.
