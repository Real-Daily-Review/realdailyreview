# Real Daily Review — The Brief

## McConnell Found Unconscious Last Month — Questions Remain

Senate Minority Leader Mitch McConnell was found unconscious in his home last month, with his last confirmed public sighting on June 12. His condition remains unknown. Whatever one thinks of McConnell's legacy — and conservatives have plenty of opinions on that — the Senate's balance of power and Republican leadership continuity are real concerns. Colorado's GOP delegation and every conservative watching the upper chamber should be paying close attention to how this story develops in the days ahead.

## Tucker Carlson Breaks With GOP, Announces New Party

Tucker Carlson says he's done with the Republican Party and is building something new, declaring that "what really matters is war and finance." Carlson's move will either fracture the conservative coalition at a critical moment or force the GOP to reckon with the populist base it has repeatedly taken for granted. For Colorado conservatives already frustrated with establishment Republicans in Washington, this is a fault line worth watching — a third-party effort could siphon votes in competitive Colorado races from the Western Slope to the suburbs.

## Wildfire Smoke Blankets Colorado — No Relief in Sight

Hazy skies and wildfire smoke are blanketing Colorado today, with conditions expected to fluctuate as dry weather and winds continue to drive fires statewide. This is what years of mismanaged federal forest policy looks like — bureaucratic paralysis over controlled burns, logging, and land management has left Colorado's forests as tinderboxes. Ranchers, farmers, and outdoor workers are paying the price. Colorado's congressional delegation should be pressing Washington hard for real forest management reform, not more regulatory red tape.

## Walz Pardons Illegal Alien Convicted of Child Sex Assault

Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz and the state Board of Pardons cleared Tou Lue Vang, a Laotian citizen and convicted child sexual assault offender, effectively shielding him from deportation. Democrats counter that the pardon process followed proper legal procedure. Conservatives say this is exactly the kind of radical sanctuary-state thinking that puts public safety second and illegal immigrants first — and it's a reminder of what Walz would have brought to the White House. Colorado's own Democrats have pushed similar soft-on-deportation policies, and voters should remember it.

## Brennan Sues Trump Admin, Claims 'Vindictive Prosecution'

Former CIA Director John Brennan — widely accused by Republicans of orchestrating the Russia collusion narrative against Donald Trump — is now suing the Trump administration and demanding preservation of investigation records, claiming vindictive prosecution. Republicans argue Brennan spent years weaponizing the intelligence community against a sitting president and is now hiding behind the courts. For Coloradans who watched the Russia hoax consume years of national political life, this lawsuit reads less like a civil liberties stand and more like a man trying to bury the evidence before it buries him.
