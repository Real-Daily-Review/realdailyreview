# Colorado's Mail-Ballot System Faces Scrutiny as Election Security Debate Heats Up

Colorado's vaunted mail-voting infrastructure is drawing renewed scrutiny after three fraudulent ballots slipped through signature verification in 2024—a stark reminder that no system is foolproof, even one touted as bulletproof by election officials.

The Denver Post reports that two Mesa County residents successfully submitted fraudulent ballots while attempting to "prove election fraud was real," with three of 12 fraudulent ballots actually counted before detection. While election officials trumpet the system's "many checks and balances," conservatives argue the incidents expose vulnerabilities that demand tighter safeguards.

Amendment 84, now before voters, would require Social Security numbers on ballot applications—a measure backers say could close the signature-verification gap that allowed the fraud to occur. The Post notes Colorado county clerks have "reliably and responsibly" managed mail voting for over a decade, yet the 2024 breaches suggest complacency may be setting in.

For Colorado taxpayers and election integrity advocates, the question isn't whether the system works *most* of the time—it's whether three undetected fraudulent ballots represent acceptable risk in a statewide election. Democrats and election officials resist additional ID requirements, claiming they burden voters; Republicans counter that basic security measures protect ballot legitimacy.

Watch whether Amendment 84 gains traction with Colorado voters tired of hearing "rare fraud" while actual fraud gets counted.
