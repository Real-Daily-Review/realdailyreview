# Trump Scraps Biden's EV Mandate, Approves New Fuel Economy Standards

Trump just killed the regulatory straitjacket that was forcing Detroit to go all-in on electric vehicles—and Colorado's auto workers and consumers should notice.

The president approved new fuel economy standards Saturday that replace Biden-era rules designed to mandate EV adoption. Trump framed the move as a win for both workers and manufacturers, noting that automakers from General Motors to Ford to Stellantis have approached him about expanding U.S. production now that the regulatory burden has lifted (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

**The conservative case:** Biden's EV mandate was regulatory overreach that picked winners and losers, threatened jobs at traditional auto plants, and forced consumers toward vehicles many didn't want. Trump's standards restore manufacturer flexibility and let the market—not Washington bureaucrats—decide what cars Americans buy.

**Democrats counter** that stricter fuel economy rules combat climate change and reduce emissions. They argue the EV transition creates jobs in new industries.

For Colorado, this matters. The state has auto manufacturing operations and workers whose livelihoods depend on competitive, flexible production standards. Removing the federal EV straitjacket means manufacturers can build what sells, not what regulators demand—potentially boosting employment and keeping costs down for Colorado consumers.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic congressional delegation pushes back or whether in-state manufacturers signal expansion plans under the new rules.
