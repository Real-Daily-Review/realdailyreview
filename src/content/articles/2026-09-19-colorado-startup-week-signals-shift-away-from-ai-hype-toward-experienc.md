# Colorado Startup Week Shows Tech Sector Still Chasing the Next Big Thing—While AI Hype Fades

Colorado's startup ecosystem is pivoting away from artificial intelligence toward "experience economy" ventures, signaling that the AI boom may be cooling faster than tech evangelists predicted. During Colorado Startup Week, the focus shifted noticeably from the AI-dominated headlines of recent years to in-person, experiential business models—a telling sign that founders and investors are hunting for the next trend.

Techstars, the prominent accelerator program that decamped to New York two years ago, made a symbolic return to Boulder during the event (CPR News), underscoring Colorado's continued relevance as a startup hub despite national competition. The Colorado Sun reports that while artificial intelligence continues to impact software development and job markets, the real momentum appears to be swinging toward businesses built on human connection and tangible experiences.

For Colorado taxpayers and business leaders, this pivot matters: it suggests the state's tech sector remains dynamic and adaptive, though it also raises questions about whether Colorado can compete for talent and capital as the industry chases each new frontier. The startup economy drives jobs, attracts young professionals, and generates tax revenue—but only if Colorado remains a destination, not a waystation.

Watch whether Techstars' return signals a broader commitment to Colorado or remains a one-week marketing exercise.
