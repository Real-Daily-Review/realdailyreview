# College Campuses Still Hostile to Conservative Speech—and Colorado Students Are Paying the Price

College campuses remain hostile territory for students willing to defend conservative principles without apology. According to the Washington Examiner, political ostracism on campus is rampant, with students across the ideological spectrum—from communists to conservatives—facing social pressure for their beliefs. Yet conservatives face a particular burden: the expectation that they'll shrink from their convictions rather than defend them.

For Colorado families paying tuition at CU Boulder, Colorado State, and other state universities, this matters. Taxpayer-funded institutions should protect intellectual diversity and free speech, not enforce progressive orthodoxy through social intimidation. When conservative students feel compelled to hide their views to avoid isolation, the university fails its core mission.

Republicans and conservatives argue that academia's leftward tilt has created a chilling effect on open debate. They contend that universities should model intellectual courage—not punish it. The left counters that "safe spaces" and social accountability for offensive speech are necessary protections for marginalized groups.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state university leadership takes concrete steps to protect viewpoint diversity and free expression—or continues enabling the silencing of conservative voices on campus.
