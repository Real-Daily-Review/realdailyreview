# Aurora's Data Center Moratorium: Another Colorado City Chooses Regulation Over Growth

Aurora city council has effectively killed a major economic opportunity by imposing a yearlong moratorium on new data centers, forcing Vantage Data Centers to abandon plans for what would have been Colorado's largest hyperscale facility (Colorado Sun). The move prioritizes bureaucratic rule-making over job creation and tax revenue.

City leaders claim the timeout will allow them to craft "new rules and regulations" around data centers (9News Denver)—code for adding red tape that will discourage investment. This is classic progressive governance: block first, regulate later, hope businesses stick around.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Colorado needs private investment, high-wage jobs, and expanding tax bases. Data centers are clean industry, attract skilled workers, and generate substantial municipal revenue without the environmental footprint of traditional manufacturing. Hyperscale facilities like Vantage's represent exactly the kind of economic diversification Aurora should welcome.

Instead, Aurora's council has signaled that it prefers lengthy permitting processes and regulatory uncertainty to decisive approval. Other states—and other Colorado cities—will happily capture this investment.

**Watch whether Aurora's new rulebook actually accelerates approvals or becomes another barrier to entry that keeps major projects out of the state.**
