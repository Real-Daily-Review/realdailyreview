# SpaceX Rocket Leaves Fresh Crater on Moon—NASA Releases Images

Private space ventures are reshaping humanity's relationship with the cosmos, and SpaceX's latest lunar impact proves the company's rockets pack real force.

NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter has captured before-and-after images of a crater carved by a SpaceX Falcon rocket upper stage that struck the moon at 5,400 mph on August 5, according to the Washington Examiner and 9News Denver. The fresh impact crater measures roughly 60 feet across and less than 10 feet deep—visible proof of the velocity and energy involved in space operations.

The images, released Tuesday by NASA, represent the sharpest views yet of the collision site. While the crater itself is modest, the documentation underscores SpaceX's growing role in lunar activities and the practical realities of high-speed space operations.

For Colorado, this development matters: SpaceX's Starship program and related ventures represent cutting-edge manufacturing and engineering jobs, many of which could benefit the state's aerospace sector. Unlike government-run space programs that drain taxpayer dollars, SpaceX operates on commercial principles—developing technology faster and cheaper than traditional contractors.

Conservatives should watch whether regulatory overreach or environmental activists attempt to slow private space development in the name of "lunar preservation"—a potential roadblock to American leadership in space commerce.
