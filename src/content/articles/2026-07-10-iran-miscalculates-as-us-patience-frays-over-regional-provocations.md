# Iran's Regional Gambit Risks Escalation as U.S. Patience Wears Thin

Tehran is overplaying a weak hand in its ongoing confrontation with the United States, betting that limited skirmishes will advance its regional ambitions without triggering full-scale conflict. According to National Review, Iran's strategic position is far less advantageous than Tehran claims—a miscalculation that could prove costly.

The Washington Examiner warns that low-level U.S.-Iran clashes are becoming the new normal. Neither side wants outright war, but Iran's anti-American posture and regional expansionism will keep it testing American resolve. The Trump administration, meanwhile, appears willing to respond firmly to provocation without seeking a decisive military confrontation.

For Colorado, this matters: energy markets remain sensitive to Middle East tensions. Any escalation could spike oil and gas prices, affecting everything from heating costs to transportation expenses for Colorado families and businesses already burdened by state regulations on energy development.

Republicans argue the Biden-era Iran nuclear deal weakness emboldened Tehran's aggression, while Democrats contend diplomacy remains preferable to military entanglement. The real question for Colorado taxpayers: Will the administration's firmer posture stabilize the region, or will continued Iranian provocations drag America into another costly conflict?

Watch whether Iran's regional proxies intensify attacks on U.S. forces or allies—the true test of whether Tehran believes it can operate with impunity.
