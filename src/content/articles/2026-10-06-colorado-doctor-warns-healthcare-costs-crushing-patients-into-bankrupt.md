# Doctor Warns: Healthcare Costs Crushing Colorado Patients

A practicing physician writing in the Washington Examiner is sounding the alarm on medical bankruptcies, arguing that healthcare costs have spiraled beyond what market competition alone can fix. The doctor frames the issue not as ideology but as lived experience: patients forced to choose between necessary procedures and financial ruin.

The piece highlights a real problem conservatives must reckon with. While the right correctly opposes government price controls and one-size-fits-all mandates that stifle innovation, Republicans have offered limited solutions to the transparency and cost-control failures that plague American medicine. The doctor's perspective suggests that pure free-market rhetoric rings hollow to Coloradans watching their premiums climb and deductibles soar.

Conservatives counter that government intervention—from certificate-of-need laws to insurance mandates—has *created* the opacity and cost inflation plaguing the system. They argue the answer is radical transparency, price competition across state lines, and removing regulatory barriers that prevent cheaper alternatives from emerging.

For Colorado, this matters acutely: state insurance markets remain tightly regulated, and rural patients face especially limited options. The question isn't whether healthcare costs are a crisis—they are. It's whether the fix comes through more government control or genuine market reform that lets patients see prices upfront and shop accordingly.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans propose concrete transparency and competition measures, or continue offering only rhetorical opposition to Democratic expansion of government healthcare programs.
