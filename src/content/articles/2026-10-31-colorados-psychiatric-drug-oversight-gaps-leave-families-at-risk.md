# Psychiatric Oversight Failure: Why Colorado's Drug Monitoring Gaps Matter

A Massachusetts tragedy exposing massive holes in prescription oversight should alarm Colorado policymakers and parents alike. Lindsay Clancy was prescribed 13 different psychiatric medications over four months—yet only four fell within the state's prescription monitoring program, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Nine others, including antidepressants, antipsychotics, and mood stabilizers, escaped routine tracking entirely.

This isn't a Massachusetts problem alone. Colorado's prescription drug monitoring program (PDMP), while more comprehensive than many states, still contains blind spots. Conservatives argue that fragmented oversight—where some medications slip through cracks while bureaucrats expand regulations elsewhere—represents government failure at its most dangerous. When the system can't track what doctors prescribe, adding more red tape for law-abiding patients and physicians solves nothing.

The real issue: psychiatrists prescribing multiple powerful drugs without adequate coordination or monitoring creates genuine public safety risks. Rather than expanding government control over prescribing practices, Colorado should demand transparency and accountability from medical boards and insurers to ensure dangerous drug combinations are caught *before* tragedy strikes.

Colorado taxpayers and families deserve a functioning system that actually prevents harm—not regulatory theater that burdens doctors while missing the obvious.

Watch whether Colorado's medical board tightens PDMP requirements or continues relying on a patchwork that failed the Clancy family.
