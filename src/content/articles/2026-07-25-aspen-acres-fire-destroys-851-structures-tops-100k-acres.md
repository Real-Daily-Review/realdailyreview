# Colorado's Largest Wildfire Rages as Heat Dome Fuels Disaster

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire—now the state's largest active wildfire—has torched over 100,000 acres and destroyed 851 structures, with crews battling dangerous conditions as a heat dome pushes temperatures above 100 degrees across the region.

As of Saturday, July 25, the fire had consumed 101,738 acres and reached 61% containment, according to the Northern Rockies Complex Incident Management Team. Some 945 personnel are actively fighting the blaze in Southern Colorado (KDVR). The disaster comes as a dangerous heat dome expands across the central U.S., creating warm and dry conditions that threaten to spark additional lightning-caused fires in the area (Colorado Sun).

The scale of destruction underscores a critical question conservatives and taxpayers should demand answered: **What forest management and land-use policies failed to prevent this catastrophe?** Years of federal mismanagement, environmental litigation blocking controlled burns, and regulatory gridlock have left Colorado's forests as tinderboxes. While firefighters deserve full support, policymakers must confront whether progressive environmental policies prioritizing litigation over active forest stewardship have made Coloradans less safe.

The state's emergency response has been swift, but the underlying cause—overgrown, fuel-choked forests on federal and state land—demands accountability and policy reform, not just crisis management.

Watch for how state and federal officials respond to calls for aggressive forest thinning and controlled burns to prevent the next megafire.
