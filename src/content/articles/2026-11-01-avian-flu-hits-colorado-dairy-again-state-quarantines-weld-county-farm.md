# Avian Flu Returns to Colorado Dairy—State Quarantines Weld County Operation

Another avian flu case has surfaced at a Colorado dairy, marking the first detection since the 2024 outbreak that roiled the state's milk supply and triggered price spikes at grocery stores. State health officials have quarantined a Weld County operation to contain spread of the virus (9News Denver).

Colorado's dairy industry—a $2.7 billion economic engine—faces renewed uncertainty. The 2024 outbreak cost producers millions and left consumers paying more at checkout. State officials stress that pasteurized milk remains safe, but the quarantine underscores how vulnerable agricultural operations remain to disease outbreaks that neither farmers nor regulators fully control.

Conservatives argue that heavy-handed government mandates and regulatory overreach often hamper agricultural producers' ability to respond quickly to crises. Rather than micromanaging farms, policymakers should trust industry expertise and market incentives to drive biosecurity improvements. Colorado's ranchers and dairy operators don't need more red tape—they need clarity, reasonable quarantine protocols, and swift resolution.

The state's response will test whether officials learned from 2024's missteps or will repeat them. Taxpayers and consumers should watch whether quarantine orders remain proportionate and science-based, or devolve into the kind of regulatory theater that damages livelihoods without meaningfully stopping disease spread.
