# College Football Playoff Explodes to 24 Teams—Conference Championships Axed in $1.7B ESPN Deal

The College Football Playoff is ditching tradition for dollars, expanding to 24 teams starting in 2027 under a $1.7 billion media rights agreement with ESPN that eliminates Power 4 conference championship games entirely (9News Denver, New York Post, Breitbart).

The move prioritizes ESPN's bottom line over competitive integrity. By eliminating conference title games, the deal strips away meaningful regular-season stakes and rewards mediocre teams with playoff berths simply for showing up. Conference championships once separated elite programs from pretenders; now, a 24-team field means nearly everyone gets in.

For Colorado, this matters. The Buffaloes and other Big 12 programs lose the leverage that conference championship games provided—both for playoff positioning and for generating revenue that flows back to athletic departments and student-athlete support. The consolidation of playoff media rights under one ESPN umbrella also reduces competitive bidding that might have benefited schools and conferences.

The deal reflects corporate sports' relentless expansion logic: more teams, more games, more eyeballs, more ad revenue. Fans lose drama; ESPN wins big.

Watch whether the Big 12, ACC, and other conferences push back on revenue-sharing terms or whether they simply accept ESPN's terms as the new reality of college sports consolidation.
