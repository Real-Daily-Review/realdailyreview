# Colorado Good Samaritan Rescues Stray Puppy From Rattlesnake Bite

A Weld County Good Samaritan stepped in where government won't—rescuing a 4-week-old stray puppy from a suspected rattlesnake bite and nursing it back to health through emergency surgery and weeks of care.

The puppy, found suffering in the field, required immediate veterinary intervention to survive the venom exposure. Thanks to the rescuer's quick action and community support, the animal recovered fully and has since found a permanent home (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The story underscores a reality Colorado conservatives know well: private charity and individual initiative often accomplish what bureaucracies cannot. No government agency was needed here—just one person's compassion and willingness to act. The rescuer absorbed the costs and effort voluntarily, demonstrating the power of civil society over state intervention.

While heartwarming, the incident also highlights the broader problem of stray animal populations in rural Colorado counties, where limited resources and vast territory make government animal control ineffective. Local communities and private rescue networks consistently fill the gap that county services cannot.

**Watch for:** whether Weld County commissioners will use this story to justify expanded (and costly) animal control budgets, or whether they'll recognize that private rescue networks already work better.
