# Colorado Voters Face Two Ballot Measures That Could Reshape State Politics and Energy Policy

Colorado voters this fall will decide whether to handcuff their own legislature through redistricting restrictions and whether to constitutionally protect natural gas use—two measures that pit voter autonomy against partisan and regulatory overreach.

Initiative #256 would ban congressional redistricting designed to favor either political party. Supporters argue the measure prevents gerrymandering and protects competitive districts. Republicans counter that such guardrails could lock in Democratic advantages in a state trending blue, while Democrats worry the measure might expose their current map to legal challenge.

Amendment 82 takes aim at progressive energy mandates by enshrining Coloradans' right to cook and heat with natural gas in their homes and businesses. The measure responds to left-wing efforts to phase out gas appliances—a regulatory push that would force costly retrofits on Colorado families and businesses already squeezed by inflation and rising utility costs. Conservatives argue the amendment protects consumer choice and energy independence; progressives claim it undermines climate goals.

Both measures reflect Colorado's growing frustration with government overreach—whether through partisan mapmaking or energy-use restrictions. The real question: will voters choose freedom over the left's vision of centralized control?

Watch whether Democrats and environmental groups mount a serious campaign against Amendment 82, signaling how aggressively they'll push gas bans if voters reject constitutional protection.
