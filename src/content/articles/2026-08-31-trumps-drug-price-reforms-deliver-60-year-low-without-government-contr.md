# Trump's Drug Price Reforms Deliver Real Relief to Colorado Families

Trump administration moves to rein in pharmacy benefit managers are producing the steepest prescription cost drops in six decades, offering Colorado families genuine relief at the pharmacy counter without government price controls.

According to the Washington Examiner, specific medications have plummeted from $1,000 to $149—a dramatic shift achieved through transparency rules and PBM reform rather than the left's favored approach of direct government price-setting. The policy targets the middlemen (pharmacy benefit managers) who have long inflated costs through opaque rebate schemes and formulary manipulation.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue this market-based approach works. By exposing PBM kickback schemes and forcing transparency, competition naturally drives prices down. No bureaucrats rationing care. No price caps strangling innovation. Just sunlight disinfecting a broken system.

**Democrats' counter:** The left typically favors Medicare price negotiation and regulatory caps, claiming market reforms move too slowly. They worry that transparency alone won't solve affordability for all Americans.

**Colorado angle:** Coloradans on fixed incomes and small-business owners buying employee health plans stand to save thousands annually. This hits home for rural Coloradans especially, where pharmacy options are limited and costs have been crushing.

Watch whether these price drops hold as PBM lobbying intensifies—and whether Colorado's Democratic legislature attempts to override federal reforms with its own price-control schemes.
