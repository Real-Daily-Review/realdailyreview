# Trump Administration Weighs Venezuela Oil Deal—But at What Cost to American Energy Independence?

The Trump administration is reportedly considering a deal to access Venezuelan oil reserves, a move that raises hard questions about whether Washington should rely on hostile regimes when American shale production remains the world's most efficient energy source.

According to *Just The News*, the rationale centers on long-term energy security: as domestic shale wells naturally decline over time, diversifying foreign reserves could sustain U.S. energy dominance. But *National Review* flags the deeper problem—such deals typically require abandoning American companies' legitimate claims to seized assets and legitimizing regimes that have stolen from U.S. investors.

**The conservative case against this:** Propping up Venezuela's authoritarian government undermines the rule of law that has made America prosperous. Colorado's oil and gas workers—already facing regulatory headwinds from the Biden years—deserve a government that champions domestic energy development, not one that cuts backroom deals with socialist dictators to compensate for declining shale output.

**The energy angle:** Colorado's energy sector depends on stable, predictable policy favoring American production. Outsourcing energy security to Venezuela signals Washington lacks confidence in domestic innovation and investment.

Republicans argue the administration should instead double down on permitting reform and investment in U.S. shale, not negotiate with hostile regimes.

**Watch:** Whether this deal advances and what concessions it demands from American energy companies already operating under Colorado's tightening regulations.
