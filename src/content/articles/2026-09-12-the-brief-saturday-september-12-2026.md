# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, September 12, 2026

The nation paused yesterday to mark 25 years since September 11 — but not everyone paused with the same reverence. Meanwhile, Colorado taxpayers have fresh reasons to stay alert as government accountability failures and national political fault lines hit close to home.

---

## Trump Leads Nation in 9/11 Remembrance — While the Left Stumbles

President Trump honored the heroes of September 11 at a Pentagon ceremony Friday, calling on Americans never to forget the lives lost and the courage shown that day. The contrast with the political left was stark: Democratic New York City mayoral candidate Zohran Mamdani drew sharp criticism from victims' families and first responders for his Ground Zero appearance, while progressive commentator Abdul El-Sayed ignited a firestorm by sharing a 9/11 message tied to Hasan Piker — a figure notorious for claiming America deserved the attacks. Twenty-five years on, some on the left still can't get this right.

---

## 9/11 at 25: Have We Kept the Memory Alive?

The solemn anniversary raises a harder question: as the generation with no living memory of that morning grows larger, are we doing enough to pass on the lessons of September 11? Conservatives argue that remembrance isn't just ritual — it's a national security obligation. For Colorado families who lost loved ones, for the military and first-responder communities that anchor so much of this state, forgetting is not an option. The left's cultural drift toward moral equivalence and anti-American grievance politics makes that preservation work harder, and more urgent.

## Inflation Holds Stubbornly at 3.4% — Midterms in Sight

The Commerce Department released August inflation figures Friday showing the year-over-year rate unchanged at 3.4%. Republicans argue that persistent inflation is the direct legacy of runaway federal spending under Democratic leadership — and that American families, including Colorado households, are still paying the price at the grocery store, the gas pump, and the hardware store. With midterm elections approaching, conservatives say voters should hold accountable every politician who voted for the spending sprees that lit this fire. The numbers don't lie, and neither does your monthly budget.

## Massachusetts Judge Rebuked for Shielding Illegal Immigrant from ICE

Massachusetts' highest court publicly reprimanded Judge Shelley Joseph after she allegedly helped an illegal immigrant evade Immigration and Customs Enforcement in her courtroom. Republicans and law enforcement advocates say this is exactly what happens when sanctuary-state ideology seeps into the judiciary — officers of the court actively undermining federal immigration law. Colorado conservatives know this problem isn't confined to New England: Colorado's own sanctuary policies have long drawn fire from sheriffs and law enforcement leaders who say they tie the hands of the people sworn to protect the public.

---

## Denver PD DNA Scandal: 13 Cases Under Review

FOX31 has learned that the Denver Police Department is re-testing 13 cases linked to former Colorado Bureau of Investigation forensic DNA analyst Yvonne "Missy" Woods. The revelation raises serious questions about the integrity of Colorado's criminal justice system and the oversight mechanisms — or lack thereof — inside state law enforcement agencies. Conservatives and crime victims' advocates will rightly demand full transparency: who knew what, when did they know it, and how many convictions or acquittals may have been tainted by compromised evidence.

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the inflation numbers heading into the fall campaign season — if 3.4% holds or rises, it becomes the defining issue of the midterms and a direct indictment of big-government spending. Watch also for any Colorado legislative response to the Denver DNA scandal; accountability in law enforcement is a conservative value, and state lawmakers should be pressed to answer for how this was allowed to happen. And as the 9/11 anniversary recedes from the headlines, watch which politicians treat it as a photo opportunity and which ones treat it as a solemn duty — the difference tells you everything.
