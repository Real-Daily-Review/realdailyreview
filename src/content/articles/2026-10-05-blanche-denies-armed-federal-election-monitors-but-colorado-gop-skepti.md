# Federal Election Monitors Won't Be Armed, AG Blanche Says—But Colorado Republicans Remain Skeptical

Attorney General Todd Blanche flatly rejected claims that armed federal agents will monitor polling places during midterm elections, calling the notion "completely false" in a Fox News Sunday appearance (Washington Examiner, Breitbart). Blanche emphasized that DOJ election monitoring is routine practice dating back to the 1960s and not a new initiative.

Republicans and Colorado conservatives, however, remain wary of expanded federal presence at the polls. The core concern: even unarmed federal monitors can chill voter confidence and create the appearance of government overreach at the ballot box—a sensitivity heightened after years of election integrity debates. Colorado, which conducts mail-in voting statewide, has its own election security protocols; federal monitors could signal distrust in state-run systems that have operated without major incident.

Blanche's reassurance may satisfy some, but conservatives argue the real question isn't whether monitors carry weapons—it's whether Washington should be second-guessing Colorado's election administration at all. State officials and local election judges have managed Colorado ballots for decades. Adding federal eyes to that process, armed or not, feels like Washington asserting control over turf that belongs to the states.
