# Trump's Iran Conflict Threatens to Overshadow Economic Agenda Ahead of Midterms

President Trump is doubling down on military action against Iran even as the five-month conflict threatens to derail his core economic message before November's midterm elections.

Trump told supporters the Iran situation will "peter out" and vowed the U.S. will "win," but acknowledged the fighting could extend through the election cycle (Fox News). However, conservative critics warn the prolonged conflict is becoming a political liability. National Review argues that by pursuing both aggressive tariffs and sustained military operations, Trump has "forfeited his signature issue of economic stewardship"—the very platform that energized his base.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado defense contractors and energy firms face uncertainty as military spending priorities shift. Meanwhile, the administration's tariff strategy directly impacts Colorado agriculture, manufacturing, and energy exports—sectors already watching how long Trump can maintain dual focus on foreign conflict and domestic economic policy.

Republicans generally support strong Iran deterrence, but fiscal conservatives worry about open-ended military commitments that drain resources from tax relief and deregulation. The question for Trump's midterm strategy: Can he thread the needle between projecting strength abroad and delivering tangible economic wins at home?

**Watch:** Whether Trump can refocus messaging on economic gains before Labor Day, or if Iran dominance continues bleeding into campaign season.
