# Colorado Parks and Wildlife Halts Wolf Killings, Leaving Ranchers Exposed

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has suspended lethal removal of wolves that attack livestock, citing low adult survival rates and falling pup counts—a move that puts the state's ranching community in the crosshairs of environmental policy.

CPW announced the temporary halt Wednesday after evaluating biological factors including translocation efforts and survival data (9News Denver). The agency says it will "continue to work with livestock producers to mitigate wolf attacks," but offers no concrete alternative to protect herds from predation.

**The ranching angle:** Colorado's livestock producers already face mounting losses to wolf predation since reintroduction. By suspending lethal removal, CPW prioritizes wolf population recovery over the economic survival of family ranches—a classic clash between environmental mandates and rural livelihoods. Ranchers argue they're being asked to absorb the costs of a policy they didn't choose.

The Colorado Sun reports CPW is concerned about lower-than-normal adult wolf survival rates and pup survival rates expected to fall further. But conservatives question whether government-imposed predator protection should override property rights and agricultural viability. If wolves are thriving enough to attack livestock regularly, the biological justification for a killing moratorium rings hollow to rural Coloradans.
