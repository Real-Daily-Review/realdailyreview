# Autism Providers Back Trump's Medicaid Fraud Crackdown—But Demand Real Care Access

Legitimate autism care providers are throwing their weight behind the Trump administration's Medicaid fraud investigation, arguing that millions allegedly diverted by bad actors should have gone directly to children with autism (Fox News Politics). The New York Post reports that providers emphasize every stolen dollar represents care a child never received—therapy, communication support, independence training.

The crackdown targets systemic theft from Medicaid, the joint federal-state health program that covers many autism services. Colorado taxpayers and families should note: Medicaid fraud drains resources meant for vulnerable children while inflating program costs that states like Colorado must help fund.

Providers, however, are sounding a critical warning: they want fraud eliminated *without* creating new regulatory barriers that make it harder for families to access legitimate services. The concern is real—aggressive enforcement can inadvertently slow approvals and increase red tape for parents already navigating a complex system.

Republicans support rooting out waste and fraud as a core principle of responsible government. The challenge is doing so without punishing honest providers or families desperate for care.

Watch whether Colorado's Medicaid administrators implement fraud controls that actually protect access, or whether bureaucratic overhead becomes the real barrier to autistic children getting help.
