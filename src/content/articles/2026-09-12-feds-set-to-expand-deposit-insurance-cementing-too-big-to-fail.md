# Federal Regulators Poised to Expand Deposit Insurance, Rewarding Bank Recklessness

Washington is about to codify the "too big to fail" doctrine that bailed out Silicon Valley Bank depositors three years ago—and Colorado taxpayers will foot the bill for Washington's moral hazard.

The Federal Reserve and FDIC rescued *every* Silicon Valley Bank depositor in 2023, including those holding balances far above the standard $250,000 insurance limit, fearing contagion elsewhere. Now regulators are moving to make that rescue permanent through expanded deposit protection, according to the Washington Examiner.

Conservatives argue this is a dangerous expansion of government backstops for financial recklessness. By guaranteeing deposits beyond the statutory limit, regulators remove the market discipline that forces banks to manage risk responsibly. Large depositors—including venture capitalists and tech firms—face no penalty for parking uninsured funds in risky institutions. Meanwhile, Main Street savers and small-business owners who play by the rules subsidize the rescue through inflation and future bailout costs.

The Examiner notes this amounts to a "$5 million upgrade" to the existing scam, cementing a two-tier system where connected elites enjoy government protection while ordinary Americans bear the risk.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republican members—will oppose this expansion or cave to banking-lobby pressure.
