# Northwest Stadium Power Failure Delays Commanders-Bengals Thriller

A brief power outage at Northwest Stadium during Monday's third quarter between the Washington Commanders and Cincinnati Bengals interrupted play and raised questions about the facility's infrastructure reliability. The lights cut out mid-game, forcing a delay while crews restored power.

The Commanders ultimately prevailed on a dramatic walk-off field goal, with rookie quarterback Jayden Daniels orchestrating a 64-yard drive in 34 seconds to set up Drew Stevens' game-winning 26-yard kick as time expired (New York Post).

While the outage was temporary and didn't derail the game's conclusion, it highlights a broader concern: aging stadium infrastructure across the nation. For Colorado sports fans and taxpayers, this serves as a cautionary tale about the hidden costs of public stadium financing. Denver's own sports venues have required substantial public investment and ongoing maintenance—expenses that often fall on local taxpayers rather than team owners or private operators.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives have long argued that public funding for sports stadiums represents wasteful government spending that diverts resources from core services like roads, schools, and public safety. The Northwest Stadium incident underscores why private operators should bear full responsibility for facility upkeep and modernization.

Watch whether Northwest Stadium's ownership addresses infrastructure vulnerabilities before the playoffs—and whether Colorado lawmakers learn the lesson about limiting public exposure in future stadium deals.
