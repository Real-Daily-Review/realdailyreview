# Pagosa Springs Issues Evacuation Order as Flooding Threatens Southwest Colorado

Pagosa Springs ordered mandatory evacuations Saturday evening for flood-prone areas as heavy rain threatens the San Juan and Piedra river valleys in Archuleta County. Forecasters predict up to 3 inches of rain across most of the county, raising serious flood risk for low-lying communities.

The evacuation underscores a persistent challenge for Colorado mountain towns: aging infrastructure and development in flood-prone zones. While state and local officials manage the immediate emergency response, conservatives argue that years of environmental regulations have restricted water management solutions—including dam maintenance and stream-clearing projects—that could mitigate flooding risk.

Pagosa Springs and surrounding Archuleta County rely heavily on tourism and outdoor recreation. Repeated flood events strain local budgets and divert resources from core services. Republicans have long contended that overly restrictive environmental rules prevent communities from implementing practical flood-control measures, forcing taxpayers to absorb disaster costs instead.

Residents in affected areas should monitor evacuation orders closely and follow local emergency management guidance. Watch whether state regulators will fast-track permits for flood-mitigation infrastructure—or whether environmental review delays will once again hamstring Colorado's ability to protect its citizens.
