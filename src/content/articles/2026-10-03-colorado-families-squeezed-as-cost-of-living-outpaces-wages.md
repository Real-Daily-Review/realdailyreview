# Cost of Living Crisis Hits Home for Colorado Families

While Washington celebrates economic growth, Coloradans are feeling the squeeze at the pump and grocery store—and wages aren't keeping up.

The disconnect between headline GDP numbers and kitchen-table reality is stark. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the cost-of-living crisis is hitting families nationwide, with grocery and gas prices outpacing wage growth across all political affiliations. That pain is real in Colorado too, where energy costs, food prices, and housing expenses have climbed faster than paychecks.

Conservatives argue that government spending, inflation, and regulatory overreach—particularly Biden-Harris administration policies on energy production and supply chains—have fueled this squeeze. Republicans point to restrictive permitting on Colorado oil and gas development, which limits domestic energy supply and keeps prices elevated. Meanwhile, progressive policies driving up labor costs and business regulations trickle down to consumers at checkout.

Democrats counter that the economy has recovered strongly post-pandemic and that wage growth is occurring in many sectors. They blame corporate profit-taking and supply-chain disruptions for persistent inflation.

The reality: Colorado families are choosing between groceries and gas. Energy workers and small-business owners face tighter margins. Renters and first-time homebuyers are priced out.
