# Thomas Warns Court on Free Speech, Pushes Second Amendment Review

Justice Clarence Thomas is sounding the alarm on two fronts that matter to Colorado conservatives: government compulsion of speech and the erosion of Second Amendment protections.

In a recent statement, Thomas criticized the Supreme Court's refusal to hear a California case involving implicit bias training, warning that states could use such rulings to force private citizens into government-mandated speech (Fox News Politics). The conservative justice argues this opens the door to unconstitutional compulsion—a concern that resonates with Colorado business owners and employers already navigating progressive workplace mandates.

Meanwhile, Thomas is pushing the Court to take up "sensitive places" gun restrictions in coming terms (Washington Examiner), even as the justices prepare to hear a major Second Amendment case on state assault weapons bans next month. Colorado gun owners should pay attention: the state's own magazine restrictions and permit requirements could face renewed constitutional scrutiny depending on how the Court rules.

**The conservative argument:** Thomas's warnings reflect a broader concern that courts are allowing states to regulate speech and gun rights in ways that contradict individual liberty. Democrats and progressives counter that such regulations serve compelling public interests—workplace equity and public safety—but conservatives argue those interests don't justify trampling constitutional rights.

**Watch next:** Whether the Supreme Court accepts Thomas's invitation to review "sensitive places" doctrine, which could reshape Colorado's gun laws statewide.
