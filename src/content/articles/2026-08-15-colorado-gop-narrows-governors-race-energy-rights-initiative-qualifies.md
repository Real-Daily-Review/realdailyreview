# Colorado Republicans Narrow Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their gubernatorial field to just two candidates advancing from the state assembly, signaling a sharper focus ahead of the general election—while voters will simultaneously decide whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution.

Out of 18 Republican gubernatorial hopefuls, only two cleared the assembly threshold, according to *Complete Colorado*. The streamlined field reflects the party's effort to consolidate behind viable nominees as Democrats continue pushing aggressive climate and energy regulations.

Meanwhile, Initiative 177—backed by Advance Colorado—has qualified for the ballot, giving Colorado voters a direct say on constitutional protection for natural gas development. The measure represents a conservative pushback against the state's progressive energy agenda, which has increasingly restricted fossil fuel operations and investment.

Democrats and environmental groups oppose the initiative, arguing it would lock in natural gas infrastructure and undermine Colorado's climate commitments. Republicans and energy advocates counter that the measure protects property rights, energy independence, and the livelihoods of thousands of Colorado workers in the oil and gas sector—while ensuring affordable, reliable power for families and businesses.

The dual developments—a tightened GOP primary and a ballot fight over energy rights—underscore 2026 as a referendum on Colorado's regulatory direction and economic priorities.

**Watch:** whether the natural gas initiative gains traction with suburban and rural voters, and how the two advancing GOP candidates position themselves on energy and regulatory reform.
