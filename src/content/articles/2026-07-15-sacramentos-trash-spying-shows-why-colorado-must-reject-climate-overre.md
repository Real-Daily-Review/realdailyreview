# Colorado Cities Dodge California-Style Trash Surveillance—For Now

While Sacramento, California descends into Orwellian waste management, Colorado's municipalities have largely avoided the government snooping that comes with aggressive climate mandates. Sacramento city workers now inspect residents' garbage bins to enforce SB 1383, a 2016 state law requiring organic waste diversion (The Daily Wire). The city justifies the intrusion as necessary to meet state climate goals—a cautionary tale for Colorado taxpayers watching similar regulatory creep.

Colorado has resisted the most draconian waste-policing schemes, but progressives in Denver and Boulder continue pushing mandatory composting and recycling mandates that inch toward Sacramento's model. Republicans argue these programs waste taxpayer money on bureaucratic enforcement while delivering minimal environmental benefit. Conservatives say the real issue is government overreach: when officials claim climate emergencies justify inspecting private property, individual liberty takes a back seat.

The lesson for Colorado: today's "voluntary" sustainability initiative becomes tomorrow's trash-bin surveillance state. Denver and other blue-led cities should resist the temptation to follow California's playbook, which trades privacy and property rights for dubious environmental gains.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature attempts to impose statewide organic waste mandates modeled on California's approach.
