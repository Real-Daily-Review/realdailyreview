# Democratic Fracture Over Israel Exposes Party's Antisemitism Problem

A significant rift has opened within Michigan's Democratic Party after Rep. Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, citing El-Sayed's record on Israel as disqualifying. Stevens, a former Democratic Senate candidate, broke ranks publicly over what *National Review* describes as El-Sayed's "hatred of Israel"—a move that has triggered fierce backlash from party leadership.

El-Sayed's campaign has capitalized on the controversy, raising $1.2 million following Stevens's endorsement of Rogers, according to the *Daily Caller*. The fundraising surge suggests El-Sayed's base remains energized despite—or perhaps because of—the intraparty conflict.

Republicans see the episode as exposing Democratic hypocrisy. The *Washington Examiner* argues that Democrats, after years of championing "democracy" and "women's voices," swiftly moved to "scold, demonize, and vilify" Stevens for following her conscience on a core issue. *National Review* reports that Stevens is far from alone; multiple Democrats harbor serious reservations about El-Sayed's positions.

**The Colorado angle:** While this fight plays out in Michigan, it reflects national Democratic divisions over antisemitism and Israel that could ripple through Colorado's own competitive races and donor networks.
