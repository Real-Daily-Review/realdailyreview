# FAA Grounds New Equipment After Botched Modernization; Duffy Eyes $50B Bailout

The Federal Aviation Administration has paused router installations after its own modernization effort triggered a nationwide flight shutdown last week—and now Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy is asking Congress for $50 billion to overhaul aging systems (9News Denver). The irony is sharp: the agency's attempt to fix decrepit infrastructure backfired spectacularly, grounding all U.S. flights for over four hours Friday before officials traced the failure to equipment replacements on those same aging systems (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative concern is straightforward.** The FAA has a track record of mismanaging technology upgrades and bloated budgets. Before throwing another $50 billion at the problem—taxpayer money—Congress should demand accountability: Why did modernization fail so catastrophically? What oversight failed? And why should Americans trust the same agency with a massive new spending package when it can't execute basic infrastructure replacement without paralyzing the nation's airspace?

Colorado's aviation industry, from Denver International Airport operations to regional carriers, felt the impact directly. The ground stop exposed not just technical incompetence but systemic risk in critical infrastructure managed by a federal agency resistant to private-sector efficiency standards.
