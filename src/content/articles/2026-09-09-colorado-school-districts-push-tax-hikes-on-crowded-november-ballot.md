# Colorado Voters Face Crowded November Ballot as Districts Push Tax Hikes

Colorado school districts are asking taxpayers to approve millions in mill levy overrides this November—a tax-hike push that arrives amid a cost-of-living crisis and a ballot already cluttered with other measures (Denver Post).

The timing is brutal for districts. Voters are stretched thin financially, and competing initiatives—including a proposed ban on partisan congressional redistricting (Initiative #256)—will fragment attention on an already crowded ballot. Districts must make their case for higher property taxes while families struggle with inflation and stagnant wages.

Conservatives argue that before asking taxpayers for more money, school districts should demonstrate fiscal discipline and explain how existing revenue is being spent. The cost-of-living crisis has hit Colorado families hard, and adding property tax burdens now feels tone-deaf to household budgets already under pressure.

Meanwhile, unaffiliated voters—a growing bloc in Colorado—remain underutilized in primary elections, where party-registered voters still hold outsized power (Complete Colorado). That dynamic could shift the outcome of November's measures if turnout patterns change.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado voters approve mill levy overrides or reject them as a sign of taxpayer fatigue—and whether Initiative #256 passes, reshaping how congressional districts are drawn.
