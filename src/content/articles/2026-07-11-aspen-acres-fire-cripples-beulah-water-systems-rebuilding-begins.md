# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has dealt a serious blow to water utilities serving Beulah, a small Colorado community of roughly 800 residents, forcing water managers into emergency recovery mode (Colorado Sun). While most active fires in Colorado have spared critical water systems, this one didn't—leaving residents facing both immediate supply challenges and a lengthy reconstruction process.

The damage raises hard questions about Colorado's wildfire preparedness and the resilience of rural water infrastructure. As communities rebuild, taxpayers will foot the bill for repairs to systems that should have been hardened against predictable risks. The state's regulatory approach to water management and land use—often prioritizing environmental restrictions over fire mitigation—deserves scrutiny.

CPR News reports that residents of the close-knit community "fought back," but individual courage cannot substitute for adequate infrastructure investment and sensible forest management policies. Colorado's progressive environmental regulations have, in some cases, hindered the kind of active forest thinning and fuel reduction that could prevent fires like this from reaching critical utilities in the first place.

Watch whether state lawmakers acknowledge that overregulation of forestry and water infrastructure has left rural Colorado vulnerable—or whether they simply throw more taxpayer money at the problem without addressing root causes.
