# RNC Challenges Colorado's Overseas Voter Residency Rule

The Republican National Committee is suing Secretary of State Jena Griswold over Colorado election rules that allow people who have never lived in the state to vote by claiming residency through a parent or spouse.

According to Complete Colorado, the RNC's complaint argues the practice is unconstitutional. The lawsuit targets what Republicans view as a backdoor to ballot access for individuals with no genuine Colorado ties—a concern that cuts to the heart of election integrity debates in a swing state where margins matter.

Griswold, a Democrat, has championed expansive voting access policies. Republicans counter that residency requirements exist for a reason: to ensure voters have a legitimate stake in the communities and policies they're voting on. Allowing proxy residency claims through family members, they argue, opens the door to out-of-state manipulation of Colorado elections.

The case will test whether Colorado courts agree that the state's interpretation of voter residency passes constitutional muster. For Colorado taxpayers and election officials already managing mail-in voting at scale, the ruling could reshape who gets to cast ballots in future races—including the 2026 midterms.

Watch whether Griswold's office mounts a vigorous defense or quietly revises the rule to avoid courtroom defeat.
