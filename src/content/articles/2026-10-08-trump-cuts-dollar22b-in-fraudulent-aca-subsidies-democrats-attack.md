# Trump Admin Cuts $2.2B in Fraudulent ACA Subsidies; Democrats Cry Foul

Vice President JD Vance announced the Trump administration will terminate subsidy payments for 760,000 fraudulent health insurance enrollments on federally administered Affordable Care Act exchanges, eliminating $2.2 billion in annual taxpayer waste.

Democrats are already attacking the move, claiming it strips healthcare from deserving Americans and will raise costs for others. But conservatives argue the real problem isn't enforcement—it's that the ACA's design enabled massive fraud in the first place. The Washington Examiner editorial board notes that a healthcare system dependent on fraudulent enrollments is fundamentally broken and that taxpayers shouldn't foot the bill for waste.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado operates its own state-based ACA exchange (Connect for Health Colorado), but many Coloradans use the federal marketplace. Eliminating fraudulent subsidies could affect coverage in the state, though the extent depends on how many of those 760,000 fraudulent enrollees are Colorado residents.

Conservatives argue this is basic fiscal responsibility—rooting out waste before expanding government programs. Democrats counter that enforcement actions will harm vulnerable populations, though they haven't addressed why fraud prevention equals coverage denial for legitimate enrollees.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado's state exchange faces pressure to adopt similar fraud-detection measures, and how many state residents are affected by the federal subsidy cuts.
