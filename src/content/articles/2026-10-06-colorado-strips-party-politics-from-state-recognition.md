# Colorado Moves to Strip Party Privileges From State Recognition

Colorado is taking a principled stand against government favoritism by separating state honors from political party affiliation—a move conservatives say should have happened long ago.

According to Complete Colorado, the state government has no legitimate business granting special recognition or privileges tied to political parties. The principle is straightforward: taxpayer-funded state institutions should remain neutral ground, not platforms for partisan advantage.

This matters in Colorado because state holidays, commemorations, and official recognitions carry real weight. When the legislature created Cabrini Day in 2020—the first state holiday honoring a woman—it set a precedent for how the state chooses to spend symbolic and administrative resources (9News Denver). The question becomes: on what basis should Colorado grant these honors? Merit and historical significance, or party alignment?

Conservatives argue that government at all levels should stick to core functions and avoid entanglement with partisan machinery. State recognition should reflect shared Colorado values and history, not reward political allies or punish opponents.

The broader implication: if Colorado can cleanse state honors of party favoritism, why not apply the same principle to regulatory agencies, hiring practices, and contract awards?

Watch whether the legislature follows through on this commitment or quietly reverts to partisan patronage once media attention fades.
