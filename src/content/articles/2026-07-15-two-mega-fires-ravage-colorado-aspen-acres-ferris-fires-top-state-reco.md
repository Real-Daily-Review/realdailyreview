# Colorado's Wildfire Season Spirals: Two Mega-Fires Now Among State's Largest on Record

Colorado is battling two catastrophic wildfires that have shattered state records and left hundreds of families homeless. The Aspen Acres Fire in southern Colorado has burned nearly 99,000 acres and destroyed over 851 structures, ranking it among the state's largest fires in history (9News). The Ferris Fire in southwest Colorado has torched more than 64,000 acres and also cracked the top 10 largest wildfires ever recorded in Colorado (9News). The Aspen Acres Fire remains only 36% contained and continues threatening the town of Rye, with a new command team now overseeing operations (Colorado Sun).

Conservatives and property owners are asking hard questions about forest management. Years of federal mismanagement—overgrown forests choked with dead timber, delayed thinning projects, and bureaucratic gridlock—have created tinderboxes across Colorado's landscape. While Democrats often blame climate change alone, Republicans point to decades of failed land stewardship and the need for aggressive, science-based forest thinning and fuel reduction on both public and private lands.

Some evacuation orders have been lifted as roads reopen, but the Aspen Acres Fire ranks among the top five most destructive in Colorado history in terms of homes lost (CPR News).
