# Senate Kicks Government Funding Can Past Midterms—Again

Senate leaders have struck a deal to fund federal agencies through December 11, conveniently postponing any fiscal reckoning until after the midterm elections. The short-term spending bill avoids a shutdown during peak campaign season, but it's classic Washington: kicking hard decisions down the road while taxpayers foot the bill.

Republicans argue this approach perpetuates the dysfunction of continuing resolutions instead of forcing Congress to pass actual budgets. By punting past November, lawmakers avoid tough votes on spending levels and priorities—exactly when voters should be holding them accountable for fiscal discipline.

For Colorado, the implications are mixed. Federal spending flows through the state's defense contractors, universities, and water projects, so a shutdown would sting. But conservatives worry the real cost comes later: another temporary measure means another opportunity for Democrats to slip through spending increases or regulatory expansions without scrutiny.

The deal reflects a bipartisan desire to avoid shutdown optics during an election cycle—but it's taxpayers who lose. Congress should pass real budgets with real oversight, not theater that lets both parties escape accountability.

**Watch for what gets attached to the December funding bill: that's when the real spending fights—and the real costs to Colorado—will emerge.**
