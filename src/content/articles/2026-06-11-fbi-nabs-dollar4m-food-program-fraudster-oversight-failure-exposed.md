# Federal Food Program Fraud: $4M Stolen While Oversight Failed

The FBI's arrest of a "most wanted fraudster" accused of stealing $4 million from a federal child nutrition program exposes a hard truth: government welfare spending attracts criminals, and bureaucratic oversight is broken.

Said Abdullahi Ereg, 47, surrendered to federal authorities after being wanted since 2024 on fraud charges tied to what The Daily Wire reports as "the nation's largest COVID-era scam." He allegedly diverted funds meant for children in need to finance a lavish personal lifestyle—while the program's guardians looked the other way.

FBI Director Kash Patel announced the arrest Wednesday. The feds had posted a $150,000 reward for information leading to Ereg's capture (Daily Caller).

**The conservative takeaway:** This case underscores why Republicans consistently warn that massive federal spending programs—especially those expanded during COVID—create breeding grounds for fraud and waste. Billions in taxpayer dollars flow through loosely monitored channels, and criminals exploit the gaps. Colorado families and workers foot the bill for programs that fail their stated mission.

The arrest is a win, but it's also a symptom. Conservatives argue Washington needs fewer, smaller, and more auditable programs—not larger ones with better intentions.

Watch whether Congress uses this case to demand tighter controls on federal welfare spending or simply moves on.
