# Federal Judge Blocks Trump DOJ Demand for Colorado's Unredacted Voter Rolls

A federal judge has dismissed the Trump administration's lawsuit seeking access to Colorado's unredacted voter registration data, dealing a setback to the Justice Department's election integrity efforts in the state.

The judge ruled that the DOJ failed to justify why it needed sensitive voter information in unredacted form, according to reporting from the *Denver Post*, *Colorado Sun*, and CPR News. Secretary of State Jena Griswold, a Democrat, declared the ruling a "victory" that protects Coloradans' private data from federal overreach.

Republicans and Trump administration officials argue that voter roll audits are essential to identifying ineligible voters and ensuring election integrity. The DOJ's push for unredacted records reflects concerns about potential vulnerabilities in state voter databases—a priority for conservatives focused on election security and preventing fraud.

Democrats and state election officials counter that such broad federal demands for sensitive voter data threaten privacy and represent an inappropriate federal intrusion into state election administration. Griswold's office has maintained Colorado's voter rolls are secure and properly maintained.

The dismissal was issued "with prejudice," meaning the case cannot be refiled in its current form—a significant legal hurdle for any future federal effort to compel the data.
