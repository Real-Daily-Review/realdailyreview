# Democratic Primary Bloodbath: Socialist Wing Challenges Establishment, Colorado Voters Exhausted by Cash Deluge

Colorado's Democratic primary has devolved into a costly civil war between the party's socialist wing and its establishment, with outside groups dumping nearly half a million dollars into ads in the final stretch—leaving voters fed up with the spending circus.

The battle centers on Rep. Diana DeGette's seat, where progressive challenger Melat Kiros has energized the Democratic Socialist caucus (Daily Caller). Pro-Choice Majority Action alone spent nearly $460,000 on TV ads backing DeGette in recent weeks (Denver Post), turning what should be a routine primary into a scorched-earth campaign that has little to do with governing Colorado.

The spending spree has backfired politically. Denver voters, already cynical about politics, say the money-soaked primary has made them more distrustful. "This campaign has just become about the money," one Uptown resident told the Denver Post. The irony is sharp: Democrats claim to oppose "dark money" in politics, yet their own super PACs are flooding Colorado airwaves with six-figure ad buys that dwarf grassroots engagement.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, this primary reveals a deeper truth—the Left's internal divisions over socialism versus establishment liberalism are real, and neither side offers fiscal restraint or limited government. Watch whether this Democratic infighting carries into the general election and whether Colorado Republicans capitalize on voter disgust with the Left's money-drenched politics.
