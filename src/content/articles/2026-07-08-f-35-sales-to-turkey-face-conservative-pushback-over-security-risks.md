# Turkey Arms Deal Raises Red Flags for Defense Hawks

**Selling advanced F-35 fighter jets to Turkey would be a strategic blunder that undermines American security interests and emboldens a NATO ally with a troubling record of unreliability.**

The Trump administration is reportedly considering F-35 sales to Ankara, but conservative defense analysts are sounding the alarm. National Review warns that arming Turkey with America's most advanced stealth technology "would needlessly jeopardize American security and service personnel"—a fair concern given Turkey's history of cozying up to adversaries and its demonstrated willingness to play both sides.

The Washington Examiner's recent column cuts deeper: Turkey is no "great ally." The piece notes that President Trump himself acknowledged during his Ankara visit that Turkey "had contemplated joining the war against Israel, and, ultimately, the United States." That's not the behavior of a trusted partner deserving access to cutting-edge American weapons systems.

For Colorado defense contractors and workers who depend on F-35 production and supply chains, the real issue isn't whether Turkey gets jets—it's whether Washington will maintain the discipline to protect American technological superiority and allied relationships that actually matter.

Conservatives should watch whether Congress demands oversight hearings before any F-35 transfer moves forward.
