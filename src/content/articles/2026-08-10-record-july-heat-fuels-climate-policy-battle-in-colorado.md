# Record Heat in July Raises Stakes for Colorado Water and Energy Policy

The contiguous U.S. recorded its hottest month ever in July, with average temperatures hitting 76.9 degrees—roughly 3 degrees above the 20th-century average, according to NOAA data reported by the Washington Examiner and 9News Denver.

For Colorado, the implications are immediate and serious. Record heat intensifies pressure on already-strained water supplies in the West, threatening agricultural operations, hydroelectric generation, and municipal reserves. Conservatives warn that climate alarmism will be weaponized to justify expansive federal regulation of energy production and land use—exactly when Colorado needs *more* domestic energy development, not less.

Republicans argue that adaptation and innovation—not punitive climate mandates—should drive policy. Colorado's energy sector, which supports thousands of jobs and funds schools and infrastructure, cannot afford the kind of regulatory overreach being pushed by the Biden administration and progressive state lawmakers. Reasonable energy development, including natural gas and responsible oil and gas operations, remains essential to Colorado's economy and grid reliability.

Democrats will likely use this data to push for accelerated renewable mandates and restrictions on fossil fuels. Conservatives counter that such policies risk energy costs and job losses without meaningfully addressing global emissions.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's Democratic legislature uses this heat record to justify new energy taxes or regulations on oil and gas operators before the 2024 session ends.
