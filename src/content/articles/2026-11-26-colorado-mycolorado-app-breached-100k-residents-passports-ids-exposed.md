# Colorado's myColorado App Breached: 100,000 Residents' Sensitive Data Exposed

Colorado's state government has bungled the security of a digital platform holding some of the most sensitive personal documents Coloradans possess. The myColorado app, which stores passport scans, vehicle registrations, and insurance cards for an estimated 100,000 residents, suffered a cyberattack with no clear timeline for restoration, according to the Office of Information Technology (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The breach exposes a fundamental problem: government agencies routinely consolidate citizens' most valuable identity documents into centralized digital repositories, then fail to protect them adequately. When private companies suffer breaches of this magnitude, they face lawsuits and market consequences. State bureaucracies? They issue press releases and move on.

Colorado residents now face potential identity theft, fraud, and the headache of monitoring their financial accounts—all because state IT officials couldn't secure a basic government app. The state has provided no timetable for restoring the service, leaving Coloradans in limbo.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that expanding government digital infrastructure without corresponding security investment creates massive liability. This breach vindicates those concerns.

Watch for whether Colorado lawmakers demand accountability from the Office of Information Technology and whether they'll finally commit serious resources to cybersecurity—or simply hope voters forget before the next election.
