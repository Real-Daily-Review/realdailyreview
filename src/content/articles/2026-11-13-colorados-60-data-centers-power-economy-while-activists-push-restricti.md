# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Critics Ignore the Reality

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are quietly becoming the backbone of American digital infrastructure, yet progressive activists continue to treat them as environmental villains rather than economic engines. Complete Colorado reports that these facilities—which process everything from cloud computing to AI training—are essential to modern life, not obstacles to it.

The data center industry represents a massive opportunity for Colorado's economy and workforce. These facilities generate substantial tax revenue, create high-skilled jobs, and position the state as a critical player in the digital economy. Yet environmental groups and left-leaning policymakers often frame data centers as water-hogging, energy-intensive threats requiring heavy regulation.

Republicans and industry advocates counter that Colorado's data centers operate efficiently and responsibly while delivering real economic benefits to communities. The facilities attract investment, support local employment, and help keep Colorado competitive in the tech sector—all without the government picking winners and losers through burdensome regulation.

The challenge ahead: Colorado lawmakers must resist pressure to impose costly restrictions on data center development that could drive this industry—and its jobs and tax revenue—to friendlier states. Watch whether the legislature prioritizes business-friendly policy or bows to environmental pressure groups demanding new regulations.
