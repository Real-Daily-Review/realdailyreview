# House Passes Daylight Saving Time Bill, But Senate Skeptics Threaten to Kill It

The House has passed the Sunshine Protection Act to make daylight saving time permanent nationwide—a rare bipartisan move that could spare Americans the twice-yearly clock shuffle. But Senate opposition appears poised to derail the effort, according to 9News Denver.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. Permanent daylight saving time would mean later sunrises in winter months, affecting ranchers, outdoor workers, and school schedules across the state. Conservatives have long argued that such federal mandates override state and local flexibility—Colorado should decide its own time policy without Washington interference.

The House passed the measure with support from both parties, but Senate critics are signaling intent to sunset the bill before it reaches a vote. National Review notes the broader dysfunction: the Senate's real problem isn't rules, it's behavior—senators need to actually negotiate on hard issues rather than obstruct.

Republicans and conservatives should scrutinize whether this federal overreach serves Colorado's interests or simply replaces one government mandate with another. The real conservative position: let states and localities choose their own time standards without federal diktat.

Watch whether Colorado's Senate delegation—currently split between parties—will prioritize state autonomy or fall in line with national partisan pressure.
