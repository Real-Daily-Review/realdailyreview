# Teens' Red Rocks Detour Ends in Rescue Operation

Two teenagers learned an expensive lesson in Colorado mountain reality this week after deciding to hike back to Morrison from a Red Rocks concert instead of taking a ride—and getting hopelessly lost in the process.

West Metro Fire Rescue responded to the lost hikers on Wednesday, according to reports from KDVR Fox31 Denver and 9News. The teens' decision to trek back on foot rather than arrange transportation turned into a full rescue operation, tying up emergency resources that could have been deployed elsewhere.

The incident underscores a recurring problem in Colorado's outdoor recreation zones: inexperienced visitors underestimating terrain, darkness, and distance. Red Rocks sits in Morrison, a scenic area that draws crowds year-round, but the surrounding foothills can turn treacherous fast for unprepared hikers—especially after dark.

While no serious injuries were reported, the rescue highlights the hidden costs of poor planning. Emergency services in Colorado's metro areas handle hundreds of preventable calls annually from tourists and locals who treat mountain recreation casually. Taxpayers foot the bill for these operations, which divert firefighters and rescue personnel from genuine emergencies.

**Conservatives and outdoor enthusiasts should watch whether local officials use this incident to push for new regulations on Red Rocks access or hiking permits—a common government response that would burden legitimate visitors rather than address personal responsibility.**
