# Trump Pardons Six in Right-to-Repair Fight, Calls Biden Prosecutions 'Weaponization'

President Trump has granted full pardons to six individuals prosecuted under the Biden administration for right-to-repair violations, calling the cases an example of government overreach and "weaponization" of the justice system.

Trump did not immediately identify the pardoned individuals, but characterized their prosecutions as persecution for the act of "fixing their car." The move signals the new administration's skepticism toward aggressive enforcement of repair restrictions—a position that aligns with conservative arguments against regulatory barriers to independent mechanics and DIY repairs.

Right-to-repair advocates argue that manufacturers use copyright and patent law to lock consumers out of fixing their own equipment, inflating repair costs and limiting consumer choice. Republicans have increasingly backed right-to-repair legislation as a free-market issue: if you own it, you should be able to fix it without corporate gatekeeping.

The Biden administration had pursued these cases as part of broader intellectual-property enforcement. Democrats argue such prosecutions protect innovation and prevent counterfeiting, though critics contend the approach prioritizes corporate interests over consumer rights.

For Colorado—home to independent mechanics, farmers, and equipment owners who depend on repair flexibility—this signals a shift toward deregulation and consumer freedom over corporate-friendly IP enforcement.

Watch for whether Trump's Justice Department reverses or deprioritizes similar pending cases.
