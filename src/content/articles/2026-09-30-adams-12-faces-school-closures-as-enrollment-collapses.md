# Adams 12 Faces Final Reckoning on School Closures as District Grapples with Enrollment Collapse

Colorado's Adams 12 school district held its final public meeting this week before the board votes on proposed school closures—a decision driven by plummeting enrollment and budget pressures that reflect a broader crisis in public education management across the state.

The district's closure proposal underscores a hard reality: when enrollment drops, fixed costs don't. Adams 12 must either consolidate facilities or watch per-pupil spending spiral upward, squeezing resources for classrooms that remain open. The district has held multiple community forums to air the plan, though parents and staff naturally oppose losing neighborhood schools.

**The conservative argument:** School closures are a symptom of failed district management and progressive policies that have driven families away from public education. Rather than closing schools, Adams 12 should examine why enrollment is falling—charter competition, remote-work migration, and parent dissatisfaction with curriculum and discipline policies all play roles. Republicans argue the district should focus on operational efficiency and academic quality to win families back, not consolidate its way to relevance.

Democrats and education bureaucrats counter that demographic shifts and funding constraints leave few options; closures are painful but necessary.

The board vote looms as a test of whether Adams 12 will make hard choices or defer the reckoning to next year—watch whether the district simultaneously commits to meaningful reforms that might reverse enrollment decline.
