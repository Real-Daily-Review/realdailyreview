# Denver's Burnham Yard Stadium Plan: Another Government Hurdle Before Broncos Can Build

Denver is moving forward with a taxpayer-funded planning process that will delay the Broncos' stadium project even further. The city released a draft "small area plan" for the Burnham Yard site—the team's preferred location—but the Denver City Council must first approve it before the franchise can even submit its rezoning application (9News Denver).

This is classic municipal bureaucracy: rather than let the private sector build, city planners insert themselves into the process with government-mandated "guidance documents" that add months to development timelines. The Broncos have already waited years for this moment.

The small area plan requirement means Denver taxpayers are footing the bill for planning staff, consultants, and public hearings—all before a single shovel hits the ground on a privately financed stadium. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that streamlining permitting and reducing regulatory friction would accelerate job creation and economic activity. Instead, Denver is choosing the slow, expensive route.

The plan also raises questions about what conditions or demands the city council might impose on the team during approval—potentially shifting costs or design requirements onto the Broncos, or the public.
