# Supreme Court Faces Competing Overreach in Trump Cases

The Supreme Court is wading into two contentious Trump disputes—one over mail-in ballots, the other a civil judgment—that pit executive authority against judicial overreach from both directions.

In the ballot case, *National Review* reports the Court confronts "an overreaching Trump order colliding with an overreaching district judge," signaling skepticism on both sides of the legal divide. The justices must navigate whether Trump's directives on mail-in voting exceed presidential power, or whether lower courts have improperly constrained it.

Separately, Trump has asked the Supreme Court to overturn an $83 million judgment to E. Jean Carroll, citing the Court's 2024 decision granting presidents sweeping immunity for official acts, according to *Just The News*. Trump argues that immunity shield should protect him from the civil liability.

**The Colorado angle:** These cases will shape how much authority sits with the executive versus the judiciary—a principle affecting everything from federal land management to energy permitting that impacts Colorado's economy and property rights.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether the Court applies its immunity doctrine consistently or carves out exceptions that could invite further judicial second-guessing of presidential decisions.
