# Supreme Court Clears Path for Texas Age-Verification Law; Colorado Could Follow

The Supreme Court declined Monday to block Texas's age-verification requirement for app store downloads, allowing the law to proceed while litigation continues—a potential model for Colorado and other states tired of Big Tech's hands-off approach to children's online safety.

Texas's law requires proof of age before minors can access social media and other apps deemed harmful to children. The Washington Examiner reports the decision represents a significant win for parental rights advocates and conservatives who argue tech companies have abdicated responsibility for protecting kids from addictive algorithms and explicit content.

Tech industry groups and progressive organizations challenged the law, claiming it violates free speech and creates privacy risks. They argue age-verification systems are impractical and could expose user data. The Supreme Court's refusal to block the law suggests the justices saw merit in Texas's child-protection rationale, even as the underlying constitutional questions remain unresolved.

For Colorado, this opens a door. State lawmakers have wrestled with social media's impact on teen mental health and parental authority. If Texas's law survives appellate review, Colorado conservatives will likely push similar legislation—giving parents tools to limit their children's access to platforms designed to maximize screen time and engagement, regardless of age-appropriateness.
