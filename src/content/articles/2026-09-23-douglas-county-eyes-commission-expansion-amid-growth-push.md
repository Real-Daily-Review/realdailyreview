# Douglas County Faces Push to Expand County Commission—and Taxpayers Should Brace for Bigger Government

Douglas County voters may soon decide whether to enlarge their county commission, a move that would add seats, staff, and overhead to local government just as the county continues rapid growth.

Boulder County Commissioner Ashley Stolzmann argues that population growth justifies expansion, telling the Denver Post that "as the size of the county has grown over time, there's more of a need for more representation" across Longmont, Boulder, and outlying areas.

Conservatives counter that bigger commissions mean bigger budgets. More commissioners typically translate to more staff, higher administrative costs, and expanded regulatory reach—all funded by county taxpayers already squeezed by inflation and property tax pressures. Douglas County has grown substantially, but growth doesn't automatically require government expansion; it often demands efficiency and smarter resource allocation instead.

The real question: Can the existing commission handle workload through better processes, or does Douglas County genuinely need permanent structural growth? Taxpayers should demand hard numbers on projected costs before voting yes to any expansion.

Watch whether Douglas County Republicans push back with a fiscal impact analysis and alternative efficiency proposals ahead of the ballot measure.
