# Colorado GOP Governor Race Roils as Marx Faces Campaign Finance Allegations

Victor Marx's Republican gubernatorial bid is hitting turbulence before the general election even begins. Colorado election officials have alleged that Marx's campaign accepted more than $78,000 in improper donations, with at least 197 contributions from 111 donors exceeding the individual limit of $1,450 (Denver Post). The complaint threatens to complicate his path to the nomination and general election.

Meanwhile, Marx announced George Markert, a fellow Marine and non-politician, as his lieutenant governor running mate, positioning the ticket as outsiders ready to shake up state government. "George is not a career politician, just like me," Marx said on radio, emphasizing Markert's leadership credentials (Denver Post).

The GOP field just got more crowded—and fractured. Greg Lopez, a former Republican congressman and repeat gubernatorial candidate, qualified for the 2026 ballot as an independent, potentially siphoning conservative votes away from Marx in a general election matchup (Denver Post).

Republicans argue that campaign finance violations—whether technical or substantive—undermine voter confidence in the integrity of elections and candidate accountability. The split between Marx and Lopez on the right could hand Democrats an opening in a state trending blue.
