# Trump Scraps Iran Deal as Tensions Reignite in Persian Gulf

The Trump administration has formally abandoned last month's memorandum of understanding with Iran, declaring it a "phony peace" as Iranian attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz reignite regional conflict (National Review). After three rounds of U.S. military strikes and continued Iranian retaliation, the strategic calculus has shifted entirely.

The core conservative argument: the deal was a capitulation that merely postponed Iranian aggression while funneling hundreds of billions in sanctions relief to Tehran's military and proxy networks. Republicans contend that weakness invites further Iranian adventurism—precisely what we're seeing now with attacks on commercial vessels and renewed proxy warfare across the Gulf.

Democrats and the deal's architects argue that diplomacy, however imperfect, prevents outright war. The left claims the agreement provided verification mechanisms and temporary constraints on Iran's nuclear program. But with fighting resumed and the deal dead, that argument has lost force on the ground.

For Colorado: energy markets and gas prices could face upward pressure if Persian Gulf instability disrupts global oil supplies, hitting Colorado consumers and businesses already burdened by state-level energy regulations.

Watch whether Congress moves to reinforce U.S. military posture in the region and whether the administration pursues tougher sanctions on Iranian oil exports.
