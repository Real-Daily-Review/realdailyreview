# Trump Scraps Iran Deal as Middle East Tensions Reignite

President Trump has declared the recent Iran memorandum of understanding "a dead letter," ending a month-old diplomatic arrangement as fighting resumes in the Persian Gulf (National Review). Iran's attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz have triggered three rounds of U.S. military strikes and continued Iranian retaliation, fundamentally shifting the strategic calculus.

Conservatives argue the so-called "phony peace" was never credible. The Washington Examiner frames the core question: whether policymakers should fund regional stability or risk enabling Iran's proxy warfare network. Republicans contend that appeasement-style diplomacy without enforcement mechanisms only emboldens Tehran's aggression and destabilizes global energy markets—a direct concern for Colorado's oil and gas sector and American energy independence.

The collapse underscores a broader conservative critique: that agreements lacking teeth and verification regimes invite adversaries to exploit ambiguity. With the Strait of Hormuz—critical to global oil transit—now a flashpoint, energy prices and Colorado's energy economy face renewed volatility.
