# Denver Schools Pouring Money Into AC While Test Scores Lag

Denver Public Schools is spending heavily on air conditioning installations across multiple campuses, but conservatives question whether facility upgrades address the real problem: bloated bureaucracies and underperforming academics.

DPS has undertaken several construction projects over the past two summers to install AC in schools currently without it (KDVR Fox31 Denver). While comfortable learning environments matter, the spending comes as Colorado education outcomes remain mediocre—and as national data reveals troubling patterns about where school dollars actually go.

The New York Post recently documented how states spending far above the national per-pupil average often see minimal academic gains, with most excess funding flowing to administrative overhead and union contracts rather than classrooms (New York Post). New York spends 81% above the national average per student, yet achievement lags comparable states.

Colorado taxpayers should demand transparency: How much is DPS spending on these projects? What's the total construction budget? And critically—are test scores, graduation rates, and college readiness improving proportionally to spending increases?

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that before DPS expands capital projects, it must demonstrate that existing resources are being deployed efficiently toward student outcomes, not administrative bloat. Watch for the final project costs and whether DPS releases comparative data on academic performance before and after these upgrades.
