# FBI Crime Database Breached as Federal Cybersecurity Crumbles

Federal agencies are hemorrhaging to hackers. The FBI's National Crime Information Center—a database relied on by law enforcement nationwide, including Colorado police departments—was breached in a cyberattack, according to Fox News and CNN reporting. The breach marks the second major FBI cyber incident in months, as the bureau scrambles to identify what data was compromised and how attackers gained entry.

The timing is damning. Just days earlier, hackers also breached the U.S. Department of Agriculture's payment system, knocking offline applications that process farmer loans, crop insurance, and direct payments—hitting Colorado's agricultural economy where it counts (The Daily Wire/NBC News).

**The conservative case:** These breaches expose the folly of centralizing critical infrastructure under federal control. When Washington concentrates sensitive data—criminal records, agricultural payments, voter rolls—in one place, it creates a single point of catastrophic failure. Republicans have long warned that bloated federal agencies lack the cybersecurity rigor of private enterprise. Colorado law enforcement and farmers now face operational chaos because federal IT systems are apparently run like a small-town library's website.

Democrats will likely demand more taxpayer money for "cybersecurity upgrades"—the same agencies that failed to secure these systems in the first place.

Watch whether Congress demands accountability from federal IT leadership or simply appropriates billions more without reform.
