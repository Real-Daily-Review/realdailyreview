# Teen Rescued From Abandoned Missile Silo Near Aurora

An 18-year-old woman was pulled from an abandoned Cold War–era missile silo near the Arapahoe County Fairgrounds Wednesday after becoming trapped on private property, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31.

Firefighters responded to the remote site and extracted the teenager, who may now face trespassing charges from the Arapahoe County Sheriff's Office. The silo, a relic of America's nuclear deterrent infrastructure, sits on private land—a reminder of how many such facilities dot Colorado's landscape, often unsecured and occasionally attracting curious trespassers.

The incident underscores a broader issue: aging Cold War infrastructure scattered across the state remains a liability. While the woman's rescue ended without serious injury, such incidents highlight the risks of abandoned military sites and the costs they impose on local emergency services.

Conservatives should note that taxpayer-funded rescue operations for trespassers on private property raise questions about liability and property rights. The property owner bears responsibility for securing the site, but emergency responders—and ultimately Colorado taxpayers—absorb the cost of rescue.

Watch whether the sheriff's office pursues trespassing charges and whether this incident prompts renewed discussion about decommissioning or securing similar sites across Colorado.
