# Supreme Court Blocks Trump Admin's Last-Minute Noncitizen Ballot Challenge

Election integrity just took a hit hours before polls opened. The Supreme Court rejected the Trump administration's emergency request to set aside mail-in ballots from suspected noncitizens in California, denying the order without explanation (The Daily Wire).

The Justice Department had filed the request Monday evening, arguing that roughly 20,000 people on a federal noncitizen list should be blocked from voting. Conservatives argue that ballot security—especially preventing noncitizen voting—is fundamental to election legitimacy. Republicans counter that courts have been reluctant to intervene in election administration on short notice, even when fraud concerns are raised.

The ruling underscores a broader tension: while Republicans push for stricter voter verification and citizenship checks, courts have repeatedly sided with states' authority over ballot procedures. Democrats argue that mass mail-in voting is secure and that last-minute legal challenges create chaos; Republicans counter that election day surprises like this erode public confidence in results.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado allows mail-in voting statewide and maintains its own voter rolls. If noncitizen voting becomes a documented problem here, expect GOP lawmakers to demand tighter verification protocols—and Democratic resistance to what they'll call "voter suppression."

Watch whether Republican-led states move to implement stricter citizenship verification before 2026, or if courts continue blocking such efforts.
