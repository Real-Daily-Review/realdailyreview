# Alaska Road Fight Exposes the Self-Interest Behind "Environmental" Opposition

A brewing dispute over a proposed road to Alaska's remote Pebble Mine reveals how environmental activism often masks naked economic self-interest—a pattern Colorado conservatives should recognize in their own backyard.

The fight pits mining companies seeking infrastructure access against an unlikely coalition: environmental groups and a local airplane pilot who currently profits from ferrying people and equipment to the site. As the *Washington Examiner* reports, the pilot's opposition to the road isn't purely ideological—it's financial. Build the road, lose the contract.

This is textbook "Baptists and bootleggers" economics: moral crusaders (environmentalists) and profit-seekers (the pilot) align against development, each for different reasons. The public hears only the environmental argument.

Colorado faces identical dynamics. Renewable energy advocates and wind-farm operators oppose traditional energy development. Outdoor recreation outfitters lobby against mining and logging. Environmental groups funded by coastal elites fight Colorado water projects and energy expansion that would benefit working families and rural economies.

Republicans argue that stripping away regulations and infrastructure barriers—whether in Alaska or Colorado—unleashes economic growth, creates jobs, and respects property rights. The left counters that environmental protection justifies limiting development.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature and governor prioritize economic opportunity or continue caving to activist pressure disguised as environmental stewardship.
