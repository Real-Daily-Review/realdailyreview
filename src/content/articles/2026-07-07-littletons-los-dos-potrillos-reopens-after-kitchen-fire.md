# Littleton's Los Dos Potrillos Reopens After Fire—Colorado Small Business Bounces Back

Colorado's small-business resilience is on display this week as Littleton's beloved Los Dos Potrillos prepares to reopen July 9, months after a devastating March kitchen fire forced closure and extensive repairs (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The family-owned Mexican restaurant, a community fixture, will return with a redesigned kitchen following the months-long rebuild.

The reopening reflects the grit of Colorado's independent restaurant sector, which has weathered pandemic lockdowns, labor shortages, and rising operational costs. For Littleton residents and the broader metro area, the return signals that local entrepreneurs can recover from major setbacks—provided they're not buried under excessive permitting delays or regulatory red tape during reconstruction.

The restaurant's ability to reopen relatively quickly also underscores the importance of reasonable building codes and fire-safety enforcement that protect both customers and business continuity without strangling small operators with bureaucratic obstacles. Colorado's restaurant industry, already squeezed by labor costs and inflation, depends on streamlined approval processes when rebuilding after disasters.

Conservatives should watch whether Littleton's permitting and inspection processes remained efficient during this rebuild—a bellwether for whether local government is helping or hindering small-business recovery across the state.
