# Colorado GOP's Messy Primary Leaves Liberty Advocates Bracing for General Election Disaster

Colorado Republicans have nominated Victor Marx, a first-time candidate and nonprofit leader with a controversial personal history, as their gubernatorial pick—and conservative observers are already sounding the alarm. Marx narrowly defeated state Sen. Barb Kirkmeyer in a race that remained too close to call for days, with a margin of roughly 2,000 votes (Denver Post).

Complete Colorado's assessment is blunt: the primary results amount to "the Colorado GOP's death wish." Out of 18 total Republican gubernatorial candidates, only two advanced from the assembly process, signaling deep fractures in the party's bench strength heading into November.

The general election matchup now pits Marx against a Democratic opponent in a state trending blue. Democrats are already framing the race around kitchen-table economics—"Can they get to the store and get food for their kids?" Democratic strategist David Boian told the Denver Post, signaling the left's focus on inflation and cost-of-living messaging.

Republicans argue Marx's unconventional candidacy and narrow primary victory leave the GOP vulnerable in a state where Democrats control the legislature and statewide offices. Conservatives worry the party's fractured primary process has weakened its nominee heading into a general election Colorado can ill afford to lose.

Watch whether Marx can consolidate GOP voters and articulate a clear conservative message on taxes, energy policy, and government overreach before November.
