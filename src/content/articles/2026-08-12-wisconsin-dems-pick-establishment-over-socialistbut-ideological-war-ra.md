# Wisconsin Democrats Pick Establishment Over Socialist—But the Real Battle Is Just Beginning

Wisconsin Democrats chose establishment moderate David Crowley over DSA member Francesca Hong in a party leadership fight that exposed deep ideological fractures, with Gov. Tony Evers backing Crowley as "the future of the party" (Fox News Politics). The victory signals Democrats' attempt to steer away from the party's socialist wing—at least for now.

But don't mistake this for a conservative win. Crowley still represents the progressive mainstream that has driven Wisconsin toward higher taxes, regulatory expansion, and anti-energy policies that mirror Colorado's own left-wing drift. Republicans argue the real issue isn't which Democrat wins internal power struggles—it's that both factions embrace big government, union dominance, and the regulatory state that strangles small business and working families.

Meanwhile, union leadership battles are roiling the left (Daily Caller), with competing factions fighting over control of labor's political direction. Conservatives note this infighting reveals the Left's fundamental contradiction: socialist activists demand "class struggle," while establishment Democrats prefer "class collaboration" with corporate interests—a hypocrisy that hurts working people either way.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the takeaway is clear: watch whether Wisconsin's Democratic establishment actually reins in its socialist flank, or whether this is merely theater masking continued leftward momentum on taxes, regulation, and energy policy.
