# Colorado Charter Schools Face Mounting Pressure as Democrats Target Education Competition

Longmont's Flagstaff Academy—a charter school serving 665 students with innovative hands-on programs like its greenhouse science lab (9News Denver)—exemplifies the education model Democrats increasingly want to restrict. According to the Washington Examiner, Democratic leaders in multiple states, including incoming Texas Gov. Gina Hinojosa, are moving to curtail charter schools, arguing they drain resources from traditional public systems.

Republicans and education choice advocates counter that charter schools provide low-income families an escape route from failing district schools and drive systemic improvement through competition. They argue that restricting charter access traps disadvantaged students in underperforming systems while protecting union interests and bureaucratic turf.

Colorado's charter sector has grown substantially, serving over 80,000 students statewide. Democrats have long sought tighter regulations on charter authorization and funding, framing it as "resource protection" for districts. Conservatives argue this is really about blocking parental choice and insulating public monopolies from accountability.

The real question: Will Colorado lawmakers follow the Democratic playbook of restricting charters, or will they protect the education freedom that allows schools like Flagstaff to innovate?
