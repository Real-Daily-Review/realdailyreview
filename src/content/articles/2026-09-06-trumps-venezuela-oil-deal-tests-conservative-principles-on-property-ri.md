# Trump's Venezuela Oil Deal Raises Questions About American Property Rights and Energy Strategy

A potential U.S.-Venezuela oil arrangement is sparking debate among conservatives over whether securing foreign energy supplies serves American interests or undermines the rule of law that built U.S. prosperity.

The deal would give the U.S. access to Venezuelan crude, addressing concerns that domestic shale production eventually declines over time (Just The News). Proponents argue diversified energy sources abroad could help America maintain global energy dominance as domestic wells age.

But National Review raises a sharper objection: the cynicism of the move. Trump has repeatedly criticized Venezuela's Chávez regime for seizing American company property—yet a U.S. oil agreement could effectively legitimize that theft by normalizing relations with the regime that committed it. National Review argues that supporting a stable world order built on the rule of law has made America the most prosperous nation in history, and abandoning that principle for short-term energy gains undermines the very foundation of American strength.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil and gas industry depends on predictable property rights and contract enforcement. Any U.S. policy that normalizes authoritarian seizure of American assets abroad sends a troubling signal about government respect for private ownership at home.

Conservatives and energy advocates should watch whether this deal includes enforceable protections for American companies or amounts to capitulation to a hostile regime.
