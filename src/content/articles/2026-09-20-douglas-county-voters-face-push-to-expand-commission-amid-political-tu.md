# Douglas County Voters Face Commission Expansion Push as Local Races Heat Up

Douglas County voters are being asked to expand their county commission—a move that will mean more government, higher costs, and less accountability for taxpayers already stretched thin.

Boulder County Commissioner Ashley Stolzmann argues that "as the size of the county has grown over time, there's more of a need for more representation" (Denver Post). But conservatives question whether adding commissioners actually improves governance or simply expands the bureaucracy. Larger commissions often mean slower decisions, more infighting, and bigger payrolls—all funded by county residents.

The expansion push comes as Douglas County's political landscape grows murkier. A Lone Tree Democrat running for Colorado's House of Representatives was arrested in May for public intoxication, according to Texas court records (Denver Post), raising questions about candidate vetting and the quality of candidates seeking office.

Douglas County has grown substantially in recent years, attracting families and businesses seeking lower taxes and less regulation than Denver proper. That growth, however, is now being weaponized by those arguing for bigger government. The real question: Will more commissioners actually serve taxpayers, or just create another layer of spending?

Watch whether Douglas County voters reject this expansion at the ballot box—a bellwether for whether Colorado's fastest-growing counties will resist the progressive push for bigger government.
