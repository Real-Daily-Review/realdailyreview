# ICE Ramps Up Deportations of Criminal Aliens as Sanctuary States Fight Back

The Trump administration's immigration enforcement is intensifying, with ICE conducting targeted operations that have netted illegal immigrants convicted of attempted murder, rape, and child sex crimes across multiple states. Meanwhile, sanctuary jurisdictions are doubling down on obstruction—and the federal government is fighting back in court.

The DOJ has sued Maryland over its "Community Trust Act," accusing the state of an "active and deliberate effort" to block ICE detainers and prevent deportations, arguing the sanctuary law violates the Constitution's supremacy clause. This mirrors a broader pattern: states like Minnesota have gone so far as to pardon criminal aliens—ICE recently deported an illegal immigrant convicted of child rape after Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz commuted his sentence.

Mexico's government has already signaled it will pursue legal action after ICE fatally shot an illegal migrant during an operation, though the migrant allegedly attempted to ram an agent with a car.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's Democratic leadership has embraced similar sanctuary policies. Conservatives argue these laws endanger taxpayers by shielding criminals and undermining federal immigration authority. Republicans say the Constitution is clear: states cannot nullify federal law.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado Democrats will face similar DOJ legal pressure—and whether Republican-controlled legislatures in red states will pass laws explicitly cooperating with ICE to expose the sanctuary movement's vulnerability.
