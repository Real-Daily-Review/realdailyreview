# Senate Republicans Must Kill Private Jet Tax Carve-Out Hidden in Safety Bill

Republicans are quietly pushing a tax giveaway for private jet owners buried inside aviation safety legislation—and conservatives say it's indefensible politics ahead of midterms.

The provision, tucked into a bill written after the deadly collision between American Airlines Flight 5342 and an Army Black Hawk helicopter that killed 67 people, offers a tax benefit to private aviation interests. According to the Washington Examiner, Senate Republicans should strip it out before it becomes a liability.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** A safety bill should address safety, not hand out tax breaks to wealthy jet owners. Republicans heading into elections have plenty of legitimate accomplishments to defend; this isn't one of them. The optics are terrible—using a tragedy that killed dozens as cover for corporate tax benefits plays directly into Democratic messaging about GOP priorities.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** Private aviation operates across the state, and any federal tax policy affecting the industry ripples through Colorado's aerospace and business communities. But conservatives argue the principle matters more than the industry benefit—Congress shouldn't weaponize tragedy legislation for special interests.

Senate Republicans should excise this carve-out now and pass a clean safety bill, or watch Democrats hammer them for choosing jet-owner tax cuts over genuine aviation safety reform.
