# Virginia County's Illegal-Immigrant Aid Program Signals Troubling Trend for Colorado Taxpayers

A Virginia county is spending taxpayer dollars to subsidize families of deported illegal immigrants—a move that should alarm Colorado conservatives watching their own government's immigration stance.

Fairfax County is distributing up to $2,000 per family through a local ministry to cover basic needs and utilities for households whose primary earner was removed by ICE, according to Fox News and the New York Post. Republicans are calling the program "alien harboring" with public funds, arguing it incentivizes illegal immigration and punishes law-abiding citizens footing the bill.

The program raises a critical question: Should American taxpayers fund benefits for families of people who violated immigration law? Conservatives say no. They argue such policies reward illegal entry, strain municipal budgets, and undermine the rule of law—especially as Colorado cities like Denver grapple with their own sanctuary policies and migrant costs.

Democrats frame the aid as humanitarian assistance for vulnerable families, but Republicans counter that compassion shouldn't mean raiding the public treasury to soften the consequences of federal immigration enforcement.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether Denver, Boulder, or other progressive municipalities attempt similar programs—and demand accountability if they do.
