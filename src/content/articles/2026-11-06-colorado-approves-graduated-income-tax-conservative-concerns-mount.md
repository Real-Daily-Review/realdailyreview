# Colorado Voters Back Graduated Income Tax Despite Conservative Opposition

Colorado Democrats won a major fiscal victory this fall when voters approved a graduated income tax—a direct assault on the state's flat-tax structure that conservatives have defended as pro-growth and fair.

The measure, which backers framed as a way to shift tax burden to high earners, faced fierce Republican opposition. Conservatives argued the tax hike would drive out businesses and high-income earners while expanding government spending without meaningful restraint. The group behind the proposal claimed victory after what CPR News called "one of the most hotly contested proposals on the fall ballot."

The graduated tax represents a fundamental shift in Colorado's tax philosophy—moving away from the flat rate that has long been a selling point for businesses considering relocation to the state. Republicans warned the change would make Colorado less competitive and ultimately shrink the tax base as earners flee to neighboring states with lower rates.

Meanwhile, a separate ballot measure—Proposal 2, billed as "money out of politics"—sparked its own First Amendment controversy. Fox News reported that while supporters claimed it would curb "pay-to-play" donations, critics argued it could restrict policy research and communications about candidates, raising serious free-speech concerns.
