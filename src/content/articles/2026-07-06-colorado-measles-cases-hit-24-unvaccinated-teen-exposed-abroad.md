# Colorado's 24th Measles Case Highlights Vaccination Gap as Travel Exposure Spreads

Colorado has now recorded 24 measles cases in 2026, with health officials confirming a new case in a Larimer County teenager who had not received the MMR vaccine and was likely exposed during recent international travel (CPR News, KDVR).

The case underscores a persistent vulnerability: unvaccinated individuals remain susceptible to diseases that routine immunization largely controls. While Colorado maintains relatively high vaccination rates statewide, pockets of lower coverage—whether due to parental choice, access barriers, or travel circumstances—create openings for imported outbreaks.

Public health officials typically emphasize vaccination as the primary defense. Conservatives and libertarians, while generally supporting vaccine availability, have long resisted government mandates and one-size-fits-all policies that override parental decision-making. The tension remains: disease prevention versus individual medical autonomy.

For Colorado, the measles resurgence also reflects broader travel patterns and global health dynamics. State health officials will likely continue tracking cases and recommending vaccination, particularly for those planning international trips.

Taxpayers should watch whether Colorado health authorities push for expanded vaccine requirements in schools or public settings—a policy flashpoint that pits public health against parental rights and government overreach concerns.
