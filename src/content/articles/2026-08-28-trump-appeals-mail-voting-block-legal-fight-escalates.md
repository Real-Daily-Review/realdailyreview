# Trump Administration Fights Court Blocks on Mail Voting Order

President Trump's push to restrict mail-in voting is facing judicial resistance, but his administration isn't backing down. The Justice Department appealed a federal judge's second temporary block on a new U.S. Postal Service rule limiting mail voting, according to the Washington Examiner. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani, an Obama appointee in Massachusetts, has now twice halted implementation of the executive order—most recently with a two-week pause—prompting the Trump team to escalate to the U.S. Court of Appeals (9News Denver).

The legal fight matters for Colorado voters and election integrity nationwide. Republicans argue mail voting creates security vulnerabilities and administrative chaos; Democrats and some Republicans counter that restricting mail access disproportionately burdens rural and military voters who depend on it. Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) claimed on CNN that Trump's order would hurt Republicans more than Democrats because conservative rural Americans rely heavily on mail ballots (Breitbart).

Separately, a Biden-appointed judge declined to block Trump's birthright citizenship order, though expressing constitutional skepticism (Just The News).
