# Justice Department Drops Suppressor Registration Rules, Expanding Second Amendment Rights

The Justice Department has stopped enforcing federal registration requirements for firearm suppressors and short-barreled rifles following a court ruling—a significant win for Second Amendment advocates and Colorado gun owners tired of federal overreach.

According to 9News Denver, the DOJ moved to allow more people to purchase these items without the previous regulatory burden. ATF Director Robert Cekada told Breitbart News that the agency applied the court ruling broadly, extending relief to "all law-abiding Americans" rather than limiting it narrowly.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and gun-rights groups have long argued that National Firearms Act registration requirements impose unconstitutional barriers on lawful citizens. Suppressors—devices that reduce noise—are legal in most states, including Colorado, yet federal red tape made them expensive and cumbersome to obtain. This ruling removes that friction without compromising public safety.

**What Democrats say:** Gun-control advocates typically argue that registration and regulation of suppressors serve law-enforcement interests, though the court disagreed.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado gun owners and hunters can now more easily access suppressors for hearing protection and hunting—a practical win for sportsmen and Second Amendment advocates in a state where outdoor traditions run deep.

Watch whether Congress attempts to legislatively override this ruling or whether other federal courts follow suit in striking down similar registration schemes.
