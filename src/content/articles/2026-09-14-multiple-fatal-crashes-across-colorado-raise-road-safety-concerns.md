# Colorado Roads Claim Lives as Traffic Deaths Mount

Colorado highways and city streets are proving deadly, with multiple fatal crashes in recent weeks underscoring persistent safety concerns across the state.

Two people died in a head-on collision on westbound I-70 near Rifle early Sunday morning around 2:30 a.m., according to 9News Denver. Days later, a motorcyclist was killed in Weld County when struck by a pickup truck near Ault, Ault Police confirmed to 9News.

In a separate incident, authorities charged a man in connection with an August crash in Lakewood that killed a 17-year-old riding an e-scooter, KDVR reported.

The cluster of fatalities raises questions about road conditions, driver behavior, and enforcement priorities on Colorado's increasingly congested corridors. I-70, a critical artery for commerce and tourism, has long been a flashpoint for safety advocates and transportation officials. Rural highways in northern Colorado also continue to present hazards, particularly during early morning hours when visibility and driver alertness are compromised.

Conservatives emphasize personal responsibility and safe driving practices over expansive regulatory responses. However, infrastructure maintenance and enforcement of existing traffic laws remain legitimate government functions that directly affect Colorado taxpayer safety.

Watch for any legislative pushes to expand traffic enforcement cameras or impose new vehicle regulations—common progressive responses to accident clusters that often burden law-abiding drivers.
