# Tennessee Governor Stands Firm on Execution of Death Row Inmate Christa Pike

Tennessee's governor has refused to intervene in the execution of Christa Pike, the only woman on the state's death row, rejecting a last-ditch clemency bid (9News Denver). Pike, convicted in 1996 for the murder of Colleen Slemmer, wrote to NBC News that she is "at peace" ahead of the execution, saying "I am not afraid to die" but acknowledged nervousness about the process itself (New York Post).

The decision reflects a broader conservative principle: that duly convicted murderers should face the sentences juries impose. Pike's case—a 30-year-old conviction upheld through the courts—represents the judicial system working as intended, without political interference or emotional clemency overrides.

This stands in sharp contrast to progressive activists' push for death penalty abolition, which has gained traction in some blue states but remains deeply unpopular in Tennessee and across much of America. Conservatives argue that capital punishment, when applied through rigorous legal process, serves justice for victims' families and upholds the rule of law.

Colorado abolished capital punishment in 2020, but the Pike case underscores why many Coloradans and Americans support the death penalty for the most heinous crimes—and why governors' refusal to second-guess juries matters.
