# Trump's Tariff Gamble Threatens Colorado's Energy and Agricultural Trade

President Trump's escalating trade war—including newly announced 50% tariffs on Canadian imports—risks real pain for Colorado's economy, even as conservatives debate whether protectionist measures serve American interests.

The tariffs target lumber, agricultural products, and consumer electronics flowing across the northern border. Colorado ranchers and farmers who depend on cross-border trade, along with energy producers shipping goods to Canada, face potential retaliatory measures and higher input costs. The Washington Examiner reports that trade negotiations with Canada broke down last week, prompting Trump's aggressive tariff announcement.

Conservatives are split on the strategy. Trump's populist wing argues tariffs protect American workers and manufacturing from unfair foreign competition. Critics—including some free-market conservatives—warn that broad tariffs function as hidden taxes on consumers and businesses, distort supply chains, and invite retaliation that hammers export-dependent sectors like agriculture and energy.

For Colorado specifically, the stakes are high: our ranchers export beef and grain; our energy sector ships equipment and materials; our manufacturers depend on integrated North American supply chains. Higher tariffs could raise costs for Colorado businesses and consumers while inviting Canadian retaliation against U.S. agricultural exports.

Watch whether Trump negotiates a deal or doubles down—and whether Colorado's Republican delegation pushes back on tariffs that hurt their constituents' bottom lines.
