# Colorado Voters Face Fourteen Ballot Measures—and a Voter ID Test for Mail Ballots

Colorado's November election will test whether voters embrace tighter election security while deciding on fourteen statewide ballot measures, including a contentious tax overhaul that would hit top earners hard.

Initiative 362, backed by election-integrity advocates, adds voter authentication requirements to Colorado's all-mail system—a move conservatives argue strengthens ballot security in a state that has faced scrutiny over election administration (Complete Colorado). Republicans have long pushed for voter ID safeguards; Democrats typically oppose such measures as barriers to participation.

The ballot also features a major tax proposal that would implement a graduated income tax structure. According to the Denver Post, the measure would cut taxes for most Coloradans by hundreds of dollars but nearly double income taxes on the top 0.01% of earners. Conservatives argue this represents classic progressive wealth redistribution; supporters counter it closes loopholes and funds education and transportation.

Mike Rosen's analysis in Complete Colorado breaks down all fourteen measures for conservative voters weighing their choices. Meanwhile, multiple candidates have declined to participate in televised debates or town halls hosted by 9News, limiting voter exposure to direct candidate comparison before Election Day.
