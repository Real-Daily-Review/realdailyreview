# DA Drops Arson Charge in Alexander Mountain Fire—Real Culprit Still Unknown

Larimer County prosecutors have dropped the arson charge against Jason Hobby in connection with the Alexander Mountain fire that torched nearly 10,000 acres and destroyed 50 structures west of Loveland in August 2024, according to CPR News and 9News Denver. Authorities say alibi evidence showed Hobby could not have started the blaze.

The dismissal leaves a critical gap: no arrests have been made, and the person actually responsible remains at large. Larimer County prosecutors expressed frustration over the development, acknowledging they cannot identify who ignited the fire that ravaged Larimer County property and threatened lives across the Front Range.

For Colorado taxpayers and property owners, the outcome underscores a troubling reality—wildfire investigations can collapse when evidence doesn't hold up, leaving communities without accountability or closure. The case also raises questions about the initial arrest decision and investigative protocols that led authorities to charge Hobby without sufficient evidence.

Colorado's wildfire season demands both aggressive prosecution of actual arsonists and rigorous investigative standards that protect innocent people from wrongful charges. Conservatives and residents should watch whether Larimer County authorities renew efforts to identify the actual fire starter or whether this case simply goes cold.
