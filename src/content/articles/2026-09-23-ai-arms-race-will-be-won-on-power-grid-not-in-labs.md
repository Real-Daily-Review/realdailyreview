# AI Competition Will Be Won or Lost on America's Power Grid—Not in Labs

The real battle with China over artificial intelligence dominance won't be decided by algorithms or export controls—it'll be decided by whether America can still build the electrical infrastructure to power AI data centers, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

As Chinese President Xi Jinping visits Washington to discuss tech competition and AI strategy, policymakers are fixating on semiconductors and compute capacity. But that misses the core problem: America's aging power grid and permitting gridlock make it nearly impossible to construct the generation and transmission capacity AI operations demand.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** regulatory red tape and environmental litigation are crippling America's ability to build critical infrastructure faster than China. While Beijing approves and constructs power plants in months, American projects languish in permitting hell for years. Colorado, a state with significant energy resources and growing tech ambitions, faces this squeeze directly—energy workers and grid operators know the bottleneck is real.

Democrats and environmental groups argue that strict environmental reviews protect public health and prevent reckless development. Republicans counter that those same reviews have become weapons of delay, allowing China to outpace us while we debate.
