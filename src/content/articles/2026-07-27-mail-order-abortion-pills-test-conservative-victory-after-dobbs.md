# Colorado Conservatives Face New Front in Abortion Debate as Mail-Order Pill Access Becomes Battleground

The post-Dobbs landscape is shifting beneath conservatives' feet—and not in the direction many expected. While the Supreme Court's 2022 overturning of Roe v. Wade handed Republicans a historic victory, the fight over abortion access has moved to a new terrain: mail-order mifepristone and the limits of state authority to restrict it.

According to the Washington Examiner, conservative legal groups like the Alliance Defending Freedom are now waging what amounts to a second front in the abortion wars, targeting the FDA's approval of mail-order abortion pills. The tension is real: Republicans won the right to ban abortion at the state level, but federal regulatory authority over medication approval—and interstate commerce—complicates enforcement.

For Colorado, where voters have consistently rejected abortion restrictions and Democrats control the legislature, this matters enormously. Mail-order pill access could render state-level bans functionally meaningless, frustrating conservatives who believed Dobbs would empower Colorado Republicans to pass meaningful restrictions.

The core conservative argument: if states can ban abortion, they must have tools to enforce those bans—including authority over drugs that circumvent state law. The counterargument from abortion-rights advocates (implicit in the sourcing): federal drug policy and interstate commerce trump state abortion bans.
