# Colorado Voters Face Three Ballot Fights Over Crime, Redistricting, and Primary Power

Colorado voters will decide three consequential measures this fall that pit public safety, electoral integrity, and party power against progressive governance norms.

**Proposition 133** elevates sexual trafficking of minors to Class 1 felony status—Colorado's most severe designation, carrying potential life sentences. Conservatives argue the measure closes a dangerous gap in state law and protects vulnerable children from predators. The proposal reflects growing frustration with lenient sentencing that leaves trafficking victims without adequate justice.

**Initiative 256** would ban partisan congressional redistricting, requiring maps drawn to avoid favoring either party. Republicans and good-government advocates see this as a check on Democratic mapmaking that has tilted Colorado's congressional delegation leftward. Democrats likely oppose guardrails that could limit their redistricting advantage.

**Unaffiliated voter growth** continues reshaping Colorado politics. Registered party members who skip caucuses cede nomination power to active partisans—a dynamic that could amplify primary-driven candidates and weaken establishment control (Complete Colorado).

These three fights expose real divisions: whether Colorado prioritizes tough-on-crime justice, fair electoral maps, or party gatekeeping. Each reflects voter appetite for checks on institutional power.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado's growing unaffiliated bloc votes to constrain both major parties' redistricting power—a sign of genuine anti-establishment sentiment or merely anti-Democrat sentiment.
