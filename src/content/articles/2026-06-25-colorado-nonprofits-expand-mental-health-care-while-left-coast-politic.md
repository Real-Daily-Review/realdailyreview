# Colorado Mental Health Nonprofits Expand Services While Left-Coast Politicians Take Extended Breaks

Colorado nonprofits are quietly doing the work that government claims to prioritize: expanding mental health care access for vulnerable populations, including LGBTQ+ Coloradans. According to 9News Denver, organizations like Next are scaling up direct services without waiting for bureaucratic approval or new tax dollars.

Meanwhile, the progressive playbook elsewhere tells a different story. In San Francisco, socialist supervisor Jackie Fielder recently took a three-month mental health leave, citing personal struggles (Daily Caller). The contrast is stark: Colorado's private and nonprofit sector delivers results; California's government workers take extended absences while their constituents struggle.

Conservatives argue this illustrates a fundamental truth: voluntary organizations and market-driven solutions respond faster and more efficiently than government programs. When nonprofits expand mental health services, they do so with donated funds and mission-driven staff—no bloated bureaucracy required.

The left counters that government-funded mental health infrastructure is essential and that public servants deserve wellness support. But Colorado taxpayers should note the real-world outcome: services are expanding here through private initiative, not government mandates or spending increases.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers use this nonprofit success as cover to avoid funding mental health through the state budget—or whether they'll demand new appropriations anyway.
