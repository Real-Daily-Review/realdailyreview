# Colorado Teen Charged as Adult in Cruise Ship Death Now Held Without Bail

A federal judge has reversed course and ordered Timothy Hudson, a Colorado teenager accused of killing his stepsister Anna Kepner aboard a Carnival Cruise, held in custody pending trial after prosecutors secured adult charges against him.

Hudson, who had been released on pretrial conditions for months following the death, now faces serious charges including sexual assault and murder. The reversal came after the case was elevated from juvenile to adult court—a significant legal shift that changed the judge's calculus on whether Hudson posed a flight risk or danger to the community.

The case highlights questions about pretrial release decisions and the threshold for holding defendants accused of violent crimes. Conservatives have long argued that judges should err on the side of public safety when serious violent felonies are involved, particularly in cases involving sexual assault and homicide.

The New York Post reports Hudson had been "walking free" for months before the custody order, raising concerns about how initial release decisions were made in a case of this severity.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether this case prompts broader scrutiny of pretrial release policies in federal and state courts handling violent crime.
