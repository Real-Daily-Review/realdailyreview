# Energy Department Employee Charged With Supporting Houthis

A Department of Energy employee has been arrested on charges of attempting to provide material support to the Houthis, raising serious questions about vetting and security at a federal agency responsible for America's nuclear arsenal.

Ashton Hamed Ellaboudy, 51, of Richland, Washington, allegedly purchased precursor chemicals and materials used to build homemade explosives and drones, according to the Department of Justice announcement reported by the *Washington Examiner* and *Just The News*. The charges underscore a troubling security gap: how did someone with access to sensitive energy infrastructure pass federal background checks while allegedly attempting to aid a foreign terrorist organization?

This incident arrives as the Biden-Harris administration has expanded federal workforce hiring without proportional investment in security protocols. Republicans have long warned that bloated government agencies lack adequate oversight, and this case validates those concerns. The DOE oversees nuclear weapons production and energy security—areas where foreign adversary infiltration poses existential risk.

Colorado taxpayers fund the federal workforce that failed to catch this alleged threat. The state also hosts critical energy infrastructure and military installations that depend on DOE security clearances and vetting standards.

Conservatives should demand a full accounting of DOE's hiring and clearance procedures—and whether similar cases have been missed.
