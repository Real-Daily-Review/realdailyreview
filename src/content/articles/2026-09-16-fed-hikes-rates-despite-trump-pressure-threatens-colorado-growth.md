# Federal Reserve Hikes Rates Despite Trump Pressure, Risking Colorado's Economic Recovery

The Federal Reserve unanimously raised its benchmark interest rate for the first time since 2023 Wednesday, defying President Trump's public demands for a cut and signaling potential additional hikes later this year (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31 Denver).

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, Trump's own appointee, lifted the key rate a quarter-point to approximately 3.9% to combat "stubbornly-high inflation" (KDVR). Warsh stated the economy "appears to be strengthening," justifying the move despite White House pressure (Breitbart).

**The conservative case for caution:** Republicans worry the rate hike could choke off economic growth and job creation just as Colorado's energy sector and small businesses are recovering. Higher borrowing costs ripple through mortgages, business loans, and consumer credit—hitting Colorado families and entrepreneurs hardest. National Review argues the Fed must retain discretion to respond to economic conditions, but critics counter that Warsh's independence from Trump signals the central bank may prioritize inflation-fighting over growth, potentially triggering recession.

Colorado's construction, real estate, and energy industries depend on affordable capital. Rate hikes make expansion projects more expensive and less attractive, threatening job growth in sectors already battered by regulatory overreach.

**Watch next:** Whether the Fed follows through on signaled additional hikes and how Colorado's housing market and small-business lending respond over the next quarter.
