# Colorado Springs Gets UFL Football Team—But Election Law Enforcement Heats Up

Colorado Springs is landing a professional football franchise as the United Football League expands into the state for the 2027 season, marking a win for the city's economic development efforts. Mayor Yemi Mobolade announced the move via social media, positioning the UFL—which merged the XFL and USFL in Spring 2024—as a major-league draw for the region (KDVR, CPR News).

The good news on sports comes as Colorado Springs faces legal pressure over election timing. The city is being sued for scheduling municipal elections in April, marking the first enforcement action under a 2025 voting law (Complete Colorado). Conservatives have long warned that shifting election dates and expanding voting rules create confusion and opportunity for irregularities—this lawsuit suggests those concerns are materializing in real disputes.

Meanwhile, unaffiliated voter registration continues climbing statewide, with primary elections increasingly deciding general outcomes. Voters registered with parties but skipping caucuses are effectively ceding influence to party insiders (Complete Colorado). Republicans argue this fragmentation weakens party discipline and rewards candidates who appeal to narrow activist bases rather than building broad coalitions.

The UFL franchise is a legitimate economic bright spot for the Springs. Watch whether the city's election law battle sets precedent for other Colorado municipalities—and whether Republicans push back on voting rules they view as administratively chaotic.
