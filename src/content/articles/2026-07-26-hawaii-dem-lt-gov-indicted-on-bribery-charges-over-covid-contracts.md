# Hawaii Democrat Lt. Gov. Indicted on Bribery Charges Over COVID Contracts

Hawaii's Democratic lieutenant governor, Sylvia Luke, has been indicted on 12 counts including criminal conspiracy to commit bribery and falsifying campaign finance reports—a stark reminder that government corruption knows no party, but Democrats' control of state spending often creates the conditions for it.

According to Fox News and the Washington Examiner, Luke allegedly solicited a $35,000 bribe tied to COVID-19 testing contracts in exchange for campaign contributions. The charges carry up to a 10-year prison sentence. Even Democratic Gov. Josh Green has called for her resignation, signaling the severity of the allegations.

**The conservative takeaway:** This case underscores why limited government and transparency matter. When government controls lucrative contracts—especially pandemic-era spending that bypassed normal scrutiny—the temptation for corruption multiplies. Luke's indictment reflects what happens when politicians treat public resources as personal leverage.

Colorado taxpayers should note: state and local officials here oversee billions in contracts annually. Watchdog groups and legislators must demand rigorous competitive bidding, audit trails, and enforcement to prevent similar schemes from taking root in Colorado's government procurement process.
