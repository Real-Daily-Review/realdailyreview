# Colorado Healthcare Faces Caregiver Shortage as System Relies on Unpaid Family Labor

Colorado's healthcare infrastructure is quietly collapsing under the weight of unpaid family caregiving—and the state's progressive establishment wants taxpayers to foot the bill for a fix.

According to the Colorado Sun, health systems across the state have built their model on the assumption that relatives will provide care at no cost. That arrangement is breaking down as demographics shift and fewer family members can afford to leave the workforce. The left's answer: state-funded recruitment and retention programs for paid caregivers, framed as "investment" in healthcare.

Conservatives counter that this is classic progressive cost-shifting. Rather than allowing market-driven solutions—higher wages, flexible scheduling, and competition among providers to attract talent—Democrats want government to subsidize the caregiver workforce, ultimately raising taxes or redirecting funds from other priorities. The real problem isn't a shortage of government spending; it's regulatory barriers and wage controls that suppress caregiver compensation in the first place.

Colorado taxpayers should demand answers: How much will this "investment" cost? Who decides which programs get funded? And why should government pick winners and losers in the healthcare labor market instead of letting supply and demand work?

Watch for legislative proposals in the 2027 session that expand state healthcare spending under the guise of workforce development.
