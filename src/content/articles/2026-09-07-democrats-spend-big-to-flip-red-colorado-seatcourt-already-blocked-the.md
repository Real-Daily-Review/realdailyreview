# Colorado Democrats Bet Big Money on Flipping Ruby-Red District—But State Court Already Blocked Their Map

Democrat Eileen Laubacher is mounting a well-funded challenge in Colorado's most Republican stronghold, a district where the GOP holds a commanding 2-to-1 voter registration advantage (Colorado Sun). The bet: that money and ground game can overcome structural headwinds in a seat Republicans have dominated for years.

But Democrats face a steeper climb than campaign cash alone can solve. Colorado's high court already rejected a Democratic gerrymandering scheme earlier this year, keeping the state out of the national redistricting wars—at least for now (Complete Colorado). That ruling preserved the current map, meaning Laubacher must win in a district drawn fairly, not engineered for Democratic advantage.

Republicans argue the real test here is whether Democrats can compete on ideas and candidate quality in genuinely conservative terrain, rather than relying on map manipulation. Conservatives note that even with aggressive spending, flipping a 2-to-1 Republican district signals Democratic desperation in what should be safe GOP turf.

For Colorado taxpayers and voters, the takeaway is clear: watch whether Democrats can actually persuade conservative voters on the merits, or whether this becomes another cautionary tale about throwing money at unwinnable seats.
