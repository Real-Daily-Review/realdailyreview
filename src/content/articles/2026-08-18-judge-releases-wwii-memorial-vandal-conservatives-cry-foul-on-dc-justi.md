# Judge Releases Woman Accused of Vandalizing WWII Memorial

A federal judge ordered the release of Melissa Farris, 41, who was arrested after allegedly livestreaming herself vandalizing the World War II Memorial on the National Mall in August, according to *Just The News* and the *Washington Examiner*. The incident drew sharp criticism from President Trump and U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro.

Farris agreed to release conditions including surrendering her passport and obtaining court approval before leaving the continental U.S., court documents show. The decision to release her pending trial has reignited conservative concerns about judicial leniency toward vandals targeting American monuments and war memorials.

Republicans and conservatives have grown increasingly frustrated with what they view as soft-on-crime policies in D.C., where progressive prosecutors and judges have been criticized for releasing defendants accused of damaging federal property and national symbols. The case underscores the broader debate over law and order in the nation's capital—a flashpoint for conservatives who argue that monuments honoring America's military sacrifice deserve maximum legal protection.

While details on the judge's reasoning remain limited, the release decision reflects the ongoing tension between law-and-order advocates and those prioritizing pretrial release standards.
