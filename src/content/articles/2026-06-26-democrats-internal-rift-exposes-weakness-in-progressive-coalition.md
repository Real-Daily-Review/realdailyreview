# Democrats' Infighting Exposes Fractures in Progressive Coalition

New York's Democratic establishment is tearing itself apart over competing visions of party leadership—a cautionary tale for Colorado progressives banking on unified messaging.

New York Attorney General Letitia James recently criticized New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani for "blowing up" the Democratic Party by backing candidates who, James argued, lack understanding of local politics and cultural nuances across districts (Washington Examiner). The spat reveals what conservatives have long argued: the Democratic coalition is held together by rhetoric, not shared values or governance philosophy.

When Democrats must choose between identity-politics candidates and locally rooted candidates, the cracks show. James's complaint—that outsider-backed candidates don't grasp on-the-ground realities—mirrors concerns Colorado Republicans raise about progressive activists imposing one-size-fits-all policies on rural counties, mountain towns, and energy-dependent communities that understand their own needs better than Denver activists do.

Colorado Democrats face similar tensions. Urban progressives push climate mandates and gun restrictions that alienate rural Democrats and working-class voters. Meanwhile, establishment Democrats worry about losing purple districts to Republicans who actually listen to local concerns.
