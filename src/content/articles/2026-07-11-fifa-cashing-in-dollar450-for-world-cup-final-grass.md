# FIFA's World Cup Turf Hustle: $450 for Grass Clippings

FIFA is monetizing everything—now literally selling the grass from the World Cup final field at MetLife Stadium for $450 per segment, according to reports from 9News Denver and the New York Post.

Each turf piece measures 17.5 by 17.5 by 17.5 units (FIFA conveniently omitted whether that's inches, centimeters, or millimeters), making the pricing scheme as murky as the organization's governance. This is classic FIFA: extract maximum revenue from fans while keeping specifications deliberately vague.

The July 19 final will be played on this grass before FIFA chops it up and sells it as memorabilia. It's a fitting metaphor for how international sports bureaucracies operate—strip-mining fan enthusiasm and nostalgia for profit margins while offering minimal transparency.

For Colorado soccer fans considering the splurge, know this: you're paying premium prices for turf of unknown dimensions from an organization with a documented history of financial opacity and corruption. There are cheaper ways to own a piece of World Cup history that don't enrich FIFA's already-bloated coffers.

Watch whether U.S. consumer protection agencies question FIFA's vague product specifications—or whether American fans simply accept the hustle as part of the soccer experience.
