# Colorado AG Joins Multi-State Lawsuit Against Trump Administration's Mail Ballot Rules

Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser has joined a Democratic-led lawsuit challenging new U.S. Postal Service rules on mail-in balloting, according to CPR News. The lawsuit, coordinated with other Democratic attorneys general including New York's Letitia James, argues that the federal government lacks jurisdiction over election administration—a power reserved to the states under the Constitution.

Republicans and conservatives argue that mail-ballot security remains a legitimate concern, and that federal oversight of postal handling of ballots serves election integrity. They contend that Democratic AGs are using litigation to block reasonable safeguards rather than address documented vulnerabilities in mail voting systems.

The Colorado angle matters: mail voting dominates the state's election system, and Weiser's decision to challenge federal postal rules signals the state will resist any Trump administration attempts to tighten mail-ballot procedures. For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, this raises questions about whether mail-voting security will remain a secondary concern to partisan litigation strategy.

Watch whether the courts side with the states' constitutional argument—or whether federal authority over the Postal Service prevails in election administration disputes.
