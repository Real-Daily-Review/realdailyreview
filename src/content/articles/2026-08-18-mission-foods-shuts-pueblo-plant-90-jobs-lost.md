# Mission Foods Shutters Pueblo Plant, 90 Colorado Jobs Gone

Mission Foods is closing its Pueblo tortilla manufacturing facility in October, eliminating 90 jobs in a blow to the region's industrial base.

The company cited operational consolidation as the reason for the permanent shutdown, effective Oct. 13 (KDVR). UFCW Local 7, the union representing affected workers, has criticized the decision, though the sources do not detail specific union demands or proposed alternatives.

The closure raises questions about Colorado's manufacturing competitiveness. While the sources do not attribute the decision to specific state policies, conservatives have long argued that excessive regulations, labor costs, and energy expenses make Colorado less attractive for industrial operations compared to neighboring states. The Pueblo facility's closure underscores the risk when manufacturers face rising operational burdens without corresponding productivity gains.

State economic development officials have not yet announced retention or retraining efforts, according to available reporting.

Colorado taxpayers and business leaders should monitor whether the state responds with targeted incentives to prevent further manufacturing departures—or whether policymakers default to union-friendly rhetoric while ignoring the competitive factors driving companies elsewhere.
