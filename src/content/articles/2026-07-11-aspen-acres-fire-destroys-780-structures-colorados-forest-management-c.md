# Aspen Acres Fire Devastation Exposes Colorado's Wildfire Crisis—and Government's Failure to Prevent It

Colorado's wildfire problem just got worse. The Aspen Acres fire has destroyed at least 780 structures across Pueblo and Custer counties, according to aerial analysis by Vexcel Data—a figure that dwarfs official county estimates still being tallied (Colorado Sun). Among the losses: a historic lodge in Arvada that served generations of Coloradans (9News Denver).

The destruction underscores a hard truth conservatives have long argued: Colorado's forests are choking with fuel, and decades of misguided environmental policies—including fire-suppression dogma and resistance to responsible timber management—have turned our state into a tinderbox. While Democrats and green activists blocked logging and fuel-reduction projects in the name of "ecosystem preservation," real Coloradans lost homes, businesses, and irreplaceable heritage.

The official damage counts remain incomplete, meaning the true toll could climb higher still. Rebuilding will strain local budgets already squeezed by inflation and government overreach. Property owners will face skyrocketing insurance premiums—if insurers stay in Colorado at all.

Conservatives should watch whether state lawmakers finally embrace forest management reforms, or whether environmental groups once again block the fuel-reduction and timber-harvesting projects that could prevent the next catastrophe.
