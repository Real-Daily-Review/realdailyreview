# U.S. Faces Critical Missile Shortage as Global Conflicts Converge

The United States is dangerously low on advanced weaponry precisely when it needs them most, according to analysis in the *Washington Examiner*. Russia's invasion of Ukraine, combined with escalating U.S. confrontation with Iran—now watched closely by China—threatens to merge overlapping geopolitical rivalries into the broadest international conflict since World War II.

The core problem: modern high-intensity warfare depletes precision munitions far faster than current production can replace them. The *Examiner* warns that Patriot missiles and other critical systems are being consumed at unsustainable rates, leaving America's defense posture increasingly vulnerable.

For Colorado, this matters directly. The state hosts critical defense contractors and military installations that would be central to any ramped-up weapons production. A prolonged shortage signals either massive new defense spending—or a strategic vulnerability conservatives argue the Biden-Harris administration failed to anticipate.

Republicans have long warned that underinvestment in military readiness and industrial capacity invites adversaries to test American resolve. Democrats counter that diplomatic solutions and restraint prevent escalation, though that argument rings hollow as conflicts multiply.

Conservatives should watch whether Congress approves emergency defense appropriations and whether Colorado's aerospace and defense sectors get the contracts to rebuild America's depleted arsenal.
