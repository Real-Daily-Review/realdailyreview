# Abortion Pill Access Faces New Court Challenge as Louisiana Holds Delayed Primaries

Federal courts are weighing whether to restrict telemedicine access to mifepristone, one of two pills used in medication abortion, according to NPR. The legal challenge threatens to limit how the drug can be prescribed and delivered to patients nationwide.

Meanwhile, Louisiana is proceeding with primary elections on May 16, though the state's House primary races have been delayed at the direction of Gov. Jeff Landry (R-LA), according to the Washington Examiner. Landry announced the postponement following the Supreme Court's recent ruling in Louisiana v. Callais, which addressed the state's creation of a second minority-majority congressional district.

The abortion pill case represents a significant legal test for medication abortion access in the United States. Courts are currently considering whether to end telemedicine availability for mifepristone, which would substantially reshape how patients access the drug.

Louisiana's primary delays reflect ongoing disputes over congressional district boundaries and voting rights following the Supreme Court decision. The state's House races remain in limbo as legal and political questions about district compliance continue.

Watch for the courts' decision on mifepristone telemedicine access and whether Louisiana's House primary races proceed as rescheduled.
