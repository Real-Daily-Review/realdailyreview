# Socialist Lawmaker's 'Biological Need' Defense for Shoplifting Signals Dangerous Erosion of Property Rights

A New York socialist assemblywoman has declared that shoplifting basic necessities shouldn't be prosecuted because theft satisfies a "biological need"—a position that exposes the left's willingness to abandon property rights protections in the name of progressive ideology.

Assemblywoman Emily Gallagher (D-Brooklyn) argued Thursday that stealing items like toothpaste and soap shouldn't result in arrest, claiming prosecution punishes people for having legitimate survival needs (Daily Caller, Breitbart, Daily Wire).

**The conservative counterargument is straightforward:** Property rights are foundational to a free society. Redefining theft as a justified response to poverty doesn't address root causes—it incentivizes crime, destabilizes retail, and punishes law-abiding business owners already struggling with organized retail theft. If someone lacks basic necessities, the answer is targeted assistance, not decriminalization of theft.

This matters for Colorado. As progressive prosecutors and lawmakers push similar soft-on-crime policies—from reduced shoplifting enforcement to catch-and-release approaches—Colorado retailers and small businesses face mounting losses. The logic Gallagher articulates has already infected Denver and Boulder courtrooms, where theft charges are routinely dismissed or downgraded.

**Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether Colorado Democrats adopt similar "biological need" language in upcoming criminal justice debates.**
