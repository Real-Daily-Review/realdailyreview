# Front Range Voters Face Major Rail Tax Hike Despite RTD's Ongoing Failures

Colorado taxpayers on the Front Range are being asked to approve a new 0.333% sales and use tax estimated to raise $295 million annually for the Colorado Connector passenger rail project—a hefty ask that comes as the state's existing commuter rail systems continue to underperform, according to *Complete Colorado*.

The ballot question would fund the southern CoCo route from Denver to Pueblo, *CPR News* reports. But conservatives question the timing and logic: why expand rail spending when RTD, Colorado's flagship transit agency, remains plagued by operational and financial problems? Throwing another tax at Front Range voters before fixing existing systems smells like government doubling down on failed models rather than learning from them.

The tax hike arrives as Colorado voters have signaled skepticism of new levies. Recent ballot cycles show Coloradans rejecting progressive tax schemes and supporting lower rates—a signal the rail district may be ignoring, *Complete Colorado* notes.

On the flip side, November will also feature a measure to cap Colorado's state income tax rate, giving voters a chance to lock in tax restraint statewide.
