# U.S. Military Sinks Drug Cartel Vessel in Pacific

The U.S. military took direct action against narco-trafficking infrastructure, sinking a vessel in the Pacific tied to Ecuador's Los Choneros organization, which the U.S. recently designated a terrorist group.

According to U.S. Southern Command, the crew was operating the vessel to support at-sea drug refueling and trafficking operations for the cartel (Just The News, New York Post). The captured crew will be turned over to Ecuadorian authorities.

The operation underscores a critical national security priority: disrupting the supply chains that flood American streets with cocaine and fentanyl. While the Biden-Harris administration has struggled to secure the southern border—allowing record drug seizures and migrant flows—direct military action against cartel infrastructure abroad represents a more muscular counternarcotics approach.

For Colorado, this matters. Fentanyl and cocaine trafficking through Mexican cartels connected to Los Choneros has devastated communities statewide. Colorado has seen overdose deaths spike as cartels flood the market with synthetic opioids. Conservatives argue that border security and aggressive interdiction must work hand-in-hand; military strikes alone cannot substitute for a sealed border and interior enforcement.

Watch whether the administration escalates similar operations or pivots resources to the southern border—where the real vulnerability lies.
