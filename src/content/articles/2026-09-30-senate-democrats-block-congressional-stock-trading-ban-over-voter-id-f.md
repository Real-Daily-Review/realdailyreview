# Senate Democrats Kill Stock Trading Ban Over Voter ID Dispute

Congress just proved it's more interested in protecting its own insider-trading privileges than cleaning up Capitol Hill corruption—and Colorado taxpayers are watching their representatives choose partisan gridlock over accountability.

The Senate voted down the "Stop Insider Trading Act" 53-47 on Sept. 30, with all Senate Democrats blocking the measure using the filibuster (Washington Examiner). The House had already passed the bill with bipartisan support, including 13 Democrats (Just The News), but Senate Democrats killed it over a voter ID requirement attached to the legislation—calling it a "poison pill" rather than addressing the stock-trading ban itself (Washington Examiner).

Republicans argue the move exposes Democratic hypocrisy: they claim to oppose corruption and insider trading, yet they unified to block a ban rather than negotiate the voter ID language. The bill would have prevented lawmakers from trading stocks while in office—a basic conflict-of-interest reform that polls show most Americans support across party lines.

Democrats counter the voter ID provision was a partisan add-on designed to poison the bill, not a genuine anti-corruption measure.

Colorado voters should demand their representatives—especially Democratic senators—explain why they chose partisan theater over stopping members of Congress from profiting off non-public information while making laws that affect Colorado's economy and energy sector.

Watch whether House Democrats will pressure Senate leadership to bring a clean stock-trading ban to a vote without the voter ID language.
