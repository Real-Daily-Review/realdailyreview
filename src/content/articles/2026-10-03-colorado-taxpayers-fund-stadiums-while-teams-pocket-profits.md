# Taxpayer-Funded Stadiums: A Raw Deal for Colorado Sports Fans

Colorado taxpayers keep bankrolling professional sports venues while teams pocket the profits—a sweetheart arrangement that exemplifies government waste masquerading as economic development.

The Washington Examiner highlights a telling symbol of this lopsided deal: foul balls and pucks that once belonged to fans now belong to teams. What was once a cherished, tangible connection between spectators and the game—a kid's glove-catching memory—has been privatized by franchises that already benefit from publicly financed stadiums.

This matters in Colorado, where taxpayers have subsidized major league facilities while teams reap ticket sales, concessions, and merchandise revenue. The arrangement follows a familiar playbook: government assumes construction and maintenance costs, teams capture the upside.

**Conservatives argue** this represents crony capitalism at its worst—politicians using public funds to attract or retain franchises, then claiming economic benefits that rarely materialize as promised. Taxpayers bear the risk; owners pocket the reward.

The broader question: Why should Colorado families struggling with property taxes and inflation subsidize billionaire team owners? Public money should fund schools, roads, and public safety—not luxury boxes and private profits.

Watch whether Colorado legislators push back on future stadium subsidy requests or continue greasing the skids for professional sports franchises.
