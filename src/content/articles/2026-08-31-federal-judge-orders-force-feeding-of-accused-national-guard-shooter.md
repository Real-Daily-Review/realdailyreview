# Colorado Angle on National Guard Shooting Case

A federal judge has ordered force-feeding for the man accused of shooting National Guard troops in Washington, D.C., after he attempted to starve himself in custody (Washington Examiner, Just The News). Rahmanullah Lakanwal stands accused of killing National Guard Specialist Sarah Beckstrom and wounding Staff Sergeant Andrew Wolfe in a downtown D.C. attack. U.S. District Judge Amit Mehta, an Obama appointee, authorized "nonconsensual nutrition and medical treatment" over the weekend.

While the case centers on the nation's capital, it underscores a broader concern for Colorado's National Guard members and military families: the vulnerability of service members to violent attack, even in supposedly secure areas. Colorado's Guard units have deployed extensively in recent years, and incidents like this raise hard questions about force protection and vetting procedures.

Lakanwal has pleaded not guilty. The case will likely draw scrutiny over how federal courts balance detention procedures with constitutional protections—a tension conservatives argue should never excuse lax security around military personnel or federal facilities.
