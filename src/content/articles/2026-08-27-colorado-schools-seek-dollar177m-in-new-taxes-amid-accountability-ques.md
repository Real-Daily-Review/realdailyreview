# Colorado School Districts Push $177M in New Tax Asks While Dodging Accountability

Jefferson County Schools is asking taxpayers for $133 million more—even as the district battles federal litigation over its transgender-student policies—while Denver Public Schools seeks a $44 million mill levy override, with $25.8 million earmarked for employee raises rather than classroom improvements.

The dual tax pushes expose a familiar pattern: Colorado school districts expanding spending requests without demonstrating fiscal discipline or addressing core competency concerns. Jeffco's ask arrives amid ongoing federal court battles, raising questions about whether the district's priorities align with parent concerns over curriculum and student safety rather than administrative overhead. Denver's proposal to funnel most new revenue toward employee compensation—not educational outcomes—illustrates how union-friendly districts prioritize payroll over performance.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue these requests ignore Colorado's broader affordability crisis. Taxpayers already shoulder substantial property tax burdens; districts should prove they're stewarding existing dollars efficiently before demanding more. Neither proposal includes meaningful accountability metrics or spending caps.

Democrats and school boards counter that teacher shortages and facility maintenance require investment, and that competitive wages attract talent.

**Watch for:** November ballot language and whether either district faces organized taxpayer opposition campaigns—a potential bellwether for Colorado's appetite for education spending in an election year.
