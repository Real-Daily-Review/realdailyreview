# St. Mary's Glacier Gone: Colorado's Iconic Landmark Melts Away

Colorado just lost one of its most recognizable natural landmarks—St. Mary's Glacier in Clear Creek County has completely melted, leaving visitors and locals stunned.

The disappearance follows an abnormally hot summer compounding a record snow drought, according to CPR News. Visitors expecting to hike to a glacier are now finding what one observer called "just a big rock pile" (CPR News). The Fox31 Denver crew documented the shock among hikers arriving to discover the landmark simply gone.

This isn't a political story—yet. But it underscores a real concern for Colorado water managers and energy producers: climate variability directly impacts snowpack, which feeds reservoirs, irrigation systems, and hydroelectric generation across the state. Colorado's water-dependent economy—agriculture, energy, municipal supplies—depends on reliable snowfall and glacier melt.

Conservatives and water-rights advocates have long argued that Colorado needs pragmatic, market-driven water management and responsible energy development to adapt to natural cycles, not restrictive regulations that hamstring agriculture and industry. The state's push toward renewable energy mandates and water restrictions on farmers should account for real-world variability, not ideology.
