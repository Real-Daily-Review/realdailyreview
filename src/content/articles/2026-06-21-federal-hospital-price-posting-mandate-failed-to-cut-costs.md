# Federal Price-Posting Mandate Failed to Lower Hospital Costs—Another Government Fix That Backfired

Washington's five-year experiment in forcing hospitals to post prices has produced massive compliance paperwork but zero market competition and no cost relief for patients.

The Obama administration bet that transparency would create a real market and drive down healthcare costs. Hospitals dutifully posted their price files—now running to "enormous scale," according to the *Washington Examiner*. The result? Researchers examining the data found the market never materialized and prices never fell.

This is textbook government overreach: regulators assume mandates will work, impose them anyway, and when they fail, double down on compliance rather than admit the premise was flawed. The posted files are so complex and voluminous that patients still can't shop effectively. Hospitals still lack real price competition. Costs keep climbing.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Government price-fixing and transparency mandates don't create markets—they create bureaucratic theater. Real healthcare cost control requires actual competition, patient choice, and removing regulatory barriers that prevent new providers from entering the market. Colorado families and businesses paying premiums for employer health plans are still waiting for relief that Washington's mandates promised but never delivered.

Watch whether Congress will admit this experiment failed or simply expand the mandate further.
