# Colorado Republicans Eye 2028 as National GOP Regroups After Midterm Losses

Republicans are already looking ahead to 2028 after what the *Washington Examiner* calls a significant midterm defeat that has scrambled the party's presidential calculus. The loss has effectively ended one potential 2028 race and opened the door to a wide-open GOP primary, according to conservative analysts.

On the Democratic side, *National Review* reports that despite the left's electoral gains, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez faces substantial headwinds should she pursue a 2028 presidential bid—suggesting the progressive wing may struggle to consolidate power even after recent victories.

For Colorado Republicans, the 2028 landscape matters enormously. The state has trended purple in recent cycles, and a fractured GOP primary could either energize the base or splinter the vote in a general election that will determine whether Colorado remains competitive for Republicans or slides further blue. Colorado's independent voters—who decide statewide races—will be watching whether the GOP can unite behind a coherent message on taxes, energy policy, and border security.

The real question for Colorado conservatives: will the party learn from midterm losses and nominate a candidate who can reclaim suburban voters without abandoning the limited-government principles that built the GOP?
