# Colorado's Data Centers Power the Digital Economy—While Activists Want to Shut Them Down

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers aren't luxury amenities—they're critical infrastructure that keeps the modern world running, yet environmental activists and progressive regulators continue treating them as obstacles to be regulated into oblivion.

Data centers are the backbone of cloud computing, artificial intelligence, streaming services, and digital commerce that millions of Americans—including Coloradans—depend on daily. *Complete Colorado* reports these facilities represent a genuine economic engine for the state, generating jobs and tax revenue while supporting innovation across every sector of the economy.

Yet the left's reflexive hostility toward energy-intensive infrastructure threatens Colorado's competitive advantage. Progressive policymakers have increasingly targeted data centers with restrictive environmental regulations and water-use restrictions, treating them as villains rather than vital economic assets. Conservatives argue that Colorado should embrace—not obstruct—the industries driving 21st-century prosperity.

The stakes are straightforward: Colorado can either position itself as a data-center hub that attracts investment and talent, or it can follow California's regulatory playbook and watch those jobs migrate to Texas, Utah, and other states with friendlier business climates.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to impose new data-center regulations or water restrictions that could push operators elsewhere.
