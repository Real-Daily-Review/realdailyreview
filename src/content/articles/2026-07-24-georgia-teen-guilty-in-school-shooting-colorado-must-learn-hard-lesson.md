# Georgia Teen Pleads Guilty in School Shooting; Colorado Faces Its Own Security Reckoning

Colt Gray, 16, pleaded guilty Friday to all 55 counts stemming from the September 2024 shooting at Apalachee High School in Georgia that killed four people—two teachers and two students. Gray entered the plea without a prosecutorial deal, according to the *Washington Examiner* and *New York Post*.

The case underscores a hard reality: no amount of gun control legislation stops determined criminals or deeply troubled minors from obtaining weapons and committing mass violence. While progressives predictably call for stricter firearms laws, conservatives argue the focus should be on mental health screening, school security hardening, and enforcing existing laws—not restricting Second Amendment rights of law-abiding citizens.

Colorado has experienced its own school shooting tragedies, including Columbine (1999) and the STEM School shooting in Highlands Ranch (2019). Rather than chase feel-good gun bans that criminals ignore, Colorado conservatives argue the state should invest in threat assessment programs, armed school resource officers, and mental health intervention—proven deterrents that respect constitutional rights.

Gray's guilty plea closes one chapter but raises urgent questions: How do we identify and intervene with at-risk youth before tragedy strikes? Colorado policymakers should demand accountability on school safety spending and results, not reflexive gun restrictions.
