# Federal Court Backs Election Integrity: Texas Mail-Ballot ID Rules Stand

A federal appeals court has sided with election security, restoring Texas's mail-in voter ID requirements after rejecting legal challenges from voting-rights groups. The 5th Circuit upheld key provisions of Senate Bill 1, affirming that states have legitimate authority to verify voter identity and prevent fraud—a principle Colorado conservatives should champion as their own state faces pressure to loosen ballot security.

Meanwhile, Georgia Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger has empaneled an election integrity task force, signaling renewed focus on election administration following the 2020 presidential contest that drew national scrutiny.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Election integrity protects every legitimate voter's ballot from dilution. Democrats and progressive groups argue that ID requirements suppress turnout, but Republicans counter that basic verification—standard in banking, voting, and countless daily transactions—is neither burdensome nor discriminatory. The Texas ruling validates this position.

**Colorado angle:** State Democrats have pushed mail-ballot expansion and resisted voter ID measures. This Texas precedent strengthens arguments for tighter ballot security in Colorado, where election integrity remains contested between those prioritizing access and those prioritizing verification.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans use this ruling to advance election-security legislation in the 2025 session—or whether Democratic majorities double down on ballot expansion despite growing judicial skepticism.
