# Trump's Press Ban Draws Fire From Right and Left—But for Different Reasons

Donald Trump's restrictions on press access are backfiring, drawing criticism even from conservative outlets that normally support his agenda. *National Review* argues Trump should rescind the ban outright, calling it "a violation of the spirit of a free press" and urging him to confront hostile reporters directly instead (*National Review*). Meanwhile, Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin compares the move to Trump's Iran policy, calling it another strategic disaster.

Republicans and conservatives have long complained about hostile media coverage. But there's a crucial distinction: opposing biased reporting is not the same as restricting press access. The First Amendment protects even annoying journalists. Trump's ban risks handing the left a legitimate grievance and undermines the conservative case for press accountability through argument, not censorship.

For Colorado, this matters. State media outlets rely on access to cover state and federal policy affecting water rights, energy development, and gun laws. Press restrictions set a precedent that could trickle down to state officials.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump reverses course—and whether Colorado Republicans distance themselves from federal press restrictions or follow suit.
