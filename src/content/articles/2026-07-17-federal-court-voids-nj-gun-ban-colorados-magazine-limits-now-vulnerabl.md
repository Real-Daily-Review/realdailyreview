# Federal Court Strikes Down New Jersey Gun Ban—Colorado's Own Restrictions Now in Legal Crosshairs

A federal appeals court has dealt a serious blow to state-level gun bans, ruling that New Jersey's restrictions on AR-15s and high-capacity magazines violate the Second Amendment. The 3rd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, in a 10-5 decision, found that firearms and magazines "in common use for lawful purposes, including self-defense," are constitutionally protected (Just The News).

**The Colorado angle matters.** Colorado has its own magazine-capacity restrictions—a 15-round limit enacted in 2013—that now face similar legal vulnerability. Conservatives argue these bans ignore Supreme Court precedent (*Heller*, *McDonald*) establishing that the Second Amendment protects commonly owned firearms for self-defense. The New Jersey ruling reinforces that argument.

Democrats and gun-control advocates counter that such restrictions serve public safety and reduce mass-shooting casualties. They typically argue states retain authority to regulate firearms under the Second Amendment's text.

The 3rd Circuit's majority concluded that semiautomatic rifles and standard magazines fall squarely within constitutional protection. This reasoning directly threatens Colorado's magazine law, which gun-rights groups have challenged repeatedly (New York Post).

**Watch for Colorado gun-rights organizations to file or accelerate lawsuits challenging the state's 15-round limit in federal court—and expect Democratic lawmakers to double down on defending it.**
