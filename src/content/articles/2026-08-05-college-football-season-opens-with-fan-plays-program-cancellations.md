# College Football Season Kicks Off With Fan-Called Plays—And Program Cancellations

The University of Akron is letting one lucky fan call plays for the team's opening drive against Robert Morris on September 12, a gimmick that underscores how creative programs are getting to engage supporters as college football season approaches. But the novelty masks a darker trend: Los Angeles Pierce College abruptly shuttered its football program just weeks before kickoff, leaving student-athletes scrambling to find new teams mid-season.

The Akron sweepstakes rewards season-ticket holders with a chance to design the Zips' first offensive series—a marketing move that generates buzz and donor engagement without breaking the bank. It's the kind of low-cost activation schools need as they navigate tighter budgets and shifting priorities.

Pierce College's sudden cancellation, however, reveals the financial pressures crushing mid-tier college athletics. The decision left players in limbo and raises questions about institutional planning and commitment to student-athletes. When a school kills a program *weeks* before the season starts, it signals either catastrophic mismanagement or a desperate cost-cutting measure.

For Colorado schools fielding college football programs—from CU Boulder to smaller regional programs—this is a cautionary tale: athletic departments must balance fan engagement gimmicks with sustainable operations, or risk becoming the next Pierce College.

Watch whether other programs follow Pierce's lead as fall enrollment and budget cycles tighten.
