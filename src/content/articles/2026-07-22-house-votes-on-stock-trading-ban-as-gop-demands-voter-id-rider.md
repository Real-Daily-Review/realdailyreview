# House Poised to Vote on Congressional Stock Trading Ban—With a Catch

Congress is finally moving to ban its own members from trading stocks while in office, but Republicans are using the moment to push voter ID requirements—a sign that even good-government reform becomes a bargaining chip in Washington.

The House is expected to vote on the Stop Insider Trading Act, which would prohibit members of Congress and their families from buying new individual stocks (Just The News). The bill doesn't prevent lawmakers from *holding* existing positions, a loophole that limits its teeth.

Republicans have added a voter ID measure to the legislation, according to CPR News, signaling they won't let Democrats claim the moral high ground on ethics without conceding ground on election integrity. It's a shrewd move: Democrats have long opposed voter ID requirements, calling them voter suppression, while Republicans argue they're essential safeguards against fraud.

The real question isn't whether Congress should stop insider trading—most Americans across the political spectrum agree it should—but whether either party genuinely wants to close loopholes or simply wants to score political points. The half-measure of allowing lawmakers to keep existing stock positions while banning new purchases suggests this is more theater than reform.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether the voter ID rider survives or gets stripped in committee—that'll tell you whether Republicans are serious about the trade-off or willing to fold.
