# New Deal Ghost Town Warns Against Government Social Engineering

A 93-year-old West Virginia community built as a New Deal experiment offers a cautionary tale for conservatives skeptical of federal social-engineering schemes. Arthurdale, established under Franklin D. Roosevelt's subsistence-homesteads program, was designed to lift impoverished families out of poverty through government-directed relocation and community planning—a model the *Washington Examiner* examines as a historical debate over socialism's practical limits.

The experiment, launched in Preston County, represents the kind of top-down federal intervention that conservatives argue inevitably fails when bureaucrats replace market forces and individual choice. Rather than sustainable prosperity, such programs typically create dependency and waste taxpayer dollars on utopian fantasies disconnected from local economic reality.

Colorado taxpayers should note the parallel: state and federal governments continue proposing similar interventions—from subsidized housing initiatives to "climate communities" and workforce-development schemes—all premised on the belief that planners in Denver or Washington know better than individuals and markets. The Arthurdale precedent suggests they don't.

As progressive policymakers push expanded government programs under the banner of "equity" and "community development," conservatives must demand evidence that such experiments actually work—not just good intentions funded by your tax dollars.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado lawmakers cite Arthurdale's lessons when evaluating new federal grant programs and state-funded social-engineering initiatives.
