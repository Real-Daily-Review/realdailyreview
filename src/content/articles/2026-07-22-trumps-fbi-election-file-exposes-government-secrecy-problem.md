# Trump's 2020 Election File Raises Questions About FBI Transparency

Trump's disclosure of a previously unreleased FBI file on 2020 election fraud allegations has reignited the transparency debate—and exposed why government agencies resist public scrutiny without legal pressure.

According to the Washington Examiner, the file's emergence underscores a fundamental problem: governments don't volunteer embarrassment. As the outlet notes, compliance officers exist precisely because institutions hide damaging information unless forced to disclose it. The principle, dating back to Justice Louis Brandeis, remains unchanged—sunlight remains the best disinfectant.

**Democrats counter** that Trump's fraud claims lack credible evidence and note, per the Denver Post's letters section, that Trump doesn't claim rigging when Republicans win elections, including his own victories. This inconsistency, critics argue, undermines the legitimacy of his allegations.

**Conservatives and Republicans** argue the real issue isn't Trump's claims but the FBI's initial withholding of documents. If the file contained nothing damaging, why wasn't it public? Colorado voters—who've endured years of election integrity debates following 2021's Tina Peters controversy—deserve to know what federal agencies know about election security vulnerabilities.

The Colorado angle: state election officials and taxpayers should demand full transparency on what federal records exist regarding voting systems and mail-in ballot security.

Watch whether Colorado's Secretary of State requests the full FBI file and what it reveals about election administration gaps.
