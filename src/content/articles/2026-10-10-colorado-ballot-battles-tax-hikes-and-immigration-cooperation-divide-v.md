# Colorado Ballot Measures Face Competing Visions on Taxes, Immigration

Colorado voters face a consequential fall ballot that pits tax-hike proponents against fiscal conservatives and puts sanctuary-law critics at odds with immigrant-rights advocates—but mainstream media is glossing over the real stakes, conservatives argue.

**Amendment 87**, a tax increase on high earners and businesses, is drawing fire from fiscal hawks who say Colorado media outlets aren't adequately explaining the full impact on job creators and working families. Complete Colorado reports that voters deserve "the full picture" on how the measure would reshape the state's tax code, yet coverage remains incomplete (Complete Colorado).

Meanwhile, **Amendment 81** would loosen Colorado's sanctuary restrictions and allow local law enforcement to cooperate with federal immigration authorities—a move that has split even some police officials. The Denver Post reports critics warn the measure could enable racial profiling and other civil-liberties risks, though supporters argue it would improve public safety coordination (Denver Post).

**Republicans argue** Amendment 87 would drive businesses and high earners out of state while funding more government bloat. **Democrats counter** that the wealthy should pay their fair share to fund schools and services.

On immigration, **conservatives say** sanctuary laws handcuff law enforcement and protect criminals; **the left warns** Amendment 81 invites discrimination.

Watch for late-October polling and whether Colorado media finally covers the full fiscal and policy implications before Election Day.
