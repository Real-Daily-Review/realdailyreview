# House Forces Disclosure of Taxpayer-Funded Sexual Misconduct Settlements

Congress just admitted it's been hiding how your tax dollars bankroll lawmakers' sexual harassment cover-ups.

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) forced a nearly unanimous House vote Tuesday requiring public disclosure of all taxpayer-funded sexual misconduct settlements involving members, according to Fox News and the Washington Examiner. The resolution directs the House Ethics Committee to preserve and release records on which congressmen have settled sexual harassment claims using federal money—settlements that have remained largely secret.

This is government accountability working as intended: sunlight as disinfectant. For years, House leadership has quietly settled misconduct allegations with taxpayer cash while keeping the public in the dark about which members were involved and how much was paid out. Massie's push exposes the cozy arrangement where Congress polices itself behind closed doors.

The near-unanimous vote suggests even Democrats recognize the political liability of defending secrecy on this issue. But the real test comes next: whether the Ethics Committee actually complies with full transparency or finds ways to redact, delay, or minimize the damage.

Colorado taxpayers deserve to know if their representatives—or any federal lawmakers—have been using their money to silence accusers.

Watch whether the House Ethics Committee drags its feet on releasing these records or moves swiftly to restore public trust.
