# Teens' Red Rocks Hike Ends in Rescue—Another Reminder That Colorado's Outdoors Demands Respect

Two teenagers learned an expensive lesson in preparedness this week when a casual hike back from a Red Rocks concert turned into a rescue operation. West Metro Fire Rescue responded to the lost hikers Wednesday evening after the teens attempted to walk from the concert venue back to Morrison without proper planning, navigation tools, or apparently a basic grasp of Colorado's terrain (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The incident underscores a growing problem: unprepared visitors treating Colorado's mountains and foothills like theme parks. While the teens were fortunate to be rescued without serious injury, taxpayers footed the bill for emergency responders—a cost that multiplies when outdoor inexperience meets overconfidence.

This isn't a knock on teenagers; it's a reality check on the culture of casual risk-taking that pervades Colorado tourism. The state's natural beauty is a draw, but it demands respect, preparation, and basic competence. No GPS? No headlamp? No water? No business being on a Colorado trail after dark.

West Metro Fire Rescue's Facebook post nailed it: "It seemed like a great idea—until it was time to find their way back."

**Watch for:** whether Colorado continues absorbing rescue costs for preventable incidents or whether outdoor venues and local governments push stronger messaging on trail safety and personal responsibility.
