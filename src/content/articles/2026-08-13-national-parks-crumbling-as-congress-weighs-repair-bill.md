# National Parks Face Crumbling Infrastructure as Congress Weighs Repair Funding

America's national parks are economic engines—but they're literally falling apart. With 332 million visits in 2024 generating $56.3 billion in economic output and supporting over 340,000 jobs nationwide, parks like Rocky Mountain and Great Sand Dunes are vital to Colorado's tourism economy and rural communities (Washington Examiner). Yet deferred maintenance threatens these crown jewels.

The challenge: Congress must decide whether to fund repairs without ballooning the federal budget. Conservatives argue that parks should operate efficiently within existing appropriations rather than demand endless new spending. Republicans counter that strategic infrastructure investment in parks pays dividends—visitor spending directly supports Colorado gateway towns, local businesses, and jobs that depend on access to public lands.

The real question for Colorado taxpayers: Will Congress fund park repairs responsibly, or use "infrastructure" as cover for wasteful spending? Colorado's outdoor economy depends on well-maintained trails, facilities, and access—but not at the cost of fiscal recklessness.

**Watch whether Congress ties park funding to spending cuts elsewhere, or simply adds to the deficit.**
