# Wyoming Graduate Student Arrested for Alleged Poisoning of Colleague

A University of Wyoming graduate student from China has been arrested at LAX while allegedly attempting to flee to China after poisoning a fellow student's tea, authorities say.

The suspect, identified in reports, allegedly purchased acrylamide—a toxic chemical—over eBay and added it to her colleague's beverage. According to the New York Post, the poisoning was discovered when the victim's wife and others tested water from the coffee maker he used to brew tea. The alleged motive centers on a dispute over pending patents, suggesting intellectual property theft may have been at play.

The arrest raises serious questions about university security protocols, international student vetting, and the ease with which dangerous chemicals remain available for online purchase. Wyoming authorities are investigating how a graduate student obtained poison without triggering red flags—a gap that should concern any institution hosting international researchers with access to sensitive work.

The case also underscores vulnerabilities in campus safety and the need for stronger oversight of chemical sales to individuals with suspicious purchasing patterns. As universities across the Mountain West host growing numbers of international students, this incident demands scrutiny of background checks and monitoring systems.
