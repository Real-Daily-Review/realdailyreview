# Trump Administration Hikes Citizenship Application Fees by $570

The Trump administration is proposing a significant increase in citizenship application fees—a move that would price out working-class immigrants while eliminating reduced-fee options and fee waivers entirely, according to 9News Denver.

The $570 fee hike represents a substantial barrier to legal immigration pathways. Conservatives are split on the move: some view it as a reasonable cost-recovery measure that discourages frivolous applications and reduces government subsidy of the naturalization process. Others, citing National Review's reporting on the administration's broader competence agenda, argue the fee structure should reflect genuine administrative costs without becoming punitive to those pursuing lawful citizenship.

The policy raises a distinctly Colorado question: how will this affect the state's workforce in agriculture, construction, and hospitality—sectors that depend on legal immigrant labor? Colorado employers already face tight labor markets; pricing citizenship applications higher could slow workforce integration and reduce tax revenue from newly naturalized workers.

Democrats will likely argue the fee hike disproportionately harms low-income applicants and contradicts pro-immigration rhetoric. Republicans counter that citizenship should have meaningful costs and that eliminating fee waivers prevents abuse of the system.

**Watch for:** Congressional pushback and whether Colorado business groups—particularly in agriculture and energy—lobby the administration to reconsider the waiver elimination.
