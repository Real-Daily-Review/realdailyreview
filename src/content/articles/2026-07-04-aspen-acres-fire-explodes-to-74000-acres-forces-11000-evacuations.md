# Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire Explodes to 8th Largest on Record as 11,000 Evacuate

Colorado is battling one of its worst fire seasons in years, with the Aspen Acres Fire southwest of Pueblo now consuming over 74,000 acres and forcing 11,000 residents to flee their homes, according to the Colorado Sun. The blaze has exhibited "extreme fire behavior" as thousands of firefighters mobilize across the state.

The fire's rapid spread underscores a critical question conservatives and taxpayers should demand answers on: What forest management policies—or lack thereof—allowed fuel loads to accumulate to dangerous levels? Years of environmental litigation blocking timber harvests and controlled burns have left Colorado's forests tinderbox-dry, a reality that should inform future land-use decisions.

On the ground, crews are showing what American ingenuity looks like. KDVR reports that a bridge destroyed by the fire has already been temporarily rebuilt as a one-lane crossing, allowing evacuation routes and supply lines to function. That's the kind of rapid, practical response that gets people to safety—no bureaucratic delays.

Containment is being gained on other fires statewide, but the Aspen Acres blaze remains a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis demands serious reckoning with forest stewardship, not just emergency response.

Watch whether state and federal officials will finally prioritize aggressive forest thinning and fuel reduction over environmental litigation that leaves our forests defenseless.
