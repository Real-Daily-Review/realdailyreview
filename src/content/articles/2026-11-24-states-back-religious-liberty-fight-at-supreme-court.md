# Religious Liberty Cases Head to Supreme Court as States Test Limits

Colorado and other states are backing religious organizations in two major Supreme Court fights that could reshape how government treats faith-based institutions—and whether states can use regulation to sidestep religious liberty protections.

A Kentucky Catholic community's Virgin Mary prayer grotto has triggered a zoning dispute now before the high court under the Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act (RLUIPA). Multiple states, including Colorado, have filed briefs supporting the church, arguing that local zoning boards shouldn't weaponize land-use rules to suppress religious exercise (Fox News Politics).

Simultaneously, Catholic schools in Maine are challenging the state's attempt to circumvent a 2022 Supreme Court ruling that required Maine to include religious schools in its tuition assistance program. Maine rewrote its funding criteria after losing that case—a move Catholic schools say is deliberate obstruction (Washington Examiner). Conservatives argue states are using regulatory creativity to accomplish through the back door what courts have already forbidden.

For Colorado, these cases matter directly. State and local governments here could face similar pressure to either fund religious institutions fairly or face constitutional challenges. The outcomes will determine whether progressive jurisdictions can use zoning, licensing, and funding rules to marginalize faith-based organizations.

Watch whether the Supreme Court signals it will police state attempts to evade religious liberty rulings through legislative workarounds.
