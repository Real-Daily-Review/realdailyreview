# Boulder Valley School District Settles with ADL Over Parental Rights Dispute

Colorado's Boulder Valley School District has capitulated to the Anti-Defamation League in a settlement that raises fresh questions about parental authority over children's classroom instruction.

The district reached an agreement with the ADL and the family the organization was representing, though specific terms remain undisclosed (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The settlement follows mounting pressure from progressive advocacy groups over curriculum and opt-out policies.

The dispute echoes a parallel case in Washington state, where a father sued his school district for denying his religious opt-out request during Pride Month lessons for his fifth-grader (New York Post). The Alliance Defending Freedom's Center for Parental Rights argued that "parents have the fundamental right to direct their children's religious upbringing and education, even at school."

**The conservative argument:** Parents—not activist organizations or school bureaucrats—should retain final say over their children's exposure to ideologically charged material, particularly on matters touching religion and sexuality. When districts deny opt-outs or settle with groups like the ADL without transparency, they signal that parental consent is negotiable.

**What's at stake:** Colorado parents should demand full disclosure of Boulder Valley's settlement terms and scrutinize whether the district has abandoned meaningful opt-out protections. Watch whether other Colorado districts face similar pressure from national advocacy groups to restrict parental involvement in curriculum decisions.
