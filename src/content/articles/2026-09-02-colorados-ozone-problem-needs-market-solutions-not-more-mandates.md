# Colorado Ozone Crisis Demands Real Solutions, Not Just Regulation

Colorado's Front Range faces a genuine air-quality problem—but the state's answer shouldn't be another round of government mandates that strangle energy development and cost taxpayers hundreds of millions without proven results.

A new study cited by 9News confirms what Coloradans already know: ozone pollution on the Front Range carries real health and economic costs. The research underscores a legitimate environmental challenge that demands attention.

Here's where conservatives and the state's environmental bureaucracy diverge. Rather than reflexively expanding regulations on oil and gas operators, power plants, and vehicles—measures that drive up energy costs and push production out of state—Colorado should pursue market-driven solutions: cleaner technology adoption incentivized by competition, not mandates; targeted infrastructure improvements in high-pollution corridors; and transparent cost-benefit analysis before imposing new rules.

The danger: Colorado regulators often impose restrictions that devastate local energy workers and businesses while merely shifting emissions to other states with looser standards. That's not environmental progress—it's economic self-sabotage.

Republicans and conservatives argue the state must balance legitimate air-quality concerns against the livelihoods of Colorado's energy sector and the affordability of power for working families. Progressives typically push harder regulations regardless of economic impact.
