# Trump Administration Approves $2.8B Weapons Sale to Israel

The Trump administration has cleared a $2.8 billion weapons package for Israel—40,000 powerful bombs including 20,000 MK-84s and 20,000 BLU-117s—reversing the Biden administration's previous hold on the sale, according to the Washington Examiner and 9News Denver.

The deal marks one of the largest U.S. weapons transactions with Israel in recent years. U.S. officials confirmed the package on Tuesday, though final approval remains pending.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the sale demonstrates Trump's commitment to Israel's security and America's Middle East alliance. Supporters contend that providing defensive capabilities strengthens a key democratic partner facing regional threats and reasserts U.S. resolve after the Biden administration's restrictions.

**The progressive pushback:** Democrats and left-leaning groups have opposed large weapons sales to Israel, citing concerns over civilian casualties in Gaza and arguing such transfers enable policies they view as excessive force.

For Colorado, this underscores the state's role in defense manufacturing and aerospace—industries that depend on stable foreign military sales and government contracts. Colorado's defense contractors and workers benefit from sustained military procurement.

Watch whether Congress moves to block the sale under the Arms Control Act, a power Democrats may attempt to exercise.
