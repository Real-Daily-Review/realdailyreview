# Former Mountain View Official Avoids Prison for Shooting Teen on His Property

A former Mountain View town councilman's light sentence—work release and probation instead of jail time—raises hard questions about self-defense law and property rights in Colorado.

Brent Metz was convicted of illegal discharge of a firearm and third-degree assault after shooting a 17-year-old who trespassed on his property in 2024 (9News Denver, CPR News). The teen and a friend had entered Metz's land to scout a photo location before homecoming.

The sentencing—three months work release and four years probation—reflects Colorado's restrictive approach to property defense. While Metz owned the land, prosecutors successfully argued the shooting was excessive, and a jury agreed. Conservatives note this outcome illustrates how Colorado's self-defense statutes have narrowed over time, making it legally risky for property owners to forcefully eject trespassers.

**The conservative concern:** Colorado law increasingly favors the trespasser's safety over the landowner's right to protect his property. Unlike states with stronger castle-doctrine protections, Colorado requires property owners to exhaust retreat options before using force—even on their own land. For ranchers and rural Coloradans already battling trespassing, poaching, and vandalism, this case signals the courts won't back aggressive property defense.
