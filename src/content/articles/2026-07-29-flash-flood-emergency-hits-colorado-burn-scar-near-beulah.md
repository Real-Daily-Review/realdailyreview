# Flash Flood Emergency Hits Colorado Burn Scar—First Since August

Colorado's vulnerability to catastrophic flooding in fire-scarred terrain struck again Tuesday when heavy rain triggered a Flash Flood Emergency near Beulah, the first such alert anywhere in the state since last August (9News Denver). The Aspen Acres burn scar—terrain stripped of vegetation by wildfire and unable to absorb rainfall—became a conduit for dangerous water flows, forcing Highway 78 closures and evacuation orders as residents scrambled to higher ground (KDVR Fox31).

The National Weather Service issued the warning until 8 p.m., with CPR News reporting that authorities urged residents to avoid unnecessary travel in the affected areas of east central Custer County and west central Pueblo County.

This incident underscores a hard reality Colorado taxpayers and property owners face: wildfire suppression and forest management failures leave landscapes defenseless against the state's intense summer monsoons. Without aggressive thinning, prescribed burns, and timber removal—efforts often delayed by environmental litigation and regulatory red tape—Colorado communities remain sitting ducks for flash flood disasters that destroy homes and infrastructure.
