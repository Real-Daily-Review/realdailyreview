# Supreme Court's Final Rulings Tuesday Could Reshape Immigration, Sports, and Campaign Finance

The Supreme Court will hand down its remaining decisions Tuesday—a blockbuster session that could reshape policy on birthright citizenship, transgender athlete eligibility, and campaign finance rules (KDVR Fox31 Denver). Chief Justice John Roberts announced the conclusion Monday after a term packed with high-stakes cases.

The birthright citizenship case carries direct implications for Colorado and Western states grappling with border security and immigration enforcement. A ruling limiting automatic citizenship to children born on U.S. soil to at least one citizen parent could reshape state policy on education, healthcare, and social services spending—areas where Colorado taxpayers already foot substantial bills.

The transgender athlete ban decision will likely affect Colorado high schools and universities, where athletic associations have wrestled with Title IX compliance and competitive fairness. Conservatives argue that biological sex distinctions in sports protect women's opportunities and fair competition; the left counters that exclusion harms transgender students.

Campaign finance rulings could reshape how Colorado political committees operate ahead of the 2026 midterms, potentially loosening or tightening donor disclosure and spending limits.

Republicans expect at least partial victories on immigration and sports; Democrats are bracing for setbacks on progressive priorities (New York Post).

**Watch for:** How Colorado's Democratic legislature responds if the Court sides with immigration restrictions or state athletic association autonomy.
