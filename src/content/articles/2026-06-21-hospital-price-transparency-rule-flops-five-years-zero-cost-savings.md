# Federal Price-Transparency Mandate Fails to Lower Hospital Costs—Another Government Fix That Backfired

Washington's five-year experiment in forcing hospitals to post prices has produced massive compliance paperwork but zero market competition and zero cost relief for patients.

The Obama-era rule required hospitals to disclose their rates publicly, betting that transparency would spark price competition and drive down costs. The theory sounded reasonable. The reality? A bust. According to the *Washington Examiner*, researchers found that despite mountains of posted data, no functioning market emerged and prices never fell.

This is textbook progressive policy failure: regulators assumed that mandates alone would fix market dysfunction, ignored how hospitals actually set prices (through opaque insurance negotiations, not consumer shopping), and created compliance burdens that hospitals absorbed—often passing costs to patients anyway.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado hospitals now spend resources posting and updating price files that patients rarely use and that don't drive actual price competition. Meanwhile, healthcare costs keep rising, and the regulatory overhead adds to operating expenses that get passed to Colorado families and small businesses buying coverage.

Conservatives have long argued that real healthcare cost control requires removing barriers to competition—allowing insurance purchases across state lines, reducing certificate-of-need laws that limit hospital supply, and letting price signals work naturally—not layering on more federal mandates that hospitals game and patients ignore.

Watch whether Congress moves to repeal this rule or doubles down with even more invasive price controls.
