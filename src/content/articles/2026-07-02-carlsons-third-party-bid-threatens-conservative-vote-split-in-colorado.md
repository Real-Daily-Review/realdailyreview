# Tucker Carlson's Third-Party Gambit Could Fracture Conservative Vote in Colorado and Beyond

Tucker Carlson announced plans to build a new political party after breaking with Republicans, declaring in a *Columbia Journalism Review* interview that he aims to "help build a third party" focused on what "benefits the country" (Washington Examiner, Daily Caller). The move marks a dramatic split from the GOP establishment, which Carlson has blasted as "lock-step" and captured by war and finance interests.

**The Conservative Case Against This Move**

Republicans worry Carlson's third-party effort could splinter the right-leaning vote precisely when conservatives need unified strength against Democratic overreach on taxes, energy policy, and gun rights. Colorado's razor-thin electoral margins—where Democrats have gained ground in recent cycles—make vote-splitting especially dangerous for conservative causes. A fractured conservative base hands Democrats exactly what they want: easier wins in statewide races and ballot initiatives.

Carlson frames his effort as principle-driven reform, but skeptics argue third parties historically serve as spoilers, not solutions. The 2000 Florida recount and 2016 swing-state dynamics offer cautionary tales.

**What's Next**

Conservatives and Colorado Republicans should watch whether Carlson's movement gains actual organizational traction or remains a media narrative—and whether it bleeds votes from GOP candidates in 2026 midterms and beyond.
