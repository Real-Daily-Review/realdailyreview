# Colorado Payback Program Returns Record $158M—But Questions Linger on Unclaimed Funds

Colorado's Great Colorado Payback program hit a new high in 2026, returning $158 million to claimants, according to the Colorado Department of the Treasury (KDVR). The initiative, which reunites Coloradans with unclaimed property held by the state, broke its previous record and underscores a simple truth: government shouldn't be sitting on citizens' money in the first place.

While the program's success is welcome, conservatives should ask harder questions about why so much property goes unclaimed initially and how efficiently the state manages these funds while holding them. The fact that $158 million needed to be "returned" highlights a systemic problem: government bureaucracy often obscures what rightfully belongs to individuals and businesses.

The payback program is one of the few government initiatives that actually works as intended—reuniting people with their own assets rather than expanding state control or spending. But taxpayers should remain vigilant about whether the Treasury is doing enough to proactively notify claimants and whether any unclaimed funds are being diverted to general revenue or pet projects.

Watch for transparency reports on how much unclaimed property remains in state coffers and whether the Treasury is maximizing outreach to Colorado residents who may not know they have claims pending.
