# Trump Administration Moves to Open Nearly 45 Million Acres of National Forest to Development

The Trump administration is moving to rescind the 2001 Roadless Area Conservation Rule, opening nearly 45 million acres of protected national forest land to logging and road construction—a major win for energy development and rural economies that Colorado conservatives have long championed.

Secretary of Agriculture Brooke Rollins announced the proposed rule change this week, signaling the administration's commitment to unlocking resources on federal land rather than locking them away in perpetuity. Conservatives argue that responsible resource extraction creates jobs, generates revenue for states and counties, and puts land to productive use—particularly critical for Colorado's timber industry and rural communities dependent on forest management revenue.

Environmentalists and the left are already sounding alarms, claiming the move threatens old-growth forests, wildlife habitat, and water supplies (9News Denver). Democrats will likely argue the rule protects watersheds and ecosystems from degradation.

Republicans counter that the decades-old roadless rule has been a de facto land lockup that prevents active forest management, increases wildfire risk through fuel buildup, and denies Colorado counties and small towns the economic benefits of timber sales and development. Proponents note that responsible logging and road access can actually improve forest health and reduce catastrophic fire danger—a real concern across the state.
