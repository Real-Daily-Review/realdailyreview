# FlyDubai Co-Pilot Banned for Radical Views Still Cleared to Fly—and Tried to Hijack Plane

A catastrophic security failure: Oman barred a co-pilot from flying over "radical ideological views," yet FlyDubai hired him anyway, allowing him to operate a flight carrying 174 passengers—nearly all Israeli citizens—between the UAE and Israel. On Wednesday, the Omani national allegedly stabbed his captain and attempted to crash the aircraft, according to reports citing Israeli officials and the Wall Street Journal.

The co-pilot had been flagged as a security risk by his home country. Despite that explicit ban, the airline's vetting process failed to catch—or ignored—the red flag. Investigators say he indicated intent to crash the jet in Israel.

This incident underscores a hard truth: no-fly lists and security designations mean nothing without enforcement. Airlines and governments must verify backgrounds rigorously; ideology-driven threats demand serious scrutiny, not bureaucratic theater.

For Colorado aviation workers and travelers, the lesson is clear: security protocols exist for a reason. Lax enforcement anywhere in the system puts everyone at risk. Watch whether regulators demand airlines tighten international vetting standards and whether governments share security data more effectively.
