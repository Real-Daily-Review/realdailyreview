# Colorado Democrats Race to Out-Litigate Each Other Against Energy Industry

Colorado's Democratic primary for Attorney General has devolved into a bidding war over who will most aggressively weaponize the office against oil and gas producers, according to reporting from Complete Colorado. The contest reflects how far left the party's base has shifted on energy policy—and signals trouble ahead for Colorado's energy workers and tax base.

Democrats are essentially competing to promise the most climate litigation against energy companies. Republicans argue this approach amounts to "lawfare"—using the courts and regulatory apparatus to punish an industry rather than govern through legitimate policy channels. Colorado's oil and gas sector generates billions in economic activity and funds schools and infrastructure; aggressive litigation threatens jobs and state revenue.

The primary dynamics also reveal a deeper structural problem: unaffiliated voters—Colorado's fastest-growing registration bloc—are increasingly shut out of races that effectively decide general elections. When party primaries determine outcomes, ideological activists set the agenda, not moderate swing voters.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the takeaway is stark: a Democratic AG willing to wage climate litigation could impose massive costs on energy producers, drive investment out of state, and shrink the tax base that funds schools and roads.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans can capitalize on this overreach by fielding a pro-energy candidate who appeals to unaffiliated voters in the general election.
