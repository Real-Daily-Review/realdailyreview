# Amendment 87's Math Doesn't Add Up—and Colorado Taxpayers Will Pay the Price

Colorado voters face a deceptively packaged tax hike disguised as "soak-the-rich" redistribution. Amendment 87 promises to raise taxes on the top 3% of earners and 5% of businesses to generate $2.7 billion annually (Colorado Sun), but conservatives warn the real damage lies in what proponents won't say: bracket creep.

Complete Colorado argues that without inflation adjustments, Amendment 87 will inevitably drag middle-class Coloradans into higher tax brackets over time, turning today's "millionaire tax" into tomorrow's middle-class tax. That's the progressive playbook—start narrow, expand quietly.

Democrats frame this as targeted relief for working families. Republicans counter that government spending always grows to match revenue, and $2.7 billion in new tax money simply fuels state bureaucracy rather than solving underlying fiscal problems. The real question: why should Colorado businesses and high earners subsidize government expansion when the state already collects record revenue?

Taxpayers should watch whether the amendment's language includes automatic inflation indexing—if not, bracket creep is the feature, not a bug.
