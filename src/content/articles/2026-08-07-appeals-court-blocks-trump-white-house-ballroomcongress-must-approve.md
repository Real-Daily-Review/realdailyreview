# Federal Court Halts Trump's White House Ballroom Project—Congress Must Approve

A federal appeals court has ordered President Trump to halt construction on his proposed White House ballroom renovation, ruling that Congress alone holds authority over such structural changes to the executive mansion. The D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals issued a 2-1 decision Friday blocking the project and giving the Trump administration 14 days to appeal to the Supreme Court.

The court determined that "Congress has exclusive authority to regulate the construction and demolition of White House structures," according to the New York Post. This represents another legal setback for the administration's ambitious renovation plans.

**The conservative angle:** While this ruling doesn't directly affect Colorado, it illustrates the ongoing tension between executive power and constitutional limits—a principle conservatives champion when it constrains government overreach. The court's insistence on congressional oversight reflects the separation-of-powers doctrine that should apply equally to all branches, not selectively based on political preference.

Republicans will likely argue the decision reflects judicial activism by a D.C. court hostile to the administration, while defenders of the ruling contend it upholds constitutional process. The Trump team has two weeks to petition the Supreme Court, where the outcome remains uncertain.
