# Massachusetts Doubles Down on Surveillance After Clancy Mistrial, While Defense Challenges Holdout Juror

A Massachusetts mother's mistrial in the deaths of her three children has sparked two competing responses: her defense team is demanding judicial scrutiny of the lone juror who blocked acquittal, while state officials are expanding government surveillance of postpartum mothers—a move conservatives warn signals dangerous mission creep.

Lindsay Clancy's case ended in mistrial after 11 of 12 jurors favored acquittal, but one holdout prevented unanimous verdict (9News Denver). Her attorney, Kevin Reddington, has now filed a motion asking the judge to preserve records and investigate the dissenting juror's conduct (The Daily Wire).

Meanwhile, Massachusetts—under Democratic leadership—is responding by launching expanded home surveillance programs targeting new mothers under the guise of postpartum depression screening (Daily Caller). 

**The conservative concern is clear:** Rather than respect jury skepticism about the case, state officials are using it as justification to expand intrusive government monitoring of families. This represents exactly the kind of regulatory overreach progressives favor when tragedy strikes—turning individual cases into pretexts for broader state control.

Colorado families should watch whether similar "wellness check" expansions gain traction here, particularly as progressive legislatures seek new tools to monitor households under mental-health justifications.
