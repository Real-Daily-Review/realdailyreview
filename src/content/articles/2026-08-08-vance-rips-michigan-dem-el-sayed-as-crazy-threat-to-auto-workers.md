# Vance Hammers Michigan Dem as 'Crazy' for Opposing Trump's Middle-Class Policies

Vice President JD Vance is taking direct aim at Michigan's Democratic Senate candidate Abdul el Sayed, calling him "crazy" and warning he would be a "terrible senator" who would hurt working people and the auto industry (Washington Examiner, New York Post).

Vance's critique centers on what Republicans see as a fundamental contradiction: el Sayed claims to champion working-class interests while opposing Trump administration policies that conservatives argue have benefited middle-class Americans. The VP characterized el Sayed as "odd" and accused him of performative populism—talking up workers while backing policies that would damage their livelihoods.

**Republicans argue** el Sayed's leftist positions on energy, regulation, and trade would devastate Michigan's auto sector and manufacturing base, which depends on competitive markets and reasonable regulatory frameworks. They contend Democrats routinely promise to help workers while pursuing policies that kill jobs.

**Democrats counter** that their candidate represents a genuine progressive alternative focused on worker protections, environmental standards, and challenging corporate power.

The exchange reflects the broader 2026 midterm battle: Republicans framing Democrats as out-of-touch elites despite their working-class rhetoric, while Democrats position themselves as defenders against corporate interests.

Conservatives should watch whether Vance's direct messaging strategy—blunt, personal, and focused on economic consequences—gains traction in union-heavy Midwest states where Trump made unexpected gains in 2024.
