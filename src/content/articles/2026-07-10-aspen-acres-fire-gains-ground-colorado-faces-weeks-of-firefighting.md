# Aspen Acres Fire Shows Progress, But Colorado Faces Weeks More of Firefighting

Firefighters are gaining ground on the massive Aspen Acres blaze in Southern Colorado, with containment now doubled—but don't expect relief anytime soon. Nearly 2,000 firefighters continue battling the 97,000-acre fire, and crews anticipate efforts will stretch for weeks (CPR News). Residents are beginning to return home as conditions improve, though Custer County has enforced strict fire restrictions to prevent new ignitions (Colorado Sun).

The scale underscores a hard reality: Colorado's fire season remains brutal. While progress is measurable, the state's wildfire challenge demands sustained resources and clear-eyed management of forest conditions—not just reactive firefighting after blazes explode.

Conservatives have long argued that overly restrictive environmental regulations and decades of fire-suppression policies have left Colorado's forests dangerously overstocked with fuel. Proper forest management, thinning, and controlled burns—not just restrictions on residents—are essential to reducing catastrophic fire risk. The question for state leaders: Will Colorado prioritize aggressive forest health measures, or continue betting on containment after fires ignite?

Watch whether state and federal agencies accelerate forest thinning projects and remove regulatory barriers to active land management—or whether they lean harder on restrictions that burden property owners while leaving forests primed to burn.
