# Colorado's myColorado App Breach Exposes 100,000 to Identity Theft Risk

Colorado's state government has bungled cybersecurity so badly that 100,000 residents' sensitive documents—passports, vehicle registrations, insurance cards—are now in the hands of unknown attackers via the myColorado app breach.

The Office of Information Technology confirmed Friday that "myDocuments" PDFs and photographs stored in the app were accessed during the cyberattack. The state has provided no timeline for restoring the service, leaving Coloradans in limbo and vulnerable to identity theft.

This is what happens when government agencies prioritize digital convenience over security fundamentals. The breach underscores a hard truth conservatives have long warned about: centralized government databases collecting sensitive personal data are attractive targets for bad actors—and bureaucrats rarely have the technical chops or accountability to protect them.

State officials have been characteristically vague about the breach's scope, timeline, and remediation plan. Coloradans deserve answers: How did this happen? Who was responsible? What's the actual cost to taxpayers for cleanup and credit monitoring?

Republicans should demand a full audit of the state's cybersecurity practices and push for legislation requiring real accountability when government loses citizens' data.

Watch for whether Colorado's Democratic leadership takes responsibility or deflects—and whether they'll finally admit that not every service needs a government app.
