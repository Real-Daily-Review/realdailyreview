# ACLU Escalates Fight Against Texas Ten Commandments Law, Eyes Supreme Court

The American Civil Liberties Union is asking the Supreme Court to strike down Texas's requirement that public schools display the Ten Commandments in every classroom—a direct challenge to a law passed by the Republican-led legislature and upheld by a federal appeals court in April (Washington Examiner).

The ACLU argues the mandate violates First Amendment rights of students and parents, claiming it constitutes government endorsement of religion. Republicans counter that displaying the Ten Commandments—foundational to Western law and American legal tradition—is historical and educational, not sectarian promotion. The law passed with strong GOP support precisely because conservatives view it as affirming the moral and legal heritage undergirding the Constitution.

**The Colorado angle:** While Texas leads on this front, Colorado's education establishment leans heavily progressive. Should SCOTUS uphold Texas's law, expect renewed pressure from conservative legislators here to restore similar displays in Colorado classrooms—and fierce resistance from the state's Democratic majority and activist groups.

This case will likely define how far states can go in acknowledging religious and historical content in public schools. Conservatives should watch whether the Supreme Court accepts the ACLU's petition and, if so, how the current conservative majority rules on First Amendment religious-liberty questions.
