# Colorado's Unclaimed Property Program Hits $1 Billion Milestone

Colorado's Great Colorado Payback Program has returned over $1 billion in unclaimed money and property to residents—a rare government success story worth noting.

The State Treasurer's Office announced the milestone recently, marking a significant achievement in reuniting Coloradans with their own funds held in state custody. Unclaimed property typically includes forgotten bank accounts, insurance payouts, utility deposits, and other assets that cycle into state hands when owners can't be located.

Unlike most government programs that expand spending and bureaucracy, this one actually works backward: it identifies money already belonging to citizens and returns it. No new taxes required. No regulatory burden. Just straightforward administration of what's rightfully yours.

The program's success underscores a conservative principle: government functions best when it stays focused on its core duty—in this case, stewarding assets until rightful owners claim them—rather than inventing new programs or expanding its footprint.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners with unclaimed property, the takeaway is simple: check the state's database. Your money might be waiting.

Watch whether the State Treasurer's Office maintains this efficiency or whether future administrations bloat the program with unnecessary overhead.
