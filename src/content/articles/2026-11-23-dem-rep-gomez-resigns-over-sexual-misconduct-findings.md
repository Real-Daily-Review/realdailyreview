# Democratic Rep. Gomez Resigns After Ethics Panel Finds Sexual Misconduct

Another Democrat faces the exit door after the House Ethics Committee documented serious misconduct—a reminder that Capitol Hill's culture problem spans both parties, though Colorado voters should note how differently the left handles its own.

Rep. Jimmy Gomez (D-California) is resigning following a House Ethics Committee report finding "substantial evidence" he made unwelcome sexual advances toward a House staffer (9News Denver). Fox News reports the committee's findings suggest his conduct could implicate D.C. laws on criminal sexual assault.

The resignation underscores a pattern: when Democrats face credible ethics allegations involving sexual misconduct, some do step down. Yet the broader question for Colorado conservatives is consistency. How aggressively does the Democratic-controlled House pursue its own members versus Republicans? And what does this say about the character and judgment of those who lead the majority?

Republicans argue the ethics process, while functioning here, often moves slowly and selectively—a concern when taxpayer-funded staffers are victimized and leadership knew or should have known. The left typically frames such cases as isolated incidents rather than symptoms of institutional rot requiring systemic reform.
