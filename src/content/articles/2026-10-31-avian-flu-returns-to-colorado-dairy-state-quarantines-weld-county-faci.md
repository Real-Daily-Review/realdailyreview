# Avian Flu Returns to Colorado Dairy—State Moves to Contain Spread

Avian flu has resurfaced at a Weld County dairy, marking the first detection since Colorado's 2024 outbreak, according to testing by the U.S. Department of Agriculture (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). State health officials have placed the facility under quarantine to prevent transmission.

The Colorado Sun reports that state health officials say humans remain safe, particularly for consumers of pasteurized milk. However, the reemergence underscores ongoing biosecurity challenges facing Colorado's dairy industry—a cornerstone of the state's agriculture economy and a major employer in Weld County.

Conservatives and agricultural advocates will be watching whether state and federal regulators impose burdensome restrictions that could disrupt production or drive up costs for Colorado dairy farmers already navigating thin margins. The dairy sector has faced mounting regulatory pressure in recent years; any heavy-handed quarantine protocols could ripple through rural economies dependent on milk production.

State officials must balance legitimate public health concerns with the economic realities facing Colorado's ranching and dairy communities. Overreach here sets a dangerous precedent.

Watch whether the quarantine remains targeted and time-limited, or expands into broader herd restrictions that could cripple operations.
