# Massive Spokane Fires Expose Western Wildfire Crisis—Colorado Should Prepare

Catastrophic wildfires near Spokane, Washington have torched over 600 structures and forced 60,000 evacuations, burning roughly 8.2 square miles over the weekend alone. Gusty winds and dangerously low humidity are accelerating fire spread and hampering containment efforts, according to forecasters cited by *Just The News*.

The Spokane fires underscore a hard reality: the West faces a genuine wildfire emergency that demands serious forest management, not ideological posturing. Conservatives argue that decades of federal land-management policies—driven by environmental litigation and restrictions on logging and prescribed burns—have created tinderbox conditions across millions of acres. Overgrown forests choked with dead timber and dense undergrowth burn hotter and faster, making suppression nearly impossible.

Colorado faces the same risk. The state's forests are similarly overstocked with fuel, and recent megafires like the Marshall Fire have devastated communities and destroyed homes. Republicans and land-use advocates have long pushed for aggressive thinning, salvage logging, and controlled burns on federal and state land—but environmental groups and progressive regulators have often blocked or delayed these efforts through lawsuits and permitting delays.

The Spokane disaster is a live-action reminder that forest management isn't an environmental luxury—it's a public-safety imperative. Colorado taxpayers and homeowners should watch whether state and federal officials finally prioritize fuel reduction over litigation-friendly inaction.
