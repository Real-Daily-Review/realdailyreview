# Colorado Should Stop Playing Favorites With Political Parties

Colorado government is granting special recognition and privileges to political parties—a practice that violates the principle of neutral state authority, according to conservative critics.

Complete Colorado argues the state has "no proper business" extending such favors, calling separation of state and party a "courageous" move that Colorado should embrace. The concern reflects a broader conservative principle: government institutions should remain neutral arbiters, not cheerleaders for any faction.

This matters for Colorado taxpayers and voters. When state agencies or officials use public resources, authority, or symbolic recognition to advantage one party over another, they blur the line between governance and partisanship. It opens the door to accusations of favoritism, erodes public trust, and sets a precedent for future administrations to do the same—only in reverse.

Republicans argue that Colorado's government should treat all political parties equally under law and policy, regardless of which holds power. The state's job is administering elections fairly, managing resources impartially, and serving all Coloradans—not promoting Democratic or Republican interests.

Democrats haven't publicly defended party-specific state recognition, but the broader left often frames such gestures as celebrating civic participation or diversity of viewpoint.
