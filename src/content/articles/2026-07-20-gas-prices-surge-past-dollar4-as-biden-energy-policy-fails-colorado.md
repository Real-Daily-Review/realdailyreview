# Gas Prices Surge Past $4 as Biden Energy Policy Fails Colorado Drivers

National average gasoline prices have climbed back above $4 per gallon, hammering Colorado families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation. According to AAA data cited by 9News Denver, the national average hit $4.003 Monday, with regional variation stark: Indiana drivers paid as little as $3.35 while California motorists faced $5.50 per gallon (Just The News).

The price spike coincides with renewed Middle East tensions, but conservatives argue the root cause lies in Biden administration energy policy. Restrictions on domestic oil production, delayed permitting for new wells, and the administration's hostility toward fossil fuels have constrained U.S. energy supply when global markets are volatile. Colorado's oil and gas workers—and the state's economy—depend on stable, affordable energy development.

Democrats counter that global oil markets and geopolitical events drive prices, not domestic policy. They point to OPEC production decisions and international conflict as primary factors.

For Colorado taxpayers and workers in energy-dependent communities, these prices represent real pain at the pump and threats to job security in the state's energy sector. Watch whether the administration moves to increase domestic production or doubles down on green-energy mandates that further restrict oil and gas development.
