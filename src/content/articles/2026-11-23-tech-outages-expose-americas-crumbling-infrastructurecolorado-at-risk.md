# Tech Outages Weren't Foreign Attack, But America's Crumbling Infrastructure Needs Urgent Fix

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton offered reassurance this week that recent technology outages sweeping the nation were neither coordinated nor state-sponsored—but the real story is far more troubling for American competitiveness and security.

Clayton told ABC News the incidents "were not coordinated and they were not state-sponsored" (Daily Caller). However, on CNN's "State of the Union," he acknowledged a harder truth: the outages stem from upgrades to America's "outdated digital infrastructure," describing the moment as "a period of technological step change" (Breitbart).

Translation: U.S. infrastructure is aging and fragile. While foreign adversaries didn't orchestrate these failures, they exposed vulnerabilities that hostile actors—China, Russia, Iran—will certainly exploit.

For Colorado, this matters acutely. The state's energy sector, water systems, and financial networks depend on reliable digital infrastructure. Outages ripple through power grids, agricultural operations, and small businesses across the state. Colorado's tech corridor and defense contractors also face heightened risk if federal systems remain vulnerable.

Conservatives argue Washington must prioritize infrastructure modernization through private-sector innovation and competition, not bloated government spending programs that historically waste taxpayer dollars. The question isn't whether to upgrade—it's whether bureaucrats or the free market will lead the charge.

Watch whether Congress demands accountability for how federal IT budgets have been spent, or simply throws more money at the problem.
