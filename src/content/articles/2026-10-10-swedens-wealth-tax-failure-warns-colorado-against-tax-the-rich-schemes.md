# Colorado Should Heed Europe's Wealth-Tax Cautionary Tale

Colorado lawmakers pushing higher taxes on the wealthy should study what happened when Sweden tried it—capital flight and economic stagnation.

The Washington Examiner reports that Sweden's 1970s experiment with aggressive wealth and inheritance taxes on the rich produced a cautionary lesson progressives often ignore: when you sharply raise taxes on entrepreneurs and investors, they leave. Supporters of wealth taxes and higher inheritance levies claim capital flight is a myth, but Sweden's experience—which saw frustrated entrepreneurs depart—contradicts that claim.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Colorado already faces competitive pressure from lower-tax states like Texas and Florida. Driving out job creators and business owners through punitive tax hikes doesn't generate sustainable revenue; it shrinks the tax base and ultimately hurts working Coloradans through reduced economic growth and fewer jobs.

Democrats counter that wealth taxes are necessary to address inequality, though they rarely acknowledge the real-world consequences of capital flight or the administrative costs of enforcement.

Colorado's economy depends on attracting and retaining entrepreneurs, investors, and energy-sector professionals. Policymakers should watch closely whether any 2025 legislative proposals resurrect wealth-tax schemes—and demand honest answers about how Sweden's failure won't repeat here.
