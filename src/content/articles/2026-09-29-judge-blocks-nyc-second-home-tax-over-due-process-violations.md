# Judge Blocks NYC Second-Home Tax After Due Process Violation

A New York judge has halted Mayor Zohran Mamdani's aggressive second-home tax scheme, ruling that city officials violated property owners' constitutional rights by issuing tax notices without verifying primary residences first (Fox News, Washington Examiner).

Justice Wayne Ozzi of Staten Island's state Supreme Court sided with homeowners who challenged the so-called pied-à-terre tax—a new levy targeting high-end second homes. The judge found the city's rollout process fundamentally flawed: officials sent notices demanding payment before confirming whether properties actually qualified as secondary residences, a basic due-process failure.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Government cannot tax first and ask questions later. Mamdani's administration bypassed elementary procedural safeguards, treating property owners as guilty until proven innocent. This is exactly the kind of regulatory overreach that chills investment and punishes success.

Democrats defend such taxes as necessary revenue tools to address housing affordability in expensive cities. But conservatives argue they discourage capital investment, drive wealth away, and represent government's reflexive impulse to seize assets rather than control spending.

**Colorado angle:** While Colorado hasn't pursued a second-home tax statewide, progressive Denver and Boulder officials have eyed similar schemes. This ruling signals courts will scrutinize sloppy implementation—a warning to state and local governments tempted by wealth-targeting taxes.
