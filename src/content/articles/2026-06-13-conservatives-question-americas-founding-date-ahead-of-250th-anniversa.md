# America's Founding Date Debate Masks Deeper Questions About National Identity

Conservatives are questioning whether America's 250th anniversary in 2026 rests on the correct founding date—a debate that cuts to the heart of how we understand our nation's constitutional origins.

National Review's analysis, drawing on political theorist Willmoore Kendall's work, challenges the conventional 1776 date, suggesting historians and policymakers may have gotten America's birthdate wrong. The question matters beyond academia: it shapes how Americans understand their founding principles, the Constitution's authority, and the continuity of limited government that conservatives champion.

Meanwhile, The Daily Wire highlights how barriers to wealth creation—including regulatory obstacles to major private offerings like SpaceX's anticipated IPO—undermine American entrepreneurship. The outlet argues that making such investments available to retail investors, not just institutional players, reflects genuine free-market principles.

For Colorado conservatives, these debates intersect: a clear-eyed understanding of America's actual founding strengthens arguments against progressive reinterpretations of constitutional limits on government power. Similarly, removing barriers to capital formation and investment access directly benefits Colorado's growing tech and energy sectors.

The real test comes when progressive politicians invoke a "living Constitution" to justify expansions of federal authority—watch whether conservatives effectively counter that argument by anchoring themselves in the actual founding principles Kendall and others have identified.
