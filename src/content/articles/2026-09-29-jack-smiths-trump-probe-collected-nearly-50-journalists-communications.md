# Jack Smith's Trump Probe Swept Up Journalists' Communications—and Republicans Want Answers

Special Counsel Jack Smith's investigation into former President Trump collected communications between nearly 50 journalists and government officials, raising serious questions about prosecutorial overreach and First Amendment protections that should alarm every American who values press freedom.

According to Fox News and Just The News, Smith's team obtained records of media communications as part of the broader Trump probe. The scope of the dragnet—capturing journalist-source exchanges—goes well beyond investigating lawmakers and ventures into territory that threatens the confidentiality relationships essential to reporting.

Senate Judiciary Chairman Chuck Grassley (R-IA) revealed the practice during a Capitol Hill hearing Tuesday, where Republicans pressed Smith over what they characterize as a "witch hunt" investigation. Republicans argue the collection of journalist communications represents an abuse of prosecutorial power and an alarming precedent for future administrations. Smith has defended his investigative decisions, but the hearing underscores GOP concerns that the probe exceeded its legitimate scope.

For Colorado conservatives, this episode exemplifies the dangers of unchecked government power—particularly when federal prosecutors can sweep up private communications without clear justification. The First Amendment protections that shield journalists from government surveillance exist precisely to prevent this kind of overreach.

Watch for congressional efforts to impose stricter limits on special counsel authority and protect journalist-source confidentiality in future investigations.
