# Colorado's Election System Under Scrutiny as Ballot-Cure Process Draws Renewed Attention

Colorado's ballot-counting procedures are facing fresh scrutiny as President Trump renews calls for election security ahead of the midterms—and conservatives say the state's system needs real reform, not just reassurance.

The Colorado Sun reports that ballot curing, tabulation of late returns, and post-election audits are drawing new attention to how the state processes votes. While election officials tout these safeguards, conservatives point to recent controversies as evidence that Colorado's system requires structural changes.

Complete Colorado argues that the "Griswold fiasco" underscores the need for election reform, particularly regarding how candidates can win crowded races with only a small minority of voter support—even when another candidate is more broadly preferred. Republicans contend this vulnerability exposes weaknesses in Colorado's electoral design that go beyond routine auditing.

The state's reliance on mail-in ballots and the ballot-cure process—allowing voters to fix rejected ballots—has drawn both praise from election administrators and skepticism from conservatives who question whether the current system adequately prevents irregularities or ensures voter intent is accurately captured.

Colorado taxpayers and election integrity advocates should watch whether Republican legislators push for concrete reforms—such as ranked-choice voting alternatives or tighter ballot-cure procedures—or whether Democratic leadership continues defending the status quo as sufficient.
