# Colorado Voters Approve Tax Hike on Groceries—Expect Higher Prices at Checkout

Colorado voters just handed grocery stores a nearly doubled tax bill through Amendment 87, and working families will pay the price at the register.

The measure targets stores making over $500,000 annually, nearly doubling their tax burden. According to Complete Colorado, those increased costs won't stay on the books—they'll flow straight to consumers through higher prices on staples like milk, bread, and produce.

Republicans and conservatives warned voters that Amendment 87 would function as a hidden sales tax on groceries, hitting low-income Coloradans hardest. The left framed it as a "fair share" measure targeting corporate profits, but the real-world effect is predictable: businesses pass costs downstream.

Colorado's grocery sector, already operating on thin margins in a competitive market, now faces a significant compliance burden. For families already squeezed by inflation, this is another blow to household budgets—particularly in rural areas where grocery options are limited and prices already run higher.

The damage is done, but taxpayers should watch whether the state legislature moves to expand similar "gross receipts" taxes to other sectors. Once one industry is targeted, the template exists for others.
