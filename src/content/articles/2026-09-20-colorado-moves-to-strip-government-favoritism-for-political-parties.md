# Colorado Should Stop Playing Favorites With Political Parties

Colorado government officials are finally acknowledging what conservatives have long argued: the state has no business granting special recognition or privileges to any political party. According to *Complete Colorado*, state leaders are moving to separate government from partisan machinery—a genuinely courageous step in an era of expanding state power.

The principle is straightforward. When government picks winners and losers among political organizations, it crosses a constitutional line. Taxpayer dollars and official state resources should serve all Coloradans equally, regardless of party affiliation. Instead, too often state agencies and elected officials use their authority to advantage favored political movements while disadvantaging others.

Republicans and conservatives argue this separation is essential to restoring faith in impartial government. Democrats, predictably, resist losing institutional advantages they've cultivated. But the real issue isn't partisan—it's constitutional: government neutrality.

This matters for Colorado because state officials control licensing, permitting, grant-making, and regulatory authority that directly affects businesses, nonprofits, and citizens across the political spectrum. When government plays favorites, everyone loses faith in the system.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature actually codifies this principle into law, or whether it remains a symbolic gesture while agencies continue the old game under new language.
