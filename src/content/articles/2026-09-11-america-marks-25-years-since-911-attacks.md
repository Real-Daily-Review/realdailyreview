# Nation Pauses to Honor 9/11's 25th Anniversary

America marked a quarter-century since the September 11 terrorist attacks fundamentally reshaped national security, military doctrine, and the role of government in citizens' lives. Ceremonies at the Pentagon and Ground Zero in New York drew officials and families of the 184 killed at the Pentagon and nearly 3,000 nationwide.

President Trump attended the Pentagon commemoration (Just The News), joining military leadership and survivors to reflect on the attacks that triggered two decades of war, massive defense spending, and the creation of the Department of Homeland Security.

The 9/11 attacks remain a defining moment for conservatives' national security posture. Republicans have consistently argued that robust military investment, intelligence capabilities, and border security are essential lessons from that day—positions that contrast sharply with progressive calls to reduce defense budgets or scale back security measures. The attacks also underscored why many conservatives oppose unilateral disarmament or isolationist foreign policy.

For Colorado specifically, the attacks claimed lives of residents and military personnel, and shaped how the state's defense contractors and Fort Carson operate today. The commemoration serves as a reminder of why conservatives prioritize military readiness and reject budget cuts that weaken America's ability to prevent future attacks.

Watch for how 9/11 anniversaries continue to frame defense spending debates in Congress and Colorado's defense-dependent economy.
