# Data Center Backlash Spreads as Democrats Restrict AI Infrastructure

Colorado could face similar anti-growth regulations if progressive lawmakers embrace Pennsylvania's model of blocking data center development.

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro (D) signed an executive order this week restricting data center expansion, citing environmental concerns—a move that has triggered pushback even from centrist Democrats. Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) publicly rejected the "doomsdaying," arguing on social media that "AI supremacy and energy dominance underpins our national security" (Washington Examiner).

The broader pattern is clear: left-wing activists are stoking hysteria about data centers and artificial intelligence, and some Democratic leaders are capitulating. National Review notes that despite "vast quantities of ink" spent addressing public anxiety about data centers, "none of it seems to matter"—the opposition persists regardless of facts.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's energy sector and tech economy depend on reliable, affordable power and business-friendly regulations. If Democratic legislators here adopt Pennsylvania's restrictive approach, Colorado risks losing data center investment, high-paying jobs, and tax revenue to states with saner policies. The state's grid operators and energy producers already face mounting pressure from renewable mandates; adding data center bans would compound the problem.

Conservatives argue that blocking AI infrastructure cedes technological and economic leadership to China and other competitors while gutting domestic energy demand and job creation.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats propose similar restrictions on data center development in the next legislative session.
