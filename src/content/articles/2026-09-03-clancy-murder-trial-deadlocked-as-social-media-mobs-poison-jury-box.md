# Jury Deadlocked in Clancy Case as Social Media Fuels Courtroom Chaos

The Lindsay Clancy triple-murder trial in Massachusetts is collapsing under the weight of social media hysteria and courtroom intimidation. Judge William Sullivan declared the jury deadlocked for the second time in as many days after nearly 28 hours of deliberations, with the nine-woman, three-man panel unable to reach unanimous verdict (The Daily Wire). Meanwhile, a woman was arrested outside the courthouse on jury intimidation charges, and TikTok has transformed a straightforward infanticide case—Clancy admitted killing her three children—into a viral cause célèbre that's warping jurors' judgment on questions of criminal responsibility and mental state.

The case has echoes beyond Massachusetts. In Frankfort, Illinois, a 2-year-old boy was killed in what police describe as a domestic-related homicide, with the child's mother hospitalized after an apparent suicide attempt (Breitbart). Locals are already drawing parallels to the Clancy saga, suggesting a disturbing pattern.

Conservatives should note the corrosive effect of social media on the justice system. When TikTok activists and online mobs can influence jury deliberations and intimidate witnesses, the rule of law itself is compromised. This isn't justice—it's mob rule with a smartphone.
