# Colorado's Business Exodus: Regulatory Burden, High Costs Driving Companies Out

Colorado is hemorrhaging businesses to lower-tax, less-regulated states, and state policymakers should take notice.

According to Complete Colorado, companies aren't closing shop due to market failure or consolidation—they're actively relocating. The pattern mirrors California's ongoing exodus: excessive regulation, high operating costs, and unfriendly business climates are pushing Colorado firms toward Texas and other competitive states.

The problem is straightforward. Colorado's regulatory burden has grown steadily as Democrats and progressive lawmakers have tightened environmental rules, labor mandates, and compliance costs. Meanwhile, housing and operational expenses have climbed, squeezing margins for manufacturers, tech firms, and service providers alike.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Colorado's path to prosperity runs through *deregulation* and tax relief—not more government intervention. They point to Texas's success attracting businesses by keeping regulatory overhead low and tax rates competitive. Colorado's energy sector, already battered by anti-fossil-fuel policies, faces particular pressure to relocate operations to states more hospitable to resource development.

Democrats rarely acknowledge the exodus directly, instead framing Colorado's regulatory environment as necessary environmental and worker protection.

The Colorado angle cuts deep: lost businesses mean lost jobs for workers, reduced tax revenue for schools and infrastructure, and declining competitiveness in attracting talent and investment.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature responds with genuine deregulation and tax cuts—or doubles down on the policies driving the exodus.
