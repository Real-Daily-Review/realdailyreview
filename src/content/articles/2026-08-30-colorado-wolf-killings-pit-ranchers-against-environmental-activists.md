# Colorado Wolf Killings Spark Debate Over Livestock Protection vs. Environmental Ideology

Colorado Parks and Wildlife's lethal wolf removal program is reigniting the clash between ranchers protecting their herds and environmental activists who oppose any culling.

The Colorado Sun's opinion section argues that killing wolves isn't genuine "management" and frames lethal removal as dishonest policy. Progressives contend Colorado should lead nationally on non-lethal conflict prevention—implying current rancher protections are inadequate.

Republicans and livestock operators counter that wolves pose real threats to cattle and sheep. Conservatives argue that Colorado ranchers, not ideological activists in Denver or out-of-state environmental groups, should drive predator policy. They point out that ranchers already bear the economic cost of livestock losses and shouldn't be forced to absorb additional burden through restrictions on lethal removal.

The core dispute: Should Colorado prioritize ranching heritage and rural economies, or defer to environmental groups pushing wolf reintroduction without bearing the financial consequences?

Meanwhile, a separate incident in Fort Collins saw a pickup truck plow into a sidewalk near the Tour de Fat bicycle festival, killing one person and injuring five (New York Post). The cause remains under investigation.
