# South Carolina GOP Senate Runoff Tests Trump's Endorsement Clout—Again

Trump's track record of backing winners faces another test this week as South Carolina Republicans head to a runoff that could reshape the party's direction in the Senate. Sen. Darline Graham Nordone, attempting to hold her brother's seat, is locked in a dead heat with Rep. Ralph Norman in a Trafalgar Group poll showing both candidates at roughly 48 percent—neither commanding majority support (Washington Examiner).

The race matters beyond South Carolina. Trump has already seen five gubernatorial endorsements fail this cycle, and this runoff will signal whether his influence remains decisive in GOP primaries or whether the party is fracturing along new lines. Graham Nordone's bid to keep the seat in her family faces stiff headwinds from Norman, who is mounting a credible challenge despite the incumbent's advantages.

For Colorado conservatives watching national GOP dynamics, this runoff illustrates a broader question: as Trump's endorsement power fluctuates, which Republican faction will dominate Senate races heading into 2026? The outcome could reshape how Colorado's own GOP establishment positions itself in upcoming statewide contests.

Watch whether Trump doubles down on Graham Nordone or shifts strategy if Norman gains momentum before Tuesday's vote.
