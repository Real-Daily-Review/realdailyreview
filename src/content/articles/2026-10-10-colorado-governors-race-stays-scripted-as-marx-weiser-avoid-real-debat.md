# Colorado Governor's Race Heads Into Final Stretch With Limited Direct Debate

Colorado Republicans have a genuine opening to reclaim the governor's office after eight years of Democratic control, but the 2026 race between Republican Victor Marx and Democrat Phil Weiser has been marked by carefully managed appearances rather than substantive clash.

The two candidates met for what amounted to a scripted forum hosted by the Colorado Chamber of Commerce, where questions were provided in advance (CPR News, Denver Post). This wasn't a traditional debate—it was a controlled event designed to minimize risk for both sides.

Weiser, the Democratic Attorney General, is running to replace term-limited Gov. Jared Polis. He's positioning himself as the continuity candidate on progressive priorities, though he hasn't detailed how he'd address Colorado's affordability crisis or energy policy without repeating Polis-era regulations that conservatives argue have driven up costs and killed jobs.

Marx, the Republican challenger, has largely stayed above the fray, which Weiser criticized as evasive conduct (Colorado Sun). Republicans argue Marx represents a return to pro-growth policies: lower taxes, less regulatory burden on energy development, and protection of property rights—priorities that resonate with Colorado's rural counties and business community.

The lack of genuine debate is telling: neither candidate seems eager for unscripted confrontation on inflation, water rights, or energy policy.

**Watch for:** Whether Marx breaks through with Colorado's independent voters before November, or if Democratic turnout machine advantages prove decisive.
