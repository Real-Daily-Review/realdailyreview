# Colorado Senators Split on Permitting Reform as Schumer Leadership Fight Looms

Colorado's two Democratic senators are charting sharply different courses on energy and infrastructure policy—and staying mum on Chuck Schumer's leadership battle.

Sen. John Hickenlooper backed a Senate permitting reform package that passed 62-33, while Sen. Michael Bennet opposed it (CPR News). The bill aims to streamline federal approval timelines for energy and infrastructure projects—a rare bipartisan win that conservatives argue removes unnecessary bureaucratic delays strangling Colorado's oil, gas, and renewable development.

Republicans contend that permitting gridlock has cost Colorado jobs and tax revenue, while environmental groups warn that faster approvals threaten public lands and water resources. Bennet's opposition signals alignment with the progressive wing skeptical of expedited energy permitting.

Meanwhile, both senators are dodging questions about whether they'll support Schumer's re-election as Senate majority leader, as Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen mounts a challenge (Denver Post). Neither Hickenlooper nor Bennet has publicly committed, suggesting internal Democratic fractures over leadership direction and policy priorities.

The divergence on permitting—Colorado's most economically consequential federal issue—underscores how little daylight exists between Denver's two senators on energy policy, despite Hickenlooper's occasional bipartisan posturing.
