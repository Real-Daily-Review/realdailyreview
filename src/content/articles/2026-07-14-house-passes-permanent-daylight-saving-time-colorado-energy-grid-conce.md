# House Passes Permanent Daylight Saving Time—But Colorado's Energy Grid Deserves Scrutiny

The House has passed the Sunshine Protection Act with rare bipartisan backing, moving to eliminate the twice-yearly time change that's plagued Americans for decades. The vote was lopsided: 308–117, with 95 Democrats joining Republicans (Fox News Politics). Rep. Vern Buchanan (R-FL) has championed the measure since 2018, and President Trump has signaled support for ending the clock shuffle.

The appeal is straightforward: no more "springing forward" and "falling back," meaning later sunsets year-round and fewer disruptions to daily life. But the Senate remains skeptical, with concerns about circadian rhythm impacts and road safety still unresolved (Fox News Politics).

**Here's what matters for Colorado:** The state's energy grid and agricultural sector deserve hard scrutiny before permanent daylight saving time takes effect. Colorado's ranchers and farmers operate on natural light cycles; permanent DST could complicate water management and livestock schedules tied to sunrise-sunset patterns. Energy demand curves may shift unpredictably, affecting rural power reliability.

Conservatives should support efficiency and less government meddling with citizens' schedules—but not at the cost of overlooking real operational impacts on Colorado's energy independence and agricultural economy. Watch whether the Senate demands a Colorado-specific impact study before advancing this bill.
