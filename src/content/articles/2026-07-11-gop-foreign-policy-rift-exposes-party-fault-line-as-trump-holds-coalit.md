# GOP Foreign Policy Divide Tests Party Unity as Trump Steers Course

The Republican Party faces its sharpest internal fracture over America's role abroad, with foreign policy hawks and restrainers locked in fundamental disagreement—and Donald Trump positioned as the only figure capable of holding the coalition together, according to *The Daily Wire*.

Hawks argue the U.S. must confront adversaries through military strength and sustained engagement. Restrainers counter that endless wars drain resources and American lives without clear national interest, pushing for a more limited interventionist posture.

Trump's appeal, analysts note, lies in his ability to bridge this divide through his distinctive brand of conservative populism. As *The Denver Post* reports, Trump has reshaped Republican identity beyond traditional conservatism, blending religious values, economic nationalism, and anti-establishment fervor into a movement that commands party loyalty across factional lines.

For Colorado Republicans, this matters: the state's defense contractors and military installations depend on sustained defense spending, while rural conservatives increasingly question costly foreign commitments that drain resources from domestic priorities like border security and energy independence.

The real test comes next: whether the GOP can maintain unity on foreign policy without Trump as its unifying force, or whether the party fractures into competing camps over America's global role.
