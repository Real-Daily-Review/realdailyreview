# Michigan Democrat's GOP Senate Endorsement Exposes Party Fracture Over Progressive Nominee

A sitting Democratic congresswoman's refusal to back her party's Senate nominee in Michigan signals deepening Democrat divisions over far-left candidates—a warning sign for Colorado's left-leaning establishment.

Rep. Haley Stevens (D-MI) endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over Democrat Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan's Senate race, and she's standing firm despite El-Sayed's primary victory. "I don't regret it," Stevens said this week, acknowledging the political cost of her cross-party move (Washington Examiner).

The defection matters because it's not isolated. National Review reports that Stevens "is not alone"—multiple Democrats harbor "serious reservations about Abdul El-Sayed," suggesting the progressive activist's nomination sparked genuine alarm within the party's moderate wing.

El-Sayed, a former Michigan health director and progressive firebrand, represents the activist-left faction Democrats have increasingly elevated in primaries. Stevens's public break signals that mainstream Democrats fear such nominees alienate swing voters and hand seats to Republicans.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado Democrats have similarly elevated progressive candidates in statewide races. If Michigan's pattern holds—where party insiders quietly doubt their own nominees—Colorado Republicans should watch whether establishment Democrats here show similar cracks when facing 2026 primary winners they view as unelectable.
