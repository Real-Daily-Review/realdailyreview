# Federal Reserve Holds Rates Steady, Signals No Cuts Coming

The Federal Reserve under new Chair Kevin Warsh held interest rates unchanged this week while signaling an end to rate-cut expectations that had buoyed markets earlier this year. In an unusually terse statement, Fed officials scrapped language suggesting their next move would be to lower rates, according to 9News Denver.

The shift reflects persistent inflation pressures. Just The News reports wholesale business inflation exceeded 6% in May, with overall consumer inflation above 4%—figures the Fed attributes partly to geopolitical tensions including the war in Iran.

**The Colorado angle:** Higher-for-longer rates squeeze Colorado homebuyers, small businesses, and agricultural operations already facing tight credit conditions. Ranchers and energy companies dependent on capital investment face continued headwinds.

**Conservative take:** Republicans and fiscal hawks argue the Fed's earlier rate-hiking campaign was necessary medicine for inflation caused by Biden-era government overspending and regulatory overreach. However, they worry prolonged high rates could trigger recession—a risk if Washington doesn't rein in deficit spending. The Warsh Fed's hawkish stance reflects acknowledgment that inflation remains a real threat, not transitory as progressives claimed.

Democrats counter that rate hikes harm working families and argue the Fed should prioritize employment alongside price stability.

**Watch:** Whether Warsh's Fed maintains discipline if political pressure mounts for rate cuts ahead of 2024.
