# Douglas County Hands Out $19M Tax Break for Data Center—With Minimal Job Gains

Douglas County commissioners just approved a $19 million tax rebate for a data center project that will create only 16 new jobs, according to 9News Denver. The deal waives taxes on business personal property for 35 years—a sweetheart arrangement that raises hard questions about whether Colorado taxpayers are subsidizing corporate profits.

The measure exemplifies a familiar conservative concern: government using tax dollars (or foregone revenue) to pick winners and losers rather than letting markets work. Supporters argue such incentives attract investment and boost the local economy. But critics counter that $19 million divided by 16 jobs amounts to roughly $1.2 million per position—a steep price for taxpayers who foot the bill through reduced services or higher taxes elsewhere.

Meanwhile, Boulder and Douglas counties are also weighing expansion of their county commissions (Denver Post), with commissioners citing population growth as justification for more government seats. Conservatives should scrutinize whether adding bureaucratic layers actually improves governance or simply expands the apparatus.

Colorado taxpayers need to watch whether these tax-break deals deliver promised economic returns—or become permanent giveaways to connected corporations.
