# U.S. Military Sinks Narco-Terrorist Supply Vessel in Pacific Operation

The U.S. military is doing what the Biden-Harris administration should have done years ago at the southern border: taking the fight to drug cartels before they reach American soil.

Marines and sailors from the USS San Antonio boarded, searched, and sank a refueling vessel in the Eastern Pacific Ocean allegedly supplying the Los Choneros narco-terrorist organization, according to the New York Post and Washington Examiner. The operation represents U.S. Southern Command's latest effort to disrupt cocaine and fentanyl trafficking networks originating in South America.

The action underscores a hard truth: when the federal government actually prioritizes interdiction and enforcement, it works. Yet while our military disrupts cartel logistics overseas, the administration has gutted border security, allowing record fentanyl seizures and migrant crossings at home. Colorado communities—from Denver to rural counties—face the downstream consequences of cartel-supplied drugs flooding American streets.

Republicans have long argued that robust military and law enforcement action against trafficking organizations must be paired with serious border enforcement. Without both, sinking one refueling vessel amounts to treating symptoms while ignoring the disease.

Conservatives should watch whether this operation signals a genuine shift in administration priorities on cartel disruption, or remains an isolated action while open-border policies persist.
