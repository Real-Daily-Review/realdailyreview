# Denver-Area Crime Wave Continues as Police Struggle to Keep Streets Safe

Denver and Aurora are reeling from a spike in violent crime that exposes the failure of progressive law-and-order policies across the metro area. Over recent weeks, police have responded to a hit-and-run that left a pedestrian seriously injured, a targeted shooting at an Aurora gas station that hospitalized two women, and a massive unlawful gathering in Aurora that required arrests of juveniles and warrants for four additional suspects (KDVR Fox31).

The incidents paint a troubling picture: drivers fleeing accident scenes, brazen daytime shootings, and street takeovers that police describe as "unlawful gatherings." Aurora police have made arrests and issued warrants, but the underlying pattern suggests law enforcement is playing catch-up rather than preventing crime.

Conservatives argue that Colorado's progressive approach to criminal justice—including lenient bail policies, reduced prosecution priorities, and underfunded police departments—has emboldened criminals and made streets less safe for ordinary Coloradans. Republicans point to these incidents as evidence that soft-on-crime policies don't work and that communities need robust law enforcement, swift prosecution, and consequences that actually deter criminal behavior.

Democrats typically counter that crime stems from poverty and lack of social services, not judicial leniency, though they've offered few solutions as incidents mount.

**Watch for:** Whether Aurora and Denver city councils will fund police adequately or double down on defunding rhetoric that's left neighborhoods vulnerable.
