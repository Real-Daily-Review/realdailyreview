# Colorado Schools Must Teach American Greatness, Not Guilt

Colorado's education establishment has spent years peddling narratives of national shame to students who deserve to inherit pride in American achievement instead.

National Review and The Daily Wire argue that schools have a civic duty to teach students they are "inheritors of the country's achievements" and stewards responsible for "maintaining this great civilization." This contrasts sharply with progressive curricula that emphasize historical grievances and systemic guilt—an approach that leaves Colorado's next generation demoralized rather than motivated to contribute.

The conservative case is straightforward: students should learn accurate history, including America's flaws, but within a framework that celebrates the nation's founding principles, constitutional protections, and unmatched record of innovation and freedom. A Canadian observer noted gratitude for American liberty and stability—a reminder that many outside our borders understand what some Colorado educators seem determined to obscure.

Democrats and progressive educators counter that acknowledging historical injustices and systemic inequities is essential to authentic citizenship. They argue that critical examination strengthens rather than weakens patriotism.

Colorado parents and taxpayers should demand transparency about what their schools teach regarding American history and national identity—and hold school boards accountable for curricula that either inspire or demoralize the next generation.
