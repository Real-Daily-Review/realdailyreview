# Jeffco School Spending Measure Barely Survives Recount—Voters Unmoved

Jefferson County Public Schools' $73 million tax increase squeaked through a recount by the narrowest of margins, exposing voter skepticism about throwing more money at Colorado's second-largest school district without proof it will improve outcomes.

Proposition NN, a mill levy override, passed after trailing by just five votes out of nearly 302,000 cast (Denver Post). The Colorado Sun's analysis noted that while the measure technically won, "support for education seems tepid"—a telling sign that even in a district where schools dominate local conversation, taxpayers aren't convinced additional spending equals better results.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives have long argued that Colorado school funding has grown substantially in recent years, yet test scores and graduation rates haven't kept pace. They question whether districts are managing existing dollars efficiently before asking voters for more. The recount-thin margin suggests many Jeffco families share that concern.

For Colorado taxpayers already burdened by rising property taxes and cost-of-living pressures, this result signals growing resistance to education spending measures that lack clear accountability benchmarks or performance guarantees.

Watch whether Jeffco's school board commits to transparent spending metrics and measurable academic gains—or simply pockets the $73 million and asks for more in two years.
