# Denver's Aging Water Infrastructure Crumbles—Again

Denver's crumbling water infrastructure is leaving residents without service and streets torn up, a stark reminder of government's failure to maintain basic utilities. A 69-year-old water main ruptured on South Holly Street in Virginia Village this weekend, forcing Denver Water to shut down the road and leave eight homes without water for days (KDVR Fox31). The repair work is expected to keep South Holly Street between East Evans and East Iliff avenues closed through Sunday night (9News).

This isn't an isolated incident. Denver's water system is aging, and the city has known for years that infrastructure replacement demands serious capital investment—yet budget priorities keep getting diverted to progressive pet projects instead. While Denver Water scrambles to patch 69-year-old pipes, taxpayers foot the bill for service disruptions and emergency repairs that proper maintenance could have prevented.

The real question: How many more main breaks will Denver residents tolerate before city leadership prioritizes fixing what actually matters? Coloradans should watch whether Denver Water and city council finally commit real dollars to systematic pipe replacement, or whether this becomes another excuse for rate hikes on residents already paying some of the nation's highest water costs.
