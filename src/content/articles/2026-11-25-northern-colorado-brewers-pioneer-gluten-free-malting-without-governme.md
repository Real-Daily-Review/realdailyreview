# Northern Colorado Brewers Bet on Gluten-Free Malting as Market Niche Grows

Northern Colorado's craft beer industry is experimenting with gluten-free malting as a competitive edge. Grouse Malt House in Wellington is leading the charge, partnering with regional brewers to develop gluten-free beer that doesn't sacrifice taste—a shift that could carve out a profitable market segment for Colorado's already robust brewing economy.

The operation removes gluten during the malting process itself, rather than relying on post-production filtering or substitutes. Local breweries including Holidaily and Golden Prairie are collaborating on product development, signaling genuine market demand beyond niche health-conscious consumers.

For Colorado's craft beer sector—a $1.7 billion industry supporting thousands of jobs statewide—this represents organic innovation driven by consumer preference, not government mandate. Unlike progressive states imposing sustainability mandates or carbon taxes on breweries, Colorado's beer makers are solving real problems through competition and entrepreneurship.

The gluten-free pivot also opens export opportunities. Celiac disease and gluten sensitivity affect millions of Americans, yet few breweries have cracked truly palatable gluten-free options. If Grouse Malt House and its partners succeed, they could establish Colorado as a national hub for premium gluten-free craft beer—without a single tax incentive or regulatory carve-out.

Watch whether this model attracts investment capital and whether other Colorado malting operations follow suit.
