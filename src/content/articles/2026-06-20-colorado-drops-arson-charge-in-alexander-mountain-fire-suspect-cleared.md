# Prosecutors Drop Arson Charge in Alexander Mountain Fire—Suspect Cleared by New Evidence

Colorado prosecutors have dismissed the arson charge against Jason Hobby in connection with the 2024 Alexander Mountain wildfire, acknowledging that new evidence proved he could not have started the blaze. The district attorney's office cited alibi evidence that exonerated the suspect, a significant reversal in a case that initially drew public attention during peak fire season.

A retired U.S. Forest Service investigator told 9News Denver that dropping charges against the wrong suspect demonstrates "high integrity" in complex wildfire investigations, noting such cases often take years to resolve properly. The decision underscores the difficulty prosecutors face in wildfire cases where evidence collection and timeline reconstruction are critical.

For Colorado taxpayers and property owners, the case highlights both the challenges law enforcement faces in rapid wildfire response and the importance of rigorous evidence standards before charges stick. Wildfire investigations often occur under intense public pressure, making prosecutorial restraint a virtue—though it also raises questions about initial investigative procedures.

Conservatives should watch whether this case prompts review of Colorado's wildfire prevention and response protocols, particularly regarding forest management practices that reduce fuel loads and fire risk on state and federal lands.
