# America's Energy Dominance Slipping While Beijing Plays Long Game in Our Backyard

The U.S. is losing ground in a strategic energy competition with China—not in the South China Sea, but in the Western Hemisphere, where Beijing is quietly consolidating control over Latin American oil and resources while American policymakers remain distracted.

According to National Review, "peace through strength" in the modern era requires energy security, not just military might. China has spent years building relationships with Venezuela, Bolivia, and other regional producers, locking in long-term supply agreements and infrastructure investments. Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner reports that Chevron and other U.S. energy firms are preparing major new investments in Venezuela—a tacit admission that American energy strategy has ceded initiative to Beijing.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil and gas industry, already battered by years of regulatory hostility from Democratic administrations, watches as China outmaneuvers the U.S. in hemispheric energy markets. Weakened domestic energy independence means higher costs for Colorado consumers and fewer jobs for Colorado workers in an industry that generates billions in state revenue.

**What conservatives argue:** Reversing this trend requires unleashing American energy production at home—not strangling it with environmental regulations. Domestic oil, gas, and mineral development aren't just economic policy; they're national security strategy.

Watch for whether the incoming administration prioritizes permitting reform and domestic energy development, or continues the regulatory drag that handed geopolitical advantage to Beijing.
