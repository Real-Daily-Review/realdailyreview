# Colorado's Driverless-Car Loophole: No Law to Ticket Waymo's Reckless Denver Stunt

A Waymo robotaxi drove straight through a closed Denver farmers market during setup—and Colorado has no legal mechanism to cite the vehicle for breaking traffic laws, according to Denver Police and Colorado State Patrol (9News Denver). Video from KDVR Fox31 shows the autonomous vehicle navigating the closed road and through the market area, raising serious questions about public safety oversight.

Here's the problem: Colorado's regulatory framework hasn't caught up to driverless-car deployment. With no statute allowing authorities to ticket an unmanned vehicle, Waymo faces zero accountability for reckless operation in a public space where families shop and gather.

Republicans and conservatives argue this exemplifies progressive government's failure to regulate new technology responsibly before deployment. Rather than rushing autonomous vehicles onto Denver streets, lawmakers should have established clear liability rules, traffic enforcement mechanisms, and safety standards first. Coloradans deserve protection—not regulatory chaos masquerading as innovation.

Democrats and tech advocates counter that autonomous vehicles are safer overall and that existing traffic laws apply to the *operators* (Waymo's remote systems), not the car itself.
