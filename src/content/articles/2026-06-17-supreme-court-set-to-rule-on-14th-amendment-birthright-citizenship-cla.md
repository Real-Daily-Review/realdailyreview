# Supreme Court Poised to Clarify 14th Amendment's Birthright Citizenship Language

The Supreme Court is preparing to rule on one of the Constitution's most litigated phrases: whether "subject to the jurisdiction thereof" in the 14th Amendment's opening sentence actually grants automatic citizenship to all children born on U.S. soil, regardless of their parents' legal status.

The 14th Amendment's first sentence contains just 28 words, but five of them—"subject to the jurisdiction thereof"—have sparked constitutional warfare since 1866. Conservatives argue the phrase was never intended to confer citizenship on children of foreign nationals or those owing allegiance to another power. Republicans contend the original understanding of the amendment supports a narrower reading that excludes certain categories of births.

The Left counters that birthright citizenship is settled law and that restricting it would upend a century-plus of precedent and create a two-tiered citizenship system. Democrats warn the ruling could affect millions of Americans and destabilize immigration policy.

For Colorado, the outcome could reshape how the state processes citizenship claims and interacts with federal immigration enforcement—particularly relevant as the state grapples with sanctuary policies and border-related population shifts.

Watch whether the Court's decision triggers legislative action in Congress or state capitals to clarify citizenship rules through statute.
