# Social Security's Ticking Time Bomb: Why Colorado Retirees Should Worry Now

Washington is finally admitting what conservatives have warned for decades: Social Security is headed for collapse. Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI), who chairs the Senate Budget Committee, has sounded the alarm in a new congressional memo, calling the program a "legal Ponzi scheme" hurtling toward insolvency by 2032—just eight years away (Fox News Politics).

Johnson's blunt assessment has drawn predictable fire from Democrats, who accuse him of "throwing in the towel" on the nation's largest entitlement program (Washington Examiner). But Republicans argue the real scandal is that Americans have been systematically misled about Social Security's viability for decades, with the program's trustees knowing full well it cannot pay promised benefits without major structural reform.

For Colorado's 900,000-plus Social Security beneficiaries and millions more counting on it for retirement, this is no academic debate. When the trust fund depletes in 2032, automatic benefit cuts of roughly 20 percent will kick in unless Congress acts. That means seniors face either massive cuts, higher payroll taxes on working Coloradans, or means-testing that punishes savers.

Democrats offer no credible fix beyond tax hikes. Republicans must push real solutions—raising the retirement age, adjusting benefit formulas for higher earners, and allowing younger workers to own personal retirement accounts.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democratic members—will propose actual solvency measures or simply defend the status quo until the crisis hits.
