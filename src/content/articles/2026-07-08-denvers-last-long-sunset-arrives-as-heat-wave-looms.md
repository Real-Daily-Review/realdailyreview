# Summer's End: Denver Faces Heat Wave as Daylight Fades

Denver is about to lose nearly three hours of evening daylight—fast—just as temperatures threaten to spike into dangerous territory this week.

The Mile High City will experience its last 8:30 p.m. sunset for 11 months, according to 9News Denver, marking the astronomical pivot toward shorter days. Meanwhile, KDVR Fox31 reports Denver could see its first 100-degree day within days, an uncommon occurrence that meteorologists suggest may happen only once this summer.

The dual shift—shrinking daylight combined with extreme heat—creates a compressed window for outdoor work and recreation. For Colorado's energy sector, the heat spike underscores why reliable power generation matters; rolling blackouts during peak demand remain a real risk as the state transitions away from traditional baseload capacity. Ranchers and agricultural workers face tighter schedules for field operations as both heat and shortened days compress the workday.

The seasonal transition also highlights Colorado's natural climate variability. Unlike progressive climate activists who weaponize every weather event, conservatives note that temperature swings and seasonal shifts are normal—and that Colorado's economy depends on adapting to them, not surrendering to alarmist policy prescriptions.
