# Poudre Schools Admit Defeat: No AC, Students Sent Home Early in Heat

Colorado taxpayers are footing the bill for a school district that can't keep classrooms cool. The Poudre School District will dismiss most elementary and middle school students two hours early Thursday due to soaring temperatures and a chronic lack of air conditioning in district buildings (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

This isn't a one-day problem. The district's infrastructure failure exposes a larger accountability issue: how much have Poudre administrators spent on administrative overhead, DEA drug-awareness programs, and other initiatives while basic climate control remains absent from classrooms?

The early dismissal affects thousands of students and creates childcare headaches for working families—a real-world consequence of poor district planning and capital management. Meanwhile, parents are left scrambling while school leadership points to the weather rather than their own facility failures.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that school districts must prioritize core classroom infrastructure—heating, cooling, and safe learning environments—before expanding programs or administrative budgets. The Left typically responds that schools are "underfunded" and need more taxpayer money, yet districts like Poudre struggle with basic maintenance despite Colorado's education funding increases.

Watch whether Poudre's board commits to a transparent, timeline-driven AC retrofit plan—or continues making excuses while Colorado families pick up the slack.
