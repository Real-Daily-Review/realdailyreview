# DOJ Cracks Down on $4M Medicare Fraud Scheme—But Colorado Taxpayers Foot the Bill for Nationwide Waste

The Department of Justice charged 19 defendants in an alleged $4 million Medicare and Medicaid fraud ring operating in Philadelphia, expanding its Northeast Health Care Fraud Strike Force to the region (Fox News). Prosecutors detailed schemes including a Medicaid recipient who filed claims for extensive home health assistance while working as a carpenter (Just The News).

The bust highlights systemic waste in federal health programs—money that ultimately comes from Colorado taxpayers. Medicare and Medicaid fraud drains resources that could reduce the tax burden on working families and small businesses across the state. While the DOJ's enforcement action is welcome, conservatives argue the real problem runs deeper: bloated federal health bureaucracies lack the incentives and accountability mechanisms of private markets to prevent such abuse in the first place.

Republicans point out that expanded government programs create sprawling opportunities for fraud. The Left counters that enforcement actions prove the system works, but that argument rings hollow when billions slip through annually before charges are filed. Colorado's share of federal health spending—and the fraud embedded within it—represents money that could stay in state coffers or reduce the federal debt burden on future generations.

Watch whether the DOJ's Philadelphia expansion leads to similar strike forces targeting fraud in Colorado's own Medicare and Medicaid programs.
