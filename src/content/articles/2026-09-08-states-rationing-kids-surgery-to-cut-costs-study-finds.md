# State Budget Cuts Are Denying Kids Surgery to Balance Books

Colorado families should know: state governments are rationing pediatric care to plug budget holes, and your child could be next in line for denial.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, a landmark study published in JAMA Pediatrics tracking data through 2022 documents how states are treating children like senior citizens in insurance coverage decisions—prioritizing cost-cutting over medical necessity. The erosion of pediatric healthcare access, once a quiet crisis, is now breaking into national view, reinforced by a January 2026 presidential brief.

The mechanism is straightforward: state Medicaid programs and insurance rules increasingly apply adult-centered rationing logic to children's cases, delaying or denying surgeries and treatments that would be approved without question in prior years. Computers running cost-containment algorithms now make life-altering decisions about whether a child gets care.

**Conservatives argue** this is what happens when government treats healthcare as a budget line item rather than a moral obligation. When states face fiscal pressure, they cut what they think won't generate political backlash—and pediatric care, lacking the organized senior-citizen lobby, becomes an easy target. The result: bureaucratic denial replacing medical judgment.

Colorado taxpayers and parents should demand transparency: which procedures are being denied, and on what cost-saving criteria? Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to audit state Medicaid denials and restore clear protections for pediatric care decisions.
