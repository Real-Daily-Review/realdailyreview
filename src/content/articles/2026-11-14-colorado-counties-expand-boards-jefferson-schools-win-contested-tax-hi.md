# Boulder, Douglas Counties Add Commissioners; Jefferson Schools Win Tax Hike

Colorado voters have delivered mixed results on governance and spending. Boulder and Douglas County voters approved measures to expand their boards of commissioners, though *Complete Colorado* reports neither side got exactly what it wanted from the outcomes.

Meanwhile, Jefferson County taxpayers narrowly approved Ballot Issue 5A, a $73 million tax increase earmarked for teacher pay and career education programs, according to CPR News. The measure passed, but a recount is underway—a reminder that even in blue-leaning Jefferson County, voter appetite for new taxes remains fragile.

**The conservative case against the tax hike:** Republicans and fiscal watchdogs argue that Colorado schools already receive substantial state funding and that throwing more money at education without structural reform—merit pay, administrative efficiency, or competitive bidding on contracts—simply rewards the status quo. They contend that Jefferson County families already shoulder heavy tax burdens and that a $73 million increase will inevitably creep higher as districts normalize the new baseline.

**The broader picture:** These results underscore a pattern: Colorado voters will approve spending measures when framed as education or local services, but margins are tightening and recounts are becoming routine. Taxpayers should watch whether the Jefferson recount shifts the outcome and whether other districts attempt similar levies before 2027.
