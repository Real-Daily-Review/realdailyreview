# California's AI Audit Rules Miss the Mark—Colorado Should Learn the Lesson

California's new artificial intelligence audit regulations, announced Sept. 9, exemplify progressive overreach: they create bureaucratic oversight without requiring the one thing that matters—proof that audits actually work.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the state's rulebook for AI auditors contains a critical flaw: nothing mandates that audits verify AI accuracy or truthfulness. An op-ed from a former public company audit board member notes California "got more right than wrong" in structure, but the fatal gap remains. Regulators imposed process requirements without demanding results.

This matters for Colorado. As tech companies and AI applications expand across the state—from energy optimization to financial services—Colorado policymakers will face pressure to adopt similar "safeguard" frameworks. Republicans and conservatives should resist the California model: regulatory theater that grows government without delivering accountability.

The real problem isn't AI itself; it's regulators pretending they can mandate truth through paperwork. Colorado's business-friendly reputation depends on avoiding this trap.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado lawmakers propose copycat AI audit bills in the 2025 session—and whether they'll demand actual performance metrics, not just compliance checkboxes.
