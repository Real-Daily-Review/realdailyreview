# Trump's Iran Diplomacy Risks Squandering U.S. Leverage Over Strait of Hormuz

The Trump administration is negotiating away American leverage in the Persian Gulf without securing meaningful concessions from Tehran, according to conservative analysts tracking the talks over the critical Strait of Hormuz.

Iran, emboldened by months of diplomatic overtures, is signaling it won't accept U.S. terms even as Washington offers concessions. The Washington Examiner reports that Trump officials have pledged to lift their blockade of Iranian ports if commercial shipping resumes—but Iran says an agreement with Oman alone won't reopen the waterway. That's a classic negotiating trap: the U.S. gives first, Iran demands more.

National Review argues the administration is essentially surrendering without extracting reciprocal commitments. Republicans contend that showing weakness on Iran invites further demands and undermines American credibility with Gulf allies—particularly Saudi Arabia and the UAE, crucial partners for Colorado's energy sector and broader Middle East stability.

The core conservative critique: Trump is repeating the same strategic error of past administrations by negotiating from a position of apparent desperation rather than strength. Iran reads that as an opening to extract maximum concessions.

Watch whether the administration secures ironclad guarantees on shipping access and port reopening before lifting any sanctions—or capitulates to Iranian demands for additional relief.
