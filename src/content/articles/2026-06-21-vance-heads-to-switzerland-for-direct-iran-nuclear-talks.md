# Vance Heads to Switzerland for Direct Iran Nuclear Talks

Vice President JD Vance arrived in Switzerland Sunday to join envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner for a new round of U.S.-Iran negotiations—signaling the Trump administration's willingness to pursue direct diplomacy on the nuclear question that has defined U.S.-Middle East policy for two decades.

President Trump has made clear the administration's red line: Iran must abandon its nuclear weapons program as a condition of any deal. This marks a sharp departure from the Obama-era Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), which conservatives argued was a capitulation that merely delayed Iranian nuclear ambitions while flooding Tehran with cash.

The timing reflects Trump's broader foreign policy doctrine—maximum pressure coupled with the credible threat of military action, paired with a willingness to negotiate from strength. Conservatives have long argued that the previous administration's approach emboldened Iran's regional proxy networks and left America's allies in the Gulf exposed.

Vance's personal involvement underscores the seriousness of these talks. The administration has signaled it will not repeat the mistakes of predecessor negotiations, though details remain sparse on what concessions—if any—the U.S. might offer beyond sanctions relief.

Watch whether these negotiations produce concrete Iranian concessions on nuclear inspections and weapons development, or whether they devolve into the same stalling tactics that plagued previous rounds.
