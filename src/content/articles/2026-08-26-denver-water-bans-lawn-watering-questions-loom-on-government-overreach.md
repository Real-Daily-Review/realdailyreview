# Denver Water Bans Lawn Watering—Another Government Mandate Over Colorado Property Rights

Denver Water has ordered a complete ban on lawn watering starting October 1, citing drought conditions and record summer heat, but the move raises familiar questions about government overreach into private property decisions.

The Denver Board of Water Commissioners approved the prohibition following what the Colorado Sun calls the "hottest metro July on record." Officials argue the ban is necessary to preserve reservoir levels during severe drought conditions (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

Conservatives and property rights advocates will likely question whether a blanket government prohibition is the right tool. Market-based approaches—tiered pricing that incentivizes conservation without banning—have proven effective elsewhere and respect homeowner autonomy. Denver Water could raise rates during drought emergencies, letting consumers decide their own water use rather than imposing one-size-fits-all mandates backed by fines.

The agency's authority to enforce fines for violations also deserves scrutiny. Coloradans already pay for water service; additional penalties for watering their own property blur the line between regulation and confiscation.

**Watch whether Denver Water considers voluntary conservation incentives or rate adjustments before expanding enforcement actions.**
