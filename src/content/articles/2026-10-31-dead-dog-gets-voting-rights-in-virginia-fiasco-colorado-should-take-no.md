# Virginia's Voting Fiasco: Dead Dog Gets Rights Restoration, Raising Election Integrity Alarms

A deceased beagle named Biscuit has become the latest embarrassment for Virginia Governor Spanberger's blanket felon rights restoration program—and conservatives say it's a cautionary tale for Colorado and other states considering similar policies.

According to Fox News and the New York Post, Biscuit, a Goochland County dog dead for five years, received an official rights restoration certificate. The confused owner told outlets: "Biscuit was a dog. Biscuit has been dead for five years." The mishap follows other documented cases of dead felons receiving restoration notices under Spanberger's executive order.

Republicans argue the fiasco exposes the recklessness of mass restoration programs that bypass individual case review. Without proper vetting, they contend, such initiatives create opportunities for fraud, administrative chaos, and voter roll contamination—risks that undermine election integrity.

Democrats and progressive advocates counter that rights restoration for non-violent offenders reflects criminal justice reform principles and shouldn't be derailed by administrative glitches.

Colorado has pursued its own criminal justice reforms, including reduced felony penalties and reentry programs. As the state considers broader voting access policies, conservatives warn that Spanberger's Virginia debacle demonstrates why election administration demands rigor, not rubber stamps.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers cite the Biscuit case when debating future felon disenfranchisement or voter roll management reforms.
