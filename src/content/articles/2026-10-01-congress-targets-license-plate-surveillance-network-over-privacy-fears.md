# License Plate Cameras Spark Privacy Showdown in Congress

Congress is waking up to a surveillance problem hiding in plain sight: Flock Safety's automated license plate reader network, which tracks millions of vehicles with virtually no oversight or accountability.

A Senate Judiciary subcommittee hearing on September 23 examined the company's sprawling camera system, raising alarms about privacy, potential misuse, and whether the public-safety benefits justify the dragnet (Washington Examiner). The next day, Rep. Greg Steube (R-FL) introduced the Facilitating Liberty and Accountability for Flock Observations Act, which would require federal agencies to obtain warrants before accessing the data—a basic constitutional safeguard currently absent.

**The conservative concern is straightforward:** government and law enforcement are deploying mass surveillance infrastructure without meaningful legal guardrails. Nobody can prove who's actually watching the data, how it's being used, or whether innocent Coloradans' movements are being tracked without cause. This is exactly the kind of creeping government overreach that erodes Fourth Amendment protections.

Democrats have largely stayed quiet on the issue, but privacy advocates across the spectrum worry that Flock's network—already deployed in hundreds of jurisdictions—operates in a legal gray zone where warrants aren't required and abuse is nearly impossible to detect.
