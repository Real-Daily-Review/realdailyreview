# Cartel Gunfire on Rio Grande Signals Escalating Border Threat to U.S. Security

Colorado's southern border is no longer a theoretical problem—it's an active national security crisis that demands federal action, not sanctuary policies.

On September 26, a Coast Guard crew patrolling the Rio Grande near Garceño, Texas, came under direct gunfire from the Mexican side of the river. The crew returned fire; no Americans were injured. A week later, U.S. Northern Command responded by deploying additional resources to the region, signaling that cartel violence has crossed a dangerous threshold (Washington Examiner).

Conservatives argue this escalation proves what border hawks have long maintained: Mexican drug cartels now operate with near-military capability and are willing to engage U.S. forces directly. The Washington Examiner frames the incident as terrorism, not mere criminal activity—a distinction that matters for how America responds.

For Colorado, this matters acutely. Cartels funnel fentanyl and other drugs through our state, devastating communities and families. They exploit open borders to move product northward while Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for overdose treatment, law enforcement, and social services.

Democrats' push for open-border policies and reduced enforcement directly undercuts the military and law enforcement personnel risking their lives on the Rio Grande. Republicans demand secure borders, adequate funding for border security, and rules of engagement that protect American personnel.

Watch whether the Biden-Harris administration treats cartel violence as the national security threat it is, or continues downplaying the crisis.
