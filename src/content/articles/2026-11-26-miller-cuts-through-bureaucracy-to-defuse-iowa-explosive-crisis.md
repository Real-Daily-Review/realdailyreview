# White House Cuts Through Red Tape to Defuse Iowa Explosive Crisis

Stephen Miller's White House chief of staff office just proved that decisive executive action beats bureaucratic paralysis—by stepping in to manage the Army's controlled detonation of 5 tons of destabilized lead azide in Iowa after the Pentagon's initial communications strategy threatened to spiral into a public relations disaster.

EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin credited Miller directly for the success, telling the Washington Examiner that "he took the reins" on the operation. According to The Daily Wire, Miller tapped Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell to handle the communications crisis after senior White House staffers grew alarmed by how the Army was bungling the messaging around the volatile materials disposal.

The episode illustrates a core conservative principle: when government agencies get tangled in their own processes, a lean, focused leadership team can cut through the noise and deliver results. Miller's intervention prevented what could have become a major environmental and political headache for the administration.

For Colorado—home to significant military installations, energy infrastructure, and hazardous materials handling operations—this signals the Trump administration's willingness to bypass sluggish agency protocols when public safety is on the line. Conservatives should watch whether this model of direct White House oversight extends to other regulatory bottlenecks that have hamstrung Colorado energy development and land management.
