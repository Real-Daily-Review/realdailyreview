# Colorado DSA Movement Faces Credibility Test as National Wing Stumbles

Colorado's Democratic Socialist Alliance is doubling down on radical rhetoric just as the national DSA movement hits electoral headwinds. *Complete Colorado* reports that state DSA chapters are embracing increasingly confrontational language reminiscent of Bolshevik ideology, while *National Review* documents a stunning primary defeat for Wisconsin DSA candidate Francesca Hong, who lost to moderate Democrat David Crowley in the gubernatorial race.

The timing matters for Colorado. As DSA activists gain influence in Denver city politics and state Democratic circles, voters should ask whether this fringe ideology translates into actual policy—or whether it remains performative grievance-mongering. Republicans argue that DSA's anti-capitalist, wealth-redistribution agenda would devastate Colorado's energy sector, small businesses, and property rights if implemented at scale.

The Wisconsin result suggests voters reject radical candidates when given a clear choice. Colorado conservatives should watch whether similar voter skepticism emerges in 2026 state races, particularly if DSA-backed candidates push for wealth taxes, energy bans, or aggressive land seizures affecting ranchers and rural communities.
