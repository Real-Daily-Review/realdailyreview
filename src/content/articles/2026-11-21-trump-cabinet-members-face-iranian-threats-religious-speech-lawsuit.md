# Trump Cabinet Members Face Legal and Security Threats as Administration Settles In

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has relocated to a military base following credible Iranian assassination threats, while Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins faces a federal court challenge over religious communications sent to USDA employees.

According to Fox News, a senior official confirmed that Iranian threats against Bessent reached an "advanced stage," prompting the security move. The development underscores ongoing tensions with Tehran over Trump administration foreign policy.

Separately, a Biden-appointed federal judge in California ruled Thursday that Rollins likely violated the Establishment Clause by sending agency-wide religious messages, including an Easter "He is risen" email and Christmas greetings (The Daily Wire). Judge Trina L. Thompson granted a preliminary injunction against the practice.

**The conservative take:** Republicans argue the ruling exemplifies judicial overreach—holiday greetings and expressions of faith are commonplace in American workplaces and hardly constitute unconstitutional coercion. The injunction reflects the left's hostility toward religious expression in government, they contend. Meanwhile, Bessent's security situation highlights real threats the administration must navigate.

**Colorado angle:** Federal employees across Colorado's USDA offices and Treasury operations could face similar restrictions on religious expression, affecting workplace culture at agencies managing public lands, water policy, and agricultural programs critical to the state's economy.

Watch for whether Rollins appeals the injunction and how courts define permissible religious speech in federal agencies going forward.
