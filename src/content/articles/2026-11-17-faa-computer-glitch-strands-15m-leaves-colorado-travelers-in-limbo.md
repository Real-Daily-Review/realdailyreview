# FAA Ground Stop Exposes Government IT Failures—Colorado Travelers Still Paying the Price

A nationwide FAA computer glitch stranded 1.5 million Americans and exposed the fragility of government-run aviation infrastructure, with Colorado travelers still grappling with cascading flight cancellations and delays days later (The Daily Wire).

The incident—triggered by what officials blamed on a "computer glitch"—left passengers with minimal information and no clear answers about what went wrong or how to prevent it. The Daily Wire reports the "disturbing truth" behind the outage points to systemic failures in how the federal government maintains critical infrastructure.

Colorado airports including Denver International bore the brunt of the disruption. KDVR Fox31 Denver reports that passengers across the state continue recovering from the fallout, with many upcoming schedules still in chaos.

**The conservative case:** This is exactly why government shouldn't monopolize critical infrastructure. The FAA's aging, poorly maintained computer systems—and lack of transparency when they fail—demonstrate why private-sector redundancy and competition work better. Coloradans depend on reliable air travel for business and tourism; federal bureaucrats have no accountability when they fail.

Democrats and the Biden administration have largely deflected blame, framing it as an isolated technical problem rather than a systemic indictment of government IT management.

Watch for whether Congress demands real accountability and considers privatizing FAA IT systems—or simply throws more taxpayer money at the problem.
