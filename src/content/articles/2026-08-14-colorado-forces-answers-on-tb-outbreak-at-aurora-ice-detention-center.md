# Colorado Health Officials Issue Order on TB at Aurora ICE Detention Center

Colorado's public health authorities have escalated their standoff with the GEO Group over a tuberculosis outbreak at an Aurora immigrant detention facility, issuing a formal health order after weeks of stonewalling by the private contractor and federal immigration officials.

The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment moved to compel access and information-sharing at the facility, where TB cases have emerged among detainees. According to CPR News, GEO Group and ICE had repeatedly denied state health authorities entry and data needed to assess the outbreak's scope and containment measures.

Conservatives and taxpayers should scrutinize this clash carefully. The GEO Group operates the facility under federal contract—meaning private operators are managing a federal immigration function while resisting state oversight. Republicans will likely argue that federal agencies must cooperate fully with state health authorities to protect Colorado residents, while also questioning whether private detention contractors should have this much autonomy over public health matters.

The Colorado Sun reports the health order represents a significant escalation in the conflict between state public health officials and the detention operator.

The key question for Colorado: Will the Trump administration and ICE respect state health authority, or will federal immigration enforcement continue to shield contractors from transparency?
