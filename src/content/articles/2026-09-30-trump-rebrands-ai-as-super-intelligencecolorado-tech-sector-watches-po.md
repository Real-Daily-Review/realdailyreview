# Trump Rebrands AI as 'Super Intelligence'—But Colorado Should Focus on Real Policy

President Trump has officially renamed artificial intelligence to "Super Intelligence" via executive order, claiming the term "artificial" undersells the technology's actual power and capability. Trump teased the rebrand during a state visit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and signed the order Tuesday evening following a White House meeting with top AI leaders (Just The News).

The president's rationale is straightforward: "We don't want to hear artificial because it's not artificial. It's very powerful, it's very brilliant" (The Daily Wire). The move signals Trump's intent to position the U.S. competitively in AI development while balancing innovation against regulation—a stance conservatives generally support as preferable to the Biden administration's heavy-handed regulatory approach.

For Colorado, the real story isn't semantics. Colorado's tech corridor, energy sector, and defense contractors increasingly rely on AI applications. A pro-innovation, light-touch regulatory stance could accelerate job creation and economic growth in the state. Conversely, if the rebrand masks continued federal overreach into AI governance—whether through data mandates, environmental compliance requirements, or labor regulations—Colorado businesses could face new compliance costs.

Conservatives argue that naming conventions matter less than actual policy: deregulation, tax certainty, and protecting intellectual property will drive innovation faster than any executive order.

Watch whether Trump's AI policy translates into concrete deregulation that benefits Colorado's private sector, or remains largely symbolic.
