# Waymo's Driverless Taxis Hit Denver Streets—But Questions Loom on Safety, Liability, and City Planning

Waymo launched its public driverless taxi service in Denver on Tuesday, covering 60 square miles of the metro area (Colorado Sun, 9News Denver). The move marks another step toward autonomous vehicle deployment in Colorado's capital—and raises immediate questions conservatives should scrutinize.

While innovation deserves room to operate, Denver's embrace of Waymo without clear liability frameworks or transparent safety data sets a troubling precedent. Who bears responsibility when a driverless car causes injury or property damage? What regulatory oversight ensures these vehicles meet Colorado safety standards? City officials have largely waved through the service with minimal public vetting of accident protocols or insurance requirements.

Republicans and free-market advocates generally support technological advancement, but not at the expense of clear rules and accountability. Denver's permissive approach risks making Colorado a testing ground for autonomous tech without adequate consumer protections or taxpayer safeguards.

The broader concern: as cities rush to embrace driverless fleets, will they demand genuine safety transparency, or simply defer to Silicon Valley's promises? Colorado taxpayers deserve answers before autonomous vehicles become ubiquitous on state roads.

Watch whether Colorado legislators demand mandatory safety reporting and liability standards before Waymo expands beyond Denver.
