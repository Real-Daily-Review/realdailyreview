# Iran War Deal Nears Completion as Mediators Finalize Terms in Tehran

The Biden administration is racing to clinch a deal ending the Iran war before leaving office, with Qatari mediators now in Tehran putting finishing touches on an agreement that could reshape Middle East stability—and U.S. energy markets.

According to reports from 9News Denver and the New York Post, negotiators are working toward a final accord. Trump has signaled that the Strait of Hormuz—critical to global oil shipping—would reopen immediately upon signing, a move that could ease energy prices and benefit Colorado's economy.

Conservatives argue the timing raises red flags. Any deal struck in the final weeks of an administration risks being reversed or renegotiated, creating uncertainty for markets and allies. Republicans have long opposed what they view as appeasement-style diplomacy with Iran, preferring maximum pressure and deterrence.

The agreement's terms remain unclear, but energy markets are watching closely. Lower oil prices could help Colorado consumers and businesses, though conservatives worry about the geopolitical cost of legitimizing Iranian power in the region.

**Watch:** Whether the incoming administration accepts, modifies, or scraps the deal—and how quickly the Strait of Hormuz reopens to stabilize global energy supplies.
