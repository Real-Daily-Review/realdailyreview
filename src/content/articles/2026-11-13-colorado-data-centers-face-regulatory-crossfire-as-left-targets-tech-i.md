# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Wage War on Progress

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are quietly becoming the backbone of American digital infrastructure, yet environmental activists and progressive regulators are increasingly targeting the industry as a threat. According to *Complete Colorado*, these facilities don't just support cloud computing and AI—they're essential to modern life itself. But as demand surges, so does pressure from the left to impose stricter environmental regulations and water-use restrictions that could cripple Colorado's competitive advantage.

Republicans and industry advocates argue that data centers represent high-value, low-impact economic development: they create jobs, generate tax revenue, and require minimal land footprint compared to traditional manufacturing. Conservatives warn that overregulation could drive this critical infrastructure to Texas, Arizona, or other states with friendlier business climates—costing Colorado workers and tax base.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that data centers consume enormous amounts of water and electricity, straining Colorado's already-stressed water supplies and complicating the state's renewable energy transition.

The real issue: Colorado's regulatory environment will determine whether the state remains a data center hub or becomes another cautionary tale of progressive policy driving business elsewhere. Watch for water-use regulations and energy mandates targeting the sector in the 2027 legislative session.
