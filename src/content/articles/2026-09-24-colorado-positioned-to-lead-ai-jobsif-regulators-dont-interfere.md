# Colorado Should Embrace AI Jobs, Not Regulate Them Into Oblivion

Colorado has a chance to lead on artificial intelligence workforce development—if lawmakers resist the urge to over-regulate the sector.

The Colorado Sun recently argued the state should channel AI skills into apprenticeships, positioning young workers in AI-enabled workplaces under clear standards. That's sensible. But the real question is whether Colorado will create the conditions for AI companies to thrive here, or whether progressive regulators will strangle the industry with red tape before it takes root.

Complete Colorado reports that a Magellan Strategies survey found solid reasons for Colorado to bet on artificial intelligence rather than panic about it. The state's tech corridor, energy infrastructure, and workforce talent make it naturally competitive for data centers and AI development—economic engines that could generate high-wage jobs and tax revenue without a single new government program.

Republicans and conservatives argue Colorado should lower barriers to AI investment: streamline permitting, keep energy costs competitive, and let the market sort out training and hiring. Democrats, by contrast, often push for government-mandated apprenticeships and regulatory frameworks that sound good but slow deployment and drive companies to friendlier states.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether the legislature resists the temptation to "manage" AI growth through new licensing, training mandates, or energy restrictions that undercut the state's competitive edge.
