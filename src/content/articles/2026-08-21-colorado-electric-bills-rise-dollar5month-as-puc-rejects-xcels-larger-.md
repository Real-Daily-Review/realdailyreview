# Colorado Electric Bills Rising Despite PUC Pushback on Xcel Rate Hike

Colorado families will see their electric bills climb $5 per month starting Aug. 29, after the state Public Utilities Commission rejected most of Xcel Energy's aggressive rate-hike request (Colorado Sun, CPR News). The utility giant originally sought a steeper increase, but regulators cut the proposal roughly in half following months of negotiations.

While the PUC's decision represents a partial win for ratepayers, conservatives argue the fundamental problem persists: Colorado's regulatory environment allows utilities to pass costs to consumers with minimal accountability. Xcel retains the right to request a rehearing, meaning the fight over rates isn't finished.

The rate increase reflects Xcel's infrastructure spending and operational costs—but taxpayers and businesses should question whether the utility's capital expenditures reflect genuine necessity or regulatory capture. Colorado's energy sector already faces mounting pressure from state mandates favoring renewables and grid modernization, costs that ultimately land on household and commercial bills.

Republicans and fiscal watchdogs contend that Colorado needs transparent cost-benefit analysis before utilities lock in permanent rate increases, especially as the state pursues aggressive decarbonization targets that drive up system-wide expenses.

Watch whether Xcel follows through on a rehearing request and how the PUC responds to future rate applications amid Colorado's energy transition.
