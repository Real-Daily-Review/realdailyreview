# Minnesota's Gun-Ban Reflex Won't Stop Gang Violence—and Colorado Should Take Note

A 100-shot gang gunfight in Bloomington, Minnesota, has exposed the left's predictable response to lawlessness: blame the gun, not the criminal. After CCTV captured a Somali gang battle that left 97 casings scattered across a neighborhood (Fox News), St. Louis Park Mayor Nadia Mohamed (D) called for a firearm ban as the solution (Breitbart). Meanwhile, Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison, facing reelection, has remained largely silent on the escalating gang violence plaguing the Twin Cities (Washington Examiner).

Republicans and Second Amendment advocates argue that disarming law-abiding citizens won't stop organized criminals from obtaining weapons—it never has. The real problem, conservatives say, is prosecutorial leniency, gang activity left unchecked, and a failure to enforce existing gun laws against actual criminals. A gun ban punishes responsible Coloradans and Minnesotans while leaving gangs armed and dangerous.

This matters in Colorado: progressive cities here have pushed similar gun-control measures while violent crime persists. Denver and Boulder have enacted restrictions that do nothing to stop criminals, only burden taxpayers and gun owners exercising constitutional rights.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats push Minnesota-style bans while violent crime climbs—and whether they'll finally acknowledge that gun control doesn't stop gangs.
