# Trump Administration Auctions Spectrum to Drive Down Broadband Costs

The Trump administration is moving to auction off 160 megahertz of Upper C-Band spectrum, letting private companies compete for government-controlled broadband access rather than letting bureaucrats control it—a market-friendly approach that should keep internet bills low for Colorado families and businesses.

The Federal Communications Commission will soon vote on the spectrum sale, part of a broader strategy to expand private-sector broadband capacity. According to the Washington Examiner, internet connectivity costs are already at historic lows, and auctioning spectrum accelerates that trend by increasing competition among providers.

For Colorado, this matters: rural broadband access remains uneven across the state, and spectrum auctions can incentivize private investment in underserved areas without forcing taxpayers to fund government-run broadband schemes. Private companies bidding for spectrum have skin in the game and profit motives to serve customers efficiently—unlike government programs that often waste money and deliver mediocre service.

Conservatives argue that spectrum auctions represent the right balance: government owns the airwaves but lets the market determine who uses them and how. This avoids the trap of government-run broadband, which progressives sometimes push as a "public utility" solution—code for another bloated bureaucracy.

Watch whether the FCC vote proceeds smoothly and whether Colorado's rural broadband gaps narrow as private carriers invest in newly auctioned spectrum.
