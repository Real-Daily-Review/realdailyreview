# Denver's Hidden Sewer Line Costs Taxpayers $18 Million—and City Council Was Left in the Dark

Denver taxpayers are footing an $18 million bill for a surprise discovery that should have been caught before construction began. When contractors renovating the 16th Street Mall unearthed a historic brick sewer system, the city's Department of Transportation and Infrastructure quietly approved the cost overrun without properly informing the city council committee responsible for oversight, according to Denver's auditor (KDVR Fox31, 9News).

The lack of transparency is the real scandal here. Conservatives argue that government agencies routinely hide cost overruns and project failures from elected officials and the public—a pattern that erodes accountability and wastes taxpayer dollars. The auditor's call for "more transparency" is bureaucratic understatement for what amounts to a breakdown in governance.

This is precisely why Colorado taxpayers should demand competitive bidding, thorough pre-construction surveys, and strict change-order limits on all municipal projects. Denver's handling of the 16th Street project exemplifies how government agencies operate without real consequences when they mismanage public money.

Watch whether Denver's city council demands actual reforms—or simply accepts the auditor's recommendations and moves on.
