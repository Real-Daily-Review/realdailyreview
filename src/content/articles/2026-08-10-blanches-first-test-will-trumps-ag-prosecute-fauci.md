# Trump's AG Pick Faces Early Test on Fauci Contempt Case

Todd Blanche's confirmation as Attorney General immediately confronts him with a politically charged decision: whether to prosecute former NIH Director Anthony Fauci on a contempt of Congress charge backed by House Republicans.

The House voted to hold Fauci in contempt over his handling of COVID-19 origins research and gain-of-function funding. Now Blanche must decide if the Justice Department will pursue the case—a move Democrats warn could set a precedent for "weaponized prosecution" (Fox News). 

Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), whose vote proved decisive in Blanche's confirmation, defended the pick as a "gradation of gray" rather than a black-and-white ideological choice (Washington Examiner). That nuance reflects the bind: Republicans want accountability for what they view as Fauci's deception and obstruction; Democrats see selective prosecution of a political opponent.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters as a test of whether the Trump administration will actually hold the federal bureaucracy accountable or retreat when pressure mounts. The Fauci case signals whether Blanche will enforce congressional subpoenas against powerful unelected officials or defer to institutional inertia.

Watch whether Blanche's DOJ moves on the contempt referral or quietly shelves it—a tell on whether this AG will challenge the deep state or manage it.
