# A Generation Removed From 9/11 Faces a Complacent America

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, a generation of Coloradans born after 2001 now views that national trauma as history rather than lived memory—a dangerous shift as radical threats persist globally.

CPR News reports that Denver-area students are grappling with 9/11 through education programs, treating the attacks as a historical event rather than a defining moment of American vulnerability. Meanwhile, National Review argues that while the immediate terror has faded, the ideological threat has not: "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still."

Conservatives warn that generational distance breeds strategic complacency. As younger Americans lack direct memory of the attacks' scale and consequences, support for robust counterterrorism measures, border security, and military readiness may erode—precisely when adversaries remain active. The left often frames post-9/11 security policies as overreach; conservatives counter that vigilance, not nostalgia, keeps Americans safe.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. The state hosts military installations, federal agencies, and critical infrastructure. Weakened public resolve on national security could translate to reduced funding, recruitment challenges, and vulnerability.

**Watch whether Colorado schools and civic institutions maintain serious counterterrorism education or allow 9/11 to become a footnote in American history.**
