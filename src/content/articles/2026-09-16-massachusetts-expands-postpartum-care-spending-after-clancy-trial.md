# Massachusetts Governor Expands Postpartum Spending After High-Profile Trial

Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey is using the Lindsay Clancy case to justify a costly expansion of government-run postpartum care—a familiar progressive playbook of turning tragedy into regulatory overreach.

Following Clancy's trial, Healey proposed mandating free home nursing visits for all new parents and tightening mental health screening requirements for postpartum mothers (New York Post). The governor framed the initiative as compassionate intervention, but conservatives question whether government-administered programs represent the most efficient or effective response to postpartum mental health crises.

Republicans argue that while postpartum support matters, Healey's proposal amounts to another unfunded mandate on taxpayers and healthcare providers. Rather than expanding state bureaucracy, conservatives contend that private healthcare providers, community organizations, and family networks are better positioned to deliver personalized care—and that government programs often waste resources on administrative overhead rather than direct patient support.

National Review cautioned against using individual cases to justify sweeping policy changes that could strip moral accountability from serious crimes, noting the broader societal implications of such precedent-setting (National Review).

**What to watch:** Whether other blue-state governors adopt similar postpartum-care mandates, and whether Colorado lawmakers propose comparable spending expansions disguised as mental health initiatives.
