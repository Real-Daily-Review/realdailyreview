# U.S. Military Stretched Thin as Allies Hedge Toward Beijing

America's Pacific deterrent is crumbling. The USS George Washington's redeployment to the Middle East has left the Western Pacific in what Chinese state media openly celebrated as a "zero-U.S.-carrier zone"—a strategic vacuum Beijing will exploit ruthlessly.

The problem runs deeper than one ship. A new Polaris National Security report titled *Stars and Stragglers* (via The Daily Wire) reveals that U.S. allies are fracturing over China policy. The analysis examined 20 countries across technology, economics, military ties, and diplomacy, exposing a growing divide: some allies are actively helping America counter Beijing, while others are "selling out to China" through economic and strategic concessions.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** America cannot simultaneously project power in the Pacific *and* the Middle East without adequate military capacity. Overcommitment weakens deterrence everywhere. When allies see the carrier gap, they lose confidence in U.S. security guarantees and drift toward Beijing's economic orbit—exactly what China wants.

The Washington Examiner (right) and The Daily Wire (right) both flag this as a credibility crisis. Allies need to see American resolve backed by steel, not promises.

**Colorado conservatives should watch whether Congress demands a serious military buildup—or whether Washington continues spreading our forces too thin.**
