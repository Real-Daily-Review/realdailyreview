# Colorado Springs Faces Dual Crises: Election Law Enforcement and Downtown Vandalism

Colorado Springs is under fire on two fronts—a lawsuit over municipal election timing and a wave of vandalism hitting local businesses—raising questions about the city's ability to enforce state law and maintain public order.

The lawsuit, reported by *Complete Colorado*, marks the first enforcement action since Colorado's 2025 voting law took effect. The city held municipal elections in April, apparently violating the state statute's requirements. Republicans argue the law exists to standardize election procedures and prevent confusion; the fact that Colorado Springs ignored it suggests either negligence or deliberate defiance of duly enacted state policy.

Meanwhile, *KDVR Fox 31* reports that Monse's Pupuseria, a popular local establishment, suffered vandalism across two locations—Old Colorado City and downtown—with damages exceeding $80,000. The incident underscores a broader pattern of lawlessness in Colorado's second-largest city.

Conservatives argue these twin failures reveal a city government that cannot—or will not—enforce the law. Whether the election violation stems from incompetence or ideological resistance to state voting standards remains unclear. Either way, Colorado Springs taxpayers are footing the bill for litigation and property damage while city leadership appears adrift.

Watch whether the lawsuit forces Colorado Springs to hold a special election and whether city officials face accountability for the initial violation.
