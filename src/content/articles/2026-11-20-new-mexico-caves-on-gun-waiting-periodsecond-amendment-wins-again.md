# New Mexico Surrenders on Gun Waiting Period—A Win for Second Amendment Rights

New Mexico has thrown in the towel on its unconstitutional gun waiting period after facing a second legal challenge, marking a significant victory for Second Amendment advocates across the West.

The state agreed to halt enforcement of the waiting period requirement, which had faced mounting legal pressure from gun rights organizations. This capitulation matters for Colorado: as neighboring states abandon anti-gun measures after court defeats, it strengthens the legal precedent that waiting periods—which Democrats and gun-control advocates argue reduce impulsive violence—violate constitutional rights to bear arms.

Republicans and Second Amendment groups have long argued that waiting periods infringe on law-abiding citizens' rights without meaningfully reducing crime. The New Mexico settlement validates that position in court, where constitutional protections trump progressive gun-control experiments.

Colorado gun owners should note that similar waiting period challenges could gain momentum in federal courts, particularly if the Supreme Court continues recognizing robust Second Amendment protections. Meanwhile, Colorado's own gun regulations—including its universal background check law and magazine capacity restrictions—remain targets for legal challenges modeled on victories like New Mexico's.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats move to defend their gun laws in court or quietly retreat as legal costs mount and precedent shifts against them.
