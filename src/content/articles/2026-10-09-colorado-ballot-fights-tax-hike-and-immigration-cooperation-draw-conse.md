# Colorado Ballot Amendments Draw Fire Over Tax Hikes and Immigration Policy

Colorado voters face two contentious ballot measures that pit conservative fiscal restraint against progressive spending and immigration activism.

**Amendment 87** would expand the state's tax base, but conservative outlets argue mainstream media is downplaying the full fiscal impact on Colorado taxpayers and businesses. Complete Colorado reports that voters aren't getting the complete picture needed to evaluate the tax change's scope and consequences.

**Amendment 81** would crack open Colorado's sanctuary-law restrictions, allowing local law enforcement greater cooperation with federal immigration authorities. The Denver Post notes the measure has drawn mixed reactions even from law enforcement, while critics warn it could enable racial profiling and undermine trust between immigrant communities and police.

Republicans argue Amendment 87 represents another step toward higher taxes on Coloradans already burdened by inflation and cost-of-living pressures. Conservatives say the state should cut spending, not expand revenue sources. Democrats counter that the tax adjustment is necessary to fund schools and services.

On immigration, conservatives argue Colorado's sanctuary policies handcuff law enforcement and create public safety gaps. The left warns that loosening those restrictions invites federal overreach and discriminatory enforcement against Hispanic residents.
