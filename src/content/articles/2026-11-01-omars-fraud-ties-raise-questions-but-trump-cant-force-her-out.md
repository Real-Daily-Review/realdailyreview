# Omar Saga Exposes Constitutional Limits on Trump's Power—But Questions Remain

Trump's call for Rep. Ilhan Omar's resignation highlights a fundamental constitutional reality: the president cannot unilaterally remove members of Congress. Only the House itself holds that power through expulsion, requiring a two-thirds majority—a high bar that underscores why Omar's alleged ties to fraud conspirators matter more than executive pressure.

According to Fox News, the controversy centers on Omar's connections to individuals involved in a massive fraud scheme. Minnesota Republican State Rep. Kristin Robbins told The Daily Wire that Omar was "thick as thieves with these people," describing relationships with fraudsters that remain largely unexplained. The New York Times has reported additional complications in Omar's background, yet Omar has not publicly addressed the specific allegations.

Republicans argue these connections demand accountability and transparency, particularly given Omar's position on the House Foreign Affairs Committee. Democrats have largely remained silent on the substance, instead framing criticism as partisan attacks.

The constitutional constraint is real: Trump cannot force Omar out. But House Republicans control the chamber and could theoretically pursue expulsion if evidence warrants it—though reaching two-thirds support would prove difficult in a divided Congress.
