# Colorado's Amendment 87 Tax Hike Would Hit High Earners—and the Broncos

Colorado voters face a choice between a flat tax that's worked and a progressive income tax that's failed elsewhere. Amendment 87 would nearly double the tax rate on top earners—from 4.4% to 8.4%—affecting everyone from Denver Broncos players to successful small-business owners across the state.

Proponents argue the tax targets only the wealthy and funds education and infrastructure. But conservatives warn the amendment mirrors failed experiments in states like New Jersey, where progressive taxes have driven out talent and businesses without delivering promised results (Complete Colorado). Colorado's current flat-tax system has fueled economic growth and kept the state competitive for talent and investment.

The Broncos example illustrates the real-world impact: star players and high-income earners could face significantly higher tax bills, potentially influencing where they choose to live and work. That matters for Colorado's economy and tax base.

Republicans argue Amendment 87 represents government overreach dressed up as fairness—a wedge toward ever-higher taxes on productive Coloradans. They point to other states' cautionary tales: once progressive taxes pass, rates climb and middle-class earners eventually get caught in the net.

Watch whether Colorado voters reject this tax hike or embrace the progressive-tax model that's already hollowed out other states' economies.
