# Colorado Must Report Illegal Immigrants on Welfare—Or Lose Federal Funds

Colorado and other states accepting federal welfare dollars now face a hard choice: report illegal immigrants enrolled in assistance programs to federal authorities, or forfeit the funding.

The Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel ruled Wednesday that states must require *all* agencies—not just welfare offices—to report known illegal immigrants to federal authorities as a condition of receiving federal welfare funds. The mandate stems from a 1996 welfare law provision that had gone largely unenforced under the Biden administration.

Republicans argue the ruling closes a loophole that allowed states to shield illegal immigrants from deportation while taxpayers footed the bill for their benefits. Conservatives say Colorado should comply immediately; Democrats and progressive advocates counter that the reporting requirement chills access to services and separates families.

The Colorado angle matters: state lawmakers have resisted federal immigration enforcement, and Denver's sanctuary policies have shielded illegal immigrants from ICE cooperation. If Colorado refuses to comply, the state risks losing federal welfare reimbursements—a significant hit to the budget.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic-controlled legislature and Gov. Jared Polis will defy the DOJ ruling or quietly comply while claiming they're "protecting vulnerable populations."
