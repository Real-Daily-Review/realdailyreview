# Colorado's Razorback Sucker Downlisted—But Environmentalists Cry Foul

Federal wildlife officials have downlisted Colorado's razorback sucker from endangered to threatened status, a move conservatives say reflects genuine recovery efforts but one that has drawn sharp criticism from environmental groups.

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service determined the fish no longer meets the legal threshold for endangered classification, particularly in Colorado River populations. However, as CPR News reports, the fish's survival remains precarious in Arizona rivers, and one national wildlife non-profit told KDVR that the species' outlook is "no better today" than when first declared endangered decades ago.

The reclassification highlights a broader tension: while the fish shows measurable population gains in some areas, skeptics argue the recovery is fragile and dependent on continued federal management and water allocations. Conservatives generally support delisting decisions based on genuine biological recovery rather than perpetual regulatory protection, viewing it as evidence that the Endangered Species Act can work without indefinite government control.

The downlisting also carries implications for Colorado water rights and energy development along the Colorado River—sectors that have chafed under strict environmental restrictions tied to endangered species protections.

Watch whether environmental groups challenge this downlisting in court, potentially tying up Colorado River water policy for years.
