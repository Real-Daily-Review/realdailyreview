# Democratic Maine Candidate Faces Mounting Sexual Assault Allegations as Party Distance Itself

A Democratic U.S. Senate candidate in Maine is imploding under a cascade of serious sexual misconduct allegations—and Democrats are quietly abandoning ship.

Graham Platner faces rape allegations from Jenny Racicot, a Maine resident who told CNN's "The Lead" that he assaulted her in 2021, claiming he "violated multiple layers of consent" (Breitbart). On Tuesday, a second ex-partner came forward to the Washington Examiner with allegations that Platner removed condoms during sex without her consent—a criminal act in many jurisdictions.

The allegations have triggered swift institutional backlash. Democrats and allied organizations have already rescinded their support, according to the Washington Examiner, signaling they view Platner as a political liability heading into the general election.

The timing is brutal for Democrats seeking Senate gains. Maine leans blue, but nominating a candidate facing credible rape allegations and criminal conduct allegations creates an opening Republicans can exploit—and raises uncomfortable questions about Democratic vetting processes and institutional accountability.

Republicans should monitor whether Maine Democrats attempt to replace Platner on the ballot and whether national Democratic figures face pressure to explain why these allegations weren't surfaced during the primary.
