# Colorado Moves to Strip State Recognition from Political Parties

Colorado is taking a rare principled stand: the state should stop granting official recognition and privileges to political parties altogether. According to *Complete Colorado*, state government has no legitimate business singling out Democratic or Republican organizations for special treatment—a move conservatives argue represents genuine separation of state and party.

The distinction matters. When government grants parties official status, it inevitably creates unequal access, preferential treatment, and entanglement between public resources and partisan machinery. Colorado taxpayers shouldn't be subsidizing either major party's infrastructure or legitimacy.

Republicans and conservatives have long complained that government agencies, schools, and bureaucracies lean left in practice. This proposal flips the script: rather than fighting over which party gets favored, eliminate the favoritism entirely. It's a clean, constitutional approach that respects both limited government and political neutrality.

Democrats will likely resist, viewing party recognition as a matter of democratic participation and representation. But the conservative case is straightforward—if Colorado truly believes in separating state from party, it should mean *all* parties.

Watch whether the state legislature takes up this proposal and whether Democrats block it, revealing their real commitment to neutrality.
