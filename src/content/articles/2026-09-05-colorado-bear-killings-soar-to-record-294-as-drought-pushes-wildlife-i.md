# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push starving wildlife into residential areas at unprecedented rates, forcing a brutal choice between public safety and animal welfare.

The surge reflects a cascade of environmental pressures: prolonged drought has decimated natural food sources, while wildfires have destroyed habitat and forced bears downslope into populated zones where they raid garbage, livestock, and bird feeders. Each encounter with humans typically ends in the bear's death, creating a grim arithmetic that wildlife managers say is unavoidable.

Conservatives and property owners argue the state must balance wildlife management with residents' right to protect their homes and families. Some point to land-use policies and forest management decisions—including restrictions on logging and prescribed burns—as contributors to catastrophic wildfire conditions that displace wildlife in the first place. Others question whether Colorado's regulatory approach to development near wildland-urban interfaces adequately protects communities.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that climate change and historical fire suppression are the root causes, and emphasize prevention through bear-resistant trash cans and community education rather than lethal control.
