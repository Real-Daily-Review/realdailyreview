# NASA Discovers Massive Lunar Crater—But Don't Expect Washington to Fund Space Exploration Responsibly

NASA researchers confirmed Wednesday that a spacecraft has detected a massive new crater on the moon, the result of a powerful impact two years ago that initially escaped notice. The crater dwarfs Rome's Colosseum and ranks among the solar system's largest known impact craters formed in recent times, according to 9News Denver and the New York Post.

The discovery underscores NASA's ongoing capability to monitor celestial bodies—but raises a familiar conservative concern: while space exploration captures headlines, Washington continues hemorrhaging taxpayer dollars on bloated bureaucracies and redundant programs. NASA's annual budget exceeds $25 billion, yet accountability remains elusive.

Colorado has a direct stake in space policy. The state hosts significant aerospace and defense contractors, plus the U.S. Space Force Academy in Colorado Springs. Private companies like SpaceX have proven that commercial space ventures can outpace government efficiency at a fraction of the cost. Conservatives argue that NASA should prioritize partnerships with private industry rather than expanding its own bloated infrastructure.

The lunar discovery itself is scientifically valuable—but taxpayers should demand that Washington justify every dollar spent on space programs against measurable returns and operational efficiency.

Watch whether Congress uses this discovery to justify another round of NASA budget increases without demanding real accountability.
