# Trump Taps Internal Talent to Replace Legislative Affairs Chief

President Trump is moving fast to fill the departing Legislative Affairs Director role—and he's looking in-house.

James Braid, who has served as White House liaison to Congress since early 2025, is stepping down in September. The Washington Examiner reports Trump plans to promote from within, with "a lot" of qualified staffers already embedded in the legislative shop ready to step up immediately. Vice President JD Vance announced Braid's departure Thursday.

Braid's job is critical: he ensures lawmakers stay aligned with Trump's agenda and shepherds administration policy through a divided Congress. That continuity matters, especially as Republicans push tax cuts, energy deregulation, and border security measures that will face Democratic resistance.

The move signals Trump's confidence in his existing team and avoids the confirmation delays that come with outside hires. For Colorado conservatives watching the legislative calendar, this stability could mean faster action on energy development, water rights, and Second Amendment protections that depend on White House-Congressional coordination.

Watch whether the new legislative director can maintain momentum on Trump's economic agenda—or whether Democratic obstruction slows the pace of conservative wins on Capitol Hill.
