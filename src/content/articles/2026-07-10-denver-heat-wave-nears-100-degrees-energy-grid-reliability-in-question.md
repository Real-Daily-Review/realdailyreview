# Denver Faces Dangerous Heat Wave as Temperatures Approach 100 Degrees

Colorado's Front Range is bracing for a potentially record-breaking heat wave this weekend, with Denver temperatures expected to near 100 degrees, according to 9News Denver. The Weather Impact Team has warned of possible heat alerts as the unusual surge moves through the region.

The timing raises questions about Colorado's energy grid reliability during peak demand periods. While the heat wave itself is a weather event, conservatives have long warned that Colorado's aggressive push toward renewable energy—driven by state mandates and progressive climate policies—could strain capacity during extreme conditions when residents depend most heavily on reliable power generation.

Extreme heat events are already triggering ecological stress elsewhere. The Daily Caller reported that a recent heat wave caused water temperatures in the Potomac River to hit a record 94 degrees since record-keeping began in 1988, triggering a significant fish kill. Similar concerns could emerge in Colorado's waterways if temperatures spike as forecast.

Coloradans should prepare for the heat with standard precautions: stay hydrated, check on vulnerable neighbors, and avoid unnecessary outdoor exertion during peak afternoon hours. The state's grid operator should be transparent about whether current generation capacity can handle sustained demand without rolling blackouts or emergency measures.

Watch whether Colorado's energy regulators acknowledge grid stress during this event—and whether they use it to justify further renewable mandates or admit the need for reliable baseload power.
