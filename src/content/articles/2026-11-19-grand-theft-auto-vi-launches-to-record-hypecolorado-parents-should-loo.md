# Grand Theft Auto VI Launches to Record-Breaking Hype—But Colorado Parents Should Know What's Inside

A video game launching Thursday could become the biggest entertainment release in history—and it's the kind of title that should concern Colorado parents and conservatives who care about what kids consume.

"Grand Theft Auto VI" arrives after a 13-year wait, with critics calling it a "masterpiece" (Breitbart). The Daily Wire reports the game is expected to generate more revenue in its opening days than "Avatar," "Thriller," or Taylor Swift's Eras Tour combined.

But here's what matters: Grand Theft Auto games are notorious for graphic violence, sexual content, and glorification of criminal behavior. The franchise has faced decades of criticism from parents' groups and conservative watchdogs concerned about desensitizing young players to brutality and vice.

While entertainment industry boosters celebrate the economic windfall, conservatives argue the gaming industry—like Hollywood—routinely pushes boundaries on content that would horrify most families. The game's Vice City setting promises the franchise's trademark lawlessness and depravity.

Colorado parents should review the ESRB rating (Mature, 17+) and consider whether this $70 purchase aligns with their values. Just because a game sells record numbers doesn't mean it belongs in your home.

Watch whether Colorado retailers enforce age restrictions and whether parent advocacy groups mount awareness campaigns about the game's actual content.
