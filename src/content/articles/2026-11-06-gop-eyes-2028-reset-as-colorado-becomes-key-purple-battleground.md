# Colorado Republicans Eye 2028 as National Party Resets After Midterm Losses

Republicans are already mapping the 2028 presidential landscape after disappointing midterm results reshaped the GOP's political calculus. The scale of Republican losses has triggered an early scramble for the party's next standard-bearer, with multiple candidates positioning themselves for a primary fight (Washington Examiner). Meanwhile, Democrats are testing their own bench—though Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez faces significant structural headwinds despite the left's electoral gains this cycle (National Review).

For Colorado conservatives, the 2028 race matters immediately. The state has trended purple in recent cycles, and whichever Republican emerges from the primary will need to rebuild support among independent voters and suburban Republicans who've drifted left. Colorado's electoral map—Denver's Democratic dominance versus Republican strength in the Western Slope and rural counties—will be a proving ground for any GOP nominee's ability to compete in purple states.

Republicans argue the party must nominate a candidate who can reclaim suburban voters without abandoning the base. Democrats counter that their coalition's diversity and turnout advantage make 2028 winnable regardless of Republican infighting.
