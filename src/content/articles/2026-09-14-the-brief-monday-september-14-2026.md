# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, September 14, 2026

Colorado conservatives, buckle up: from a crop insurance fraud scandal on the Eastern Plains to a national reckoning with AI risk and the 25th anniversary of 9/11, today's news demands clear eyes and no illusions about what government — and the people gaming it — can get away with.

---

## Colorado Ranchers Busted in Elaborate Crop Fraud Scheme

Two ranchers on the Colorado and Kansas plains conspired to falsify drought data by tampering with rain gauges, generating millions of dollars in fraudulent federal crop insurance claims. The scheme involved whistleblowers, million-dollar payouts, and a manhunt before it unraveled. This is exactly what conservatives have long warned about: federal subsidy programs large enough to attract serious fraud, with oversight too slow and too thin to catch it quickly. Colorado taxpayers and honest agricultural producers deserve better than a system this easy to game.

## 9/11 at 25: The Threat Didn't Retire When the Headlines Did

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, students who weren't yet born that day are now grappling with what was once a defining national trauma — and analysts warn the underlying threat has not disappeared. Conservatives argue that the bipartisan failure to maintain a serious, sustained counterterrorism posture — replaced by two decades of nation-building misadventures and domestic political distraction — has left America more exposed than the anniversary speeches will admit. Coloradans with family in the military and intelligence communities know the cost of complacency.

## AI CEO Warns of Internet Takeover Risk — Within Months

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has warned that artificial intelligence could, within six to 12 months, be capable of leading a coordinated attack that takes over the entire internet, among other catastrophic risks. Conservatives are right to be skeptical of both the technology's unchecked expansion and of Washington's instinct to respond with a sprawling new regulatory bureaucracy. The real question for Colorado's growing tech sector: who controls the guardrails — Silicon Valley insiders or accountable, elected representatives answerable to the American people?

---

## Denver Police Retesting 13 Cases After DNA Analyst Misconduct

The Denver Police Department is retesting 13 criminal cases tied to the alleged misconduct of former Colorado Bureau of Investigation forensic DNA analyst Yvonne "Missy" Woods. The integrity of the criminal justice system depends entirely on the reliability of the evidence it produces — and when a single bad actor inside a state agency can compromise more than a dozen cases, that is a government accountability failure, plain and simple. Coloradans deserve to know how this was allowed to happen and who else may be affected.

## Republicans Chart a Winning Formula: Growth Over Government

Republican strategists and conservative commentators are coalescing around a straightforward midterm message: hit the gas on American economic growth and slam the brakes on Washington spending. The argument is that voters — including Coloradans squeezed by high costs, energy regulations, and an ever-expanding state government in Denver — respond to a clear contrast between pro-growth conservatism and the left's appetite for taxation and control. Whether Colorado Republicans can execute that message in competitive districts up and down the Front Range will define the state's political trajectory this fall.
