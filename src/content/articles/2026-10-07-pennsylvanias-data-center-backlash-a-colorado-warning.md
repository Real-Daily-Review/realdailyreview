# Data Center Backlash Warns Colorado: Don't Let AI Hype Override Local Concerns

Pennsylvania's sudden turn against data centers—once hailed as economic saviors—offers Colorado a cautionary tale about managing expectations around AI infrastructure booms.

According to the Washington Examiner, data centers that were celebrated just months ago in Pennsylvania have become "public enemy No. 1" as communities grapple with the real-world costs of rapid expansion. Local officials initially pitched the facilities as economic engines, but residents and local leaders are now questioning the trade-offs: energy consumption, water usage, property tax impacts, and land-use changes.

Colorado faces similar pressures. The state's abundant water, relatively cheap electricity, and tech-friendly reputation make it attractive to data center developers eyeing AI expansion. But conservatives and taxpayers should scrutinize the actual terms: Do these projects genuinely benefit local communities, or do they primarily enrich out-of-state corporations while straining Colorado's finite water resources and grid capacity?

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that government shouldn't subsidize data center development through tax breaks or infrastructure spending without rigorous cost-benefit analysis. The Left often frames such facilities as inevitable progress, but Colorado voters deserve transparency about environmental and fiscal trade-offs before deals are locked in.

Watch for whether Colorado's legislature and local governments demand genuine community benefit agreements—not just corporate cheerleading—before approving major data center projects.
