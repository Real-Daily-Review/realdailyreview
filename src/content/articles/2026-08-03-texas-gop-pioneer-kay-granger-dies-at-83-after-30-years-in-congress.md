# Texas Republican Pioneer Kay Granger Dies at 83

A stalwart of the Republican Party and a barrier-breaker in her own right, former Rep. Kay Granger of Texas has passed away at 83, leaving behind nearly three decades of House service and a legacy as Fort Worth's first female mayor.

Granger, who represented Texas in Congress for almost 30 years, carved out a distinctly conservative career in an era when female Republican leadership was rare. She rose through the ranks to become a respected voice on defense and appropriations—areas where her fiscal discipline and hawkish instincts shaped Republican priorities.

Her death marks the loss of a generation of GOP stalwarts who built the modern conservative movement without the identity-politics framing that dominates today's political discourse. Granger's career demonstrated that Republican women could lead on substance—defense budgets, fiscal restraint, constituent service—rather than symbolic gestures.

Colorado Republicans, who have struggled to recruit and retain strong female candidates in statewide races, should study Granger's playbook: focus on policy chops, not demographic messaging, and let results speak louder than rhetoric.

Watch for how national Republicans honor her legacy—and whether they learn from her example of principled, results-driven conservatism.
