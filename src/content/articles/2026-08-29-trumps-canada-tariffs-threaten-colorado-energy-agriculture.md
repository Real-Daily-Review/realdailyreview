# Trump's Tariff Gamble Threatens Colorado Energy and Agriculture

President Trump's escalating trade war—now targeting Canada with 50% tariffs on lumber, agricultural products, and electronics—risks collateral damage to Colorado's economy, even as conservatives debate whether protectionism serves Main Street or Wall Street.

The tariffs hit Colorado where it counts. Lumber prices will spike for Colorado homebuilders already squeezed by housing costs. Agricultural exporters dependent on cross-border trade face retaliatory measures. Energy companies with integrated North American supply chains confront supply-chain chaos and higher input costs.

Republicans are split. Trump's base argues tariffs force China and other bad actors to negotiate fairly and protect American workers from unfair competition. Critics—including many free-market conservatives—warn that broad tariffs function as a hidden tax on consumers and businesses, distort markets, and invite retaliation that hammers exporters in agricultural and energy states like Colorado (Washington Examiner).

The National Review flags the policy as economically counterintuitive, though the outlet stops short of endorsing either position outright.

Colorado taxpayers and business owners should watch whether Trump negotiates these tariffs down before they cascade through supply chains, or whether the administration doubles down on protectionism as a long-term doctrine.
