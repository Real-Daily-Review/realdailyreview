# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are being forced to subsidize primary elections for political parties they may not belong to—a government overreach that conservatives say has no place in a free state.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state grants special recognition and privileges to political parties, effectively making taxpayers foot the bill for partisan machinery. The outlet argues that Colorado government has "no proper business" extending such favors, and questions why citizens are compelled to finance primaries for parties to which they don't even belong.

Conservatives frame this as a fundamental fairness issue: independent voters and those outside the two-party system shouldn't be forced to subsidize Democratic or Republican operations. It's government picking winners and losers in the political marketplace—exactly the kind of crony capitalism conservatives oppose.

The argument cuts deeper than partisan grievance. Separating state from party would mean Colorado stops using taxpayer dollars as a slush fund for party machinery, treating all voters equally regardless of party affiliation. *Complete Colorado* calls such a move "courageous," suggesting political will to challenge entrenched party interests remains rare.

Watch whether Colorado legislators introduce bills to end taxpayer-funded party primaries and whether the state's independent voter population—growing steadily—demands action on this issue.
