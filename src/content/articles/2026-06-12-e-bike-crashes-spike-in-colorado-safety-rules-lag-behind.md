# E-Bike Safety Concerns Mount as Denver-Area Crashes Spike

Colorado's e-bike boom is colliding with reality—literally. A Fort Collins man died in an e-bike crash days after his father's funeral, while a separate incident on Interstate 70 in Denver sent another rider to the hospital after a collision with a motorist (9News Denver).

The I-70 crash closed westbound lanes as Denver Police investigated. One person was transported for treatment, though details on injuries remain limited (KDVR Fox31 Denver, 9News Denver).

These incidents underscore a growing problem conservatives and safety advocates have flagged: e-bikes are increasingly prevalent on Colorado roads, but regulations governing their use, speed limits, and operator licensing lag far behind. Unlike traditional bicycles, many e-bikes can exceed 28 mph—speeds that pose genuine hazards on highways and city streets.

The left has championed e-bikes as a "green transportation" solution, often subsidizing purchases through tax credits and expanding bike lanes at taxpayer expense. But absent clear safety standards, licensing requirements, and enforcement mechanisms, Colorado is essentially conducting a real-time experiment on public roads—with residents' lives at stake.

Conservatives argue that before expanding e-bike infrastructure further, Colorado lawmakers must establish enforceable safety protocols, operator training, and liability standards.

Watch for whether Colorado legislators will prioritize rider safety over progressive transportation ideology in the next session.
