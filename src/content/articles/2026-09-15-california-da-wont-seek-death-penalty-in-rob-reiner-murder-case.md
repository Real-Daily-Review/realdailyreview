# California DA Won't Seek Death Penalty for Nick Reiner in Parents' Murders

Los Angeles County District Attorney Nathan Hochman announced Tuesday that prosecutors will not pursue capital punishment against Nick Reiner, accused of killing his parents—actor and director Rob Reiner and photographer Michele Reiner—in 2025.

According to reports, Hochman cited respect for the Reiners' known opposition to capital punishment in making the decision (9News Denver, The Daily Wire). The move reflects a broader trend in California's criminal justice system: the state has not executed a prisoner since 2006, and Governor Gavin Newsom imposed a moratorium on executions in 2019.

**The conservative angle:** While California's death penalty remains technically available, progressive prosecutors and Democratic leadership have effectively rendered it symbolic. Hochman's decision—framed as deference to victims' families' values—illustrates how prosecutorial discretion, not law, determines whether capital cases proceed. Conservatives argue this undermines accountability for heinous crimes and reflects the left's ideological opposition to capital punishment, regardless of case severity.

Colorado has no death penalty (abolished in 2020 under Democratic control), so this case carries limited direct state impact. However, it underscores how progressive prosecutors nationwide shape criminal justice outcomes independent of legislative intent.

Watch whether Hochman's office secures a conviction and what sentence Reiner ultimately receives—a bellwether for how California's justice system handles high-profile violent crime.
