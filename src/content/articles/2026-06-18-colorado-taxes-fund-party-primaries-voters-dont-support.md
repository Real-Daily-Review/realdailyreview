# Colorado Taxpayers Forced to Fund Primaries for Parties They Don't Support

Colorado forces taxpayers to finance primary elections for political parties—even when those voters belong to no party at all. That's the core complaint from conservatives at Complete Colorado, who argue the state is essentially compelling citizens to subsidize partisan machinery they may actively oppose.

The issue cuts to a fundamental conservative principle: government shouldn't compel citizens to fund causes they reject. Colorado's primary system, funded by general tax revenue, benefits Democratic and Republican party operations regardless of individual voter preference. Independents and third-party voters have no say in how their tax dollars support these contests.

Meanwhile, Colorado political campaigns are deploying deceptive advertising tactics. According to 9News Denver, candidates are using video and audio clips of figures like Pope Francis, Barack Obama, and Ruth Bader Ginsburg in ways that imply endorsement—without actual approval from these figures. The practice blurs the line between legitimate political speech and outright misrepresentation.

Republicans and conservatives argue both issues reflect government overreach and campaign dishonesty: forcing taxpayer funding of partisan primaries violates individual liberty, while fake endorsements mislead voters. Democrats have not publicly countered the primary-funding criticism in available reporting.
