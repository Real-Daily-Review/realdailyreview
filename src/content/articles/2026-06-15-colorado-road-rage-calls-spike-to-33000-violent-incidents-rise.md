# Road Rage Spike Signals Colorado's Growing Civility Crisis

Colorado State Patrol fielded more than 33,000 aggressive driving complaints last year—a troubling surge that reflects deteriorating public behavior on state roads. The spike comes as violent incidents escalate: two suspects were arrested following a road rage shooting in Boulder County this week, underscoring how quickly heated exchanges turn lethal (KDVR).

The numbers demand attention. CSP's aggressive driving report line is logging calls at levels that suggest Colorado drivers are increasingly hostile behind the wheel. While Democrats and progressive cities often blame "gun culture" for such violence, conservatives point to a deeper breakdown in personal responsibility, civility, and respect for others—problems no regulation can fix.

The real issue isn't the availability of firearms; it's the erosion of self-control and community standards. Colorado's growing population, urban congestion, and the stress of daily life may explain part of the trend, but the solution lies in individual accountability, not more government intervention or restrictions on law-abiding gun owners.

Law enforcement is responding appropriately by tracking and prosecuting offenders. Coloradans should demand that courts impose meaningful consequences for road rage violence and that drivers themselves recommit to basic courtesy on the road.

Watch whether CSP's data prompts calls for new traffic regulations or gun restrictions—or whether lawmakers focus instead on prosecuting violent offenders and restoring personal responsibility.
