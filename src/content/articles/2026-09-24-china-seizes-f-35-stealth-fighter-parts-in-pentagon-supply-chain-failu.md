# Pentagon Scrambles After China Obtains Classified F-35 Fighter Jet Parts

The Pentagon is in damage-control mode after China seized critical components from America's most advanced stealth fighter—a security breach that underscores how supply-chain vulnerabilities can hand adversaries access to classified military technology.

According to Bloomberg reporting covered by 9News Denver, a shipment of F-35 parts, including a cockpit canopy and weapons-bay door, was unexpectedly diverted to Hong Kong en route from Australia to the U.S. The Chinese government seized the materials. The New York Post reports the Pentagon is "actively working" to retrieve the components, though the damage may already be done: China now has direct access to highly-classified stealth technology that took American defense contractors years and billions to develop.

This incident raises hard questions conservatives have long posed about outsourcing defense manufacturing and relying on complex global supply chains for weapons systems. Republicans argue the U.S. military-industrial base has grown dangerously dependent on foreign logistics and vulnerable routing—a vulnerability adversaries exploit ruthlessly.

For Colorado, home to major defense contractors and aerospace firms, this breach is a stark reminder that national security demands domestic production resilience and ironclad supply-chain oversight. Cutting corners on security protocols or outsourcing critical defense work to save money is penny-wise and pound-foolish.

Watch whether Congress demands a full accounting of how this diversion occurred and what safeguards will prevent the next breach.
