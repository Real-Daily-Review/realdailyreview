# Federal Border Agents Are Erasing Americans' Phone Privacy Rights

The federal government is claiming the power to search and seize your phone at the airport—and now, wiping your own device to protect your privacy could land you in legal trouble. Customs and Border Protection searched over 55,000 phones, laptops, and tablets at the border last year, a sixfold increase since 2015, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Most searches yield nothing.

Republicans and privacy advocates argue this represents dangerous government overreach. The *Examiner* warns that deleting your own data to prevent warrantless searches now exposes Americans to federal charges—a Catch-22 that strips citizens of Fourth Amendment protections without due process. Conservatives say the border search "exception" has metastasized into a tool for mass surveillance, not security.

Democrats and federal agencies counter that border security requires broad search authority to detect smuggling, contraband, and national security threats. They argue the searches are lawful under existing precedent and necessary.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado travelers and business owners face these searches regularly. Tech workers, energy sector employees, and anyone crossing into or out of the state could have sensitive business data, client lists, or personal information seized without warrant.

Watch for legal challenges to border phone searches—and whether Colorado's congressional delegation will push back on this federal power grab.
