# Progressive Education Ideology Harms Individual Students, Conservative Critics Say

Nikole Hannah-Jones's recent defense of race-focused education policies has drawn sharp criticism from conservatives who argue that progressive schools systematically subordinate individual student achievement to ideological goals. National Review contends that Hannah-Jones and like-minded educators have "long subordinated individual students to the cause of racial justice," while The Federalist's John Daniel Davidson, cited in the New York Post, argues Hannah-Jones "evidently doesn't know what education is, or its purpose."

The dispute centers on whether schools should prioritize individual student outcomes or use education as a vehicle for progressive social engineering. Republicans and conservatives argue that when ideology drives curriculum and discipline decisions, working-class and middle-class families—including minorities seeking quality instruction—suffer most. They contend that lowered academic standards, politicized teaching, and race-conscious policies undermine the fundamental mission of schools: equipping students with skills and knowledge.

Democrats and progressives counter that addressing systemic inequities requires intentional policy shifts, though Hannah-Jones's recent commentary has drawn criticism even from some education reformers concerned about implementation.

Colorado parents and taxpayers should monitor whether similar ideological priorities are influencing curriculum decisions and resource allocation in their own school districts.
