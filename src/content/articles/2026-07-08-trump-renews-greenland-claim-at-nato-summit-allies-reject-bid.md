# Trump Pushes Greenland Claim at NATO Summit, Draws Pushback from Allies

Trump is doubling down on U.S. control of Greenland, arguing at a NATO summit in Turkey that America's security interests in the Arctic outweigh Denmark's sovereignty over the Danish territory.

According to 9News Denver and The Daily Wire, Trump told NATO allies that the U.S. should control Greenland for "global protection" and claimed America essentially inherited responsibility for the island after Nazi Germany occupied Denmark during World War II. Trump contends that Greenland matters far more to Washington's strategic posture than to Copenhagen.

**The conservative case:** Trump's argument rests on geopolitical realism—the Arctic is becoming a critical theater for U.S.-China competition, and Greenland's location and resources make it strategically vital. Supporters say the U.S. already shoulders NATO's defense burden; securing key territory is a logical extension of that responsibility.

**NATO allies' response:** Denmark and other European partners have rejected the overture, reaffirming Greenland's status as a Danish territory. The pushback signals friction within the alliance over Trump's transactional approach to collective security.

**Colorado angle:** While Greenland sits far from the Rockies, Arctic resource competition directly affects U.S. energy independence—a priority for Colorado's oil and gas sector and conservatives skeptical of green-energy mandates.
