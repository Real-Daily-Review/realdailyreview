# Colorado Democrats Offer Voters Little Choice on Policy Direction

Colorado's Democratic gubernatorial race between U.S. Senator Michael Bennet and Attorney General Phil Weiser is shaping up as a contest of personalities rather than principle—a troubling sign for voters seeking real ideological alternatives.

According to the Colorado Sun, the two Democrats "haven't battled over issues like housing and taxes," instead focusing their contrasts on attacks against President Trump. For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this means neither candidate is offering a serious alternative to the progressive regulatory agenda that has dominated state government.

Bennet, leveraging his long tenure as an elected official and government insider (Denver Post), just loaned his campaign $1 million—a sign the race is tighter than expected. The Colorado Sun's Mike Littwin notes that Bennet's campaign is "worried about the race," suggesting internal Democratic anxiety about voter enthusiasm.

Republicans and conservatives should note: while Democrats squabble over Trump messaging, neither candidate appears willing to challenge the state's tax burden, housing-cost crisis driven by regulation, or the energy policies that threaten Colorado's workforce. Weiser's record as attorney general shows aggressive regulatory overreach; Bennet's Senate votes align with the progressive wing on spending and climate mandates.

Watch whether either candidate breaks ranks to address Colorado's affordability crisis—or whether they'll simply offer voters more of the same progressive governance that's made Colorado less affordable for working families.
