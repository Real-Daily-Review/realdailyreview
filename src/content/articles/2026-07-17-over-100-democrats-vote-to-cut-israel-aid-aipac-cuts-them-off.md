# Democratic Israel-Aid Vote Exposes Party's Foreign Policy Fracture—and AIPAC's Reckoning

Over 100 House Democrats voted this week to cut off U.S. aid to Israel, a stunning rebuke that reveals deepening divisions within the party over Middle East policy. The measure failed due to overwhelming Republican support for the ally, but the vote's margin—nearly half the Democratic caucus backing the cut—signals how far the party has drifted from traditional pro-Israel consensus.

The political blowback is swift. According to the Washington Examiner, those 103 Democrats have collectively received nearly $11 million from AIPAC since the 2022 cycle. Just The News reports that AIPAC is now blocking donations to some of these lawmakers, a dramatic shift for the pro-Israel PAC that historically backed Democrats.

**The conservative take:** Republicans argue this vote exposes the left's anti-Israel tilt and shows how progressive activists have captured Democratic foreign policy. The fact that AIPAC—long a bipartisan player—is now cutting off Democratic donors underscores how far the party's base has moved.

For Colorado, this matters: Rep. Joe Neguse (D-CO) and other state Democrats' positions on Israel funding could shape how Colorado Jewish voters and pro-defense conservatives view 2024 races.

Watch whether AIPAC's donor freeze accelerates, forcing Democratic leaders to choose between progressive activists and traditional supporters.
