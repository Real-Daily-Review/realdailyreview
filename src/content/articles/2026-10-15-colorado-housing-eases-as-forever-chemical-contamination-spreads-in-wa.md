# Colorado Housing Slightly More Affordable—But Waterway Contamination Raises New Concerns

Colorado's housing market is finally showing modest relief, with falling home prices and rising wages easing affordability pressure on families—a rare bright spot in a state where homeownership has become increasingly out of reach for middle-class workers.

According to the Colorado Sun, median prices have declined and wage growth is outpacing housing cost increases, though the state remains unaffordable by historical standards. For conservatives, this underscores why excessive zoning restrictions, environmental regulations, and local government red tape drive up construction costs and limit housing supply—the real culprit behind affordability crises.

But Colorado faces a parallel crisis: a new advocacy group report cited by KDVR Fox31 Denver reveals that 63% of Colorado waterways tested contain "forever chemicals"—persistent pollutants that don't break down naturally and accumulate in drinking water supplies.

The contamination raises hard questions about regulatory effectiveness. Conservatives will ask whether existing EPA and state environmental rules are actually protecting Colorado's water resources, or whether the regulatory apparatus is failing taxpayers despite decades of compliance costs imposed on industry and municipalities.

**Watch for:** Whether state regulators move to impose new restrictions on manufacturers and water utilities—and whether those rules will meaningfully reduce contamination or simply shift costs to Colorado families and businesses.
