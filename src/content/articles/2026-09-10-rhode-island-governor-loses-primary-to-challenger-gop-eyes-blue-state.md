# Rhode Island Primary Shocker: Incumbent Governor Loses Renomination

An incumbent Democratic governor just got booted from his own party's primary—a rare and telling sign of voter revolt against establishment politics.

Rhode Island Gov. Dan McKee (D) lost his Democratic primary Wednesday to Helena Buonanno Foulkes, a former CVS executive, according to the Washington Examiner. With 80% of votes counted, Foulkes led 62.5% to 37.5%, making McKee the first sitting governor in years nationwide to lose renomination. The result signals deep dissatisfaction among Democratic voters with McKee's record.

Meanwhile, on the Republican side, the GOP primary remains a toss-up between ex-NFL cheerleader Elaine Pelino and Aaron Guckian, a former gubernatorial aide, per Fox News Politics. Republicans see Rhode Island—historically a blue stronghold—as potentially competitive this cycle.

The McKee loss underscores a broader trend: voters across the political spectrum are punishing incumbents they view as out of touch or ineffective. For Colorado conservatives watching national dynamics, the lesson is clear: even in deep-blue states, voter frustration with incumbent governance can shift terrain rapidly. Whether that energy translates to GOP gains in Rhode Island or signals broader anti-incumbent sentiment heading into 2024 remains to be seen.

Watch whether Foulkes, as the Democratic nominee, can hold the seat or if Republican momentum in a traditionally Democratic state gains real traction.
