# Colorado GOP Faces Reckoning: Caucus System Under Fire After Statewide Losses

Colorado Republicans are confronting an uncomfortable truth: the party's caucus system is costing them elections in a state where unaffiliated voters now comprise 52 percent of the electorate.

A guest editorial in *Complete Colorado* argues bluntly that Republicans cannot win statewide races with party members alone—and the caucus process, which typically attracts ideologically committed activists, may be narrowing the GOP's appeal to the moderate and independent voters Colorado elections now demand. The piece calls for abandoning the caucus model to broaden the party's reach.

The *Denver Post* editorial board, while offering a center-left critique, agrees the Colorado GOP faces structural problems, citing "gross ineptitude of the state GOP and laughable weakness of most of the Republican candidates for statewide office" as contributing factors to recent losses.

The tension is real: caucuses reward candidates who energize the base but can alienate swing voters essential to winning purple Colorado. Republicans must decide whether to prioritize ideological purity or electoral viability—a choice that will define the party's direction heading into 2028.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado Republicans move toward primary elections and whether the state party can recruit and message candidates capable of competing for independent voters without surrendering conservative principles.
