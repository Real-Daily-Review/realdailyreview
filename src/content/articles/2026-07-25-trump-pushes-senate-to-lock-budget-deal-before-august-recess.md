# Senate Budget Showdown Looms as Congress Heads Home for August

Congress is racing the clock on a budget deal before lawmakers scatter for August recess—and President Trump is pushing the Senate to lock in the House's resolution before members vanish until mid-September.

The House adjourned Thursday for a five-week recess, with members heading home to campaign and court voters ahead of November's general elections (Washington Examiner). The Senate departs Aug. 7 and won't return until Sept. 14, leaving a narrow window for budget negotiations.

Trump's pressure on Senate Republicans to adopt the House framework reflects conservative priorities: avoiding last-minute spending deals that typically bloat federal budgets and hand Democrats leverage. Republicans argue that passing a clean budget resolution now prevents the usual September scramble that leads to omnibus spending bills laden with wasteful provisions and regulatory riders.

For Colorado, the stakes include how any budget deal addresses energy policy, water rights, and federal land management—issues where conservative lawmakers have pushed back against Biden-era restrictions on oil and gas development and water allocations affecting the state's agriculture and energy sectors.

The real test comes in September: whether Senate Republicans hold the line on spending restraint or cave to Democratic demands for tax-and-spend provisions.
