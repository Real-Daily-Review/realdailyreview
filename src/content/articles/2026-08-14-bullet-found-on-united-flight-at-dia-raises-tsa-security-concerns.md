# Bullet Found on United Flight at Denver International Airport Raises Security Questions

A live bullet discovered aboard a United Airlines flight at Denver International Airport Thursday forced an evacuation and security sweep, raising fresh concerns about airport screening protocols at one of the nation's busiest hubs.

According to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31, crew members found the round before departure, prompting immediate passenger evacuation and a full security investigation. The incident underscores vulnerabilities in TSA screening procedures that Colorado travelers depend on daily.

While authorities have not disclosed how the bullet bypassed security checkpoints, the discovery highlights a critical gap: either screening equipment failed to detect the round, or it was introduced post-screening—both scenarios demand answers from TSA leadership and DIA management.

For Colorado gun owners and Second Amendment advocates, the incident cuts both ways. Conservatives argue it demonstrates that security theater often fails at its core mission, while simultaneously being used to justify further restrictions on law-abiding citizens' rights. The real question: why are resources spent on invasive screening of grandmothers and businessmen when actual threats slip through?

United and DIA officials have not released details on the investigation's findings or whether criminal charges will follow.

Coloradans should watch whether this incident triggers calls from progressive lawmakers to expand TSA authority or impose new restrictions on firearms—rather than demanding accountability for existing security failures.
