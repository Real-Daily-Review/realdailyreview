# Denver Voters Face Four New Tax and Spending Questions This November

Denver's City Council just handed voters four ballot measures that will expand government control over energy and municipal spending—exactly the kind of regulatory overreach conservatives should scrutinize before Election Day.

Referred Questions 2H through 2K, approved by the council and headed to the November ballot, include a new Xcel Energy franchise agreement and changes to city budgeting procedures (Denver Post). While the council framed these as routine governance matters, conservatives argue that expanding municipal authority over utility franchises and altering budget rules typically means more government spending and less fiscal restraint.

The Xcel franchise question is particularly worth watching. Utility franchise agreements determine rates, service standards, and regulatory requirements—decisions that ripple through Colorado's energy sector and affect both residential ratepayers and businesses. Any expansion of Denver's regulatory grip on Xcel could set a precedent for other Colorado municipalities seeking similar control.

The budgeting changes are equally concerning. Progressive-led cities often use "budget reforms" to lock in spending increases or create new revenue streams. Coloradans should demand transparency: What exactly are these measures changing, and who pays?

**Watch for the full text of all four measures and demand clear answers on whether they increase taxes, expand government authority, or restrict future budget flexibility.**
