# Trump Rejects Iran's Strait of Hormuz Deal—Colorado Energy Markets Watch Closely

Iran's latest pitch to reopen the Strait of Hormuz has hit a wall: Trump flatly rejected it, saying the terms are unacceptable (Just The News). The Iranian Foreign Minister claimed Tehran is ready to negotiate, but Trump isn't biting—and reports suggest he's preparing military options post-midterms (Washington Examiner).

Here's why Colorado should care. The Strait of Hormuz is a global oil chokepoint; disruptions ripple through U.S. energy markets and gas prices at the pump. Colorado's energy sector—oil, gas, and refineries—depends on stable global supply chains. Any escalation with Iran threatens energy costs for Colorado families and businesses already squeezed by inflation.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Iran has a track record of bad-faith negotiations and regional aggression. Trump's skepticism reflects that reality. Democrats and the international left often push appeasement; conservatives argue strength and clear red lines work better. A weak deal that lets Iran off the hook while keeping the Strait closed helps no one—least of all Colorado consumers.

The speedier timeline Iran is now offering (9News) doesn't change the fundamental problem: Tehran's demands likely remain unreasonable, and capitulating would embolden further hostility.

**Watch whether Trump follows through on military options if Iran continues blocking the Strait—and how that affects Colorado gas prices heading into winter.**
