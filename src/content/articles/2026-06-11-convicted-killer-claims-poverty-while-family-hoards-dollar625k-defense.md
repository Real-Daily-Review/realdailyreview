# Convicted Killer Claims Poverty While Family Sits on $625K Defense Fund

A Texas jury's guilty verdict in the Karmelo Anthony murder case has exposed the hollow victimhood narrative his family and celebrity allies are peddling. Anthony, 19, was convicted this week of murdering 17-year-old Austin Metcalf and sentenced to 35 years in prison. Now he's claiming he's broke and can't afford an appeal lawyer—despite his family raising over $625,000 through crowdfunding (Breitbart, Daily Wire).

The hypocrisy is staggering. Anthony's parents have spent their post-verdict interviews casting themselves as victims, insisting their son acted in self-defense and claiming the trial was "predetermined" (Daily Wire). Meanwhile, celebrities like Cardi B are amplifying the grievance, calling the verdict "disgusting" and claiming it's "trying to make an example" rather than serving justice (Daily Wire).

Even former ESPN commentator Jemele Hill broke ranks, offering what the Daily Caller called "rare common sense," suggesting the case should be a cautionary lesson.

This case illustrates a broader problem: the weaponization of identity politics to obscure personal accountability. A young man murdered another young man. A jury heard the evidence and convicted him. That's the system working—not a conspiracy.

Coloradans should watch whether similar defense-fund schemes emerge in state criminal cases, and whether local media will apply the same critical scrutiny to victimhood narratives.
