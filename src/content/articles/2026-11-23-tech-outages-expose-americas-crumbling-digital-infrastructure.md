# Tech Outages Weren't Foreign Attack, But America's Crumbling Infrastructure Needs a Fix

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton ruled out coordinated or state-sponsored cyberattacks behind this week's widespread technology outages, telling ABC News the incidents were "not coordinated and not state-sponsored" (Daily Caller). On CNN, Clayton attributed the disruptions to upgrades within America's "outdated digital infrastructure," framing the problem as part of a broader "period of technological step change" (Breitbart).

While the all-clear on foreign adversaries offers some relief, the real story should alarm Colorado businesses and taxpayers: U.S. digital infrastructure is aging and fragile enough to cause major outages during routine maintenance. That's not reassuring.

The outages exposed how dependent modern commerce, communications, and critical services have become on systems that can't handle basic upgrades without cascading failures. For Colorado's energy sector, financial services, and agriculture—all reliant on robust connectivity—this is a wake-up call about infrastructure vulnerability.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that government should prioritize core infrastructure maintenance over wasteful spending on progressive pet projects. If Washington wants to address genuine national security risks, fixing crumbling digital systems should rank higher than climate virtue-signaling or regulatory bloat.

Taxpayers should watch whether Congress actually funds infrastructure modernization or simply throws more borrowed money at the problem without accountability.
