# Democrats' Dark Money Debate Rule Is All Theater, No Teeth

The Democratic National Committee's new 2028 primary debate rules requiring candidates to denounce dark money from super PACs and 501(c)(4) groups sound tough—until you realize the DNC has zero power to enforce them.

Under the new framework, Democratic hopefuls must pledge to reject outside spending to qualify for 12 debate stages. But as the *Washington Examiner* reports, the rule amounts to political theater: the DNC cannot actually stop dark money from flowing into campaigns, only require candidates to make symbolic denunciations.

Republicans argue this exposes Democratic hypocrisy. For years, progressives have hammered the right over Citizens United and unlimited spending, yet Democrats have eagerly benefited from the same dark money channels they now claim to oppose. The new rule lets candidates have it both ways—rake in unlimited cash while checking a debate-qualification box.

The irony cuts deeper: if a candidate accepts the dark money anyway, what's the DNC's recourse? Debate exclusion? That's a toothless threat when the real money flows through independent groups beyond party control.

For Colorado voters watching 2028 unfold, this signals that neither party is serious about money-in-politics reform—only about appearing so. Watch whether any Democratic candidate actually turns down dark money support or whether this becomes a forgotten pledge by Iowa caucus time.
