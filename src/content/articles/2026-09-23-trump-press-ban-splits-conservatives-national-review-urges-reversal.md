# Trump's Press Ban Draws Fire From Right and Left—But for Different Reasons

Donald Trump's reported ban on certain press outlets has triggered rare bipartisan criticism, though conservatives and progressives are divided on the reasoning.

The Colorado Sun's Mike Littwin argues Trump's "war on the press" mirrors his foreign policy failures, framing media restrictions as fundamentally destructive. Democrats predictably invoke First Amendment concerns and warn of authoritarian overreach.

But National Review—a flagship conservative publication—breaks with Trump on this one, arguing the ban violates "the spirit of a free press" and that Trump would be "better served by confronting reporters directly" rather than silencing them. The magazine's position reflects a core conservative principle: free speech and open debate strengthen rather than weaken conservative arguments.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, press freedom matters. A functioning media—even one hostile to conservative ideas—keeps government accountable and protects property rights, regulatory transparency, and economic liberty. When any administration, left or right, restricts press access, it creates opportunities for hidden spending, regulatory abuse, and backroom dealing that hurt ordinary citizens.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump doubles down on the ban or heeds the National Review's counsel to engage critics openly instead.
