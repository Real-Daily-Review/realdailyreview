# Political Ad Spending Hits Record $11.6B in 2026—But Colorado Conservatives Should Focus on Message, Not Just Money

Political campaigns are about to burn through a staggering $11.6 billion on advertising in the 2026 midterm cycle, according to AdImpact projections—a jaw-dropping sum that underscores how bloated modern elections have become. This marks the first time a midterm election is projected to outspend the preceding presidential cycle, eclipsing the $11.2 billion spent in 2024 and the $8.9 billion record from 2022 (Washington Examiner).

The explosion in ad spending raises a hard question for Colorado Republicans and conservative donors: Is more money actually winning elections, or are campaigns simply wasting resources on ineffective messaging? The sheer volume of cash flooding into political advertising suggests campaigns are betting heavily on saturation—but history shows that speed, message discipline, and authentic voter connection often matter more than raw spending.

For Colorado conservatives, this trend carries real implications. As national money floods into state and local races, homegrown candidates and grassroots movements risk being drowned out by well-funded establishment players. The question isn't whether campaigns will spend more—they will. The question is whether Colorado Republicans will demand that every dollar actually persuades voters rather than simply padding media consultant pockets.

Watch whether Colorado's 2026 races break the national spending trend by proving that disciplined, rapid-response messaging beats bloated ad budgets.
