# Colorado Election Officials Brush Off White House Pressure on Mail-in Voting

Colorado's election bureaucracy is digging in against Trump administration pressure to curtail mail-in ballots ahead of 2026—but conservatives say the state's electoral system still needs fundamental reform.

The White House has threatened to cut funding, filed lawsuits over voter registration data, and pushed executive orders limiting mail-in voting (Denver Post). Colorado election officials, however, say they're unfazed and expect little disruption to the general election.

Republicans counter that Colorado's current system invites problems. The state's ranked-choice primary—which produced the "Griswold fiasco" when Secretary of State Jena Griswold won her party's nomination despite polling behind other candidates—demonstrates why electoral reform is overdue (Complete Colorado). Conservatives argue that allowing a candidate to win with a small plurality in a crowded field undermines genuine voter preference and legitimacy.

The tension reflects a broader conservative critique: Colorado's mail-heavy voting and primary structure prioritize convenience over security and clarity. While election officials project confidence, skeptics worry the state remains vulnerable to both fraud and procedural chaos that erodes public trust.

**Watch for:** Whether the Trump administration follows through on funding threats and whether Colorado Republicans push for primary system reforms in the state legislature.
