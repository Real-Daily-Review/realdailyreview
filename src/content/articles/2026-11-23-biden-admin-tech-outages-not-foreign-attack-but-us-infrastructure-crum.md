# Tech Outages Weren't Foreign Attack, But America's Crumbling Digital Infrastructure Is Still a Problem

The Biden administration's Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton confirmed this week that recent technology outages affecting American systems were not coordinated foreign cyberattacks or state-sponsored operations—a relief for national security, but a damning indictment of Washington's neglect of critical infrastructure.

According to Clayton's statements on ABC News and CNN, the outages stemmed from upgrades to America's "outdated digital infrastructure" as the nation enters what he called "a period of technological step change" (Breitbart). While Clayton was clear the incidents were "not coordinated and not state-sponsored" (Daily Caller), the root cause exposes a deeper vulnerability: decades of underinvestment and bureaucratic mismanagement have left essential systems fragile and aging.

For Colorado, this matters. Our energy grid, water systems, and financial infrastructure depend on reliable digital networks. When federal systems fail due to neglect, states and private operators bear the cost of workarounds and emergency fixes.

**The conservative concern:** Washington has spent trillions on wasteful programs while allowing the backbone of American infrastructure to deteriorate. Rather than bloated new spending initiatives, policymakers should prioritize modernizing critical systems efficiently and removing regulatory barriers that slow private-sector upgrades.

Coloradans should watch whether Congress actually funds infrastructure modernization through competitive bidding and private partnerships—or simply throws more taxpayer money at the same broken federal bureaucracy.
