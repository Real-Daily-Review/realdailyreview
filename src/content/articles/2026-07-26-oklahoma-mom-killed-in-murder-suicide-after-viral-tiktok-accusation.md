# Oklahoma Woman Killed After TikTok Accusation Highlights Dangers of Social Media Justice

A tragic case out of Oklahoma underscores the real-world dangers when social media becomes judge, jury, and executioner—and when accused individuals face public condemnation before due process.

Sara Gilson, an Oklahoma mother, was shot and killed in an apparent murder-suicide by her estranged husband less than two weeks after posting a viral TikTok accusing him of serious crimes, according to 9News Denver. The New York Post reports that 911 dispatchers heard "screaming and crying" and "some sort of loud bang" during the final call.

The case raises uncomfortable questions about social media's role in escalating domestic conflicts. While Gilson's initial post may have been motivated by legitimate safety concerns, the viral amplification created a pressure-cooker environment that appears to have accelerated tragedy rather than prevented it.

**What conservatives should watch:** As social media platforms face renewed scrutiny over their role in real-world harm, expect renewed debate over Section 230 liability protections and whether Big Tech bears responsibility when viral accusations—accurate or not—contribute to violence. Colorado lawmakers should resist the urge to regulate speech while strengthening actual due-process protections for the accused.
