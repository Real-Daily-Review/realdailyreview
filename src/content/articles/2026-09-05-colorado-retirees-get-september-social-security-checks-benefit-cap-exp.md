# Social Security Payments Resume; Colorado Retirees to Receive September Checks

Colorado retirees are about to get a reality check on what Washington considers "adequate" retirement security. The first round of September Social Security payments—capped at $5,181 per month—will hit bank accounts Wednesday, Sept. 9, for beneficiaries born on or before the 10th of any month, according to the Washington Examiner.

The second payment batch follows Sept. 16 for those born between the 11th and the end of the month.

For Colorado's growing retiree population, these payments represent the backbone of retirement income for millions who paid into the system for decades. Yet the benefit cap underscores a hard truth conservatives have long warned about: Social Security's current structure leaves many seniors dependent on government checks that haven't kept pace with real inflation and cost-of-living increases, particularly in Colorado's rising housing markets.

Republicans and conservatives argue the program's long-term solvency crisis demands structural reform—not higher payroll taxes on working Coloradans, but means-testing, gradual retirement-age adjustments, and market-based personal accounts that would let younger workers build real wealth.

Democrats resist any overhaul, framing Social Security as untouchable.

Watch whether Congress addresses the program's 2033 trust-fund depletion before the election cycle makes reform politically impossible.
