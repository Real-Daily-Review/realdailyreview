# Mile High United Way's Turkey Trot Draws Record Crowd—But Don't Expect Government to Stay Out of Charity

Colorado's Mile High United Way shattered its own attendance and fundraising records at Thursday's 53rd Turkey Trot in Washington Park, drawing thousands of participants who showed up to support the nonprofit's mission before Thanksgiving.

The event, which has become a Denver institution, demonstrates what private charitable giving and community volunteerism can accomplish without government mandates or taxpayer subsidies. Mile High United Way relies on individual donations and corporate sponsorships—not state grants or public funding—to fuel its operations and outreach programs.

That's the model conservatives prefer: citizens and businesses freely choosing to support causes they believe in, rather than government taking tax dollars and deciding where charity goes. When nonprofits thrive on voluntary giving, they remain accountable to donors and the community, not bureaucrats.

The record turnout reflects genuine grassroots enthusiasm. No government coercion required.

As progressive politicians continue pushing for expanded social programs and higher taxes to fund government-run initiatives, Colorado taxpayers should remember that private charity—like the Turkey Trot—often delivers results more efficiently and responsibly than bloated government agencies ever could.

Watch whether Denver or state officials attempt to regulate, tax, or co-opt future Turkey Trot events under the guise of "equity" or "public health" mandates.
