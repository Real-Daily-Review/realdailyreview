# Aurora Road Rage Shooting Highlights Colorado's Growing Street Violence Problem

Colorado highways are becoming increasingly dangerous, with another road rage shooting erupting on I-225 in Aurora early Monday morning, leaving one man injured. Police are searching for a red or burgundy Mercedes Benz involved in the reckless driving incident around 1:30 a.m. (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The shooting underscores a troubling trend of violent confrontations on Denver-metro roads. Just days earlier, a separate shooting in Golden left an officer injured and a suspect dead (KDVR Fox31), signaling that Colorado's public safety challenges extend well beyond isolated incidents.

**The conservative concern:** While progressives push gun-control measures targeting law-abiding citizens, actual street violence—often involving criminals with illegal weapons—continues unchecked. Colorado Democrats have pursued strict firearm regulations and magazine limits, yet criminals ignore those laws entirely. The real problem isn't Second Amendment rights; it's inadequate prosecution, soft-on-crime policies, and failure to enforce existing statutes against violent offenders.

Coloradans deserve safe highways and neighborhoods. That requires holding criminals accountable—not restricting the rights of responsible gun owners who carry for self-defense on increasingly dangerous roads.

**Watch:** Whether Aurora and Golden police make arrests and what charges prosecutors pursue—or if catch-and-release policies again undermine public safety.
