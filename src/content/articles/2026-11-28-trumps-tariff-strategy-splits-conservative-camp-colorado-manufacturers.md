# Trump's Tariff Gamble Divides Right; Colorado Manufacturers Brace for Impact

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire even from conservative allies, with critics warning the approach risks economic damage without delivering promised results. The Washington Examiner's opinion section notes Trump's approval ratings slid in April 2025 following his "Liberation Day" tariff announcement, which it describes as "chaotic" with "absurd" justifications.

National Review argues Trump should adopt Reagan's Cold War playbook—building strength before negotiating down—suggesting the current tariff approach lacks strategic coherence. The tension reflects a broader conservative split: Trump's base supports tariffs as leverage against China and allies seen as free-riding on U.S. security; fiscal conservatives and business-minded Republicans worry tariffs function as hidden taxes on American consumers and manufacturers.

**Colorado angle:** The state's manufacturing sector, from aerospace suppliers to food processors, faces real exposure. Higher input costs ripple through supply chains, potentially raising prices for Colorado consumers and pressuring employers already navigating labor-market tightness. Colorado's energy sector—dependent on equipment imports and export markets—could also face headwinds if retaliatory tariffs materialize.

Republicans and conservatives should watch whether Trump's tariff revenue claims materialize or whether the policy simply shifts costs to Main Street businesses and working families—a distinction that will shape 2026 midterm messaging in purple Colorado.
