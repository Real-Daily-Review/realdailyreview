# Colorado Congressional Races Heat Up as Candidates Burn Through Cash

Colorado's 2026 primary battles are shaping up as expensive slugfests, with candidates across the state raising and spending heavily to secure their party nominations before the general election (CPR News). In tight races, the money chase has become the primary story—and a warning sign for taxpayers watching how political insiders operate.

Republicans argue that the explosion in campaign spending reflects the stakes of reclaiming seats lost to progressive overreach on energy policy, gun rights, and inflation-driven economic pain. Conservative candidates are competing fiercely to prove they can deliver on border security, energy independence, and tax relief—core issues driving Colorado voters rightward.

Democrats counter that robust fundraising signals strong grassroots enthusiasm for their candidates' vision on climate, healthcare, and gun control. The left frames high spending as evidence of engaged voters willing to finance their preferred candidates.

What's clear: whoever wins these primaries will face general elections where Colorado's suburban swing voters—particularly in districts around Denver and the Front Range—will decide whether Republicans can flip seats or Democrats hold ground. The primary spending wars are just the opening act.

**Watch for:** which candidates' cash advantages translate into actual primary victories, and whether Colorado Republicans can convert fundraising momentum into November wins.
