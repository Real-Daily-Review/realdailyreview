# Colorado Springs Faces Lawsuit Over April Municipal Elections

Colorado Springs is being sued for scheduling municipal elections in April—the first enforcement action since a 2025 voting law took effect, according to *Complete Colorado*. The lawsuit challenges the city's decision to hold elections outside the traditional November general election cycle.

Republicans and election-integrity advocates argue that consolidating municipal elections into November reduces voter confusion, cuts administrative costs, and strengthens turnout by aligning local races with statewide ballots. Holding separate April elections, they contend, fragments the electoral calendar and burdens taxpayers with duplicate election infrastructure.

Democrats and progressive groups counter that municipal elections should remain flexible to serve local needs and that April voting allows communities to focus on local issues without national politics overshadowing races.

The case marks the first real test of Colorado's 2025 voting law and signals that enforcement will be contested. Colorado Springs—the state's second-largest city and a Republican stronghold—now faces legal pressure to realign its election schedule.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether this lawsuit succeeds and whether other Colorado municipalities face similar challenges, potentially forcing costly election-calendar overhauls across the state.
