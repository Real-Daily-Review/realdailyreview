# Colorado Wildfire Season Spirals: Two Mega-Fires Now Among State's Largest on Record

Colorado is facing a catastrophic wildfire season, with two fires already ranking among the state's largest and most destructive on record. The Aspen Acres Fire in southern Colorado has burned nearly 99,000 acres and destroyed over 800 structures, making it one of the top five most destructive fires in state history by homes lost (9News). Meanwhile, the Ferris Fire has scorched more than 64,000 acres in southwest Colorado, becoming the second fire this summer to crack the state's all-time top 10 list (9News).

The Aspen Acres Fire remains 36% contained and continues threatening the town of Rye, with a new command team recently taking over operations (Colorado Sun). The fire has prompted widespread evacuations, though some roads have reopened and evacuation orders have been lifted in certain areas (CPR News).

These twin disasters underscore a critical question conservatives and Colorado taxpayers must confront: How much of this catastrophe stems from decades of forest mismanagement, fuel buildup, and resistance to aggressive timber harvesting and controlled burns? Environmental regulations have often blocked the aggressive land management that could reduce wildfire risk. As recovery costs mount and families lose homes, Coloradans should demand accountability for whether progressive environmental policies have worsened fire conditions.

Watch for how state and federal officials respond to calls for streamlined forest management authority and whether they'll finally prioritize fuel reduction over regulatory gridlock.
