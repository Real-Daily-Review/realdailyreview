# Federal Power Grab Threatens Private 401(k)s, Conservative Critics Warn

Congress is quietly sneaking government control over private retirement accounts into the National Defense Authorization Act—a move that should alarm every Colorado saver and investor.

Section 815 of the Senate's NDAA would require certain companies doing business with the federal government to submit to Washington's oversight of how they deploy their own earnings, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The provision blurs a critical line: while taxpayers rightly expect accountability for federal dollars, the government has no business dictating how private firms spend private capital—or how those decisions affect the 401(k)s of Colorado workers invested in those companies.

**Conservatives argue** this represents regulatory overreach that will chill business investment, reduce returns for retirement savers, and hand bureaucrats veto power over corporate decisions. The precedent is dangerous: once Congress claims authority over "defense contractor" spending, mission creep inevitably follows.

Democrats haven't articulated a clear public defense of Section 815, but the underlying logic—that federal leverage justifies federal control—is classic progressive paternalism: Washington knows better than markets and investors.

For Colorado's energy sector, defense contractors, and aerospace firms, this could mean federal meddling in capital allocation decisions that directly affect shareholder returns and employee retirement security.

Watch whether Republican senators kill Section 815 before the NDAA passes, or whether this Trojan horse makes it into law.
