# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, September 11, 2026

Twenty-five years after the towers fell, America is again being tested — and today, Colorado conservatives pause to honor both the heroes of 9/11 and a man struck down for simply speaking his mind on a college campus. It's a heavy Friday, but the news doesn't stop, and neither do we.

---

## One Year Since Charlie Kirk Was Murdered on Camera

One year ago today, Turning Point USA founder Charlie Kirk was shot and killed during a live public policy debate at a university — murdered in front of the country for the act of speaking. Today, Turning Point USA unveiled an 8-foot-tall statue in his honor at their Phoenix headquarters, and memorial events are being held nationwide. Republicans and conservatives argue Kirk's murder was the most violent proof yet of what happens when political hatred is allowed to fester unchecked. Colorado conservatives who valued his voice should be at those memorials in spirit, if not in person.

## Yampa Valley Transit Authority Puts Sales Tax on November Ballot

The Yampa Valley transit authority — still in its early operational phase — is asking voters to approve a sales tax increase this November to fund basic operations. Republicans argue that fledgling government agencies have a habit of using ballot measures to lock in permanent spending before taxpayers fully understand what they're funding. Northwest Colorado voters, many of them ranchers and small business owners already squeezed by inflation, should read the fine print carefully before handing a new bureaucracy a dedicated revenue stream.

## Trump's 'Grand Conspiracy' Prosecutor Resigns

Joseph diGenova, the Reagan-era prosecutor brought in to investigate the alleged "Grand Conspiracy" surrounding the Trump-Russia investigations, has resigned from his Justice Department role, citing "differences of opinion." Conservatives have long argued that the original Trump-Russia probe was a politically weaponized operation, and diGenova's departure raises legitimate questions about whether the full truth will ever be pursued with the seriousness it deserves. Colorado voters who want accountability in federal law enforcement should be watching this one closely.

## Undercover Video Catches Dem Senate Nominee Cozying Up to Socialists

Undercover video has surfaced showing North Carolina Democratic Senate nominee Roy Cooper — the former governor — signaling openness to working alongside Democratic Socialists of America, reportedly saying they've "got to be together," even as he publicly declines to repudiate their influence. Democrats counter that Cooper is simply building a coalition. Conservatives say the video shows exactly what the modern Democratic Party is: a party that talks moderate in public and governs hard left. Colorado Democrats running statewide in 2026 should be asked the same question on camera.

## Houthis Seize Red Sea Port, Threatening Global Energy Supplies

Iran-backed Houthi forces have reportedly taken over a key Red Sea port, further tightening their grip on one of the world's most critical shipping lanes — a group that has already conducted strikes on Israel and clashed with the U.S. Navy. Conservatives argue that emboldened Iran-backed proxies are a direct consequence of weak American deterrence. For Colorado's energy sector, any sustained disruption to global oil and gas supply chains means price pressure at the pump and on the oilfield — a reminder that energy security abroad is economic security at home.
