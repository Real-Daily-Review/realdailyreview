# School Choice: The Real Accountability Tool Government Bureaucrats Don't Want

Colorado parents are tired of waiting years for test scores and compliance reports to tell them their child is stuck in a failing school—and conservatives argue that school choice, not more government oversight, is the answer.

The Washington Examiner makes the case that traditional accountability measures—standardized tests, filing requirements, compliance audits—are fundamentally broken. They take years to surface problems, leaving kids trapped in underperforming environments while bureaucrats shuffle paperwork.

Republicans counter that the real accountability tool is parental choice: when parents can move their children to better schools—whether charter, private, or public alternatives—schools must compete or lose enrollment and funding. That creates immediate, market-driven pressure to improve.

For Colorado, this matters. The state's public school system faces persistent achievement gaps and budget pressures. Expanding education savings accounts, charter school access, and tuition tax credits would let Colorado families vote with their feet rather than wait for the next state audit cycle.

Democrats typically oppose school choice initiatives, arguing they drain resources from traditional public schools and benefit wealthy families. Conservatives counter that public education monopolies have had decades to improve and haven't—and that every dollar should follow the child, not the institution.

Watch for Colorado legislative pushback on any school choice expansion bills this session.
