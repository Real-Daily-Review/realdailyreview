# Trump, Fetterman Push AI Development Over Caution—China Threat Looms

The Trump administration and key Democrats are aligning on aggressive AI development, rejecting what they call excessive regulation that could hand technological dominance to China.

Sen. John Fetterman (D-Pa.) rejected "AI doomsdaying" and backed Trump's push for unfettered AI progress, warning that U.S. "overreaction" risks ceding leadership to Beijing (Fox News). Trump himself declared Sunday that America leads the AI race "by a lot" and framed rapid development as essential to national competitiveness, calling it "a renaissance" (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case is straightforward:** excessive regulation stifles innovation and hands geopolitical advantage to authoritarian competitors. Republicans argue that American free-market competition—not government guardrails—will drive both progress and safety. Overregulation, they contend, is the real threat.

**The left's concern,** though muted here, centers on AI safety, bias, and labor displacement—issues progressives typically want addressed through regulatory frameworks before deployment accelerates.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts growing AI and tech sectors in Denver and Boulder; aggressive federal deregulation could attract investment and talent, while heavy-handed rules could drive companies and jobs elsewhere. Colorado's energy sector also intersects with AI's massive power demands—a potential economic opportunity if state policymakers don't strangle development with climate mandates.
