# Colorado's Water Crisis: Front Range Growth Comes at Rural's Expense

Colorado's water transfer scheme is hollowing out the Arkansas River Valley to slake Front Range thirst—and conservatives should ask whether permanent farmland loss is worth the urban sprawl trade-off.

A new CPR News resource documents the mounting toll: agricultural land drying up as Denver, Colorado Springs, and other cities siphon water rights downstream. The transfers fuel suburban expansion, but at what cost to rural Colorado's economy, food production, and way of life?

Water advocates are raising the hard question: "As we permanently dry up more farmland to fuel urban growth, are we building a better Colorado for our grandchildren or trading away the very things that make Colorado worth calling home?" (CPR News). That's not left-wing nostalgia—it's economic reality. Family farms and ranches that built Colorado's character are being sacrificed to government-enabled water grabs that benefit urban developers and municipal bureaucrats.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that water policy divorced from property rights and market principles breeds waste and injustice. When cities use regulatory power to redirect water from productive agricultural use to sprawl, taxpayers foot the bill through higher water costs and lost rural tax base.

The real question: Will Colorado's legislature protect water rights and rural communities, or continue rubber-stamping transfers that enrich urban elites while erasing Colorado's agricultural heritage?

Watch for legislative pushback on water transfers in the 2027 session—and whether rural Republicans demand a seat at the table.
