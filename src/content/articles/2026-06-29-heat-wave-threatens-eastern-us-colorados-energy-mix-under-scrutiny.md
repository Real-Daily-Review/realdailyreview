# Eastern U.S. Braces for Dangerous Heat Wave as Colorado Dodges Worst

A major heat wave is set to pummel the central and eastern United States this week, with the National Weather Service warning of dangerously high temperatures and humidity ahead of the July Fourth holiday (KDVR, 9News). Parts of the country, particularly Phoenix, are already experiencing extreme conditions.

While Colorado's Front Range typically escapes the worst of such events due to higher elevation and lower humidity, the state's energy grid could face strain if the heat spreads westward. Conservative policymakers have long argued that Colorado's energy independence—bolstered by responsible natural gas and coal development—provides resilience during peak demand periods. Progressive restrictions on fossil fuel generation, however, have narrowed the state's power supply flexibility, a concern energy analysts say could complicate grid stability during extended heat events.

The heat wave underscores why Colorado's diverse energy portfolio matters. As the Left pushes for aggressive renewable-only mandates, Republicans and industry advocates warn that over-reliance on wind and solar without adequate baseload capacity leaves the state vulnerable when weather extremes hit.

Coloradans should monitor whether state regulators use this event to justify further restrictions on natural gas plants—or whether they acknowledge the practical need for reliable, dispatchable power sources.
