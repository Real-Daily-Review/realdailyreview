# Federal Judge Blocks New York's Anti-ICE Masking Law—A Win for Federal Authority

A federal judge has temporarily blocked New York's attempt to strip ICE agents of operational flexibility by banning masks and requiring visible identification during enforcement actions.

Judge Mae D'Agostino of the Northern District of New York granted the Department of Justice's request for a preliminary injunction, finding the federal government is likely to succeed at trial (Just The News). The ruling undercuts New York's effort to hamstring immigration enforcement through state-level restrictions—a tactic progressives have deployed across sanctuary jurisdictions.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Federal immigration enforcement is a constitutional federal power. States cannot use identification and masking requirements to obstruct lawful ICE operations or tip off targets. New York's law was designed precisely to chill enforcement, making agents' jobs harder and more dangerous.

Colorado hasn't pursued similar legislation, but the ruling matters here. If sanctuary-minded Democrats in Denver or Boulder attempt comparable restrictions on federal agents—whether ICE, ATF, or DEA—this precedent establishes that federal authority trumps state obstruction.

Republicans argue that states attempting to block federal law enforcement undermine the rule of law and border security. Democrats counter that such measures protect immigrant communities from what they characterize as overreach.
