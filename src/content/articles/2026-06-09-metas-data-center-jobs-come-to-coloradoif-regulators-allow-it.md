# Meta's Data Center Push Offers Colorado Jobs—If State Gets Out of the Way

Meta is launching a worker training program to fill positions at its expanding data center operations, a potential economic bright spot for Colorado—but only if state regulators don't strangle the projects with red tape and environmental delays.

The tech giant's move (reported by 9News Denver) reflects the industry-wide scramble to staff massive AI infrastructure buildouts. Colorado has positioned itself as a data center hub, and Meta's commitment could mean thousands of good-paying jobs for workers without four-year degrees.

Here's the catch: Colorado's permitting process for large industrial projects has become a minefield of progressive environmental reviews and community opposition. While Meta invests in training, state agencies and local governments often drag their feet on approvals, citing climate concerns and water usage—legitimate questions, but ones that shouldn't become excuses for indefinite delays.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Colorado's regulatory burden is already driving tech investment to friendlier states. If the state wants these jobs and the tax revenue they generate, it needs to streamline permitting, respect property rights, and stop treating data centers like pariahs.

**Watch whether Colorado's Democrat-controlled legislature and governor's office will prioritize job creation over environmental activism—or whether they'll regulate Meta's expansion into oblivion.**
