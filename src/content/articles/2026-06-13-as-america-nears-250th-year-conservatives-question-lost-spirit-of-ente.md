# America's 250th Birthday Raises Questions About National Direction

As America approaches its 250th anniversary in 2026, conservatives are asking whether the nation still embodies the principles of limited government and free enterprise that built it. The sources raise a deeper concern: has America lost the entrepreneurial spirit and celebration of achievement that once defined the nation.

The Daily Wire notes that Elon Musk's upcoming SpaceX IPO—potentially the largest in history and widely available to retail investors—represents a rare moment when ordinary Americans can participate in genuine innovation and wealth creation. Yet conservatives argue this opportunity highlights a troubling reality: government increasingly penalizes success through taxation, regulation, and anti-business policies that discourage the very entrepreneurship that built American greatness.

The Washington Examiner recalls the 1876 Centennial Exhibition in Philadelphia, where nearly 10 million visitors celebrated a century of American achievement in agriculture, machinery, mining, and invention. That fair showcased a nation confident in its future and proud of its accomplishments—a stark contrast to today's progressive narrative that treats American success with suspicion.

National Review raises a philosophical question: when exactly did America begin? The answer matters because it frames how we understand our founding principles and national identity.
