# School Districts, Media Soft-Pedal 9/11 Truth on 25th Anniversary

A quarter-century after nearly 3,000 Americans died in terrorist attacks, some school districts and news outlets are downplaying or reframing how they teach the attacks—a troubling sign that institutional denialism is eroding historical memory among Gen Z, according to *Just The News*.

The choices by educators and media organizations about language and framing matter enormously: a generation with no direct memory of 9/11 is learning a sanitized version of who attacked America and why. This isn't neutral pedagogy—it's ideological gatekeeping that weakens civic understanding of terrorism and national security threats.

Meanwhile, the U.S. Mint honored the anniversary more straightforwardly, unveiling the Semiquincentennial Enduring Liberty Half Dollar this week. The Mint presented 2,983 half-dollar coins to the National September 11 Memorial &amp; Museum—one for each victim—a tangible reminder of the human cost of that day.

**What conservatives and taxpayers should watch:** whether Colorado school districts are similarly downplaying 9/11's origins and lessons, and whether parents will demand transparency about how their kids' schools teach this foundational American tragedy.
