# Trump Predicts Oil Price Drop After Midterms—But Colorado Voters Paying $4.22 a Gallon Now

Former President Trump says oil prices will tumble "right after the election," but Colorado drivers facing $4.22-per-gallon gas aren't waiting for November relief (Just The News). Trump's prediction amounts to an admission that energy markets—and voter wallets—will remain squeezed through the midterms, a politically awkward reality for Republicans hoping to capitalize on economic discontent.

The price spike stems partly from ongoing Iran tensions destabilizing global oil supplies. While Trump argues his energy policies would unlock domestic production and lower prices long-term, the current squeeze exposes the lag between policy and pump relief. Colorado's oil and gas workers and rural families dependent on affordable fuel have little comfort in post-election promises.

Republicans counter that Biden-era restrictions on domestic drilling and refining capacity created this vulnerability. They argue aggressive permitting for federal lands and pipeline projects—critical to Colorado's energy economy—would have prevented the current crunch. Democrats maintain that renewable energy investment and reduced oil dependence offer the real solution.

For Colorado conservatives, the message is clear: energy independence requires *now*, not November.

**Watch whether Trump or Republican candidates propose specific, immediate steps to increase domestic production before voters head to the polls.**
