# Colorado Should Stop Playing Favorites With Political Parties

Colorado government is quietly granting special recognition and privileges to political parties—a practice that betrays the state's obligation to remain neutral, according to conservative critics.

Complete Colorado argues that state government has "no proper business" extending such favors, calling the practice a departure from the principle that public institutions should serve all citizens equally, regardless of party affiliation. The concern reflects a broader conservative principle: government should not pick winners and losers, whether in business, speech, or politics.

This matters to Colorado taxpayers because state resources—funding, access, official recognition—should never be weaponized to advantage one party over another. When government blurs the line between neutral arbiter and partisan player, it corrodes public trust and invites the opposite party to do the same once power shifts.

The issue also touches on Colorado's identity as a swing state where both parties compete fiercely. Allowing government to tilt the playing field, even symbolically, sets a dangerous precedent that could escalate partisan tensions during elections.

Conservatives should watch whether Democratic-controlled state agencies continue expanding party-specific programs or whether lawmakers move to codify genuine government neutrality.
