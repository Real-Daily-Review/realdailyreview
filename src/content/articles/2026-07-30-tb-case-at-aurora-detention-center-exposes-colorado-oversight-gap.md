# TB Outbreak at Aurora Detention Center Raises Questions About State Law Compliance

A tuberculosis case confirmed at the GEO Group's immigration detention facility in Aurora has reignited scrutiny over whether Colorado's detention standards are being enforced—and whether state officials are serious about oversight.

CPR News reports that weeks after the TB case was confirmed, answers remain scarce about the facility's response and whether it complied with state law requirements. The outbreak underscores a persistent conservative concern: federal immigration detention centers operating in Colorado often operate with minimal transparency and accountability to state regulators.

Republicans argue that lax facility management and unclear reporting protocols endanger both detainees and surrounding communities. The incident also fuels broader GOP frustration with what conservatives see as insufficient state-level enforcement of detention standards—a gap that becomes especially glaring when public health is at stake.

Democrats and immigration advocates typically emphasize humane conditions and due process, but the TB case shifts focus to basic safety compliance that transcends ideology.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state health officials demand full transparency from GEO and whether Governor Polis's administration tightens inspection protocols or continues hands-off management of federal detention operations within state borders.
