# U.S. Pauses $14 Billion Taiwan Arms Sale to Prioritize Iran Conflict

The Trump administration is pausing a $14 billion weapons sale to Taiwan to ensure munitions availability for its war with Iran, Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao said Thursday during a Senate hearing.

Cao told the Senate Appropriations Defense Subcommittee that the U.S. maintains "plenty" of missiles and interceptors despite growing concerns about dwindling American munitions stockpiles, according to The Hill. The pause reflects the administration's prioritization of the Iran conflict over the Taiwan sale, though Cao did not specify a timeline for resuming the transaction.

The decision comes as President Trump has sent mixed signals regarding the sale, according to Al Jazeera. The move underscores tensions between two competing U.S. security commitments: supporting Taiwan, a key ally in the Indo-Pacific, and sustaining military operations in the Middle East.

Taiwan has long relied on U.S. military support as it faces pressure from China. The $14 billion sale would have included advanced weaponry critical to the island's defense capabilities.

Watch for whether the administration provides a timeline for resuming the Taiwan sale and how Congress responds to concerns about U.S. munitions depletion across multiple theaters.
