# Supreme Court's Mail-In Ballot Ruling Sparks Conservative Backlash Over Election Integrity

Colorado Republicans are joining nationwide GOP outcry over the Supreme Court's decision to allow states counting mail-in ballots received after Election Day—a ruling conservatives warn invites voter fraud and undermines election confidence.

Justice Samuel Alito warned the decision creates "severe" risks to election integrity, according to *Fox News*. Republicans, outraged by the ruling, are mobilizing around the SAVE America Act to enforce stricter ballot-counting deadlines. Missouri Senator Eric Schmitt called the decision "shockingly wrong," per *The Daily Wire*.

The ruling matters directly to Colorado, a mail-in ballot state where election security remains a flashpoint. Republicans argue that counting late-arriving ballots—even if postmarked on time—creates opportunities for fraud and delays final results, eroding public trust in elections. Democrats counter that mail delays are inevitable and that rejecting ballots due to postal delays disenfranchises legitimate voters.

Colorado's Republican Party has long pushed for tighter ballot-handling procedures. The state's Democratic-controlled legislature has resisted, citing accessibility concerns. This Supreme Court decision effectively sides with the Democratic position, allowing Colorado and other states to count ballots arriving days after Election Day.
