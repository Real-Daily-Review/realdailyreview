# National Debt Hits $40 Trillion: Colorado Taxpayers Face the Bill

America's national debt has crossed $40 trillion—a fiscal catastrophe that will eventually land on Colorado families and businesses through inflation, higher taxes, or both.

The milestone reflects decades of bipartisan spending excess. The War on Terror alone has cost nearly $10 trillion over 25 years (Just The News), while entitlements, defense, and discretionary programs continue their unchecked climb. National Review and the Washington Examiner warn the trajectory is unsustainable: at current rates, interest payments alone will consume an ever-larger share of the federal budget, crowding out productive investment.

**What conservatives argue:** Republicans say Washington has abandoned fiscal discipline. Every dollar borrowed today is a dollar Colorado taxpayers—and their children—will repay through debasement of the currency or explicit tax hikes. The Left's answer is always more spending; the right demands spending cuts, entitlement reform, and a return to constitutional limits on federal authority.

Democrats counter that the debt reflects necessary investments in infrastructure and social programs, and that the real problem is insufficient tax revenue from the wealthy.

**The Colorado angle:** As the federal government tightens, expect pressure to shift costs to states and individuals. Colorado's energy sector, agriculture, and small businesses will feel the squeeze first—either through inflation eroding purchasing power or through federal mandates that bypass the ballot box.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation demands a serious debt-reduction plan or continues enabling Washington's spending addiction.
