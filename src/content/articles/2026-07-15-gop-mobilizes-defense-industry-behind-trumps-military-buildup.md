# Defense Industry Mobilizes Behind Trump's Strength Agenda

Sen. Dave McCormick (R-PA) convened hundreds of defense contractors at Pennsylvania's Defense and Innovation Summit this week, signaling Republican commitment to rebuilding American military capability under the Trump administration's "peace through strength" doctrine. The gathering at the Army War College near Harrisburg brought together private defense firms seeking partnership opportunities with the Department of Defense—a direct pivot from years of regulatory uncertainty and budget constraints under the Biden administration.

McCormick framed the summit as essential to reinforcing Trump's defense posture amid global instability. Republicans argue that robust defense spending and streamlined public-private partnerships strengthen deterrence and protect American interests without unnecessary bureaucratic delays. The summit reflects GOP confidence that private industry innovation, when properly aligned with military needs, delivers superior warfighting capability at competitive cost.

For Colorado, this matters: the state hosts significant defense and aerospace operations, including Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, and major contractors like Lockheed Martin and Ball Aerospace. A pro-business defense environment could expand Colorado's defense sector workforce and tax base—or it could shift opportunities eastward if Pennsylvania-based firms gain preferential access.

Conservatives should watch whether McCormick's model translates into actual contract awards and whether Colorado defense firms gain equal footing in the new administration's procurement priorities.
