# Justice System Works: Karmelo Anthony Convicted Despite Media Narrative Push

A Texas jury has delivered a guilty verdict and 35-year sentence against Karmelo Anthony for the murder of 17-year-old Austin Metcalf—a case that exposed how far the mainstream media will go to manufacture victimhood narratives.

Anthony, 19, was convicted this week after a full trial. Yet his parents have since launched a coordinated media campaign claiming their son acted in self-defense and that the trial was "predetermined," according to *The Daily Wire*. They've also raised over $635,000 through crowdfunding while Anthony now claims he's broke and needs a court-appointed lawyer—a transparent attempt to game the system.

What's notable here isn't the conviction itself; it's what *The Daily Wire* calls the media's abandonment of the story now that Anthony is "no longer useful." The national press, which had amplified claims of injustice and racial bias, has gone silent. No riots materialized. The narrative collapsed under scrutiny.

Even former ESPN commentator Jemele Hill broke ranks, offering what *Daily Caller* describes as "rare common sense," suggesting the case should serve as a cautionary lesson about rushing to judgment.

This case underscores a critical truth: when facts matter more than ideology, the justice system still works. Colorado conservatives should watch whether local media outlets apply the same skepticism to future high-profile cases or continue chasing progressive narratives over evidence.
