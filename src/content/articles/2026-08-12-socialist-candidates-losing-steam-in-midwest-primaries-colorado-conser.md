# Colorado Conservatives Should Watch: Socialist Candidates Losing Traction in Midwest Primaries

The far-left's grip on Democratic primary politics is loosening—and that's a warning sign for Colorado progressives banking on radical candidates to drive turnout.

In Wisconsin's Democratic gubernatorial primary, socialist candidate Francesca Hong lost her race Tuesday, triggering meltdowns among far-left activists. According to the Washington Examiner, Milwaukee's election commission cited "human error" in absentee ballot processing that delayed results, though Republicans questioned the outcome. Meanwhile, in Minnesota, GOP candidate Michele Tafoya won her Senate primary by pitching "common sense" directly to disaffected Democrats tired of what she calls the state's "socialist lurch" (Fox News Politics).

The pattern matters for Colorado. As Democrats nationwide drift left on energy policy, gun control, and spending, moderate and working-class voters are abandoning the party. Tafoya's strategy—appealing to fiscal conservatives and Second Amendment supporters within Democratic ranks—mirrors the playbook Colorado Republicans should deploy against vulnerable Democratic incumbents in 2024 and 2026.

The left's internal fracture over "establishment" Democrats rejecting socialist candidates shows the progressive coalition is splintering. Colorado's energy workers, ranchers, and small-business owners—traditionally Democratic constituencies—are increasingly receptive to conservative messaging on jobs and government overreach.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats double down on progressive primary candidates or pivot toward the center to survive general elections.**
