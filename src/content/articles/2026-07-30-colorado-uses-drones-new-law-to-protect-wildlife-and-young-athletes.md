# Colorado Deploys Drone Tech to Protect Wildlife Habitat—and Turkey Hunting

Colorado wildlife managers are using precision-spray drones to combat invasive species threatening wild turkey habitat in the northeast, a practical example of technology solving environmental problems without heavy-handed regulation.

The effort employs a 13-gallon spray tank and mapping drone to target weeds that degrade native grasslands where turkeys forage, according to the *Colorado Sun*. The approach represents smart stewardship: using innovation to restore habitat rather than imposing blanket restrictions on land use or hunting.

Meanwhile, Colorado lawmakers passed the Alyssa's Youth Concussion and Mental Health Protection Act, named after 13-year-old athlete Alyssa Peterson, to strengthen concussion protocols and mental health support for young athletes (*KDVR Fox31*). The bipartisan measure reflects legitimate concern for youth safety without the regulatory overreach that often accompanies education-focused legislation.

Both initiatives address real Colorado problems—habitat degradation and athlete welfare—through targeted solutions rather than sweeping mandates. The drone program particularly demonstrates how private innovation and voluntary cooperation between landowners and wildlife agencies can achieve conservation goals efficiently.

Conservatives should monitor whether future habitat-restoration efforts remain voluntary and market-driven, or whether the state uses them as a pretext for expanded land-use controls affecting Colorado ranchers and private property owners.
