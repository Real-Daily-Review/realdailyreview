# Colorado's Minimum Wage Hike Heads Higher in 2027—But Job Growth Forecasts Are Falling

Colorado's minimum wage will climb again in 2027 as the state's constitutional automatic-adjustment mechanism kicks in, raising labor costs for small businesses across the state just as job growth forecasts are being cut (Colorado Sun). The increase continues a pattern that has pushed the state's wage floor significantly above the federal minimum, a policy conservatives argue prices low-skilled workers out of entry-level jobs and squeezes already-thin margins at family restaurants and retailers.

The Colorado Sun reports that lowest-paid food workers have seen the biggest pay boost so far, though broader wage gains for other workers remain modest. Meanwhile, CU's latest economic forecast downgraded job growth projections—a warning sign that higher labor mandates may be dampening hiring momentum even as Denver inflation sits at 3.9%.

Republicans and business groups have long opposed Colorado's automatic wage indexing, contending it removes legislative flexibility and ignores regional economic differences. They argue that mandated wage hikes, however well-intentioned, reduce hours, accelerate automation, and make it harder for struggling small businesses to compete.

**Watch whether the 2027 increase triggers renewed calls from the business community for legislative relief or a recalibration of the state's automatic-adjustment formula.**
