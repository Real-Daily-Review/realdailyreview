# Denver's Homeless Crisis and Rising Crime: Two Sides of Progressive Policy Failure

Denver's homelessness and crime spirals are colliding in real time—and Colorado taxpayers are footing the bill for both failures.

The Colorado Sun documented one homeless couple's four-month odyssey through Denver's tent communities, cycling through sweeps, weather, and displacement. Meanwhile, 9News reports two arrests following a brazen home invasion where a Denver couple operating an in-home jewelry business were shot during a robbery, suffering serious injuries.

These stories illustrate what conservatives have long warned: progressive policies prioritizing homeless encampments over public safety and property rights create conditions where crime flourishes. Democrats and homeless advocates argue Denver needs more shelter funding and housing-first approaches. Republicans counter that permissive tent-city policies, inadequate law enforcement, and catch-and-release prosecution enable both chronic homelessness and predatory crime—leaving law-abiding Coloradans vulnerable in their own homes.

Denver's homeless population has exploded under years of left-wing management. The city's refusal to enforce ordinances against street camping, combined with soft-on-crime policies, has transformed neighborhoods into open-air drug markets and crime zones. Meanwhile, small business owners and residents face escalating violence.
