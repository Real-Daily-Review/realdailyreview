# Denver's Civil Service Commission Tightens Police, Firefighter Hiring After Audit Finds Gaps

Denver's government is finally addressing what an audit exposed: sloppy hiring practices for police and firefighter positions. The Civil Service Commission has adopted most recommendations to tighten software controls and security measures in the recruitment process (KDVR Fox31).

The move comes as Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for public safety operations—and hiring the wrong people costs more than just money. Better vetting procedures mean fewer liability headaches and stronger departments. Conservatives argue that government agencies should operate with the same rigor private employers demand; this audit suggests Denver's bureaucracy had fallen short.

Meanwhile, a separate incident underscores why accountability matters: a Thornton police sergeant is under internal investigation following a train crash that will cost RTD over $150,000 in repairs (9News). The incident highlights how individual misconduct ripples across taxpayer-funded agencies.

Denver's Civil Service Commission deserves credit for acting on the audit's findings rather than burying them. The real test: whether these improvements stick and whether similar audits of other city departments reveal comparable gaps in hiring discipline.

Watch whether the city extends these reforms to other departments—or whether this remains a one-off fix.
