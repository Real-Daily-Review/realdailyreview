# Michigan Democrat Breaks Ranks, Endorses Republican Over Antisemitic Ad Dispute

A sitting Democratic congresswoman just handed Republicans a gift in a competitive Senate race—and it exposes the left's tolerance problem with antisemitism.

Rep. Haley Stevens (D-MI) publicly endorsed Republican Mike Rogers for Senate after Abdul El-Sayed's campaign uninvited her over an antisemitic ad controversy (Washington Examiner). Stevens appeared alongside Rogers at a Detroit church, effectively abandoning the Democratic nominee mid-campaign (Fox News Politics).

The split reveals a fracture in Michigan's Democratic coalition. El-Sayed's campaign distanced itself from Stevens after the antisemitic ad dispute, leaving her no choice but to walk. Rather than stay silent, Stevens chose principle—or at least public principle—and backed the Republican.

**What conservatives should note:** This isn't just Michigan theater. When a Democrat of Stevens's stature flips to endorse a Republican, it signals weakness in the Democratic base and raises hard questions about El-Sayed's judgment and the left's actual commitment to combating antisemitism versus performative outrage.

Democrats will argue this is an isolated incident driven by personal grievance, not ideology. But the optics matter: a Democratic congresswoman found her party's nominee so objectionable she went public with a Republican endorsement.

Watch whether this defection moves independent and Jewish voters in Michigan—and whether national Democrats distance themselves from El-Sayed or double down.
