# Colorado Aerospace Industry Losing Competitive Edge Without State Support

Colorado's aerospace sector—once a crown jewel of the state economy—risks falling behind competitors because policymakers aren't doing enough to back the industry, according to a new Colorado Chamber Foundation report cited by the *Colorado Sun*.

The state remains a leader in aerospace manufacturing and defense contracting, but job growth has flatlined. Republicans and business leaders argue that Colorado needs targeted workforce development, tax incentives, and regulatory streamlining to keep aerospace companies from relocating to states offering more aggressive support. Without action, they warn, high-paying aerospace jobs will migrate elsewhere—taking tax revenue and economic opportunity with them.

Democrats typically counter that broad-based education and apprenticeship programs serve aerospace workers alongside other sectors, though the *Colorado Sun* notes apprenticeship demand is surging across industries. The question for state lawmakers: whether Colorado will compete aggressively for aerospace talent and investment, or let regulatory burden and passive policy drift cost the state thousands of jobs in one of its most valuable sectors.

Conservatives should watch whether the legislature prioritizes aerospace-specific incentives or continues relying on generic workforce programs that fail to address industry-specific needs.
