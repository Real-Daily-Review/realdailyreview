# Colorado Faces Dangerous Heat and Wildfire Smoke Monday Before Relief Arrives

Denver braces for another dangerously hot day Monday with near-record triple-digit temperatures and thick wildfire smoke blanketing the Front Range—conditions that will test Colorado's power grid and strain air quality for vulnerable residents.

The National Weather Service has issued a Heat Advisory for the Denver metro area, with highs expected to approach or exceed 100 degrees before an evening cold front delivers significant relief by Tuesday (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The combination of extreme heat and smoke from regional wildfires is creating hazardous conditions for outdoor work, agriculture, and public health—particularly for elderly Coloradans and those with respiratory issues.

Tuesday's forecast shows a dramatic shift: cooler temperatures and isolated showers are expected as the system moves through, providing the first meaningful break from the heat wave that has gripped the state.

For Colorado's energy sector and taxpayers, the recurring heat waves underscore the importance of reliable baseload power generation—a reality that renewable-only mandates struggle to address during peak demand periods. Conservatives argue that Colorado's aggressive push toward wind and solar, without adequate natural gas or nuclear backup, leaves the state vulnerable to rolling blackouts during exactly these conditions.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's grid operators implement demand-response measures or rolling outages, and how state energy regulators respond to capacity concerns.
