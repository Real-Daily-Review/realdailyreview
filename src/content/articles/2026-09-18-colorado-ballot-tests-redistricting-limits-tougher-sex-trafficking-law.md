# Colorado Ballot Will Test Voter Appetite for Redistricting Limits and Tougher Sex-Trafficking Laws

Colorado voters face two consequential measures this fall that pit process reform against crime severity—and both deserve conservative scrutiny.

Initiative #256 would impose new guardrails on congressional redistricting, banning maps drawn explicitly to favor either party. Supporters argue the measure protects electoral integrity; critics worry it could hamstring Republicans' ability to compete in a state trending blue. The real question: does Colorado trust its legislature to draw fair lines, or does it need a straitjacket?

Proposition 133 takes a harder line. It elevates sexual trafficking of minors to a Class 1 felony—Colorado's most severe classification—triggering mandatory life sentences. Conservatives have long argued that child predators deserve maximum punishment; this measure puts teeth behind that principle. Expect Democrats to howl about "mandatory minimums" and "prison overcrowding," but Coloradans protecting their kids won't apologize.

Both measures reflect voter frustration: one with partisan gerrymandering, one with lenient sentencing. Neither is a slam dunk. Redistricting limits could backfire if they produce chaotic maps; sex-trafficking enhancements could face legal challenges on proportionality grounds.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican base turns out to defend tough-on-crime measures while Democrats push back on both fronts.
