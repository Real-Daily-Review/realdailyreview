# Colorado's TABOR Under Siege as Loopholes Drain Taxpayer Protections

Colorado's Taxpayer Bill of Rights—once a model for fiscal restraint nationwide—is being hollowed out by decades of legislative workarounds that let politicians spend more without voter approval, according to conservative watchdogs.

Complete Colorado reports that TABOR, the 1992 constitutional amendment limiting government revenue growth, now resembles "Swiss cheese" thanks to creative political maneuvering. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue the erosion undermines the law's core purpose: protecting taxpayers from unchecked spending and forcing lawmakers to prioritize existing dollars over endless tax hikes.

The concern cuts to Colorado's affordability crisis. As government finds new ways to circumvent TABOR's spending caps—through enterprise funds, special districts, and other mechanisms—taxpayers foot the bill through higher costs of living while their voices are sidelined.

Democrats typically counter that TABOR's rigidity hampers necessary investments in education, infrastructure, and services. But conservatives argue that argument ignores the real problem: government bloat, not insufficient revenue.

Meanwhile, the Trump administration is moving to kill the federal "roadless rule" and increase logging on national forests (CPR News), a move that could affect Colorado's public lands and timber interests—though the state has its own protections in place.

**Watch whether Colorado lawmakers move to plug TABOR loopholes or let the erosion continue unchecked.**
