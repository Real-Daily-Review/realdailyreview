# Republicans Face Pressure to Raise Debt Ceiling Without Spending Restraint

Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) has signaled Republicans may accept a lame-duck debt ceiling increase through the end of President Trump's term, according to *Politico*, raising conservative alarm about another blank check for federal spending.

The GOP leadership's openness to a ceiling hike—acknowledged "multiple times since July"—comes as Republicans eye defense spending and consider reviving a shelved reconciliation package worth roughly $100 billion for defense and farm aid. Conservatives argue this approach repeats a familiar trap: raising the debt limit without meaningful spending reforms or a credible repayment plan.

**The conservative case:** Republicans should demand concrete fiscal restraint—spending caps, entitlement reform, or revenue-neutral offsets—before voting to increase borrowing authority. Raising the ceiling without conditions simply enables Washington to spend more taxpayer money and kick the fiscal crisis down the road.

**Democrats counter** that defense and agricultural support are essential investments that justify higher borrowing.

For Colorado, this matters. Federal spending fuels inflation that erodes household purchasing power, while debt service crowds out funding for infrastructure and defense priorities that benefit the state's military installations and economy.

Watch whether House Republicans—particularly Colorado's delegation—demand spending conditions or cave to leadership pressure for a clean debt increase.
