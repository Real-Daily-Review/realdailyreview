# Alaska GOP Rejects Trump's Election Lawsuit; FBI Probes 'Decoy Dan' Scheme

Alaska's Republican state government just dealt Trump a stinging rebuke—rejecting his demand to invalidate Senate ballots and calling his lawsuit "meritless" (Washington Examiner). The move underscores how even GOP-controlled states won't bend election law to suit political pressure, a principle Colorado conservatives should note as ballot integrity debates intensify nationwide.

Meanwhile, federal agents are investigating the so-called "Decoy Dan" scheme, where operatives allegedly fielded a second "Dan Sullivan" candidate to split votes and hand the Senate seat to Democrats (New York Post). Conservative commentator Mark Levin called it a "conspiracy to steal the United States Senate" (Breitbart), and the FBI has contacted donors across Alaska and Massachusetts as the probe expands.

The dual developments expose two competing conservative concerns: distrust of election administration versus rejection of post-election litigation that ignores statutory limits. Alaska's GOP leadership—hardly Trump allies—sided with rule of law over political expediency, signaling that even red-state Republicans won't weaponize courts to overturn results.

For Colorado, the lesson cuts both ways: election integrity matters, but so does respecting constitutional guardrails. Conservatives should demand transparent ballot procedures and voter ID protections *before* Election Day, not courtroom Hail Marys after.

Watch whether the FBI's "Decoy Dan" investigation yields charges and whether Trump's legal strategy shifts toward prevention rather than reversal.
