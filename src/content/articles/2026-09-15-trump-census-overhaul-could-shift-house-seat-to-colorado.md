# Trump Moves to Strip Non-Citizens From Census Count—Colorado Could Gain House Seat

President Trump's administration is overhauling how the U.S. Census counts population for congressional apportionment, excluding non-citizens from the tally used to divvy up House seats. The Washington Examiner reports this marks Trump's most significant push yet on an issue he's championed for six years: preventing illegal immigrants from inflating Democratic-leaning states' representation in Congress.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the current system dilutes the voting power of American citizens. By counting non-citizens—legal and illegal—toward House seats, states with large immigrant populations gain unearned representation. Colorado could benefit: excluding non-citizens could shift a seat toward red or purple states from blue strongholds like California and New York.

**Democrats' likely counter:** The left will argue the Constitution requires counting all residents, not just citizens, for apportionment purposes. They'll claim the move is xenophobic and designed to suppress immigrant communities' political voice.

Trump has also signaled skepticism of AI regulation concerns, calling them a "hoax" while backing continued data-center expansion (Just The News). His administration is pushing forward on both fronts—reshaping representation and accelerating tech development.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado gains a House seat under the new census rules and how courts respond to inevitable legal challenges.
