# Kennedy Center Removes Iconic 'Blue' Sculpture in Trump Renovation Push

The Kennedy Center is yanking its outdoor "Blue" sculpture as part of Trump administration renovations—and the lack of transparency is raising questions about how federal cultural institutions handle public assets.

The center announced the removal late Tuesday, with the sculpture coming down Wednesday (9News Denver). The move is part of broader Trump-era upgrades to the federally funded venue.

But here's where it gets sticky: Josef Palermo, the center's former curator of visual arts, was laid off in March and objected to the removal's lack of notice. He's calling for more transparency when pieces held in trust are removed (Just The News). His complaint highlights a legitimate conservative concern—federal institutions should answer to taxpayers about decisions affecting public property.

The Kennedy Center receives federal funding, meaning Colorado taxpayers help foot the bill. When government agencies make unilateral decisions about cultural assets without public input or clear justification, it's the kind of bureaucratic opacity conservatives rightly criticize, regardless of which administration is in charge.

Republicans and conservatives should watch whether the Kennedy Center provides a full accounting of what's being removed, why, and what happens to these pieces—setting a standard for transparency at all federally funded cultural institutions.
