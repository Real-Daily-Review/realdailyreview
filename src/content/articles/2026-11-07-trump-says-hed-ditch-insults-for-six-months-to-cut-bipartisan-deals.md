# Trump Says He'd Curb Rhetoric for Bipartisan Deals—If Democrats Reciprocate

President Trump claimed he could "very easily" stop insulting Democrats for six months to broker bipartisan agreements in a Democrat-controlled Congress, according to preview clips from his upcoming *60 Minutes* interview (Washington Examiner, New York Post).

The statement signals potential willingness to negotiate on major issues—though conservatives should remain skeptical about whether Democrats will meet him halfway. Republicans argue that bipartisan deals require good faith from both sides, and past experience suggests the left often uses civility as a one-way street while pursuing aggressive progressive agendas.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. If Trump can negotiate with a Democratic Congress, energy policy, water rights, and federal land management could be on the table—areas where Colorado's economy and water security hang in the balance. Conservatives worry that any "deal" might sacrifice Colorado's oil and gas interests or water allocations to appease environmental activists in Democratic leadership.

The real test: whether Democrats reciprocate Trump's olive branch or use any softening in rhetoric as cover to ram through tax hikes, regulatory overreach, and green mandates that hurt Colorado workers and businesses.

Watch whether House and Senate Democrats actually engage in good-faith negotiation or simply exploit any shift in Trump's tone to advance their own agenda.
