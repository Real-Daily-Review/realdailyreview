# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, July 27, 2026

Colorado is on fire — literally — and the political landscape isn't much cooler. From a Sedalia wildfire forcing families from their homes to a Democratic governor suddenly discovering the dangers of socialism, today's news demands your attention.

---

## 🔥 Wildfire West of Sedalia Forces Mandatory Evacuations

A forest fire burning west of Sedalia prompted mandatory evacuations for residents Sunday evening, putting Colorado families and property at risk as fire season bears down on the Front Range. This is exactly why conservatives have long argued that responsible forest management — not federal land-locking and green-activist obstruction — is the only real wildfire prevention strategy. Colorado ranchers and rural homeowners deserve a government that manages public lands, not one that treats them as untouchable wilderness preserves while communities burn.

---

## 🤔 Polis Declares War on Socialism — Seriously

Colorado's Democratic Gov. Jared Polis, described by columnist Mike Littwin as "very capitalist," has apparently launched a war against socialism. Republicans will note the irony: Polis has presided over years of expanded government spending, regulatory growth, and progressive policy experiments in Colorado — and now wants credit for opposing socialism. Conservatives say the proof is in the policy, not the press release. Colorado taxpayers will be watching to see whether this is a genuine course correction or an election-cycle rebranding ahead of whatever Polis's next political ambition turns out to be.

---

## 🗽 Trump's 'Spirit of '76' Honors Everyday American Heroes

The Trump administration's "Spirit of '76" exhibit at Freedom Plaza — featuring the Caesar Rodney statue and 22 other installations — puts ordinary Revolutionary War heroes front and center as America marks its 250th anniversary. This is the kind of unapologetic American patriotism that the left spent years trying to erase from public spaces and school curricula. For Coloradans who watched progressive activists tear down statues and rewrite history, seeing the federal government restore and celebrate the founding generation is a welcome and overdue corrective.

---

## 👶 Pronatalism in the Trump White House: A Start, Not a Solution

The Trump administration's embrace of pronatalism — spotlighting babies born to administration staffers and officials — is generating conversation about family, culture, and government's role in encouraging birth rates. Conservatives rightly note that pronatalism is not automatically pro-family: real family policy means getting government out of the way, cutting the tax burden on young families, and rebuilding a culture that values marriage and parenthood. For Colorado families already squeezed by high housing costs and a cost-of-living crisis, the message matters — but the policy has to follow.

---

## 🏛️ The Family Is the Original Government — And the Left Has Been Replacing It

A sharp piece making the rounds argues that America's constitutional order was never invented from scratch — it was modeled on the family unit, with a mother who invests and a father who provides and defends. The left's multi-decade project of replacing the family with government dependency has produced exactly what conservatives predicted: broken communities, declining competence, and an ever-expanding welfare state. Colorado conservatives understand this instinctively. The answer isn't more government programs — it's restoring the conditions under which strong families can thrive.

## 📍 What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Sedalia fire — evacuation zones and land management decisions in the coming days will tell you everything about whether Colorado's leadership is serious about protecting rural communities. Watch Gov. Polis's anti-socialism posture closely; if it doesn't come with actual policy rollbacks, it's nothing but positioning. And as Washington debates family and demographic policy, Colorado taxpayers should demand that any federal or state "pro-family" agenda starts with tax relief — not new spending programs dressed up in wholesome language.
