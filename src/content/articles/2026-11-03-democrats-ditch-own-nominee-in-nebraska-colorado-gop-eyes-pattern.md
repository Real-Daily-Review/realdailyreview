# Democrats' Bait-and-Switch in Nebraska Signals Desperation on Senate Map

Colorado Republicans should take note: Democrats are resorting to procedural gimmicks to salvage winnable races they can't win straight up.

In Nebraska, the Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate, Cindy Burbank, executed a pre-planned exit after winning the May primary, clearing the field for an "independent" candidate, Dan Osborn, to challenge Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts. According to Nebraska Secretary of State Bob Evnen, Burbank had announced this strategy in advance—a coordinated maneuver designed to exploit Nebraska's ballot access rules and avoid the Democratic label's electoral liability (The Daily Wire).

Republicans argue this is a textbook example of Democratic cynicism: when the party brand becomes toxic, Democrats simply hide it. The tactic underscores how unpopular the national Democratic agenda has become, even in traditionally Democratic strongholds.

The broader pattern is telling. Political analyst James Carville has suggested Democrats might actually benefit from losing control of Congress, signaling deep fractures within the party over its direction (Washington Examiner).

For Colorado, this matters. If Democrats are this desperate to distance themselves from their own nominees in deep-red Nebraska, what does that say about their prospects in purple Colorado races? Taxpayers and conservatives should watch whether Colorado Democrats attempt similar maneuvers in 2026 state and federal races.
