# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary to just two candidates after their assembly process, signaling a sharply focused race ahead. Meanwhile, voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution through Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado.

The assembly outcome reflects Republicans' effort to consolidate behind viable nominees—a stark contrast to the sprawling 18-candidate field that entered the process. Only two advanced to the primary ballot, according to *Complete Colorado*.

The natural gas initiative represents a direct conservative pushback against Colorado's progressive energy agenda. Democrats and environmental groups have long pushed the state toward renewable-only mandates and restrictions on fossil fuel development—policies that threaten Colorado's energy workers and rural economies. Republicans argue that enshrining a constitutional right to natural gas protects property rights, affordable energy, and the state's oil-and-gas industry from further regulatory strangulation.

Initiative 177's qualification for the ballot, confirmed by the Colorado Secretary of State's office (*Denver Post*), signals growing grassroots concern about energy mandates. Conservatives contend that Colorado's aggressive climate policies risk blackouts, job losses, and higher utility bills for families and businesses.

Watch whether Democrats mount a costly campaign to defeat Initiative 177—and whether the narrowed GOP primary produces a nominee who can capitalize on voter frustration over energy costs and regulatory overreach.
