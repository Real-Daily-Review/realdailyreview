# Colorado Should Watch How Democrats Vet Candidates After Platner Fiasco

Democrats' rush to embrace unvetted candidates in pursuit of anti-Trump fervor has backfired spectacularly, and Colorado voters should take note as the party prepares for the next election cycle.

The Graham Platner disaster—a candidate Democrats elevated despite serious red flags—exemplifies what National Review calls "Trump Derangement Syndrome." Rather than conduct rigorous vetting, Democrats prioritized finding anyone willing to run against Republicans, according to conservative outlets covering the implosion.

**Republicans argue** this reflects a broader Democratic weakness: ideology and Trump hatred trump competence and character. The Washington Examiner notes that operatives behind Platner's failed campaign will likely resurface with new candidates, suggesting the party hasn't learned its lesson about due diligence.

**Democrats counter** (implicitly, through silence) that one candidate's collapse doesn't define their entire recruitment strategy—though they've offered little public accountability for how Platner advanced so far.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. State Democrats control the legislature and governor's mansion. If the party's vetting apparatus remains broken by anti-Trump obsession rather than grounded in actual candidate quality, Colorado Republicans have an opening to exploit weak Democratic nominees in 2026 and beyond.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats implement serious candidate screening reforms—or whether they repeat the Platner pattern with fresh faces.
