# Aurora Police Defend Flock Cameras After Student Arrests

Aurora police are doubling down on surveillance technology at schools—and Colorado conservatives should ask hard questions about the trade-off between security and privacy.

The Aurora Police Department cited a recent case involving four arrested students to justify the use of Automatic License Plate Readers (ALPRs), known as Flock cameras, positioned outside high schools (KDVR Fox31 Denver). Police argue the technology helped identify suspects in the case, framing it as a public safety win.

But here's the rub: Flock cameras create a permanent digital record of every vehicle entering and leaving school parking lots—a mass surveillance tool that collects data on thousands of law-abiding citizens to catch a handful of suspects. This is government overreach wrapped in a security blanket.

Conservatives rightly worry about mission creep. Today it's catching four students; tomorrow it's tracking your movements without warrant or probable cause. Colorado has a strong tradition of individual liberty and skepticism toward unchecked government power. Blanket surveillance of school zones—even with good intentions—sets a dangerous precedent.

The real question: Did Aurora exhaust traditional investigative methods before deploying mass surveillance? And who oversees how that data is stored, accessed, and shared?
