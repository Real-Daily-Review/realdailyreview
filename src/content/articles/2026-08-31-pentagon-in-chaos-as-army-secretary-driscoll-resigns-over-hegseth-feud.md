# Army Secretary Driscoll Exits Pentagon Amid Leadership Turmoil

The Trump administration's Defense Department is in disarray: Army Secretary Dan Driscoll has resigned after months of public tension with War Secretary Pete Hegseth, leaving the Pentagon without any Senate-confirmed civilian or military leaders (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

Driscoll's departure marks the latest in a series of military leadership shakeups under Trump (9News Denver). According to reporting, Hegseth had feared being replaced by Driscoll—a college friend of Vice President JD Vance—prompting the friction that ultimately forced Driscoll out (New York Post). The White House has not yet formally accepted the resignation, though Driscoll is expected to leave in the coming days.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that Pentagon leadership instability weakens military readiness and command structure at a critical time. A fully staffed, confirmed defense hierarchy is essential for national security and for executing Trump's military agenda without bureaucratic obstruction.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** The state hosts significant military installations (Fort Carson, Schriever Space Force Base, NORAD) and defense contractors. Leadership chaos at the Pentagon can ripple through defense spending, base operations, and contractor relationships affecting Colorado jobs and the state economy.

**Watch next:** Whether Trump moves quickly to nominate and confirm replacement leadership, or whether this signals deeper dysfunction in his defense team.
