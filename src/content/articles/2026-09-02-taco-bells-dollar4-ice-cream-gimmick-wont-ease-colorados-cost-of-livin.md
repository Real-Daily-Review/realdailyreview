# Taco Bell's New Ice Cream Taco Won't Fix Colorado's Real Problems

Taco Bell is rolling out another gimmick dessert in partnership with Portland's Salt &amp; Straw ice cream, this time an ice cream taco featuring chocolate and churro elements priced around $4 (New York Post). The chain is banking on nostalgia and novelty—much like last year's Tacolate waffle cone hybrid—to drive foot traffic and sales.

While Coloradans enjoy affordable fast food as much as anyone, this feel-good consumer story masks a harder reality: inflation has eroded purchasing power across the state. A $4 dessert that would've cost $2.50 five years ago is a small reminder of why Colorado families are tightening budgets.

The partnership with a Portland-based company also highlights how major chains increasingly source from coastal progressive strongholds rather than supporting local Colorado businesses and suppliers. When corporations chase trendy partnerships over regional relationships, local economies lose.

Republicans and conservatives argue that real relief comes not from novelty menu items, but from policies that lower the cost of living—tax relief, regulatory rollback, and energy independence that keeps utility bills manageable.
