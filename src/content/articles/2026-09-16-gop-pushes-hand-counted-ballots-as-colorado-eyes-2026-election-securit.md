# Colorado GOP Eyes Election Integrity Push as Libertarian Candidate Drama Unfolds

Colorado Republicans are making election security a centerpiece of their 2026 strategy, with GOP secretary of state candidate James Wiley championing in-person voting and hand-counted ballots—a direct challenge to Colorado's mail-heavy system that Democrats have defended as secure and convenient.

Wiley, who previously led the Colorado Libertarians before switching to the Republican ticket (CPR News), argues that tightening voting procedures protects election integrity. Republicans contend that Colorado's reliance on mail voting creates unnecessary vulnerabilities and that hand-counting provides transparency voters deserve.

Democrats counter that Colorado's mail ballot system has operated reliably for years and that in-person voting requirements would suppress turnout and burden election officials.

The race has been complicated by Libertarian chaos: candidate Alex Astley initially dropped out, then won court reinstatement on the November ballot (Denver Post). Astley denied timing his departure to help Wiley, but the episode highlights fractures in Colorado's third-party landscape.

**The Colorado angle:** Election administration is a secretary of state power. How Colorado votes—mail versus in-person, machine versus hand-count—directly affects turnout, cost, and voter confidence. Republicans see this race as pivotal to restoring what they view as election transparency.
