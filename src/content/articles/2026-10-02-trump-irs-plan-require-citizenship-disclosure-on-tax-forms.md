# Trump Admin Pushes IRS Citizenship Disclosure—Colorado Taxpayers Face New Compliance Burden

The Trump administration is proposing to require tax filers to certify their immigration status directly to the IRS, a move aimed at preventing ineligible immigrants from accessing federal benefits (Fox News Politics, Just The News).

**The Conservative Case:** The administration argues the requirement would tighten eligibility verification for federal programs and reduce improper benefit payouts. Supporters say it closes a loophole that allows non-citizens to claim credits and deductions they shouldn't receive.

**The Opposition:** Critics warn the proposal could weaponize the IRS as an immigration enforcement tool, potentially discouraging legal immigrants and citizens from filing taxes altogether. Privacy advocates worry about IRS data-sharing with immigration authorities.

**Colorado Angle:** Colorado taxpayers—already burdened by state income taxes and federal compliance costs—would face additional paperwork and potential delays. The requirement could complicate filings for Colorado's growing immigrant workforce and create administrative headaches for small businesses managing payroll taxes.

The proposal raises a legitimate question: Should the IRS become an immigration enforcement agency, or should that remain the domain of dedicated immigration authorities? Conservatives should watch whether this survives legal challenge and how Colorado's tax-filing infrastructure adapts to the new requirement.
