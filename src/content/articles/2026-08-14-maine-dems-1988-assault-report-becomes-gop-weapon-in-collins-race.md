# Maine Democrat's Assault Past Haunts Senate Bid—But Colorado Voters Should Note the Pattern

Troy Jackson, Maine's Democratic Senate nominee challenging Republican Susan Collins, is facing withering GOP attacks after a 1988 police report surfaced detailing assault allegations and a late-night brawl. Jackson has acknowledged the incident, telling reporters "I take responsibility," but Republicans smell blood—the National Republican Senatorial Committee immediately demanded Senate Democrats condemn their own nominee (Fox News).

The comparison is sharp: conservatives are calling this "Graham Platner 2.0," referencing the former Maine Democratic nominee whose scandal-plagued campaign imploded. The NRSC is pressing the point that Democrats have a pattern of nominating candidates with serious character questions.

**The Colorado angle:** While this race unfolds in Maine, it underscores a broader conservative argument about Democratic vetting and standards. Colorado Republicans should watch how national Democrats handle their own nominee's baggage—and whether the media applies the same scrutiny to Democratic candidates it reserves for Republicans. If Jackson's past doesn't derail his campaign, it signals Democrats are willing to overlook personal conduct if the politics align.

**What to watch:** Whether Senate Democrats actually distance themselves from Jackson or circle the wagons—a tell on how seriously they take character in their own ranks.
