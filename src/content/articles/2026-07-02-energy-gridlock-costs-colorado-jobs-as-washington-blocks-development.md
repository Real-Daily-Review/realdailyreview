# Energy Paralysis Is Strangling Colorado's Future—and Washington Won't Get Out of the Way

America's energy boom is bypassing Colorado because Washington has made building power infrastructure nearly impossible. The nation is entering its biggest energy expansion since the post-war era, driven by artificial intelligence and data center construction, yet regulatory gridlock and endless permitting delays are crippling domestic energy development (Washington Examiner).

Over 2,000 gigawatts of new power generation are needed nationwide, but the "power to block has systematically triumphed over the freedom to build" (Washington Examiner). Natural gas pipelines, transmission lines, and mining projects face years of permits, lawsuits, and regulatory obstacles—exactly the kind of government overreach that kills jobs and investment.

For Colorado, this means lost opportunity. Energy workers, ranchers dependent on reliable power, and businesses seeking to expand face skyrocketing costs and years-long delays. Meanwhile, competitors in states with streamlined permitting are racing ahead.

Republicans and conservatives argue that America's 250-year track record proves we innovate fastest when government gets out of the way (Breitbart). Regulatory reform—cutting permitting timelines, eliminating frivolous lawsuit delays, and trusting markets—isn't anti-environment; it's pro-prosperity.

Democrats have largely defended the current system as necessary environmental protection, though some acknowledge energy paralysis threatens competitiveness.
