# Aurora ICE Facility TB Case Exposes Federal Overreach—and Local Government Capitulation

Colorado health authorities have been stonewalled by federal immigration officials investigating an active tuberculosis case at the GEO Group's Aurora detention facility, and Adams County has essentially surrendered the fight. According to CPR News, ICE halted compliance with local public health investigations after a TB case was confirmed in June. The Colorado Sun reports that Adams County officials say they're unlikely to pursue further legal action against the private detention operator, citing an ongoing lawsuit.

This is government dysfunction on two fronts. First, federal immigration authorities are obstructing state and local public health officials from doing their jobs—a clear case of federal overreach that undermines Colorado's ability to protect its residents. Second, Adams County's decision to back down signals that private contractors operating detention facilities can ignore local oversight with impunity.

Republicans and conservatives argue that federal agencies must cooperate with state health authorities, not hide behind litigation threats. The detention facility's silence only fuels public concern and erodes trust in both ICE and the contractor managing the facility. Colorado taxpayers deserve transparency about health risks in facilities operating within their state.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature or Attorney General will challenge federal obstruction of state public health authority.
