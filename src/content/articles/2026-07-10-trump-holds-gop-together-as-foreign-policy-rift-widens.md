# Republican Party Fractures Over Foreign Policy as Trump Holds Coalition Together

Donald Trump remains the central figure holding together a fractured Republican Party increasingly divided over America's role in global conflicts, according to reporting from *The Daily Wire*. The core disagreement pits foreign policy "hawks" who favor continued military confrontation against "restrainers" who want the U.S. out of endless wars—a split that threatens GOP unity heading into 2026.

Meanwhile, *Denver Post* political analyst Seth Masket notes that Trump has successfully branded Republican populism with his own distinct flavor, cementing his grip on the party base even as ideological tensions simmer beneath the surface.

For Colorado Republicans and conservatives, this matters: the party's direction on spending, military commitments, and foreign aid directly affects federal budget priorities and tax policy. A party divided on foreign intervention could mean either restraint on defense spending (favored by some conservatives) or continued commitments that drain resources from domestic priorities.

The real test comes when Trump's unifying influence weakens or when the party must choose a successor. Without his personal brand holding the coalition together, Republicans risk splintering into competing camps—potentially handing Democrats openings in purple states like Colorado.

**Watch for:** whether Colorado GOP candidates in 2026 embrace Trump's foreign policy restraint or align with traditional hawk positions.
