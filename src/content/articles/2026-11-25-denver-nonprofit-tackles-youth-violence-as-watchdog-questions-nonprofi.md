# Community Center Tackles Youth Violence, But Watchdog Raises Questions on Nonprofit Accountability

A Denver nonprofit's community center in Montbello is stepping up to address youth violence and mental health—work that matters in a neighborhood that needs it. The Struggle of Love held its 19th annual "Need to Feed" event over the weekend, positioning its "Love Center" as a hub for local problem-solving (CPR News).

But while community-based solutions deserve support, conservatives should pay attention to how nonprofits spend tax-advantaged dollars. A government watchdog recently filed IRS complaints against six mental health nonprofits alleging racial discrimination and LGBT preference in hiring and services (Washington Examiner)—raising accountability questions that apply broadly across the nonprofit sector.

Republicans argue that tax-exempt status comes with real responsibility: organizations receiving charitable deductions should operate transparently and without ideological discrimination. When nonprofits prioritize identity politics over merit-based hiring or universal service delivery, they betray donor trust and public confidence.

Denver's community violence problem is real and urgent. But Coloradans funding these efforts—whether through donations or tax forgiveness—deserve assurance that their money goes to effective, accountable organizations serving everyone equally.

Watch whether the IRS follows through on these complaints and what transparency standards emerge for Colorado nonprofits claiming tax exemption.
