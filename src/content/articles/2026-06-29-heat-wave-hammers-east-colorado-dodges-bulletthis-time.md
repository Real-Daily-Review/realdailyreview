# Heat Wave Headed East; Colorado Likely Spared—For Now

A dangerous heat wave will pummel much of the central and eastern United States this week ahead of July Fourth, the National Weather Service warns, with dangerously high humidity making temperatures feel even worse (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The combination poses real risks to public health and energy grids already straining under peak summer demand.

Colorado's Front Range and Western Slope should largely dodge the worst of it—a rare break for a state that has endured its share of brutal heat in recent years. But don't celebrate yet. The pattern underscores a hard truth: America's aging power infrastructure, hamstrung by years of regulatory delays and underinvestment, struggles to handle peak-load summers. Progressive energy policies that prioritize wind and solar over reliable baseload generation only worsen the problem when the grid needs it most.

Coloradans dependent on affordable, dependable electricity—especially rural communities and small businesses—should pay close attention to how state and federal regulators respond. Will they finally greenlight natural gas plants and nuclear capacity? Or will they double down on unreliable renewables while grid operators cross their fingers?

Watch whether Colorado's Public Utilities Commission prioritizes grid reliability over ideological energy mandates when the next heat emergency hits the state.
