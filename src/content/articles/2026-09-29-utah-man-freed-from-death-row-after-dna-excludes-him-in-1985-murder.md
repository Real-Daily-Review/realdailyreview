# Utah Death Row Inmate Released After DNA Clears Him in 1985 Murder

A Utah judge has ordered the release on bail of Douglas Stewart Carter, 71, who spent decades on death row after being convicted in 1985 of killing Eva Olesen—a case now undermined by DNA evidence that excludes him as the perpetrator (New York Post). Carter was sentenced to death based on a jury verdict four decades ago, but advances in forensic science have since cast serious doubt on his guilt.

The case underscores a critical conservative principle: the justice system must remain anchored to evidence, not emotion or political pressure. DNA technology has proven invaluable in exonerating the wrongly convicted—a win for due process and individual liberty that transcends partisan lines. Carter now awaits a new trial.

While this case doesn't directly implicate Colorado policy, it highlights why conservatives should remain vigilant about prosecutorial overreach and the importance of rigorous evidentiary standards in capital cases. Death penalty cases demand the highest scrutiny; sloppy investigations or junk science betray both victims and the accused.

Taxpayers should watch whether Utah's legal system conducts a thorough review of how Carter's conviction withstood scrutiny for so long—and whether similar cases in other states, including Colorado, warrant fresh examination under modern forensic standards.
