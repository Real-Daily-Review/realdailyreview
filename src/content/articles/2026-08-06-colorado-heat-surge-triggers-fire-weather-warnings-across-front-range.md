# Colorado Faces Dangerous Fire Season as Heat Returns to Front Range

Colorado's brief respite from dangerous heat ends this weekend as temperatures surge back toward record highs, intensifying wildfire risk across the state.

The National Weather Service has issued a Fire Weather Warning for mountain areas, while Air Quality Alerts cover Denver and the Front Range due to wildfire smoke and haze (9News Denver). After a couple days of below-normal temperatures, the Denver metro will see another sharp temperature spike with potential record highs possible this weekend (KDVR Fox31).

The timing is critical: Colorado's fire season typically peaks in late summer and early fall, when dry conditions and high heat create tinderbox conditions across forests and grasslands. With water resources already strained from drought conditions affecting the state's agriculture and energy sectors, elevated fire danger compounds pressure on Colorado's water management and emergency response systems.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether state and local governments use fire season conditions to justify expanded environmental regulations or land-use restrictions that could limit ranching, timber management, or energy development—rather than focusing on proven forest management practices and controlled burns that reduce fuel loads.
