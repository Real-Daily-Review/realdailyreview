# Trump's Venezuelan Oil Play Raises Questions About Government Overreach

President Trump's move to seize control of Venezuelan oil assets marks a troubling expansion of executive power that conservatives should scrutinize—even when directed at a hostile regime. National Review warns the strategy amounts to "the American nationalization of Venezuelan oil," effectively turning the U.S. government into a "hedge fund" abroad.

The approach raises a fundamental question: if Washington can nationalize foreign assets today, what's to stop future administrations from targeting domestic energy companies or private holdings? Colorado's oil and gas industry—already under siege from state regulators and federal land-use restrictions—should take note.

Republicans argue the move targets a communist dictatorship and serves national security interests. But conservatives warn that normalizing government seizure of private assets, even internationally, sets a dangerous precedent for state power. The line between strategic foreign policy and creeping authoritarianism can blur quickly.

Meanwhile, Complete Colorado notes the irony of progressive politicians like Bernie Sanders railing against billionaires while accumulating wealth themselves—a reminder that anti-capitalist rhetoric often masks political theater rather than principled opposition to concentrated power.

**Watch for:** Congressional pushback on whether Trump's Venezuelan oil strategy requires legislative approval or violates constitutional limits on executive authority.
