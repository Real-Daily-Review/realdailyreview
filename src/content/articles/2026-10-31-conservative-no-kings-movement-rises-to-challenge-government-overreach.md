# Conservative Grassroots Movement Emerges to Counter Progressive Overreach

A new grassroots conservative movement dubbed "No Kings" is gaining traction as a counter to what Republicans view as government overreach—much like the Tea Party movement that erupted in response to Obama-era spending and regulatory expansion (Washington Examiner).

The parallel is instructive. When Obama took office in 2009, center-right opposition to his agenda crystallized around fiscal restraint and constitutional limits on federal power. CNBC's Rick Santelli's famous February 2009 rant captured the sentiment: ordinary Americans rejecting government-driven solutions to economic problems.

Today's "No Kings" movement reflects similar frustrations with what conservatives argue is unchecked executive authority and spending. Republicans contend that progressive administrations have expanded government reach into energy policy, healthcare, education, and local governance—areas where Coloradans have historically favored local control and market solutions.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. The state has seen federal overreach on water rights, energy development, and land-use decisions that pit Washington bureaucrats against ranchers, energy workers, and property owners. A revitalized grassroots conservative movement could amplify Colorado voices demanding that power return to states and individuals.

Watch whether this "No Kings" energy translates into concrete primary challenges and turnout in 2026—the real test of whether grassroots conservatism can reshape Republican politics as it did in 2010.
