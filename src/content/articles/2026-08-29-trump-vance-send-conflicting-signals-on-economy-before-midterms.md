# Republicans Split Message on Economy as Midterms Loom

President Trump is telling voters America enjoys "the greatest economy in our history," while his running mate paints a darker picture of deindustrialization and decay—leaving Republicans with a muddled pitch heading into midterm elections.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, Trump has touted manufacturing booms, exploding growth, and defeated inflation in recent campaign stops. Yet Vice President Vance simultaneously warns of a "deindustrialized dystopia," creating what observers call a fundamental contradiction in the GOP's economic narrative.

The mixed messaging matters for Colorado. Republicans need clarity on whether they're defending current conditions or promising transformation—especially as Colorado voters weigh energy policy, manufacturing competitiveness, and job creation in sectors like oil and gas, advanced manufacturing, and agriculture. Coloradans tired of progressive regulatory overreach want to know: Are Republicans confident in free-market recovery, or do they believe the economy requires major structural reform?

Conservatives argue the economy's strength depends on rolling back Biden-era regulations, protecting energy development, and keeping taxes low. The question is whether Trump and Vance can settle on a single, compelling answer—or whether voters will see the contradiction as a sign Republicans don't have a coherent plan.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans clarify their economic message before the general election campaign intensifies.
