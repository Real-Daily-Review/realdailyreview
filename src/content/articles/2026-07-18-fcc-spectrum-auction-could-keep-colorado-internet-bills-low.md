# FCC Spectrum Auction Could Keep Colorado Internet Bills Low

The Trump administration's push to auction off more government-controlled broadband spectrum to private companies could help keep internet costs down for Colorado families and businesses.

The Federal Communications Commission is preparing to auction 160 megahertz of spectrum in the Upper C-Band, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The move continues the administration's market-friendly approach: let private companies compete for airwaves rather than have government control the resource.

Conservatives argue this approach works. Internet connectivity costs have hit historic lows, and auctioning spectrum to the private sector—rather than letting bureaucrats manage it—unleashes competition that benefits consumers. When companies bid for spectrum rights, they have incentive to deploy service efficiently and price competitively to win customers.

The contrast is stark: government-controlled spectrum typically means slower innovation, higher prices, and less incentive for providers to expand rural broadband into Colorado's mountain communities and agricultural regions. Private auction models reward companies that serve underserved areas and invest in infrastructure.

For Colorado taxpayers and rural residents especially, this matters. Broadband access drives economic development, remote work, and agricultural productivity across the state.

Watch whether the FCC auction proceeds on schedule and how many bidders compete—competition is the real test of whether spectrum policy actually delivers lower bills.
