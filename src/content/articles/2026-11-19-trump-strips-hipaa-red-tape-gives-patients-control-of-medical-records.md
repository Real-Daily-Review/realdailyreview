# Trump's HIPAA Overhaul Puts Medical Records in Patients' Hands, Not Bureaucrats'

The Trump administration is stripping away outdated HIPAA rules that force patients to jump through hoops just to access their own medical records, according to the Washington Examiner.

Under current regulations, patients often become "medical record archivists" instead of focusing on actual health outcomes. The overhaul aims to digitize record access and reduce compliance costs that providers pass along to patients and insurers. By empowering individuals to control their own health data, the reform shifts power away from government gatekeeping and toward patient choice—a core conservative principle.

For Colorado patients and healthcare providers, this means faster access to records, lower administrative burdens, and reduced costs. Rural Colorado clinics and smaller practices, which often struggle with HIPAA compliance expenses, could see meaningful relief.

The change reflects the Trump administration's broader deregulation agenda: remove rules that don't protect patients but do protect bureaucratic turf. Rather than expanding government oversight of healthcare, this approach trusts individuals to manage their own information.

Colorado conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether state regulators attempt to impose additional restrictions that undermine the federal reform.
