# Brown University President Steps Down as Elite Institutions Face Trump Scrutiny

Brown University President Christina Paxson is stepping down at the end of the 2026-27 academic year, ending a 15-year tenure as the longest-serving Ivy League president. Her departure comes as elite universities face intensifying pressure from the Trump administration over campus free speech, antisemitism, and ideological imbalance in hiring and curriculum.

Paxson navigated a contentious period marked by a mass shooting on campus and mounting criticism of Brown's progressive policies. Her exit signals broader turbulence in higher education leadership as conservative scrutiny of Ivy League institutions—long criticized by Republicans for left-wing activism and DEI bureaucracies—reaches a crescendo.

The timing matters: as federal funding and accreditation standards face potential overhaul under Republican leadership, university presidents are increasingly vulnerable to political pressure. Conservatives argue that institutions like Brown have abandoned intellectual diversity and merit-based admissions in favor of progressive ideology, squandering endowments and donor trust.

Colorado families paying six-figure tuition bills to send their kids to elite schools should watch whether Brown's next president signals genuine reform—or doubles down on the same ideological commitments that triggered this reckoning.
