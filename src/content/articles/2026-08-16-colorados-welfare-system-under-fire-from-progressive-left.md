# Colorado Faces Pressure to Expand Welfare as Work Requirements Face National Assault

Colorado's welfare system—built on the 1996 reform principle that work matters—is under renewed attack from progressives who want to weaken employment incentives and expand government dependency.

National Review reports that despite welfare reform's documented success in moving Americans toward self-sufficiency, the left continues targeting work requirements and time limits. The Daily Wire notes that current welfare programs have created perverse incentives: work is discouraged, families remain trapped in poverty cycles, and taxpayers foot an ever-growing bill.

Conservatives argue that welfare's purpose should be temporary relief for genuine hardship, not a permanent lifestyle. Work requirements and benefit limits have proven effective at breaking dependency and restoring dignity through employment. Progressives counter that safety nets should be more generous and less conditional, framing work requirements as punitive.

For Colorado, this matters directly. State lawmakers face pressure to loosen work rules and expand benefits—moves that would increase state spending, raise taxes on working families, and potentially trap more Coloradans in government dependence rather than encouraging the self-reliance that built this state.

Watch for legislative proposals in Denver this session that would weaken Colorado's work-requirement standards or expand eligibility without employment conditions.
