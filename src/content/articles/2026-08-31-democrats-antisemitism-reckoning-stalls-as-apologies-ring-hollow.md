# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Michigan Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abdul El-Sayed met with the state's Democratic Jewish Caucus to apologize for past remarks, but Republicans say the gesture rings hollow. Mike Rogers, his GOP opponent, called it a "sham apology" (Fox News), while the Washington Examiner notes that despite media claims Democrats have "distanced themselves" from streamer Hasan Piker—who has made antisemitic remarks and expressed anti-American views—only six Democrats have explicitly condemned his latest statements blaming Jews for potential violence against them.

The controversy exposes a deeper problem: the Democratic Party's struggle to police antisemitism within its ranks without alienating its progressive base. El-Sayed's refusal to fully dump Piker suggests where his political priorities lie.

**Colorado angle:** While this plays out in Michigan, Colorado Democrats face similar pressure. The state's progressive wing has increasingly embraced identity-politics rhetoric that can veer into antisemitic tropes. Colorado voters should ask their Democratic representatives: Will they clearly condemn antisemitism, or will they hedge their bets to keep their activist base happy?
