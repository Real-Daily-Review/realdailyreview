# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Hunting Rights Amendment Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary to just two candidates through the party assembly process, signaling a sharper focus heading into the general election cycle (Complete Colorado). Meanwhile, Colorado voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a constitutional right to hunt and fish—a measure that requires 55 percent supermajority approval to amend the state Constitution (Complete Colorado).

The hunting and fishing amendment reflects conservative priorities around outdoor heritage and property rights. Supporters argue that codifying these rights protects Colorado's hunting and angling traditions against future regulatory encroachment by state agencies or environmental restrictions. The supermajority threshold means the measure faces a genuine test of voter support, not just a simple majority.

Republicans' streamlined primary field suggests the party is consolidating behind fewer candidates after the assembly eliminated 16 of 18 contenders—a disciplined approach to avoid a fractured primary that could weaken the eventual nominee against Democratic incumbent Jared Polis.

**Watch:** Whether the hunting and fishing amendment clears the 55 percent bar will signal how Colorado voters view constitutional protections for outdoor recreation and land-use rights—a bellwether for conservative ballot initiatives heading into 2026.
