# Mistrial in Clancy Case Exposes Jury System Vulnerabilities as Holdout Jurors Prioritize Mental Health Activism Over Justice

A Massachusetts mistrial in the Lindsay Clancy infanticide case has laid bare how activist jurors can derail criminal accountability—a cautionary tale for Colorado courts and citizens concerned about jury integrity.

Clancy was accused of killing her three young children. The jury deadlocked after holdout jurors, including Michael P. Desronvil and Paula Devlin, pushed a not-criminally-responsible verdict based on postpartum mental health claims. Desronvil told the New York Post he "didn't have any doubts" about his position, while Devlin laughed during an NBC 10 interview when noting the trial had succeeded in raising awareness about postpartum issues—suggesting the jury's focus had shifted from guilt or innocence to social messaging (Daily Wire).

**The conservative concern:** Jurors are supposed to apply law and evidence, not advance policy agendas or mental health activism. When jurors prioritize a cause over their oath, defendants escape accountability and victims' families are denied justice. This undermines the rule of law.

Colorado juries face similar pressures. Prosecutors and defense attorneys must remain vigilant during jury selection to identify ideologically-driven jurors who view trials as platforms for social change rather than fact-finding bodies.
