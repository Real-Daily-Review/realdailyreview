# New York's Rent Freeze Shows Why Price Controls Always Backfire

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's rent freeze is a textbook lesson in how progressive price controls destroy the very housing market they claim to fix. With roughly half of NYC's rental apartments already rent-stabilized, Mamdani's freeze shifts costs onto landlords and reduces incentives for new construction—leaving renters worse off, not better (Washington Examiner).

The policy exemplifies the left's faith in government mandates over market reality. By freezing rents on stabilized units, the city artificially suppresses supply and discourages investment in housing stock. Landlords facing frozen revenues cut maintenance, defer repairs, and abandon marginal properties. Meanwhile, renters in the *other* half of the market—those not protected by rent control—face higher prices as demand concentrates there.

Republicans and conservatives argue that rent control is economic theater: it feels good politically but solves nothing. The real solution is removing zoning restrictions, cutting red tape, and letting developers build. More supply, not government price-fixing, brings rents down.

Mamdani also drew mockery for ordering New Yorkers to set thermostats to 78 degrees during a heat wave—collectivist micromanagement that ignores individual choice and comfort (Daily Wire).

**Colorado angle:** Denver and Boulder progressives have flirted with similar rent-control schemes. Coloradans should watch whether state lawmakers preempt local rent-control bans before they take root here.
