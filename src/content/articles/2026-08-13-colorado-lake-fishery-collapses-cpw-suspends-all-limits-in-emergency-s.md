# Colorado Lake Faces Fishery Collapse as Drought Deepens Water Crisis

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has suspended all fishing limits at McKay Lake in Westminster—a stark admission that drought conditions are killing the fishery faster than regulation can manage it. CPW is now urging anglers to participate in an emergency public salvage, essentially asking Coloradans to catch and remove as many fish as possible before the lake becomes uninhabitable.

This is what happens when years of water mismanagement and over-allocation meet Western drought reality. McKay Lake's collapse underscores a hard truth: Colorado's water crisis isn't theoretical anymore. It's destroying recreational opportunities and threatening the ecosystems that depend on adequate flows.

Conservatives have long warned that Colorado's Byzantine water-rights system, combined with interstate compacts that prioritize downstream states over Colorado's own needs, leaves the state vulnerable to exactly this scenario. Meanwhile, environmental regulations often prevent the kind of aggressive water management—dam operations, reservoir maintenance, and sensible allocation—that could mitigate these emergencies.

The suspension signals CPW's recognition that normal rules no longer apply when a fishery is dying. But this is a band-aid on a much larger wound: Colorado's water supply is under structural stress, and bureaucratic tinkering won't fix it.

Watch whether state lawmakers push for genuine water-infrastructure investment and interstate compact renegotiation, or continue hiding behind environmental restrictions while lakes dry up.
