# Michigan Democrat's Cross-Party Endorsement Exposes Deep Party Rifts

A Michigan Democratic congresswoman's endorsement of a Republican Senate candidate over her party's nominee reveals growing fractures within the Democratic coalition—and signals that moderate Democrats are increasingly willing to break ranks when they view their own party's direction as untenable.

Rep. Haley Stevens (D-MI) publicly backed Republican Mike Rogers instead of Democrat Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan's Senate race, a move that drew fire from party leadership but reflects what *National Review* reports are "serious reservations" about El-Sayed among Democrats themselves. Stevens doubled down on her decision after El-Sayed's victory, telling the *Washington Examiner* she doesn't regret the endorsement despite its consequences.

**The conservative takeaway:** This isn't just Michigan infighting. It demonstrates that even Democrats recognize when their party nominates candidates too far left for general-election viability. Stevens' willingness to endorse across party lines—and her refusal to apologize—suggests moderate Democrats are prioritizing principle over party loyalty, a dynamic Republicans can exploit in 2026 and beyond.

For Colorado Republicans, the lesson is clear: highlight Democratic divisions on spending, energy policy, and regulatory overreach. Moderate Democrats' hesitation about their own nominees creates openings.

Watch whether other moderate Democrats follow Stevens' example in upcoming races, or whether party pressure forces them back in line.
