# Big Pharma's Patent Monopoly Is Squeezing Colorado Paychecks and Business Growth

Pharmaceutical patent protections are inflating healthcare costs so aggressively that U.S. employers are forced to redirect wages and investment dollars toward insurance premiums instead of employee raises and business expansion, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The problem hits Colorado workers and employers hard. As healthcare costs surge, Colorado businesses—from tech firms in Boulder to manufacturers along the Front Range—face mounting pressure to absorb premium increases. That means less money for wage growth, hiring, and capital investment that drives economic opportunity.

Families already stretched thin on affordability are forced into impossible choices about which care they can access. The *Examiner* frames this as a direct result of Big Pharma's monopolistic patent regime, which artificially inflates drug prices and cascades through the entire healthcare system.

**The conservative case:** Free-market reformers argue that patent monopolies distort competition and protect inefficient pricing. They contend that targeted patent reform—not government price controls—could restore market discipline while preserving innovation incentives. Republicans generally oppose heavy-handed regulation but support measures that increase transparency and competition in pharmaceutical markets.

The question for Colorado policymakers: Will they pursue market-based solutions to bring down costs, or follow the left's instinct toward price controls and government mandates that could chill innovation?
