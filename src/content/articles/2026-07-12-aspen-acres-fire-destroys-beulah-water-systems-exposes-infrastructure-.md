# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Colorado Town

The Aspen Acres fire has devastated Beulah's water systems, forcing residents and local officials into a costly recovery that underscores how Colorado's aging infrastructure remains vulnerable to natural disaster—and how quickly government-dependent communities face crisis when that infrastructure fails.

The fire roared through the small town of roughly 800 residents, destroying homes and, critically, water utilities that serve the community (Colorado Sun). Water managers are now scrambling to restore clean water access, a process officials acknowledge will take months (CPR News).

The damage raises hard questions for Colorado taxpayers: Who bears the cost of rebuilding? Will state or federal disaster funds cover it, or will local ratepayers foot the bill through higher water rates? And why, in a state where water is life, do so many rural communities operate on infrastructure that can be wiped out in hours?

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that over-reliance on government services—without adequate local preparedness and private-sector resilience—leaves communities exposed. The Beulah crisis illustrates that point sharply. While most other active Colorado fires missed critical water systems, this one didn't, and luck won't rebuild pipes.

Watch for how state and federal disaster declarations allocate recovery funds—and whether Beulah's residents will see real accountability for infrastructure readiness.
