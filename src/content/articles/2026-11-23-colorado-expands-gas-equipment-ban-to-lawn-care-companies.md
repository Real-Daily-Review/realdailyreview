# Colorado Regulators Expand Gas-Equipment Ban to Lawn Care—Another Burden on Small Business

Colorado regulators are now mandating that Front Range lawn care companies phase out gas-powered equipment during summer months, extending the state's green-energy agenda into yet another corner of the private sector.

The new rules, reported by the *Colorado Sun* and *CPR News*, target commercial mowing, trimming, and blowing services as part of an effort to reduce ozone pollution. Regulators cite data showing the lawn and garden sector accounts for 13% of Front Range ozone pollution, according to *CPR News*.

Republicans and conservatives argue the mandate imposes steep costs on small landscaping businesses already squeezed by labor shortages and inflation. Switching fleets to electric equipment requires capital investment many contractors can't easily absorb—and battery-powered tools often lack the runtime and power of gas models for full workdays. Democrats counter that ozone pollution harms public health and that the phased approach gives businesses time to adapt.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this is another example of top-down regulation prioritizing environmental mandates over economic reality. The lawn care industry employs thousands across the Front Range; forced equipment transitions will likely raise costs for homeowners and commercial clients.

Watch whether the state expands these restrictions to other equipment categories—or whether small-business pressure forces regulators to extend compliance timelines.
