# Federal Raid on Rep. Omar's Home Reveals Constitutional Concerns Over DOJ Overreach

A federal judge warned the Justice Department that its search warrant for Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington, D.C. home could violate the Speech and Debate Clause before agents seized her phone and passport, according to unsealed court documents reported by Fox News.

The raid, conducted by Homeland Security Investigations, targeted the Minnesota Democrat's residence as part of a decades-long investigation. Notably, the House general counsel refused involvement in the operation, per the Washington Examiner, signaling institutional hesitation about the DOJ's approach.

**The conservative concern:** Republicans and constitutional watchdogs argue the raid exemplifies federal overreach against a sitting member of Congress. A federal judge's pre-raid warning about potential constitutional violations underscores the troubling precedent—using law enforcement as a political cudgel against political opponents, regardless of party affiliation.

The Speech and Debate Clause protects lawmakers from prosecution for legislative acts. If the DOJ proceeded despite judicial warnings, it raises hard questions about whether the Biden-era Justice Department weaponized federal power against political adversaries.

**Colorado angle:** While Omar represents Minnesota, this case matters to Colorado conservatives concerned about federal agencies operating without meaningful restraint. The precedent affects how DOJ power is wielded against any political figure.

Watch whether House Republicans demand a full accounting of DOJ decision-making and whether similar constitutional protections are applied consistently across party lines.
