# FAA Probes Near-Miss at Boston Logan as Safety Questions Mount

The Federal Aviation Administration is investigating a close call Saturday morning at Boston Logan International Airport, where a Delta flight executing a go-around narrowly avoided an American Airlines plane departing from an intersecting runway, according to 9News Denver and the New York Post.

The incident underscores growing concerns about air traffic control operations and runway safety protocols at one of the nation's busiest hubs. While both aircraft were separated before any collision occurred, the near-miss raises questions about whether current FAA staffing and procedures are adequate to prevent catastrophic failures.

For Colorado travelers—who depend on reliable connections through major East Coast hubs—this incident is a reminder that federal aviation safety cannot be taken for granted. The FAA has faced criticism in recent years over controller shortages and aging infrastructure, issues that demand serious attention regardless of political affiliation.

Republicans and safety advocates argue the FAA must prioritize modernization and adequate staffing over bureaucratic expansion. The agency should focus resources on core safety missions rather than regulatory mission creep.

Watch for the FAA's formal investigation findings and whether Congress demands accountability for any systemic failures in air traffic management.
