# Massive Aspen Acres Fire Forces Evacuations Across Southern Colorado

Colorado's wildfire season is hitting hard: the Aspen Acres fire has scorched 35,000 acres across Pueblo and Custer counties, forcing new evacuation orders in Rye, Beulah, San Isabel, and surrounding communities (Colorado Sun). Authorities have mobilized roughly 650 firefighters to battle the blaze, which sits just 2 miles from the popular Bishop Castle roadside attraction and threatens the unincorporated community of Wetmore (CPR News, 9News Denver).

The fire underscores a persistent Colorado problem: wildfire management on federal and state lands. While resources are being deployed aggressively, conservatives have long argued that decades of federal forest mismanagement—excessive regulation, environmental litigation blocking thinning projects, and fuel-load accumulation—have made Colorado's forests tinderboxes. Proper forest stewardship, not just firefighting budgets, prevents catastrophes like this.

Residents in affected areas face real hardship: evacuations disrupt livelihoods, property sits at risk, and firefighting costs mount. The state's ability to respond reflects years of budget allocation to emergency services, but the root issue remains forest health policy at the federal level.

Watch whether state and federal officials acknowledge the role of forest management failures in enabling this fire's rapid spread—or whether they simply treat it as a weather problem requiring more spending.
