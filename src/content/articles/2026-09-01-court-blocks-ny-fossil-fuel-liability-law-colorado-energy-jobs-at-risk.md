# Court Blocks New York's Fossil Fuel Liability Grab—A Win for Energy Industry and Colorado Workers

A court has struck down New York's 2024 law that would have made fossil fuel companies liable for climate change damages, dealing a significant blow to Democratic efforts to weaponize the courts against the energy sector (Just The News).

The law represented a coordinated progressive strategy: use state litigation to impose massive financial penalties on oil, gas, and coal companies without going through legislatures. Democrats argue climate change demands aggressive accountability; Republicans counter that such laws are backdoor carbon taxes that punish energy producers, drive up costs for consumers, and devastate energy-dependent economies.

**The Colorado angle matters here.** Colorado's oil and gas industry supports thousands of jobs and generates substantial state revenue. If similar liability schemes spread to blue states bordering Colorado or gain traction in the legislature, they could create legal and financial pressure on producers operating across state lines—ultimately raising energy costs for Colorado families and threatening the livelihoods of workers in energy-rich regions like the Western Slope.

Meanwhile, a new study cited by 9News suggests El Niño events have intensified over 40 years due to climate change. Conservatives note that weather patterns are complex; blaming every weather event on human activity while pursuing economically destructive policies is poor science and worse policy.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats attempt similar fossil fuel liability legislation in the 2025 session.**
