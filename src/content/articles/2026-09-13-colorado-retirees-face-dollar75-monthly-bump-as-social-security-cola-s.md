# Social Security COLA Bump Signals Inflation Pressure on Colorado Retirees

Colorado seniors could see Social Security checks rise by roughly $75 monthly in 2027 if AARP's estimate of a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) materializes—a sign that inflation remains a real burden on fixed-income households, even as Washington claims price pressures have eased.

The projected increase reflects what retirees already know: their dollars don't stretch as far. According to 9News Denver, the COLA adjustment would be the agency's response to rising living costs. Meanwhile, October 2026 Supplemental Security Income payments worth up to $994 are heading out to beneficiaries this month (Washington Examiner), underscoring the ongoing reliance on federal support for elderly and disabled Americans.

For Colorado conservatives, the picture is mixed. While higher COLA payments help seniors avoid poverty, they also highlight a deeper problem: government dependency and the erosion of purchasing power caused by years of federal spending and monetary expansion. Republicans argue that true relief requires controlling inflation through fiscal discipline—not simply adjusting benefit formulas after the fact.

The real question isn't whether seniors deserve adequate support; it's whether Washington will ever address the root cause of inflation rather than perpetually chasing it with benefit increases.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for spending restraint in the next budget cycle—or simply accepts higher COLAs as the new normal.
