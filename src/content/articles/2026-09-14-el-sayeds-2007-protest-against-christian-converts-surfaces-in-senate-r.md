# Democratic Senate Nominee's 2007 Protest Against Christian Converts Raises Questions About Judgment

A newly unearthed report reveals that Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed led a 2007 University of Michigan protest against former terrorists who had converted to Christianity, raising fresh questions about his judgment and priorities among Colorado voters watching the race.

According to Fox News and the New York Post, El-Sayed organized the protest against speakers described as "ex-terrorists" who had embraced Christian faith. El-Sayed's campaign spokesperson Cole Wozniak defended the action, claiming the speakers were "widely condemned," but offered no substantive explanation for why a future political figure would mobilize opposition to individuals who had renounced terrorism and found religion.

Republicans argue the incident reveals troubling priorities: protesting reformed extremists rather than celebrating their conversion away from violence. Conservatives question what motivated El-Sayed to organize opposition to people who had fundamentally changed their lives and beliefs—and what that says about his worldview heading into a Senate race.

The timing matters. As Democrats nationally grapple with perceptions of anti-religious bias and hostility toward faith communities, this 2007 episode feeds conservative concerns about the left's relationship with Christianity and redemption.

Colorado voters should watch whether mainstream media outlets covering the Senate race will press El-Sayed on the specifics of his objections and whether his campaign offers a fuller accounting of the incident.
