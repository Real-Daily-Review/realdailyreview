# Colorado Quietly Strips Official Party Recognition—A Win for Limited Government

Colorado has moved to separate state government from formal political party privileges, a rare display of constitutional restraint that conservatives say resets the proper relationship between government and partisan machinery.

According to Complete Colorado, the state has eliminated special recognition and privileges previously granted to political parties, a shift described as "courageous" by limited-government advocates. The move reflects a straightforward principle: government resources and official status should not subsidize partisan operations.

**Why this matters to Colorado taxpayers:** State-granted party privileges often translate to taxpayer-funded perks, preferential access, or regulatory advantages that benefit Democratic and Republican machines at public expense. By stripping these benefits, Colorado reduces the machinery through which government picks winners and losers based on party affiliation.

Conservatives argue this is exactly what the Constitution demands—a government neutral on its face, not in the business of empowering political parties. The state exists to serve citizens, not to fund or legitimize partisan infrastructure.

This stands in sharp contrast to progressive-led states that have expanded government entanglement with party structures, often under the guise of "voter engagement" or "civic participation."

**Watch next:** Whether other Colorado agencies attempt to restore party privileges through the back door—via grants, advisory boards, or regulatory carve-outs.
