# Colorado Faces Cyclosporiasis Cases as Parasite Spreads Across U.S.

Colorado is reporting dozens of cyclosporiasis cases this year, though state health officials say they're not tied to the larger national outbreak affecting hundreds of people across multiple states.

The parasite, which causes severe gastrointestinal illness, has sickened people nationwide in recent weeks (KDVR Fox31). Colorado Sun reports the state's cases remain isolated and unlinked to those multistate clusters, suggesting local transmission or contaminated food sources specific to Colorado.

Health authorities haven't yet identified a common source in Colorado cases, leaving consumers and restaurants in a holding pattern. The outbreak underscores a persistent vulnerability in America's food supply chain—one that neither state nor federal regulators have fully solved despite years of warnings from industry and consumer advocates.

For Colorado families and businesses, the immediate concern is clear: know the symptoms (severe diarrhea, nausea, fatigue), practice food safety, and demand transparency from retailers and restaurants about produce sourcing. The state health department should accelerate its investigation to pinpoint the source and prevent further spread.

Watch whether Colorado's response exposes gaps in FDA oversight and state food-safety enforcement—and whether regulators finally hold suppliers accountable.
