# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field at Assembly

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their crowded gubernatorial primary field down to just two candidates through the party assembly process, a sharp filtering mechanism that bypassed the broader primary ballot entirely. Out of 18 total Republican gubernatorial hopefuls, only two advanced past the assembly threshold, according to *Complete Colorado*.

The assembly system—where party delegates rather than all primary voters select nominees—reflects conservatives' preference for a more ideologically aligned vetting process. Republicans argue the method ensures nominees reflect core party principles and avoids fractured general-election matchups. Democrats, by contrast, favor open primaries that allow any registered voter to participate.

For Colorado conservatives, the outcome matters: the two assembly-approved candidates will face the Democratic nominee in a state that has trended blue in recent cycles. Whoever emerges will need to appeal to independent voters in Denver suburbs and mountain counties while energizing the GOP base in El Paso and rural Colorado.

The assembly process also signals Republican confidence in grassroots organization heading into 2026—a year when control of the governor's mansion and state legislature will shape Colorado's regulatory environment, tax policy, and energy development for years to come.

Watch whether the two finalists can unify the broader Republican coalition and articulate a clear contrast with Democratic incumbents on taxes, government overreach, and education.
