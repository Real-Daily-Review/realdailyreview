# Trump's 2020 Election Claims Risk GOP's Fall Agenda—and Colorado's China Trade

President Trump's continued focus on 2020 election grievances threatens to overshadow Republican messaging heading into the midterms, even as declassified intelligence reveals China's alleged efforts to manipulate U.S. media against him during that cycle.

A declassified CIA note obtained by Breitbart shows the Chinese government sought to pay American journalists to publish negative stories about Trump in 2019 to influence the 2020 election—a genuine foreign interference concern. Yet Trump's repeated invocation of "stolen election" claims, which remain widely unpopular with voters, complicates the GOP's ability to focus on kitchen-table issues that matter to Colorado families: inflation, energy policy, and border security.

National Review warns that relitigating 2020 won't help Republicans this fall, while the Washington Examiner reports Trump's election-integrity rhetoric has already reignited U.S.-China tensions ahead of a planned Xi Jinping summit—potentially jeopardizing trade negotiations that affect Colorado agriculture and energy exports.

Republicans argue the focus should pivot to forward-looking priorities: securing elections going forward, holding the Biden administration accountable on inflation and regulatory overreach, and protecting American interests in competition with China. Conservatives worry that dwelling on past grievances dilutes the party's message on issues where voters actually hold Democrats accountable.

**Watch whether Trump's messaging discipline holds through the fall campaign—or whether Colorado Republicans distance themselves from 2020 rhetoric to protect vulnerable seats.**
