# Australian Women Charged With ISIS-Linked Crimes Against Humanity

Three Australian women who returned from Syria have been charged in connection with Islamic State activities, according to the BBC. Two face crimes-against-humanity charges linked to ISIS, while a third was charged with joining a terrorist organization.

Janai Safar, 32, was arrested at Sydney airport Thursday evening and appeared before New South Wales bail court Friday, where she was refused bail, according to The Guardian. Her lawyer raised concerns about the decision's impact on her family connections. Two other women facing separate charges are expected to apply for bail in Melbourne on Monday, the BBC reports.

The charges mark an escalation in Australia's legal response to citizens who traveled to Syria to support or join ISIS during the group's territorial control of parts of the country. Australian authorities have previously grappled with how to prosecute returnees, balancing security concerns against legal standards for evidence and due process.

Watch for bail hearings in Melbourne early next week and any statements from Australian prosecutors on the specific allegations against the women.
