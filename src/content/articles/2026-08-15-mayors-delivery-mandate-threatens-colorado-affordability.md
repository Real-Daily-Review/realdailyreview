# Mayor's Delivery Mandate Threatens Colorado Affordability Ahead of Midterms

A mayor's push for one-size-fits-all delivery driver regulations risks making online shopping more expensive for Colorado families already squeezed by inflation.

The proposal, endorsed by the mayor, would impose a uniform employer mandate on delivery services—a move conservatives argue will drive up costs for consumers and reduce competition in the gig economy. National Review notes the plan amounts to a "one-employer-fits-all" regulatory straitjacket that ignores market realities.

Republicans counter that such mandates, while marketed as worker protections, ultimately harm the affordability crisis voters care most about heading into the midterms. The Washington Examiner warns the timing is particularly damaging: with elections looming and voters already concerned about price pressures, heavy-handed regulations that increase delivery costs send the wrong message about Republican commitment to keeping everyday expenses down.

Conservatives argue the real solution isn't government mandates but competitive markets—letting multiple delivery platforms compete freely drives innovation and keeps prices low for Colorado consumers. Imposing rigid employer requirements eliminates that flexibility and passes costs directly to shoppers.

The broader concern: as progressive cities experiment with gig-economy regulations, Colorado businesses and consumers face pressure to follow suit, potentially making the state less competitive and less affordable.
