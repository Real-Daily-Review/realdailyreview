# Denver Braces for Unseasonable October Heat Wave

Denver is about to sweat through an October that feels more like midsummer, with temperatures approaching or matching records across the Front Range this week, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31.

Mainly sunny skies will push highs near record territory Monday and sustain warm conditions throughout the week, with meteorologists noting the temperatures will carry "more of a July and August feel" despite the calendar turning to fall (9News Denver). The Front Range will bear the brunt of the heat dome.

For Colorado taxpayers and families, this means higher cooling costs heading into what should be the season of falling utility bills. It also underscores the reality that Colorado's weather remains variable and sometimes extreme—a reminder that energy reliability and affordable power matter year-round, not just in winter.

Conservatives have long argued that Colorado's energy independence depends on diverse, reliable sources including natural gas and coal-fired generation. Premature retirement of baseload power plants in pursuit of aggressive renewable mandates could leave the state vulnerable to exactly these kinds of demand spikes when temperatures spike unexpectedly.

Watch whether this heat wave strains Colorado's grid and whether state energy regulators acknowledge the need for dispatchable power sources alongside renewables.
