# Colorado Springs AI Center Stalled; Proposition 137 Draws Fire from Taxpayer Advocates

Colorado Springs city council is playing politics with an AI center proposal, postponing a civility vote until after the election rather than deciding on the merits now—a classic move when officials want to dodge accountability (CPR News).

Meanwhile, conservative critics are sounding the alarm on Proposition 137, the statewide ballot measure that would funnel taxpayer dollars to wealthy environmental nonprofits. Complete Colorado reports that The Nature Conservancy—the proposition's main sponsor—stands to benefit directly, raising questions about whether this is genuine conservation or a subsidy scheme for well-heeled NGOs masquerading as grassroots environmentalism.

Republicans and taxpayer advocates argue Prop 137 represents exactly the kind of government overreach Coloradans should reject: using the ballot to lock in permanent spending for politically connected organizations. Rather than letting markets and private landowners manage conservation, the measure would create another avenue for government to redistribute wealth to the activist left.

The Colorado Springs council's delay tactic on the AI center signals the same pattern—decisions deferred, accountability avoided, elections prioritized over transparency.

**Watch for:** whether Colorado voters reject Prop 137 in November, and whether the Springs council finally votes on the AI center or keeps kicking the can.
