# Data Center Boom Hits Speed Bump as Communities Demand Accountability

Even Trump's home county is pumping the brakes on the AI data center gold rush, signaling that communities nationwide—including Colorado—won't rubber-stamp tech infrastructure without answers on costs and consequences.

Palm Beach County passed a one-year moratorium on new data centers, citing environmental impact, noise pollution, and skyrocketing electric bills (Fox News). The move reflects growing voter skepticism about whether rapid AI facility expansion serves local residents or just corporate bottom lines.

Congressional candidate Stephen Wagley told Breitbart that voters' concerns are "very valid" and that officials owe communities transparency about the real costs of hosting these power-hungry operations.

**The conservative case:** Data centers promise jobs and tax revenue, but not if they drive up electricity costs for working families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation. Colorado has seen similar pressure as tech companies eye our abundant water and cheap power. Conservatives argue that local control and environmental stewardship aren't anti-business—they're pro-*sustainable* business. Communities deserve honest accounting of infrastructure strain before signing away decades of resources.

**What to watch:** Whether Colorado counties and municipalities demand similar impact assessments and cost-sharing agreements before approving new data center projects in their jurisdictions.
