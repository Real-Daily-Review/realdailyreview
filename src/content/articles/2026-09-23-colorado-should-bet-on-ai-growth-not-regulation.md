# Colorado Should Embrace AI Growth, Not Strangle It With Regulation

Colorado has a chance to lead the artificial intelligence economy—but only if state lawmakers resist the urge to regulate the industry into irrelevance.

President Trump declared this week that the U.S. will rebrand artificial intelligence as "super intelligence" and reject international schemes to control its development (Breitbart). That's the right instinct. Meanwhile, a new Magellan Strategies survey shows Colorado voters are ready to move past AI panic and focus on economic opportunity (Complete Colorado).

The conservative case is straightforward: AI and data centers represent massive job creation, tax revenue, and competitive advantage for Colorado. Heavy-handed regulation—the kind progressives favor—will only drive investment to friendlier states. Democrats typically push for government oversight of emerging tech, citing vague "safety" and "equity" concerns. But those arguments ignore the real cost: lost opportunity for Colorado workers and businesses.

Colorado's energy infrastructure, workforce, and business climate make the state uniquely positioned to attract AI investment. But that advantage evaporates if the state legislature treats the industry as a threat rather than an engine of growth.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats push for AI regulation in the 2027 legislative session—and whether business groups and Republicans push back hard enough to stop it.
