# Colorado's Primary Problem: Unaffiliateds and Progressive Money Are Reshaping State Elections

Colorado's primary system is quietly handing control of the state to the most ideologically committed—and best-funded—activists, while millions of registered Republicans and Democrats sit on the sidelines.

According to Complete Colorado, unaffiliated voters are rising in Colorado, but the real power lies with those who show up to caucuses. Party-registered voters who skip the process simply cede their voice to primary participants, who tend to be more ideologically extreme than general-election voters.

That dynamic played out starkly in recent Democratic primaries. The Denver Post reports that progressive statehouse candidates swept several metro Denver contests, unseating two sitting lawmakers despite "hefty dark-money spending" in opposition. Translation: left-wing groups and donors outmaneuvered establishment Democrats and their outside allies.

**The Colorado angle:** When primaries determine winners in safe Democratic districts—which increasingly cover the Front Range—general elections become irrelevant. Republicans and moderate Democrats lose leverage. Progressives, energized and organized, set the agenda for taxes, regulation, and gun control.

**The conservative case:** Republicans must either boost primary turnout or watch Colorado's legislature drift further left. Unaffiliated voters, now a plurality statewide, have no dog in party primaries—a structural weakness conservatives should exploit.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans invest in caucus organization and primary messaging before 2026 general-election season arrives.
