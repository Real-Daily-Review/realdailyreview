# Senate Passes Trump-Backed Housing Bill—But Colorado Families Should Ask What Washington Really Changed

The Senate overwhelmingly passed the 21st Century Road to Housing Act on a bipartisan 85-5 vote, sending Trump-backed legislation to the House that aims to prevent Wall Street investors from monopolizing single-family home purchases (Fox News, New York Post). The bill targets institutional investors buying up residential properties, a practice conservatives argue has priced ordinary Americans—including Coloradans—out of homeownership.

Republicans frame this as a pro-homeowner measure addressing a genuine market distortion: mega-funds and private equity firms have systematically acquired starter homes, converting them to rentals and inflating prices. The legislation would restrict such purchases, theoretically freeing inventory for owner-occupants.

However, conservatives should scrutinize whether federal intervention actually solves the problem or creates new ones. The real culprit behind Colorado's housing crunch isn't just investor buying—it's zoning restrictions, permitting delays, and local government red tape that choke supply. A Washington-centered fix risks missing the mark while expanding federal authority over real estate markets.

The House must still approve the Senate version (Just The News). Coloradans should watch whether this bill meaningfully addresses supply-side barriers or merely reshuffles deck chairs while leaving the regulatory stranglehold intact.
