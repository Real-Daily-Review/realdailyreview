# Data Center Boom Hits Local Resistance Wall; Colorado Should Take Note

Tech companies banking on AI data center expansion are facing a grassroots backlash that's grinding thousands of projects to a halt, and Colorado could be next. Texas Gov. Greg Abbott (R) recently declared that data center developers "dug their own grave" by failing to engage local communities before breaking ground, prompting him to impose a moratorium on new projects until state regulators audit existing sites (Washington Examiner, Breitbart).

The problem is real: National Review reports "vast quantities of ink have been expended" trying to address public anxiety about data centers, yet "none of it seems to matter." The Daily Caller documents how the backlash has brought "thousands of projects to a screeching halt" nationwide.

**The Colorado angle matters here.** Data centers consume enormous amounts of water and electricity—both critical resources in water-scarce Colorado. If tech companies don't build local consensus before proposing massive facilities, Colorado counties and municipalities could block projects that would otherwise generate tax revenue and jobs for rural communities.

Abbott's lesson is clear: companies can't steamroll communities. They need buy-in from local officials and residents upfront, or face years of regulatory delays and political opposition.

**Watch whether Colorado's county commissioners and water boards demand similar community engagement protocols before approving data center permits.**
