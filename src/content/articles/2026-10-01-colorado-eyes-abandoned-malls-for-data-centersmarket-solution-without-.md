# Data Centers in Abandoned Malls: A Conservative Case for Adaptive Reuse Over Regulation

Colorado and other states are eyeing abandoned shopping malls as potential sites for data centers—a market-driven solution that could revitalize dead commercial real estate without government subsidy or new construction sprawl.

The Daily Wire reports that converting vacant malls into data center hubs makes economic sense: existing infrastructure, available power, and ready-made buildings eliminate wasteful new development. The Washington Examiner's "Daily on Energy" column highlights momentum behind this adaptive reuse model, noting it sidesteps the typical regulatory gauntlet that slows infrastructure projects.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Let property owners and tech companies negotiate deals without bureaucratic interference. No tax breaks needed. No environmental impact statements for buildings already standing. No zoning fights. The market identifies the problem (dead malls) and the solution (data centers) faster than any government planning committee.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. The state's energy grid and water resources face pressure from rapid growth. Data centers demand reliable power, but repurposing existing structures beats greenfield development that triggers environmental reviews and community opposition.

**Watch whether Colorado regulators resist the trend or embrace it**—the answer will reveal whether state and local officials trust market solutions or default to the permitting-and-study playbook that kills projects.
