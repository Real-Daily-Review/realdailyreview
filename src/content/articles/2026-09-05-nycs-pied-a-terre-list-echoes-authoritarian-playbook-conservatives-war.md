# NYC Mayor's Pied-à-Terre List Signals Dangerous Government Overreach

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's plan to publicly list second-home owners signals the kind of government targeting that historically precedes authoritarian crackdowns, conservatives warn.

Mamdani, a progressive Democrat, has proposed compiling a registry of pied-à-terre owners as part of a rent-control scheme. The Washington Examiner's Emzari Gelashvili drew a stark historical parallel: such proscription lists have preceded democratic collapse, comparing Mamdani's approach to Stalin's targeting of kulaks before the Holodomor.

Beyond the chilling precedent, the policy itself is economically flawed. National Review notes that 80 years of economic history demonstrate rent ceilings consistently produce housing shortages—the opposite of Mamdani's stated goal. When government artificially caps prices, landlords reduce supply, renovation, and maintenance, worsening affordability.

**The Colorado angle:** While Denver and Boulder have flirted with rent-control proposals, conservatives should recognize this New York experiment as a cautionary tale. Government-compiled lists of property owners—ostensibly for "fairness"—create infrastructure for confiscation and political retaliation.

Republicans argue that expanding housing supply through deregulation and market competition solves affordability far better than price controls and surveillance. The left counters that second-home ownership exacerbates shortages, justifying intervention.
