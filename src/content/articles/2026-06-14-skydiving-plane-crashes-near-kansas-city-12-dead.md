# Skydiving Plane Crashes Near Kansas City; 12 Dead

A twin-engine aircraft carrying skydivers crashed in flames near Butler, Missouri, on Sunday morning, killing all 12 people aboard—11 jumpers and the pilot.

The Missouri State Highway Patrol responded to the scene around 11:30 a.m. after reports of a burning aircraft near Butler Memorial Airport. First responders found the plane engulfed in flames. The cause of the crash remains under investigation.

This tragedy underscores the inherent risks of extreme sports and raises questions about aircraft maintenance, pilot certification, and operator safety protocols—areas where federal oversight through the FAA plays a critical role. While conservatives generally oppose regulatory overreach, aviation safety standards exist for sound reasons rooted in protecting lives.

The identities of the victims have not yet been released. The National Transportation Safety Board is expected to conduct a full investigation into what caused the crash.

Colorado skydiving operations, which operate under the same FAA framework, will likely face renewed scrutiny from customers and insurers in the coming weeks. Taxpayers and operators should monitor whether this incident triggers calls for additional federal regulations that could burden small aviation businesses without meaningfully improving safety outcomes.
