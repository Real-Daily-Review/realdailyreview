# Denver Post Owner Settles $13.5M Rent Debt with City, Exits Downtown Building

The Denver Post's parent company has agreed to pay the city $13.5 million to settle years of unpaid rent and will vacate its iconic downtown headquarters by June 30—a stark reminder that even legacy media outlets aren't immune to financial reality when government landlords finally collect what they're owed.

According to the Colorado Sun, the newspaper's lease was originally set to run through 2029, but the settlement effectively ends the arrangement early. The Post's departure marks another shift in Denver's media landscape and raises questions about the sustainability of traditional newsroom operations in an era of declining print revenue.

The settlement itself is straightforward: the company pays what it owes, removes its sign, and leaves. No special favors, no extended grace periods. That's how government contracts should work—consistently applied, regardless of the tenant's cultural prominence.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the lesson is clear: municipal real estate deals need teeth. When a private company falls behind on rent, eviction follows. Government should apply the same standard to all lessees, without exception or sentiment.

Watch whether Denver's city leadership uses this windfall responsibly or simply redirects it into new spending programs.
