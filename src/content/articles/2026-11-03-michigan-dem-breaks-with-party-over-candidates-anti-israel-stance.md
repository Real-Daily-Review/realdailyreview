# Michigan Democrat Breaks Ranks Over Candidate's Israel Stance

A prominent Michigan Democrat has publicly withdrawn support from a party-backed candidate over anti-Israel positions, exposing deep fractures within the Democratic coalition that conservatives say reveal the party's moral confusion.

Haley Stevens, a former Democratic Senate candidate, cited what she characterized as Abdul El-Sayed's "hatred of Israel" (National Review) as her reason for abandoning the endorsement. In a statement to the New York Post, Stevens said her decision "was not made for me by anyone else," and she criticized fellow Democrats' swift condemnation of her move as evidence that "something deeply troubling" is "missing in today's Democratic Party."

National Review reports that Stevens is far from alone—multiple Democrats harbor serious reservations about El-Sayed but have remained silent, suggesting the party's leadership is willing to tolerate anti-Israel rhetoric to maintain coalition unity.

Conservatives argue this episode exposes the Democratic Party's inability to reconcile its pro-Israel moderates with its increasingly vocal progressive wing hostile to Israeli interests. The willingness of party figures to pressure Stevens for breaking ranks, rather than address El-Sayed's positions directly, underscores what Republicans see as a fundamental loss of principle on the left.
