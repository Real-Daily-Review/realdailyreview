# Douglas County Moves to Regulate E-Scooters, Off-Highway Vehicles on Public Roads

Douglas County commissioners are pushing new regulations on e-scooters and off-highway vehicles after injury reports spike among riders. The proposed ordinance, which passed first reading Tuesday before the Board of Commissioners, would tighten rules on machines capable of reaching 75 mph on public roads, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver.

County officials and emergency physicians say existing regulations haven't kept pace with the technology. The Douglas County Sheriff's Office detailed enforcement concerns during the hearing.

**The conservative question:** Does Douglas County need another layer of regulation, or should liability rest with riders and property owners? Republicans typically argue that government rules often lag behind market realities—but they also recognize legitimate public safety concerns when vehicles designed for off-road use migrate to streets where they endanger pedestrians and drivers.

The ordinance reflects a real problem: riders on high-speed devices in areas with pedestrian traffic. But conservatives should scrutinize whether the new rules are narrowly tailored to safety or become another excuse for government to micromanage personal behavior and small business operations.

Watch whether Douglas County's ordinance becomes a template for Denver and other Front Range jurisdictions—and whether it includes reasonable exemptions for private property and rural use.
