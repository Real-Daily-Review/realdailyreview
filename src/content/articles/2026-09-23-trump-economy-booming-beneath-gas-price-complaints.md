# Trump Economy Outperforming Expectations Despite Gas Price Complaints

Colorado taxpayers and workers are benefiting from an economy that's firing on more cylinders than the media admits, even as pump prices sting wallets at the register.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, real gross domestic product grew at a 1.5% annualized rate in the second quarter—evidence that the underlying economic machinery is humming despite widespread frustration over gasoline costs. The disconnect matters: Americans can legitimately complain about energy prices while acknowledging that job creation, wage growth, and business activity are outpacing predictions from skeptics who declared the Trump administration's pro-growth policies would fail.

For Colorado specifically, a booming economy translates to stronger labor markets in energy, agriculture, and small business—sectors that depend on reasonable regulatory burdens and access to capital. Lower corporate tax rates and reduced red tape have freed up resources for expansion and hiring across the state.

Democrats and their media allies have largely ignored the positive economic data, preferring to hammer the administration on gas prices and inflation concerns. Republicans counter that energy markets are global, that price volatility reflects supply shocks beyond any president's control, and that the fundamentals—GDP growth, employment, wage gains—tell the real story.

Watch for how Colorado's energy sector responds if the administration continues rolling back Obama-era regulations on oil and gas development.
