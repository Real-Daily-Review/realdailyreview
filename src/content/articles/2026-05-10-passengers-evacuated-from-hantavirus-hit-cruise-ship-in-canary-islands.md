# Cruise Ship Hantavirus Outbreak: Passengers Evacuated as Global Risk Remains Low

Passengers aboard the MV Hondius cruise ship have begun evacuating after a hantavirus outbreak, with military and government planes transporting them home from the Canary Islands, according to PBS NewsHour and Al Jazeera.

Hantavirus typically spreads through contact with infected rodents, though person-to-person transmission has been documented in rare cases. The Guardian notes that Argentina documented the first person-to-person transmission three decades ago in Patagonia, with another outbreak nearly a decade later providing detailed evidence of human-to-human spread.

Health experts emphasize the current risk to the broader public remains limited. Ars Technica reports that "this is not COVID," citing low public risk levels, while prediction markets tracked by CNBC show slim odds of the outbreak becoming a matter of international concern.

Scientists cited by The Guardian have raised concerns about climate change potentially increasing global exposure to hantavirus, though the immediate outbreak appears contained to the cruise ship's passengers and crew.

Watch for updates on passenger health screenings and whether additional cases emerge among those evacuated.
