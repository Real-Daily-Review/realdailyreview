# Senate Advances Measure to End Iran Military Action as Talks Continue

The Senate is advancing legislation to end military operations in Iran, marking a congressional rebuke to the Trump administration as the conflict has exceeded the 60-day threshold mandated by the War Powers Act for presidential authorization, according to CNBC.

The measure comes as diplomatic efforts continue. Vice President JD Vance said the US has made "good progress" in Iran talks, according to Deutsche Welle, while simultaneously warning that the US is "locked and loaded" for military action if Iran refuses to agree to a nuclear deal, according to Al Jazeera.

The competing signals underscore tension between Congress and the administration over Iran policy. Lawmakers are moving to reassert their constitutional authority over military commitments, while the administration maintains it is pursuing both diplomatic and military options.

The Senate action does not necessarily reflect a unified position on Iran strategy—rather, it signals that some lawmakers believe the conflict has proceeded without proper congressional oversight and authorization. The administration's dual messaging suggests ongoing negotiations while preserving the threat of force.

Watch whether the Senate measure gains enough support to override a potential presidential veto, and whether Iran responds to the administration's diplomatic overtures.
