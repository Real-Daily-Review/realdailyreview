# Teens' Red Rocks Hike Ends in Costly Rescue—Another Reminder of Personal Responsibility

Two teenagers who decided to hike back to Morrison from a Red Rocks concert Wednesday ended up lost and requiring rescue by West Metro Fire Rescue, according to reports from 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31. What seemed like a good idea in the moment turned into a taxpayer-funded emergency response when the hikers couldn't find their way in the dark.

The incident underscores a growing problem: outdoor recreation decisions that sound adventurous often land on the tab of local fire and rescue services. While West Metro Fire Rescue handled the call professionally, Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for search-and-rescue operations that could be prevented through basic planning—knowing the terrain, carrying a map or GPS, understanding daylight windows, and having an exit strategy before heading into the foothills.

This isn't about blaming kids for a mistake; it's about the broader culture of assuming someone else will bail you out. Colorado's outdoor recreation is world-class, but it demands respect and preparation. Too many visitors and residents treat the mountains like an amusement park with a safety net.

Conservatives should watch whether local governments push for stricter permitting or fees for backcountry access—the progressive instinct is always to regulate and monetize rather than promote personal accountability.
