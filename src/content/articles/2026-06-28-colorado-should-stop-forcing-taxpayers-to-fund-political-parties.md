# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are bankrolling primary elections for political parties whether they belong to them or not—a government overreach that conservatives say has no place in a free state.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state government grants special recognition and privileges to political parties, effectively forcing non-members to subsidize partisan primaries. The outlet argues this represents an improper entanglement of state resources with party machinery.

The core conservative complaint is straightforward: Why should a Colorado taxpayer who is unaffiliated, Libertarian, or simply disengaged be forced to finance Democratic or Republican primary contests? *Complete Colorado* frames this as a question of principle—government should remain neutral on party affiliation, not use public funds to prop up partisan infrastructure.

Republicans and conservatives argue that ending this subsidy would align Colorado law with free-market principles and individual liberty. If parties want to hold primaries, they should fund them through member dues and voluntary donations, not tax dollars extracted from all Coloradans.

Democrats have not publicly articulated a defense of the current system in these sources, leaving the field to conservative critics.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado legislators move to eliminate taxpayer funding of party primaries or double down on the status quo.
