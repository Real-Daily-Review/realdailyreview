# Colorado Conservatives Should Demand Full Truth From Washington Democrats on Policy Claims

Rep. Ted Lieu (D-CA) exemplifies a broader problem in Washington: selective storytelling that omits inconvenient historical context. According to the *Washington Examiner*, Lieu's most effective political arguments aren't built on outright falsehoods—they're built on half-truths that conveniently ignore complications that undermine his conclusions.

This matters for Colorado. When national Democrats push policies affecting energy development, water rights, or gun regulations, they often deploy the same selective-history playbook: cherry-picked facts that support their narrative while burying context that reveals trade-offs or unintended consequences.

Conservatives argue that voters deserve the full picture, not just the convenient half. When a Democrat claims a regulation will create jobs without mentioning job losses in competing sectors, or touts renewable mandates without acknowledging grid reliability concerns, they're doing what Lieu does—telling a true fact in a false context.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses should demand that their representatives—and media—call out this tactic. Half-truths are how bad policy gets sold as common sense.

Watch for how Colorado's Democratic delegation frames upcoming votes on energy, water, and spending bills—and whether they acknowledge the full cost-benefit analysis or just the talking points.
