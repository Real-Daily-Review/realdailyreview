# Fort Morgan Cargill Plant Ends 70-Day Lockout With Tentative Deal

Colorado's beef processing sector—a pillar of the state's agriculture economy—gets relief after nearly 10 weeks of labor standoff at Cargill's Fort Morgan facility.

The 1,700 meatpackers locked out since May 20 have reached a tentative contract agreement with Cargill Meat Solutions, according to reporting from the Colorado Sun and 9News Denver. The deal, if ratified by union members, would end one of Colorado's longest recent labor disputes and restore production at a plant critical to the state's $7 billion beef industry.

Details of the agreement remain sparse, but the lockout itself underscores tensions between labor demands and operational costs in meat processing—an industry already squeezed by inflation, labor shortages, and supply-chain disruption. Conservatives have long argued that aggressive union wage demands, without corresponding productivity gains, risk pricing American beef out of global markets and accelerating automation that ultimately costs workers jobs.

The Fort Morgan plant's idleness has rippled through Colorado's ranching communities and downstream food producers who depend on reliable processing capacity. Any contract that restores production without crippling the plant's competitiveness is a win for the state's economy.

Watch whether the ratified deal's terms become public—and whether they signal a sustainable path forward for Colorado's beef processors or set a precedent that invites further labor friction.
