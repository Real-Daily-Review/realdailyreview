# Colorado High Court Blocks Democratic Gerrymandering Scheme

Colorado's Supreme Court has shut down a Democrat-backed redistricting plan that would have redrawn the state's congressional maps outside the normal decennial cycle—a power grab that conservatives say would have handed Democrats an unfair electoral advantage.

The ruling keeps Colorado from joining the national gerrymandering arms race, at least temporarily. Republicans argue that Democrats sought to manipulate district lines mid-decade to benefit their incumbents, a tactic conservatives view as naked partisan overreach dressed up in procedural language.

The Denver Post's editorial board, while criticizing gerrymandering broadly, notably condemned Texas Republicans for similar mid-cycle redistricting schemes—underscoring that gerrymandering is a bipartisan sin. However, conservatives point out that Colorado Democrats initiated this particular fight, and the state court was right to reject it.

The decision matters for Colorado taxpayers and voters: fair districts mean competitive races, accountability, and representation that actually reflects voter intent rather than partisan mapmaking. When politicians choose their voters instead of the reverse, government becomes less responsive to ordinary Coloradans.

Watch whether Democrats attempt another redistricting maneuver before 2030, or whether this ruling settles the question for the decade.
