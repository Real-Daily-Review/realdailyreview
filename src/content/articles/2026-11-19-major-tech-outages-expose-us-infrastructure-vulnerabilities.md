# Tech Outages Raise Questions About U.S. Infrastructure Resilience

Colorado businesses and residents are caught in a wave of major technology failures that should alarm anyone dependent on digital infrastructure—from Denver's airports to local banks and small employers relying on Microsoft services.

In just ten days, according to the Daily Wire, outages have crippled every U.S. airport, Apple Pay, Microsoft Outlook and Teams, JPMorgan Chase, and other critical systems. The Washington Examiner reports President Trump characterized the incidents as "individual events," urging the public to "relax."

**The conservative concern:** These cascading failures expose dangerous vulnerabilities in America's digital backbone. Whether the result of cyberattack, software failure, or regulatory neglect, the pattern demands answers. Republicans and conservatives argue that government has failed to adequately secure critical infrastructure—a core federal responsibility—while simultaneously imposing compliance burdens that distract private companies from actual security.

Colorado's economy depends on reliable tech: energy companies managing the grid, financial services, agriculture operations, and small businesses. When major platforms go dark, Colorado workers and taxpayers pay the price in lost productivity and eroded confidence.

**Watch next:** Demand transparency on whether these outages stem from foreign adversaries, negligent software updates, or systemic vulnerabilities—and whether the Biden-Trump transition exposed gaps in cybersecurity coordination.
