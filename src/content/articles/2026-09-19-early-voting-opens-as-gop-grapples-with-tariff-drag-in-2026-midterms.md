# Early Voting Opens as GOP Faces Midterm Headwinds Over Trump Tariffs

Early voting has begun in the 2026 midterm elections, with control of Congress hanging in the balance—Republicans currently hold the majority, but Democrats are positioned to compete for seats (9News Denver). The race comes as internal GOP tensions simmer over economic policy, particularly President Trump's tariff strategy.

The Washington Examiner's conservative critics argue that Trump's tariff regime—which the president has touted as economically successful—is actually weighing down Republican candidates. Trump claimed his tariffs produced "the most successful two years financially … in the history of the presidency," but right-leaning analysts warn the trade taxes are damaging GOP midterm prospects by raising costs for consumers and businesses.

For Colorado, the tariff debate carries real stakes. The state's agricultural sector, manufacturers, and energy producers all face exposure to retaliatory tariffs and higher input costs. Colorado Republicans must navigate voter concerns about inflation and household budgets while defending—or distancing themselves from—the Trump administration's trade policy.

Conservatives are split: some back Trump's protectionist approach as necessary to counter China and rebuild domestic industry; others argue tariffs function as hidden taxes that hurt working families and small business.

Watch whether Colorado GOP candidates embrace or distance themselves from Trump's tariff messaging as early voting accelerates.
