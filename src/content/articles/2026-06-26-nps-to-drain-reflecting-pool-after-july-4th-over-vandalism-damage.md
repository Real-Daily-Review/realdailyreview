# Reflecting Pool Vandalism Forces July 4th Repairs, Raising Questions About National Park Security

The National Park Service will drain the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool immediately after July Fourth to repair deliberate damage to the pool's liner, according to a court filing revealed by the *Washington Examiner*. The NPS confirmed that the pool's caulk and foam sealant were cut with a sharp knife or razor, destroying delaminating material, *9News Denver* reported.

The timing is awkward: repairs begin right after America's 250th birthday celebration, potentially disrupting one of the nation's most iconic Independence Day venues. The incident raises uncomfortable questions about security at a major federal monument during high-traffic periods.

**The conservative angle:** Vandalism of a national landmark reflects broader concerns about law-and-order enforcement and adequate security staffing at federal properties. Republicans have long argued that government agencies—including the NPS—face resource constraints and management challenges that compromise their core missions. Whether the Park Service had sufficient personnel to prevent or quickly detect the damage remains unclear.

The repair timeline and full cost estimate have not been disclosed. Coloradans should watch whether the NPS provides a transparent accounting of security gaps and repair expenses—and whether Congress demands accountability for protecting America's monuments.
