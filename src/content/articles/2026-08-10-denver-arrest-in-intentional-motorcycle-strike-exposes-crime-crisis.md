# Denver Faces Rising Violent Crime as Intentional Hit-and-Run Claims Another Life

Denver's crime problem just claimed another victim—this time, a motorcyclist struck intentionally by a vehicle driver, according to Denver Police investigators (9News Denver). The suspect has been arrested, but the incident underscores a troubling pattern: Colorado's capital city continues to struggle with violent crime despite years of progressive criminal justice policies.

Meanwhile, the Eaton community is organizing a fundraiser for three high school graduates killed in a separate vehicle crash last month (KDVR Fox31), highlighting how traffic violence—whether criminal or accidental—is devastating Colorado families.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that Denver's soft-on-crime approach—including reduced prosecution of violent offenses and early release programs—emboldens criminals and fails victims. The intentional nature of this motorcycle strike suggests premeditated violence, not a momentary lapse in judgment.

Denver taxpayers deserve answers: Are current law enforcement resources adequate? Are prosecutors pursuing violent offenders aggressively enough? Progressive activists often blame "systemic issues" rather than individual criminal responsibility, but families burying loved ones need real accountability and deterrence, not excuses.

Watch whether Denver's district attorney's office treats this as the serious felony it appears to be—or whether it gets plea-bargained down in the name of "criminal justice reform."
