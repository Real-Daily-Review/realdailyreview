# Colorado Wildfires Scorch 129,000 Acres as State Battles Multiple Blazes

Colorado is facing a critical wildfire season, with over 129,000 acres burned and the Aspen Acres fire—the state's largest active blaze at 50,187 acres—remaining completely uncontained despite efforts by more than 300 firefighters (Colorado Sun). Wildfires are burning across Pueblo, Custer, Dolores, Mesa, Lake, and Ouray counties, with officials warning that growth is inevitable as heat and dry conditions persist (9News Denver).

The scale of the crisis has prompted United Way to launch a relief fund in Southern Colorado to help residents affected by the blazes, working alongside local emergency agencies and community partners (CPR News).

For Colorado taxpayers and property owners, this underscores a hard reality: state and federal land management policies—including decades of fire suppression and resistance to active forest thinning and controlled burns—have left Colorado's forests dangerously overgrown and vulnerable. Conservatives have long argued that aggressive forest management, not just reactive firefighting, is essential to reducing catastrophic wildfire risk. The state's environmental regulations often slow timber harvesting and fuel-reduction projects that could mitigate these disasters.

Watch for how state officials respond: Will Colorado prioritize aggressive forest thinning and timber management, or continue policies that prioritize environmental restrictions over fire prevention?
