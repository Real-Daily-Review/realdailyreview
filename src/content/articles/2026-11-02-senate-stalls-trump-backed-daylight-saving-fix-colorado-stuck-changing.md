# Senate Gridlock Leaves Colorado Stuck in Twice-Yearly Clock-Change Limbo

Congress remains deadlocked on permanent daylight saving time, forcing Coloradans to reset their clocks again this fall—a hassle conservatives argue could be solved with straightforward legislation.

President Trump backs the Sunlight Protection Act, which would make daylight saving time permanent year-round. According to Fox News, the bill has stalled in the Senate despite Trump's efforts to pressure lawmakers, including sharing Sen. Tom Cotton's cellphone number publicly to build momentum (Fox News Politics).

Republicans and conservatives argue the twice-yearly clock changes are an outdated nuisance that disrupts sleep schedules, productivity, and business operations. The solution, they contend, is simple: pass permanent daylight saving time and eliminate the biannual disruption.

Democrats and some Republicans have raised concerns about permanent daylight saving time, citing potential impacts on morning darkness in winter months and school schedules—though these objections remain largely procedural rather than principled.

For Colorado, the clock changes affect everything from farm operations to energy consumption to school start times. Ranchers and small-business owners have long complained the twice-yearly disruption wastes time and creates scheduling headaches.
