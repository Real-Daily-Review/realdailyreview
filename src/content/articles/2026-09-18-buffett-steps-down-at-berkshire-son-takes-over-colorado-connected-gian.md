# Warren Buffett Steps Down at Berkshire Hathaway; Son Howard Takes Helm

At 96, Warren Buffett—one of America's most influential investors and a Nebraska resident whose company holds significant Colorado business interests—has stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, handing the role to his son Howard.

Buffett will assume the title of chairman emeritus and "continue to offer his valued judgment and perspective" to the company, according to announcements from Berkshire Hathaway (9News Denver, Just The News). The transition marks a significant moment for the conglomerate, which operates across insurance, energy, utilities, and manufacturing—sectors that touch Colorado's economy directly.

Berkshire Hathaway's energy holdings include substantial investments in renewable and traditional power generation. The company's influence on regulatory and tax policy affecting Colorado businesses and energy development warrants close attention from state policymakers and taxpayers concerned about corporate influence on state energy mandates and grid reliability.

Howard Buffett's leadership style and policy positions remain less publicly defined than his father's, who has long advocated for higher taxes on the wealthy—a position at odds with Colorado conservatives' tax-cutting agenda. The succession raises questions about whether Berkshire's political and philanthropic priorities will shift.

Watch for how Howard Buffett's tenure affects the company's stance on Colorado energy regulation, tax policy, and renewable energy mandates that could impact Colorado ratepayers and workers in traditional energy sectors.
