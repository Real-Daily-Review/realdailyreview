# Boulder and Douglas Counties Face Commission Expansion Push—While Democrat Candidate Faces Intoxication Arrest

Colorado's Front Range counties are pushing to expand their boards of supervisors, a move that would increase government overhead and taxpayer costs just as voters grow weary of bloated bureaucracy.

Boulder and Douglas counties are pursuing ballot measures to add commissioners, with Boulder County Commissioner Ashley Stolzmann arguing that population growth justifies more representation (Denver Post). Conservatives should ask the hard question: Does adding commissioners reduce costs or simply create another layer of government spending? Larger boards often mean higher salaries, staff, and administrative expenses—burdens ultimately borne by property taxpayers already squeezed by Colorado's rising cost of living.

Meanwhile, a Democratic candidate running for Colorado's House seat representing Douglas County was arrested in May for public intoxication, according to Texas court records (Denver Post). The arrest raises questions about judgment and fitness for office at a moment when Colorado voters are scrutinizing candidate character.

On a brighter note, two Boulder County communities—Gold Hill and Wondervu—earned Firewise USA designations through youth-led vegetation-clearing crews, demonstrating that private initiative and community action, not government mandates, effectively reduce wildfire risk (9News Denver).
