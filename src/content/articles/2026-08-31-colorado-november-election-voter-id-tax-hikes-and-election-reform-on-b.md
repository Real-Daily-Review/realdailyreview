# Colorado's November Election Shaping Up as Battleground Over Voting Rules and School Spending

Colorado voters will decide far more than candidates this November—they'll settle fundamental questions about how the state conducts elections and how much taxpayers must fork over for schools.

Initiative 362, backed by election-integrity advocates, would add voter ID requirements to Colorado's all-mail voting system (Complete Colorado). Supporters argue the measure strengthens authentication in a system that has faced national scrutiny. The initiative arrives as Colorado election officials insist they'll continue mailing ballots to all voters regardless of White House pressure or executive orders limiting mail-in voting (Denver Post).

Meanwhile, school districts across the state are asking voters to approve mill levy overrides—essentially property tax hikes—during a cost-of-living crisis when families are already squeezed. Districts face a crowded ballot and must make their case for higher taxes while competing for voter attention (Denver Post).

The state also faces renewed push for election system overhauls. A new coalition is laying groundwork for another ranked-choice voting campaign after voters rejected the idea two years ago (Denver Post).

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether Initiative 362 passes and whether Colorado taxpayers reject school tax hikes amid economic headwinds.
