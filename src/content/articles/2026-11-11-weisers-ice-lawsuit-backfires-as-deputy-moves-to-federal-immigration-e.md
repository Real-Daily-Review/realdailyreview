# Colorado Deputy's ICE Move Highlights Weiser's Immigration Overreach

A former Mesa County Sheriff's deputy disciplined for working with federal immigration authorities has landed a job at ICE—a move that underscores Colorado's Attorney General Phil Weiser's aggressive stance against law enforcement cooperation with immigration enforcement.

Alexander Zwinck resigned last year after facing disciplinary action and a lawsuit from Weiser's office for collaborating with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (Colorado Sun). The AG's legal pressure on local deputies for assisting federal immigration authorities reflects the progressive playbook: obstruct immigration enforcement and punish officers who cooperate with it.

Republicans and law-and-order conservatives argue that Weiser's lawsuit against Zwinck chilled legitimate cooperation between state and federal law enforcement on immigration matters—exactly what sanctuary-state advocates want. They contend that Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for Weiser's litigation while federal immigration enforcement becomes harder to coordinate at the local level.

Democrats and immigration advocates counter that local police shouldn't be deputized as ICE agents, citing concerns about community trust and racial profiling.

Zwinck's move to ICE suggests the disciplinary action didn't deter federal immigration work—it simply relocated it. Conservatives should watch whether Weiser's office pursues similar legal actions against other Colorado officers cooperating with federal immigration authorities.
