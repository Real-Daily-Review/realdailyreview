# House Dem Leader Hedges on Court-Packing While Distancing From Socialist Allies

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries is calling for "dramatic" Supreme Court reform—including potential court expansion and term limits—while simultaneously trying to separate himself from the Democratic Socialists of America, a contradiction that exposes the left's incoherent governing vision.

On *Meet the Press*, Jeffries refused to rule out court-packing, claiming the Supreme Court faces a "legitimacy problem" (Washington Examiner). Yet days later, he insisted he doesn't support the DSA agenda on policing, immigration, and other issues (The Daily Wire). The problem: Democrats have increasingly embraced socialist positions over capitalism, according to recent polling (Daily Caller), making Jeffries' distinction ring hollow.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters. Court-packing would entrench progressive judicial activism that threatens Second Amendment rights, energy development, and property rights. Jeffries' hedging reveals Democrats know their radical agenda polls poorly—so they're trying to have it both ways: adopt socialist policies while denying the label.

The Washington Examiner rightly notes that both court-packing and Trump's court-ownership rhetoric are wrong approaches. The real issue: Democrats want to weaponize the judiciary when they lose elections rather than accept constitutional limits on power.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic delegation—Sens. Michael Bennet and John Hickenlooper, plus House members—endorse Jeffries' court-packing push or distance themselves ahead of 2026.
