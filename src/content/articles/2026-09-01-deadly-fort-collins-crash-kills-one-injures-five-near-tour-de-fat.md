# Deadly Fort Collins Crash Raises Questions About Pedestrian Safety on Colorado Streets

A pickup truck plowed into pedestrians on a Fort Collins sidewalk near Tour de Fat festivities Saturday, killing one person and injuring five others in what witnesses described as a terrifying collision that could have been far worse.

According to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31, the vehicle struck the crowd near Walrus Ice Cream, with one witness recalling how a tree may have prevented additional casualties. "If the tree hadn't been there," the witness told 9News, the outcome could have been deadlier. Another witness told KDVR the tree was visibly "shaking" after impact.

The incident underscores a recurring concern for Colorado communities: vehicle safety barriers and pedestrian protection in high-traffic public spaces, particularly during crowded events. Fort Collins officials will likely face pressure to review whether adequate safety measures—bollards, barriers, or traffic controls—were in place around the festival area.

While initial reports do not indicate criminal intent, the crash highlights the tension between public access and public safety that city planners across Colorado must navigate. Conservatives and public safety advocates will want to see whether the city's existing protocols were followed and whether additional infrastructure investments are warranted—or whether the focus should remain on driver accountability.

Watch for Fort Collins city council discussions on pedestrian safety infrastructure and whether new spending or regulations emerge in response.
