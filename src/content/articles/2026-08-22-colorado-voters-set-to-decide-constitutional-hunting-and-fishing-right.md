# Colorado Voters to Decide Constitutional Right to Hunt and Fish

Colorado's hunting and fishing heritage faces a pivotal moment: voters will decide whether to enshrine the right to hunt and fish directly in the state Constitution. This constitutional amendment, which requires a 55 percent supermajority to pass, represents a conservative push to protect outdoor traditions against future regulatory encroachment by state agencies or environmental activists.

The measure reflects growing concern among Colorado's hunting and fishing communities that bureaucratic overreach could gradually restrict access to public lands and game. By constitutionalizing these rights, proponents argue they're safeguarding what generations of Coloradans have taken for granted—the ability to pursue elk, trout, and other game without government permission slipping away through administrative rule-making.

According to Complete Colorado, the amendment will appear on the ballot, forcing voters to weigh constitutional protections for outdoor heritage against potential objections from environmental groups who may argue the language could limit wildlife management flexibility.

For Colorado's ranchers, hunters, and rural communities dependent on outdoor recreation and game management, this vote carries real stakes. The 55 percent threshold is deliberately high, meaning supporters must build broad coalition support across urban and rural Colorado.
