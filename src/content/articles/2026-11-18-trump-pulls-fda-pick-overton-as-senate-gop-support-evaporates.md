# Trump Pulls FDA Pick Overton as Senate GOP Support Crumbles

The White House abruptly withdrew Dr. Heidi Overton's FDA nomination Tuesday, just hours before a Senate committee vote—a rare retreat that signals fracturing Republican unity on Trump's regulatory agenda.

Overton, a physician and deputy director at the Office of Management and Budget, faced wavering support from Senate Republicans ahead of the Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee hearing scheduled for Wednesday, according to 9News Denver. Fox News and the Washington Examiner confirmed the White House official's statement pulling the nomination.

The withdrawal raises questions about what specific objections derailed her candidacy—whether concerns over her regulatory philosophy, past statements, or other factors. For Colorado conservatives, the FDA pick matters: the agency's decisions on drug approvals, food safety rules, and medical device regulations directly affect Colorado's biotech sector, agricultural exports, and healthcare costs.

Republicans have long criticized FDA overreach and regulatory delays that stifle innovation. Conservatives typically favor FDA leadership willing to streamline approval processes and reduce bureaucratic friction. The collapse of Overton's nomination suggests Senate GOP members may have had substantive concerns—or that internal Trump-administration disagreements undermined her support.
