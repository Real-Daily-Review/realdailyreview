# State's MyColorado App Down Ahead of Holiday Travel—150K Coloradans' Data Potentially Exposed

Colorado's digital ID app went dark just as thousands of residents hit the roads for Thanksgiving, leaving travelers without a key tool for real-time travel updates and state services. The Colorado Sun reports the cybersecurity incident may have compromised data for up to 150,000 people, with no restoration timeline announced by state officials.

The outage underscores a growing vulnerability: Colorado's reliance on centralized digital platforms for essential services. When government systems fail—especially during peak travel periods—citizens have no backup. Conservatives argue this illustrates why government should stick to core functions rather than expanding into digital infrastructure where private-sector solutions often prove more reliable and secure.

State officials have offered no explanation for how the breach occurred or why recovery estimates remain vague. For Coloradans dependent on the app for travel information and ID verification, the silence is frustrating. The incident also raises questions about data security protocols at the state level and whether taxpayer dollars are being spent wisely on cybersecurity measures.

Watch for legislative demands next session to audit how Colorado manages citizen data and whether the state should consolidate or decentralize its digital platforms.
