# Trump Administration Tightens H-1B Visa Rules, Eliminates Job-Loss Grace Period

The Trump administration is moving to eliminate a 60-day grace period that allows H-1B and other visa holders to remain in the U.S. after losing their jobs, according to Fox News and the Washington Examiner. The Department of Homeland Security and USCIS proposal would require foreign workers to leave immediately upon job termination, scrapping an Obama-era policy.

Conservatives argue the change strengthens immigration enforcement and prioritizes American workers during economic uncertainty. The move aligns with Trump's broader push to tighten visa programs and reduce reliance on foreign labor, particularly in tech and skilled sectors where H-1B visas are concentrated.

Tech industry groups and business advocates have historically opposed stricter H-1B rules, contending they need access to specialized talent. However, Republicans counter that American workers should get first priority and that visa programs shouldn't serve as a backdoor to cheaper labor that undercuts domestic wages.

For Colorado—home to a growing tech corridor in Denver and Boulder—the rule could affect employers in software development, engineering, and aerospace who rely on H-1B workers. It may also tighten labor markets and potentially raise hiring costs for companies unable to fill positions domestically.

Watch for industry lobbying pushback and whether Colorado's business community will pressure state Republicans to seek exemptions or delays in implementation.
