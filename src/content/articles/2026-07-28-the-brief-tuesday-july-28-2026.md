# Real Daily Review — The Brief

## Trump Pushes Supreme Court to Restore Mail-In Voting Order

The Trump administration is asking the Supreme Court to unblock an executive order that would direct the Department of Homeland Security to compile verified voter rolls and bar the U.S. Postal Service from delivering absentee ballots to voters not on state-approved lists. Lower courts blocked the order, but the White House is fighting back. For Colorado — a universal mail-in ballot state — this case could directly reshape how elections are administered here. Conservatives say election integrity starts with knowing who's actually on the rolls.

---

## Douglas County Helitack Crew Steps Up as Wildfire Season Heats Up

Douglas County's helitack crew is now launching its helicopter for every confirmed fire in the county — no matter the size. That kind of aggressive, local-first response is exactly what Colorado's Front Range communities need as dry conditions push wildfire risk higher. This is government working the way it should: at the county level, fast, and focused on protecting property and lives. Colorado ranchers and rural homeowners know better than anyone that the first 20 minutes of a wildfire are the only ones that matter.

## Biden's Hur Interview Tapes Cleared for Release

Joe Biden's attorneys have dropped their legal fight to keep audio recordings from special counsel Robert Hur's classified documents probe under wraps, clearing the way for Congress to access the tapes. Republicans have long argued the recordings would confirm what the Hur report already suggested — that Biden was a diminished figure who mishandled classified material and couldn't be held accountable for it. Colorado taxpayers who funded that investigation deserve to know what their government was hiding and why it took this long.

## T-Mobile Outage Leaves Thousands in 'SOS' Mode

Thousands of T-Mobile customers across major U.S. cities were knocked off service Monday, with phones dropping to "SOS only" mode and texting, internet, and streaming going dark, according to Downdetector. No cause has been publicly confirmed. For Coloradans in rural counties, mountain communities, and areas where T-Mobile is the primary carrier, outages like this aren't a minor inconvenience — they're a safety issue. It's a reminder that communications infrastructure reliability matters, and that consumers deserve straight answers fast.
