# Michigan Democrat's DSA-Backed Senate Win Signals Radical Shift in Party's Mainstream

Michigan Democrats just nominated a candidate so far left that even his own party is nervous. Dr. Abdul El-Sayed, who won the state's Democratic Senate primary, openly embraces Democratic Socialists of America politics and has called for abolishing institutions—a sign of how thoroughly progressives have captured the Democratic establishment.

National Review notes El-Sayed "does not disclaim the DSA's radical politics" and maintains a lengthy list of institutions he'd like dismantled. The Washington Examiner calls him "an American tragedy," warning his nomination reflects the party's ideological capture. Even Democratic State Sen. Mallory McMorrow, who opposed him in the primary, felt compelled to offer damage-control advice: don't "turn everybody into an enemy."

This matters for Colorado. As national Democrats drift further left, pressure mounts on Colorado's congressional delegation and state legislature to follow suit—threatening energy jobs, Second Amendment rights, and tax policy. El-Sayed's nomination signals Democrats are doubling down on socialism rather than moderating, which will shape the midterm battlefield and define what "mainstream" Democratic policy looks like in 2026.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats distance themselves from El-Sayed's radical platform or embrace it as the party's future direction.
