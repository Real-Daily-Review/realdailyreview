# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, August 21, 2026

Colorado conservatives head into the weekend watching their electric bills climb, their state government flirt with common sense on party separation, and Washington Democrats sharpening their impeachment knives before the midterms even arrive.

---

## Colorado Utility Regulators Trim — But Don't Kill — Xcel's Rate Hike

The Colorado Public Utilities Commission approved a rate increase for Xcel Energy customers, landing at roughly $5 per month — about half of what Xcel originally requested. Regulators cut the ask, but make no mistake: your bill is still going up. Conservatives argue that Colorado's aggressive green energy mandates are the root driver of rising utility costs, and until the state stops forcing expensive generation requirements onto ratepayers, these fights before the PUC will keep coming. Xcel can still request a rehearing, so this may not be the final word.

---

## Colorado Takes a Step Toward Keeping Government Out of Party Politics

A move is underway in Colorado to separate state government from the business of granting special recognition or privileges to political parties — and conservatives are calling it the right call. Government has no proper role propping up or legitimizing any party apparatus with taxpayer resources or official standing. Complete Colorado is framing this as a "courageous" move, and they're right. If Colorado Republicans and Democrats alike want to run their internal affairs, they can do it on their own dime.

---

## Scalise Sounds the Alarm: Democrats Will Impeach Trump If They Win the House

House Republican leader Steve Scalise is warning voters plainly: if Democrats retake the House majority, they will move to impeach President Trump on day one. Scalise points to socialist insurgents reshaping the Democratic Party as evidence that any restraint from the left is gone. For Colorado conservatives, this is a reminder that the 2026 midterms aren't abstract — Colorado's competitive congressional districts could be part of what hands Democrats the gavel, and with it, two years of weaponized investigations instead of governance.

---

## North Korea Launches 10 Ballistic Missiles as U.S. Scales Back Drills

North Korea fired approximately 10 short-range ballistic missiles, launched from around its capital, as the United States scaled back joint military exercises with South Korea. Republicans argue that projecting strength — not scaling back deterrence — is the only language Pyongyang understands. For Colorado, home to NORAD, Space Command, and multiple major military installations, a destabilized Pacific theater is never just a foreign-policy abstraction. Colorado's defense community has a direct stake in how Washington manages this threat.

---

## Melania Trump's $2 Million Scholarship Program Targets Foster Youth

First Lady Melania Trump announced a $2 million scholarship program aimed at helping foster children achieve financial independence once they age out of the system. The White House first announced the initiative in June. Conservatives say this is exactly the kind of targeted, results-oriented private support that beats a government bureaucracy every time — helping vulnerable young people build self-sufficiency rather than dependency. Colorado has thousands of foster youth who age out of the system each year and face long odds without this kind of direct support.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eye on the Xcel rate case — if the utility requests a rehearing, Colorado ratepayers could be back in the same fight before winter heating season arrives. On the national front, watch how Colorado's congressional candidates respond to Scalise's impeachment warning; their answers will tell you everything about whether they're running to govern or to perform. And with North Korea back to missile launches, pay attention to whether the administration recalibrates its posture toward the Korean Peninsula before the situation escalates further.
