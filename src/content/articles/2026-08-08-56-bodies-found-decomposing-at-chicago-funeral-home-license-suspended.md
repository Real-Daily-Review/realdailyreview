# Chicago Funeral Home Horror Shows Why Regulation Matters—and When It Fails

A gruesome discovery in Chicago exposes what happens when government oversight collapses: authorities found the remains of 56 people improperly stored and decomposing at South Chicago Chapel, with some bodies left in unrefrigerated areas under "deplorable conditions," according to 9News Denver and Just The News.

Illinois state officials suspended the license of one of the funeral home's directors—a response that came only *after* the bodies were discovered, not before. This is a rare case where conservatives and progressives should agree: basic regulatory enforcement of funeral licensing exists for good reason. Families entrust funeral homes with their loved ones' remains. That trust demands accountability.

The question conservatives must ask: Why didn't existing state inspections catch this sooner? Funeral home licensing is one of the few regulations with clear public-safety justification. When government fails at its core function—protecting citizens from fraud and abuse—it undermines the case for *any* regulation.

Colorado funeral homes operate under state licensing through the Department of Regulatory Agencies. Coloradans should demand that our regulators actually inspect facilities regularly, not just rubber-stamp licenses and investigate only after families suffer.

Watch whether Illinois tightens funeral home inspection protocols—or whether this becomes another case of regulatory theater that punishes the industry after the fact.
