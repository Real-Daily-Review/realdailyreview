# Congress Demands AI Guardrails as Billionaire Defies Oversight

Congress is tightening the screws on artificial intelligence oversight, with lawmakers pressing Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on whether tech companies can simply walk away from AI safety commitments. Meanwhile, the House Oversight Committee voted to hold billionaire Leon Black in contempt for dodging subpoenas related to his ties to convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

On AI, Republicans and Democrats alike are skeptical of self-regulation. According to the Daily Caller, lawmakers confronted Bessent over assurances that companies "can stop any time they want to"—a posture conservatives argue amounts to gambling with Americans' livelihoods and security. The GOP position: AI development requires enforceable guardrails, not corporate pinky promises.

The Black contempt vote reflects bipartisan frustration with wealthy elites sidestepping congressional authority. Black sued to block subpoenas rather than answer questions about payments to Epstein, according to Just The News. Conservatives see this as exactly the kind of accountability failure that erodes public trust in institutions.

For Colorado, the AI debate carries economic weight: the state's growing tech sector and workforce face regulatory uncertainty, while energy companies worry about AI's power demands and environmental mandates. Taxpayers should watch whether Congress actually enforces contempt votes or lets billionaires lawyer their way out—a test of whether oversight means anything.
