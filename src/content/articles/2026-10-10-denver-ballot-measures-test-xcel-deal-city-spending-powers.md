# Denver Voters Face Four Ballot Questions on Xcel Deal, City Spending

Denver taxpayers will decide this November whether to rubber-stamp a new Xcel Energy franchise agreement and hand City Council more budgeting flexibility—moves that conservatives say deserve scrutiny before passage.

The Denver City Council approved four referred questions (2H through 2K) for the ballot, according to the *Denver Post*. The measures span a new Xcel Energy franchise agreement and changes to city budgeting procedures. While specifics remain sparse, conservatives should examine whether the Xcel deal locks in favorable rates for residents or simply extends corporate favoritism, and whether the budgeting changes grant City Council unchecked spending authority.

Republicans and fiscal watchdogs argue that bundling multiple questions on one ballot often obscures the real cost to taxpayers. The Xcel franchise—a long-term contract governing rates and service—warrants line-by-line public debate, not a package deal. Similarly, any expansion of council budgeting powers risks opening the door to tax-and-spend governance without voter approval on specific expenditures.

Democrats frame the measures as routine municipal housekeeping necessary for efficient city management.

Coloradans should demand full transparency on Xcel's rate structure and demand that City Council justify any new spending authority before November.
