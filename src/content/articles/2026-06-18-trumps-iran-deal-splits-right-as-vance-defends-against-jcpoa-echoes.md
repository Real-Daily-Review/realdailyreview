# Trump Administration's Iran Deal Draws Fire From Right-Wing Hawks Over Vance's Defense

President Trump's new Iran memorandum of understanding is already splitting conservatives, with Vice President JD Vance forced into an awkward defensive position as hawks question whether the deal merely repackages Obama-era logic under different branding.

Vance has rejected comparisons to the failed JCPOA, arguing that Trump's "carrot-and-stick" approach reverses Obama's failed strategy (Fox News). Trump himself doubled down at the G7, calling the original deal a "catastrophe" and claiming he "finished the job" that Netanyahu couldn't accomplish (Fox News). Yet the Washington Examiner's analysis suggests the administration's messaging chaos—with the memo's text leaked during a reporter briefing—has left Vance exposed defending an agreement that skeptical conservatives worry echoes the very diplomacy they spent years opposing.

For Colorado energy interests and taxpayers, the stakes matter: any Iran deal affects oil markets, energy prices, and broader Middle East stability that ripples through the American economy. Conservatives have long argued that appeasement-style diplomacy with Tehran undermines U.S. leverage and regional allies.
