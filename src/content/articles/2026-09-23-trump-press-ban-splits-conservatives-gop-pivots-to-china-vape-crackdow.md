# Trump's Press Restrictions Draw Fire From Right and Left—But Senate GOP Focuses on China Vape Crackdown

Donald Trump's restrictions on press access have drawn rare bipartisan criticism, with even conservative outlets questioning the move. *National Review* argues Trump "should rescind the press ban," calling it "a violation of the spirit of a free press" and urging him to confront reporters directly instead (National Review). Meanwhile, *Colorado Sun* columnist Mike Littwin compares the effort to Trump's foreign policy missteps, framing it as a strategic blunder.

Republicans, however, are divided. While some conservatives defend press freedom as foundational, Senate Republicans led by Sen. Ted Budd (R-NC) are pressing Trump on a different media concern: the flood of illicit Chinese-made vapes into U.S. markets. In a letter ahead of Trump's meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping, Budd and 10 GOP colleagues urged the president to demand Beijing crack down on unauthorized vape manufacturing and distribution (Washington Examiner).

For Colorado, the vape issue carries weight—youth nicotine addiction remains a public health concern, and illicit products undercut legitimate Colorado vape retailers and tax revenue. Republicans argue enforcing existing regulations and pressuring foreign suppliers beats domestic bans.

**Watch:** Whether Trump prioritizes press access or the vape crackdown in his Xi meeting will signal whether he's listening to his base or his critics.
