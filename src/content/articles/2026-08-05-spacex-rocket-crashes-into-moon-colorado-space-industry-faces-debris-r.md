# SpaceX Rocket Slams Into Moon; What Colorado's Space Industry Should Learn

A discarded upper stage from a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket crashed into the moon early Wednesday morning at roughly 5,400 miles per hour near Einstein crater, creating a crater about 60 feet wide, according to NASA estimates (The Daily Wire). Scientists had predicted the impact was inevitable given the rocket segment's trajectory.

The incident underscores a growing problem in space operations: orbital debris and end-of-life rocket stages that become hazards. While SpaceX remains the dominant commercial launch provider—and a major economic driver for Colorado's aerospace sector—the crash raises questions about industry accountability and whether current regulations adequately address space debris management.

Colorado hosts significant SpaceX operations and a thriving aerospace ecosystem. Conservatives and industry advocates argue that space commerce thrives best with light-touch regulation and market incentives rather than heavy-handed government mandates. However, some argue the federal government should establish clearer liability standards and debris-mitigation requirements to prevent future incidents that could damage lunar science missions or endanger other spacecraft.

The broader issue: as commercial space activity accelerates, Colorado businesses and workers depend on a regulatory environment that encourages innovation without creating unnecessary bureaucratic friction.

Watch whether Congress or the FAA moves to impose new space-debris rules that could affect Colorado aerospace contractors and launch operations.
