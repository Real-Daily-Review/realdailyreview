# Cambridge Dictionary's 'Human' Pick Signals Growing Unease Over AI's Grip on Society

Cambridge Dictionary's selection of "human" as its 2026 word of the year reflects deepening cultural anxiety about artificial intelligence—a concern that should resonate with Colorado conservatives worried about government-mandated AI regulation and tech monopolies.

The choice, reported by both 9News Denver and the New York Post, signals that fears about AI's societal impact have moved beyond Silicon Valley boardrooms into mainstream consciousness. The dictionary reached back to 15th-century roots to find a word capturing what many see as a fundamental question: what does it mean to remain distinctly human in an age of algorithmic decision-making?

For Colorado, this matters. As AI systems increasingly influence everything from hiring to loan approvals to law enforcement, conservatives should watch whether state lawmakers respond with heavy-handed regulation—the progressive instinct—or market-driven solutions that preserve innovation and individual choice. The Left will likely push for government oversight of AI development; Republicans should argue that competitive markets, transparency requirements, and property-rights protections offer better safeguards than bureaucratic mandates that stifle American tech leadership.

The real risk: Colorado becomes a testing ground for restrictive AI policy that drives tech investment and jobs to friendlier states.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats introduce AI regulation bills in the 2027 legislative session.
