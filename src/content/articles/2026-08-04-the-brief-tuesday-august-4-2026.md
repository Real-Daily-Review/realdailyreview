# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Tuesday, August 4, 2026

Colorado taxpayers are footing the bill for government inefficiency, activist judges are defying the Supreme Court, and the Trump administration is delivering on enforcement promises that the left said couldn't be done. Here's what you need to know this morning.

---

## Colorado's SNAP Error Rate Is Costing You Money

Colorado is leaving tens of millions of dollars on the table — and putting taxpayers on the hook — because state bureaucrats can't process food stamp benefits accurately. According to Complete Colorado, the state could recover significant savings before the fiscal year ends if it simply improves SNAP processing. This is the kind of government incompetence that conservatives have warned about for years: bloated welfare administration that fails both recipients and the taxpayers funding it. Coloradans deserve accountability, not excuses, from state agencies managing federal dollars.

---

## Colorado Springs Dragged Into Court Over Election Timing

Colorado Springs is being sued for holding its municipal elections in April, the first enforcement action since a 2025 state voting law took effect. Republicans argue election-timing laws like this one are less about voter access and more about political engineering — consolidating local races into higher-turnout statewide elections that favor Democrats. Colorado Springs residents elected their leaders under a system that worked. Now the courts will decide whether the state can dictate when a city runs its own elections. Watch this one closely — it sets a precedent for local control across Colorado.

---

## Trump's Deportation Numbers Are Impossible to Ignore

The left and some Republicans have spent months attacking the administration's immigration enforcement — but the numbers don't lie. President Trump's team deported 46,000 illegal aliens in July alone, with ICE arresting 51,000 immigrants that same month, averaging 1,645 arrests per day. Those are record-breaking figures no previous president has matched. For Colorado, a state that has seen the consequences of sanctuary policies and porous borders firsthand, this is exactly the kind of enforcement that voters demanded in 2024. The anti-enforcement crowd is losing the argument and the numbers prove it.

---

## Grassley Puts Jack Smith in the Hot Seat

Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA) has invited former special counsel Jack Smith to testify before the Senate Judiciary Committee over his handling of Operation Arctic Frost. Republicans argue Smith weaponized federal prosecutorial power for political ends, and they want answers under oath. This matters for Colorado conservatives who watched the DOJ spend years pursuing politically motivated investigations while real threats went unaddressed. Grassley's move signals that congressional Republicans aren't letting the accountability question die — and they shouldn't.

---

## Activist Judge Faces Impeachment Push After Defying SCOTUS

Rep. Andy Ogles has filed an impeachment resolution against Obama-appointed Judge Allison Burroughs after she blocked a Trump administration Temporary Protected Status order affecting Somali nationals — an action Republicans say directly defies a Supreme Court ruling. Conservatives say this is exactly the kind of judicial overreach that has turned unelected federal judges into a one-branch veto on the democratic process. When a judge substitutes her policy preferences for a Supreme Court ruling, impeachment isn't a threat — it's a constitutional remedy.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado Springs election lawsuit — if the courts side with the state, it opens the door to stripping local governments of control over their own electoral calendars, a direct assault on the home-rule principles Colorado conservatives have long defended. On the federal front, watch whether Jack Smith actually shows up before Grassley's committee or hides behind legal maneuvering. And as the SNAP error-rate story develops, demand your state legislators ask the hard question: how much Colorado taxpayer exposure has bureaucratic sloppiness already created?
