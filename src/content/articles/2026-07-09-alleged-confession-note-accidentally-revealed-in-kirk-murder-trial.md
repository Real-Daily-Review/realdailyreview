# Alleged Confession Note Surfaces in Charlie Kirk Murder Case

A handwritten note allegedly written by Tyler Robinson, the 23-year-old charged in the death of conservative activist Charlie Kirk, was accidentally displayed during a Utah court hearing Thursday—despite a judge's prior order keeping the document sealed before jury selection.

The disclosure, flagged by the *Washington Examiner* and *Daily Caller*, occurred during an evidentiary hearing as a camera operator briefly revealed the document to the public. The timing is significant: judges typically restrict such evidence to prevent prejudicing potential jurors.

According to bombshell testimony reported by the *New York Post*, Robinson spent weeks planning Kirk's murder and allegedly asked his trans lover about an engraver tool for bullets—suggesting premeditation and deliberate preparation.

The case has drawn national attention from conservative circles, with Kirk's death marking a rare high-profile killing of a right-wing political figure. Robinson's defense will likely argue the accidental disclosure violated his right to a fair trial, potentially complicating prosecution efforts.
