# White House Weaponizes Federal Agencies Against Colorado Over Tina Peters Case

The Trump White House summoned five federal agencies to a Colorado-focused meeting to discuss "immediate actions" against the state, according to emails obtained by the *Denver Post*—a stunning display of executive overreach that conservatives say proves the danger of unchecked federal power.

Trump staffer Emily Underwood orchestrated the directive asking departments to review their grants and support for Colorado amid the administration's push to free Tina Peters, the former Mesa County clerk convicted of election interference. The *Denver Post* called it a "smoking gun" showing political retaliation disguised as policy.

Republicans argue this weaponization of federal agencies against a state for refusing to pardon a Trump ally represents exactly the kind of government abuse the Constitution was designed to prevent. Colorado taxpayers and businesses could face real consequences if Washington freezes federal funding over a state court conviction.

Underwood has since left her position, but the episode raises urgent questions about whether the White House views federal agencies as tools for political vengeance rather than neutral administrators of law.

Conservatives should monitor whether other states face similar pressure and whether Congress demands answers on the scope of this operation.
