# Senate Moves to Regulate College Sports—But at What Cost to Colorado Schools?

A federal overhaul of college athletics is advancing through Congress, and Colorado universities could face significant new compliance burdens if the Protect College Sports Act becomes law.

The bill, which cleared an early Senate hurdle, aims to impose federal guardrails on name, image, and likeness (NIL) deals, transfer portal rules, and revenue-sharing caps—an attempt to rein in what Fox News calls the college sports "Wild West" as spending spirals out of control.

Conservatives raise a fundamental concern: Washington shouldn't be micromanaging private contracts between athletes and schools. Federal regulation of NIL deals and transfer rules could stifle competition, reduce athlete earnings, and saddle universities with compliance costs that ultimately hit Colorado taxpayers funding public institutions like CU Boulder and Colorado State.

The bill's supporters argue escalating spending is unsustainable and that federal standards are necessary to level the playing field. But skeptics worry this is regulatory overreach dressed up as reform—another example of Washington deciding what states and schools should do.

Colorado's flagship universities could face new administrative expenses and restrictions on recruiting and athlete compensation. Watch whether CU and CSU leadership push back against federal mandates or quietly accept them as the price of NCAA membership.
