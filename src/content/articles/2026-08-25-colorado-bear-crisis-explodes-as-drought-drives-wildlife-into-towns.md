# Colorado Bear Crisis Escalates as Drought Starves Wildlife Into Populated Areas

Colorado is facing a dangerous wildlife crisis that government land-management policies have failed to prevent. Record bear reports and multiple attacks in a single week signal a breakdown in the state's ability to manage human-wildlife conflict as drought, wildfire, and low snowpack devastate natural food sources.

According to 9News Denver, animals across Colorado have been severely impacted by drought conditions and wildfire damage, driving bears into residential areas at unprecedented rates. KDVR Fox31 reported four bear attacks in one week alone, with one incident sending a Jefferson County man to the hospital on Friday.

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has documented the surge, but critics argue the agency's approach—focused on public education and bear relocations—treats symptoms rather than causes. Conservatives point out that decades of fire-suppression policies and water-management failures have created the drought conditions now pushing wildlife into towns. Rather than address root causes like forest management and water infrastructure, state officials rely on reactive measures that leave Colorado families and property at risk.

The real question: Will state leadership acknowledge that government mismanagement of Colorado's natural resources created this crisis, or will they double down on the same failed policies?
