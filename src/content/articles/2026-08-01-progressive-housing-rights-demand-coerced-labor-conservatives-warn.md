# Housing as a 'Right' Means Forcing Someone Else to Pay

The progressive left's insistence on treating housing as a fundamental human right ignores basic economics—and threatens individual liberty, according to conservative analysis.

The *Washington Examiner* argues that declaring housing a "right" without production or voluntary exchange simply conscripts other people's labor and resources. "Matter and energy do not obey political declarations," the outlet writes. "Someone must cut the timber, raise the walls, grow the grain, and haul the water. If a person is declared to have a right to those things without producing or exchanging for them, then another person must be compelled to supply them."

This framing cuts to the heart of the conservative critique: progressive "rights" rhetoric masks coercive redistribution. When government mandates housing as an entitlement, it inevitably means higher taxes on Colorado workers and businesses, increased regulation of construction and property, and reduced incentives for the private development that actually builds homes.

Democrats and housing advocates counter that safe, affordable shelter is essential and that market forces alone have failed low-income communities. They argue government intervention corrects market failures.

Conservatives reject this premise, insisting that overregulation, zoning restrictions, and permitting delays—often imposed by progressive cities—are the real culprits strangling housing supply and affordability.
