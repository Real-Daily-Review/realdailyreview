# Boebert Denies Ethics Complaint Over Alleged Staff Relationships

Colorado's Rep. Lauren Boebert flatly rejected a sworn ethics complaint accusing her of sexual relationships with three congressional staffers, saying she has "never, ever been in any inappropriate relationship, nor have I ever been sexually involved with any staff" (Daily Wire).

The complaint, filed with the House Ethics Committee, alleges conduct that would violate House rules if substantiated. Boebert's response was direct: "I'm not a lesbian or bisexual," she stated Saturday, dismissing the claims as baseless (Daily Wire, New York Post).

The allegations strike at a sitting Republican representative from a purple Colorado district during an election cycle when her seat remains competitive. Ethics complaints against members of Congress are not uncommon, but they carry political weight—especially when they touch on personal conduct and potential abuse of power over subordinates.

Conservatives and Republicans will likely argue that unsubstantiated allegations should not be weaponized for political gain, and that Boebert deserves due process before judgment. The ethics process itself will determine whether the complaint merits investigation or dismissal.

Taxpayers and Colorado voters should watch whether the House Ethics Committee opens a formal investigation and what timeline it sets for resolution—particularly given the proximity to election season.
