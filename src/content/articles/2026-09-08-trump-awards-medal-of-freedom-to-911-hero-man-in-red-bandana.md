# Trump Honors 9/11 Heroes With Medal of Freedom, Coast Guard Cutter

President Trump posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom to Welles Crowther, the "man in the red bandana" who rescued up to 18 people from the World Trade Center before his death on September 11, 2001 (Fox News). During the same White House ceremony, Trump announced that the U.S. Coast Guard will commission a new fast-response cutter vessel named after Jeffrey Palazzo, a Coast Guard reservist and New York City firefighter who also perished in the attacks (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

Crowther, a 24-year-old equities trader, became a symbol of American heroism after witnesses described him descending repeatedly into the smoke-filled South Tower to guide trapped workers to safety. His red bandana, which he used to shield his face, became iconic in the aftermath of the attacks. Palazzo's recognition honors the sacrifice of first responders who ran toward danger when others fled.

The honors reflect Trump's emphasis on recognizing American valor and the debt owed to those who died defending the nation. Both awards underscore the enduring legacy of 9/11 and the courage of ordinary Americans in extraordinary circumstances.

Watch for continued efforts by the Trump administration to memorialize 9/11 heroes and strengthen support for first responders and their families.
