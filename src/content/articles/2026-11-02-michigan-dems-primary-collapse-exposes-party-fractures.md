# Michigan Democrat's Primary Squeaker Signals Fractures in Left-Wing Coalition

Michigan Democrats' supposed "unity" campaign imploded this summer when Abdul El-Sayed's expected 10-point primary romp nearly collapsed into a nail-biter—he won by just 1 percentage point (Washington Examiner). The underperformance exposed deep rifts within the party's coalition, driven by moderate Democrats and Jewish voters who broke ranks over El-Sayed's record and positioning.

Rather than consolidate support, El-Sayed's general election push has descended into damage control. He's now leaning on left-wing celebrity endorsements—comedian D.L. Hughley recently appeared at campaign events (Breitbart)—a sign his campaign is hemorrhaging grassroots Democratic support.

**The Colorado angle:** While this Michigan race plays out 1,500 miles away, it mirrors fractures visible in Colorado's own Democratic establishment. Progressive candidates who energize the party's activist base often alienate moderates and swing voters—a dynamic that could reshape Colorado's 2026 Senate race and down-ballot contests.

Republicans should note: when Democrats' internal divisions run this deep, it signals vulnerability. Moderate voters fleeing a candidate this early suggest broader dissatisfaction with the party's direction on issues from public safety to economic policy.

**Watch next:** whether El-Sayed can rebuild Democratic turnout before November, or whether his primary weakness becomes a general-election liability Republicans can exploit.
