# Colorado's 3rd District Stays Red as Hurd Edges Out Democrat Challenger

Republicans held the Western Slope and southern Colorado in a tight race that underscores the region's enduring conservative lean. Jeff Hurd won Colorado's 3rd Congressional District against Democrat Dwayne Romero in what the Denver Post called a close contest, maintaining GOP control of a seat Republicans have held since 2011.

The 3rd District—spanning Colorado's rugged Western Slope, ranching country, and energy-producing regions—remains a reliable Republican stronghold despite Democratic efforts to flip it. The victory reflects voter preference for conservative representation on issues critical to the region: water rights, energy development, and limited federal land restrictions that threaten local economies.

Democrats have targeted the district in recent cycles, viewing it as winnable terrain. Romero's campaign likely emphasized progressive priorities on climate and environmental regulation—positions that typically underperform in rural Colorado counties dependent on oil, gas, and mining.

Hurd's win signals that Western Slope voters remain skeptical of the left's regulatory agenda and committed to representatives who defend state sovereignty over federal overreach on land and resource management. The margin matters: narrow victories invite aggressive Democratic recruitment and spending in 2028.

Conservatives should monitor whether national Democrats double down on the 3rd District in the next cycle and how Hurd votes on energy and water issues that define his district.
