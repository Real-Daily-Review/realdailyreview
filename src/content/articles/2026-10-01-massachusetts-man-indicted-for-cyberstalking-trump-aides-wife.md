# Massachusetts Man Charged in Cyberstalking Case Against Trump Aide's Wife

Federal prosecutors have indicted a Massachusetts resident for cyberstalking Katie Miller, wife of White House deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller—a reminder that political figures and their families remain targets for harassment and intimidation from the far left.

John Anthony Proia III, 41, faces charges after allegedly targeting Miller, described by the Department of Justice as a "prominent" Republican living in the Washington, D.C., area (Washington Examiner). The indictment underscores a troubling pattern: conservative figures and their families have faced sustained online harassment campaigns, doxxing, and threats with minimal media attention compared to similar incidents targeting progressives.

The case illustrates why conservatives argue for stronger enforcement of existing cyberstalking and harassment laws—and why social media platforms must enforce their terms of service consistently, regardless of the target's political affiliation. Too often, conservative voices report that Big Tech companies move slowly or selectively when enforcing rules against harassment of right-leaning figures.

Stephen Miller, a key architect of Trump administration immigration policy, has long been a lightning rod for progressive activists. His family's safety matters as much as any other American's.

Colorado conservatives should watch whether this prosecution moves swiftly and whether media coverage matches the intensity applied to similar cases targeting Democratic figures.
