# Virginia Declaration of Rights Turns 250—A Forgotten Founder's Blueprint for Liberty That America Needs Now

George Mason's Virginia Declaration of Rights, adopted June 12, 1776, deserves far more attention than it gets in American civic life. Mason, often dismissed as "The Forgotten Founder," authored a document that preceded the Declaration of Independence and directly influenced the Bill of Rights—yet his memorial sits obscurely between the Lincoln and Jefferson memorials, a fitting metaphor for his erasure from popular memory.

The Virginia Declaration established principles conservatives still champion: natural rights, limited government, the right to alter or abolish tyrannical government, and the notion that power derives from the people, not the state. Mason's assertion that "all men are by nature equally free and independent" became foundational to American liberty—yet modern progressives have systematically abandoned these principles in favor of expansive government control.

For Coloradans, Mason's framework matters. His insistence on individual rights and resistance to centralized power speaks directly to contemporary battles over Second Amendment protections, property rights, and regulatory overreach that plague the state.

As government grows and constitutional limits erode, conservatives should ask: How many of Mason's principles survive in 21st-century America?
