# Congress Surrenders War Powers to Executive Branch Without Meaningful Resistance

Congress has effectively ceded its constitutional authority over military action, with even Republican dissenters facing political consequences for pushback. Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), already targeted by President Trump for primary opposition in May, cast a lonely vote Tuesday attempting to restrain executive war powers—a move that underscores why he became a target in the first place, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The episode reveals a troubling pattern: lawmakers unwilling to defend their institutional prerogatives against executive overreach, regardless of party. Cassidy's political isolation—having already lost Trump's backing and his Senate seat—demonstrates the cost of challenging presidential war authority, even on constitutional grounds.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans traditionally champion constitutional limits on executive power and separation of powers. Yet Trump's dominance over the GOP base has muted institutional resistance. Conservatives should worry that surrendering war powers sets precedent for future administrations, Democratic or otherwise, to wage military action without congressional consent—a direct violation of Article I authority.

**Colorado Angle:** Colorado's military installations and defense contractors depend on stable, constitutional governance. Erosion of congressional oversight threatens the rule of law that undergirds defense spending and military readiness.

Watch whether Republican senators develop backbone to defend constitutional war powers in future votes, or whether Trump loyalty continues to trump constitutional duty.
