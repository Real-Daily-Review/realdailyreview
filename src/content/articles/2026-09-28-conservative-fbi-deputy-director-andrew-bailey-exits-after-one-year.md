# Conservative FBI Deputy Director Andrew Bailey Steps Down After Year Under Trump

FBI Deputy Director Andrew Bailey, a conservative favorite tapped by the Trump administration to help reform the bureau, announced his departure Monday, citing family reasons and a return to Missouri (The Daily Wire). Bailey's exit marks another leadership shuffle at an agency conservatives have long criticized for politicization and institutional drift.

Bailey's one-year tenure coincided with Trump's push to reshape federal law enforcement after years of what Republicans argue were weaponized investigations targeting conservatives and Trump allies. His departure raises questions about continuity in FBI reform efforts—a priority for Colorado Republicans skeptical of federal overreach in state matters, particularly regarding election integrity and Second Amendment enforcement.

The timing matters: as the Trump administration pursues its agenda on border security, election integrity, and reducing regulatory capture across federal agencies, losing a trusted reformer at the FBI could slow momentum on internal accountability measures conservatives view as essential.

Democrats have characterized FBI leadership changes under Trump as attempts to politicize the agency; Republicans counter that the bureau requires serious reform after years of perceived bias against conservative targets.
