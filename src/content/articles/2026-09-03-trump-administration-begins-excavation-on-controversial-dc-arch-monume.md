# Trump Administration Breaks Ground on Controversial D.C. Monument

The Trump administration is moving forward with excavation work on a 250-foot gilded arch near Arlington National Cemetery, with Interior Secretary Doug Burgum announcing the project will begin within two weeks (Washington Examiner).

The so-called "Great Triumphal Arch" has already drawn criticism from preservationists and D.C. officials who argue the structure will disrupt historic sightlines in the nation's capital (9News Denver). The administration frames the project as long overdue, with Burgum noting the delay has stretched over 125 years.

**The conservative case:** Republicans backing the project see it as a legitimate commemoration of Trump's presidency and a symbol of American strength. The administration has the authority to proceed with federally approved construction on federal land.

**The opposition:** Critics—including some Republicans concerned about fiscal responsibility and historic preservation—worry the arch represents wasteful spending and architectural overreach in a protected area. Concerns about cost, maintenance, and visual impact on Arlington's solemn character have been raised.

For Colorado taxpayers and conservatives, this raises a broader question: Should federal resources go toward monumental construction projects, or toward deficit reduction and core government functions? Watch whether Congress attempts to block or defund the project through appropriations riders.
