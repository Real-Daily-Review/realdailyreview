# Cartels Deploy Hundreds of Drones at Border; Army Fights Back

The southern border crisis just got a high-tech dimension: the Department of Homeland Security has detected 378 cartel drones in just over a month, prompting U.S. Army counter-drone operations in Arizona and beyond (Fox News). Acting Secretary of the Army Adam Telle told The Daily Wire that soldiers are taking out cartel drones daily using "cutting-edge tools and techniques," underscoring how criminal organizations are weaponizing unmanned systems to scout smuggling routes and evade detection.

This escalation exposes the Biden-Harris administration's border failures in stark terms. While federal agencies scramble to deploy military assets to counter drone threats, cartels operate with impunity—a direct consequence of open-border policies and inadequate enforcement. Republicans argue that lax immigration enforcement and sanctuary policies have emboldened cartels to operate more brazenly and invest in sophisticated technology.

For Colorado, this matters: cartel drug trafficking networks funnel fentanyl and methamphetamine through the Southwest into mountain communities, fueling overdose deaths and crime. The drone surge signals cartels are expanding operations and confidence in their ability to move product north.

Conservatives say the real solution isn't just shooting down drones—it's securing the border, enforcing immigration law, and dismantling the cartels' supply chains through aggressive prosecution and cooperation with Mexico.
