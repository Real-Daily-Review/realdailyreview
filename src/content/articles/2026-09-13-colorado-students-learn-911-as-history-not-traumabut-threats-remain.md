# A Quarter-Century Later: Colorado Grapples With 9/11's Fading Legacy

A generation of Colorado students now studies 9/11 as history rather than lived trauma—and that shift raises hard questions about what America has learned, and what it's forgetting.

Twenty-five years after the attacks, younger Coloradans who never experienced that September morning are encountering the event through classroom curricula and memorial observances, according to CPR News. The distance is real: for many high schoolers, 9/11 belongs to textbooks, not memory.

But conservatives argue the nation faces a deeper reckoning. National Review warns that while "the terror is past," ideological threats persist—specifically that "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still." This framing challenges the progressive narrative that counterterrorism efforts have largely succeeded and that the post-9/11 security apparatus should be rolled back.

For Colorado, the stakes include how schools teach national security, immigration, and the balance between liberty and safety—perennial flashpoints in a state with significant military installations and a diverse population. The question isn't just historical: it's whether Colorado's next generation understands the stakes of vigilance or assumes the threat has vanished.
