# Denver Mayor Johnston's Reelection Bid Faces Unexpected Headwinds

Denver Mayor Mike Johnston's path to a second term is crumbling faster than his progressive allies expected. A new poll shows his favorability declining while challenger Melat Kiros gains ground—a dramatic shift from Johnston's 2023 victory (Denver Post). The political environment has shifted dramatically, and Johnston appears to lack the campaign urgency needed to recover (Colorado Sun).

The trouble deepens: the Denver Democratic Socialists of America endorsed Shontel Lewis against Johnston, signaling fracture within the city's left-wing base (Denver Post). For Colorado conservatives, this matters. Johnston's first term has pushed aggressive spending, homelessness policies, and regulatory expansion that have made Denver less business-friendly and more chaotic. His stumble suggests voters are tiring of progressive governance—a warning sign for similar policies statewide.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that Johnston's declining support reflects voter frustration with downtown deterioration, rising crime, and wasteful municipal spending. Democrats counter that Johnston simply needs better messaging and campaign organization.

Watch whether Johnston's weakness emboldens Republican candidates in statewide races, or whether Denver's leftward primary shift produces an even more progressive successor—either way, Colorado taxpayers should monitor how the winner's policies affect the state's largest city and its business climate.
