# Colorado Supreme Court Blocks Xcel's Power-Line Land Grab

Colorado's highest court just rejected Xcel Energy's attempt to seize 550 acres of Cheyenne County ranchland through eminent domain—a win for property rights and a check on corporate overreach dressed up as green energy.

Xcel wanted to condemn a strip of private land to connect two wind farms to a substation, but the Colorado Supreme Court rebuffed the utility's fast-track condemnation bid (Colorado Sun). The decision protects a fundamental principle conservatives hold dear: government and corporations shouldn't steamroll private landowners in the name of climate policy.

This matters beyond one ranch. As Colorado pushes aggressive renewable energy mandates, utilities increasingly rely on eminent domain to seize private property for transmission lines. Landowners—especially ranchers and farmers in rural counties—face mounting pressure to surrender their land at government-set prices, often far below market value.

The court's decision signals that even in a state trending progressive on energy policy, property rights still carry weight. Republicans and property-rights advocates argue that if wind and solar projects are truly economically viable, companies should negotiate fairly with landowners rather than resort to government seizure.

Meanwhile, Colorado's water future remains unsettled. Upper Basin states, including Colorado, narrowly avoided deeper cuts to Colorado River allocations in the latest federal decision—but as *Complete Colorado* notes, that reprieve is temporary (Complete Colorado). Watch for the next round of water negotiations and whether Xcel or other utilities attempt similar land-seizure tactics elsewhere in the state.
