# Social Media Stunts Leave Two Seriously Injured in Reckless Road Accidents

Chasing viral moments on social media continues to exact a painful price—literally. A Florida teenager attempting to film a haircut in the middle of a busy road was struck by a pickup truck involved in a separate crash, leaving him seriously injured (New York Post). Meanwhile, in Greeley, a 25-year-old e-scooter rider was determined at fault for a crash that caused serious injuries to herself while recording content last weekend (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

Both incidents underscore a troubling pattern: young adults prioritizing online clout over basic personal safety and public responsibility. The Florida case is particularly egregious—a late-night stunt that endangered not only the teen but also other motorists and first responders.

For Colorado, the Greeley incident raises questions about e-scooter regulations and rider accountability. Cities across the state have expanded scooter programs with minimal enforcement of safety rules. When riders ignore traffic laws to chase content, taxpayers foot the bill for emergency services and hospital care.

Conservatives argue these incidents reflect a broader cultural problem: the erosion of personal accountability and common sense, amplified by social media's gamification of risk-taking. Parents and schools should reinforce that no TikTok view is worth a trip to the ICU.

Watch for whether Colorado municipalities tighten e-scooter liability rules or impose stricter penalties for reckless riding.
