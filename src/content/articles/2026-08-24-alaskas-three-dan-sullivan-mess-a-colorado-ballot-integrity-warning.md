# Alaska's Dan Sullivan Chaos Is a Cautionary Tale for Colorado Ballot Access

Alaska's Senate race has devolved into a three-ring circus that should alarm Colorado conservatives about ballot integrity and candidate vetting. Two legitimate Dan Sullivans are running—incumbent Sen. Dan S. Sullivan and former Anchorage Mayor Dan A. Sullivan—while a third Dan Sullivan, whom Republicans argue is a Democratic plant, has muddied an already confusing race (Washington Examiner). The former mayor's endorsement of the incumbent underscores GOP concerns that ballot confusion could split the conservative vote and hand Democrats a seat they shouldn't win.

This mess exposes a real vulnerability in state election systems. Colorado's ballot access rules, while stricter than Alaska's, still permit candidates with identical or near-identical names to appear on the same ballot—a recipe for voter confusion and potential fraud. When voters can't easily distinguish between candidates, bad actors exploit the chaos.

Republicans have spent months denouncing what they see as a coordinated Democratic strategy to fracture the GOP vote through name confusion. The Washington Examiner reports this as "one of the most bewildering sagas of the 2026 political cycle"—and they're right. It's a cautionary tale about lax ballot access enforcement.

Colorado conservatives should demand the state legislature tighten candidate name-matching rules before 2026 to prevent similar confusion in our own races.
