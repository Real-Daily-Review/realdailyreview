# FAA Probes Safety Lapse in Trump Marine One Incident at Reagan National

The Federal Aviation Administration is investigating a potential safety violation after President Trump's Marine One helicopter departed the White House without air traffic controllers implementing standard hold procedures on commercial flights at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport, according to Just The News.

All aircraft landed safely and no injuries were reported, 9News Denver confirmed. The incident occurred Tuesday afternoon when the presidential helicopter took off while commercial traffic was still arriving and departing the airport—a procedural gap that typically triggers mandatory flight holds for presidential movements.

The investigation centers on whether FAA protocols were properly followed. Controllers are supposed to halt commercial operations during sensitive presidential flights to prevent airspace conflicts. The lapse raises questions about coordination between the White House, Secret Service, and FAA during high-security movements.

This incident underscores broader concerns about federal agency coordination and whether standard safety procedures are consistently enforced across different government operations. The FAA's investigation will determine whether procedural failures or miscommunication led to the oversight.

Coloradans should watch whether this prompts FAA policy reviews or congressional scrutiny of airport safety protocols affecting Denver International and other major hubs.
