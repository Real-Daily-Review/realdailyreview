# Colorado Faces Enrollment Cliff as High School Graduates Plummet 14% by 2041

Colorado's colleges are staring down a demographic crisis: the state's high school graduate pool is shrinking fast, with a projected 14% decline by 2041 driven largely by lower birth rates (Colorado Sun). This enrollment cliff threatens tuition revenue and state funding formulas built on growth assumptions—a reality Colorado higher education hasn't seriously reckoned with in decades.

The good news: the University of Northern Colorado is moving aggressively. UNC opened Colorado's third medical school, the College of Osteopathic Medicine, creating a direct pipeline to address the state's physician shortage (CPR News). That's market-driven problem-solving: identify a gap, fill it, train workers Colorado actually needs.

But here's the conservative concern: as enrollment drops, expect colleges to demand more taxpayer subsidies rather than right-sizing operations. Colorado universities have grown bloated with administrative overhead and ideological overhead alike. The enrollment cliff is an opportunity to cut waste, not a reason to raid the state treasury.

Conservatives should watch whether UNC's medical school success spurs other institutions to pursue practical, workforce-aligned programs—or whether they simply lobby for bigger state appropriations to prop up declining enrollment.
