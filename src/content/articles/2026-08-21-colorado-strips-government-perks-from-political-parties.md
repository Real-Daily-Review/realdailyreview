# Colorado Moves to Strip Government Perks From Political Parties

Colorado is taking a rare principled stand: ending state government's practice of granting special recognition and privileges to political parties, according to *Complete Colorado*. 

The move reflects a conservative argument that government should remain neutral territory, not a patronage machine for either major party. Republicans and conservatives have long objected to government agencies using taxpayer resources to elevate partisan interests—whether through special access, funding, or official endorsements.

This matters for Colorado taxpayers because it closes a backdoor through which public resources can flow to party machinery. When state government grants "special recognition or privileges" to parties, it's effectively using taxpayer dollars to subsidize partisan operations. That's corporate welfare for politics.

The decision aligns with limited-government principles: government should serve all citizens equally, not pick favorites among political organizations. It's a rebuke to the progressive tendency to use state power to advance preferred ideologies and parties.

Democrats haven't yet articulated a formal response, but the left typically argues that such recognition reflects democratic participation and civic engagement—framing that obscures the core issue: taxpayer money shouldn't fund party machinery.

Watch whether other Colorado agencies attempt to circumvent this policy or whether the state legislature tries to codify it into law.
