# Colorado Democrats Face Court Rebuke, Internal Fractures

Colorado's Democrat-controlled legislature just took a major hit: the state's high court blocked a gerrymandering scheme designed to redraw congressional districts in Democrats' favor (Complete Colorado). The ruling keeps Colorado out of the national gerrymandering wars—at least for now—and signals judicial skepticism of partisan mapmaking, regardless of which party attempts it.

The court's decision arrives as Colorado Democrats face deepening internal divisions. Complete Colorado reports that the party is experiencing "ravenous and public factionalism" reminiscent of Republican infighting from the past two decades. Progressive and moderate wings are clashing over spending priorities, energy policy, and regulatory overreach—fractures that could weaken Democratic unity heading into the next election cycle.

**The conservative angle:** Republicans argue that Democrats' attempt to gerrymander congressional seats exposed the party's willingness to abandon fair elections when convenient. The court's rejection of that plan vindicated Colorado's independent redistricting commission process, which conservatives have championed as a check on partisan manipulation.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, internal Democrat dysfunction could mean either gridlock (limiting new spending and regulation) or chaotic lurches between competing factions. Watch whether these divisions translate into actual legislative paralysis or whether Democrats paper over differences to pass new tax hikes and green mandates.
