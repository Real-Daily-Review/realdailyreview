# Trump Warns of Iran Assassination Threat as Security Concerns Mount

Former President Donald Trump is publicly declaring himself Iran's "No. 1" assassination target, citing longstanding tensions with the Islamic Republic and what he describes as credible threats to his life. Speaking at the NATO summit, Trump told the New York Post he has "left instructions" in case Iran succeeds in any plot against him, signaling serious concern about the threat level.

The warnings come amid renewed U.S.-Iran conflict and a mysterious Air Force One aircraft switch that raised security alarms, according to Fox News Politics. Trump said he's "been on their list for a long time," underscoring years of adversarial relations dating to his maximum-pressure Iran policy and the 2020 killing of Iranian General Qasem Soleimani.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue Trump's willingness to confront Iran directly—rather than appease it through deals like the Obama-era JCPOA—makes him a target but also demonstrates strength. Conservatives contend that weakness invites aggression, and Trump's deterrent posture protects American interests and allies.

Democrats have criticized Trump's Iran policy as reckless escalation, though specifics on their current response remain limited in available reporting.
