# Colorado School Districts Demand $177M More From Taxpayers While Dodging Accountability

Jefferson County Schools is asking voters for a $133 million tax increase even as the district battles federal litigation over its transgender-student policies—a stark reminder that Colorado's education establishment prioritizes spending over results.

Meanwhile, Denver Public Schools is pushing a $44 million mill levy override in November, with $25.8 million earmarked for employee raises rather than classroom improvements (Denver Post). Combined, these two major districts are demanding nearly $177 million in fresh tax dollars from already-burdened Colorado families.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Before asking taxpayers for more money, school boards should demonstrate fiscal responsibility and educational outcomes. Jeffco's federal lawsuit suggests the district is more focused on progressive social policies than core academics. Denver's plan to funnel most new revenue to employee compensation—not student achievement—raises hard questions about priorities.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue Colorado schools have received substantial funding increases in recent years. The question voters should ask: Where did the last round of tax hikes go, and why haven't test scores and graduation rates improved proportionally?

**Watch whether these ballot measures pass and how school boards spend the money if they do—it will signal whether Colorado voters still believe in accountability for public education.**
