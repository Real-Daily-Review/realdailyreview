# Denver-Area Motorcycle Fatalities Raise Road Safety Concerns

Two fatal motorcycle crashes in the Denver metro area within days underscore Colorado's persistent traffic safety challenge on urban and mountain corridors.

A motorcyclist was killed Sunday at the intersection of Speer Boulevard and Wewatta Street in Denver, according to 9News. Days earlier, Colorado State Patrol responded to a three-vehicle crash Friday at Highway 285 and County Road 62 near Guanella Pass that also claimed a motorcyclist's life (KDVR Fox31).

The incidents highlight vulnerabilities on both city streets and high-altitude mountain passes that draw riders year-round. Conservatives and safety advocates have long argued that Colorado's transportation infrastructure—particularly aging urban intersections and winding mountain roads—requires targeted maintenance investment rather than broad tax increases or transit-focused spending that diverts resources from road repair.

Republicans have consistently opposed Denver's push to redirect highway funding toward light rail and bus systems, contending that basic road maintenance and intersection safety improvements should take priority. The state's motorcycle fatality rate remains a concern for riders and families across Colorado's diverse terrain.

Taxpayers should monitor whether state transportation officials prioritize intersection safety upgrades and mountain corridor improvements over new transit projects.
