# Polish Court Crisis Escalates as President Races Home to Defend Judiciary

Poland's constitutional order is under siege. President Karol Nawrocki abruptly cut short a diplomatic trip to Senegal and raced back to Warsaw after Prime Minister Donald Tusk orchestrated a surprise removal of the Constitutional Tribunal's president—a move conservatives are calling a judicial power grab (Washington Examiner, Daily Wire).

Nawrocki arrived at the high court overnight Saturday to find riot police surrounding the building and his supporters clashing with officers outside. The dramatic confrontation underscores deepening fractures between Poland's conservative presidency and its left-leaning government over control of the judiciary.

Tusk's operation to suspend Tribunal President Bogdan Swieczkowski and install an interim replacement has drawn accusations of a "coup" from the right (Daily Wire). Police reported that demonstrators threw objects at officers, with three arrests made as crowds tried to enter the court complex (Daily Wire).

The standoff reflects a broader European pattern: progressive governments using institutional leverage to reshape judiciaries, often against conservative opposition. For Colorado conservatives watching judicial independence battles at home—from ballot initiatives to court-packing proposals—Poland's turmoil offers a cautionary tale about what happens when one party weaponizes the bench.
