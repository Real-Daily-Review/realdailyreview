# Trump's Endorsement Machine Rolls On—But Louisiana GOP Primary Was Uglier Than It Needed to Be

Trump's backing of Rep. Julia Letlow proved decisive in Louisiana's Republican Senate runoff, as she crushed state Treasurer John Fleming with nearly 45% of the vote to his roughly 28% (Washington Examiner). The victory extends Trump's track record of primary kingmaking and sets up a general election matchup against Democrat Jamie Davis, a rural farmer who won his party's runoff.

For conservatives, the result underscores Trump's continued grip on GOP primary voters—a fact that matters as Republicans eye Senate control heading into 2026. Letlow will face an uphill climb against Davis in the reliably red state, but the real story is how fractious the Republican primary became before Trump's endorsement settled the race.

Democrats, meanwhile, are banking on rural messaging and anti-establishment sentiment to chip away at GOP margins in red states. Davis's nomination signals the left's strategy of recruiting local candidates in traditionally Republican strongholds, though his path to victory in Louisiana remains steep.

Colorado Republicans should note the lesson: Trump's endorsement still moves primary voters decisively. As Colorado's own 2026 races take shape, expect similar dynamics to play out in GOP primaries here—and watch whether Democrats can replicate Davis's rural appeal in purple mountain districts.
