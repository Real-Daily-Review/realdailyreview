# EPA's Data-Center Loophole Won't Solve Colorado's Energy Planning Crisis

The Biden EPA's temporary guidance on data-center permitting is a classic bait-and-switch that leaves Colorado energy producers and taxpayers holding the bag. National Review reports the agency's short-term loophole won't give businesses the certainty needed for responsible long-term planning—meaning Colorado's power grid, already strained by progressive climate mandates, faces continued uncertainty as AI-driven demand explodes.

Here's the problem: temporary EPA guidance creates a regulatory mirage. Companies can't commit to major infrastructure investments—the kind that would mean good-paying jobs for Colorado workers and stable tax revenue for counties—without knowing the rules will stick around. Instead, businesses hedge their bets, delay decisions, and look elsewhere.

Republicans and energy advocates argue the EPA should issue clear, durable permitting standards that let the market work. Colorado's natural gas and coal regions have the infrastructure and workforce to power the AI economy, but not under a regime of regulatory whiplash.

Democrats counter that environmental safeguards must remain flexible to protect air and water quality.

The real issue: Colorado can't afford to lose energy-sector jobs and investment to states with clearer rules. Watch whether the incoming administration commits to permanent, predictable permitting standards—or whether the EPA keeps playing games.
