# NPS Offers Free Veterans Day Access to 64 National Parks

The National Park Service is waiving admission fees across all 64 national parks on Wednesday, November 11, honoring Veterans Day—a gesture conservatives can support as a modest, cost-effective way to thank those who served.

According to 9News Denver, the free-admission day removes typical entrance barriers for veterans and their families visiting iconic Colorado destinations like Rocky Mountain National Park and Great Sand Dunes. The move carries minimal fiscal impact compared to other government programs and aligns with bipartisan respect for military service.

However, conservatives should note the broader context: the NPS operates under federal budget constraints and regulatory frameworks that often prioritize environmental restrictions over public access and economic development. While this particular initiative is reasonable, taxpayers should remain vigilant about whether the agency uses such goodwill gestures to justify expanded budgets or additional land-use restrictions that limit hunting, energy development, or private recreation opportunities on public lands.

The free-admission policy is a one-day event with no permanent budget implications, making it uncontroversial. Still, Colorado conservatives should watch whether the NPS uses Veterans Day momentum to lobby for increased appropriations or regulatory authority—particularly regarding water rights, mineral leasing, or grazing permits that affect Colorado ranchers and energy workers.
