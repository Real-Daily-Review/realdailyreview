# Minneapolis Mayor Lifts Bathhouse Ban; Conservatives Warn of Public Health Risks

A Democratic Minneapolis mayor has signed an ordinance repealing the city's decades-old ban on gay sex bathhouses, citing support for "LGBTQIA+ neighbors." Mayor Jacob Frey's move has drawn sharp criticism from conservatives who argue the decision ignores lessons from the AIDS epidemic and prioritizes ideology over public health.

According to Breitbart, the ordinance removes restrictions on bathhouses that have been in place for years. The Daily Caller's analysis notes that critics say Frey "learned the wrong lesson from the AIDS epidemic," suggesting the policy reversal disregards documented disease transmission risks in such venues.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans and public health advocates argue that lifting disease-prevention ordinances in the name of progressive politics is reckless. They contend that bathhouses historically served as vectors for rapid disease spread and that repealing safety measures contradicts basic epidemiology—especially relevant post-COVID, when government health authority should be questioned, not expanded selectively.

**Democrats' Position:** City officials frame the repeal as anti-discrimination policy and personal liberty, arguing modern testing and prevention methods reduce historical risks.

**Colorado Angle:** While this Minneapolis decision doesn't directly affect Colorado law, it reflects the progressive regulatory philosophy that often influences Denver and Boulder policymaking. Coloradans should watch whether similar "equity-based" public health rollbacks gain traction in state or local government.
