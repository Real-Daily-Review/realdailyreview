# Kennedy Center Standoff: Feds vs. Preservationists as Trump Eyes Overhaul

Hundreds gathered Friday outside Washington's Kennedy Center in a human chain protest, fearing the Trump administration may demolish or radically alter the federally funded arts venue—raising hard questions about presidential power, congressional oversight, and whether taxpayer-funded cultural institutions should survive executive whim.

Actor Lynda Carter and other preservationists worry demolition is "a very real possibility," according to National Review. The controversy hinges on Trump's push to rename or reshape the center, which conservatives argue exemplifies how federal spending on cultural projects often escapes meaningful accountability.

National Review editors, while skeptical of Trump's methods, note the real problem: Congress has abdicated its constitutional duty. "This is yet another abuse of presidential power that only Congress can address," the outlet argues. Republicans should demand that lawmakers—not a president, regardless of party—control the fate of federal properties and the billions in taxpayer dollars flowing to arts institutions.

The Kennedy Center receives substantial federal appropriations annually. Coloradans footing the bill deserve clarity: Is this a legitimate debate about government waste and mission creep, or political theater? Either way, Congress must reassert control over federal assets before executive overreach becomes normalized.

Watch whether Republican lawmakers demand Congress reclaim its constitutional power of the purse on federal cultural spending.
