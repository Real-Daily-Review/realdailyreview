# DOJ Sues Colorado Over In-State Tuition for Undocumented Immigrants

The Trump administration is challenging Colorado's decision to offer in-state tuition and financial aid to students without legal status—a direct clash between state policy and federal law that puts Colorado taxpayers in the crosshairs.

The Justice Department filed suit against Colorado over the state's ASSET Act, which allows undocumented immigrants to qualify for reduced tuition rates at public universities and colleges (Fox News). The DOJ argues the policy unconstitutionally conflicts with federal immigration law and discriminates against U.S. citizens and legal residents who don't receive the same tuition breaks (9News Denver).

This marks the Trump administration's 14th lawsuit challenging similar in-state tuition programs nationwide. Republicans argue that Colorado taxpayers shouldn't subsidize education benefits for people in the country illegally—especially when U.S. citizens and legal immigrants don't get preferential rates. The policy effectively shifts education costs onto Colorado families and legal residents.

Democrats and progressive advocates counter that the ASSET Act helps talented students contribute to Colorado's economy and workforce, regardless of immigration status. They frame it as an education and economic development issue, not an immigration enforcement matter.

The case will likely hinge on whether states can offer tuition benefits based on residency alone, or whether federal immigration law preempts such policies. Colorado taxpayers should watch whether the state defends the ASSET Act aggressively or seeks a settlement—either path will signal how far Democratic leadership is willing to push back against federal authority.
