# Texas Bible Reading Mandate Reignites School Curriculum Wars—Colorado Should Take Note

Texas just became the first state in recent history to mandate Bible stories in K-12 public school curricula, and the move is already sparking a national debate over what belongs in classrooms.

The Texas State Board of Education, controlled by Republicans, voted Friday to approve the reading list. Supporters argue the Bible is foundational to Western literature, history, and law—and that teaching it doesn't violate the First Amendment, which permits states to include religious texts in secular educational contexts (Washington Examiner).

Critics, however, contend the mandate crosses the line between teaching *about* religion and promoting Christianity in public schools funded by all taxpayers, regardless of faith. They warn it sets a precedent for government-mandated religious instruction.

**The Colorado angle:** This matters here. Colorado's education establishment leans progressive, and the state's school boards have resisted similar curricular pushes. If Texas's move gains traction nationally, expect Colorado Democrats to dig in against any similar proposals—while conservative parents and school board candidates may cite Texas as proof that parental choice and traditional curricula deserve a hearing.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's Republican-leaning school board candidates adopt this as a campaign issue, and whether the state's education bureaucracy moves to preempt any local Bible-curriculum initiatives.
