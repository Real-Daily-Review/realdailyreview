# Colorado Voters Reject Polis-Era Growth Mandates in Twin Referendums

Colorado voters are drawing a clear line against state-imposed development schemes, rejecting both data center expansion and housing density mandates in back-to-back electoral rebukes to progressive planning.

A Fox News poll shows voters opposing data center construction by a 40-point margin, citing legitimate concerns over energy consumption, water depletion, and environmental degradation—issues that hit hard in water-scarce Colorado. Meanwhile, Lakewood voters rejected their city council's housing density push, which the council explicitly tied to Governor Polis' statewide density mandates.

The pattern is unmistakable: Coloradans are tired of top-down mandates from Denver that prioritize growth metrics over quality of life, water security, and neighborhood character. Polis' housing density push—framed as a solution to affordability—has instead become a vehicle for eroding local control and forcing density on communities that don't want it.

Republicans argue these votes vindicate their position that housing and infrastructure decisions belong at the local level, not in the governor's office. Democrats counter that density is necessary to address Colorado's housing shortage, though voters clearly disagree with the one-size-fits-all approach.

The real question: Will the legislature respect these electoral signals, or will Polis double down on state mandates that Colorado communities are actively rejecting?
