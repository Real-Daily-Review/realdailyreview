# Colorado Primary Turnout Hinges on Unaffiliated Voters as Both Parties Narrow Fields

Colorado's Republican and Democratic parties have winnowed their statewide primary fields through assembly processes, but the real test arrives as unaffiliated voters—now a majority of the electorate—receive primary ballots for the first time (Denver Post).

On the GOP side, only two of 18 gubernatorial candidates cleared the Republican assembly, a sharp culling that reflects party establishment control over the nomination process. Meanwhile, Democrats notably excluded heavyweight incumbents John Hickenlooper and Michael Bennet from their assembly proceedings (Complete Colorado), signaling potential fractures or strategic repositioning within the state party.

The historic shift: unaffiliated voters now outnumber both Democrats and Republicans in Colorado's registered electorate. Primary ballots began mailing Monday, meaning hundreds of thousands of no-party-affiliation voters will influence which candidates advance to November—a development that could reshape Colorado politics if turnout among this cohort exceeds traditional party-line participation.

Conservatives should watch whether unaffiliated voters break toward Republican nominees or splinter the GOP vote, and whether Democratic heavyweights' absence signals weakness or a calculated move to avoid primary bloodshed.
