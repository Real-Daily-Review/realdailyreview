# Trump's Kim Meeting Risks Legitimizing a Brutal Regime Without Concrete Gains for America

The Trump administration is preparing direct talks with North Korean dictator Kim Jong-un, a move that conservatives warn could hand propaganda wins to one of the world's most repressive regimes without securing meaningful concessions on nuclear weapons or human rights.

According to *Just The News*, Trump's planned meeting follows the administration's decision to downscale joint military exercises with South Korea—a move Trump attributed to Seoul's reluctance to support U.S. operations in Iran. The shift signals a dramatic recalibration of America's Korea strategy.

**The conservative concern:** *National Review* notes that North Korea remains "firmly within the China and Russia axis," meaning any bilateral U.S. engagement risks strengthening Beijing and Moscow's regional influence without weakening Pyongyang's nuclear arsenal. *Washington Examiner* opinion argues that Kim fears genuine peace with the South because it would expose his regime's economic and political failures to his own people.

Republicans worry that direct talks without preconditions—especially paired with reduced military readiness on the peninsula—could legitimize Kim's brutal dictatorship while yielding nothing on denuclearization, a core U.S. objective since the 1990s.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether Trump secures verifiable nuclear commitments or simply grants Kim the international legitimacy he craves.
