# Marine Vet's 16-Year Battle for Desert Storm Memorial Finally Pays Off

A North Carolina Marine veteran just proved that persistence beats bureaucratic foot-dragging: Scott Stump won his 16-year fight to build a Desert Storm memorial on the National Mall, *Fox News* reports. After years of navigating Congress and federal red tape, Stump's determination to honor America's forgotten heroes succeeded where most would have quit.

The victory matters beyond the Beltway. It's a reminder that government institutions often require relentless citizen pressure to do the right thing—and that veterans' causes shouldn't need a decade-and-a-half of fighting to get approved. Meanwhile, *The Daily Wire* notes that hundreds of American businesses are stepping up where government moves slowly, offering Veterans Day discounts, free meals, and special deals to active-duty service members and veterans on November 11.

The contrast is telling: private enterprise mobilizes quickly to honor service; federal bureaucracy grinds along at a snail's pace. Colorado veterans and business owners should take note of both examples—one showing what persistence can accomplish, the other showing that free-market patriotism often outpaces government action.

Watch whether Congress accelerates approval timelines for future veteran memorials, or whether citizens will need to wage another 16-year campaign.
