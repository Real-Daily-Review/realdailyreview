# Trump: Deportation Push Sparing Low-Skill Workers—For Now

President Trump says his mass deportation agenda is targeting violent criminals and drug dealers, not farm workers and hospitality staff. "Not even close," Trump told reporters, signaling the administration's enforcement priorities focus on criminal aliens rather than economic migrants filling labor gaps.

The distinction matters for Colorado's agricultural and tourism sectors, which depend heavily on migrant labor. Farm groups and hospitality operators have warned that aggressive deportations could cripple seasonal workforces and drive up labor costs—ultimately hitting consumers and small businesses.

Republicans argue the deportation push must distinguish between criminal aliens and workers, preventing unnecessary economic disruption while protecting public safety. Democrats and labor advocates counter that any mass deportation will inevitably separate families and destabilize industries reliant on immigrant labor, regardless of stated priorities.

Colorado ranchers and agribusinesses have quietly lobbied for clarity on enforcement targets. The state's $47 billion agricultural economy depends on migrant workers during harvest seasons—a reality that complicates hardline immigration rhetoric.

**Watch whether Trump's enforcement agencies actually follow through on the "violent criminals first" pledge, or whether labor shortages force a policy reversal.**
