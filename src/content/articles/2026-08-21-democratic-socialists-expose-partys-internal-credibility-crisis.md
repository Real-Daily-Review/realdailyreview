# Democratic Socialists Expose Party's Credibility Crisis

The Democratic Party's embrace of socialist candidates is creating visible fractures within its own ranks, as establishment figures dodge questions about their commitment to the movement's radical wing.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer refused to say whether he'd campaign with Michigan Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed, a socialist who won't commit to backing Schumer as party leader (Fox News). Meanwhile, Los Angeles socialist mayoral candidate Nithya Raman drew groans from a Democratic audience over her hiring decisions, and New York congressional candidate Darializa Avila Chevalier claimed focus on her general election campaign while touring Uruguay with fellow leftists (Breitbart).

**Why this matters for Colorado:** The Democratic Party's internal contradictions over socialism signal weakness heading into elections. Colorado Democrats, already struggling with moderate voter defection in swing districts, face pressure to distance themselves from or embrace these candidates—neither option polls well in purple regions like the Western Slope or suburban Denver.

Republicans argue the spectacle exposes Democrats' inability to police their own party's ideological drift. Schumer's dodge suggests even party leadership recognizes socialist candidates are electoral liabilities, yet lacks the spine to say so publicly.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado Democratic candidates running in competitive races distance themselves from national socialist figures or double down, revealing their true base.
