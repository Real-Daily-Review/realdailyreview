# Democrats' Leadership Fight Could Reshape Senate Agenda—Colorado Senators Stay Silent

Colorado's two Democratic senators are keeping their cards close as Senate Democrats battle over who runs their caucus next. New York Sen. Chuck Schumer faces a challenge from Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen in a secret-ballot contest that will hand unusual leverage to a dozen incoming freshman Democrats, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The stakes matter for Colorado. Van Hollen's backers—including Maine senator-elect Troy Jackson—are explicitly campaigning on eliminating the filibuster, ramming through Medicare for All, and hiking the minimum wage, per *Breitbart*. Those are precisely the kinds of federal mandates that would bypass state-level pushback and constrain Colorado businesses and workers.

Sen. Michael Bennet and Sen. John Hickenlooper have declined to publicly commit to either candidate, the *Denver Post* reports. That silence is telling: neither wants to alienate the party's progressive wing ahead of a leadership vote that could reshape Senate priorities for the next two years.

Republicans argue that a Van Hollen-led Senate would accelerate radical spending and regulatory overreach. Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's senators ultimately back the filibuster-killer—a vote that would signal how hard they're willing to push back on their party's left flank.
