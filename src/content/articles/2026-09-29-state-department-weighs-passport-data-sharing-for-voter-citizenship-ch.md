# State Department Eyes Passport Data to Verify Voter Citizenship—Colorado Should Demand Transparency

The State Department is exploring ways to share passport records with state and local election officials to cross-check voter registration rolls against citizenship status—a move conservatives say is overdue election security, while Democrats warn of privacy risks.

According to Fox News Politics, the proposal would allow officials to verify that only citizens are registered to vote, addressing long-standing concerns about non-citizen registration slipping through the cracks. Republicans have pushed for stronger citizenship verification mechanisms, arguing that election integrity depends on confirming voters are legally eligible.

Democrats counter that sharing federal passport data raises privacy concerns and could chill legitimate voter participation through bureaucratic friction. They've historically resisted citizenship verification efforts, framing them as voter suppression tactics.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado election officials should clarify whether they'd participate in such data-sharing and under what safeguards. Given Colorado's mail-in voting system and past disputes over voter rolls, transparency about citizenship verification protocols matters to taxpayers and election integrity advocates alike. The state's Democratic leadership will likely resist federal data-sharing without explicit statutory authority.

Conservatives should watch whether the Biden-Harris administration actually implements this or shelves it under pressure from the progressive wing—and whether Colorado Republicans demand their state participate.
