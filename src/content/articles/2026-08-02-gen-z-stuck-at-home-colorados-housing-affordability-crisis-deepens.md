# Gen Z's Housing Crisis: Multi-Generational Living Rises as Young Adults Struggle to Launch

Colorado's youngest workers are increasingly trapped in their parents' homes—a sign that progressive housing policies and economic headwinds are pricing an entire generation out of independence.

The Washington Examiner reports that Gen Z, with oldest members now pushing 30, faces a "failure to launch" crisis, with far more young adults living at home than previous generations. But there's a silver lining: a measurable uptick in multi-generational households, where grandparents, parents, and adult children share roofs rather than young people languishing in basements alone.

For Colorado specifically, this trend reflects the state's housing affordability crunch. Restrictive zoning laws, environmental regulations, and construction costs have priced starter homes beyond reach for entry-level workers—precisely the demographic that once drove Colorado's economy and tax base. When young people can't afford to buy or rent independently, they delay marriage, children, and consumer spending that fuels local economies.

Conservatives argue that loosening land-use restrictions, streamlining permitting, and reducing regulatory overhead on builders would unlock supply and lower prices. The multi-generational trend, while culturally valuable, shouldn't mask the underlying failure: government policy that makes it nearly impossible for a 28-year-old to afford a modest home in the state where they work.
