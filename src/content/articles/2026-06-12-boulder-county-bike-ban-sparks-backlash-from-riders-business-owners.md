# Boulder County's Bike Ban Threatens Local Economy and Recreation Freedom

Boulder County commissioners are pushing a plan to restrict mountain biking at Hall Ranch and Heil Valley Ranch—a move that's drawing fire from Lyons riders and business owners who say the county is overreaching into recreation policy without considering economic consequences (Colorado Sun).

The proposal would limit access to trails that draw tourists and support local shops. Mountain bikers argue the restrictions ignore their community's cultural and financial stake in the parks. They're warning commissioners that arbitrary trail closures will hurt small businesses dependent on outdoor recreation traffic—a familiar pattern of progressive land-use restrictions that prioritize environmental ideology over economic reality.

Boulder County hasn't released detailed justification for the limits, but the backlash signals what happens when government agencies make recreation decisions behind closed doors. Local business owners are rightfully pushing back: these are public lands that should serve the public, not be carved up by bureaucratic decree.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether county commissioners ignore community input and ram through the restrictions anyway—a test of whether local government still listens to constituents or simply imposes top-down policy.
