# Colorado's Health Care Cost Spiral: State Subsidies Making Problem Worse, Not Better

State subsidies meant to lower Colorado health care costs are instead propping up an expensive system that keeps getting pricier, according to analysis from Complete Colorado. Health care is Colorado's largest employment sector, yet costs continue climbing—a sign that government intervention isn't solving the underlying problem.

The real issue: when government subsidizes demand without addressing supply-side inefficiencies and regulatory barriers, prices rise to meet the subsidy. Colorado taxpayers foot the bill while hospitals and insurers pocket the difference. This is Economics 101, yet state policymakers keep doubling down on the subsidy model.

Meanwhile, rural Coloradans face a separate crisis. A state report cited by 9News found that American Indian and Alaska Native populations in rural communities experience premature death rates nearly double the statewide average—a stark health equity gap that subsidies alone won't fix. Rural Colorado needs better access to care and competition, not more government spending.

Conservatives argue the state should focus on deregulation, price transparency, and removing barriers to rural clinics and telemedicine rather than expanding subsidies that inflate costs system-wide. Taxpayers should watch whether the legislature pursues market-based reforms or simply raises taxes to fund more of the same failed approach.
