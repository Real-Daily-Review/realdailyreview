# Twenty-Five Years After 9/11: America Still Grappling With War's Unfinished Business

A quarter-century after the September 11 attacks, the nation confronts an uncomfortable truth: the war born from that day remains unresolved, and Congress continues to dodge accountability for how it's waged.

Students in Denver and across Colorado now study 9/11 as history rather than lived trauma (CPR News), a generational shift that underscores how far removed we've become from that defining moment. Yet the consequences of the post-9/11 era persist. The *Washington Examiner* reports that military families still bear the weight of decisions made in Washington—families like that of Jerry, whose brother was killed in Iraq, alongside friends Tony and Jeremy, who fell in Afghanistan in 2005, leaving behind widows and children.

The core conservative complaint: Congress has abdicated its constitutional war-powers responsibility. Rather than formally declaring war or regularly reassessing military commitments, lawmakers hide behind executive authority and indefinite authorizations passed decades ago. The *National Review* warns that ideological threats—"Sharia supremacism"—remain ascendant even as Americans assume the terror threat has passed (National Review).

For Colorado, this matters. Our military families and veterans communities continue sacrificing while political leaders avoid hard votes on foreign policy.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether Congress finally demands transparency and constitutional accountability for America's longest wars—or continues the abdication that honors neither the fallen nor the living.
