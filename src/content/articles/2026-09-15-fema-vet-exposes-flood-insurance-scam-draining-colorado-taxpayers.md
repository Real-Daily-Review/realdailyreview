# Federal Flood Insurance Subsidizes Wealthy Coastal Residents While Colorado Taxpayers Foot the Bill

A former FEMA director is calling out the National Flood Insurance Program as a taxpayer-funded giveaway to rich beachfront property owners—a scheme that drains resources from inland states like Colorado.

In a new book, the ex-FEMA official argues the program should be "shut down or radically transformed," noting he overrode Department of Homeland Security decisions to expose how the agency perpetuates coastal subsidies (Washington Examiner). The National Flood Insurance Program forces American taxpayers nationwide to underwrite below-market premiums for high-risk properties, effectively transferring wealth from middle-class families in landlocked Colorado to wealthy coastal developers and second-home owners.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Federal flood insurance is corporate welfare dressed up as disaster relief. It artificially props up coastal real estate values, removes market discipline from risky building decisions, and makes Colorado families subsidize someone else's oceanfront mansion. Taxpayers shouldn't be forced to absorb losses from predictable, recurring flooding in known high-risk zones.

Democrats and progressive coastal advocates defend the program as a safety net for disaster victims, but conservatives counter that it's become a permanent entitlement protecting the wealthy while distorting market signals about where development should occur.

Watch for whether Congress moves to reform or privatize flood insurance—a rare bipartisan frustration point that could finally crack open this decades-old subsidy scheme.
