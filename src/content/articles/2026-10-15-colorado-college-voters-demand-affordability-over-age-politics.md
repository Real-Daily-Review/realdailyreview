# Colorado Young Voters Demand Affordability Over Age Politics in Senate Race

Colorado's college voters are signaling they care far less about candidate age than about concrete solutions to skyrocketing housing and living costs—a reality that should alarm Democrats banking on generational turnout.

According to 9News Denver, six students at MSU Denver spelled out their Senate race priorities, with affordability taking center stage over the typical progressive fixation on candidate demographics. That's a warning sign for Democrats who've leaned heavily on "democracy is on the ballot" messaging while Colorado families struggle to afford rent and groceries.

Republicans argue that Democrats have spent years expanding government programs and regulations that drive up costs—from zoning restrictions that limit housing supply to energy mandates that spike utility bills. Conservatives say voters want results: lower housing costs, affordable energy, and fiscal responsibility—not lectures about age or identity politics.

The affordability crisis is real in Colorado. Young workers face median home prices that have climbed well beyond historical norms, and progressive policies in Denver and statewide have made it worse, not better. When college voters are more focused on their wallets than on partisan talking points, campaigns built on cultural grievances lose traction.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans capitalize on this opening by offering specific, market-based solutions to housing and cost-of-living crises—or whether they squander the opportunity.
