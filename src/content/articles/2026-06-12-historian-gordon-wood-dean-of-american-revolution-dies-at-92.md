# Historian Gordon Wood, Dean of American Revolution Scholarship, Dies at 92

Gordon S. Wood, the preeminent historian of the American Revolution whose rigorous scholarship shaped how generations understood the nation's founding, died in a traffic accident in East Providence, Rhode Island. He was 92.

Wood's interpretation of American history was both forward-looking and painstakingly committed to the truth—a standard increasingly rare in modern academia, where ideological narratives often trump factual rigor. His work on the founding generation provided conservatives and constitutionalists with scholarly ammunition against progressive reinterpretations of the Constitution and the founders' intent.

The "Dean of the American Revolution," as he was widely regarded, managed the rare feat of entering popular culture while maintaining academic credibility. His books and essays offered Americans—particularly those skeptical of revisionist history—a grounded, evidence-based understanding of what the founders actually believed and why they structured the republic as they did.

Wood's death removes a towering figure from public discourse at a moment when competing narratives about American history dominate political debate. His commitment to truth over ideology will be sorely missed as the nation approaches its 250th anniversary in 2026.

Watch for how universities and media outlets eulogize Wood—and whether they acknowledge his intellectual honesty or attempt to retrofit his legacy into progressive frameworks.
