# Colorado's Hickenlooper Signals 2026 Reelection Push as National GOP Fractures Over Trump

Colorado's Democratic Senator John Hickenlooper is already positioning himself for a 2026 reelection campaign, following a narrow primary victory and signaling priorities including opposition to Iran conflict and stricter immigration enforcement (CPR News). Meanwhile, national Republican fractures are widening: GOP Senator Mike Rounds of South Dakota has scrubbed Trump mentions from his campaign website as the president's approval ratings sink weeks before election day, a striking retreat that signals deepening doubts within the party (Fox News Politics).

For Colorado conservatives, Hickenlooper's early 2026 positioning matters. The senator has consistently backed Biden-Harris energy regulations that have hamstrung Colorado's oil and gas industry and water policies that pit Front Range growth against agricultural interests. Republicans argue his opposition to immigration enforcement and Iran hawkishness reflect a left-wing base that doesn't represent Colorado's working families and energy workers.

The Rounds defection underscores a larger GOP problem: when Trump's numbers crater, establishment Republicans abandon ship rather than defend or distance themselves clearly. That weakness invites Democratic challengers like Hickenlooper to run as the "reasonable" alternative.
