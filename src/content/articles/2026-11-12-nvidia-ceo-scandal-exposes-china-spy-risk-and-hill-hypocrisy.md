# Nvidia Scandal Exposes Silicon Valley's China Problem—and Congressional Hypocrisy

A national security crisis at Nvidia should alarm every American worried about tech dominance and foreign espionage—and it's already exposing the phoniness of Capitol Hill's stock-trading reformers.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang faces a federal investigation after allegations surfaced that he had an extramarital affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy (Breitbart). The company's board has hired a former federal prosecutor to probe the scandal, which threatens U.S. semiconductor security at a critical moment.

But here's the kicker: Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA), one of Congress's loudest advocates for banning lawmakers' stock trades to prevent conflicts of interest, quietly sold his Nvidia shares before the scandal broke public (The Daily Wire). So much for principle.

National Review notes the broader lesson: the U.S. should never have allowed the sale of advanced chips to China in the first place. When foreign adversaries are actively trying to penetrate American tech companies through espionage, selling them cutting-edge semiconductors is strategic suicide.

For Colorado, this matters. Our state hosts tech workers, data centers, and defense contractors who depend on a secure supply chain and trustworthy leadership in Silicon Valley.

Watch whether Congress actually tightens export controls on semiconductors to China—or whether this scandal quietly fades while politicians pocket their gains.
