# Governor Polis Orders State Agencies to Restrict Pesticides, Expand Pollinator Habitat

Governor Jared Polis signed an executive order Wednesday directing state agencies to reduce pesticide use and expand bee and butterfly habitat on state-owned lands—another example of Colorado's Democratic leadership using unilateral executive action to reshape environmental policy without legislative debate.

The order (per Colorado Sun and CPR News) mandates that state agencies monitor pollinator populations, plant native vegetation for pollinators, and curtail pesticide applications. Polis, drawing on his background as a beekeeper, framed the move as protecting Colorado's agricultural and natural ecosystems.

**The conservative concern:** Executive orders bypass the legislature and taxpayers' representatives. Republicans and limited-government advocates argue that broad pesticide restrictions—even on state land—can cascade into regulatory pressure on private farms and businesses. Colorado's agricultural sector, already facing water scarcity and regulatory headwinds, doesn't need unelected bureaucrats tightening chemical restrictions without cost-benefit analysis or stakeholder input. If the state wants to manage its own lands differently, fine—but Polis's track record suggests this is a template for broader restrictions.

Democrats counter that pollinator decline threatens crop yields and the state's $15 billion agricultural economy, requiring swift executive action.

**Watch next:** Whether state agencies use this order as a springboard to propose pesticide regulations affecting private agricultural operations and whether the legislature pushes back in 2027.
