# France Bans Social Media for Kids Under 15—Should America Follow?

France just became the first EU nation to ban social media for children under 15, and the move is sparking a debate that could reach American legislatures—including Colorado's.

French lawmakers voted Tuesday to approve the sweeping restriction, delivering a policy win to President Emmanuel Macron. According to France's health watchdog, roughly half of French teenagers spend two to five hours daily on smartphones, fueling concerns about digital harm to minors.

The ban reflects growing global momentum to regulate tech platforms' access to children. But conservatives and free-market advocates should pump the brakes here. While protecting kids matters, government-imposed blanket bans raise thorny questions: Who enforces age verification without creating a surveillance state? How do you prevent workarounds? And does this set a precedent for broader government control over speech and digital platforms?

Republicans have rightly criticized Big Tech's algorithmic manipulation of minors, but the answer isn't necessarily government prohibition—it's parental authority, transparency requirements, and market competition. Colorado parents and policymakers should watch whether progressive lawmakers use France's move to justify similar restrictions here, potentially empowering state bureaucrats to regulate tech in ways that could threaten free speech and innovation.
