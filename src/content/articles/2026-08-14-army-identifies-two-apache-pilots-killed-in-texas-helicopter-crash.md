# Army Identifies Two Fort Hood Pilots Killed in Apache Helicopter Crash

The Army has identified two Apache helicopter pilots killed in a crash near Fort Hood, Texas—a sobering reminder of the risks our military personnel face in training and operations.

Chief Warrant Officer 2 Deontre T. Huey, 34, and Warrant Officer Seth L. Olmstead, 25, were assigned to Bravo Company, 1st Battalion, 227th Aviation Regiment, 1st Air Cavalry Division. Their AH-64 Apache crashed in a field approximately 30 miles from Fort Hood on Tuesday; both were pronounced dead Wednesday (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

The cause of the mishap remains under investigation, with the Army yet to release details on what led to the incident.

Huey and Olmstead's deaths underscore the inherent dangers of military aviation operations—dangers that demand rigorous training standards, proper maintenance protocols, and accountability in our defense establishment. As the Army investigates, Colorado families with loved ones in military service will be watching closely for transparency on what happened and what safeguards exist to prevent similar tragedies.

Conservatives and military families should demand a thorough, public accounting of the investigation's findings once complete.
