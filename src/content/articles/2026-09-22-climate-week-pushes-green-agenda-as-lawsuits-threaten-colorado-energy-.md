# Climate Week Pushes Clean Energy While Courts Threaten Energy Sector Stability

Colorado's energy economy faces mounting legal jeopardy as global climate activists push aggressive net-zero agendas at UN Climate Week in New York, even as artificial intelligence and fossil fuel price volatility reshape the energy landscape (9News Denver).

The real threat isn't climate—it's the litigation tsunami. National Review warns that a "climate litigation free-for-all" is targeting the energy sector with lawsuits designed to bankrupt producers through the courts rather than democratic process. Republicans and industry advocates argue this weaponized litigation undermines energy independence, destroys jobs, and transfers wealth to trial lawyers—not to working families or the environment.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. The state's oil and gas industry supports thousands of jobs and generates billions in tax revenue for schools and infrastructure. Frivolous climate lawsuits threaten that economic engine and could force energy companies to relocate operations to friendlier jurisdictions, hollowing out rural Colorado communities that depend on energy development.

The Supreme Court has an opportunity to rein in this abuse. Conservatives argue courts should reject climate litigation designed to circumvent the legislative process and punish energy producers for lawful operations.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's congressional delegation and state leaders will defend the energy sector from coordinated litigation campaigns targeting Colorado-based producers.
