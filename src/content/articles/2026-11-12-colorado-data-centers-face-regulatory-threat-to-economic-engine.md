# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—If Regulators Don't Kill It

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are the invisible backbone of modern life, yet they face mounting regulatory pressure from environmental activists and progressive policymakers who view them as energy hogs rather than economic engines.

According to *Complete Colorado*, these facilities are essential infrastructure—not enemies of progress. They process the cloud computing, streaming, and AI applications that power everything from business operations to entertainment. Colorado's geography, reliable power grid, and fiber infrastructure have made the state a data center hub.

Yet conservatives warn that overregulation threatens this competitive advantage. As energy costs rise and environmental mandates multiply, data center operators may relocate to friendlier states. Colorado risks losing high-wage jobs and tax revenue if lawmakers treat these facilities as problems to be solved rather than assets to be protected.

The Left argues data centers consume excessive electricity and strain water resources—concerns that deserve serious consideration. But Republicans counter that blanket restrictions or punitive taxation would simply export jobs and economic activity elsewhere, leaving Colorado worse off.

**Watch for:** Whether the Colorado legislature pursues new data center regulations or tax increases in the 2027 session that could drive operators to Texas, Arizona, or other business-friendly states.
