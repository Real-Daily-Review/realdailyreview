# DOJ Targets Noncitizen Voting Fraud Across 17 States—Colorado Among Scrutinized Jurisdictions

The Justice Department has charged 64 foreign nationals with illegal voting and voter registration fraud across 17 states in a five-week enforcement push, raising fresh questions about election integrity in tight races nationwide (Fox News).

The crackdown underscores a core conservative concern: that lax voter verification systems enable noncitizens to cast ballots, potentially swinging close elections. Republicans have long argued that voter ID requirements and citizenship checks are essential safeguards, not barriers to legitimate voting. Democrats typically counter that noncitizen voting is rare and that stricter ID laws disproportionately burden legal voters.

Colorado, a swing state with razor-thin margins in recent elections, sits squarely in the DOJ's enforcement zone. The state's voter rolls and registration processes face renewed scrutiny as election officials confront questions about how noncitizens gained ballot access. Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold's office has not yet detailed how many noncitizens may have voted in the state or what corrective measures are underway.

The timing matters: with elections approaching, voters deserve confidence that only citizens cast ballots. Conservatives argue the DOJ action vindicates calls for tighter verification protocols; progressives warn that aggressive enforcement could chill legitimate voter participation.

Watch whether Colorado election officials release a full audit of noncitizen registrations and voting patterns—and whether the state tightens citizenship verification going forward.
