# NYC High-Rise Collapse Exposes Building Code Failures in Progressive Cities

A Midtown Manhattan construction site descended into chaos Tuesday when structural columns buckled and five floors caved in, forcing mass evacuations near Grand Central Terminal and the Chrysler Building. Falling bricks sent firefighters rushing to the busy corridor as officials declared the structure unstable.

The incident underscores a broader problem conservatives have long flagged: progressive cities' regulatory bloat often fails to prevent catastrophic failures. New York's Byzantine permitting system, dominated by union rules and Byzantine approval processes, creates perverse incentives—developers cut corners, inspectors miss critical flaws, and public safety suffers.

According to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31, the FDNY mounted a large-scale response as structural engineers assessed the damage. No deaths were immediately reported, but the evacuation radius expanded as officials determined the building posed ongoing danger to nearby workers and pedestrians.

Colorado developers and contractors should note the lesson: when government adds layers of regulation without improving actual safety oversight, corners get cut and disasters happen. The answer isn't more rules—it's competent, streamlined inspection and accountability for negligent contractors.

Watch whether New York's progressive leadership blames the collapse on "deregulation" rather than their own failed oversight apparatus.
