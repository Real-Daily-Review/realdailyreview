# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has devastated Beulah's water systems—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis now threatens the utilities and infrastructure that keep communities alive. According to the Colorado Sun, water managers are scrambling to restore clean water service after the blaze tore through the small town of roughly 800 residents, destroying homes and damaging critical water infrastructure. CPR News reports that residents mounted a fierce defense, but the fire's reach extended to utilities most other active Colorado fires have spared.

This is precisely the kind of cascading disaster conservatives warned about: when government fails to manage forests properly through thinning, controlled burns, and timber harvesting, the resulting megafires don't just burn homes—they cripple the basic services taxpayers depend on. Water restoration will be expensive and lengthy, placing additional strain on local budgets already stretched thin.

The broader problem: Colorado's regulatory environment has made forest management slower and costlier, leaving millions of acres overgrown and primed to burn. Beulah's water crisis is a direct consequence of that policy failure.

Watch whether state and local officials finally embrace aggressive forest management—or double down on restrictions that leave communities vulnerable.
