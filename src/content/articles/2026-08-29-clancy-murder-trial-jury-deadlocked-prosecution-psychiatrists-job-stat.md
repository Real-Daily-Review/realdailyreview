# Jury Deadlocked in Clancy Case as Key Prosecution Psychiatrist's Employment Status Raises Questions

A Massachusetts jury remains hung after 10 hours of deliberation in the Lindsay Clancy murder trial, with jurors requesting to examine evidence—including a knife and pill bottles—before being sent home for the weekend (New York Post). The case hinges on whether Clancy, a mother accused of killing her three children, was criminally responsible at the time, turning on psychiatric testimony about her mental state.

Complicating matters: the Daily Caller reports that Dr. Avram Mack, a psychiatrist who testified for the prosecution, appears to have been terminated from his position at Nemours Children's Health, listed as "no longer employed." The timing and circumstances surrounding his departure could fuel defense arguments about credibility and the reliability of expert testimony—a critical factor when jurors are already struggling to reach consensus.

The jury's specific requests for physical evidence suggest deliberators are closely examining the mechanics of the alleged crimes rather than accepting psychiatric conclusions wholesale. This signals potential skepticism about the prosecution's mental-state narrative.

Conservatives and skeptics of expansive insanity defenses will watch whether this hung jury leads to retrial, acquittal, or plea negotiation—and whether questions about the prosecution's key expert witness undermine confidence in the case's foundation.
