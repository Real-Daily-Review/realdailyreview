# Trump's Iran Strategy Fractures as Domestic Support Crumbles

A June memorandum of understanding between the U.S. and Iran has unraveled faster than most diplomatic agreements, with the Trump administration resuming full-scale strikes on Iranian targets by mid-July, according to the *Washington Examiner*. What was billed as a Middle East reset has instead reignited conflict—and Colorado voters are noticing the cost.

Support among Trump's own base is eroding. A *Politico* survey shows backing for continued Iran operations dropped from 50% in May to 37% in July among Trump supporters. Nearly one in five MAGA voters now favor ending the fighting regardless of consequences, while 57% cite rising gas prices as a direct concern tied to the conflict.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy prices matter here. Higher crude costs ripple through Colorado's economy—from pump prices to heating costs for rural families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation.

Republicans argue the administration must balance Middle East security interests against domestic economic pain. Conservatives worry that indefinite military engagement abroad diverts resources and political capital from border security and domestic priorities. Taxpayers are asking a straightforward question: what's the endgame, and who's paying for it?

Watch whether Trump recalibrates Iran policy before 2024 polling tightens further—or doubles down and risks alienating swing voters in energy-dependent states like Colorado.
