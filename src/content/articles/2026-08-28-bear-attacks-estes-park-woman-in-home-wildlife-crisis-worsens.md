# Bear Invades Estes Park Home, Injures Elderly Resident in Escalating Wildlife Crisis

Colorado's exploding bear population is now attacking residents inside their own homes—a stark reminder that progressive wildlife mismanagement and urban sprawl are colliding with predictable consequences.

An Estes Park woman in her 80s was attacked Thursday morning when a bear entered her kitchen, knocking her to the floor and scratching her abdomen, according to Colorado Parks and Wildlife and reports from 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31. The Colorado Sun characterized the incident as part of an "extraordinary bear year," underscoring a pattern of increasing human-wildlife conflict across the state.

The attack raises hard questions about Colorado's approach to bear management. As development pushes further into mountain communities and bear populations grow unchecked, residents face rising danger. Wildlife officials have long resisted aggressive population controls, citing environmental concerns—yet homeowners now pay the price in blood and fear.

Estes Park sits at the gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park, where bears roam freely and habituation to human areas has become routine. Without decisive action—including targeted removal of problem bears and stricter enforcement of food-storage regulations—these incidents will multiply.

Conservatives argue Colorado needs less environmental ideology and more practical wildlife management that prioritizes human safety over abstract preservation goals.

Watch for whether Colorado Parks and Wildlife will finally authorize aggressive bear removal or continue prioritizing bear welfare over resident protection.
