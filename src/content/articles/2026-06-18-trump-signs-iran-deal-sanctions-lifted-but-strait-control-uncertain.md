# US-Iran Deal Lifts Sanctions, Opens Strait—But Leaves Long-Term Control to Tehran

The Trump administration signed a memorandum of understanding with Iran on Wednesday that ends hostilities, reopens the Strait of Hormuz, and lifts U.S. sanctions—allowing Iran to sell oil freely, according to details from the White House and confirmed by 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31.

The agreement restores the status quo before the war and restarts nuclear negotiations. Iran will immediately reopen the critical shipping lane, while the U.S. lifts its naval blockade. The deal guarantees 60 days of toll-free passage through the strait.

**But conservatives flag a critical vulnerability:** The Daily Wire reports the agreement leaves long-term control of the Strait of Hormuz in Iran's hands alongside Oman and other Gulf states—opening the door to Iranian tolls that could choke global energy markets and Colorado's economy. Republicans worry the concessions hand Tehran a major win without permanent safeguards.

For Colorado, energy prices and oil-dependent sectors hinge on stable Middle East policy. If Iran gains leverage over shipping lanes, domestic energy development becomes more critical—yet the Biden-Harris administration has consistently opposed Colorado's oil and gas industry.

**Watch whether Congress demands oversight of the deal's long-term enforcement mechanisms and whether energy markets react to Iran's newfound leverage.**
