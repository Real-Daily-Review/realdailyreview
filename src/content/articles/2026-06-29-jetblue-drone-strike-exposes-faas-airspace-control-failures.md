# JetBlue Drone Strike Raises Questions About FAA's Airspace Control

A JetBlue Airbus A321 returning from Las Vegas to New York's JFK airport struck a drone mid-air Monday morning, according to pilot reports—a near-miss that underscores growing concerns about the FAA's ability to police American airspace against unauthorized unmanned aircraft.

The plane landed safely with no reported damage, but the incident highlights a regulatory gap that conservatives argue reflects broader government incompetence. While the FAA (Washington Examiner) launched an investigation into the collision, questions linger about how a drone penetrated controlled airspace near one of the nation's busiest airports.

Drone incidents have multiplied as the technology proliferates and regulations lag. The FAA has struggled to enforce airspace rules, particularly near major hubs where commercial traffic demands strict separation standards. This collision—occurring in daylight during approach to a major metropolitan airport—suggests enforcement remains inadequate.

For Colorado, where Denver International Airport handles significant traffic and where recreational and commercial drone use is expanding, the incident raises stakes for airspace safety and regulatory clarity. DIA and regional airports depend on FAA competence to prevent similar incidents.

Conservatives should watch whether the FAA tightens enforcement or whether bureaucratic foot-dragging continues while risks mount.
