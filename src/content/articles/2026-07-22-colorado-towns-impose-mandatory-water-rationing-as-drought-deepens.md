# Colorado Towns Tighten Water Rationing as Drought Drains Reserves

Castle Rock and La Veta are imposing mandatory water restrictions as Colorado's drought crisis deepens—a stark reminder that government planning failures and overallocation of water rights are catching up with Front Range growth.

Castle Rock has declared Stage 2 drought restrictions after water consumption remained well above sustainable levels, according to 9News Denver. Meanwhile, La Veta faces an even grimmer outlook: CPR News reports the mountain town has less than 150 days of water in reserve and has declared "worst case" restrictions, with supplies potentially exhausted by mid-December without improvement.

These crises expose a fundamental problem conservatives have long warned about: Colorado's water system was built on assumptions of reliable snowpack and river flows that climate variability and upstream demands have shattered. Rather than confront hard choices about growth limits or water rights reform, many local governments and the state have kicked the can down the road.

Residents now face mandatory rationing—a government-imposed restriction on personal water use—while politicians debate long-term solutions. Conservatives argue that market-based water trading and removing regulatory barriers to new storage and infrastructure would be more effective than rationing. Democrats and environmentalists typically resist such approaches, favoring conservation mandates instead.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state lawmakers will finally tackle water rights reform or continue betting on voluntary conservation and rationing.
