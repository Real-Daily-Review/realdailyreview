## Colorado Flood Victims Won't Go Without a Thanksgiving Meal

Volunteers are driving eight hours to deliver Thanksgiving meals to flood victims in Pagosa Springs — a round trip that will soon be cut in half as infrastructure recovers. This is what real community looks like: neighbors helping neighbors, no government program required. Southwest Colorado took a hard hit from flooding, and the private, volunteer-driven response is a reminder that civil society — not bureaucracy — is the first and best line of relief for rural Coloradans.

## Catholic Schools Take Maine's School Funding Shell Game to the Supreme Court

Catholic schools are asking the Supreme Court to strike down Maine's attempt to "outmaneuver" a prior ruling on public funding for religious schools. Separately, a Kentucky Catholic community's Virgin Mary prayer grotto has triggered a zoning fight now before the Court under the Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act. Conservatives say both cases cut to the heart of the First Amendment: government cannot selectively exclude religious institutions from public benefits or use local zoning as a weapon against faith communities. Colorado parents fighting for school choice and religious freedom should be paying close attention.
