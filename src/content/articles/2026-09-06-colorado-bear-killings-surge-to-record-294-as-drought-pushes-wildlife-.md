# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record High as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Suburbs

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push starving wildlife into residential areas across the state. The surge in human-bear encounters reflects a cascade of environmental pressures that state wildlife managers say leave few alternatives when bears become habituated to human food sources.

According to the Colorado Sun, the spike correlates directly with water scarcity and fire damage reducing natural food availability in the high country. Bears descending into subdivisions and towns create public safety hazards, forcing CPW's hand on lethal removal.

Conservatives and property owners argue the state must balance wildlife management with protecting Colorado families and their homes. Some question whether years of fire-suppression policy and water-management decisions have exacerbated conditions driving bears downslope. Others contend that stricter enforcement of garbage-storage rules and development setbacks in wildland-urban interfaces could reduce preventable conflicts before bears reach the kill list.

CPW maintains that education and deterrence work only when bears haven't already learned to raid trash cans and bird feeders. Once habituated, euthanasia becomes the only option.

Watch for CPW's 2027 budget requests and whether Colorado lawmakers will fund expanded wildlife corridors or stricter building codes in bear country—decisions that pit conservation against development and property rights.
