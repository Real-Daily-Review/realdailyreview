# Colorado Moves to Strip Special Privileges from Political Parties

Colorado is taking a rare principled stand: ending government favoritism toward political parties. According to Complete Colorado, the state is moving to separate government recognition and special privileges from party affiliation—a move the outlet calls "courageous" in an era of partisan overreach.

The initiative addresses a straightforward conservative concern: government should remain neutral and not grant official status or advantages to any political organization. Republicans and conservatives have long argued that state resources and recognition should serve taxpayers and citizens, not party machines. When government picks favorites among parties, it corrupts the purpose of public institutions and wastes taxpayer dollars on partisan infrastructure.

This Colorado action stands apart from the typical progressive expansion of government power. Rather than creating new programs or regulations, the state is actually *reducing* government's entanglement with party politics—a refreshing contrast to how Democratic-led legislatures typically expand state involvement in every sector of life.

The move reflects growing frustration across the political spectrum with partisan capture of state institutions. Coloradans should watch whether this principle extends to other areas where government has carved out special treatment for favored groups or ideologies.
