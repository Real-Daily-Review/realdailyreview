# Colorado Must Not Forget 9/11's Lessons on Security and Resolve

As another September 11 anniversary passes, Colorado conservatives face a hard question: Are we still committed to the vigilance and strength that tragedy demanded?

Twenty-plus years after the attacks, the memory has faded for many Americans—especially younger generations who didn't live through that morning. National Review warns that we risk losing the historical reckoning itself (National Review). Breitbart's visual chronicle documents not just the attacks but the two decades of military response that followed, underscoring the cost of American resolve in the post-9/11 world.

The deeper conservative concern is this: as memory dims, so does the political will to maintain the security posture and military readiness that 9/11 proved necessary. The left has long pushed to wind down overseas commitments and reduce defense spending—arguments that gain traction when the urgency of that September morning feels distant.

For Colorado, home to major military installations, defense contractors, and veterans communities, the stakes are direct. Weakening America's security posture or gutting defense budgets hits Colorado jobs and our ability to deter future threats.

Conservatives argue that remembering 9/11 means more than ceremony; it means sustaining the military strength, intelligence capabilities, and border security that prevent another attack.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—especially those facing 2026 races—will defend robust defense spending when progressive voices demand cuts.
