# U.S. Indicts Former Cuban President Raúl Castro Over 1996 Plane Shootdown

The Trump administration has indicted Raúl Castro, Cuba's 94-year-old former president, on charges including conspiracy to kill U.S. nationals, four counts of murder, and two counts of destruction of aircraft, according to NPR and The Guardian. The charges stem from a 1996 incident in which Cuban forces allegedly shot down planes operated by Brothers to the Rescue, a humanitarian group of exiles.

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the indictment in South Florida during a ceremony honoring the victims, according to the Washington Examiner. The move marks a significant escalation in tensions between Washington and Havana, according to Al Jazeera, and reflects the Trump administration's broader pressure campaign against Cuba's communist government.

Castro, who led Cuba for more than a decade before stepping down in 2008, is one of five others also charged in connection with the incident. The indictment targets a political figure outside U.S. borders, raising questions about the Justice Department's expanding approach to prosecuting foreign officials, according to The Hill.
