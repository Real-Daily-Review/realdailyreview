# Socialist Airline Scheme Shows Why Colorado Must Reject Left-Wing Economics

A New York state Democratic socialist is pushing a government-run airline—and conservatives are sounding the alarm that this fantasy belongs nowhere near Colorado's economy or national policy.

State legislator Zohran Mamdani has proposed a publicly owned airline, framed as a solution to high airfares. But the Washington Examiner and New York Post warn this is socialism dressed up in modern language. One op-ed writer, who lived through Soviet-era bread lines, argues America doesn't need a "sequel" to failed command economies—and that Mamdani's proposal masks the "cold cruelty" of central planning that leaves citizens worse off.

Republicans argue government-run airlines inevitably become bloated, inefficient, and dependent on endless taxpayer subsidies. They point to historical failures: Aeroflot's notorious service, and domestic experiments that burned through public money. The free market, conservatives say, drives down prices and improves service far better than bureaucrats ever could.

For Colorado, this matters. Denver International Airport serves as a major hub; any federal push toward government aviation would threaten private carriers, jobs, and the competitive market that keeps fares reasonable for Coloradans traveling to ski resorts, business centers, and family.

Watch whether this fringe idea gains traction in Democratic leadership—and whether Colorado's delegation pushes back or entertains it.
