# ActBlue Co-Founder Invokes Fifth Amendment as GOP Probes Fraud Controls

ActBlue co-founder Matt DeBergalis refused to answer questions during a closed-door House GOP deposition, pleading the Fifth Amendment rather than address concerns about the Democratic fundraising platform's fraud prevention standards, according to Fox News and Just The News.

DeBergalis joins ActBlue CEO Regina Wallace-Jones in invoking Fifth Amendment protections during congressional questioning. Wallace-Jones did the same during a House Administration Committee hearing in June, signaling a pattern of silence from ActBlue leadership as Republicans investigate whether the platform adequately safeguards against fraudulent donations.

Republicans have raised alarm over ActBlue's role in processing millions in small-dollar donations for Democratic candidates and causes, questioning whether lax verification procedures enabled bad actors to exploit the system. The silence from top executives fuels conservative concerns that ActBlue may have prioritized transaction volume over security—a particular worry for Colorado Republicans watching how national Democratic infrastructure operates.

Democrats have defended ActBlue's security measures, but the repeated invocation of Fifth Amendment rights by company leadership suggests the investigation is hitting sensitive ground. House Republicans are unlikely to accept silence as an answer, and expect pressure to mount for voluntary testimony or subpoenas.

Watch whether House Republicans escalate to compulsory testimony or refer the matter to the Justice Department for criminal investigation.
