# Trump Administration Moves on Border Security, Nuclear Strategy

President Trump is closing legal loopholes that have exploited American citizenship law while simultaneously expanding tactical nuclear options to counter rising global threats.

The administration ended "birth tourism"—the practice of foreign nationals entering the U.S. specifically to give birth and secure citizenship for their children—a move conservatives have long demanded. The Daily Wire reports this addresses what critics call a massive loophole in birthright citizenship that has been systematically abused. Republicans argue the policy corrects a vulnerability in immigration enforcement that has cost taxpayers resources while undermining the integrity of citizenship itself.

Separately, NBC reports the Pentagon is developing new tactical nuclear weapons options under a classified strategy led by Elbridge Colby, positioning the Trump administration to respond more flexibly to threats from China, Russia, and other adversaries. This represents a shift toward credible deterrence capabilities that conservatives say are necessary given deteriorating global security conditions.

For Colorado, these moves signal a hardline on border security that aligns with state concerns over illegal immigration's fiscal impact, while the nuclear posture reflects the administration's commitment to American military strength—relevant to Colorado's defense industry and military installations.

Watch whether Congress will fund these nuclear modernization efforts and how the birth tourism ban withstands inevitable legal challenges from open-borders advocates.
