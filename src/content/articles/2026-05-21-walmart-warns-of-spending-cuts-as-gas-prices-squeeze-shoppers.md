# US Retailers Split on Consumer Spending as Gas Prices Bite

Walmart warned that American shoppers are cutting spending due to elevated gas prices, signaling a potential slowdown in consumer demand, while telecommunications giant Verizon reported strong earnings boosted by price increases and wireless customer gains.

Walmart issued a worse-than-expected outlook for coming months, citing pressure from higher fuel costs on household budgets, according to CNBC. The retailer's stock fell seven percent following the announcement. CFO John David Rainey noted that higher tax returns helped offset some of the gas-price impact in the first quarter, per CNBC.

The divergence reflects different dynamics across sectors. Verizon's quarterly earnings beat Wall Street forecasts as the company raised prices and added wireless customers, according to the Wall Street Journal. The telecommunications firm's ability to pass costs to consumers contrasts with Walmart's more cautious consumer outlook.

Walmart's warning suggests price-sensitive shoppers—a key demographic for the discount retailer—are adjusting purchasing habits in response to fuel costs. The company's guidance carries weight as a bellwether for broader consumer health.

Watch for whether other major retailers echo Walmart's concerns in coming earnings reports, and whether Verizon's pricing power holds as consumer pressure potentially mounts.
