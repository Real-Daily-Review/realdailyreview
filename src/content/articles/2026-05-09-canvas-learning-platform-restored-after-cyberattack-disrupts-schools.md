# Canvas Learning Platform Restored After Cyberattack Disrupts Schools

The Canvas online education platform returned to service Friday following a cyberattack that forced thousands of schools and universities to suspend operations during a critical period for students and instructors.

The outage caused widespread disruption across the country, with schools postponing year-end tests and exams as students and teachers lost access to the platform, according to Ars Technica. The incident has intensified scrutiny of educational institutions' reliance on centralized digital platforms and their cybersecurity preparedness.

Threat intelligence analyst Luke Connolly told PBS NewsHour that the attack underscores broader vulnerabilities in schools' infrastructure and raises questions about the risks inherent in depending heavily on single platforms for educational delivery. The cyberattack has prompted renewed discussion about institutional safeguards and contingency planning for digital learning systems.

Canvas, which serves as a learning management system for educational institutions nationwide, restored most functionality by Friday, though the full scope of the attack's impact remains under assessment. The incident comes as schools increasingly rely on cloud-based platforms for instruction, assessment, and administrative functions.

Watch for statements from Canvas's parent company and school districts regarding security measures and backup protocols implemented in response to the breach.
