# 1619 Project Creator's School Gamble Exposes Progressive Education Hypocrisy

Nikole Hannah-Jones, the architect of the 1619 Project, enrolled her daughter in a failing New York City public school to prove she wasn't a hypocrite—then pulled her out anyway. Conservative outlets note the real scandal: Hannah-Jones, a multimillionaire, had the means to choose better but deliberately subjected her child to a failing system to make an ideological point (National Review, New York Post).

The move reveals what conservatives have long argued about progressive education activists: they demand other families accept government-run schools while protecting their own children from the consequences. Hannah-Jones's subsequent acknowledgment that the school failed her daughter rings hollow to critics who say she should have directed her fire at the teachers union and bureaucratic dysfunction—not pretended the system works when it clearly doesn't.

For Colorado families, this story underscores a familiar pattern: wealthy progressives and union allies preach public-school loyalty while opting out themselves. Meanwhile, middle-class Colorado parents face the same failing schools without the financial escape route. The hypocrisy exposes why school choice—vouchers, education savings accounts, charter expansion—remains essential for families locked out of private options.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats acknowledge this contradiction as school-choice legislation returns to the legislature.
