# Denver's Water Grab From Western Slope Exposes Front Range Conservation Failure

Denver-area residents failed to conserve water this summer, and now the Western Slope is paying the price. The Colorado Sun reports that tepid conservation efforts on the Front Range have forced water managers to tap deeper into Western Slope reserves—a move that rankles rural Colorado communities already wary of Front Range water politics.

The core problem: Denver-area residents didn't do their part. Rather than tighten their own belts, Front Range water districts are reaching across the Continental Divide to make up the shortfall. Western Coloradans see this as confirmation of a long-standing grievance—that the Front Range treats the Western Slope as a water piggy bank while ignoring its own conservation obligations.

Conservatives and rural Colorado leaders argue this exposes the failure of voluntary conservation messaging. When government relies on residents to self-police water use and they don't comply, bureaucrats simply redirect supply from someone else's backyard instead of enforcing real accountability on heavy users and wasteful practices.

The real issue: Denver-area water policy has become a subsidy for non-conservation. Until Front Range residents face genuine scarcity—higher rates, mandatory restrictions, or actual supply limits—they have no incentive to change behavior. Meanwhile, Western Slope agriculture and communities absorb the cost.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature will demand Front Range accountability or continue enabling the status quo.
