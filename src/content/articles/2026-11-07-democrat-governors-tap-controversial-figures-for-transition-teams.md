# Democrat Governors Tap Controversial Figures for Transition Teams, Raising Vetting Questions

Democrat governors-elect are stacking their transition teams with figures shadowed by serious controversies, raising questions about their vetting standards and judgment.

Michigan Governor-elect Jocelyn Benson appointed a Nation of Islam leader with a documented history of antisemitic rhetoric and defense of Louis Farrakhan to her transition team—months after an antisemitism coalition had already removed the same individual from an advisory role for those exact affiliations, according to The Daily Wire. Meanwhile, Georgia's Keisha Lance Bottoms named media mogul Tyler Perry to her transition team despite ongoing sexual assault and harassment allegations against him, Breitbart reports.

Republicans argue these appointments signal either reckless vetting or a troubling willingness to overlook serious character concerns in pursuit of political allies. The pattern raises concerns about the judgment these incoming administrations will bring to executive decisions affecting their states' economies, law enforcement, and regulatory environments.

Colorado conservatives should note: these transition team choices often preview the caliber of appointees who will fill cabinet and agency positions. Watch whether these governors follow through on controversial hires and how their early personnel decisions shape their administrations' policy direction.
