# Congress Takes Bipartisan Step on Housing Supply—But Colorado's Real Problem Remains Regulatory Overreach

Congress has finally acknowledged what free-market advocates have long argued: America's housing crisis stems from insufficient supply, not market failure. Recent bipartisan legislation addresses housing production, a welcome recognition that policymakers can no longer ignore the nation's acute shortage of homes (Washington Examiner).

However, the real affordability challenge extends beyond supply. Rising construction costs—driven by labor shortages, material inflation, and regulatory compliance—continue to price out middle-class families across Colorado and the nation. Congress addressed supply; the next step must tackle the cost side of the equation.

For Colorado specifically, this matters enormously. Denver and Front Range communities face explosive population growth, yet zoning restrictions, environmental reviews, and local permitting delays artificially constrain new housing. Conservatives argue that reducing regulatory friction—not expanding government programs—offers the most direct path to affordability.

Taxpayers should watch whether Colorado's legislature follows Congress's lead by streamlining local zoning codes and cutting red tape for developers, or whether it doubles down on subsidies and rent control schemes that only worsen shortages.
