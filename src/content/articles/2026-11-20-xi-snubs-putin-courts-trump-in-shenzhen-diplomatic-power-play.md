# Trump's Shenzhen Summit Shows Xi Playing Putin for a Fool

President Trump's high-profile meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen this week delivered a stark diplomatic message: Beijing is recalibrating its global alliances, and Moscow is getting the short end of the stick.

Xi broke decades of protocol by meeting Trump at the tarmac—a ceremonial honor he withheld when Trump visited Beijing in May (Washington Examiner). The gesture signals China's prioritization of U.S. engagement over Russian interests, even as Putin attended the same APAC summit. According to the Examiner's analysis, Beijing's diplomatic choreography ranked its guests carefully, with Putin landing in a secondary position despite his ongoing war in Ukraine.

The optics matter. By elevating Trump while keeping Putin at arm's length, Xi is hedging Beijing's bets in a multipolar world. Russia's isolation deepens as China pursues its own strategic interests—a reality conservatives should welcome as a potential wedge in the authoritarian axis.

For Colorado, this realignment underscores why energy independence and strong American leadership remain critical. A fractured China-Russia partnership weakens adversarial pressure on U.S. interests, including our ability to develop domestic energy resources without foreign interference.

Watch whether Trump leverages this diplomatic advantage into concrete concessions on trade, Taiwan, or technology—or whether Beijing's charm offensive proves merely tactical.
