# Knicks Accept Trump White House Invite After Championship Win

The New York Knicks are heading to the White House—a stark contrast to the NBA's recent pattern of snubbing Republican administrations.

Knicks owner James Dolan announced Wednesday that the team accepted President Donald Trump's invitation following their first NBA championship since 1973, which they clinched last week by defeating the San Antonio Spurs 4-1 (Just The News). Dolan, who attended Game 3 of the Finals sitting with Trump, made the announcement directly.

The visit marks a cultural shift. Under previous administrations, championship teams—particularly those with outspoken progressive players—frequently declined White House invitations or made them politically contentious affairs. The Knicks' acceptance signals either a thaw in the NBA's relationship with Republican leadership or, more likely, recognition that championship celebrations transcend partisan grievance.

Meanwhile, the Trump administration has been actively courting sports figures. UFC President Dana White recently gave Trump's granddaughter Kai Trump a behind-the-scenes look at UFC 250 Freedom, held on the White House lawn June 14 (The Daily Wire), underscoring the administration's effort to reclaim sports as nonpolitical American traditions rather than platforms for progressive activism.
