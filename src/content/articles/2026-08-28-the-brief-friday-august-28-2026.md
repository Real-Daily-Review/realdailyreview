# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, August 28, 2026

Colorado conservatives head into the weekend watching a governor use his final year in office to hand out pardons like candy, a congressional race tighten in ways nobody expected, and local governments wrestling with surveillance cameras and election-law fights. Buckle up.

---

## Colorado Springs Faces Lawsuit Over April Municipal Elections

Colorado Springs is being sued over its practice of holding municipal elections in April — a scheduling choice critics argue suppresses turnout and insulates incumbents from broader electoral accountability. Republicans and election-integrity advocates have long argued that odd-date, low-turnout elections benefit entrenched political interests rather than the voters they're supposed to serve. Colorado taxpayers deserve elections held when the most citizens can participate, and courts should scrutinize whether April scheduling games the system against ordinary Coloradans.

---

## Polis Hands Out 29 More Pardons in Second Clemency Round This Year

Governor Jared Polis issued 29 pardons in his second clemency round of 2026, with more pardons and commutations still possible before he leaves office. This follows his earlier commutation of former Mesa County Clerk Tina Peters' sentence. Conservatives should ask plainly: who is Polis pardoning, on what grounds, and why the rush in his final year? Clemency power exists for genuine justice — not as a parting gift to favored constituencies. Colorado voters deserve a full accounting of every name on that list before Polis walks out the door.

---

## Rep. Jeff Hurd Distances Himself From Trump as CD-3 Race Tightens

Congressman Jeff Hurd is publicly touting his disagreements with President Trump as his 3rd Congressional District reelection bid grows unexpectedly competitive. What looked like a comfortable race at the start of the cycle has tightened, and Hurd is apparently calculating that daylight from Trump helps him survive. Colorado conservatives in the Western Slope district should watch carefully: running away from the president's agenda may play well in a Denver newsroom, but it's a risky bet with a base that sent a Trump-aligned representative to Washington in the first place.

---

## Summit County Pauses Flock Cameras Amid Public Backlash

Summit County has shut off its Flock license-plate surveillance cameras for 30 days following public concerns, though the cameras won't issue tickets during a warning period running through Sept. 24. The pause is a small but real win for Coloradans who are rightly skeptical of government building out surveillance infrastructure under the guise of public safety. Conservatives say the burden of proof is on government to justify mass data collection on law-abiding citizens — not on citizens to prove they deserve privacy.

---

## HHS Study Links Transgender Ideology to Justification of Political Violence

A new HHS study has found a link between transgender ideology and the justification of political violence, with the agency calling for more rigorous research into the findings. The left will dismiss this, but conservatives argue that government-funded research should follow the evidence wherever it leads — not bury findings that challenge progressive orthodoxy. For Colorado parents, school boards, and policymakers already fighting battles over gender ideology in classrooms, this study is another data point that demands honest public debate, not suppression.
