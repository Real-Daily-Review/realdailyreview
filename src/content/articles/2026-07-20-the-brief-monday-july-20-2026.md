# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, July 20, 2026

Colorado conservatives start the week with a homegrown legal fight over election law, a push to untangle state government from partisan politics, and a sobering reminder that Denver's public safety crisis isn't going away on its own.

---

## Colorado Springs Faces Lawsuit Over April Municipal Elections

Colorado Springs is being sued for holding its municipal elections in April rather than consolidating them with November elections — the first enforcement action since a 2025 state voting law took effect. Republicans and election-integrity advocates have long argued that off-cycle elections suppress turnout and benefit entrenched political interests. Colorado conservatives should watch this closely: if the courts side with plaintiffs, it could reshape how dozens of Colorado municipalities run their elections — and who shows up to decide local races.

---

## Colorado Moves to Separate State Government From Political Parties

A new push in Colorado would strip state government of its role in granting special recognition or privileges to political parties — and conservatives are calling it the right call. Complete Colorado frames the move as "courageous," and they're not wrong. Government has no business picking winners among political organizations, full stop. This is the kind of principled, limited-government reform that should have happened years ago, and Colorado taxpayers shouldn't be footing the bill for party infrastructure dressed up as civic function.

---

## Double Shooting Near Congress Park Leaves One Dead

Denver police are investigating a double shooting in the 1100-block of Columbine Street near Congress Park that left one person dead. This is not an isolated incident — it's part of a pattern in a city where progressive leadership has spent years undermining law enforcement, resisting serious consequences for violent crime, and treating public safety as an afterthought. Denver residents deserve better than a city government more focused on social experiments than keeping neighborhoods safe.

---

## Trump Joins Spain Onstage as World Cup Wraps in the U.S.

President Trump appeared onstage with FIFA President Gianni Infantino to celebrate Spain's dramatic 1-0 extra-time victory over Argentina in the World Cup final — a tournament hosted, in part, on American soil. Whatever your feelings about soccer, the optics matter: the United States pulled off a massive international event, and the President was front and center for the moment. For Colorado, which hosted World Cup matches, the successful tournament is a win for the state's economy and its reputation as a world-class venue.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado Springs election lawsuit — it's the first test of the 2025 consolidation law, and the outcome will set precedent for municipal elections statewide. Watch also for any legislative or legal response to the state-party separation proposal; the political class rarely gives up its perks without a fight. And Denver's ongoing crime problem demands accountability from city leadership — conservatives should be pressing hard on whether Mayor Mike Johnston's policies are making the streets safer or just making excuses.
