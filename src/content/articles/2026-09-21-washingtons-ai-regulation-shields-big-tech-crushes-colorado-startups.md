# Big Tech's Regulatory Robbery: How Washington Protects Giants at Taxpayers' Expense

Washington's AI regulation regime isn't capturing big tech—it's *becoming* big tech's best friend, and Colorado taxpayers and small businesses are footing the bill.

The Washington Examiner warns that while regulators claim to police artificial intelligence companies, the real effect is regulatory capture in disguise. Government rules designed to "control" AI giants actually entrench their market dominance by imposing compliance costs that smaller competitors and startups cannot afford. The result: fewer choices, higher barriers to entry, and innovation crushed under bureaucratic weight.

This pattern is familiar to anyone watching Washington work. Regulators end up serving the regulated—not the public. Big corporations lobby for rules that sound tough but are engineered to eliminate competition. Smaller firms, workers, and customers pay the price.

**The Colorado angle:** As tech companies expand operations across the Mountain West, overregulation tilts the playing field away from Colorado startups and entrepreneurs who lack the legal armies of Silicon Valley giants. Burdensome federal AI rules will flow downstream to Colorado businesses trying to adopt the technology.

Republicans argue that innovation thrives under light-touch regulation, not bureaucratic permission slips. Democrats counter that AI requires guardrails—but their guardrails consistently benefit the incumbents they claim to restrain.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes back against federal AI rules that would handicap local tech entrepreneurs.
