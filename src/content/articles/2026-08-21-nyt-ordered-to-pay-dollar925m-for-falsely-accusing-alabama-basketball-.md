# New York Times Hit With $9.25M Defamation Judgment for False Murder Accusation

A federal jury has ordered The New York Times to pay $9.25 million in damages to Kai Spears, a former University of Alabama basketball player, for falsely identifying him as a suspect in a 2023 murder investigation. According to *Just The News*, sports writer Billy Witz erroneously reported that the 18-year-old walk-on was present at the scene of a fatal shooting in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, in January 2023.

The Times' reckless reporting destroyed Spears' reputation and forced him to defend himself against baseless accusations. The jury's verdict—reported by *The Daily Wire* and *Daily Caller*—sends a rare but necessary message to legacy media outlets that fabricating details about ordinary citizens carries real legal consequences.

This case underscores what conservatives have long argued: the mainstream press operates with minimal accountability, routinely advancing narratives without rigorous fact-checking. The Times' deep pockets allow it to absorb such judgments, but the reputational damage and the precedent matter. Spears' vindication proves that even the nation's most powerful newsroom can be held to account when it crosses the line from opinion into defamation.

Watch whether this judgment prompts other outlets to tighten editorial standards—or whether legacy media continues operating as if consequences don't apply to them.
