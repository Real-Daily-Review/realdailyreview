# Colorado Honors Pilot Who Gave His Life Fighting Wildfire

Nicholas Dale, a British Columbia helicopter pilot, will be remembered across Colorado this week after his death while battling the Gold Mountain Fire—a stark reminder of the real costs of wildfire suppression in the West.

Dale died fighting one of Colorado's active fire threats. His memorial procession will travel Monday morning from southwest Colorado to Denver International Airport, honoring his sacrifice and the dangerous work of aerial firefighting crews who risk their lives each season.

Wildfire suppression in Colorado has become increasingly costly and complex. As fires grow larger and more frequent—driven by forest management policies that have left federal lands overgrown and fuel-laden—the demand for experienced pilots and specialized aircraft has surged. The state's wildfire response depends heavily on private contractors and out-of-state personnel like Dale, underscoring how Colorado's preparedness relies on skilled professionals willing to work in hazardous conditions.

Dale's death also highlights the broader challenge: Colorado's forests remain dangerously overstocked with timber and deadwood, a condition conservatives argue stems from decades of federal mismanagement and environmental litigation that has blocked thinning and controlled burns. While the state and feds spend heavily on suppression, they've been slower to invest in prevention—the more cost-effective long-term strategy.

Coloradans should watch whether state and federal leaders use this tragedy to accelerate forest thinning and fuel reduction projects, or continue prioritizing reactive spending over preventive land management.
