# Senate Democrats Block Data Center Bill, Leaving Colorado Ratepayers Vulnerable

Senate Democrats killed a bipartisan bill that would have protected Colorado electricity consumers from skyrocketing rates driven by data center demand—handing Republicans a midterm talking point while leaving the state's power grid and wallet exposed.

The Ratepayer Protection Act, backed by GOP senators, aimed to require states to consider imposing higher electricity fees on data centers before they drain resources from residential and business ratepayers. According to the *Daily Caller*, the bill's language was deliberately weak—states would only have to *consider* such measures, not mandate them—yet Democrats still blocked it.

The *Washington Examiner* reports the vote exposed fractures within the Democratic caucus ahead of midterm elections, with party strategists divided over how aggressively to regulate the booming data center industry.

**The Colorado angle:** Data centers are multiplying across the state, particularly around Denver and Northern Colorado, consuming massive amounts of electricity. Without guardrails, Colorado ratepayers—already facing inflationary pressure—could see their bills climb as tech giants monopolize grid capacity. Republicans argue Democrats chose corporate interests over working families; Democrats counter that overly restrictive rules would drive investment elsewhere.

Conservatives should monitor whether Colorado's legislature acts unilaterally to protect ratepayers, or whether the state becomes a cautionary tale of regulatory timidity.
