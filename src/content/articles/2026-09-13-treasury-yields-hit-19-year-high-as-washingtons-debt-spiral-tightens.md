# Treasury Yields Hit 19-Year High as Washington's Debt Binge Catches Up

Washington's reckless spending spree is finally hitting the wallet of every American—and Colorado taxpayers should brace for the fallout.

The Wall Street Journal reports that a punishing bond selloff has pushed the 30-year Treasury yield to a 19-year high, with the benchmark 10-year yield approaching 5% (Washington Examiner). For Coloradans already squeezed by rising living costs—from energy bills to groceries—this signals deeper trouble ahead: higher borrowing costs ripple through the entire economy, making mortgages, car loans, and business expansion more expensive.

Republicans argue that Washington's borrowing binge is unsustainable and that fiscal responsibility must become central to economic policy. The debt spiral forces the Federal Reserve into a corner, constrains private investment, and ultimately punishes working families and small businesses that fuel Colorado's economy.

Democrats typically counter that government spending supports growth and social programs, but they've offered little credible plan to address the structural deficit driving these yields higher.

The real issue: when Washington borrows at historic rates, it crowds out private capital and inflates costs across the board. Colorado's energy sector, agriculture, and small businesses all suffer when capital becomes scarce and expensive.

**Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republicans—will push for genuine spending restraint or fold under pressure from their own party's big-spending wing.**
