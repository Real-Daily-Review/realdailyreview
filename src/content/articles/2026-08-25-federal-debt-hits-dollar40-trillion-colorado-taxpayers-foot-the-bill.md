# Federal Debt Hits $40 Trillion: A Reckoning for Colorado Taxpayers

Washington just crossed a catastrophic threshold: the federal government's debt burden surpassed $40 trillion, a milestone that should alarm every Colorado family watching their paychecks shrink and their cost of living soar.

The Washington Examiner calls it what it is—a symptom of runaway spending, not undertaxation. Democrats have long argued that higher taxes on corporations and the wealthy are the answer to fiscal imbalance. Republicans counter that the real culprit is a federal government that spends money it doesn't have, year after year, with no meaningful restraint.

For Colorado, this matters directly. As the debt balloons, so does inflation pressure on energy costs, food prices, and housing—hitting working families and retirees hardest. Colorado's energy sector, already burdened by federal overreach on land use and permitting, faces additional economic headwinds from a weakening dollar and rising interest rates tied to debt service.

Conservatives argue Washington must cut spending and restore fiscal discipline. Without structural reform—not tax hikes that fund more government—Colorado taxpayers will continue subsidizing federal profligacy through inflation and economic stagnation.

Watch for whether Colorado's congressional delegation demands real spending cuts or caves to the bipartisan spending consensus that got us here.
