# America's Energy Dominance Is Slipping While China Plays Hardball in Our Backyard

Colorado's energy sector—and America's geopolitical standing—faces a mounting threat as China and Russia expand their grip on Latin American oil and mineral supplies while Washington dithers on domestic energy development.

Recent reports show Chevron and other U.S. energy companies preparing major investments in Venezuela, a move *National Review* frames as part of a larger strategic failure: "Peace through strength doesn't just come through military strength." The real competition, according to the *Washington Examiner*, is unfolding "much closer to home," where China and Russia have long understood the strategic value of controlling energy resources across the Western Hemisphere.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. State energy workers and companies depend on competitive global markets and stable U.S. energy policy. Yet federal overregulation, permitting delays, and anti-fossil-fuel rhetoric have hamstrung domestic production—exactly when America needs energy independence and leverage against authoritarian rivals.

Republicans argue that unleashing American oil, gas, and mineral development—including in Colorado—is essential to countering Chinese and Russian influence. Democrats, by contrast, prioritize climate restrictions that cede energy dominance to adversaries who ignore environmental standards.
