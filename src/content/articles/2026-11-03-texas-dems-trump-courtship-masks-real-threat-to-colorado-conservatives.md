# Colorado Conservatives Should Ignore Texas Democrat's Trump Courtship

A Texas Democratic Senate nominee's sudden pivot toward "bipartisan cooperation" with President Trump is a transparent play for moderate voters—and a reminder that Colorado Republicans should stay focused on their own state's battles rather than chasing Democratic defectors.

James Talarico, running for Senate in Texas, told a podcast audience he'd "love" to work with Trump to fulfill the MAGA agenda (Washington Examiner). The move signals Democrats recognize Trump's political strength and are scrambling to appear reasonable. But conservatives should be skeptical: Talarico's party has spent years obstructing Trump's policies on energy, immigration, and regulatory reform. A single candidate's sudden friendliness doesn't change the Democratic Party's fundamental opposition to limited government and free markets.

For Colorado, the lesson is clear. While national Democrats court Trump rhetorically, progressive Colorado lawmakers continue pushing higher taxes, energy restrictions, and gun control—policies that directly harm Colorado workers, ranchers, and business owners. Governor Jared Polis and his legislative allies have shown no interest in "bipartisan cooperation" on issues that matter to Colorado conservatives: water rights, oil and gas development, or Second Amendment protections.

Republicans should watch whether Talarico's overture signals a broader Democratic strategy to soften opposition to Trump's second term—or if it's merely campaign theater that evaporates after Election Day.
