# San Diego's Flock Camera Push Highlights Surveillance Divide

A man dressed as Darth Vader showed up at San Diego city council to joke about license plate reader cameras—but the underlying debate is no laughing matter for Colorado privacy advocates and Second Amendment supporters.

Flock Safety's automatic license plate readers (ALPRs) are spreading across American cities, and Colorado communities are watching closely. Supporters, including *National Review*, argue the cameras help law enforcement track stolen vehicles and fugitives. But conservatives and civil libertarians worry these systems create mass surveillance infrastructure that chills freedom of movement and can be weaponized against lawful gun owners, protesters, and political opponents.

The tension is real: Republicans generally back law enforcement tools, but many also oppose government surveillance that lacks warrant requirements or clear oversight. Colorado's gun-owning and liberty-minded voters should ask hard questions about how Flock data is stored, who accesses it, and whether it requires judicial approval.

San Diego's embrace of the technology signals a national trend. Cities are installing these cameras with minimal public debate, often justified by public safety rhetoric that obscures the permanent tracking infrastructure being built.

Colorado taxpayers and county commissioners should demand transparency: What's the cost? Who controls the data? And will it require a warrant to access?
