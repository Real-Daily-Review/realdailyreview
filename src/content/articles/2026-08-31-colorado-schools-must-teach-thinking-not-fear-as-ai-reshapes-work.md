# Colorado Schools Must Teach Critical Thinking, Not Fear, as AI Reshapes the Workforce

Colorado parents and educators are right to think hard about artificial intelligence—but panic and regulatory overreach won't prepare kids for tomorrow's economy. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the real problem isn't AI itself; it's an education system that trains students to be "copy machines" instead of independent thinkers.

The concern is legitimate: AI will disrupt job markets, and Colorado's workforce needs to adapt. But the solution isn't to demonize the technology or demand government mandates on how schools teach it. Instead, Colorado schools should double down on critical thinking, problem-solving, and creativity—skills machines can't replicate. Students who can analyze, innovate, and communicate will thrive regardless of technological change.

The risk conservatives should watch: progressive educators and bureaucrats will use AI anxiety as cover to expand government control over curriculum, push more standardized testing, or demand costly new regulations on schools. That's the wrong answer. Colorado taxpayers are already footing a massive education bill; they shouldn't pay more for feel-good AI "safety" mandates that don't actually prepare kids for real work.

The real "AI-proofing" happens when parents demand schools teach their children to think—not comply.

**Watch whether Colorado's education establishment pushes for new AI-related spending or regulations instead of trusting teachers and parents to drive curriculum innovation.**
