# Michigan Democrat's 9/11 Messaging Raises Questions About Past Associations

A Michigan Democratic Senate candidate's carefully crafted 9/11 tribute has reignited scrutiny over his past associations and rhetoric surrounding the terrorist attacks.

Abdul El-Sayed, running to become the first Muslim U.S. Senator, released a somber video Friday honoring 9/11 victims. But the move drew immediate backlash after reports surfaced linking him to Hasan Piker, a streamer who infamously claimed America deserved the attacks (Fox News).

According to The Daily Wire, El-Sayed's current messaging represents "a far cry" from past comments he made about 9/11, raising questions about the authenticity of his pivot.

**The conservative concern:** Republicans and commentators argue El-Sayed's sudden shift in tone—coupled with documented associations—suggests political calculation rather than genuine conviction. Critics note the contrast between his current rhetoric and previous statements, questioning whether voters can trust his stated positions on national security and American values.

Democrats have not publicly responded to the specific allegations, though El-Sayed's campaign could argue his current message reflects his true beliefs.

**What to watch:** Whether national Republican groups amplify this narrative in Michigan's competitive Senate race, and whether El-Sayed's past associations become a defining issue in his campaign.
