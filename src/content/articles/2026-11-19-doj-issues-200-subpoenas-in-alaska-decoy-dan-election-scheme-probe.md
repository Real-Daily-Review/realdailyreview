# DOJ's 'Decoy Dan' Probe Expands as Alaska Democrat Wins Senate Seat

The Justice Department has issued more than 200 subpoenas across multiple states in a criminal investigation into an alleged scheme to recruit a "decoy" candidate named Dan Sullivan to split the Republican vote in Alaska's Senate race—a tactic that may have helped Democrat Mary Peltola defeat the actual Republican incumbent, Sen. Dan Sullivan (R-AK), according to Fox News and the Washington Examiner.

Law enforcement sources confirmed the sprawling probe involves multiple criminal targets and spans beyond Alaska. The investigation centers on whether operatives deliberately fielded a second Dan Sullivan to confuse voters and fracture GOP support in a race Democrats needed to win.

Republicans argue the scheme—if proven—represents a brazen attempt to manipulate an election through deception. The timing raises questions: Peltola's victory came after the decoy candidate's involvement, suggesting the strategy may have succeeded in its apparent aim.

Colorado conservatives should note this case as a cautionary tale about election integrity vulnerabilities. While Alaska's race is decided, the DOJ's aggressive subpoena campaign signals federal prosecutors are taking ballot-manipulation allegations seriously—a standard Colorado Republicans should demand be applied uniformly to any similar schemes, regardless of which party benefits.

Watch whether the investigation yields indictments and whether media outlets that downplayed the "Decoy Dan" story during the race now cover the DOJ's findings.
