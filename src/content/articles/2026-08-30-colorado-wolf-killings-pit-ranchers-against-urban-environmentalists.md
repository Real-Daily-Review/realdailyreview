# Wolf Killings Spark Debate Over Colorado Wildlife Management

Colorado Parks and Wildlife's lethal wolf removal program is reigniting the state's perennial clash between rural ranchers and urban environmentalists—with real money and land-use authority on the line.

The Colorado Sun's opinion section argues that killing wolves isn't genuine wildlife "management" and that the state should lead nationally on non-lethal conflict prevention instead. That framing, however, glosses over ranchers' core complaint: wolves kill livestock, and prevention methods—fencing, guard animals, compensation programs—don't stop predation entirely.

Republicans and rural Colorado conservatives counter that wolves were reintroduced to the state via ballot measure (Proposition 114 in 2020) over the objections of agricultural communities, and that lethal removal is a legitimate tool when predators threaten livelihoods. They argue the state has a duty to balance wildlife restoration with the economic survival of working ranches—not to prioritize wolves over people.

The real tension: Colorado's urban majority voted to restore wolves; rural counties bear the costs. Lethal removal remains politically contentious, but ranchers say non-lethal methods alone won't protect herds from an expanding predator population.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado Parks and Wildlife expands lethal removal permits or bows to pressure from environmental groups demanding stricter limits.
