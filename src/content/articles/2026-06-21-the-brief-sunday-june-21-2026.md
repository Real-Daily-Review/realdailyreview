## Iran Deal Divides GOP — And the Stakes Are Real

President Trump's memorandum of understanding with Iran is drawing fire from within his own party. Republican skeptics warn the deal hands Tehran economic benefits with no credible enforcement mechanism, while Vice President JD Vance is pushing back hard, insisting the United States "wins either way." Senator Tuberville is backing the president; others aren't so sure. Conservatives are right to demand answers: any deal that frees up Iranian oil revenues reshapes global energy markets — and that hits Colorado's energy sector directly.

## Vance Holds the Line — But GOP Skeptics Aren't Buying It

Vance took point this week defending the Trump-Iran deal against Republican critics who argue the administration gave Tehran too much and got too little in return. The vice president's "United States wins either way" framing is a tough sell for conservatives who watched Iran fund proxy wars for decades. Colorado's defense contractors, military families at Fort Carson and Buckley, and the broader national security community deserve a straight accounting of what enforcement looks like — not a PR offensive.

## Biden Ghostwriter Tapes: A Judge Says No to Burial

A federal judge rejected Joe Biden's attempt to suppress tapes made by his ghostwriter, though the release has been paused pending appeal. The judge found Biden had not identified any public harm that would arise from their release — a pointed rebuke. For conservatives who spent years watching the media dismiss concerns about Biden's cognitive fitness, these tapes represent accountability long overdue. Colorado voters who were told everything was fine deserve to know what was actually on the record.

## Italy Snubs Washington, Trump Fires Back

President Trump confirmed that Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni refused to allow the U.S. to use Italian landing strips during operations against Iran — and Trump made clear he wasn't pleased, saying Meloni "wants to be friends again" after the rebuff. The episode is a reminder that even allied nations have limits, and that America's forward posture in Europe is more complicated than NATO talking points suggest. For Colorado's military and defense community, alliance reliability is not an abstraction.

## James Burrows, Television Giant, Dead at 85

James Burrows, the director and co-creator of *Cheers* — one of the greatest American sitcoms ever produced — died at 85. Burrows earned 46 Emmy nominations and won 11 over a career that defined network television comedy for a generation. Before the culture wars swallowed Hollywood whole, craftsmen like Burrows made shows that united the country around a bar in Boston. Rest well.
