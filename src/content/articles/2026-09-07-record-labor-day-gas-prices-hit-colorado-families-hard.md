# Record Gas Prices Hammer Colorado Drivers Heading Into Labor Day

Colorado families heading out for the long weekend are facing the highest gas prices ever recorded for Labor Day—a direct hit to household budgets and a reminder of the energy policy failures driving inflation across the nation.

The national average hit $4.15 per gallon, according to AAA, with diesel climbing to an all-time high of $5.90 (9News Denver, Washington Examiner). For Colorado drivers already dealing with above-average state fuel taxes and refinery constraints, the pain at the pump is acute.

Republicans and conservatives argue these record prices stem from the Biden-Harris administration's war on domestic energy production—from killing the Keystone XL pipeline to restricting federal leases and pushing aggressive climate mandates that discourage oil and gas development. Democrats counter that global supply disruptions and OPEC production cuts drive prices, not U.S. policy.

The timing is brutal: Labor Day is one of the year's heaviest travel weekends. Colorado's tourism-dependent economy, ranchers hauling livestock, and working families simply trying to visit relatives are all absorbing the cost of ideological energy policy.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's Democratic leadership will acknowledge the connection between restrictive energy policies and soaring prices—or continue blaming market forces beyond their control.
