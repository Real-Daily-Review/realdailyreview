# Trump's Midterm Setback Emboldens Adversaries—But America Still Holds the Cards

Republicans took a bloodied nose in the midterms, and America's enemies are already circling. Iran's leadership wasted no time celebrating what they read as a U.S. retreat: on the 47th anniversary of the Tehran embassy seizure, Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf posted that "Americans said NO TO WAR," framing the GOP losses as a decisive defeat for American resolve (Washington Examiner).

But here's what Tehran got wrong: Trump still controls foreign policy until January, and the next Congress will inherit a geopolitical landscape where weakness invites aggression. Iran's premature victory lap—with Tehran's mayor declaring a "decisive defeat of the United States and the Zionist regime"—reveals exactly why American strength, not retreat, matters (Washington Examiner).

For Colorado, this matters. Energy policy, military readiness, and border security all hinge on whether America projects power or hesitation. Republicans now face a reckoning: National Review asks bluntly, "What comes next for the GOP?" The answer will shape whether the party rebuilds on limited-government, America-First principles or splinters further.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether the incoming Congress stands firm on defense spending and energy independence—or caves to progressive demands for unilateral restraint.
