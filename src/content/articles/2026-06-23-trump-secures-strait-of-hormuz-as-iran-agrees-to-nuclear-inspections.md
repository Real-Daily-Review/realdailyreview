# Strait of Hormuz: Trump Secures Passage as Iran Backs Down on Nuclear Inspections

Trump's negotiating strategy in the Middle East has yielded tangible results—the Strait of Hormuz remains open to international shipping after Iran agreed to allow nuclear inspectors back into the country, a significant reversal on a key security issue.

According to *Just The News*, Trump announced the breakthrough, criticizing media coverage for downplaying Iran's commitment to "nuclear honesty" and renewed inspections. Shipping data from *Breitbart* confirms the diplomatic win: traffic through the critical waterway has actually increased since U.S.-Iran talks began in Switzerland, directly contradicting Tehran's earlier closure threats.

Iran and Oman are now planning joint vessel management services in the strait, with *Just The News* reporting their statement references "costs associated" with these services "in accordance with international standards"—a face-saving mechanism that avoids the appearance of capitulation.

Meanwhile, *National Review* warns that any unilateral U.S. toll-imposition scheme on the strait would exceed presidential authority, a constitutional guardrail worth noting as negotiations continue.

For Colorado energy interests and consumers, keeping the Strait of Hormuz open matters: roughly one-third of global seaborne oil transits these waters. Stable passage protects domestic energy markets and supports American energy independence goals.

Watch whether Iran honors its nuclear inspection commitments or uses the agreement as cover for further regional maneuvering.
