# Denver's Crumbling Water Infrastructure Exposes Years of Deferred Maintenance

Denver's aging water system is literally breaking down—again. A 69-year-old cast iron water main ruptured on South Holly Street between Evans and Iliff avenues, forcing the city to close the road for days and leaving eight homes without water while Denver Water scrambles to make repairs (KDVR Fox31).

This isn't an isolated incident. Repeated water main breaks across Denver reveal what happens when a government utility defers maintenance and prioritizes spending on pet projects over core infrastructure. Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for both the emergency repairs and the service disruptions that follow.

Denver Water's aging pipe network—much of it dating back decades—demands serious capital investment and honest prioritization. Instead of addressing this fundamental problem, city leadership has often chased trendy initiatives while letting essential systems deteriorate. The result: residents lose water service, streets get torn up, and repair costs balloon.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue Denver needs to get back to basics: maintain the infrastructure taxpayers already paid for before launching new programs. The utility should conduct a comprehensive audit of its pipe network, publish a realistic repair timeline, and explain to residents why their water bills keep rising if maintenance isn't happening.

Watch whether Denver Water's leadership acknowledges the scope of the infrastructure crisis or continues blaming external factors.
