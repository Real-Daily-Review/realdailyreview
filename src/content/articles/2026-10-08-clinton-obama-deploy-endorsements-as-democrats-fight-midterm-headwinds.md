# Clinton, Obama Mobilize for Democratic Midterm Push—But Colorado Races Remain Competitive

Hillary Clinton and Barack Obama are flooding the midterm battlefield with endorsements, betting that star power from the Democratic establishment can salvage what polls suggest is a difficult cycle for the party. Clinton endorsed Minnesota Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan for U.S. Senate (Fox News), while Obama has strategically backed Senate and gubernatorial candidates in competitive races, notably avoiding House endorsements (Washington Examiner).

The Democratic strategy reflects anxiety about November. Republicans argue the endorsements signal panic—that even A-list Democrats recognize their party's vulnerability on inflation, crime, and border security. Conservatives counter that celebrity endorsements won't move voters focused on kitchen-table economics and government overreach.

For Colorado, the playbook matters. Democrats will likely deploy similar tactics in the state's competitive Senate and gubernatorial races, where turnout and persuasion remain fluid. Republicans should expect a final-month blitz of national Democratic figures campaigning in Colorado, targeting suburban and youth voters while glossing over the state's energy sector struggles and regulatory burden.

Watch whether Colorado voters—particularly independents and rural Democrats—respond to national Democratic messaging or prioritize state-specific concerns like water rights, affordable energy, and government spending restraint.
