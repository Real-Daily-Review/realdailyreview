# State Land Grab Proposal Masks Private Property Rights Crisis in San Luis Valley

Gov. Jared Polis is floating a state purchase of part of Cielo Vista Ranch in Costilla County, according to a report from former U.S. Sen. Ken Salazar, whom Polis tapped to broker a solution to the long-running land dispute (Colorado Sun). The proposal signals the administration's preference for government intervention over resolving competing private claims through courts and existing law.

Conservatives worry the move sets a dangerous precedent: when disputes arise, does Colorado simply buy its way in rather than respect property rights and contractual obligations? State acquisition of private land—especially in rural Colorado—raises hard questions about how taxpayer dollars get deployed and whether government should be in the land-holding business at all.

The San Luis Valley, already squeezed by water scarcity and agricultural pressures, doesn't need another layer of state bureaucracy managing ranch operations. Republicans and property-rights advocates argue the Polis administration should let the courts sort competing claims instead of using public funds to engineer a political settlement.

The real test: will Polis commit to a transparent accounting of how much this "solution" costs Colorado taxpayers, and will the legislature demand a full cost-benefit analysis before any purchase moves forward?
