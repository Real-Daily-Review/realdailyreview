# Highway 6 Closed Through Clear Creek Canyon After Heavy Rain Triggers Rockslides

Heavy rain triggered multiple rockslides that forced Colorado Department of Transportation to shut down U.S. 6 in both directions through Clear Creek Canyon Thursday morning, cutting off a critical corridor for commuters and commerce between the Front Range and mountain communities.

The closure spans between Highway 58 in Golden and Highway 119, according to CDOT (9News Denver). KDVR Fox31 Denver reports the slides were caused by heavy precipitation overnight.

The Clear Creek Canyon route is a vital artery for Colorado's economy—connecting Denver-area workers to mountain towns, ski resorts, and rural communities while serving as an alternative to I-70 during winter weather events. Extended closures ripple across the state's transportation network and hit small mountain businesses dependent on reliable access.

This incident underscores a broader infrastructure challenge: Colorado's aging mountain highways face increasing pressure from weather events, and maintenance backlogs leave critical routes vulnerable. Conservatives and taxpayers should demand that state transportation dollars prioritize safety and reliability over pet projects in urban corridors.

Watch for CDOT's timeline on reopening and whether the agency uses this as justification for new spending requests—or whether it finally addresses deferred maintenance on routes that actually move Coloradans.
