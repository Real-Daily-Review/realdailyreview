# Denver Water Main Failure Exposes Aging Infrastructure Crisis

Denver's crumbling water infrastructure struck again this weekend as a 69-year-old main ruptured on South Holly Street, leaving eight homes without water and shutting down the road until Sunday night. The burst highlights a growing problem: Colorado's capital has deferred critical maintenance on aging pipes for years, and taxpayers are now footing the bill for emergency repairs instead of preventive investment.

According to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31, the break between East Evans and East Iliff avenues forced Denver Water into damage-control mode, disrupting traffic and service across the Virginia Village neighborhood. This isn't an isolated incident—Denver's water system includes thousands of miles of pipe, much of it decades old and vulnerable to failure.

**The conservative concern:** Rather than embrace market-driven efficiency or demand fiscal accountability from Denver Water's management, city officials have historically resisted rate structures that incentivize conservation and infrastructure upkeep. Taxpayers absorb the cost of neglect through emergency repairs, service disruptions, and eventual rate hikes—the classic government mismanagement cycle.

Colorado's water challenges extend beyond Denver's pipes. As the state faces population growth and drought pressure, aging infrastructure across the Front Range demands serious capital planning—not more bureaucratic studies or federal dependency.

Watch whether Denver Water's board demands accountability for deferred maintenance and whether rate-payers demand transparency on long-term infrastructure spending.
