# Texas Jury Convicts Teen in Track Meet Stabbing; Self-Defense Claim Rejected

A Collin County, Texas jury found 19-year-old Karmelo Anthony guilty of murder Tuesday in the fatal stabbing of 17-year-old Austin Metcalf during a track meet in Frisco last April. Anthony, tried as an adult despite being 17 at the time of the killing, now faces five years to life in prison on the first-degree murder conviction (The Daily Wire).

The defense argued self-defense, but jurors rejected that claim swiftly. Anthony's mother, Kala Hayes, sobbed before the jury during sentencing phase, pleading for mercy (New York Post). Outside the courthouse, pro-Anthony supporters staged protests demanding his release, according to reports of "hate-filled" demonstrations (New York Post).

The case drew national attention and underscores ongoing debates over trying juveniles as adults in serious violent crimes. While Colorado has its own juvenile justice framework—generally more lenient than Texas—this verdict illustrates how different states handle violent teenage offenders.

Conservatives argue swift justice and adult accountability for serious crimes deter violence; critics counter that rehabilitation should remain the focus for young offenders. Colorado taxpayers should monitor whether similar cases in state courts receive comparable scrutiny and sentencing consistency.
