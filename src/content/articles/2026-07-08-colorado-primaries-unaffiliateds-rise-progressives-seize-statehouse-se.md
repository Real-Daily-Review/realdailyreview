# Colorado Primaries Show Unaffiliated Surge, Progressive Gains in Statehouse Races

Colorado's primary system is quietly reshaping the state's political map—and unaffiliated voters are the wild card nobody's talking about. While progressive Democrats notched victories in metro Denver statehouse primaries, unseating two sitting lawmakers despite millions in opposition spending, the real story is the growing power of Colorado's unaffiliated electorate, which now dominates primary outcomes under the state's caucus-and-assembly system.

Here's the problem conservatives face: party-registered voters who skip caucuses effectively surrender their voice to activists and organized progressives. Meanwhile, unaffiliated voters—Colorado's fastest-growing bloc—can cherry-pick primaries, amplifying whichever party's race interests them most. That's a structural advantage for Democrats in metro Denver, where progressive turnout is strongest.

The Denver Post reports that progressive candidates crushed opposition spending to win key statehouse races, signaling a leftward shift in Colorado's legislature. Republicans argue this reflects the state's demographic drift toward urban centers and progressive activism, not a mandate for higher taxes or regulatory expansion.

The takeaway: Colorado conservatives need to understand that primary participation isn't optional—it's the only lever they have left in a system increasingly tilted toward unaffiliated voters and organized progressive machines.
