# Progressive Education Advocate's Private School Choice Exposes Public School Failure

Nikole Hannah-Jones, the 1619 Project architect who has long championed racial equity narratives in public education, enrolled her own daughter in an elite private school—undercutting her own advocacy and revealing what conservatives have long argued: progressive ideology doesn't fix failing schools; it obscures the real problems.

According to National Review and the New York Post, Hannah-Jones's decision contradicts her public messaging about systemic racism driving educational disparities. Conservative critics argue her move proves that when given a choice, even progressive activists abandon the public system they defend for others. A New York City parent writing in the Post countered Hannah-Jones's racial framing directly: "Race and income doesn't drive educational failure: Low standards and uncommitted teachers do."

National Review noted the irony—Hannah-Jones refuses to "press charges" against the system that "mugged her," maintaining ideological loyalty even as her personal choices betray her public positions.

**The Colorado angle:** This hypocrisy matters here too. As progressive activists push equity-focused curricula and oppose school choice expansion in Colorado, families increasingly vote with their feet, enrolling kids in charter schools, private institutions, or homeschooling. Hannah-Jones's private school choice is a tacit admission that progressive education theory fails in practice.
