# Verizon Outage Exposes Vulnerability in Critical Infrastructure—and Regulatory Gaps

A massive Verizon outage Saturday left thousands of customers across the U.S. unable to make or receive calls, with Southern California hit particularly hard. According to the New York Post, the disruption had a criminal cause, leaving residents and emergency networks disconnected across the region.

The incident underscores a troubling reality: America's telecommunications infrastructure remains vulnerable to both malicious actors and operational failures—yet federal regulators have done little to mandate the redundancy and security standards that should protect critical services. While the FCC oversees telecom carriers, conservatives argue the agency's approach relies too heavily on voluntary compliance rather than enforceable infrastructure hardening requirements.

For Colorado, the outage is a cautionary tale. Rural broadband expansion and emergency communications systems depend on reliable carrier networks. If a single incident can cripple service across multiple states, what happens when Colorado's 911 systems, rural hospitals, or agricultural operations lose connectivity during a crisis?

Republicans and industry advocates say carriers must invest in resilient, redundant systems—but that requires regulatory certainty and protection from frivolous litigation, not more government mandates that drive up costs for consumers.

Watch whether Congress demands new telecom regulations or whether carriers voluntarily strengthen infrastructure before the next outage hits.
