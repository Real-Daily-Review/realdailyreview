# America's Founding Story Fades as Pride in Nation Hits Record Low

Weeks before America's 250th birthday, NBC News polling shows a "record-low number" of Americans are "extremely proud" to be American—a troubling sign that our founding principles are being crowded out of classrooms and culture.

Conservative outlets are sounding the alarm. The Daily Wire argues that too few kids hear the actual story of America's founding, while the Washington Examiner is highlighting Colonial Williamsburg and the nation's historic triangle—Jamestown, Yorktown, and Colonial Williamsburg—as reminders of where our constitutional republic began and how independence was won.

The concern runs deep: if young Americans don't understand the principles of limited government, individual liberty, and self-governance that animated 1776, they're vulnerable to progressive narratives that frame America primarily through grievance rather than achievement. That matters in Colorado, where schools increasingly emphasize revisionist history over the Founders' actual words and intent.

Republicans and conservatives argue that civics education must center on the Declaration, Constitution, and the hard-won battles for freedom—not activist reinterpretations. Without that foundation, they warn, citizens lose sight of why constitutional limits on government power exist in the first place.

Watch whether Colorado's State Board of Education doubles down on standards that teach founding documents as living texts to be rewritten, or returns to teaching them as written.
