# Colorado Tightens Screws on Suncor Refinery; Lawmakers Eye Nonprofit Pay Limits

Colorado regulators are moving to impose stricter air pollution limits on Suncor's Commerce City refinery, the state's only oil refinery, following repeated violations (Colorado Sun). The crackdown reflects growing frustration with the facility's environmental record, though conservatives worry that overly aggressive regulations could threaten jobs and energy production in a state still dependent on oil refining.

Republicans and industry groups argue that Colorado must balance environmental compliance with economic reality. Suncor employs hundreds of workers, and excessive penalties or operational restrictions could drive investment elsewhere—a risk in an energy sector already facing headwinds from progressive state policies.

Meanwhile, state Senator proposes legislation to bar tax dollars from funding nonprofit salaries and benefits paid to Colorado lawmakers (Complete Colorado). The bill targets a common practice where legislators draw dual paychecks through nonprofit board positions, often funded indirectly by government grants. Conservatives see this as a straightforward ethics fix; Democrats typically defend the practice as legitimate outside income.

Both moves reflect competing visions: tighter environmental and ethics enforcement versus concerns about regulatory burden and government overreach.

**Watch:** Whether Suncor negotiates compliance upgrades or challenges the new rules in court—and whether the nonprofit salary bill gains traction in a Democratic-controlled legislature.
