# Bipartisan Revolt Against AI Surveillance Cameras Gains Steam—But Colorado Should Demand Accountability

Americans are pushing back hard against AI-powered surveillance cameras flooding city streets, and a bipartisan group of lawmakers is finally listening. A new bill would prohibit federal taxpayer funding for these systems—a direct rebuke to the surveillance-industrial complex that conservatives have long warned about.

The backlash is real. According to *Just The News*, U.S. Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) and allies across the aisle are sponsoring legislation to cut off public money for AI camera networks like Flock, which uses facial recognition and license-plate readers to track citizens. *The Daily Wire* notes Americans are increasingly concerned about pervasive surveillance—from smartphones to social media algorithms to street-level cameras—all collecting data with minimal oversight.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Taxpayers shouldn't fund tools that enable government tracking without warrant or consent. This isn't about opposing public safety; it's about rejecting mass surveillance infrastructure that erodes privacy and individual liberty.

Colorado cities and counties have quietly adopted similar systems. Taxpayers here deserve to know whether their dollars are funding AI cameras in Denver, Boulder, or elsewhere—and whether local officials have even considered the privacy implications before deploying them.

Watch whether Colorado's delegation supports this bill and whether state legislators move to restrict AI surveillance funding at the local level.
