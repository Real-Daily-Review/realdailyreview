# Colorado Faces Triple-Digit Heat Dome This Weekend

Another dangerous heat wave is bearing down on Colorado, with temperatures expected to hit 100 degrees or higher across the Front Range through Monday.

The National Weather Service has issued heat advisories for the weekend, with CPR News reporting highs in the hundreds statewide. 9News Denver confirms a Weather Impact Alert for Sunday and Monday, with Denver specifically forecast to reach 100 degrees both days.

This marks the latest in a series of extreme heat events Colorado has endured this summer. While weather patterns are natural and cyclical, conservatives have long criticized progressive energy policies that restrict domestic energy production and drive up electricity costs—making it harder for working families and seniors on fixed incomes to afford air conditioning during dangerous heat events.

Colorado's grid reliability has become increasingly strained as the state transitions away from reliable baseload power. Republicans argue that premature coal plant closures and overreliance on intermittent renewables without adequate storage threaten grid stability during peak-demand periods like this weekend's heat emergency.

Coloradans should stock water, check on vulnerable neighbors, and monitor whether the state's grid holds steady under the strain—a real test of whether Colorado's energy transition can actually keep the lights on when it matters most.
