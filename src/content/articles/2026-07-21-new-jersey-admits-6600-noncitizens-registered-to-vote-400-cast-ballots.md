# New Jersey's Voter Roll Fiasco Proves Election Security Warnings Were Right

A "software error" in New Jersey's motor vehicle system registered roughly 6,600 noncitizens to vote—and approximately 400 of them actually cast ballots since 2023, according to Democratic Gov. Mikie Sherrill. The discovery vindicates years of Republican warnings that lax voter registration protocols invite fraud and undermine election integrity.

Sherrill revealed the breach last week after the Motor Vehicle Commission's system failed to properly flag noncitizen status during registration. While officials are framing this as a technical glitch, conservatives argue it exposes the dangers of automatic voter registration schemes and insufficient citizenship verification—exactly the vulnerabilities Republicans have flagged repeatedly.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado operates its own motor voter system tied to DMV records. If New Jersey's safeguards failed this spectacularly, taxpayers should demand state election officials audit Colorado's registration database for similar vulnerabilities. Colorado Republicans have long pushed for stricter voter ID requirements and citizenship verification; this incident strengthens their case.

Democrats will likely dismiss this as an isolated error. Republicans counter that "isolated errors" affecting thousands of votes are precisely why election security—not just ballot access—must be the priority.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's Secretary of State conducts a full audit of noncitizen registrations in our state and whether this fuels renewed GOP momentum for voter ID legislation.
