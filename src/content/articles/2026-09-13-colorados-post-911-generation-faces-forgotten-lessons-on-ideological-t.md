# A Generation Born After 9/11 Now Faces a Threat America Hasn't Defeated

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students who never knew that day as lived experience are confronting uncomfortable truths: the ideological war that spawned the terrorism hasn't ended—it's evolved.

CPR News reports that younger Coloradans are grappling with 9/11 as historical event rather than national trauma, learning its lessons through education rather than memory. That's a problem, conservatives argue. National Review warns that while the immediate terror threat has receded, "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still"—suggesting that complacency about ideological threats leaves America vulnerable to the very forces that motivated the 2001 attacks.

The generational gap matters for Colorado specifically. As younger voters enter the electorate, their understanding of national security, counterterrorism, and foreign policy will shape how they vote on defense spending, military readiness, and border security—issues directly affecting Colorado's military installations and defense contractors.

Republicans contend that forgetting 9/11's lessons invites strategic blindness. When a generation views terrorism as history rather than ongoing threat, support for robust counterterrorism measures and military strength erodes—exactly when vigilance remains necessary.
