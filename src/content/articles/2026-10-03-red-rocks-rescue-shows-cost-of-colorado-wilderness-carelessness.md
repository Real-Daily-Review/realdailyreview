# Red Rocks Rescue: Another Reminder That Colorado Wilderness Demands Respect

Two teenagers learned an expensive lesson in poor planning when they got lost hiking back from a Red Rocks concert Wednesday night, requiring West Metro Fire Rescue to mount a search-and-rescue operation (KDVR, 9News). What seemed like a shortcut from Morrison turned into a costly mistake—one that tied up emergency resources and taxpayer dollars.

The teens' decision to hike unfamiliar terrain in darkness after an evening concert exemplifies a growing problem: outdoor recreation without basic preparation. Colorado's natural beauty attracts millions annually, but too many visitors treat our mountains and canyons like theme parks rather than genuine wilderness that demands respect, navigation skills, and common sense.

Search-and-rescue operations in Colorado counties are increasingly strained. While volunteer SAR teams do heroic work, taxpayers ultimately foot the bill for preventable rescues. State officials have long resisted mandatory wilderness-safety education or permit requirements—reasonable positions given Colorado's libertarian streak—but the cost of avoidable emergencies keeps climbing.

The lesson here isn't complicated: know your route, carry a map and light, tell someone where you're going, and respect the terrain. Red Rocks is stunning. Getting lost there is expensive.
