# Alan Greenspan, Fed Chair Who Shaped Markets for Two Decades, Dies at 100

Alan Greenspan, the Federal Reserve chairman who steered U.S. monetary policy through the Reagan era and the post-9/11 boom, has died at 100 from complications of Parkinson's Disease, his wife, NBC News correspondent Andrea Mitchell, confirmed this week.

Greenspan, appointed by Ronald Reagan in 1987, led the Fed for 19 years until 2006, serving under four presidents. His tenure spanned some of the most consequential economic periods in modern history—from the savings-and-loan crisis through the dot-com bubble and the early years of the housing boom that would later trigger the 2008 financial crisis.

Conservatives have long debated Greenspan's legacy. While he championed free-market principles and warned against excessive government regulation, critics—including some on the right—argue his low-interest-rate policies in the early 2000s fueled the housing bubble that nearly collapsed the economy. His later acknowledgment that his faith in market self-correction was misplaced became a flashpoint in debates over Fed accountability.

For Colorado, Greenspan's monetary policies shaped the state's energy sector, real estate markets, and banking landscape for two decades. His influence on interest rates directly affected Colorado homebuyers, ranchers, and small businesses navigating credit markets.

Watch for renewed debate over Federal Reserve independence and whether his successors have learned the lessons of pre-2008 monetary excess.
