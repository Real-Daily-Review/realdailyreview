# Jack Smith's Festival Appearance Raises Questions About Prosecutorial Impartiality

Former Special Counsel Jack Smith attended a left-wing "Power to the People Fest" in Maryland over the weekend, where he was photographed wearing a Bruce Springsteen-inspired "No Kings" shirt—widely interpreted as anti-Trump messaging (Breitbart). The festival, headlined by Springsteen and featuring Dave Grohl, was explicitly framed as a get-out-the-vote event opposing Trump.

Trump responded sharply, calling Smith "deranged" and a "crooked prosecutor" (Just The News). The appearance fuels Republican concerns about Smith's impartiality during his prosecution of Trump. Conservatives argue that a federal prosecutor's public participation in overtly partisan political events—especially while still associated with high-profile cases—undermines public confidence in the justice system's neutrality.

Democrats and Smith's allies would likely counter that private citizens, including former prosecutors, retain First Amendment rights to political expression. They may argue the appearance was personal, not official.

For Colorado conservatives, this episode exemplifies a broader frustration: the weaponization of federal law enforcement against political opponents. It reinforces skepticism about the DOJ's independence and fuels demands for accountability.

**Watch for:** Republican calls to investigate Smith's conduct and potential congressional hearings on prosecutorial bias.
