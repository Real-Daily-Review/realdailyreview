# Social Security's Design Flaw: A System Built on Outdated Life Expectancy

Social Security was never meant to pay benefits to people who live as long as modern Americans do—and that structural problem is catching up with Colorado retirees and younger workers footing the bill.

The program, established in 1935, set retirement age at 65 when life expectancy was roughly 60 (Washington Examiner). Today, Americans routinely live into their 80s and beyond, fundamentally breaking the math behind a system designed when most beneficiaries wouldn't collect for long.

Conservatives argue the solution is straightforward: gradually raise the full retirement age to reflect actual longevity. Democrats typically resist, framing it as a "cut" to seniors—though the real cut is happening to younger Colorado workers and employers paying into a system heading toward insolvency.

Meanwhile, December 2026 Supplemental Security Income payments of up to $994 continue flowing to eligible recipients, underscoring the program's immediate obligations even as its long-term funding crisis deepens (Washington Examiner).

Colorado taxpayers—especially younger workers and small-business owners—are subsidizing a benefit structure that hasn't been updated for 70 years of increased lifespans. Watch whether Congress acts on demographic reality or kicks the can further down the road.
