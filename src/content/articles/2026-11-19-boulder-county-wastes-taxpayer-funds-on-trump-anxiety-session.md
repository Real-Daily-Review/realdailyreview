# Boulder County's Taxpayer-Funded Trump Anxiety Session

Boulder County officials turned a public event into what amounted to a taxpayer-funded therapy session for residents anxious about Trump, according to Complete Colorado.

The county's so-called "election champions" presentation devolved into hand-wringing rather than substantive discussion of election administration. Conservatives argue this is precisely the kind of wasteful use of public resources that erodes confidence in government—turning county resources into platforms for progressive political grievance rather than neutral election management.

Boulder County, reliably Democratic and home to University of Colorado's liberal activist base, has positioned itself as an "election champion" county. But critics contend that framing masks a partisan agenda. When government-funded events become venues for expressing anxiety about political opponents rather than addressing actual election integrity concerns, taxpayers foot the bill for what amounts to opposition research and political organizing.

The incident highlights a broader conservative concern: how progressive-controlled local governments in Colorado use public resources and official platforms to advance Democratic messaging. Election administration should be boring, professional, and nonpartisan—not a stage for partisan anxiety.

Watch whether Republican county commissioners or state election officials challenge Boulder County's use of public funds for these events, or whether similar "election champion" programs expand to other Colorado counties.
