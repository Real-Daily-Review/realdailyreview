# A Generation That Never Knew 9/11 Faces a Threat Its Parents Forgot

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students born after 2001 are encountering the terrorist assault as history rather than lived trauma—and that generational distance carries real security risks.

CPR News reports that Denver-area educators are grappling with how to teach the attacks' significance to students who have no memory of the event or its immediate aftermath. The challenge is real: without visceral understanding of what radical Islamic terrorism accomplished that day, younger Coloradans may lack the vigilance their parents' generation developed.

National Review warns that complacency carries consequences. While the acute terror threat has diminished, the ideological war continues: "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still." Conservatives argue that forgetting 9/11's lessons—about the nature of the enemy, the cost of unpreparedness, and the necessity of strong defense—invites vulnerability.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. The state hosts military installations, tech hubs, and critical infrastructure. Homeland security readiness depends on a population that understands the threat landscape, not one treating 9/11 as a distant historical footnote.

Watch whether Colorado schools and civic institutions prioritize honest counterterrorism education or retreat into sanitized, politically correct versions of events that strip away the ideological reality of what we faced.
