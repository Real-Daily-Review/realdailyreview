# House Bill Targets Trump's $1.8B "Anti-Weaponization" Fund

A bipartisan pair of House lawmakers is moving to block federal funding for the Justice Department's newly established "anti-weaponization" fund, which was created through a settlement of Trump's lawsuit against the IRS.

Reps. Brian Fitzpatrick (R-PA) and Tom Suozzi (D-NY) introduced legislation to ban the use of federal money for the $1.776 billion fund, according to CBS News and the Washington Examiner. The fund, established Monday, is designed to provide payouts and "formal apologies" to those who claim the government wronged them through weaponized legal action, according to The Hill.

The fund has drawn immediate scrutiny on Capitol Hill. Critics worry the broad eligibility criteria could direct money to individuals involved in the January 6 Capitol attack, according to CNBC. Some Republican senators have also expressed skepticism—Sen. Thom Tillis called it "stupid," per CNBC.

Legal experts have raised questions about the fund's constitutionality, implementation, and enforcement mechanisms, CBS News reported. The DOJ provided Republican senators with a one-page summary of how the fund will operate, according to PBS NewsHour.

Both Democratic and Republican lawmakers have criticized the fund's vague eligibility standards, the Washington Examiner noted.

Watch for congressional votes on the Fitzpatrick-Suozzi bill and any legal challenges to the fund's establishment.
