# Victor Marx Taps Retired Marine for Colorado Governor's Ticket—Anti-Establishment Play Signals GOP Outsider Strategy

Victor Marx, the Republican gubernatorial nominee, has selected George Markert, a 54-year-old retired Marine, as his running mate—a move that doubles down on the GOP ticket's outsider credentials ahead of November's general election.

Markert, who initially sought the U.S. Senate seat this cycle, failed to secure a spot on the Republican primary ballot before pivoting to the lieutenant governor race (Colorado Sun). Marx announced the pick on radio, emphasizing both candidates' distance from career politics: "George is not a career politician, just like me. He's a proven leader who has spent his life accomplishing missions" (Denver Post).

The selection reflects a deliberate strategy to present Colorado Republicans as anti-establishment alternatives to the state's Democratic leadership. With Colorado's governorship currently held by Democrats and the state trending purple in statewide races, Marx's campaign is betting that military service and outsider status will resonate with voters frustrated by government overreach and progressive policies on energy, water, and gun rights.

Conservatives should watch whether this ticket gains traction among rural and suburban Colorado voters—and whether the Marx-Markert campaign can effectively contrast their limited-government platform against Democratic incumbents' regulatory expansion and spending priorities.
