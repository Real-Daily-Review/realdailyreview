# Colorado Rails Aren't a Nudist Beach: Man Arrested for Naked Locomotive Stunt

An Arizona man was arrested after allegedly stripping down and surfing atop a moving locomotive in what law enforcement is calling a dangerous and bizarre stunt. According to the Sedgwick County Sheriff's Office, the suspect broke a window to board the train before climbing onto the roof while it was in motion—a reckless act that endangered himself and rail operations.

The incident underscores a broader problem: trespassing on railroad property and interfering with train operations is illegal for good reason. Trains don't stop on a dime, and stunts like this can derail critical freight and passenger service. Colorado's rail corridors move goods and people across the state daily; disruptions ripple through our economy and supply chains.

While this particular case involves an out-of-state offender, Colorado law enforcement and the railroad industry take such violations seriously. Trespassing on active rail lines carries real penalties, and rightfully so.

Conservatives and rail advocates argue that protecting critical infrastructure—including our transportation networks—requires enforcing existing laws without apology. The left's soft-on-crime approach has emboldened disorder in public spaces; this case, while unusual, reflects a broader erosion of respect for property and safety.
