# Hospital Billing Scams Are Bleeding Colorado Patients—Congress Must Act

Corporate hospital chains are systematically overcharging patients through fraudulent billing practices, and Colorado families are caught in the crosshairs. According to the *Washington Examiner*, millions of Americans face inflated charges for care never rendered—routine 15-minute clinic visits billed as high-severity emergencies, phantom procedures, and thousands in bogus fees appearing on medical statements.

This isn't market failure; it's regulatory capture. Hospital monopolies exploit opaque billing systems and weak enforcement, knowing most patients lack the time or expertise to challenge charges. Colorado's healthcare market has consolidated significantly, giving large systems pricing power without accountability.

**Conservatives argue** Congress must impose real transparency requirements and enforce existing anti-fraud statutes aggressively. Republicans say the solution isn't more government healthcare—it's sunlight: mandatory itemized billing, price disclosure upfront, and swift penalties for documented overbilling. The free market works only when consumers have information and choice.

**The left typically responds** by calling for expanded government price controls or a public option, which would entrench the very bureaucratic opacity enabling these abuses today.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses funding employee health plans are subsidizing this fraud through higher premiums. Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly on healthcare committees—demands real billing transparency or settles for performative hearings.
