# Net Migration Turns Negative: What It Means for Colorado

**For the first time since the Great Depression, America is experiencing net negative migration—and it signals a dramatic shift in immigration policy that could reshape Colorado's labor market and tax base.**

Census Bureau data cited by National Review show the U.S. lost more people to outbound migration than it gained from inbound movement, marking a historic reversal. The Daily Wire notes this represents a fundamental break from decades of immigration expansion, arguing the shift reflects tighter enforcement and border security measures that skeptics once dismissed as "far-right" fantasy.

Republicans argue stricter immigration policies—including workplace enforcement and asylum restrictions—are finally working to restore rule of law and prioritize American workers' wages and job availability. Democrats counter that reduced immigration threatens economic growth, labor supply in key sectors, and tax revenue needed for government services.

For Colorado specifically, the implications cut both ways. Tighter labor markets could benefit construction and energy workers competing for jobs, but agriculture, hospitality, and tech sectors dependent on immigrant workers may face staffing pressures. Rural Colorado counties relying on seasonal labor could see real disruption.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature attempts to offset labor shortages through visa expansions or state-level immigration incentives—moves that would undermine federal enforcement gains.
