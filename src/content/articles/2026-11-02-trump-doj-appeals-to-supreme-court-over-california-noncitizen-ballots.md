# Trump Administration Escalates Fight Over Noncitizen Ballots in California

The Trump administration is taking its battle over election integrity to the Supreme Court, appealing a lower court's refusal to set aside roughly 20,000 disputed California mail ballots allegedly cast by noncitizens.

The Justice Department asked the high court to temporarily block rulings that denied its request to segregate the contested ballots ahead of the midterm election (Fox News, Daily Wire). Lower courts had already rejected the administration's earlier push to isolate these votes, prompting the escalation.

Republicans argue that election security demands scrutiny of voter eligibility and that noncitizen voting—however rare—undermines the integrity of the ballot box. The Trump administration's persistence signals conservative concern that California's mail-in voting system may lack adequate safeguards.

Democrats and California election officials counter that the ballots in question have already been processed through existing verification procedures, and that the administration's request amounts to unnecessary interference in state election administration.

**The Colorado angle:** While this fight plays out in California, Colorado conservatives should watch whether similar challenges emerge here. Colorado's mail-ballot system and voter registration processes could face comparable scrutiny if noncitizen voting becomes a broader national election issue.

Watch for the Supreme Court's response and whether this case influences how states handle mail-ballot verification going forward.
