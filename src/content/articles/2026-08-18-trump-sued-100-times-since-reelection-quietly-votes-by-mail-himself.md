# Trump Faces Pre-Election Lawsuit Blitz While Embracing Mail Voting He Once Scorned

President Trump says he's "not surprised" by revelations that Democratic state attorneys general were planning lawsuits against him months before his 2024 reelection victory—a coordinated legal assault that has now materialized into roughly 100 suits since January 2025, according to *Just The News*.

The lawsuit barrage underscores the partisan weaponization of state-level litigation that conservatives have long warned about. Democrats are using their AG offices as political cudgels, filing cases on everything from environmental regulations to immigration enforcement. Colorado voters should take note: Democratic AG Phil Weiser has been a reliable foot soldier in this national legal offensive against Republican administrations.

There's an ironic twist: Trump himself voted by mail in Florida's Republican primary this year—the second time he's cast an absentee ballot in 2025—despite spending years hammering mail-in voting as a vector for fraud. *Washington Examiner* reports the president and his team have quietly embraced mail voting when tactically convenient, a pragmatic retreat from his 2020 rhetoric that undercuts his credibility on election integrity arguments.

The contradiction matters. If mail voting is genuinely insecure, it's insecure regardless of who uses it. If it's safe enough for Trump, conservatives need a clearer, more consistent message heading into 2026.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans demand the same voting-integrity standards applied uniformly—or whether they'll quietly accept the mail-voting status quo now that it benefits their side.
