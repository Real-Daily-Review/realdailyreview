# Democrats Target School Choice as Colorado Faces Tight Funding Vote

Colorado's education establishment is doubling down on the traditional public school monopoly just as Jefferson County voters narrowly reject a $73 million tax hike.

Jefferson County Public Schools' Ballot Issue 5A, which would have raised $73 million through a mill levy override, failed by just five votes out of 301,789 cast, triggering an automatic recount (Denver Post). The razor-thin margin underscores taxpayer skepticism about throwing more money at struggling systems without reform.

Meanwhile, Democrats nationwide are moving to restrict school choice—the one proven escape route for low-income families trapped in failing schools. Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa of Texas has signaled an incoming "war against charter schools," claiming they drain resources from traditional public schools (Washington Examiner). Republicans counter that charter schools provide competition, innovation, and accountability that benefit all students—and that blocking choice punishes poor families while protecting union interests and bureaucratic turf.

**The Colorado angle:** As Jeffco voters reject another tax increase, state Democrats should ask themselves whether Coloradans trust government to spend more wisely, or whether families simply want options beyond the system that's already consuming their tax dollars.

**Watch this:** Whether the Jeffco recount stands and how Colorado's legislature responds to the message voters just sent on education funding.
