# Colorado's Wealth-Creation Problem: When Envy Replaces Enterprise

Colorado's business climate faces a quiet but corrosive threat: the cultural demonization of wealth and entrepreneurship that has poisoned economic growth across Europe.

A *Washington Examiner* analysis comparing 12 countries found a striking pattern—nations that view the rich as role models (Vietnam) outpace those that treat them as scapegoats (Germany) in economic dynamism and job creation. The psychological climate matters. When politicians and media routinely vilify successful business owners, investors flee, startups stall, and opportunity dries up.

Colorado has historically attracted entrepreneurs and risk-takers. But progressive rhetoric increasingly frames wealth as inherently suspect—something to be taxed, regulated, and apologized for rather than celebrated. This mindset, if it takes root, will hollow out the state's competitive edge.

The stakes are concrete: Colorado's energy sector, tech corridor, and small-business ecosystem depend on capital formation and entrepreneurial confidence. When the cultural message shifts from "build something great" to "you didn't build that," capital and talent migrate to friendlier jurisdictions.

Republicans and conservatives argue that restoring respect for wealth creation—not through propaganda, but through policy that rewards success and punishes failure fairly—is essential to Colorado's future prosperity. The left counters that inequality demands aggressive redistribution.
