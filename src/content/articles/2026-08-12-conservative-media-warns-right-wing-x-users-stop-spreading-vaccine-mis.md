# Conservative Media Warns Against Spreading Unverified Vaccine Claims

Conservative outlets are sounding the alarm: right-leaning social media users are amplifying false claims about COVID-19 vaccines and miscarriage risk—and it's undermining credibility on legitimate health skepticism.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the viral narrative gained traction after Republican senators released documents showing Dr. Anthony Fauci had privately raised *hypothetical* concerns about potential vaccine risks. The problem: conservatives are weaponizing incomplete context into definitive proof of harm, despite no credible evidence supporting widespread miscarriage links to the vaccines.

This matters for Colorado conservatives. The GOP's credibility on government overreach—from regulatory capture to public health mandates—depends on sticking to facts. When right-wing media amplifies unverified claims, it hands Democrats a cudgel to dismiss *all* conservative criticism of federal health bureaucracy as conspiracy-mongering. That's a tactical loss.

Republicans rightly questioned Fauci's transparency and the government's heavy-handed vaccine promotion. But there's a difference between demanding accountability and spreading medical misinformation. Conservatives arguing for medical freedom and skepticism of federal health agencies have legitimate points—but only if they're tethered to evidence, not speculation.

Colorado taxpayers and parents deserve honest information about vaccine safety *and* honest criticism of government overreach. Conflating the two weakens both arguments.

Watch whether conservative media outlets double down on viral claims or course-correct toward fact-based accountability journalism.
