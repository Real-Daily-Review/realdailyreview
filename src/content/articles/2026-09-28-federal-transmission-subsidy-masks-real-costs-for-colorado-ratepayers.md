# Federal Transmission Subsidy Will Hike Colorado Electric Bills

A $1.2 billion federally-funded transmission project in eastern Colorado will ultimately stick ratepayers with the bill, even as Washington throws taxpayer money at the infrastructure. The 28-mile project, backed by federal cash, aims to boost electricity reliability—but Colorado Sun reports most costs will be passed directly to customers through rate increases.

Republicans and energy advocates are raising red flags. Just The News reports a legislator has asked the USDA to intervene in the broader Permian Basin Reliability Plan, which would construct over 1,200 miles of transmission lines stretching from east Texas to the energy-rich Permian Basin. The concern: federal subsidies often mask the true cost burden, which lands on ratepayers and taxpayers anyway.

Conservatives argue this exemplifies Washington's playbook—announce a "federal investment," claim victory, then let utilities and regulators pass the real expenses to families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation. Colorado's energy costs are already climbing; this project could accelerate that trend.

The transmission work may improve grid reliability, a legitimate goal. But the subsidy structure obscures who actually pays. Coloradans should watch whether the Public Utilities Commission rubber-stamps rate hikes without demanding Xcel Energy justify every penny of cost-shifting to consumers.
