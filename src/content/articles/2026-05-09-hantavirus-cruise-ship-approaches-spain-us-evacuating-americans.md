# Hantavirus Cruise Ship Nears Spain as US Arranges Evacuation

The MV Hondius, a research vessel with confirmed hantavirus cases aboard, is set to dock off Spain's coast this weekend, prompting mixed reactions from residents and coordinated international response efforts.

The U.S. State Department is sending a charter flight to evacuate the remaining 17 Americans aboard once the ship arrives, according to The Hill. Spain is preparing to receive the vessel, according to Deutsche Welle, though some Tenerife residents expressed concern about potential health risks from the arrival, the BBC reported.

Two British passengers have already been medically evacuated. A 69-year-old man was airlifted to Johannesburg on April 27 and is receiving care at a private health facility, while expedition guide Martin Anstee, 56, is being treated in the Netherlands. Both are improving, according to global health officials cited by The Guardian.

Key details about the outbreak remain unclear. PBS NewsHour noted that while authorities and the cruise operator have provided updates, significant information gaps persist about the scope and nature of the hantavirus cases aboard.

Hantavirus is a rare but serious illness transmitted through contact with infected rodent droppings or saliva. Watch for updates on the ship's docking procedures and whether additional cases emerge among the remaining passengers and crew.
