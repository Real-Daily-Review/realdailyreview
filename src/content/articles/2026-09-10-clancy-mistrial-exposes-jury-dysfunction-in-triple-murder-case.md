# Mistrial in Clancy Case Exposes Jury Dysfunction—and Raises Questions About Justice

A Massachusetts murder trial ended in mistrial this week when jurors couldn't reach consensus on whether Lindsay Clancy killed her three young children. But the real scandal isn't the hung jury—it's what jurors said afterward.

According to the New York Post, fellow juror Nick Dargie said the lone holdout "did not provide a single piece of evidence" to support his not-guilty vote. Even more damning, Breitbart reports that one juror who voted to acquit admitted she changed her vote mid-deliberation, saying "maybe I still believe" Clancy is guilty "in a certain way."

Prosecutors allege Clancy, obsessed with another woman's social media presence, hanged her 2-year-old son and attempted to kill her other two children. The Daily Wire notes she also allegedly made statements about poisoning her husband with a drink and had multiple prescription bottles on hand.

The juror interviews—conducted almost immediately after the mistrial—drew sharp criticism from The Daily Wire for prioritizing media attention over deliberative responsibility. Meanwhile, Clancy's defense attorney has been forced to publicly debunk TikTok conspiracy theories accusing her ex-husband of involvement.

The case underscores a troubling reality: jury selection and deliberation quality directly determine whether justice is served or criminals walk free.
