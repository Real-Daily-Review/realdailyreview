# Trump Pressures Cotton on Daylight Saving—But the Real Issue Is What Works for Colorado

President Trump published Sen. Tom Cotton's personal cellphone number Saturday and urged Arkansas residents to pressure the Republican into supporting permanent daylight saving time legislation—a move that exposes a genuine split among conservatives over federal overreach disguised as convenience.

Cotton, a reliable Republican, is pushing back hard. "Arkansans don't want their kids going to school in the pitch dark, and I'll always put Arkansas first," he wrote (Just The News). He's also right on the merits: Congress has enacted permanent daylight saving time multiple times before and repealed it each time, according to Breitbart. The lesson is clear—what sounds good in theory often fails in practice.

For Colorado, this matters. Mountain Time already puts us at odds with Pacific zones on business and media scheduling. Permanent daylight saving would shift sunrise times even later in winter, affecting ranchers, school schedules, and outdoor recreation—the backbone of Colorado's economy and lifestyle. Coloradans value getting things right, not rushing federal mandates.

Trump's tactic of publishing a senator's phone number is also worth noting: it's pressure politics that bypasses deliberation, which conservatives should be skeptical of regardless of the cause.

Watch whether Colorado's delegation—particularly in the House—gets dragged into this fight, and whether they'll defend state-level flexibility over federal one-size-fits-all rules.
