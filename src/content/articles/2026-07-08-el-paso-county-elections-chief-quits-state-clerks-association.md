# El Paso County Elections Chief Breaks with State Clerks Association Over Policy Disputes

Colorado's election integrity debate just got sharper: El Paso County Clerk Chuck Schleiker has withdrawn from the Colorado County Clerks Association and pulled his county out of membership, signaling a significant rift over the group's direction (Complete Colorado).

Schleiker's departure reflects growing conservative frustration with how state election officials are handling election administration. Republicans have long argued that some clerks associations lean too far toward progressive voting-access policies at the expense of election security and ballot integrity. By stepping down and removing El Paso County—one of Colorado's largest and most Republican counties—Schleiker is making a statement about where he stands on those priorities.

The move matters beyond internal politics. El Paso County's 2024 election performance and election security record have made it a flashpoint in Colorado's broader debate over voting procedures, mail-in ballots, and voter verification standards. Conservatives view clerks associations as potential vectors for imposing uniform policies that may not reflect local county values or security concerns.

Schleiker's exit could embolden other Republican-leaning counties to reconsider their membership and chart independent courses on election administration—a development that could reshape how Colorado's 64 counties coordinate on voting procedures.
