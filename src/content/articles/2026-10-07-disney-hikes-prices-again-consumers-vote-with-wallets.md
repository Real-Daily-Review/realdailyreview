# Disney's Latest Price Hike Shows Free Market at Work—and Consumers Voting With Their Wallets

Disney jacked up theme park ticket prices again this week, with Magic Kingdom's premium one-day admission hitting $229 (9News Denver), while some premium tiers near $500 (Breitbart). The company left its cheapest tickets—around $120 for Animal Kingdom—unchanged, a classic strategy to shield entry-level pricing while milking high-end consumers.

The move sparked predictable backlash. Breitbart reports fans are furious, with social media erupting over the increases. But here's the conservative take: Disney isn't breaking any laws. It's a private company charging what the market will bear. If families decide the price isn't worth it, they'll spend their entertainment dollars elsewhere—on Colorado ski resorts, national parks, or local attractions that offer better value.

This is exactly how capitalism *should* work. No government mandate forced Disney to raise prices. No regulatory agency approved the hike. Disney made a business calculation, consumers are responding, and the market adjusts. Some will pay; many won't. That's competition.

For Colorado families watching their own budgets tighten under inflation and state tax burdens, Disney's pricing is a reminder: government doesn't need to "do something" about private companies' pricing. The real issue is whether Colorado's own tax and regulatory climate is pricing out middle-class residents from staying and thriving here.

Watch whether Disney's gamble pays off—or whether consumer pushback forces a correction.
