# Boulder's Climate Lawsuit Reaches Supreme Court—But Conservatives Say It's Judicial Overreach

Colorado's Marshall Fire has become a vehicle for climate activists to bypass the ballot box and impose energy policy through the courts. The Supreme Court will hear Boulder's lawsuit against oil companies this term, marking the culmination of an eight-year legal battle that could reshape energy liability nationwide.

Boulder argues that Suncor Energy and other defendants should pay for wildfire damages tied to climate change. The case is one of dozens filed across the country using similar logic—holding energy producers liable for warming-linked disasters (Colorado Sun, CPR News).

**Conservatives and Republicans counter that this represents judicial activism run amok.** National Review argues that climate activists, "unable to elect legislative majorities," are weaponizing courts to impose energy policies voters have rejected at the ballot box. The strategy bypasses democratic processes and threatens to saddle energy companies with retroactive liability for lawful operations—a precedent that could chill investment and raise energy costs for Colorado families and businesses.

The case also raises federalism questions: should local courts rewrite national energy policy, or does that belong to Congress and state legislatures?

**Watch whether the Court limits climate liability claims or opens the floodgates to similar suits targeting Colorado's oil and gas sector.**
