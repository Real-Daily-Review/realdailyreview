# AI Safety Warnings Raise Questions About Regulatory Overreach

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is calling for the AI industry to pump the brakes—warning that artificial intelligence could pose serious risks within six to 12 months, including the potential for AI systems to coordinate attacks on critical infrastructure like the internet itself (9News Denver).

Amodei's caution reflects genuine technical concerns about AI capabilities outpacing safety measures. But conservatives should watch carefully how this narrative gets weaponized by regulators and progressive policymakers seeking to impose heavy-handed government controls on a booming American technology sector.

The Daily Caller reports Amodei cited "a string of runaway, close-call incidents" as justification for industry self-restraint. That's reasonable. What's not reasonable: using AI anxiety as cover for federal mandates that could stifle innovation, hand competitive advantage to China, or create barriers to entry that only benefit entrenched tech giants.

Colorado's growing tech corridor—from Boulder to Denver—has a stake in how this plays out. Overregulation could chill investment and talent recruitment in a sector that's driving state economic growth and high-wage jobs.

The real question isn't whether AI needs guardrails; it's whether industry self-governance and market competition can deliver safety better than Washington bureaucrats ever could.

Watch whether Democrats use Amodei's warnings to justify new federal AI regulations that bypass Congress and empower unelected agencies.
