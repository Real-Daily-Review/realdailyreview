# Denver Election Audit Raises Questions About Post-Election Procedures

Denver's election office conducted a routine ballot audit just two weeks after the midterm election—a timeline that raises concerns among conservatives about whether Colorado's largest city has adequate safeguards in place (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The audit itself is standard procedure, but the timing underscores a broader conservative worry: election integrity protocols must be ironclad, especially in heavily Democratic jurisdictions where oversight can slip.

Republicans argue that post-election audits should occur sooner and with greater transparency to catch irregularities before they're buried in bureaucratic paperwork. The left counters that routine audits, conducted weeks after voting ends, are sufficient and that questioning their timing fuels unfounded skepticism about election security.

Colorado conservatives should demand clarity on what Denver's audit actually checked—voter roll accuracy, ballot chain-of-custody, machine calibration—and whether results are made fully public. Election integrity isn't partisan; it's foundational. Coloradans deserve to know their votes count and that Denver's procedures match the gold standard, not just the minimum.
