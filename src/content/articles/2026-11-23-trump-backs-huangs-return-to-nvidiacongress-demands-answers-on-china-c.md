# Trump Brokers Nvidia CEO Return—But Congress Wants Answers on China Chip Leaks

President Trump orchestrated Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO on Monday, but the victory lap masks a serious problem: lawmakers are demanding sworn testimony over allegations that Commerce Department officials let China circumvent U.S. chip export controls.

Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.) is pushing for Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to testify under oath about the disputed claims, according to Fox News. The dispute centers on whether Trump administration officials improperly facilitated Chinese access to advanced semiconductor technology—a critical national security issue that directly threatens American technological dominance and Colorado's tech and defense sectors.

Trump told CNBC he brokered the "deal" to bring Huang back and dismissed concerns that Huang's partner was a Chinese spy (The Daily Wire). Nvidia's board unanimously voted to reinstate Huang Monday, with the CEO calling it an honor to lead "one of the most consequential technology companies in the world."

But conservatives and Republicans are right to demand clarity: if the Commerce Department actually compromised U.S. export controls on chips, that's not a personnel squabble—it's a potential breach of national security policy. Colorado's defense contractors and tech firms depend on a level playing field against Chinese competitors.

Watch whether Congress forces those sworn depositions and what they reveal about Commerce's China dealings.
