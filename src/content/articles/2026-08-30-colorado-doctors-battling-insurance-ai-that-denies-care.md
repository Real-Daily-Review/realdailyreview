# Prior Authorization Chaos: AI Denying Colorado Patients Care While Bureaucrats Call the Shots

Doctors across Colorado are increasingly locked in battles with insurance company AI systems that deny or delay life-saving treatments—a regulatory nightmare that puts bureaucratic efficiency ahead of patient survival.

The Washington Examiner reports that prior authorization, the insurance industry's gatekeeper process, has become dominated by artificial intelligence algorithms that physicians say lack medical judgment and often contradict sound clinical practice. Rather than doctors deciding treatment, insurance companies deploy AI to second-guess diagnoses and block prescriptions, forcing Colorado physicians to spend hours appealing denials instead of treating patients.

Republicans and conservatives argue this represents government-enabled regulatory overreach that prioritizes cost-cutting over care. The system, they contend, exemplifies how progressive healthcare mandates and insurance regulations create perverse incentives—insurers use AI to maximize denials, knowing most patients won't fight back, while doctors waste time on paperwork rather than medicine.

Democrats defend prior authorization as necessary cost control in a system they claim would otherwise spiral. Yet even left-leaning healthcare advocates acknowledge the current AI-driven process is broken.

For Colorado patients—especially seniors on Medicare and rural residents with limited specialist access—these delays can mean the difference between recovery and permanent disability. The state legislature should examine whether Colorado's insurance regulations are enabling this abuse.
