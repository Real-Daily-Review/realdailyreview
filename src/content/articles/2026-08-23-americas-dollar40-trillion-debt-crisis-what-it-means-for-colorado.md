# America's $40 Trillion Debt Hits Catastrophic Milestone—Colorado Taxpayers Will Pay the Price

The national debt has crossed $40 trillion, a fiscal catastrophe that should alarm every Colorado family watching their tax dollars vanish into Washington's bottomless pit.

The milestone reflects decades of bipartisan spending excess. The War on Terror alone has cost nearly $10 trillion since 2001 (Just The News), while entitlements, defense, and interest payments continue their relentless climb. The Washington Examiner warns the nation is "heading for bankruptcy"—a crisis that will inevitably hit Colorado's economy through inflation, higher interest rates, and pressure for tax increases on working families and small businesses.

Republicans argue Washington must immediately cut spending and reform entitlements before the debt becomes unpayable. Democrats typically counter that the real problem is insufficient tax revenue, pushing for higher levies on corporations and the wealthy—a position that threatens Colorado's competitive business environment and job creation.

For Colorado specifically, this debt spiral means less federal funding flexibility for water projects, energy development, and infrastructure—while pressure mounts to raid state budgets to cover federal shortfalls. Higher federal borrowing costs will also drive up mortgage rates and business lending, directly hurting Colorado homebuyers and entrepreneurs.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democrats—will demand real spending cuts or simply call for more taxes on Colorado workers and businesses.
