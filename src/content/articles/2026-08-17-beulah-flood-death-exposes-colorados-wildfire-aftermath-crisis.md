# Flash Flood Kills Beulah Resident; Burn Scar Vulnerability Exposes Colorado's Wildfire-Preparedness Gap

An 80-year-old Pueblo County man, Henry R. Humphreys, died Sunday when flash flooding swept through Beulah following 1 to 1½ inches of rain—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis creates cascading dangers long after flames are extinguished (Colorado Sun, KDVR Fox31 Denver).

The fatal flooding struck an area still recovering from the Aspen Acres fire, which destroyed over 300 homes earlier this summer. Burn scars leave soil unable to absorb water, turning modest rainfall into dangerous torrents. More rain is forecast this week across Colorado burn areas.

**The conservative concern:** While Democrats and environmental groups blame climate change and demand more federal spending on green energy transitions, Republicans point out that Colorado's real vulnerability stems from years of mismanaged forests—overgrown with dead timber and fuel buildup—and inadequate forest management. Conservatives argue that aggressive timber harvesting, controlled burns, and fuel reduction on state and federal lands would reduce both wildfire severity and post-fire flooding risks far more effectively than climate spending.

Coloradans should demand that state and federal land managers prioritize forest health over environmental litigation that delays necessary thinning and prescribed burns.

Watch whether state officials will accelerate forest management on Colorado's public lands or continue letting bureaucratic delays compound the danger.
