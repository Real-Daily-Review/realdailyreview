# SEC's Accredited-Investor Rules Lock Out Middle-Class Coloradans From Capital Markets

The Securities and Exchange Commission's restrictions on who can invest in private securities are strangling opportunity for ordinary Americans—including Colorado entrepreneurs and savers who lack seven-figure net worth or inherited wealth.

The SEC's "accredited investor" rules create a two-tiered system: the wealthy can access dynamic private capital markets, while middle-class professionals—doctors, engineers, small-business owners—are deemed too unsophisticated to manage their own money, regardless of education or expertise (Washington Examiner). Conservatives argue these paternalistic regulations contradict free-market principles and the American dream itself. If someone with an economics doctorate can't invest without SEC permission, the agency has abandoned its duty to facilitate capital formation and embraced bureaucratic gatekeeping.

For Colorado's business community, the rules mean fewer investors for startups and growth companies. Talented engineers in Denver or Fort Collins who want to back promising ventures are blocked by arbitrary wealth thresholds. Republicans and free-market advocates say the SEC should trust Americans to assess risk themselves rather than imposing one-size-fits-all restrictions that protect no one while limiting opportunity.

The agency claims these rules protect retail investors from fraud. Conservatives counter that transparency and fraud prosecution work better than blanket exclusions.

Watch whether Congress moves to reform or eliminate accredited-investor restrictions—a litmus test for whether Republicans will actually deregulate or just talk about it.
