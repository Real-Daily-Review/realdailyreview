# Woke Ideology Isn't Dead—It's Just Rebranding

Colorado voters should brace for a resurgence of progressive cultural activism as Democrats position themselves for 2026 midterm gains, conservative analysts warn.

National Review and the Daily Caller report that so-called "woke" ideology hasn't disappeared—it's evolved. Rather than retreating, the movement is shifting tactics and messaging as Democratic political fortunes improve. The Washington Examiner's opinion section argues that what conservatives call "Woke 2.0" will prove even more aggressive than its predecessor, with Democrats quietly abandoning public criticism of the original movement while preparing to advance similar policies under new framing.

Republicans argue this rebranding masks continuity: the same progressive priorities on race, gender, and identity politics, now dressed in softer language to avoid electoral backlash. Conservatives say voters should watch closely for how these ideas resurface in education policy, corporate governance, and local government—areas where Colorado has already seen pitched battles over curriculum and DEI initiatives.

The concern: Democrats may use the appearance of moderation to lower public guard while advancing the same expansive government and cultural agenda that animated the 2020-2024 period.

**Watch for:** How Colorado school boards and state education officials respond to renewed pressure on gender and race-based curriculum changes heading into 2026.
