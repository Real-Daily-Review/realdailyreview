# U.S. Resumes Pacific Drug-Trafficking Strikes After Two-Month Pause

The U.S. military has resumed its bombing campaign against suspected drug-trafficking vessels in the eastern Pacific, killing two people in the latest strike (Fox News). The operation marks the first announced attack in roughly two months as part of a nearly yearlong campaign that has now exceeded 210 deaths across more than 60 strikes, according to 9News Denver.

The military characterized the targets as "narco-terrorists" involved in trafficking operations, though details on vessel identification and civilian casualty protocols remain limited. The campaign reflects the Pentagon's aggressive posture toward transnational criminal organizations moving product toward U.S. shores—a priority that has intensified amid the fentanyl crisis.

For Colorado, the stakes are direct: fentanyl deaths continue ravaging communities across the state, making supply-chain disruption a legitimate law-enforcement interest. However, conservatives should scrutinize the legal and operational transparency of these strikes. Questions linger about rules of engagement, intelligence verification, and whether maritime interdiction paired with stronger border security and port screening might achieve results with clearer accountability.

The broader concern: without congressional oversight and clear authorization parameters, even well-intentioned military operations risk mission creep and unintended consequences.
