# Colorado Voters Get Chance to Lock Highway Money Into Roads

Colorado taxpayers will finally get a say in whether gas and motor vehicle tax revenue actually stays dedicated to fixing roads instead of vanishing into the state budget's general fund.

A ballot measure heading to November—Initiative 175, per the Denver Post—would require state agencies to spend all motor vehicle and gas tax revenue on transportation infrastructure. It's a straightforward accountability measure: money collected for roads goes to roads.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that Colorado's transportation funding gets diverted for other state priorities, undermining the original intent of fuel taxes. The measure represents a direct response to that concern, giving voters a mechanism to enforce fiscal discipline on state government.

Democrats haven't mounted a visible public opposition campaign yet, though the measure will likely face scrutiny during the fall campaign season. The real question: will state bureaucrats and Democratic legislators who benefit from flexible budget authority fight to preserve their discretion?

This matters for Colorado's crumbling infrastructure and for the principle that dedicated revenue should mean what it says. Watch whether the state's transportation lobby and business groups—who depend on road quality—actively campaign for the measure's passage.
