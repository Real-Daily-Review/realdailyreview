# Colorado Loses Labor Icon, But Her Progressive Legacy Endures

Dolores Huerta, the legendary labor organizer and social-justice activist who shaped Colorado's political left for decades, died this week at 96. According to CPR News, Huerta's influence on Colorado women leaders—particularly in progressive causes—remains substantial, with her advocacy for workers' rights and social justice continuing to inspire a new generation of Democratic activists and policymakers across the state.

Huerta co-founded the United Farm Workers movement alongside César Chávez and spent her life organizing low-wage workers and pushing for expanded government protections and union power. While conservatives acknowledge her historical significance and personal dedication, they argue her legacy reflects the very labor-regulation and big-government approaches that have made Colorado's business climate less competitive than neighboring states.

Republicans note that while Huerta championed worker organizing, her model of expanded union influence and regulatory mandates has contributed to rising labor costs and reduced business flexibility—concerns that remain central to conservative economic policy debates in Colorado today.

Her passing marks the end of an era for Colorado's progressive movement, which has increasingly dominated state politics over the past two decades.

Watch for how Colorado Democrats invoke Huerta's memory in upcoming labor and wage-policy debates—and whether Republicans can articulate a free-market alternative that resonates with working Coloradans tired of government overreach.
