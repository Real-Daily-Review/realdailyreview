# Colorado's 2026 CMAS Results Expose Persistent Education Gaps—and Questions About State Spending

Colorado released its 2026 CMAS (Colorado Measures of Academic Success) scores this week, offering a searchable breakdown of how schools and districts performed in math and literacy across the state (CPR News). The results reveal uneven achievement—a reality that demands scrutiny of how the state deploys its education budget.

Colorado has steadily increased K-12 spending over the past decade, yet proficiency gaps remain stubborn. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that throwing more money at struggling districts without demanding accountability and structural reform—like expanding school choice, merit-based teacher pay, and reducing administrative bloat—simply perpetuates mediocrity. Democrats counter that underfunding remains the core problem and call for additional investment.

The data matters for Colorado taxpayers footing the bill. Parents in underperforming districts deserve answers: Where is the money going? Why aren't results improving proportionally? Are bureaucratic overhead and one-size-fits-all curricula part of the problem?

Meanwhile, Colorado School of Mines welcomed its freshman class Friday with the traditional "M" climb on Mount Zion in Golden (KDVR), a reminder that Colorado's top-tier institutions thrive—but access and quality remain unequal statewide.
