# Data Center Boom Hits Speed Bump as Communities Demand Answers on Costs

Even Trump's home county is pumping the brakes on AI data centers—and Colorado should be paying attention.

Palm Beach County recently imposed a one-year moratorium on new data center development, citing environmental concerns, noise pollution, and skyrocketing electric bills (Fox News). The move signals growing pushback from communities worried that the rush to build AI infrastructure is being imposed without proper public input or cost analysis.

Congressional candidate Stephen Wagley echoed voter concerns, telling Breitbart that residents' worries about data center sprawl are "very valid" and that officials owe communities transparency about the real costs and impacts.

**The Colorado angle:** As tech companies eye the Front Range for data center expansion, state and local officials must answer hard questions about who pays for grid upgrades, water consumption, and infrastructure strain. Conservatives and taxpayers should demand that any data center deals include genuine cost-benefit analysis—not just corporate tax breaks and vague promises of jobs.

The pattern is clear: communities are tired of being sold a bill of goods. Developers get subsidies and fast-track approvals; residents get higher utility bills and environmental degradation. Colorado's legislature and county commissions need to learn from Palm Beach's caution before signing away the state's resources to another tech boom-and-bust cycle.
