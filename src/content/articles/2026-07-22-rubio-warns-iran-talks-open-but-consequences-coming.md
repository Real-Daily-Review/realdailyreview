# Rubio Draws Line on Iran: Talks Yes, Appeasement No

Secretary of State Marco Rubio is signaling resolve on Iran while keeping diplomatic channels nominally open—a sharp departure from the Obama-era capitulation that conservatives warned would embolden Tehran. Rubio warned that Iran faces "consequences" for violating commitments and demanding illegal control over the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy markets that directly affects Colorado's economy and energy sector.

"The problem we're having right now is they're not serious about talks. If they're serious, we're serious," Rubio said, according to the New York Post. The message is clear: the Trump administration won't repeat the mistakes of the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), which Republicans argue was a one-sided surrender that left Iran's nuclear ambitions intact while flooding the regime with cash.

For Colorado, Iran's regional aggression and nuclear ambitions carry real stakes. Instability in the Middle East drives oil prices higher, affecting energy workers and consumers across the state. A stronger U.S. posture—backed by credible consequences—protects American energy independence and Colorado's oil and gas industry from geopolitical shocks.

Conservatives should watch whether Rubio's tough rhetoric translates into actual sanctions enforcement and whether Iran tests American resolve with further provocations.
