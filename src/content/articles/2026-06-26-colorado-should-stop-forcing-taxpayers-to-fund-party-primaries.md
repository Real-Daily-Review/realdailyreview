# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Party Politics

Colorado taxpayers are being forced to subsidize primary elections for political parties they may not even support—a clear case of government overreach that conservatives say must end.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state has no legitimate business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties, let alone financing their primaries with public money. The argument is straightforward: why should a Colorado taxpayer who belongs to no party, or who supports a different party, be compelled to foot the bill for Democratic or Republican primary contests?

This isn't a partisan complaint—it's a matter of principle. Conservatives argue that political parties are private organizations and should fund their own operations. When government forces citizens to finance party machinery, it violates basic principles of individual liberty and limited government.

*Complete Colorado* frames this as a "courageous" move toward separating state from party, suggesting Colorado could lead on this issue. The practical effect would be significant: Colorado voters and taxpayers would no longer subsidize primary elections they may oppose or refuse to participate in.

Watch for legislative proposals to shift primary costs entirely to the parties themselves—a test of whether Colorado's elected officials truly believe in fiscal responsibility and individual freedom.
