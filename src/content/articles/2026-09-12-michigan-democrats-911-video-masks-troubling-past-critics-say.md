# Michigan Democrat's 9/11 Messaging Raises Questions About Past Associations

A Michigan Democratic Senate candidate's carefully crafted 9/11 tribute video is drawing scrutiny over his past associations and deleted comments about the terrorist attack. Abdul El-Sayed, who is running to become the first Muslim U.S. Senator, released a somber video Friday honoring 9/11 victims—a stark departure from his previous public statements on the subject, according to Fox News and The Daily Wire.

The backlash centers on El-Sayed's connection to Hasan Piker, a commentator who infamously claimed America deserved the 9/11 attacks. El-Sayed's video message prompted fierce online criticism over the association, with conservatives questioning the authenticity of his sudden shift in tone.

Republicans argue the contrast between El-Sayed's current messaging and his deleted past comments reveals a pattern of political calculation rather than genuine conviction. Conservatives say voters deserve clarity on a candidate's actual views on national security and American values, not carefully timed statements designed to manage optics during election season.

While this race unfolds in Michigan, Colorado Republicans should note how national Democratic figures navigate identity politics and past statements—lessons applicable to state and local races where similar dynamics may emerge.
