# Colorado's Business Climate Problem Demands Real Reform, Not More Government Tools

Colorado's reputation as a business-friendly state is eroding—and state leaders are responding with the predictable government solution: another tool, another grant, another bureaucratic layer.

Business leaders have grown frustrated with Colorado's deteriorating business climate, according to the *Colorado Sun*. Companies cite regulatory burdens, tax policy, and workforce challenges as key obstacles. One entrepreneur summed up the stakes: "I want this to be the place that my kid chooses to raise their kids."

The state's answer? The Office of Economic Development and International Trade received a SIPA GovGrant to redesign its Business Resource Navigation Tool, with work beginning in November 2026, *KDVR Fox31 Denver* reports.

Here's the conservative concern: Colorado doesn't need another government database or navigation portal. Businesses need *less* regulation, lower taxes, and a streamlined permitting process. If entrepreneurs can't figure out how to do business in Colorado without a state-funded tech tool, the problem isn't information access—it's that government has made compliance too complicated.

Real reform means cutting red tape, not digitizing it. Conservatives should demand that state leaders address the actual barriers: excessive regulations, high tax rates, and a permitting process that punishes job creators.

Watch whether lawmakers push for genuine deregulation or simply throw more grant money at the symptom while ignoring the disease.
