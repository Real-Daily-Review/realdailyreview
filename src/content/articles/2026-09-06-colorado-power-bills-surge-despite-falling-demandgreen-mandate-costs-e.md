# Colorado Faces Rising Power Bills While Demand Drops—A Warning Sign for Green Energy Policy

Colorado households are watching electricity prices climb faster than inflation, a trend that defies the green-energy promise that renewable mandates would lower costs. According to the *Washington Examiner*, states experiencing the steepest price hikes over the past five years aren't those attracting industrial growth—like Texas—but states where demand has actually fallen. Nationally, electricity prices rose 4.2% over the past year alone, outpacing broader inflation.

The pattern raises hard questions for Colorado policymakers. The state has aggressively pursued renewable energy mandates and grid modernization, yet ratepayers are footing increasingly expensive bills. Conservatives argue this is the predictable result of top-down energy policy: when government mandates expensive generation sources and forces utilities to retire reliable coal and natural gas plants before replacements are ready, costs inevitably rise—especially when demand softens and fixed infrastructure costs get spread across fewer kilowatt-hours.

Democrats and renewable advocates counter that transition costs are temporary and necessary for climate goals. Republicans and taxpayer groups say Colorado should demand transparency from the Public Utilities Commission on whether mandates are delivering promised savings, or whether ratepayers are simply subsidizing an ideological experiment.

Watch for Colorado's next PUC rate case and whether legislators push utilities to justify why prices keep climbing while demand stalls.
