# PBMs, Not Drugmakers, Are the Real Villains in Prescription Drug Pricing

Colorado families paying sky-high pharmacy bills should know the real culprit isn't the pharmaceutical companies—it's the middlemen siphoning profits at every turn.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) and insurers are the primary drivers of inflated drug costs, capturing a growing share of total drug spending while inflation-adjusted net drug prices remain relatively stable. Rather than target actual manufacturers, politicians and voters are being steered toward blaming Big Pharma—a convenient distraction that lets the real profiteers operate in the shadows.

This matters for Colorado taxpayers and workers covered by employer health plans. PBMs control which drugs insurers cover, negotiate rebates that rarely reach patients, and pocket the spread between wholesale and retail prices. The system is rigged to benefit intermediaries, not consumers or innovators.

Republicans and conservatives argue that instead of imposing price controls that would chill drug innovation, policymakers should demand transparency from PBMs and reduce their gatekeeping power. Allowing direct negotiation between patients, doctors, and manufacturers—without middlemen skimming profits—would drive real savings.

Watch for Colorado legislators pushing "PBM reform" bills that actually increase transparency and competition rather than simply shifting blame to pharmaceutical companies.
