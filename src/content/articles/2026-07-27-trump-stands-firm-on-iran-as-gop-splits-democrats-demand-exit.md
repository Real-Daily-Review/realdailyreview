# Trump, Republicans Clash Over Iran Strategy as Democrats Push Immediate Pullout

President Trump is standing firm on military readiness in the Iran conflict, dismissing concerns about munitions shortages and rejecting calls for immediate withdrawal. Speaking to the Wall Street Journal, Trump said the U.S. military has "far more munitions than anyone in the world, and far more than we need" (Breitbart).

Republicans are split on next steps. Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) warned against "cut and run" diplomacy, telling CBS's *Face the Nation* that the U.S. should maintain resolve rather than negotiate from weakness (Washington Examiner). But Kennedy also criticized Senate Majority Leader John Thune for being "too cautious" on advancing Trump's broader agenda (New York Post).

Democrats, meanwhile, are pushing for immediate de-escalation. Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) called on Trump to "end" the conflict "right now" on ABC's *This Week* (Breitbart)—a position conservatives argue abandons American leverage and emboldens Tehran.

**The Colorado angle:** Defense contractors and energy firms with Colorado ties watch closely; any Iran escalation affects oil markets and defense spending that supports local jobs.

**Watch next:** Whether Trump's munitions confidence translates to sustained military commitment or if Democratic pressure for talks gains traction in Congress.
