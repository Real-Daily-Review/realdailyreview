# Trump's Press Ban Draws Fire From Right and Left—But for Different Reasons

Donald Trump's restrictions on press access are backfiring, drawing criticism even from conservative outlets that normally support his agenda. *National Review* argues Trump should rescind the ban outright, calling it "a violation of the spirit of a free press" and contending he'd be better served confronting hostile reporters directly rather than silencing them (National Review). Meanwhile, *Colorado Sun* columnist Mike Littwin compares the move to Trump's foreign policy missteps, framing it as another strategic disaster.

The split is instructive. Conservatives who champion the First Amendment—a cornerstone of limited government and individual liberty—recognize that Trump's approach undermines the constitutional principles his supporters claim to defend. Republicans argue that winning the argument in the court of public opinion requires engagement, not avoidance. Silencing the press, they contend, only feeds the narrative that Trump fears scrutiny.

For Colorado voters and taxpayers, the broader lesson cuts deeper: government power—whether wielded by Trump or any executive—expands when unchecked. A free press, however imperfect, remains essential to holding power accountable at every level, from Washington to the State Capitol.

Watch whether Trump reverses course or doubles down on press restrictions ahead of 2026.
