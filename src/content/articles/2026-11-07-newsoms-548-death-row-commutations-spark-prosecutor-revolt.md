# California Governor's Mass Death-Row Commutations Signal Radical Shift on Criminal Justice

California Gov. Gavin Newsom's plan to commute all 548 death sentences to life without parole represents a stunning override of jury verdicts and victim families—and signals how far progressive governance will go to dismantle traditional criminal justice.

Prosecutors are in open revolt. California district attorneys and law enforcement unions have blasted Newsom for "putting politics over people" (Fox News), accusing him of betraying victims' families and ignoring the jury process that convicted these inmates. A former deputy state attorney general called the move "pompous" and a betrayal of the entire justice system (New York Post).

Republicans argue this exemplifies progressive governance run amok: elected officials substituting their ideology for the rule of law and the democratic verdict of juries. Conservatives say it sends a message that even the most serious crimes—murder convictions upheld through trial—can be erased by executive whim.

**The Colorado angle:** While Colorado hasn't pursued mass commutations, progressive prosecutors and judges here have pushed similar soft-on-crime policies. Coloradans should watch whether this California model influences state criminal justice debates, particularly as Democrats control the legislature and governor's office.

Watch whether California's prosecutors mount legal challenges to Newsom's commutation authority—and whether those arguments gain traction in other states considering similar policies.
