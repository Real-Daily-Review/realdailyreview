# E-Bike Crash Snarls I-70 Traffic in Denver

A crash between a motorist and an e-bike rider shut down westbound lanes on Interstate 70 in Denver, highlighting the growing friction between traditional vehicles and the proliferation of e-bikes on Colorado roadways.

Denver Police investigated the incident, which sent one person to the hospital (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). Several westbound lanes were closed during the response, creating traffic delays during peak hours.

The collision underscores a broader concern among Colorado drivers and transportation advocates: e-bikes operating in high-speed highway corridors designed for motor vehicles create unpredictable hazards. While e-bike advocates tout environmental benefits, conservatives and commuters point out that mixing lightweight electric bicycles with 70-mph traffic on interstate highways is a recipe for injury—and that Denver's infrastructure planning has failed to separate these modes safely.

The incident also raises questions about liability and enforcement. Who bears responsibility when an e-bike enters a highway? Are current traffic laws and safety regulations adequate, or does Denver need clearer rules about where e-bikes can legally operate?

Watch for whether Denver or Colorado state lawmakers respond with new regulations on e-bike use in high-traffic corridors—and whether those rules actually prioritize public safety over green-energy ideology.
