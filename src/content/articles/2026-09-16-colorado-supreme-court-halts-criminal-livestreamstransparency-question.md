# Colorado Supreme Court Halts Criminal Court Livestreams—Citing Concerns Over Judicial Independence

Colorado's Supreme Court has pumped the brakes on livestreaming criminal proceedings, imposing a temporary 90-day pause that Chief Justice Monica Márquez announced Tuesday (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The move raises questions about transparency and who decides what Coloradans get to see in their own courtrooms.

The court cited concerns about judicial independence and courtroom decorum as justification for the blackout. But conservatives and transparency advocates argue that public access to criminal proceedings is foundational to the rule of law—and that judges shouldn't get to hide behind closed doors simply because cameras make them uncomfortable.

The pause comes as the Supreme Court weighs permanent rules on livestreaming. That decision will determine whether Colorado citizens can watch criminal trials remotely or whether the judiciary maintains gatekeeping power over public proceedings.

Meanwhile, the broader fight over executive power and regulatory overreach continues nationally. The Daily Wire notes that the Supreme Court's recent ruling in *Trump v. Slaughter* corrected a 1935 precedent that had hobbled presidential authority over the executive branch—a win for those seeking to rein in the administrative state's unelected bureaucrats.
