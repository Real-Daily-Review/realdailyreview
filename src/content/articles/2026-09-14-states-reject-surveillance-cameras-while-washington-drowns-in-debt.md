# States Pump Brakes on Mass Surveillance Cameras—But Washington's Spending Binge Keeps Accelerating

Across the country, states are finally waking up to the dangers of unchecked surveillance infrastructure. According to the Daily Caller, Americans are rejecting the expansion of automatic license plate reader (ALPR) networks that track vehicle movements in real time—a bipartisan concern gaining traction from Pennsylvania to California.

The pushback reflects legitimate conservative worries about government overreach and privacy invasion. Republicans and Democrats alike recognize that mass surveillance systems, once deployed, rarely face meaningful restraint. Colorado should take note: as cities and counties consider ALPR expansion, taxpayers deserve transparency about costs, data retention, and who accesses the footage.

Meanwhile, Washington's fiscal recklessness continues unchecked. The Washington Examiner reports that Treasury yields have hit 19-year highs as the bond market signals alarm over federal borrowing. The 10-year yield approached 5%, squeezing Americans already battered by inflation and rising living costs. Republicans argue the spending binge must stop—fiscal responsibility cannot be optional.

The contrast is stark: states are finally saying no to invasive government tools, yet Washington keeps spending money it doesn't have. Colorado families and businesses feel both pressures: surveillance creep from above, inflation from Washington's debt addiction.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand real answers on ALPR costs and data security before green-lighting expansion.
