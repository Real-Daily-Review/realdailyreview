# Colorado Springs Faces Lawsuit Over April Election Timing—First Test of 2025 Voting Law

Colorado Springs is being sued for scheduling municipal elections in April, marking the first enforcement action under a voting law that took effect in 2025, according to Complete Colorado. The lawsuit challenges whether the city's election timing complies with state requirements—a critical test case for how Colorado's election rules will be applied going forward.

The suit arrives as Colorado grapples with a separate economic headwind: a labor shortage that's constraining growth across the state and neighboring regions. The Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City reports the worker deficit is spreading beyond traditional sectors, limiting job creation and business expansion statewide, per CPR News.

Republicans have long argued that election timing and administration matter for voter participation and election integrity. This lawsuit will clarify whether municipalities can set their own election schedules or must conform to state-mandated timelines—a question with implications for how Colorado conducts local races.

The labor shortage, meanwhile, underscores a broader concern among conservatives: Colorado's regulatory and tax environment may be discouraging workforce participation and business investment. As the state continues to add regulations and progressive policies, employers face mounting pressure to operate elsewhere.

**Watch whether Colorado Springs prevails in court and whether other municipalities face similar challenges—or whether the state moves to clarify election rules before more lawsuits follow.**
