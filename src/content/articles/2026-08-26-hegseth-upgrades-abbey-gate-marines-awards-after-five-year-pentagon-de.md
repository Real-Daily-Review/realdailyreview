# Pentagon Finally Honors Abbey Gate Heroes—Five Years Too Late

War Secretary Pete Hegseth awarded upgraded Bronze Star Medals with valor devices to three Marines who risked their lives during the 2021 Abbey Gate bombing in Kabul, marking a rare moment of accountability for the catastrophic Afghanistan withdrawal. The ceremony comes five years after ISIS-K terrorists killed 13 American service members in one of the war's deadliest incidents.

Hegseth's decision followed witness accounts and direct meetings with personnel present at the gate. Sean Parnell, leading the Pentagon's special review panel on the withdrawal, confirmed that "highly relevant" classified documents about the operation were previously hidden—material that "should have been disclosed" (Just The News). The upgrade signals a sharp break from the Biden administration's handling of the withdrawal disaster.

President Trump honored the fallen on the anniversary, underscoring conservative demands for accountability. Republicans have long argued the 2021 withdrawal was bungled at the highest levels, leaving Americans vulnerable and emboldening terrorist groups.

The ceremony reflects what conservatives see as overdue recognition of military heroism—but also exposes how the Pentagon buried critical records about one of America's most humiliating foreign policy failures. Coloradans should watch whether the ongoing Pentagon review produces consequences for the officials who ordered the withdrawal and concealed evidence.
