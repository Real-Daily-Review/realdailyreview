# Michigan, Maine Senate Races Heat Up as Democrats Deploy Personal Attacks in Debate Season

Republican Mike Rogers and Democrat Abdul El-Sayed clashed Thursday night in Michigan's first Senate debate, trading personal attacks over Israel, Iran, and domestic policy in what *Just The News* called "one of the most closely watched elections of the 2026 midterms." Rogers is fighting to flip the seat for the GOP against the progressive El-Sayed.

Meanwhile, in Maine, Democratic challenger Troy Jackson accused incumbent Sen. Susan Collins of profiting from Middle East conflicts during their Thursday debate—a charge Collins flatly rejected and demanded he retract, according to the *New York Post*. Jackson argued Collins wasn't using her leverage to stop regional wars because she and her husband allegedly benefit financially.

**The conservative takeaway:** Democrats are leaning hard on personal attacks and unsubstantiated financial accusations rather than defending their own records on inflation, border security, or energy policy. These tactics signal weakness on the issues that matter to voters—and suggest the left knows its progressive agenda is a liability heading into 2026.

Republicans should watch whether these personal-attack strategies backfire with independent voters who want solutions, not scorched-earth politics.
