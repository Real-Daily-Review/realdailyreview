# Corporate Vote-Buying Stunt Exposes Left's Election Machine

Patagonia handed out $100 credits to 112,000 customers who pledged to vote in the midterm election—a brazen corporate attempt to mobilize Democratic-leaning voters under the guise of civic duty, according to 9News Denver. The outdoor apparel company has made voter mobilization a corporate habit, shuttering U.S. offices and stores during every federal election since 2016.

The move reveals how progressive corporations weaponize their market position to engineer electoral outcomes. Conservatives argue this crosses the line from free speech into corporate manipulation of the electorate. When a company with a built-in customer base—largely affluent, urban progressives—offers financial incentives tied to voting behavior, it's not grassroots activism; it's astroturf with a price tag.

Meanwhile, Republicans are mounting their own midterm push. Former President Trump is hitting the campaign trail in Texas and Oklahoma, touting his administration's economic and trade record to energize GOP voters (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

The contrast is stark: one side deploys corporate cash to incentivize votes; the other relies on candidate-driven rallies and message discipline. Colorado conservatives should note how national corporations increasingly use their platforms to advance left-wing electoral goals—a reminder that voting with your wallet matters just as much as voting at the ballot box.

Watch whether Colorado's business community follows Patagonia's playbook in 2024.
