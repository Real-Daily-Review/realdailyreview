# Colorado Braces for Dangerous Heat Wave as Temperatures Soar Past 105 Degrees

Another brutal heat wave is bearing down on Colorado this weekend, with temperatures expected to exceed 105 degrees across the Front Range, Southern Colorado, and the Western Slope through at least Tuesday, according to CPR News and KDVR Fox31 Denver.

The National Weather Service has issued Heat Advisories for metro Denver and surrounding communities, with both Saturday and Sunday designated as Pinpoint Weather Alert Days due to the intensity of the conditions. Residents across affected regions should prepare for dangerous heat that will strain power grids, stress livestock operations, and pose serious health risks—particularly for elderly Coloradans and those without adequate air conditioning.

For Colorado's energy sector and agriculture, extreme heat events like this underscore the critical importance of reliable baseload power generation. Conservatives argue that Colorado's aggressive push toward renewable-only electricity mandates leaves the state vulnerable to grid failures during peak demand periods when solar and wind generation may be insufficient. Reliable natural gas and coal plants provide the dispatchable power necessary to keep air conditioning running when Coloradans need it most.

Ranchers and farmers should monitor livestock closely and ensure adequate water supplies. Outdoor workers must take precautions against heat exhaustion.

Watch for whether Colorado's grid operator can reliably manage demand without rolling blackouts—and whether state policymakers will acknowledge the grid resilience case for maintaining diverse energy sources.
