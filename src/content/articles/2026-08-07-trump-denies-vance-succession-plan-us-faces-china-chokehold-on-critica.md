# Trump Downplays Succession Talk as GOP Eyes 2028

Trump is pushing back hard against reports he's already anointed JD Vance as his MAGA successor, dismissing a Washington Post story citing anonymous sources claiming the president privately encouraged donors to back Vance's future presidential ambitions. Trump's denial underscores the ongoing tension within Republican circles over who will lead the movement beyond his tenure—a question that matters deeply to Colorado conservatives watching the party's direction on energy, border security, and economic policy.

Meanwhile, national security hawks are sounding alarms over America's dangerous dependence on China for critical minerals refining. The Washington Examiner reports that U.S. industrial capacity to transform rare earth elements into usable materials remains a chokepoint, threatening everything from military vehicles to data centers. For Colorado—home to significant mineral deposits and energy infrastructure—this dependency represents both a vulnerability and an opportunity: domestic refining capacity could anchor good-paying jobs and energy independence while reducing reliance on Beijing.

Republicans argue that rebuilding American mineral refining capacity requires streamlining environmental regulations and fast-tracking domestic projects. Democrats typically counter that such expansion risks environmental damage.

**Watch:** Whether Trump's 2028 succession strategy clarifies and how aggressively his administration moves to reclaim critical minerals supply chains from Chinese control.
