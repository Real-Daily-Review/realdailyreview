# Poudre School District Eyes Closing Nine Schools as Colorado Enrollment Craters

Colorado's Poudre School District is doubling down on school closures to plug a budget hole created by plummeting enrollment—the second major consolidation push in recent years. The district's Comprehensive Planning Committee has recommended shuttering nine schools, according to reporting from CPR News and KDVR Fox31 Denver.

This is a predictable outcome of Colorado's one-size-fits-all education funding model, which ties school budgets to student headcount rather than rewarding districts that operate efficiently. As families flee urban districts for charter schools, homeschooling, or relocation, traditional public school systems face a reckoning: either cut overhead or watch per-pupil spending explode.

**The conservative argument:** School closures are a symptom, not a cure. Colorado taxpayers have poured billions into K-12 education while enrollment has declined and test scores have stagnated. Rather than consolidating buildings, the state should empower parents with education choice—expanded charter access, education savings accounts, and school choice scholarships—so funding follows students to schools that actually deliver results.

Democrats and education unions typically resist consolidation as painful but necessary, while opposing the market-based reforms that would make closures unnecessary in the first place.

**Watch:** Whether the Poudre board approves the closures and whether Colorado lawmakers consider education choice legislation in the 2027 session.
