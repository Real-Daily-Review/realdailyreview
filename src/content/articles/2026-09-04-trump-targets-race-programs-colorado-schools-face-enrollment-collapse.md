# Trump Administration Targets Race-Based School Programs; Colorado Districts Face Enrollment Crisis

The Trump administration is moving to strip tax-exempt status from private schools that operate race-based programs—a direct challenge to diversity initiatives that have proliferated in American education over the past decade.

Meanwhile, Colorado school districts are grappling with a different crisis: enrollment collapse. Adams 12, serving the Denver metro area, is proposing to close six schools as student populations shrink (9News Denver). The district joins others statewide confronting the hard math of fewer families, fewer kids, and mounting budget pressure.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that race-based school programs represent illegal discrimination masquerading as equity. Stripping tax breaks from schools using such initiatives enforces equal treatment under law—a principle Colorado taxpayers should support. The administration contends these programs violate civil rights statutes and waste resources on identity politics rather than academic excellence.

**The Colorado angle:** As enrollment drops across the state, districts face painful choices between closures and tax hikes. Families are voting with their feet, moving out or choosing alternatives. Colorado policymakers should ask whether bloated administrative overhead and ideological programming—not just demographics—are driving enrollment losses.

Watch for whether Colorado school boards resist the federal tax-exempt rules or comply, and whether enrollment trends accelerate as families seek schools focused on merit and academics over race-conscious policies.
