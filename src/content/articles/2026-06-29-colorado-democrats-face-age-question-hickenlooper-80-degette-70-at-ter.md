# Colorado Democrats Face Age Question as Hickenlooper, DeGette Eye Another Term

Colorado Democrats are banking on incumbency, but voters are asking whether 80-year-old Sen. John Hickenlooper and 70-year-old Rep. Diana DeGette should seek another term—or step aside for younger talent.

The *Denver Post* editorial board noted that both would be octogenarians and septuagenarians respectively by the end of another term, raising legitimate questions about stamina and long-term vision. "There are many talented younger candidates on the Democrats' primary ballot," the board wrote, suggesting voters consider generational change.

Republicans see an opening. Aging incumbents often struggle with energy and constituent responsiveness—especially when they've held power for decades. Colorado conservatives argue that term limits and fresh leadership would better serve the state's interests, particularly on fiscal restraint and regulatory reform where Hickenlooper's record as governor drew criticism from the right.

Democrats counter that experience matters and that both lawmakers have delivered for their districts. The party will likely rally behind incumbency unless primary challengers gain real traction.

The real test comes in the primary. If Colorado Democrats nominate younger progressives, they signal renewal; if they stick with aging icons, they risk appearing out of touch in a state trending younger and more suburban.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic primary becomes a generational battle—or a coronation.
