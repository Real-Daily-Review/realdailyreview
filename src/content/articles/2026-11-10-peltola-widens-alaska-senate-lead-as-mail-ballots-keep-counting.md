# Alaska Senate Race Tightens as Mail Ballots Reshape Western Politics

Democrat Mary Peltola's narrow lead over Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan in Alaska's U.S. Senate race is widening—a troubling sign for Republicans hoping to solidify Senate control heading into 2026.

According to Fox News, Peltola's advantage grew to 2,517 votes after Monday's ballot drop, though tens of thousands of absentee ballots remain uncounted. The race underscores a persistent Republican vulnerability: mail-in voting patterns that favor Democrats in Western states, even in traditionally conservative Alaska.

Republicans argue that extended mail-ballot counting windows create uncertainty and opportunity for procedural challenges. They point to Alaska's shift leftward in recent cycles—Peltola flipped the seat in 2022—as evidence that GOP messaging and turnout operations need sharpening in the Last Frontier.

Democrats counter that mail voting expands access and that Peltola's performance reflects voter preference, not process failure.

For Colorado conservatives, the Alaska race matters: it signals whether Republicans can hold Senate seats in purple Western states where mail voting dominates. Colorado itself relies heavily on mail ballots, making ballot-counting efficiency and election integrity central to GOP strategy in 2026.

Watch whether Sullivan's campaign challenges ballot validity or whether Republican turnout operations adapt to mail-heavy electorates before the next cycle.
