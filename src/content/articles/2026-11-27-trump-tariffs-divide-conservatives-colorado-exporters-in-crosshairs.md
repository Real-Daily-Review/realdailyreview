# Trump's Tariff Gamble Divides Right; Colorado Manufacturers Watch Closely

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire from within conservative ranks, even as his administration doubles down on trade protectionism—a move that could squeeze Colorado manufacturers and agricultural exporters already navigating volatile markets.

The "Liberation Day" tariffs, announced in April 2025, sparked immediate backlash. *Washington Examiner* opinion writers argue Trump's approach mirrors failed policies, noting his approval ratings slid when the tariff rollout proved "chaotic" with "absurd" justifications. Critics worry the strategy risks economic self-harm rather than leverage against rivals.

Yet Trump's team frames tariffs as essential to reshoring American manufacturing and countering unfair trade practices—a position that resonates with some conservatives who prioritize domestic production over free-market orthodoxy.

**The Colorado angle:** Tariffs threaten Colorado's export-dependent sectors. Agricultural producers selling internationally face retaliatory tariffs on beef, wheat, and specialty crops. Tech and manufacturing firms relying on imported components face higher input costs, potentially stalling hiring and investment across the Front Range.

*National Review* suggests Trump study Reagan's Cold War playbook—building strength before negotiating down—implying a more measured approach to tariff deployment.

Coloradans should monitor whether tariff exemptions materialize for key state industries, and whether trade negotiations produce actual market access or merely entrench protectionism.
