# Mudslides Close I-70 in Western Colorado; Infrastructure Vulnerability Exposed

Flash flooding and mudslides have shut down Interstate 70 between exits 97 and 105 near Silt and New Castle in Garfield County, disrupting travel and commerce across western Colorado (Colorado Sun, 9News Denver). The Garfield County Sheriff's Office has urged caution as debris and mud block the critical corridor.

I-70 is Colorado's economic lifeline—connecting Denver to the Western Slope's energy sector, agriculture, and tourism. Closures like this expose the state's aging infrastructure and the real costs of deferred maintenance. While Democrats in Denver push billions into transit schemes and green mandates, critical highway infrastructure remains vulnerable to weather events that could have been mitigated with proper upkeep and drainage systems.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that Colorado's transportation budget should prioritize maintaining existing roads and bridges over experimental projects. The state's ability to move goods, workers, and emergency services depends on I-70 staying open—not on rail fantasies or EV charging stations.

The closure also threatens supply chains for Colorado's oil and gas operations, agriculture exports, and small businesses dependent on reliable access to markets.
