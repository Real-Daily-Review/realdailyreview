# Supreme Court Ruling Unleashes Wave of Partisan Redistricting

The Supreme Court has cleared the way for states to redraw congressional districts along partisan lines, ruling that the Voting Rights Act prohibits racial gerrymandering but permits political gerrymandering. The 6-3 decision struck down Louisiana's addition of a second majority-Black congressional district, declaring it an unconstitutional racial gerrymander.

The ruling has triggered a redistricting sprint across Southern states. Tennessee Republicans approved a new map Thursday that threatens to unseat the state's lone House Democrat, according to The Hill. Other red states are advancing similar efforts to bolster GOP prospects ahead of the fall elections.

**Critics argue** the decision undermines voting rights protections. Democratic Sen. Cory Booker said the court sent the country "backwards in time" by restricting tools used to ensure minority representation, according to The Hill.

**Supporters of the ruling** contend that the Voting Rights Act's text does not permit race-based redistricting, even when intended to increase minority representation.

The decision has reignited what observers describe as a "redistricting arms race," with states moving quickly to reshape electoral maps under the new legal framework. Watch for additional state-level redistricting efforts and potential legal challenges to newly drawn maps before the 2024 elections.
