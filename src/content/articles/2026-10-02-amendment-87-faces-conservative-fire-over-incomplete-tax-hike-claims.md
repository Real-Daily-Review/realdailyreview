# Colorado's Amendment 87 Faces Conservative Skepticism Over Tax Hike Claims

Colorado Democrats are pushing Amendment 87 with what conservatives say are misleading arguments about its true fiscal impact. According to *Complete Colorado*, the amendment is "being buttressed by incomplete arguments in the media and by the legislature," suggesting proponents aren't being straight with voters about what the measure actually costs.

The amendment has drawn scrutiny from taxpayer advocates who argue the state and its allied media outlets are obscuring the real burden on Colorado families and businesses. Republicans contend that Democrats routinely frame tax increases as "investments" or "revenue measures" rather than what they are: money taken from paychecks and profits.

Meanwhile, Colorado's election integrity continues to draw attention. The *Denver Post* reported that three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024 before detection, though the outlet notes this reflects Colorado's "many checks and balances." Amendment 84, which would require Social Security numbers for mail-in voting, could tighten verification further—a proposal conservatives support as common-sense election security.

The broader pattern: Democrats advance spending measures with incomplete disclosure, while Republicans push for tighter election rules. Colorado taxpayers should watch whether Amendment 87's actual cost gets disclosed before November ballots are cast.
