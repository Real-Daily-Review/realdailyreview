# Trump's Press Ban Draws Fire From Right and Left—But for Different Reasons

Donald Trump's restrictions on press access are backfiring, drawing criticism from both conservative and progressive outlets—though for starkly different reasons.

The Colorado Sun's Mike Littwin frames the ban as a "disaster" comparable to Trump's foreign policy missteps, echoing the left's standard complaint that limiting press access threatens democratic norms. That's the progressive playbook: equate any pushback against hostile media with authoritarianism.

But here's where it gets interesting: *National Review*, the flagship conservative publication, also opposes the ban—not on democratic principle grounds, but on pragmatic ones. The magazine argues Trump "would be better served by confronting reporters directly" rather than restricting access, suggesting the ban is tactically weak and undermines his ability to control the narrative.

**The conservative case is clearer than the left's hand-wringing:** Trump's restrictions look defensive and invite accusations of thin-skinned governance. Conservatives who champion free speech and limited government can't credibly argue for government-imposed press blackouts, even against hostile media. Direct confrontation—Trump's historical strength—works better than avoidance.

For Colorado, this matters: state Republicans are watching how Trump handles media relations as they navigate their own press strategy in a purple state where earned media skepticism runs deep.

Watch whether Trump reverses course or doubles down on access restrictions ahead of 2026.
