# Fed Rate Hike Defies Trump, Squeezes Colorado Borrowers

The Federal Reserve just raised interest rates a quarter-point to 3.75%-4.0%—its first hike in over three years—directly contradicting President Trump's push for lower borrowing costs (The Daily Wire).

The move will sting Colorado homebuyers and small-business owners already navigating a tight lending environment. Credit-card balances and new loans will cost more (Daily Caller), while savers may finally see better yields on savings accounts and CDs (9News Denver). For working Coloradans carrying debt, this is bad timing: higher mortgage rates mean pricier home purchases in an already expensive Front Range market, and small-business borrowing costs climb just as entrepreneurs try to expand.

Republicans argue the Fed's independence shouldn't mean ignoring economic reality. Trump wanted rate cuts to ease borrowing pressure; instead, the central bank doubled down on inflation-fighting measures that slow growth. Conservatives contend the Fed's rate-hiking cycle—driven by persistent inflation that government spending helped fuel—punishes savers and borrowers alike while the damage is already baked in.

Democrats and Fed officials counter that higher rates are necessary to combat lingering inflation and protect long-term price stability.
