# Colorado Republicans Eye 2028 as National GOP Regroups After Midterm Losses

Republicans are already looking past this cycle's defeats to position themselves for 2028, even as Democrats claim momentum from recent electoral gains. The midterm results—described by the Washington Examiner as a "sheer" Republican defeat—have scrambled the presidential field on both sides, with National Review questioning whether figures like Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez can overcome structural headwinds within the Democratic Party.

For Colorado conservatives, the immediate question is which Republican candidate will appeal to the state's swing voters while holding the party's base. Colorado has trended purple in recent cycles, making it a bellwether for national GOP strategy heading into the next presidential race.

Republicans argue the party needs to refocus on kitchen-table issues—inflation, border security, and government overreach—rather than getting pulled into circular primary battles. Conservatives also warn against allowing the left to define the 2028 narrative around abortion and democracy, issues Democrats weaponized effectively in the midterms.

The early jockeying suggests a wide-open field on the Republican side, with no clear frontrunner emerging yet. Expect Colorado party operatives to play kingmaker in the primary season ahead.
