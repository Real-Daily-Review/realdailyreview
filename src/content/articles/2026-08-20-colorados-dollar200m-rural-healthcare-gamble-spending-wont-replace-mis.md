# Colorado's $200M Rural Healthcare Bet: More Money Won't Fix Shortage of Doctors and Nurses

Colorado is about to spend $200 million over five years on rural healthcare—but throwing money at the problem won't solve the real crisis: a shortage of actual clinicians willing to work in underserved areas.

State officials are rolling out the Rural Health Transformation Program, according to the *Colorado Sun*, with relief for overworked rural doctors and nurses positioned as a near-term priority alongside longer-term infrastructure projects. That's the right instinct. Rural Colorado communities face genuine access problems, and burnout is driving experienced providers out of practice.

But conservatives should ask the hard question: Why does government spending precede market-driven solutions? Rural healthcare shortages stem partly from regulatory barriers—licensing reciprocity across state lines, restrictive scope-of-practice laws, and insurance reimbursement rules that make rural clinics uneconomical. Before Colorado commits a quarter-billion dollars in taxpayer money, legislators should eliminate the red tape preventing private providers and telehealth from filling gaps naturally.

The *Denver Post* also covered antisemitism concerns in Colorado politics, highlighting how public debate often misses the lived experiences of affected communities.

Watch whether Colorado's rural healthcare dollars actually reach frontline clinicians or get absorbed by administrative overhead and consulting contracts.
