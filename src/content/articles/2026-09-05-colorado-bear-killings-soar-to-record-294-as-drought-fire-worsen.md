# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push starving wildlife into residential areas across the state.

The surge reflects a hard reality: when government land management fails to prevent catastrophic wildfires and water scarcity intensifies, Coloradans and wildlife both pay the price. Bears seeking food in garbage cans and yards become public safety hazards, leaving CPW with few options beyond lethal removal (Colorado Sun).

Conservatives argue that decades of federal fire suppression policies, combined with mismanagement of national forests and water resources, have created tinderbox conditions across Colorado. The result: ecosystem collapse that forces painful choices—dead bears instead of healthy forests and adequate water supplies. Environmentalists' opposition to timber harvesting, controlled burns, and responsible water development has left the state vulnerable to exactly this scenario.

CPW officials cite human-wildlife conflict as the primary driver, but the root cause traces to land and water policy failures that progressive environmental groups have actively blocked. Until Colorado addresses forest health and water management head-on, expect more dead bears and more angry residents.

Watch whether state lawmakers will finally prioritize forest management and water infrastructure over environmental litigation and regulatory delay.
