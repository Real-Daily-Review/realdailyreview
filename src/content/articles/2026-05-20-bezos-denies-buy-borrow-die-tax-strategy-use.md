# Bezos Denies Using 'Buy, Borrow, Die' Tax Strategy

Jeff Bezos said Wednesday there is "no truth" to reports that he employs the "buy, borrow, die" tax strategy, a practice where wealthy individuals borrow against assets and use loan proceeds as income to avoid capital gains taxes, according to CNBC.

During a CNBC appearance, Bezos pivoted to broader tax concerns, asking "Why is a nurse in Queens who makes $75,000 a year paying more than $1,000 a month in taxes?" according to TechCrunch. He framed the comment as an argument for tax relief for middle-income earners.

The "buy, borrow, die" strategy has drawn scrutiny from policymakers and tax advocates as a mechanism wealthy individuals use to minimize tax liability. Bezos's denial comes as the strategy faces increased public and legislative attention.

His comments drew mixed reactions. While Bezos raised a legitimate point about middle-class tax burdens, critics noted the irony of a billionaire—whose net worth derives largely from unrealized stock gains—advocating for tax changes while denying use of sophisticated wealth-preservation tactics.
