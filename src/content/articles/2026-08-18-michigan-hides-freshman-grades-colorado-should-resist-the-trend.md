# Michigan's Grade-Hiding Scheme Shows Why Higher Ed Standards Are Collapsing

University of Michigan just announced it will hide first-semester grades for incoming freshmen in its Literature, Science and Arts programs, issuing only "pass" or "no pass" marks instead of letter grades. The policy epitomizes the "everyone gets a trophy" mentality rotting American higher education, according to critics at the *Washington Examiner*.

The move strips away meaningful feedback that students—and employers—need to assess actual academic performance. By obscuring freshman grades, Michigan removes accountability for both students and instructors, making it harder for employers to distinguish capable graduates from those coasting through on participation points.

**Why Colorado should care:** Colorado universities increasingly mimic elite institutions' progressive policies. If CU Boulder, Colorado State, or other state schools follow Michigan's lead, Colorado employers and taxpayers funding higher ed will lose transparency into whether their tuition dollars produce graduates with genuine skills and knowledge.

Republicans and conservatives argue that grade inflation and hidden performance metrics undermine meritocracy, weaken workforce readiness, and betray students who deserve honest assessment. The left frames such policies as reducing "anxiety" and promoting "equity," but critics say they simply lower standards across the board.

Watch whether Colorado's Board of Education or university regents consider similar grade-masking policies—and whether business leaders and taxpayers push back.
