# Diesel Prices Spike to Record Highs, Squeezing Colorado Truckers and Consumers

Diesel prices have hit all-time highs, with the national average approaching $6.50 per gallon—up nearly 73% year-over-year, according to AAA data cited by *The Daily Wire*. For Colorado's trucking, agriculture, and freight industries, the surge means sharply higher transportation costs that will ripple through supply chains and consumer prices at the pump and grocery store.

Diesel fuels the backbone of American logistics. When prices spike this dramatically, every Colorado business relying on freight—from ranchers shipping cattle to retailers stocking shelves—faces margin pressure. Small trucking operations and owner-operators are particularly vulnerable to fuel-cost shocks that eat into already-thin profits.

*9News Denver* notes the broader economic pain: higher diesel costs translate directly into elevated prices for everyday goods and services across the state.

Republicans and conservatives have pointed to energy policy uncertainty and regulatory constraints on domestic production as contributing factors to sustained fuel-price volatility. Democrats argue global market forces and geopolitical disruption drive prices, though they've resisted expanding domestic drilling and refining capacity.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses should watch whether state and federal policymakers move to ease permitting for energy development or whether they double down on restrictions that limit supply.
