# Trump Blasts Fed Rate Hike, Signals Warsh Has His Backing

President Trump attacked the Federal Reserve's first interest rate increase in over three years as economically misguided—but notably spared newly appointed Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh from criticism, signaling confidence in his hand-picked successor.

Speaking to reporters in North Carolina, Trump called the Fed "hostile" and argued that U.S. interest rates should be "1%, or less, because we are the best credit in the world—by far," according to the *Washington Examiner*. The Fed voted to raise its benchmark rate by a quarter-point on Wednesday, a move Trump views as counterproductive to economic growth.

The distinction matters: Trump has been openly critical of the Federal Reserve's independence and monetary policy decisions, but his restraint toward Warsh—whom he tapped to lead the central bank—suggests the president expects his appointee to align with administration priorities for lower rates and easier credit conditions.

For Colorado's economy, lower interest rates could ease borrowing costs for businesses and consumers, though the Fed's independence means Trump's preferences don't automatically translate to policy. Energy companies, real estate developers, and small businesses across the state watch Fed decisions closely.

**Watch whether Warsh's Fed moves align with Trump's rate-cut expectations—or whether the chairman asserts institutional independence.**
