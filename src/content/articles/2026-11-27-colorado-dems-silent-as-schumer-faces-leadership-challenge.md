# Colorado Senators Silent on Schumer Leadership Fight as Democrats Ignore Midterm Mandate

Colorado's two Democratic senators are dodging questions about whether they'll back Chuck Schumer to remain Senate majority leader—a telling sign that even blue-state Democrats sense trouble ahead.

Schumer faces a challenge from Maryland's Chris Van Hollen in an internal party fight that reveals deeper fractures. The New York Post argues the race between Schumer and Van Hollen exposes Democrats' blindness to the midterms' central lesson: voters rejected their ideological direction. Both candidates, the outlet notes, function as "ideological weathervanes" rather than principled leaders willing to chart a different course.

Sens. Michael Bennet and John Hickenlooper have refused to publicly commit to either side, according to the Denver Post. Their silence is strategic—and telling. Colorado voters punished progressive overreach on spending, energy policy, and regulatory excess. By staying mum, Bennet and Hickenlooper avoid antagonizing either the party establishment or the moderate voters they'll need in 2026.

Republicans argue this leadership vacuum proves Democrats haven't learned that Americans want fiscal restraint, energy independence, and less government interference. The party's internal chaos over who leads them forward suggests no serious course correction is coming.

Watch whether Colorado's senators eventually endorse a candidate—and whether that choice signals any meaningful pivot away from the progressive agenda that cost Democrats ground nationwide.
