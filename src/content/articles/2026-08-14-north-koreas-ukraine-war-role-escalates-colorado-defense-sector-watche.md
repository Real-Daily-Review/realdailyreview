# North Korea's Escalation in Ukraine Should Alarm Colorado Defense Contractors

North Korea is no longer a distant threat—it's actively fighting alongside Russia in Ukraine, and that should worry Colorado's aerospace and defense sector.

According to National Review, the DPRK has significantly deepened its military commitment to Russia's war effort, moving beyond rhetoric to sustained troop deployments and weapons support. The Washington Examiner warns that repeated North Korean provocations—including a missile launch on Aug. 6—are becoming routine enough that Western attention is fading, which is precisely Pyongyang's strategy.

**Why Colorado cares:** Defense contractors across the state, from Lockheed Martin to smaller suppliers, depend on U.S. military readiness and spending. A destabilized Europe and emboldened North Korea-Russia axis could reshape defense budgets and procurement priorities—potentially boosting Colorado's defense economy, or leaving it vulnerable if policymakers lose focus.

Republicans and defense hawks argue that sustained U.S. commitment to Ukraine and robust deterrence in the Pacific are essential to preventing further escalation. The Biden-Harris administration has supported Ukraine aid, though critics contend it's been inconsistent and reactive rather than strategically forward-thinking.

**Watch next:** Whether the Trump administration—or whoever wins in 2024—commits to a coherent strategy against the Russia-North Korea alliance, and how that shapes defense spending that flows to Colorado manufacturers.
