# Colorado's Foster Care Adoption Crisis Demands Real Solutions, Not Just More Spending

Colorado's foster care system is failing vulnerable children at an alarming rate, with broken adoptions persisting despite years of state intervention—a stark reminder that throwing taxpayer dollars at bureaucracies doesn't solve systemic problems.

According to a Colorado Sun analysis of state data, roughly 10% of adoptions in Colorado's foster care system fail, and that rate isn't improving. The Sun reports that programs designed to support these placements have actually been cut, leaving families and children in limbo.

The Left's typical response: demand more state funding for county departments. But Complete Colorado argues that simply increasing government spending without reforming how those dollars are deployed won't help foster kids build stable futures. The real issue, conservatives contend, is whether existing resources are being managed effectively and whether the state's bureaucratic approach is actually serving children or just perpetuating dependency on government systems.

Colorado taxpayers deserve answers: Why are adoption support programs being slashed while the state continues expanding other social spending? Are county departments accountable for outcomes, or just budget increases?
