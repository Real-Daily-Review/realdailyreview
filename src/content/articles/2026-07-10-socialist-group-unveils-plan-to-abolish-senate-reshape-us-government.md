# Radical Left Pushes Plan to Scrap Senate, Reshape U.S. Government

The Democratic Socialists of America are moving beyond fringe rhetoric to concrete constitutional overhaul—proposing to abolish the U.S. Senate and replace the presidency with a congressionally appointed executive, according to Fox News.

The DSA platform represents the logical endpoint of progressive governance: eliminate constitutional checks, concentrate power in a single legislative body, and dismantle the federalism that protects Colorado's sovereignty over water, energy, and land-use decisions. A Congress-appointed executive would answer to partisan majorities, not voters—a blueprint for unchecked spending and regulatory expansion that would devastate Colorado's energy sector and ranching communities.

Washington Examiner columnist Joe Concha warned that DSA-backed candidates are winning Democratic primaries nationwide, signaling the movement's growing influence within the party. "They want to tear the whole thing down," Concha said on Fox &amp; Friends, describing the socialist agenda as fundamentally transformative rather than reformist.

Republicans argue the DSA platform exposes the true ambitions of the progressive wing: dismantling constitutional limits on government power. Democrats have largely distanced themselves from the proposal, though the fact that DSA candidates are advancing through primary elections raises questions about the party's ideological center of gravity.

Coloradans should monitor whether Democratic candidates for statewide office distance themselves from or embrace DSA-style constitutional restructuring.
