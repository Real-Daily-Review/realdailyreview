# Trump Claims Election Vulnerabilities Without New Evidence; Democrats Dismiss Noncitizen Voting Concerns

President Trump raised alarm about voting system vulnerabilities in a primetime address but offered no new evidence of actual fraudulent votes, according to CPR News. The President claimed the country's election systems are susceptible to being "rigged and stolen," though specifics remained absent from his remarks.

Democrats quickly pushed back. Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) told CNN's *State of the Union* there was "no evidence" supporting the Trump administration's allegations that noncitizens voted in 2020, citing his experience as Pennsylvania's lieutenant governor during that election (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that election integrity concerns—whether about noncitizen participation, mail-in ballot vulnerabilities, or voter roll maintenance—deserve serious investigation and tighter verification protocols. Conservatives say Democrats' reflexive dismissal of these questions without rigorous audit undermines public confidence in elections.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's mail-in voting system and voter registration practices have drawn scrutiny from election-integrity advocates. State officials should clarify verification procedures and noncitizen screening mechanisms to address taxpayer concerns about ballot security.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado election officials will conduct transparent audits of voter rolls and noncitizen registration data—or continue deflecting accountability questions.
