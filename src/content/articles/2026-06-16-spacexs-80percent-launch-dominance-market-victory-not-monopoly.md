# SpaceX's Orbital Dominance Is Market Success, Not a Problem

SpaceX controls over 80% of global orbital payload launches—a commanding position that has triggered predictable calls for antitrust action from the left. But conservatives argue this dominance reflects exactly what free markets should produce: a company that innovated relentlessly, cut costs dramatically, and outcompeted rivals through superior execution.

In 2025 alone, SpaceX delivered more than 2,200 tons to orbit, accounting for over 80% of global orbital mass, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The company's own IPO prospectus confirms this lead has held since 2023, driven by high launch cadence and Starlink expansion.

The conservative case is straightforward: SpaceX didn't achieve this through government mandate or regulatory capture—it earned market share by being better and cheaper. There are no barriers preventing competitors from launching rockets. Blue Origin, Axiom Space, and others operate freely. If they can't match SpaceX's efficiency, that's a market signal, not evidence of monopoly abuse.

Progressives will inevitably demand regulatory intervention to "level the playing field." Conservatives should resist. Breaking up or constraining a company for winning is economic illiteracy dressed as populism.

Watch whether the Biden-Harris administration moves to weaponize antitrust law against SpaceX before leaving office—or whether the incoming Trump administration protects American space leadership from bureaucratic sabotage.
