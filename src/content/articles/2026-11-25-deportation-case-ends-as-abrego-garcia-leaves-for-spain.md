# Deportation Case Closes as Abrego Garcia Leaves US for Spain

A high-profile immigration case that became a flashpoint in Trump's deportation enforcement has ended with the subject voluntarily leaving the country. Kilmar Ábrego García, who spent 20 months fighting the Trump administration after being mistakenly deported to El Salvador, departed the US for Spain on Tuesday under a deal with the Department of Homeland Security, according to Fox News and Breitbart.

García's case drew national attention as a symbol of both immigration enforcement challenges and due-process concerns. After his initial mistaken deportation, he pursued legal remedies to return and remain in the US—a fight that consumed significant resources and media oxygen.

Republicans, including Rep. Markwayne Mullin, celebrated the voluntary departure (Fox News). Conservatives argue the case underscores the complexity of immigration enforcement and the importance of getting deportations right the first time—a message relevant to Colorado voters concerned about border security and rule of law.

The deal reportedly allows García to avoid forced deportation to Liberia and instead relocate to Spain, though it may affect his ability to return under future administrations (Breitbart).
