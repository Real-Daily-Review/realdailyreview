# Village People's Victor Willis Dead at 74; Trump Pays Tribute

Victor Willis, the iconic lead singer of the Village People best known for belting out "Y.M.C.A.," has died at 74. Willis, who performed as the group's leather-clad motorcycle cop and naval officer, helped define one of pop music's most recognizable anthems—a song that has become synonymous with President Trump's political rallies over the past decade.

Trump issued a statement mourning Willis's passing, noting that the singer "was a great and happy guy who loved that I used his group's song, YMCA, at my Rallies" (Washington Examiner). The president's embrace of the Village People's 1978 disco classic has made it a fixture at conservative political events, introducing the song to a new generation of supporters who may have missed its original run.

Willis's death marks the loss of a cultural figure whose music transcended generational and political divides—no small feat in today's polarized landscape. The Village People's campy, patriotic aesthetic and working-class imagery resonated across the political spectrum, though the group's later embrace by the right has given their legacy fresh relevance in conservative circles.

The music world and political figures across the spectrum are expected to continue paying respects in the coming days.
