# USS Abraham Lincoln Returns After Extended Deployment

The USS Abraham Lincoln pulled into San Diego after a grueling deployment that tested America's naval readiness in an increasingly unstable world. The carrier, with roughly 5,000 sailors and Marines aboard, departed last November for Pacific operations before being redirected to support Middle East missions—with its deployment extended in May, pushing the crew to near-record endurance levels.

The extended deployment underscores a hard reality conservatives have long warned about: the U.S. military is stretched thin managing global commitments with aging platforms and personnel fatigue. Republicans argue that sustained operations like this demand serious investment in shipbuilding, maintenance, and crew retention—not the budget games and readiness cuts that have plagued the Pentagon under divided government.

For Colorado, the implications matter. Defense contractors and suppliers across the state depend on robust naval procurement and sustainment budgets. Extended deployments also strain military families, many stationed at Colorado bases, who bear the cost of prolonged separations.

The crew's reunion with families is welcome news, but conservatives should watch whether Congress adequately funds the Navy's modernization and personnel needs—or whether political gridlock leaves our fleet overextended and vulnerable.
