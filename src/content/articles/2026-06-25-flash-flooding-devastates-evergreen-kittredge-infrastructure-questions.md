# Flash Flooding Ravages Evergreen, Kittredge—Questions Loom Over Watershed Management

A violent overnight rainstorm dumped six inches of rain in two hours across Jefferson County, turning Bear Creek into a destructive torrent that washed out bridges, triggered rockslides, and left homes and businesses damaged across Evergreen and Kittredge (Colorado Sun, 9News Denver). The Jefferson County Sheriff's Office reported debris-choked roads and multiple water rescues as the creek spilled its banks.

While extreme weather happens, conservatives should ask hard questions about Colorado's watershed and flood-control infrastructure. For decades, environmental groups have blocked or delayed critical water projects and dam maintenance in the name of "ecosystem protection"—leaving communities vulnerable when nature strikes. Meanwhile, state and local governments have approved dense development in flood-prone areas without adequate drainage planning.

The damage underscores why Colorado needs pragmatic, market-driven solutions: properly maintained water infrastructure, streamlined permitting for flood-mitigation projects, and land-use policies that respect both development and public safety. Instead, progressive regulators often prioritize environmental litigation over engineering.

Taxpayers should watch whether Jefferson County uses this disaster to finally push back against the environmental lobby and invest in real flood protection—or whether bureaucratic delays and green-group lawsuits once again prevent necessary infrastructure upgrades.
