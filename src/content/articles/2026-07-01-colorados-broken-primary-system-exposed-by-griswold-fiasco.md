# Colorado Election System Needs Overhaul After Griswold Debacle

Colorado's plurality-voting system allowed a deeply flawed Democratic candidate to win a crowded primary field despite broader voter preference for alternatives—a cautionary tale that demands electoral reform.

According to Complete Colorado, the Griswold fiasco exposes a fundamental weakness: in multi-candidate races, a nominee can prevail with only a small minority of total votes, even when voters collectively prefer someone else. This isn't a quirk—it's a design flaw that produces unrepresentative outcomes and weakens general-election viability.

Conservatives argue Colorado should consider ranked-choice voting or runoff systems that ensure nominees reflect genuine majority support. When a Democratic primary produces a nominee most Democrats didn't actually want, it signals the system itself is broken. The same vulnerability threatens Republican primaries too.

The broader concern: Colorado voters deserve election rules that produce candidates with genuine consensus backing, not just the loudest activist base or best name recognition in a crowded field. Weak nominees lose general elections and hand seats to the opposition.

Watch for whether Colorado legislative Republicans push electoral reform bills in the 2027 session—and whether Democrats, having just lived through this embarrassment, are willing to fix the system or defend the status quo.
