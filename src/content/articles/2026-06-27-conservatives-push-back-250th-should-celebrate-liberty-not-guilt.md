# America's 250th Should Celebrate Liberty, Not Self-Flagellation

As the nation approaches its 250th anniversary, conservatives are pushing back against what they see as a narrative of national guilt overshadowing the milestone.

National Review argues the commemoration should highlight America's genuine achievements—including often-overlooked international support during the Revolution, such as Spain's crucial backing of the Patriot cause. Rather than centering on oppression narratives, the outlet contends organizers should emphasize the founding principles of individual liberty and constitutional governance that built the world's freest nation.

The Washington Examiner's opinion section similarly frames the 250th as an opportunity to reflect on "founding principles while looking ahead," stressing "shared heritage, individual contributions, and the enduring" character of American institutions. The piece emphasizes faith and free expression as core to that legacy.

**The Colorado angle:** As progressive activists increasingly dominate public commemoration spaces—from school curricula to monument dedications—Colorado conservatives should ensure the state's bicentennial events celebrate the pioneers, ranchers, and entrepreneurs who built Colorado's prosperity, not guilt-ridden revisionism.
