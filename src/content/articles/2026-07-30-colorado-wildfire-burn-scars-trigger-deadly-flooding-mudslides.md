# Colorado Faces Cascading Disasters as Wildfire Burn Scars Trigger Flooding and Mudslides

Heavy rains have turned Colorado's recent wildfire burn scars into death traps, with emergency evacuations ordered in Ouray County as mudslides closed Red Mountain Pass and floodwaters swept through Pueblo County (Colorado Sun). The disaster has claimed at least four firefighter lives, including Nathan Matthews, whose procession is scheduled for Friday morning (9News Denver).

The flooding underscores a hard reality: Colorado's forest management crisis—years of fuel buildup, beetle-killed timber, and regulatory delays in thinning operations—leaves the state vulnerable to cascading natural disasters. When wildfires strip vegetation that normally absorbs rainfall, even moderate storms become catastrophic.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that aggressive forest thinning, controlled burns, and responsible timber harvesting reduce fuel loads and prevent exactly these scenarios. Yet environmental regulations and litigation often slow or block such preventive work. The Biden-Harris administration's push to restrict energy development and logging on federal lands—which comprise vast Colorado acreage—has further constrained forest management options.

Taxpayers will foot the bill for emergency response, evacuation, and recovery. The state should prioritize streamlined permitting for forest thinning and fuel reduction on public and private lands.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature and governor prioritize forest management reform or continue deferring to environmental groups that oppose active land stewardship.
