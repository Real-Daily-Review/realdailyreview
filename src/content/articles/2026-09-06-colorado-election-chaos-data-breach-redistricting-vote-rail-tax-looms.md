# Colorado's Election Security Stumble and Ballot Box Ahead

Colorado's Secretary of State's Office bungled voter privacy this month—exposing confidential voter information for over 130,000 Coloradans online for a week following a website update error, the *Denver Post* reports. The breach underscores why election administration demands competence, not just good intentions.

Meanwhile, voters face a November ballot packed with consequential measures. Initiative #256 would ban partisan congressional redistricting, a guardrail conservatives should scrutinize: while anti-gerrymandering language sounds appealing, the devil lies in implementation and whether it truly constrains both parties equally (*Complete Colorado*).

Front Range voters will also decide a "hefty" passenger rail tax hike for the CoCo commuter rail plan—despite Colorado's track record of commuter rail failures, notably RTD's ongoing struggles. Taxpayers should ask hard questions: Why throw more money at rail when existing systems hemorrhage cash? What's the actual ridership forecast, and who bears the cost?

Colorado's unaffiliated voter ranks continue rising, shifting primary power dynamics and potentially fragmenting both major parties' nominating processes (*Complete Colorado*).

**Watch:** Whether the Secretary of State's office implements real safeguards to prevent future data breaches—and whether voters reject another rail tax that smells like throwing good money after bad.
