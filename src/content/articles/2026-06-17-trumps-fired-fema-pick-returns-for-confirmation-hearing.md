# Trump's FEMA Pick Faces Confirmation After Earlier Firing

Cameron Hamilton, Trump's interim FEMA director who was fired last year, is back for a confirmation hearing—a move that undercuts the administration's earlier rhetoric about dismantling the agency entirely.

Hamilton previously told Congress he opposed eliminating FEMA, a position that aligned with the Trump administration's recent retreat from campaign promises to abolish the federal disaster-response bureaucracy. The backpedal reflects political reality: Colorado and other disaster-prone states rely heavily on FEMA funding and coordination, making wholesale elimination politically untenable.

Republicans are pushing Hamilton's confirmation as part of broader efforts to staff key national-security and intelligence positions. However, the confirmation process has hit turbulence. The Washington Examiner reports Trump abruptly "canceled" a confirmation hearing for Jay Clayton, his intelligence director nominee, in a dispute over unrelated Senate appointments—a move that signals internal friction over the confirmation calendar.

For Colorado taxpayers and disaster-prone communities, Hamilton's confirmation matters: FEMA decisions directly affect wildfire response, flood recovery, and federal disaster declarations that unlock state aid. Conservatives should scrutinize whether Hamilton will reform the agency's bloated bureaucracy or simply manage it as-is.

Watch whether Senate Republicans can actually move these confirmations through, or whether Trump's hardball tactics further delay filling critical national-security posts.
