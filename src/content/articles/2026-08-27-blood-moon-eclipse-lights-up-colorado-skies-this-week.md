# Colorado Gets Front-Row Seat to Rare Blood Moon Eclipse

Colorado skywatchers will have a rare treat this week: a near-total lunar eclipse turning the moon blood red—a celestial show that won't require government permission or a permit.

The eclipse peaks around 9:45 to 10 p.m., with approximately 95% of the moon covered by Earth's shadow (CPR News). The best viewing will be across northeastern Colorado—Fort Morgan, Sterling, Holyoke, and Julesburg have the clearest sightlines (9News Denver), though Front Range observers should also catch solid views (Colorado Sun). Farther south, conditions are "hit or miss" depending on cloud cover.

No special equipment is required, though binoculars or a telescope will enhance the view. Unlike solar eclipses, lunar eclipses are safe to observe with the naked eye.

This is one of those rare moments when Coloradans can simply step outside and enjoy nature's spectacle without worrying about regulatory hurdles, environmental impact studies, or tax implications—a refreshing change of pace.

Watch for clear skies; cloud cover could spoil the show for southern Colorado observers.
