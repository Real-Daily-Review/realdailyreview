# Teens' Red Rocks Detour Triggers Costly Rescue Operation

Two teenagers who decided to hike back to Morrison after a Red Rocks concert ended up costing taxpayers a rescue operation when they got lost on the trail. West Metro Fire Rescue responded to the incident Wednesday, pulling the teens from the backcountry after what should have been a straightforward walk home turned into a search-and-rescue call.

The incident underscores a recurring problem in Colorado's outdoor recreation areas: poor planning and overconfidence leading to preventable emergency responses. While the teens were ultimately recovered safely, the resources deployed—firefighters, rescue personnel, and coordination—represent real costs borne by local taxpayers.

Red Rocks Amphitheatre sits in a scenic but rugged area west of Denver. Hikers unfamiliar with the terrain, especially in fading daylight, face genuine navigation hazards. The teens' decision to attempt the hike without proper preparation or navigation tools is a cautionary tale about personal responsibility in Colorado's backcountry.

West Metro Fire Rescue's Facebook post framed the rescue matter-of-factly: "It seemed like a great idea – until it was time to find their way back" (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The agency didn't publicly criticize the teens, but the implicit message is clear—preparation matters.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether local authorities increase public education campaigns about backcountry safety, or whether rescue costs begin driving policy discussions around user fees for high-traffic outdoor areas.
