# Florida Executes Serial Killer Suspect; Colorado Should Note Contrast in Justice System

Florida executed Daniel Owen Conahan Jr. on Thursday for a 1996 strangulation, marking the state's 15th execution this year—a stark reminder that some states still enforce capital punishment while Colorado abolished it in 2020.

Conahan was convicted of one murder but authorities believe he may be linked to at least six additional killings connected to the "Hog Trail Murders" series from the 1990s (9News Denver, New York Post). He was put to death after decades on death row.

The execution underscores a widening divide in American criminal justice. Florida, under Republican leadership, maintains and actively uses capital punishment for the most heinous crimes. Colorado's Democratic-controlled legislature eliminated the death penalty a decade ago, despite public support for it in serious cases. Conservatives argue that swift, certain punishment deters violent crime and provides justice for victims' families; progressives contend capital punishment is irreversible and prone to error.

For Colorado, the contrast raises questions about whether the state's approach to violent offenders adequately protects public safety and serves justice. While Colorado voters have consistently supported capital punishment in polls, lawmakers have rejected their will—a pattern taxpayers should watch as violent crime remains a concern in Denver and across the state.
