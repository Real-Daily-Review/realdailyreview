# Colorado Rejects Party Favoritism in Government—A Rare Win for Principle

Colorado is taking a stand against the creeping politicization of state government by rejecting special recognition or privileges for political parties, according to *Complete Colorado*. The move represents a rare moment of clarity: government should serve all Coloradans equally, not pick winners among partisan organizations.

This principle matters. When state agencies begin granting favors to one party over another—whether through funding, access, or official recognition—they corrupt the impartiality taxpayers deserve. Colorado's position aligns with conservative arguments that government should be neutral arbiter of law, not a tool for partisan advantage.

Meanwhile, Colorado's new state historian, Nicki Gonzales, is taking office with a more modest mission: encouraging Coloradans to study their history and learn from past mistakes, *CPR News* reports. Her one-year tenure will focus on making history accessible and relevant to residents.

The historian role is nonpartisan and appropriately focused on education rather than ideology—a refreshing contrast to how some states weaponize cultural institutions. If Gonzales sticks to teaching Colorado's actual history—mining booms, frontier settlement, constitutional principles—rather than imposing contemporary political narratives, the position could serve a legitimate public purpose.

**Watch whether state agencies continue respecting the principle of political neutrality, or whether future administrations quietly reverse course.**
