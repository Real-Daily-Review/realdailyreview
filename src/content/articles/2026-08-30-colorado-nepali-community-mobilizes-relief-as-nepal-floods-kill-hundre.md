# Colorado's Nepali Community Mobilizes as Catastrophic Floods Devastate Homeland

Colorado's Nepali community is racing to help loved ones after devastating floods swept through Nepal, leaving rescue crews searching for survivors. Members of Colorado's substantial Nepali population—drawn to the state partly by its mountain terrain—are now coordinating relief efforts through organizations like the Colorado Nepal Alliance (KDVR).

The disaster has hit close to home for many Coloradans with family ties to Nepal. According to CPR News, community members are anxiously waiting for word from relatives as rescue operations continue. The flooding represents a humanitarian crisis that has prompted local Nepali organizations to mobilize aid and support networks.

Unlike many disaster-response stories, this one carries no partisan policy angle—it's straightforward: Coloradans with family abroad need help, and community groups are stepping up. Private charitable organizations and mutual-aid networks, not government mandates, are leading the charge.

For Colorado conservatives, this is a reminder that civil society and voluntary community action—not government programs—often respond fastest to human need. Coloradans helping Coloradans help their families abroad is the free-market, freedom-based approach to compassion that works.

Watch whether state or federal officials attempt to insert themselves into relief coordination or use the crisis to justify new foreign-aid spending.
