# Washington's Geopolitical Blindness Leaves America Vulnerable to Adversary Land Grabs

Washington's defense establishment is asleep at the wheel while China, Russia, and other revisionist powers quietly buy up the critical infrastructure that controls global commerce, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Instead of obsessing over hypothetical high-tech naval wars in the Strait of Hormuz, American planners are ignoring the real threat: adversaries systematically acquiring ports, undersea cables, and maritime chokepoints that function as de facto territorial control.

The problem, conservatives argue, is bureaucratic stovepiping—defense and commerce operate in separate silos, leaving gaping vulnerabilities. While Washington dithers, hostile regimes are executing a long-game strategy to strangle American trade routes and leverage economic coercion without firing a shot.

For Colorado, this matters. Supply-chain disruptions ripple through our energy sector, agriculture, and manufacturing base. If adversaries control the infrastructure moving American goods and resources, Colorado businesses and workers pay the price through inflation, job losses, and reduced competitiveness.

Republicans and national security hawks say the Trump administration must abandon the fantasy of "rules-based order" and adopt transactional realism: treat critical infrastructure as a national security asset, not a free-market commodity open to foreign bidders.

Watch for whether the next administration actually blocks hostile foreign investment in American ports and undersea infrastructure—or merely talks tough while Beijing keeps buying.
