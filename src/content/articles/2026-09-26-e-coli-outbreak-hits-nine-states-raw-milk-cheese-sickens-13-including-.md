# Raw Milk Cheese Outbreak Spans Nine States, Raises Food Safety Questions

Colorado consumers should be alert: a multistate E. coli outbreak tied to raw milk cheese is spreading across nine states, with Sierra Nevada Cheese Co.'s "The Graziers" brand at the center of the contamination.

According to 9News Denver, 13 people have fallen ill, with more than half under age 5—a particularly vulnerable population. The New York Post reports that toddlers have been hospitalized as cases mount across the country.

Raw milk products have long been a flashpoint between food safety regulators and artisanal producers who argue unpasteurized dairy offers superior flavor and nutritional benefits. Conservatives generally favor consumer choice and minimal government interference in food production, but this outbreak underscores a real tension: when unregulated products sicken children, even free-market advocates acknowledge the case for basic safety standards.

The FDA and state health departments are investigating. Colorado residents who purchased The Graziers cheese should check their refrigerators immediately and contact their doctor if they experience E. coli symptoms (severe diarrhea, abdominal cramps, fever).

Watch whether this outbreak prompts calls for stricter raw milk regulations—and whether Colorado's raw milk community can demonstrate adequate safety protocols before politicians use this crisis to justify broader dairy industry restrictions.
