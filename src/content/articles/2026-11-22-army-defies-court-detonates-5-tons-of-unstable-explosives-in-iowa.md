# Army Moves Forward With Risky Explosive Detonation Despite Court Order

The U.S. Army is pressing ahead with a controlled detonation of nearly 5 tons of crystallized lead azide at an Iowa munitions plant by Tuesday, defying a federal court's temporary halt (Washington Examiner). The unstable explosive—stored in 68 drums at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown—has degraded over time, creating what the military views as an unmanageable safety hazard (9News Denver).

**The conservative concern:** This move raises questions about federal overreach and bureaucratic judgment. The Army is essentially overriding judicial process to solve a problem of its own making—decades of poor storage management. Conservatives argue the military should have maintained these materials properly or disposed of them safely years ago, rather than forcing an emergency detonation now.

**Colorado angle:** While the detonation occurs in Iowa, Colorado's defense and munitions infrastructure—including Fort Carson and weapons facilities—depends on similar Army management protocols. If the Pentagon can ignore court orders for explosive disposal, what does that mean for Colorado's military installations and the contractors who work there?

Republicans and fiscal hawks should scrutinize whether this reflects broader Pentagon mismanagement and waste of taxpayer resources.

Watch for whether Congress demands an investigation into how the Army allowed this hazard to accumulate in the first place.
