# Boulder Valley Schools Face Enrollment Crisis as District Plans Closures

Boulder Valley School District's board has approved closing four schools—a stark reminder that progressive education spending doesn't guarantee enrollment or results. The consolidation plan comes as the district grapples with shrinking student numbers and parent backlash, forcing taxpayers to confront the reality that throwing money at schools doesn't stop families from voting with their feet.

The closures reflect a broader trend: Colorado families are increasingly choosing alternatives—charter schools, homeschooling, and private education—over traditional public systems. When districts lose enrollment, they lose state funding, yet administrative overhead often remains bloated. Boulder Valley now faces the painful math of consolidation rather than the harder work of operational efficiency and academic accountability.

Parents opposing the closures argue the district should have managed resources better. Conservatives counter that the real problem is systemic: bloated bureaucracies, ideological curriculum choices (like the 9/11 guidance controversy now plaguing Fairfax County schools in Virginia), and a one-size-fits-all model that ignores parental choice.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether the district uses this crisis to genuinely streamline administration or simply passes pain to remaining schools while protecting central office jobs.
