# Florida Man's 34 Open Cans Highlight Why Tough DUI Enforcement Matters for Colorado Roads

A Florida man's arrest with 34 open White Claw cans in his vehicle—and a blood-alcohol content of 0.177, more than twice the legal limit—serves as a stark reminder of why Colorado needs uncompromising DUI enforcement and personal accountability on our roads.

The 33-year-old's staggering impairment level underscores a simple truth: some people will ignore the law and endanger everyone around them. Colorado has made progress on DUI prevention, but the state continues to see preventable deaths and injuries from drunk drivers who think the rules don't apply to them.

This isn't a case for more government programs or feel-good awareness campaigns. It's a case for swift, certain consequences—police doing their job, prosecutors pressing charges, and judges handing down sentences that stick. Coloradans deserve roads where law enforcement takes impaired driving seriously and doesn't treat repeat offenders or egregious cases with kid gloves.

The broader lesson: personal responsibility and enforcement of existing law work better than expanding the regulatory state. Colorado should focus resources on catching dangerous drivers, not creating new bureaucracies.
