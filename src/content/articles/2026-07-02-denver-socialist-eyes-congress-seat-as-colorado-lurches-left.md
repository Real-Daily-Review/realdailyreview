# Denver Socialist Candidate's Rise Signals Radical Shift in Colorado's Political Center

A 29-year-old Democratic Socialist could soon represent Denver in Congress, raising alarms among Colorado conservatives about the state's leftward drift.

Melat Kiros, profiled by CPR News, is gaining traction in a district that has grown increasingly progressive. The Democratic Socialists of America (DSA)—an organization advocating for government control of major industries and wealth redistribution—backs her candidacy. Republicans argue that DSA policies would devastate Colorado's energy sector, which employs thousands and generates crucial tax revenue, while driving up costs for working families already squeezed by inflation and housing prices.

Conservatives worry that a DSA representative would push for aggressive climate regulations, higher corporate taxes, and expanded government programs—all funded by Colorado taxpayers. The group's platform fundamentally opposes free-market capitalism and private enterprise, positions that clash sharply with Colorado's business community and energy workers.

Democrats counter that Kiros represents younger voters demanding action on climate change and economic inequality. They frame DSA policies as necessary corrections to corporate excess.

The race reflects a broader pattern: Colorado's urban centers are drifting further left while rural and suburban areas remain conservative strongholds. Taxpayers should watch whether Kiros advances to the general election and whether her radical platform energizes—or alienates—moderate Denver voters.
