# Colorado Democrats Poised to Gut Conservative Ballot Victories Through Regulatory Delay

Colorado voters approved three ballot measures backed by Protect Kids Colorado—but Governor-elect Phil Weiser is already signaling he'll slow-walk implementation, the *Denver Post* reports. Weiser said the measures targeting transgender policies and fentanyl penalties will need "a long, hard look" before taking effect, effectively threatening to undermine the will of voters through bureaucratic obstruction.

This comes as Colorado Democrats seized supermajorities in both chambers of the legislature, positioning them to rewrite or repeal conservative-backed measures through legislation—a pattern progressives have deployed repeatedly against ballot initiatives they oppose.

Conservatives argue the maneuver is textbook Democratic overreach: voters spoke clearly, yet unelected officials and partisan majorities now plan to neuter those decisions through delay tactics and legislative reversal. Republicans counter that Weiser's hesitation signals the left's contempt for direct democracy when results don't align with progressive ideology.

For Colorado taxpayers and families, this sets a dangerous precedent: ballot measures—the people's direct check on legislative power—become mere suggestions when Democrats control the executive and legislature. The state's supermajority can now rewrite voter-approved policy without facing meaningful opposition.

Watch whether Republicans mount legal challenges to any legislative gutting of these measures, or whether conservative groups organize recall efforts against legislators who vote to overturn them.
