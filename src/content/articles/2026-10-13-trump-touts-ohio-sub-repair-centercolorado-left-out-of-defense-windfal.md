# Trump Touts Submarine Repair Center in Ohio—But Colorado Should Demand Defense Investment Too

President Trump announced a new U.S. Submarine Component Repair Center in Cleveland, Ohio, as part of what *Just The News* calls his "job-creation juggernaut" ahead of Election Day. The move underscores Trump's strategy of highlighting defense manufacturing and federal investment in battleground states.

Here's what conservatives should note: while Ohio gets a major defense facility, Colorado—home to NORAD, Space Force installations, and a robust defense industrial base—remains underutilized in Trump's infrastructure rollout. Colorado's aerospace and defense sector, centered around Colorado Springs and the Front Range, has the talent, infrastructure, and security clearances to absorb significant submarine component work or related manufacturing.

Republicans argue that strategic defense investment should flow to regions with proven capacity and existing military infrastructure, not just swing states. The submarine repair center is smart policy—maintaining U.S. naval readiness matters—but the distribution of federal defense dollars shouldn't be purely electoral calculus.

Colorado taxpayers and business leaders should ask: where's our seat at the table? If Trump wins and continues prioritizing defense manufacturing, Colorado must compete aggressively for contracts that leverage our existing military-industrial ecosystem.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican delegation pushes Trump's team to direct comparable defense investment to the state before 2025.
