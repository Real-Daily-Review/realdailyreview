# Fed Holds Rates Steady Under New Chair Warsh; Inflation Remains Obstacle to Relief

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh kept interest rates unchanged at his first policy meeting, disappointing those hoping for relief from the high borrowing costs that have squeezed Colorado businesses and homebuyers for years.

The Fed's monetary policy committee dropped previous language suggesting rate cuts were coming, signaling a more cautious stance. According to *Just The News*, wholesale business inflation exceeded 6% in May, with overall consumer inflation above 4%—stubborn figures that constrain the central bank's ability to ease policy.

**The conservative case:** Republicans, including President Trump, have argued the Fed should lower rates to stimulate economic growth and reduce the debt-service burden on American households and businesses. Colorado's construction sector, small manufacturers, and real estate markets have all felt the pinch of elevated borrowing costs. Warsh's hold suggests inflation—not growth—remains the Fed's priority.

**The Fed's position:** Officials maintain that premature rate cuts risk reigniting price pressures, keeping monetary policy restrictive until inflation moves closer to their 2% target.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the takeaway is clear: mortgage rates, auto loans, and commercial credit will remain elevated. Watch whether inflation data improves enough in coming months to give Warsh room to cut—or whether rate pressure persists through 2025.
