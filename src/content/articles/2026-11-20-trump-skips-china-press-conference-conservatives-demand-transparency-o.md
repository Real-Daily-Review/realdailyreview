# Trump Skips Press Conference in China; Conservative Critics Say He Should Leverage Xi Meeting on Human Rights

President Trump concluded his China trip without holding a formal press conference—a break from typical presidential protocol that drew scrutiny from both the media and conservative commentators. While Trump met with Xi Jinping and reportedly Putin, he declined to take questions from reporters, leaving key details of the discussions unclear.

National Review argues Trump missed an opportunity to publicly pressure Beijing on human rights, specifically calling on him to demand the release of democracy activist Joshua Wong, who was sentenced for meeting with American lawmakers. Conservatives say the U.S. should use high-level diplomatic moments to advance freedom and hold authoritarian regimes accountable—not paper over disagreements for the sake of optics.

CPR News notes that formal press conferences are standard practice after presidential foreign trips, making Trump's absence noteworthy. The lack of transparency raises questions about what was negotiated on trade, Taiwan, or other issues affecting American interests and Colorado's export-dependent economy.

Republicans and conservatives should demand clarity on whether Trump's team secured commitments on fair trade practices, intellectual property protection, or strategic concessions—details voters and business leaders need to assess the trip's actual value.
