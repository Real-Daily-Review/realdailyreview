# China's Ballistic Missile Test Signals Need for Stronger U.S. Defense Posture

China's second major open-ocean ballistic missile test in less than two years—this week's launch from a nuclear-powered submarine into the South Pacific—should alarm every American concerned about national security and America's ability to defend its interests and allies in the Pacific.

The test, described by U.S. officials as "a great concern to the region and the world" (Washington Examiner), underscores a hard reality: the Chinese military is advancing its capabilities at a pace that demands a serious American response. While the Biden-Harris administration and congressional Democrats have prioritized green-energy spending and domestic social programs, defense budgets have stagnated relative to the threat.

Conservatives argue that a credible deterrent—one that convinces Beijing that military adventurism carries unacceptable costs—requires sustained investment in advanced naval capabilities, missile defense systems, and technological superiority. The alternative is a hollow military unable to back up American commitments to Taiwan, Japan, South Korea, and other allies who depend on U.S. strength.

For Colorado, this means supporting defense contractors and skilled manufacturing jobs that depend on robust Pentagon budgets—and resisting the progressive left's push to divert resources from defense to social experiments.

Watch whether Congress will prioritize genuine defense modernization or continue underfunding readiness in favor of progressive spending priorities.
