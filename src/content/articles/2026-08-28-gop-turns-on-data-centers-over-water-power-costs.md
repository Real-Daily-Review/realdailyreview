# Data Center Backlash Hits Red States—Colorado Should Pump the Brakes

Republican leaders across conservative strongholds are reversing course on data center development, citing legitimate concerns about water depletion and electricity costs that threaten rural communities and agricultural interests.

Once hailed as economic engines for AI infrastructure, data centers now face skepticism from GOP officials in Texas, Arizona, and beyond. The Daily Wire reports that Republican constituents are increasingly vocal about the hidden costs: strain on already-scarce water supplies in arid regions and upward pressure on power bills for families and farms.

National Review's analysis clarifies the real culprit: while data centers themselves use modest amounts of water, the *irrigation agriculture* supporting those same arid communities consumes the lion's share of available resources. The tension isn't new—it's a zero-sum fight over finite water in the West.

**The Colorado angle matters.** Colorado's Front Range faces similar pressures. Any expansion of data center corridors here would compete directly with agricultural water rights, municipal supplies, and energy grids already stressed by population growth. Conservatives rightly worry that federal cheerleading for AI infrastructure could override local water management and property rights.

Republicans argue that economic development shouldn't come at the cost of rural livelihoods or energy affordability for working families.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's GOP legislature and local officials will demand binding water and energy impact assessments before approving new data center projects.
