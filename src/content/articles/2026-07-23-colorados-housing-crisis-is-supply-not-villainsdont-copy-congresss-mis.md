# Housing Scarcity, Not Villains, Drives Colorado's Affordability Crisis

Colorado's housing crunch won't be solved by scapegoating landlords or out-of-state buyers—it's a supply problem, and state lawmakers should resist copying Congress's failed interventions.

National Review argues that politicians across the country reflexively blame Wall Street, developers, and foreign investors for housing shortages, but the real culprit is zoning restrictions and regulatory barriers that prevent new construction. When supply can't meet demand, prices rise. It's Economics 101.

President Trump recently refused to sign a federal housing bill, citing unrelated provisions (the Daily Caller reports he objected to SAVE America Act language embedded in the measure). The veto underscores Republican skepticism toward top-down federal housing mandates.

Colorado faces acute affordability pressures in Denver, Boulder, and surrounding metros. Rather than pursuing price controls, rent caps, or developer taxes—progressive favorites that shrink supply further—state Republicans should push for streamlined permitting, reduced zoning restrictions, and lower regulatory costs on builders. The Washington Examiner warns against Congress picking winners and losers through spending (citing a proposed $1 billion Delphi pension bailout); the same logic applies to state housing policy.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats propose new housing taxes or regulations that further restrict development—and whether Republicans effectively counter with supply-side alternatives.**
