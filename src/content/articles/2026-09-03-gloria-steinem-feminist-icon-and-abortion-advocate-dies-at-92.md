# Gloria Steinem, Feminist Icon and Abortion Advocate, Dead at 92

Gloria Steinem, the pioneering feminist activist who shaped the women's liberation movement of the 1970s, has died at age 92, according to statements from her family and confirmed by multiple outlets (Fox News, 9News Denver). Steinem passed away Wednesday at her New York home surrounded by loved ones.

The co-founder of Ms. Magazine rose to prominence as a leading voice of second-wave feminism, becoming known for her distinctive blonde-streaked hair, trademark glasses, and fierce advocacy for abortion access. Just The News notes her role as a "fierce abortion advocate" throughout her decades-long career in progressive activism.

Steinem's legacy reflects the cultural battles that continue to divide America. Conservatives have long opposed the radical feminist agenda she championed—including unrestricted abortion access and expansive government programs framed as "women's issues"—arguing instead for traditional family structures, parental rights, and market-based solutions to economic challenges. Republicans contend that progressive feminism has undermined personal responsibility and family stability while expanding government dependency.

Her death marks the end of an era for the progressive left, which views her as a transformational figure in American social movements.
