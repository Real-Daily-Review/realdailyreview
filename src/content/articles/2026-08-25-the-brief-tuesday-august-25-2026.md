# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Tuesday, August 25, 2026

Colorado conservatives have plenty to watch today — from a fresh Second Amendment fight landing in court to a string of bear attacks reminding Front Range residents why wildlife management matters. Let's get into it.

---

## Vance Calls Out Canada's China-Friendly Trade Double Standard

Vice President JD Vance told Maine voters what a lot of American farmers and ranchers already know: Canada is playing favorites — treating Chinese goods more generously than U.S. farm products — and it's derailing trade negotiations. Vance called it "insanity," and he's right. Colorado's agricultural producers, who compete in the same export markets, have a direct stake in any North American trade framework that lets Ottawa play both sides. If Canada won't deal fairly, Washington should hold the line.

---

## Colorado Gun Rights Lawsuit Takes Aim at Carry Restrictions

A new legal complaint is challenging Colorado's state and local restrictions on carrying firearms, marking the latest in a growing string of Second Amendment lawsuits targeting the state's gun control regime. Republicans and gun rights advocates argue Colorado's patchwork of carry restrictions tramples on constitutional rights affirmed by the Supreme Court. Colorado gun owners should pay close attention — this case could set precedent that either rolls back years of legislative overreach from Denver Democrats or signals how far the courts are willing to let the left go.

---

## Thornton Youth Pastor Gets 13 Years to Life for Child Sex Assault

A former youth pastor in Thornton has been sentenced to 13 years to life in prison following his conviction on child sex assault charges. This is the justice system working as it should — protecting the most vulnerable and holding predators accountable with serious prison time. Colorado families deserve to know their communities are safe, and sentences like this one send the right message. No policy angle needed here: a predator is off the streets, and that's what matters.

---

## Four Bear Attacks in One Week — Colorado Parks and Wildlife Sounds Alarm

Colorado Parks and Wildlife is reporting four bear attacks in a single week, including one that sent a Jefferson County man to the hospital Friday. Bear activity is ramping up, and Front Range communities are feeling it. Conservatives have long argued that sound wildlife management — not feel-good policies driven by urban environmentalists — is what keeps people safe. Colorado's growing population, combined with land-use policies that push development into bear habitat without adequate management tools, is making this problem worse, not better.

## U.S. Removes Syria's State Sponsor of Terrorism Designation

Secretary of State Marco Rubio has formally notified Congress of the U.S. intention to remove Syria's state sponsor of terrorism designation, citing counterterrorism actions and positive changes under President Ahmed al-Sharaa's government. This is a significant geopolitical shift that the Trump administration is managing carefully — rewarding demonstrated behavioral change rather than making concessions up front. Conservatives will want to watch whether Syria's new government continues to deliver, or whether this designation removal becomes a diplomatic liability down the road.
