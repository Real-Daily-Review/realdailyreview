# Reflecting Pool Vandalism Exposes National Park Service Management Failures

Vandals deliberately damaged the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool's liner with a sharp knife or razor, forcing the National Park Service to drain the iconic monument after July Fourth for repairs—yet another embarrassment for an agency struggling to maintain America's most visited landmarks.

According to a police report cited by 9News Denver, the damage includes "caulk over the foam sealant that was cut with a sharp knife and destruction of delaminating material." The Washington Examiner reports the NPS will begin draining the pool immediately after Independence Day to assess and repair the liner.

The incident underscores a broader conservative concern: federal agencies tasked with protecting national treasures often lack the resources, accountability, or operational discipline to do the job effectively. The NPS manages over 400 sites with a sprawling bureaucracy that frequently prioritizes administrative overhead over maintenance and security.

**The Colorado angle:** While this damage occurred in Washington, D.C., Colorado's national parks and monuments—from Rocky Mountain to Mesa Verde—depend on the same NPS apparatus. Coloradans should ask whether their public lands are receiving adequate stewardship or whether federal mismanagement is the norm.

Watch for whether the NPS identifies and prosecutes the vandals, or whether this incident simply disappears into the agency's typical cycle of deferred maintenance and excuses.
