# Mega Millions Jackpot Climbs Past $800M as Colorado Lottery Players Chase Long Odds

Colorado lottery players are chasing an $800 million Mega Millions jackpot—the tenth-largest in history—after Friday's drawing produced no grand-prize winner, according to the New York Post. The jackpot has ballooned over a four-month winless streak, with 9News Denver reporting the prize had already hit $743 million by late July, making it the ninth-largest ever.

For Colorado taxpayers, lottery winnings carry a federal tax hit of roughly 37 percent, plus state income tax obligations. Winners should understand that the advertised jackpot is an annuity; the actual cash payout is substantially lower—a reality the lottery industry doesn't emphasize in marketing.

While lottery revenue does fund Colorado education and parks, conservatives rightly note that gambling is a regressive tax on lower-income Coloradans who spend disproportionately on tickets chasing unlikely odds. The state's cut is real money, but it comes at the cost of promoting a poor financial bet to citizens who can least afford it.
