# Trump's Iran Deal Faces GOP Skepticism Over Missing Details and Congressional Authority

President Trump's announced Iran agreement is drawing fire from Republicans who say the administration is hiding the ball—and bypassing Congress in the process.

Trump declared an Iran deal done, but GOP senators say they've been kept in the dark on specifics (Fox News). The core issues remain murky: sanctions relief, nuclear concessions, and the actual terms of any final agreement are reportedly unresolved, with officials suggesting major details will be hammered out later.

National Review calls for transparency, arguing the text must be released to cut through conflicting claims about what was actually agreed to. Former Vice President Mike Pence went further, telling CNN the deal "smacks of appeasement" (Breitbart).

Republicans are demanding a congressional vote to lock in any final agreement—a constitutional check Trump appears to be sidestepping. The Washington Examiner raises a deeper question: if the U.S. military strike on Iranian nuclear facilities in June 2025 succeeded, why negotiate at all?

For Colorado conservatives, the Iran question matters. A weak deal that leaves Tehran's nuclear ambitions intact risks broader Middle East instability, higher energy costs, and renewed military entanglement—all hitting Colorado's economy and defense-sector jobs.

Watch whether Trump releases the full text and whether Congress forces a vote before any final signature.
