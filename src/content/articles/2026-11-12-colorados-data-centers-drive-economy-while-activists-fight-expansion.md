# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Push Back

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are quietly becoming the backbone of American digital infrastructure, yet progressive activists continue to resist their expansion as an environmental threat.

According to *Complete Colorado*, these facilities aren't luxuries—they're essential to modern life, handling everything from cloud computing to AI workloads that power the economy nationwide. Data centers represent a genuine economic opportunity for Colorado: high-wage jobs, tax revenue, and energy-sector stability in an era of transition away from traditional power sources.

But here's the catch: environmental groups and progressive lawmakers have increasingly targeted data center development, citing water usage and energy demands. They frame expansion as incompatible with Colorado's climate goals, ignoring that modern data centers are far more efficient than legacy infrastructure and that relocating them to less-regulated states simply exports the problem.

Republicans and free-market advocates argue Colorado should embrace this competitive advantage. The state's geography, existing infrastructure, and workforce make it uniquely positioned to lead. Blocking growth here doesn't stop data centers—it just moves jobs and tax dollars to Texas, Arizona, or overseas.

The real question: Will Colorado's elected officials choose economic growth and energy independence, or will they let activist pressure kill a thriving industry?

Watch whether the state legislature attempts new restrictions on data center permitting or energy allocation in the 2027 session.
