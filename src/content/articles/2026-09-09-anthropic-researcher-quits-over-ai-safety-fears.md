# AI Researcher's Exodus Raises Questions About Lab Safety—And Colorado's Tech Future

A senior researcher at Anthropic, one of the nation's leading artificial intelligence firms, publicly quit this week over concerns that AI development is racing toward existential risk without adequate safeguards. Jacob Coxon, who spent three years in pre-training research at Anthropic and OpenAI, warned on social media that the industry is "gambling with our lives" and that current AI could "kill us all by the end of the decade" (Daily Caller, Daily Wire).

Coxon's departure underscores a growing fault line in Silicon Valley: while AI labs pursue ever-larger models and capabilities, some researchers argue the industry lacks meaningful safety protocols. The Washington Examiner reports Coxon cited concerns about the "direction" AI development is heading—a rebuke to his former employer's approach.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts growing tech clusters in Denver and Boulder, and AI development increasingly touches energy grids, water management, and critical infrastructure. If major AI labs face talent exodus over safety concerns, Colorado's ability to attract frontier tech talent—and the high-wage jobs that follow—could suffer.

Conservatives should watch whether Congress moves to impose AI regulation that could stifle innovation or hand regulatory power to progressive bureaucrats who'll weaponize it against competitive markets.
