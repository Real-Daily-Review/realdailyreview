# Republicans Invoke Founders' Tax Revolt as Socialist Candidates Gain Ground

As America marks 250 years since the Declaration of Independence, Republicans are weaponizing the Founders' anti-government message against what they see as creeping socialism in Democratic primaries.

Rep. Matt Van Epps unveiled legislation reaffirming the Declaration, explicitly framing it as a response to socialist candidates gaining traction (Fox News Politics). The move reflects conservative anxiety that progressive Democrats are abandoning the nation's founding principles of limited government and individual liberty.

Conservative analysts argue the Declaration's economic grievances remain strikingly relevant. The Founders' core complaints—excessive taxation, government overreach, and loss of economic control—parallel modern Republican opposition to Biden-era spending, regulatory expansion, and tax hikes (Breitbart). The parallel is direct: colonists rebelled against taxation without representation; today's conservatives fight what they view as unaccountable bureaucratic rule.

For Colorado specifically, this framing matters. State Republicans have long opposed Denver's progressive tax-and-spend agenda, from recent ballot measures to proposed wealth taxes. The Declaration's anti-tax message resonates with Colorado's independent streak and energy-sector workers wary of federal climate mandates.

National Review's coverage of Monticello as a "scholarly center" underscores how conservatives are reclaiming the Founders' intellectual legacy from progressive reinterpretation.
