# Surveillance Creep: Colorado Law Enforcement Eyes Flock Cameras as Privacy Concerns Mount

A quirky San Diego city council hearing—where a Darth Vader impersonator testified in favor of license plate reader cameras—has thrust the Flock surveillance debate into the national spotlight, with Colorado law enforcement quietly watching (9News Denver). The technology automatically scans and logs license plates, feeding data into searchable databases that police say aid investigations.

But conservatives and privacy advocates are sounding the alarm. Republicans argue that mass surveillance infrastructure, however useful for cops, represents exactly the kind of government overreach the Constitution was designed to prevent. National Review warns that while Flock cameras may help law enforcement, rolling back the technology won't reverse "decades" of surveillance expansion—suggesting the real problem runs deeper than any single tool.

Colorado taxpayers should be asking hard questions: Who owns the data? How long is it retained? What prevents mission creep from traffic stops to political targeting? Law enforcement will claim Flock solves crimes efficiently. Conservatives counter that efficiency doesn't trump Fourth Amendment protections against unreasonable searches.

Watch whether Colorado cities—Denver, Boulder, Colorado Springs—adopt Flock systems and what privacy safeguards, if any, they demand before deployment.
