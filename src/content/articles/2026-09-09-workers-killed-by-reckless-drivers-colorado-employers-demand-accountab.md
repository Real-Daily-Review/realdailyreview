# Workplace Safety Crisis: Workers Killed by Reckless Drivers Demands Accountability

Colorado employers and workers face a growing threat from impaired and reckless drivers on job sites and in work zones—a crisis demanding swift prosecution and workplace safety reform.

A UPS worker of 44 years, Paul Roberts, was struck and killed by a vehicle at a Missouri facility Friday night, just two weeks before his planned retirement (9News Denver). Meanwhile, a drugged driver on Long Island plowed through a red light, killing a construction worker and injuring two others—the motorist now faces homicide charges (New York Post).

These aren't isolated incidents. Conservatives argue that workplace fatalities from vehicular negligence reflect a broader failure: inadequate enforcement of impaired-driving laws, insufficient workplace safety protocols, and courts too lenient on dangerous drivers. Republicans point to the Long Island case as proof that drug-fueled recklessness must carry serious criminal consequences, not mere citations.

Colorado's construction and logistics industries employ thousands in high-risk environments. State employers and unions should demand tougher penalties for impaired driving near work zones and better site security. Workers deserve protection from preventable deaths caused by negligent motorists.

Watch for whether Colorado lawmakers push for enhanced penalties for impaired driving in work zones and whether OSHA strengthens vehicle-safety protocols at distribution and construction sites.
