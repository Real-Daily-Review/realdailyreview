# Colorado's Air Quality Crisis Demands Real Solutions, Not Green Mandates

Denver's air quality has hit crisis levels—ranked worst in the nation and 10th worst globally—but Colorado's progressive leadership should resist the urge to weaponize the problem against energy producers and taxpayers.

Wildfire smoke is the primary culprit driving air quality alerts across the metro (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). More residents are seeking medical care as smoke impacts worsen. The situation is genuinely serious and demands attention.

But here's where conservatives and the left diverge sharply: Democrats will likely use this crisis to justify stricter emissions regulations, accelerated coal plant closures, and expanded renewable mandates—policies that have already gutted Colorado's energy workforce and driven up electricity costs. Republicans argue the real issue is forest management. Decades of federal mismanagement have left Colorado's national forests choked with dead timber and overgrown brush—tinderboxes waiting to ignite. Aggressive thinning, controlled burns, and timber harvesting would reduce catastrophic wildfires and the smoke they generate far more effectively than punishing Colorado's remaining energy sector.

Coloradans deserve clean air *and* affordable energy. The state should prioritize forest health over regulatory theater.

Watch for whether Gov. Jared Polis's administration uses this air quality crisis to justify new energy taxes or accelerated coal retirements—moves that would hurt working families without addressing the real problem: forest fuel loads.
