# Denver Heat Wave Breaks Before Week's End

Denver faces another scorcher this week before relief arrives—a reminder that Colorado's unpredictable weather demands reliable energy infrastructure to keep the lights on and air conditioning running.

Temperatures are climbing into the 90s across the Front Range with hazy conditions persisting through midweek, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31. The heat wave brings isolated storm chances but no significant precipitation relief yet.

The good news: a weather pattern shift is moving in, bringing cooler temperatures and more substantial rain chances by week's end, meteorologists say.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses, the timing matters. Peak summer demand strains the grid—and that's precisely when reliable, affordable power generation becomes critical. Colorado's energy mix includes natural gas plants that provide dependable baseload power during heat waves. Yet state regulators continue pushing aggressive renewable mandates that threaten grid stability without adequate backup capacity.

Conservative energy advocates argue Colorado should maintain a balanced portfolio of proven generation sources—natural gas, coal, and hydro—alongside renewables, rather than rushing toward ideological targets that could leave residents vulnerable to blackouts during extreme weather.

Watch whether state energy officials acknowledge the grid's performance during this heat event and whether they adjust unrealistic decarbonization timelines accordingly.
