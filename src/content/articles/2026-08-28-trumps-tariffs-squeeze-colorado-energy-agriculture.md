# Trump's Tariff Gambit Threatens Colorado's Energy and Agriculture Sectors

President Trump's escalating trade war—now targeting Canada with 50% tariffs on lumber, agricultural products, and electronics—risks real pain for Colorado's economy, even as conservatives debate whether protectionism serves Main Street or Wall Street.

The new tariffs hit Colorado hard. Lumber tariffs threaten construction costs across the state; agricultural duties endanger Western Slope farmers who depend on cross-border trade. Energy companies face uncertainty over Canadian imports critical to oil and gas operations. (Washington Examiner)

**The conservative split is sharp.** Trump's populist base argues tariffs protect American workers and manufacturing from unfair competition. Critics—including free-market conservatives—warn that broad tariffs function as hidden taxes on consumers and businesses, raising prices at the pump and the grocery store without delivering promised reshoring. (National Review)

Colorado Republicans face a real tension: the state's energy and ag sectors thrive on predictable, open trade. Tariff uncertainty creates the opposite. Ranchers, oil executives, and construction firms need clarity, not economic whiplash.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican delegation—particularly those representing energy and agricultural districts—breaks with Trump on tariff scope or doubles down on his nationalist economic doctrine.
