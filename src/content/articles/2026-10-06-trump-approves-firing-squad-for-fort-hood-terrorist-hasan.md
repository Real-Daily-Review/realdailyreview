# Trump Approves Firing Squad for Fort Hood Terrorist After 17-Year Legal Stall

President Trump has authorized the execution of Nidal Malik Hasan by firing squad, clearing a nearly two-decade legal logjam that delayed justice for the 13 soldiers killed in the 2009 Fort Hood massacre. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth recommended the execution, and Trump approved it, according to Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell (Washington Examiner, Fox News).

Hasan, a U.S. Army psychiatrist, opened fire at the Texas base in one of the deadliest mass shootings on a military installation. His case languished in the military justice system for 17 years—a stark reminder of how federal bureaucracy can obstruct accountability even in the most clear-cut cases of terrorism on American soil.

The decision reflects Trump's and Hegseth's commitment to swift military justice and restoring order within the armed forces. Conservatives have long argued that excessive legal delays undermine military discipline and dishonor victims' families. The Fort Hood case exemplifies how progressive legal frameworks can paralyze even straightforward capital cases.

For Colorado service members and military families, this signals a return to decisive leadership at the Pentagon after years of woke-focused priorities that many argue distracted from core military readiness and justice.
