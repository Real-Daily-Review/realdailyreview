# DOJ Blocks More Epstein Files; Trump Admin Seeks Delay on Transparency

The Trump administration's Justice Department is resisting court pressure to release additional unredacted Jeffrey Epstein records, arguing it has already met federal transparency requirements while requesting a 60-day extension to consider appealing any order to disclose more material.

In a 17-page filing Thursday, the DOJ told a federal judge that further document releases aren't necessary under law (Washington Examiner). The move contradicts public demands—particularly from victims' advocates and congressional Republicans—for full transparency into Epstein's network and any potential connections to powerful figures.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and transparency advocates argue the public has a right to know the full scope of Epstein's crimes and associates. Withholding records fuels conspiracy theories and protects the powerful from accountability. Even under a Republican administration, stonewalling raises questions about whose interests the DOJ prioritizes.

**The counterargument:** The Justice Department contends that releasing certain materials could compromise ongoing investigations, violate privacy of non-targets, or expose intelligence methods.

Colorado taxpayers and voters should watch whether this delay tactic succeeds—and whether Republican lawmakers, who've called for transparency, pressure their own administration to reverse course (KDVR Fox31 Denver).
