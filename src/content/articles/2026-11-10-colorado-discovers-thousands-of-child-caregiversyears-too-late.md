# Colorado's Hidden Child Caregiver Crisis Exposes Gaps in State Support Systems

Colorado has joined just two other states in actually counting how many children serve as primary caregivers for aging relatives—a tacit admission that government hasn't been tracking a problem affecting thousands of young Coloradans. According to CPR News, national estimates suggest more than 5 million children nationwide fill this role, yet Colorado only recently began documenting the scope locally.

The issue highlights a conservative concern: when government fails at basic data collection and family support infrastructure, it often responds by expanding bureaucracy rather than empowering families and communities. Instead of creating new state programs, policymakers should ask why Colorado families lack adequate access to affordable elder care, respite services, and flexible work arrangements that would reduce the burden on children.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that bloated Medicaid systems and regulatory barriers to private elder-care providers have artificially constrained options for aging Coloradans and their families. The real solution, they contend, lies in removing red tape, expanding market competition in home care services, and letting families—not government—decide how best to manage multigenerational care.

Colorado Sun reporting confirms the state is still growing, though demographers note Colorado is losing competitive edge—a sign that tax burdens and regulatory costs may be driving talent elsewhere.

**Watch for:** Whether state lawmakers respond with new spending programs or market-friendly reforms that actually expand affordable care options.
