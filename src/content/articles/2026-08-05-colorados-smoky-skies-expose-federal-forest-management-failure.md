# Colorado Choked by Out-of-State Wildfire Smoke—Again

Colorado's air quality crisis this week isn't homegrown—it's a direct result of uncontrolled wildfires raging across neighboring states, according to CPR News. The thick smoke blanketing Denver and the Front Range underscores a familiar problem: Colorado taxpayers and residents bear the health costs of fire management failures upstream.

The smoke isn't merely an inconvenience. KDVR reports that local animal experts warn the poor air quality affects pets as severely as people, with respiratory risks spiking for vulnerable animals during extended smoke events.

This recurring scenario raises hard questions conservatives should ask: Why do neighboring states' forest management policies—often hamstrung by federal land restrictions and environmental litigation—create air quality emergencies that Colorado can't control? Federal lands comprise roughly 36% of Colorado's territory, yet Washington's mismanagement of Western forests cascades downwind onto Colorado families and small businesses.

Republicans have long argued that active forest management, timber harvesting, and controlled burns—resisted by progressive environmental groups—would reduce catastrophic wildfire risk. Instead, Coloradans inhale the consequences of regulatory gridlock and federal bureaucratic inaction.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation demands accountability from federal land managers and pushes for streamlined forest management authority at the state level.
