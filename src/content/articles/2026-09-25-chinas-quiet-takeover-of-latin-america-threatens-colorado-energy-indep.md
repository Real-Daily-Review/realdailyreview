# China's Latin America Grip Tightens While Washington Slept

Washington's assumption that geography alone secures American influence in Latin America has proven dangerously naive. According to the *Washington Examiner*, while U.S. foreign-policy attention scattered across distant conflicts, China methodically built economic and strategic dominance across the Western Hemisphere—controlling trade routes, infrastructure, ports, energy assets, telecommunications networks, mining operations, and access to critical minerals Washington now desperately needs.

For Colorado, this matters directly. American energy independence and domestic mineral production—critical to manufacturing, defense, and grid reliability—face intensifying competition from Chinese-controlled supply chains in our hemisphere. When China controls Latin American mining and energy resources, Colorado's own resource development becomes strategically vital, yet remains hamstrung by federal environmental regulations and permitting delays that Beijing's state-backed competitors never face.

Conservatives argue the Biden-Harris administration's inattention to Western Hemisphere strategy while pursuing costly foreign interventions elsewhere represents catastrophic strategic mismanagement. Republicans contend that rebuilding American economic and energy independence requires unleashing domestic production—not surrendering resource dominance to Beijing's patient, decades-long strategy.

The Trump administration's renewed focus on Latin America signals a potential shift, but Colorado taxpayers and energy workers should watch whether Washington backs that rhetoric with actual deregulation and permitting reform to compete.
