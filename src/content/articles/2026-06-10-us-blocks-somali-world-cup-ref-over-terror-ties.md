# U.S. Customs Blocks Somali World Cup Referee Over Terror Ties

U.S. Customs and Border Protection did its job last weekend, turning away a Somali World Cup referee at Miami International Airport over suspected ties to terrorist organizations.

Omar Artan, scheduled to officiate matches for the 2026 FIFA World Cup tournament in the United States, arrived Saturday from Istanbul and was denied entry, according to reporting from the Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire. CBP cited Artan's associations with "suspected members of terror organizations" as grounds for the denial.

The decision underscores a critical point conservatives have long pressed: vetting matters. While the left often frames immigration and border security concerns as xenophobic, this case demonstrates why thorough background checks and willingness to deny entry based on legitimate security threats remain essential—especially when foreign nationals are granted access to high-profile events on American soil.

The 2026 World Cup will draw hundreds of thousands of international visitors and require coordination across federal agencies. Whether CBP's vetting protocols extend uniformly across all tournament personnel and whether FIFA will cooperate fully with U.S. security standards remain open questions worth monitoring.

Watch whether the State Department and CBP maintain consistent enforcement on remaining World Cup officials and whether FIFA pushes back against American security requirements.
