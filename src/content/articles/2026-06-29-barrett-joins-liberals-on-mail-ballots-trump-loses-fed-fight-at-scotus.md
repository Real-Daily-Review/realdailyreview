# Supreme Court Splits on Election Integrity, Deals Trump Loss on Fed Removal

Colorado conservatives face a troubling Supreme Court term: Justice Amy Coney Barrett has joined the liberal wing to allow mail-in ballots counted after Election Day, while Chief Justice John Roberts blocked President Trump from removing a Federal Reserve governor he views as unfit.

In a 5-4 decision, Barrett authored the majority opinion permitting late-arriving mail ballots—a move that sparked immediate backlash from the right. Justice Samuel Alito warned the ruling creates an "invitation to voter fraud risks" and threatens public confidence in election integrity (Fox News). Conservatives argue that counting ballots after Election Day undermines the fundamental principle of a fixed voting deadline and opens the door to manipulation—a particular concern in Colorado, where mail-in voting dominates.

Separately, Roberts sided with liberals in blocking Trump's attempt to fire Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook over mortgage fraud allegations. The 5-4 ruling handed the White House a defeat and exposed fractures within the conservative majority on executive power and regulatory independence.

For Colorado voters and taxpayers, the Barrett ruling threatens election security in a state already dependent on mail-in ballots. The Fed decision signals that even Trump-appointed justices may constrain presidential authority over financial regulators.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans push for stricter mail-ballot deadlines in state law—the only remedy available after this SCOTUS setback.
