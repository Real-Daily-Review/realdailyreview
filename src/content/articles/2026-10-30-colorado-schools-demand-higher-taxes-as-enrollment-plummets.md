# Colorado Schools Face Enrollment Cliff as Taxpayers Foot Bigger Bills for Fewer Kids

Colorado school districts are pushing higher taxes while enrollment continues its steady decline—a mismatch that should alarm taxpayers already squeezed by the state's cost-of-living surge.

According to Complete Colorado, districts across the state face a structural problem: they're asking voters for more money even as student populations shrink. The trend shows no sign of reversing in the foreseeable future. Yet rather than right-sizing budgets to match enrollment, many districts pursue tax increases justified by appeals to "fund our kids"—a framing that obscures the real issue: declining enrollment means fewer students per tax dollar spent.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this reveals the true priority of education bureaucracies: protecting spending levels and workforce size, not adapting to demographic reality. When enrollment drops, they say, per-pupil spending should improve without new taxes. Instead, districts lobby for revenue increases, effectively asking Colorado families to pay more for a shrinking service.

Meanwhile, the Colorado Sun reports that the state's child welfare system continues struggling with racial disparities in foster care placements, suggesting Colorado's government agencies face deeper management challenges beyond just education funding.

**Watch for:** whether Colorado voters reject school tax measures in 2026 and 2027, signaling taxpayer fatigue with districts that won't cut costs alongside enrollment.
