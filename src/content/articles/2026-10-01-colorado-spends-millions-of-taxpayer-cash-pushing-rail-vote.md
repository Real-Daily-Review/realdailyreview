# Millions in Taxpayer Cash Fueling Slick Pro-Rail Campaign While Coloradans Foot the Bill

Colorado officials are spending millions in public money to sell voters on the Front Range rail project—a textbook case of government using taxpayer dollars to lobby taxpayers into approving more spending.

According to CPR News, the campaign includes paid Instagram influencers and targeted mailers promoting the Colorado Connector with "positive messages." That's your money bankrolling a sales pitch.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue the project itself is the real problem. Complete Colorado lays out the core objection: voters face a choice between "blowing billions on a train few will ride and keeping that money in their own pockets." The rail line would consume vast resources for a service with questionable ridership demand and uncertain long-term viability.

The broader concern: government agencies shouldn't be using public funds to manufacture consent for their own pet projects. If the Colorado Connector were genuinely popular, it wouldn't need a multimillion-dollar influencer campaign to pass.

Democrats and rail advocates counter that public education campaigns are standard practice for major infrastructure initiatives, but conservatives see this differently—it's corporate welfare dressed up as transit planning, funded by Colorado taxpayers who may never use it.

Watch whether the campaign spending disclosures reveal the full scope of public money flowing into this push before Election Day.
