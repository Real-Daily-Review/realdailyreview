# Colorado Ski Industry Pushes Back Against "Lock the Clock" Daylight Saving Legislation

Colorado's ski resort operators are mounting a defense against federal legislation that would eliminate daylight saving time—a move that could reshape mountain town economics and visitor patterns across the state.

The Sunshine Protection Act, which would lock the nation into permanent daylight saving time year-round, faces pushback from the ski industry, which argues the change would disrupt seasonal operations and visitor behavior (Colorado Sun). Ski areas contend that permanent daylight saving time creates economic and safety complications for mountain communities dependent on tourism revenue.

The proposal has bipartisan appeal at the federal level—President Trump has championed ending the clock-switching ritual (9News Denver)—but the U.S. Senate has shown no movement on the measure, leaving the status quo intact for now.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. Mountain resort towns depend on predictable seasonal patterns, and any federal mandate altering daylight hours could ripple through lodging, dining, and retail operations that cater to visitors. Conservatives should be skeptical of federal overreach into state timekeeping decisions; this is precisely the kind of one-size-fits-all Washington legislation that ignores local economic realities.
