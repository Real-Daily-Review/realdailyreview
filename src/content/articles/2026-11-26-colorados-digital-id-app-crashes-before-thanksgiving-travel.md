# Colorado's Digital ID App Down Before Holiday Travel Crunch

Colorado's myColorado digital identification app has crashed at precisely the worst moment—right before Thanksgiving travel season—leaving up to 150,000 residents potentially exposed to a cybersecurity breach with no restoration timeline in sight (Colorado Sun). The state-run platform, meant to streamline ID verification and government services, is now offline when Coloradans need it most for holiday travel and transactions.

This failure underscores a conservative concern: government-managed digital systems often lack the security rigor and operational reliability of private-sector alternatives. When the state consolidates sensitive personal data into a single app, it creates a single point of catastrophic failure—exactly what just happened. Coloradans who relied on myColorado for travel credentials or ID verification now face real inconvenience during peak travel days.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this incident validates skepticism about expanding government digital infrastructure. Private companies managing payment systems, travel apps, and identity verification typically respond faster to breaches and maintain higher uptime standards because their business depends on it. Government agencies, by contrast, operate without competitive pressure or profit incentives to innovate or secure systems properly.

The state has offered no timeline for restoring the app, leaving residents stranded during one of the year's busiest travel periods.
