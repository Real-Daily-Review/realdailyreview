# Colorado Should Stop Subsidizing Political Parties With Taxpayer Cash

Colorado taxpayers are bankrolling primary elections for political parties—whether they belong to them or not, and whether they like it or not.

Complete Colorado has raised a straightforward conservative complaint: state government has no business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties, let alone forcing citizens to finance their primaries. The core issue is simple: why should a Colorado taxpayer who is unaffiliated, or who belongs to a party other than the Democrats or Republicans, subsidize primary elections they cannot even participate in?

This is government overreach dressed up as electoral administration. Conservatives argue the state should get out of the party-financing business entirely. Taxpayer dollars should not flow to private political organizations—period. If the Democratic and Republican parties want to hold primaries, they should fund them themselves.

The arrangement amounts to a hidden tax on Colorado voters and a government-granted privilege that independent candidates and minor parties do not enjoy. It's corporate welfare for the two major parties, paid for by ordinary Coloradans.

Complete Colorado has framed ending this subsidy as a "courageous" move—and they're right. It would require political will to stand against both major parties, but it's the fiscally conservative and constitutionally sound position.

Watch for whether any Colorado legislators have the spine to introduce legislation eliminating taxpayer funding for party primaries.
