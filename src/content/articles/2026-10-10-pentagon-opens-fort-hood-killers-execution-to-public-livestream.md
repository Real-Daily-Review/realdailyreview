# Fort Hood Killer to Face Public Execution as Pentagon Opens Firing Squad to Livestream

The Pentagon will allow Americans to witness the execution of Nidal Hasan, the Army psychiatrist who murdered 13 people at Fort Hood in 2009, via livestream—a rare move that reflects shifting attitudes toward military justice and public accountability.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the decision to permit public viewing of the firing squad execution, marking a departure from typical closed-door military proceedings. According to the *Daily Caller*, the livestream access will give Americans direct visibility into the consequences of one of the deadliest mass shootings on a U.S. military installation.

A Fort Hood survivor shot seven times by Hasan supports witnessing the execution but issued a stark warning: Americans must not treat it as entertainment. *Fox News* reported the veteran's plea that the proceeding be approached with solemnity, not spectacle—a reasonable concern given the media circus that often surrounds high-profile executions.

Conservatives have long argued that transparency in military justice strengthens public trust and ensures accountability. The decision aligns with calls for open government proceedings, though it raises legitimate questions about the appropriate role of public participation in capital punishment.

The execution represents closure for victims' families and validates the military justice system's ability to prosecute and punish even the most heinous crimes within its ranks.
