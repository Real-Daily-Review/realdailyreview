# Police Officer Killed by Career Criminal in South Carolina; Gun Rights Debate Looms

A 29-year-old Columbia, South Carolina police officer, Christopher DeLong, was shot and killed Saturday morning while responding to a domestic disturbance call in a park—and the shooter had an extensive criminal record, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The incident underscores a recurring conservative argument: criminals ignore gun laws. The suspect, despite his rap sheet, obtained a firearm and opened fire on officers responding to a restraining-order violation. A second officer was also wounded in the attack.

**The Colorado angle:** South Carolina's tragedy will inevitably fuel national gun-control demands from the left, even though existing laws already prohibited the shooter from possessing a weapon. Colorado Democrats, who have pushed universal background checks and magazine restrictions in recent years, will likely cite such incidents to justify further restrictions on law-abiding gun owners—ignoring that enforcement of *existing* laws against criminals remains the real problem.

Republicans argue that hardening enforcement against criminals, not expanding restrictions on constitutional rights, prevents tragedies. Conservatives point out that Colorado's own background-check regime hasn't stopped criminals from obtaining firearms illegally.

Watch for Democratic lawmakers to weaponize this tragedy in the 2026 legislative session to advance gun-control measures that won't touch the criminal element.
