# Colorado Medicaid Audit Exposes $8M Shortfall in Disability Care Program

Colorado's Medicaid program is on the hook for at least $8 million in federal funds it shouldn't have spent on in-home disability care, according to an Office of Inspector General audit reported by the *Colorado Sun*. The federal watchdog flagged an additional $45 million for further review.

State officials deny wrongdoing, insisting there was no fraud—a claim that will face scrutiny as the audit unfolds. The distinction matters: if the state simply mismanaged taxpayer dollars through sloppy accounting rather than intentional theft, Colorado still owes the money back.

This comes as Colorado's Medicaid rolls remain bloated from pandemic-era enrollment expansions. Republicans have long warned that the state's generous disability benefits and loose eligibility rules invite waste and overspending. The audit suggests those concerns weren't baseless.

The *Colorado Sun* reports state officials are cooperating with the review, but the real question for taxpayers is whether this $8 million represents a one-time accounting error or a symptom of deeper program mismanagement. Conservatives should watch whether the state aggressively pursues cost controls and tighter eligibility standards—or simply writes a check and moves on.
