# Harmeet Dhillon's DOJ Exit Signals Shift in Civil Rights Enforcement

Harmeet Dhillon, the Trump administration's assistant attorney general for the Civil Rights Division, resigned Thursday—a departure that marks a significant change in how the Justice Department will handle civil rights cases going forward.

Dhillon announced her exit moments before addressing the Conservative Political Action Conference at Mar-a-Lago, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The move represents what conservatives view as a loss of institutional momentum on cases challenging progressive legal orthodoxy, particularly on issues like affirmative action, DEI mandates, and gender ideology in schools and workplaces.

Dhillon's tenure at the Civil Rights Division was marked by aggressive litigation against what conservatives argue were unconstitutional applications of civil rights law—including challenges to race-based admissions and federal overreach on transgender policies. Her departure raises questions about whether the administration will maintain that aggressive posture or pivot strategy.

For Colorado, the resignation matters: state universities and school districts have faced scrutiny over DEI spending and admissions policies. Dhillon's Civil Rights Division had become a potential ally for Colorado taxpayers and parents challenging progressive policies in education and employment.

Watch whether the Trump administration replaces Dhillon with someone equally committed to reining in civil rights bureaucracy, or whether the division's enforcement priorities shift.
