# Thanksgiving Storm Batters Nation; Colorado Braces for Winter Weather Pattern

Torrential rain and destructive winds hammered much of the United States over Thanksgiving, leaving the East Coast waterlogged, knocking out power to nearly a million customers, and forcing the first-ever postponement of Macy's Thanksgiving Day parade (KDVR Fox31).

The widespread flooding and severe weather underscores what Colorado residents know well: the nation's weather patterns are increasingly volatile and unpredictable. While climate activists use such events to justify expansive green-energy mandates and carbon regulations, conservatives argue that Colorado's energy independence—built on responsible oil, gas, and coal development—provides the reliable baseload power necessary to respond to emergencies and keep communities safe during extreme weather events.

The storm's impact on infrastructure and power grids raises a critical question for Colorado policymakers: as the state pursues aggressive renewable-energy targets, are we adequately maintaining the dispatchable power sources needed when wind and solar fail during severe weather? Blackouts during winter storms pose real risks to vulnerable populations.

Colorado's own weather patterns this season will test whether the state's energy transition strategy can deliver both affordability and reliability when it matters most.

Watch for how Colorado utilities respond to winter demand and whether state regulators acknowledge the need for dependable backup power sources.
