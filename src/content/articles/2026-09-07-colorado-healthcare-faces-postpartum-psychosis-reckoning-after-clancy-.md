# Colorado Healthcare System Must Confront Postpartum Psychosis Gap After Clancy Mistrial

The Lindsay Clancy mistrial has exposed a critical blind spot in Colorado's healthcare infrastructure: our state's medical establishment remains dangerously unprepared to identify and treat postpartum psychosis before tragedy strikes.

The high-profile Massachusetts case—which ended in a hung jury after Clancy's defense centered on untreated postpartum psychosis—has drawn national attention to a condition that remains poorly understood by hospitals and clinicians. A University of Denver social work professor told KDVR Fox31 Denver that the healthcare industry must use this moment to strengthen screening, diagnosis, and intervention protocols (KDVR). The concern isn't academic: Daily Caller reporting suggests the Clancy case may have inspired copycat incidents, underscoring how public trials can influence vulnerable individuals.

**The conservative angle:** Colorado taxpayers should demand accountability from state health regulators and hospital administrators. If our healthcare system failed to catch warning signs in similar cases, that's a systems failure—not an excuse for expanded government programs. Better training, clearer protocols, and professional liability matter more than throwing money at new bureaucracies.

Conservatives also note that high-profile criminal defenses, while legally necessary, shouldn't become templates for excusing violent crime. Personal responsibility and mental health treatment must coexist.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado's Department of Health and Environment issues binding guidelines on postpartum psychosis screening—and whether those guidelines actually improve outcomes without ballooning costs.
