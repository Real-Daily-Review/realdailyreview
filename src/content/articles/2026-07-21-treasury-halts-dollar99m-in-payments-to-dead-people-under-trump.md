# Treasury Catches $99M in Payments to Dead People—Finally

The Trump administration's Treasury Department has stopped nearly $100 million in federal payments destined for deceased recipients, exposing what conservatives say is a damning indictment of government waste and lax oversight.

Since March 2025, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's office screened 885 million federal payments against death records and flagged 4,900 disbursements worth approximately $99 million headed to dead people (Fox News, Just The News). The new anti-fraud process prevented those funds from leaving taxpayer coffers.

The catch underscores a broader conservative argument: federal agencies routinely hemorrhage money through sloppy controls and minimal accountability. Republicans have long warned that bloated government bureaucracies lack basic safeguards, and this case proves the point. Billions more likely slip through undetected across Social Security, Medicare, welfare programs, and other entitlements.

For Colorado taxpayers already burdened by state and federal taxes, the revelation stings. Money that should fund roads, schools, or stay in workers' pockets instead vanishes into administrative black holes—until someone finally implements competent oversight.

The Treasury's success here raises an uncomfortable question: How many other fraud-prevention measures could save hundreds of millions annually if implemented across all federal agencies? Conservatives should demand a full audit of government payment systems and permanent, automated death-record screening across all benefit programs.
