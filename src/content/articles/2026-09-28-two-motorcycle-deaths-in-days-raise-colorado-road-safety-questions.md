# Denver-Area Motorcycle Fatalities Highlight Road Safety Concerns

Colorado's roads claimed at least two motorcycle riders in recent days, underscoring persistent dangers on Denver-area highways and mountain passes.

A motorcyclist died Sunday at the intersection of Speer Boulevard and Wewatta Street in Denver, according to 9News Denver. Days earlier, Colorado State Patrol responded to a fatal three-vehicle crash Friday near Guanella Pass on Highway 285 at County Road 62, where another motorcyclist was killed (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

While tragic, these incidents raise questions conservatives should press: Are Colorado's roads adequately maintained? Are traffic enforcement priorities aligned with public safety? The state's transportation budget—often bloated with pet projects and green-energy mandates—should prioritize basic road conditions and accident prevention over ideological spending.

Motorcycle riders, who represent a disproportionate share of road fatalities, deserve infrastructure that works. That means pothole repairs, clear signage, and enforcement of traffic laws—not more government studies or bike-lane experiments that divert resources from core safety.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado transportation officials will commit to transparent, outcome-focused spending on road maintenance and safety rather than expanding bureaucratic oversight.
