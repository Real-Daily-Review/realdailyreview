# Colorado's Western Slope Ablaze: Over 84K Acres Burning as State Faces Catastrophic Fire Season

Colorado's wildfire crisis is spiraling out of control, with multiple blazes across the Western Slope and southern regions destroying over 84,000 acres and forcing mass evacuations over the weekend.

The Snyder Fire near Fruita and the Gold Mountain Fire near Ouray are among the most destructive, with residents and ranchers scrambling to evacuate livestock and families. One rancher near Ouray reported loading 21 horses into trailers as ash fell and flames crept within a mile and a half of his property (KDVR). Officials have established command centers to coordinate response efforts across the region (CPR News).

**The conservative concern:** Colorado's catastrophic fire seasons demand honest answers about forest management. While the left often blames climate change and calls for more federal land restrictions, Republicans and land-use experts argue that decades of mismanagement—overgrown forests, fuel buildup, and environmental litigation blocking thinning projects—have created tinderboxes across the state. Proper forest stewardship, not regulatory expansion, prevents these disasters.

The evacuations underscore the real-world cost of failed land policy. Ranchers and rural Coloradans bear the brunt while progressive environmental groups block the active management that could reduce catastrophic fire risk.

Watch whether state and federal officials will finally embrace aggressive forest thinning and fuel reduction, or double down on restrictions that leave Colorado's forests as kindling.
