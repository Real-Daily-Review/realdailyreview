# Aspen Acres Fire Scorches Nearly 94,000 Acres; Volunteer Firefighters Lose Homes While Defending Communities

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire has grown to nearly 94,000 acres, destroying homes across Pueblo County while volunteer firefighters—the backbone of rural Colorado fire response—sacrifice their own property to protect neighbors (Colorado Sun).

The human cost is staggering. A Red Creek volunteer fire chief and his firefighter wife lost their home while he stayed behind to help evacuate residents (9News). In Beulah, volunteer crews continued battling the blaze even after learning their own homes had burned (9News). One Pueblo County resident watched his home burn during a medical appointment (KDVR).

Forecasters offered a glimmer of hope: scattered showers Wednesday could bring temporary relief. But hot, dry, and windy conditions are expected to return by the weekend, threatening to reignite the fire's spread.

This disaster underscores a critical reality conservatives and rural Coloradans know well: when government fails to manage forests properly—years of regulatory gridlock preventing timber thinning and fuel reduction—communities depend on volunteer firefighters who receive minimal compensation and often lose everything in the process. These aren't government employees with pension protections; they're neighbors who show up because it's right.

Watch whether state and federal officials finally prioritize forest management reform or continue blaming "climate" while blocking the mechanical thinning that prevents catastrophic burns.
