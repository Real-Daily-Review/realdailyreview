## Trump Hits Canada with 50% Tariffs — Colorado Businesses, Take Note

President Trump announced sweeping 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, covering everything from wine and cement to hockey sticks, under three separate proclamations. The tariffs do not apply to products already subject to Section 232 duties. Colorado imports Canadian goods across agriculture, construction, and energy sectors — businesses here need to understand how these new levies affect their supply chains and bottom lines. Republicans argue the tariffs are a necessary pressure tool; the question for Colorado industries is how long they can absorb the cost before it hits consumers.

## Colorado Health Care Costs Keep Climbing — and State Subsidies Are Making It Worse

Colorado's largest employment sector — health care — is only getting more expensive, and a new report from Complete Colorado points the finger squarely at state subsidies as a driving force behind runaway costs. Conservatives have long argued that government interference in health care markets distorts pricing and crowds out competition. Colorado taxpayers are footing the bill for a system that rewards dependency over efficiency, and the legislature's appetite for expanding subsidies shows no sign of slowing.

## Colorado Springs Sued Over April Municipal Elections

Colorado Springs is facing a lawsuit for holding its municipal elections in April, marking the first enforcement action since a 2025 state voting law took effect. The law appears designed to consolidate elections — a move critics say is less about voter convenience and more about tilting turnout dynamics in favor of Democrats. Colorado Springs is one of the state's most reliably conservative cities, and Republicans argue this kind of top-down election scheduling mandate is exactly the sort of government overreach that erodes local control.
