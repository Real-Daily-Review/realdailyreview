# Colorado Schools Deploy Therapy Dogs After Shooting; Soft-on-Crime Bail Policies Remain Unchecked

A Colorado high school is turning to therapy dogs to help students process trauma following a shooting—a band-aid approach that highlights a deeper problem: judges and prosecutors across the country are releasing violent suspects on minimal or no bail, enabling repeat offenses and putting communities at risk.

At Evergreen High School, four-legged counselors now walk hallways and visit classrooms to provide "a loving, calming presence" to students still reeling from the incident (KDVR Fox31 Denver). While the gesture is well-intentioned, it underscores a troubling pattern. In Minnesota, a suspect accused of opening fire at a high school football game was released from jail just two days after arrest—a decision that infuriates conservatives who see it as prosecutorial malpractice (New York Post). Meanwhile, a separate shooting in Pueblo left a suspect dead and two officers injured Saturday night (9News Denver).

Republicans and conservatives argue that lenient bail policies—often championed by progressive district attorneys—prioritize criminal defendants' rights over public safety. When accused shooters walk free on minimal conditions, communities pay the price in fear and bloodshed.

Colorado parents and taxpayers should demand accountability: which judges approved these releases, and why are violent felony suspects treated as low-risk?
