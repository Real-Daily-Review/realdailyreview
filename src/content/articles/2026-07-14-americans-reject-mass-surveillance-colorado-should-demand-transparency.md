# Americans Reject Mass Surveillance—And Some Are Fighting Back

Americans are fed up with Big Tech's surveillance state, and Coloradans should pay attention to what comes next.

New Gallup data reveal growing distrust of major technology companies including Meta, Apple, and Google, as well as Flock Safety, an AI-powered surveillance camera operator expanding into communities nationwide (Washington Examiner). The backlash is real: some citizens are taking direct action by destroying surveillance cameras appearing in their neighborhoods.

This reflects a broader conservative concern about unchecked corporate surveillance and government-enabled data collection. While progressives often frame surveillance as a public safety tool, conservatives argue it represents a dangerous erosion of privacy and personal liberty—especially when local governments partner with private surveillance firms without robust public debate or consent.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. As Flock Safety and similar companies expand their camera networks in Denver suburbs and mountain communities, residents should demand transparency about where cameras are placed, who accesses the footage, and whether local law enforcement is using the data without warrants. Colorado's strong libertarian streak values privacy and skepticism of government overreach.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado municipalities will adopt surveillance-camera policies that protect Fourth Amendment rights and require explicit community approval before deployment.
