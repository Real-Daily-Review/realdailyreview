# Fort Morgan Cargill Lockout Drags On as Workers Reject Contract Deal

Over 1,700 meatpackers at Cargill's Fort Morgan facility remain locked out after rejecting the company's latest contract offer, extending a labor standoff that began in May when wage and benefits negotiations collapsed.

Union members narrowly voted down the tentative agreement, according to reports from Colorado Sun, 9News Denver, and CPR News. The workers have been off the job for months, demanding higher wages and improved benefits. Cargill, one of Colorado's largest food processors, has maintained its position through the impasse.

**The conservative angle:** This lockout highlights why Colorado's business climate matters. Fort Morgan's meatpacking industry is critical to the state's agricultural economy and rural employment. Extended labor disputes like this one disrupt supply chains, raise costs for consumers, and threaten the viability of food processing operations in Colorado—especially when regulatory burdens and labor activism combine to squeeze margins.

Republicans and business advocates argue that while workers deserve fair compensation, Colorado's regulatory environment and pro-union policies make it harder for employers to operate profitably and competitively. They warn that prolonged disputes risk pushing operations to more business-friendly states.

Democrats and unions counter that workers need stronger protections and wage guarantees in an industry known for tough conditions.
