# GOP Splits on Iran Exit as Democrats Push Immediate Pullout

Republican Senator John Kennedy of Louisiana is pushing back hard against any quick U.S. withdrawal from the Iran conflict, calling it a "huge, huge mistake" and warning against what he calls "cut and run" diplomacy (Washington Examiner). Kennedy told CBS's Margaret Brennan that messaging around the mission needs improvement, but abandoning it altogether would be strategically reckless.

Meanwhile, Democrats are demanding the opposite. Senator Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) told ABC's "This Week" that Trump should end the conflict "right now" (Breitbart), reflecting the left's preference for immediate negotiation over sustained military presence.

Trump himself weighed in Sunday, dismissing concerns about munitions shortages and telling the Wall Street Journal the U.S. military has "far more munitions than anyone in the world, and far more than we need" (Breitbart).

The split exposes a real fault line: Republicans divided between those favoring continued engagement and those open to Trump's negotiating posture, while Democrats uniformly demand immediate exit. For Colorado taxpayers footing the bill for extended Middle East operations, the debate matters—every month of conflict means defense spending that could otherwise reduce the deficit or fund domestic priorities conservatives prefer.
