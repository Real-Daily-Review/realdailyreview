# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Sunday, November 8, 2026

Colorado conservatives woke up this morning to a state still counting votes on a tax hike that would gut the flat tax, a federal president playing the blame game, and regulators quietly strangling the energy system that keeps your heat on. Buckle up.

---

## Colorado's Flat Tax Hangs by a Thread

The amendment to scrap Colorado's flat income tax in favor of a six-bracket progressive system remains too close to call. This is exactly the kind of slow-motion government expansion that Colorado taxpayers should fear most — a system designed to punish success and hand politicians a bigger lever over your wallet. Meanwhile, Denver has found time to ban foie gras, proving that when the left runs out of big ideas, it meddles in your dinner plate. Colorado conservatives need every vote counted before celebrating or mourning.

---

## PUC Puts Xcel's Natural Gas System on a Death Clock

The Colorado Public Utilities Commission is managing Xcel Energy's natural gas infrastructure toward retirement on a policy-driven timeline — not a market-driven one. That's a bureaucratic chokehold on the system that heats Colorado homes, powers Colorado businesses, and employs Colorado energy workers. Conservatives argue this is exactly the kind of regulatory overreach that drives up costs for working families while satisfying an ideological agenda that has no patience for reliable, affordable energy. If your gas bill spikes this winter, look to the PUC.

---

## Trump Points Fingers After Election Setbacks

President Trump spent the early hours after Tuesday's results blaming everyone but himself for Republican underperformance — a pattern that conservatives who want the party to grow and win should find frustrating rather than reassuring. Republicans argue that the path forward requires honest self-assessment, not a circular firing squad aimed outward. For Colorado Republicans, who are fighting uphill battles in a purple-to-blue state, the national party's ability to run disciplined, forward-looking campaigns matters enormously. Finger-pointing doesn't win seats.

## New Democratic Senator-Elect Goes Full Combative

Abdul El-Sayed, the newly elected Democratic senator, told Pod Save America that Democrats must treat President Trump as an "unserious fool" and abandon political decorum entirely. Conservatives say this kind of scorched-earth posturing tells you everything about where the Democratic Party is headed — further left, louder, and less interested in governing than in performing for the base. For Colorado, where Democrats already control the governorship and legislature, this national tone sets the table for more aggressive progressive policy pushes at the state level.

## Israel's Netanyahu Faces Coalition Challenge

Israeli President Isaac Herzog has tapped opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to attempt forming a coalition government, dealing a significant blow to Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Conservatives and pro-Israel Republicans have long viewed Netanyahu as a reliable partner in confronting Iran and maintaining regional stability. A government led by the opposition introduces uncertainty at a moment when the Middle East can least afford it — and when American foreign policy under Trump depends heavily on a coherent Israeli partner.

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado progressive income tax count — if that amendment passes, it rewrites the state's tax structure in ways that will take years to unwind and will hit small business owners and middle-class earners harder than advertised. Watch the PUC's next moves on Xcel's natural gas system; the regulatory pressure being applied now will determine whether Coloradans have affordable, reliable heat through the next decade. And watch whether Colorado Republicans draw the right lessons from Tuesday's results — the state is winnable, but only with candidates and messaging that speak to the voters who haven't yet been written off.
