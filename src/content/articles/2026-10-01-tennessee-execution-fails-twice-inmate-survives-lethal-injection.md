# Tennessee Execution Botch Raises Questions About State Protocol

Tennessee's execution of death row inmate Christa Pike went catastrophically wrong Wednesday when she survived two rounds of lethal injection, forcing state officials to halt the procedure and rush her to a hospital as her legal team filed emergency motions to block a third attempt.

Pike, convicted of murdering classmate Colleen Slemmer in 1995, was reportedly heard "snoring" after the initial injection failed, according to *The Daily Wire*. Her attorneys argue the botched execution violated her constitutional rights against cruel and unusual punishment and filed litigation to prevent the state from proceeding further (*Just The News*).

The failed execution exposes serious competency gaps in Tennessee's death penalty protocols—a concern that transcends partisan lines. While capital punishment remains broadly supported among conservatives, the execution's failure underscores why proper procedure matters: if the state cannot carry out a lawful sentence competently, it raises legitimate questions about government capacity and legitimacy.

Tennessee now faces a legal and practical reckoning. The state must either demonstrate it can execute the sentence properly or confront mounting litigation costs and reputational damage. For Colorado—which abolished capital punishment in 2020—this serves as a cautionary tale about the administrative burdens and litigation expenses that accompany the death penalty, even for states that support it.
