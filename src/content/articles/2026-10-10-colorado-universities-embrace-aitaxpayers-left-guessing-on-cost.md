# Colorado Universities Join State's AI 'Engagement' Push—At What Cost to Taxpayers?

Colorado's higher education establishment is embracing artificial intelligence through a new state honor program, with six colleges and universities now part of the first "AI Engaged Campus Designation" cohort, according to CPR News. The initiative recognizes institutions for intentional AI deployment in academic and administrative operations.

While proponents frame AI adoption as forward-thinking innovation, conservatives should ask hard questions: What's the actual cost to Colorado taxpayers? Are these universities using state funding to experiment with technology that may displace workers or compromise academic rigor? The higher ed sector has a track record of bloated administrative spending and mission creep—adding AI initiatives without clear ROI metrics is par for the course.

The designation itself carries no apparent accountability mechanism. There's no reporting requirement on how AI affects enrollment, graduation rates, or job placement. Colorado's public universities already consume substantial tax dollars; adding AI infrastructure and staff without transparent cost-benefit analysis is another example of government institutions spending first and asking questions later.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives should demand that Colorado's Board of Education and university trustees publish detailed breakdowns: total AI spending per institution, job impacts, and measurable outcomes tied to student success—not just institutional prestige.

Watch whether the state legislature appropriates additional funding for these AI initiatives without demanding hard performance metrics.
