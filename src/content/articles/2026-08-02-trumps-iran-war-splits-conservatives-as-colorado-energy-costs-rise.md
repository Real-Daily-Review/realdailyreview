# Trump's Iran Strategy Divides Conservatives as Colorado Energy Sector Watches

President Trump's escalating military posture toward Iran is fracturing his own coalition, with some conservatives warning that prolonged conflict could derail his economic agenda and drive up energy costs for Colorado families and businesses.

Trump has threatened additional strikes on Iran while pushing to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global oil chokepoint (9News Denver). The administration says it will halt strikes once deal parameters are reached to end the conflict.

**The conservative split:** National Review argues Trump has "forfeited his signature issue of economic stewardship" by pursuing both tariffs and Iran military action simultaneously. Critics worry sustained conflict will inflate energy prices and distract from tax reform and deregulation—core Trump promises to his base.

**Colorado angle:** Energy prices directly hit Colorado's working families at the pump and heating bill. Any prolonged Middle East conflict risks spiking crude costs, undercutting the economic growth Trump promised. Colorado's oil and gas industry, already navigating regulatory headwinds, faces added market uncertainty.

Trump says a deal framework could end strikes, but the timeline remains unclear. Conservatives should monitor whether the administration can negotiate an exit quickly—or whether Iran policy becomes another drag on the economy heading into 2026.
