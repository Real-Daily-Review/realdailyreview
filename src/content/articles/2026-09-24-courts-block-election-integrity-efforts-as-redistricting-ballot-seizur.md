# Supreme Court Chaos in Missouri, California Sheriff's Ballot Seizure Blocked

Election integrity disputes are consuming courts nationwide—and Colorado should pay attention to how judicial overreach undermines election administration.

Missouri's redistricting battle has spiraled into a Supreme Court emergency. The Eighth Circuit ruled the state must use its 2025 congressional map for the general election, but the U.S. Supreme Court could intervene before Sept. 28 (Fox News Politics). The ruling leaves Republicans and Democrats scrambling over which districts will actually be in play.

Meanwhile, California's Supreme Court dealt a setback to election integrity efforts when it ordered Riverside County Sheriff Chad Bianco to return over 650,000 ballots he seized in February (Washington Examiner). Bianco, a Republican and former 2026 gubernatorial candidate, had sought to investigate alleged election fraud ahead of the November 2025 election. The court found he broke election law—a ruling that conservatives argue hamstrings legitimate ballot audits while the left claims it protects election administration from political interference.

**The Colorado angle:** These cases expose how courts increasingly second-guess election officials and law enforcement when they pursue fraud investigations. Colorado election officials and county clerks should watch whether courts here adopt similar restrictions on ballot access or audit authority.

**Watch next:** Whether the U.S. Supreme Court intervenes in Missouri's map before the Sept. 28 deadline—and whether Colorado faces similar redistricting litigation heading into 2026.
