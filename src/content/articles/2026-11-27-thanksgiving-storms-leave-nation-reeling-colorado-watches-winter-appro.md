# Thanksgiving Storms Hammer Nation; Colorado Braces for Winter Pattern Shift

Torrential rain and destructive winds battered much of the United States over Thanksgiving, leaving both coasts underwater and forcing the first-ever postponement of Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade (KDVR). Nearly a million customers lost power as the storm system moved across the country, with widespread flooding reported from coast to coast (9News Denver).

The severe weather pattern underscores the unpredictability of seasonal storms—a reminder that Colorado's water management infrastructure and emergency preparedness systems remain critical as winter approaches. While the nation's coasts absorbed the brunt of this system, Colorado's Front Range and mountain communities could face significant precipitation as the pattern shifts eastward.

Conservatives and energy advocates have long argued that Colorado's grid resilience depends on reliable, dispatchable power sources—coal, natural gas, and hydroelectric capacity—rather than over-reliance on intermittent renewables. Major power outages like those seen across the holiday weekend highlight why premature retirement of traditional generation capacity poses real risks to public safety and economic stability.

State and local officials should use this weather event to audit emergency response protocols and ensure backup power systems are operational before winter storms intensify.
