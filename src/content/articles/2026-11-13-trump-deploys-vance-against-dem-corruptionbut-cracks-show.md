# Trump Taps Vance to Hunt Corruption in Democrat-Run Cities—But Ideological Rifts Remain

President Trump is directing Vice President JD Vance to focus on rooting out alleged corruption and crime in Democratic-controlled cities and states, claiming billions are stolen monthly from government programs (Fox News). Trump specifically named Minnesota, California, and Illinois as targets for Vance's oversight efforts.

The move signals Trump's intent to weaponize the vice presidency against progressive governance failures. Republicans argue that Democrat-run urban centers have become laboratories for wasteful spending, regulatory capture, and mismanagement—a message that resonates with Colorado taxpayers tired of seeing their tax dollars vanish into bloated government bureaucracies.

However, cracks are already showing in the Trump-Vance partnership. National Review reports a "Trump-Vance fissure" on major policy questions, noting that ideological disagreement between a president and VP is "particularly treacherous." Additionally, Trump's recent amnesty talk has drawn skepticism from conservatives, with National Review questioning whether any credible deal with Democrats is even possible.

The tension underscores a broader question: can Vance credibly investigate Democratic corruption while maintaining philosophical alignment with Trump on immigration, trade, and nationalist economics? Colorado conservatives should watch whether Vance's anti-corruption mandate extends to examining federal land grabs, regulatory overreach on energy development, and water-rights overreach—or remains narrowly focused on urban Democratic failures.
