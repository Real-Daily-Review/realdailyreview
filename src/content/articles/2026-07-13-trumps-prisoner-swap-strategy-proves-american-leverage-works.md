# U.S. Leverage on Political Prisoners Works—When America Uses It

The Trump administration's willingness to negotiate the release of political prisoners abroad demonstrates that American power still matters on the world stage. According to the Washington Examiner, President Trump's envoys secured the release of dozens of political prisoners from Belarus after direct engagement with Alexander Lukashenko, with sanctions relief on the Belarusian airline serving as the negotiating currency. National Review notes that Pastor Ezra Jin's case exemplifies how American advocacy can advance human freedom globally.

Republicans argue this transactional approach—trading sanctions relief for prisoner releases—proves that direct presidential engagement and economic leverage work where diplomatic niceties fail. The strategy signals to authoritarian regimes that the U.S. will use its economic tools strategically to protect political dissidents.

Democrats have historically criticized such deals as legitimizing authoritarian leaders, though the tangible results here are difficult to dismiss: actual prisoners walked free.

The broader lesson, conservatives say, is that American strength—economic and diplomatic—remains a force for liberty when wielded with clear purpose and without apology. This contrasts sharply with the previous administration's approach of multilateral consensus-building that often produced rhetoric without results.

Watch whether the Biden-Harris administration adopts or abandons this model in future negotiations with authoritarian states.
