# Lakewood Council Punishes Voters for Rejecting State Housing Mandates

Lakewood's city council is making it harder for residents to use the ballot initiative process—a move that looks like retaliation for voters' April rejection of forced housing density, according to *Complete Colorado*.

In April, Lakewood residents voted down the council's push to dramatically increase housing density, a mandate the council claimed came from Governor Polis' state-wide housing density rules. Rather than accept the verdict, the council has now imposed new burdens on the citizens' initiative process itself, raising barriers for residents who want to bypass city hall and go straight to voters.

Conservatives argue this is textbook government overreach: when citizens exercise their constitutional right to petition for ballot measures, elected officials shouldn't retaliate by making that process harder. The move also exposes the real problem—state mandates from Denver forcing one-size-fits-all housing policy on Colorado communities, then local officials punishing taxpayers who resist.

Lakewood residents have already made clear they want to preserve neighborhood character and local control over zoning. The council's response: make it tougher to challenge them next time.
