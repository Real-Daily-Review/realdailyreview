# FAA Ground Stops at O'Hare Expose Aging Infrastructure Failures During Peak Travel

Another FAA ground stop at Chicago's O'Hare International Airport—the second in two days during Thanksgiving week—reveals the real cost of deferred maintenance and federal mismanagement of critical aviation infrastructure.

The outages stemmed from equipment failures, including surface radar problems monitoring runways at one of America's busiest hubs (The Daily Wire). While the halts were temporary, they snarled holiday travel for thousands during peak season, underscoring a broader problem: the federal government's inability to maintain and upgrade essential systems.

Conservatives argue that chronic underinvestment in infrastructure maintenance—despite decades of federal spending and regulation—demonstrates why government-run systems often underperform. The FAA's aging equipment failures suggest that throwing more taxpayer dollars at federal agencies without accountability produces predictable results: delays, frustration, and economic drag.

Colorado travelers and businesses dependent on reliable air connectivity to major hubs face real consequences when federal infrastructure fails. Denver International Airport's operations depend partly on the smooth functioning of connecting hubs like O'Hare; cascading delays ripple through the entire system.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued for modernizing air traffic control through private-sector innovation and competition—a model that could reduce outages and improve reliability without expanding federal bureaucracy.

Watch whether Congress demands accountability from FAA leadership or simply appropriates more funds without structural reform.
