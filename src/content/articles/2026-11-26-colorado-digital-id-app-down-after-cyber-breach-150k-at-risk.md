# Colorado's Digital ID App Down as Thanksgiving Travel Chaos Peaks

Colorado's myColorado digital ID app—a government-managed system designed to replace physical IDs—remains offline after a cybersecurity breach, leaving up to 150,000 residents' data potentially compromised just as Thanksgiving travel season hits peak chaos.

The Colorado Sun reports the state has offered no timeline for restoration. The timing couldn't be worse: as the Washington Examiner notes, a massive coastal storm is already hammering the eastern U.S. with over 550 flight delays and 366 cancellations as of Thursday morning, plus widespread power outages.

Conservatives have long warned that consolidating citizen data into government-managed digital systems creates unnecessary security risks and privacy vulnerabilities. This breach underscores that concern—government agencies, despite assurances, remain attractive targets for hackers and often lack the cybersecurity infrastructure of private firms.

The incident also highlights a deeper problem: citizens increasingly depend on government apps and digital platforms for basic functions like travel and identification, reducing individual autonomy and creating single points of failure. When the system goes down, there's no backup—no paper alternative, no quick workaround.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state officials acknowledge the security failures that enabled this breach and whether they commit to hardening the system or, better yet, reconsidering the digital-ID model altogether.
