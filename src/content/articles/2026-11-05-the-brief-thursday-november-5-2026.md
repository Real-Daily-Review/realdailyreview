# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, November 5, 2026

The blue wave crashed ashore last night, and Colorado conservatives need to understand exactly what hit them — and what's coming next. Democrats now control both chambers of Congress, and the left is already moving to make the most of it.

---

## Democrats Lawyer Up and Move Fast After Midterm Wins

Democrats aren't wasting a single morning. Reports indicate party leaders are already hiring investigators — a clear signal they intend to use their new congressional majority as a weapon against the Trump administration rather than governing. For Colorado taxpayers, this means the next two years in Washington will be defined by partisan warfare and subpoenas, not spending restraint or regulatory relief. Republicans argue Democrats are prioritizing political revenge over the kitchen-table issues — affordability, energy costs, border security — that actually matter to working Coloradans.

---

## The Midterm Reckoning: What Went Wrong for Republicans

There's no sugarcoating it: Tuesday night was a disaster. Democrats retook both the House and the Senate, winning in areas Republicans had carried before. Conservatives say the rout was, in large part, a referendum on the president — and that Republicans failed to deliver a compelling answer on affordability and cost of living before voters rendered their verdict. United Auto Workers President Shawn Fain is already warning Democrats that union loyalty in 2028 is not guaranteed without action on wages, meaning the left's coalition is already fracturing before they've passed a single bill.

---

## Radical Socialists Enter Congress — Colorado Should Pay Attention

The wave didn't just bring mainstream Democrats back to power. According to reporting from the right, a cohort of hard-left, self-described socialist candidates slipped into Congress on the coattails of anti-Trump sentiment. Conservatives warn these aren't your grandfather's Democrats — they will push for massive government expansion, Green New Deal-style energy mandates, and tax hikes that would hammer Colorado's oil and gas industry, ranchers, and small businesses. Colorado's delegation and conservative advocacy groups need to be ready to fight on day one.

---

## Pro-Life Movement Faces Its Toughest Fight Yet

With Democrats controlling Congress, pro-life advocates are sounding the alarm: a reinstated nationwide federal abortion mandate is now squarely on the table. Conservatives say this is no longer a hypothetical — it is the left's stated legislative priority. For Colorado, which already operates under some of the most permissive abortion laws in the country thanks to Polis-era policy, a federal mandate would lock in that extreme framework nationwide and strip states of any remaining ability to protect unborn life. The Alliance Defending Freedom and other conservative legal groups are preparing for the fight.

---

## Girls' Sports, Federal Power, and What Democrats Will Do With Both

Two female high school athletes in Maine are fighting back after being forced to compete against biological males — and the Alliance Defending Freedom is asking federal officials to intervene. The timing couldn't be more consequential: with Democrats now controlling Congress, conservatives warn that any remaining federal protections for women's sports are at serious risk of being gutted. Colorado parents and coaches who have watched this debate play out in their own school districts should expect the pressure to intensify dramatically.

---

## What to Watch

Republicans need an answer on affordability — fast — or 2028 will look even worse. Watch whether Colorado's Republican congressional members and state party leadership move quickly to reframe a conservative economic agenda centered on energy production, tax relief, and cost-of-living concerns that actually resonate with working Coloradans. And keep your eyes on the new Congress: the first bills Democrats introduce will tell you everything about whether they intend to govern or simply punish.
