# Trump Renews Push to Restrict Mail Ballots; Supreme Court Shows Opening

The Trump administration is doubling down on election integrity, asking the Supreme Court to allow restrictions on mail-in voting after a federal judge blocked new rules ahead of the midterms (9News Denver). Solicitor General D. John Sauer requested that justices permit the administration to prevent the U.S. Postal Service from sending mail ballots to residents of states lacking verified citizen voter lists (Just The News).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue mail-ballot security requires citizenship verification and state-level voter roll accuracy—basic safeguards against fraud and administrative chaos. The Trump administration's position reflects longstanding conservative concerns that loose mail-voting procedures invite vulnerabilities.

**Democrats counter** that such restrictions disenfranchise eligible voters and that mail voting has proven secure. They frame the restrictions as voter suppression.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado conducts mail-only elections statewide. Any Supreme Court ruling on federal mail-ballot authority could reshape how the state administers elections and manages voter eligibility verification—a flashpoint for election-integrity advocates who've long questioned Colorado's ballot-chain-of-custody protocols.

The Washington Examiner cautions that Monday's Supreme Court order, while favorable to Trump, merely criticized a district judge's speed—not endorsing the plan's legality. Congress may need to act legislatively to cement election-integrity standards.

**Watch:** Whether the Supreme Court grants full review and how Colorado election officials respond to any federal mandate.
