# Breckenridge Closes Parking, Launches Shuttle—Another Government "Solution" to a Seasonal Problem

Breckenridge is closing a parking lot and launching a shuttle service between the ice rink and Aspen Alley to manage fall leaf-viewing crowds, according to 9News Denver. The town is also increasing ticketing enforcement to discourage illegal parking during peak season (KDVR Fox31).

The move reflects a familiar government pattern: when private behavior creates congestion, officials respond with closures, new services, and stricter enforcement rather than letting market forces work. Conservatives argue that seasonal tourism surges are temporary and self-correcting—visitors who face hassles simply go elsewhere or adjust timing. A paid parking system or dynamic pricing would address demand without requiring taxpayers to fund shuttle operations year-round.

The shuttle itself raises questions about cost and sustainability. Who funds ongoing operations? Will Breckenridge residents subsidize leaf-peeper convenience? And does closing parking actually solve the problem, or just shift it to nearby neighborhoods?

For Colorado mountain towns, this reflects a broader tension: welcoming tourism revenue while managing its downsides. The conservative approach would emphasize private solutions—parking apps, private shuttle companies, or market-rate fees—over government-run infrastructure.

Watch whether Breckenridge's shuttle becomes a permanent, taxpayer-funded fixture or a seasonal experiment that quietly gets abandoned when costs mount.
