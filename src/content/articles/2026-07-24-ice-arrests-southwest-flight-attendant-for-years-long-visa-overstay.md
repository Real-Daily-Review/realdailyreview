# ICE Arrests Southwest Flight Attendant for Visa Overstay—Enforcement Spotlight Shifts to Workplace

Federal immigration enforcement is working as intended: U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents arrested Southwest Airlines flight attendant Lorenzo Thompson at Nashville International Airport for allegedly overstaying his visa since October 2021 (New York Post).

The arrest underscores a critical gap in visa compliance. Thompson, employed by a major U.S. carrier, reportedly remained in the country years beyond his authorized stay—a violation that should have triggered deportation proceedings long before his workplace detention.

Republicans and immigration hawks argue this case validates the need for stricter employer verification systems and workplace enforcement. The arrest demonstrates that ICE can and should conduct targeted operations at transportation hubs and major employers where visa violators often work. Conservatives contend that lax enforcement of existing immigration law has enabled millions to overstay visas without consequence, undermining both border security and the rule of law.

Democrats and open-borders advocates typically oppose workplace raids, citing concerns about due process and workplace disruption. However, the facts here are straightforward: a visa violation occurred, and federal law was enforced.

For Colorado taxpayers and employers, this case raises questions about visa compliance obligations and whether local businesses are adequately screening workers' immigration status.
