# Fort Hood Killer's Last-Ditch Legal Gambit Delays Justice for 13 Murdered Soldiers

Nidal Hasan, the Army psychiatrist who murdered 13 people at Fort Hood in 2009, filed an emergency motion Tuesday to halt his December 3 firing-squad execution and challenge his death sentence in civilian court, according to The Daily Wire and Washington Examiner.

The former major's legal maneuver—supported by the ACLU and its Kansas affiliate—represents another delay in a case that has already dragged on for over 15 years. Hasan was convicted and sentenced to death by military jury for the 2009 shooting spree that left 13 soldiers dead and others wounded.

Conservatives argue that capital cases involving mass murder on military installations demand swift, certain justice. The ACLU's intervention underscores how anti-death-penalty activism routinely exploits procedural mechanisms to forestall executions, even in cases with overwhelming evidence and military conviction.

This execution has particular resonance for Colorado military families and veterans who understand the stakes of terrorism on American soil. Fort Hood remains one of the deadliest mass shootings at a U.S. military installation.

Watch whether federal courts grant Hasan's stay request and how quickly the appeals process moves—or stalls.
