# Holdout Juror in High-Profile Murder Trial Faces Mob Justice After Breaking Silence

A Massachusetts juror who refused to acquit a mother accused of killing her three children is now in protective custody—a chilling reminder that jury intimidation and mob pressure can undermine the justice system itself.

Michael P. Desronvil, 48, was the lone holdout in the Lindsay Clancy trial, believing prosecutors presented "enough proof that she knew exactly what she was doing" (New York Post). Eleven other jurors wanted to find Clancy not criminally responsible due to postpartum mental illness. After Desronvil spoke publicly about his reasoning, a fundraiser for him flooded with donations—and his attorney moved him to a "secure location" (Washington Examiner).

This case exposes a dangerous trend: jurors who vote their conscience face public vilification and safety threats. One juror who favored acquittal laughed when discussing the trial's focus on postpartum mental health, suggesting the verdict became less about evidence than ideology (Daily Wire).

**The Colorado angle:** While this trial occurred in Massachusetts, it underscores why jury independence matters everywhere. Colorado courts depend on jurors willing to apply the law fairly, not buckle to social pressure or activist narratives about mental health defenses.

Conservatives should watch whether media and activist groups continue targeting jurors who reject progressive framings of criminal responsibility—a trend that corrodes the rule of law.
