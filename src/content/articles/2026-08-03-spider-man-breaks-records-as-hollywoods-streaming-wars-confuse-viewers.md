# Spider-Man Smashes Box Office, But Hollywood's Streaming Wars Leave Viewers Confused

Tom Holland's latest web-slinging blockbuster is proving Americans still pack theaters for big-budget spectacle—despite Hollywood's obsession with streaming fragmentation.

"Spider-Man: Brand New Day" crushed expectations with a $355 million North American opening, the second-biggest debut ever, according to Breitbart. The film has already crossed $927 million globally, signaling that theatrical releases remain a powerful draw when studios actually commit to the format.

But the victory masks a deeper problem: the so-called "Sony-Marvel-Disney debacle" (New York Post) over streaming rights. Viewers face a bewildering maze of competing platforms—Netflix, Disney+, and others—each claiming exclusive windows, making it harder for consumers to simply watch what they want, when they want it.

This is what happens when government-enabled corporate consolidation meets digital-age monopoly thinking. Instead of competing on price and service, studios use licensing agreements and exclusivity deals to extract maximum revenue from fragmented audiences. It's the opposite of free-market competition.

For Colorado families, the takeaway is simple: theatrical releases remain the best value for blockbuster entertainment, while the streaming wars continue rewinding consumer choice.

Watch whether studios finally abandon the streaming-exclusivity model in favor of day-and-date releases that actually respect paying customers.
