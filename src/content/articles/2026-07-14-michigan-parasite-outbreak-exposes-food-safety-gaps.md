# Michigan Cyclosporiasis Outbreak Raises Food Safety Questions

A parasite outbreak sickening thousands in Michigan is exposing gaps in America's food safety oversight—and raising questions about supply chain transparency that Colorado produce growers and consumers should monitor closely.

Michigan health officials are investigating lettuce and salad greens as the likely source of cyclosporiasis, a parasitic illness causing severe gastrointestinal distress (9News Denver). Taco Bell is also under investigation for a possible connection to the outbreak, one of the largest cyclosporiasis cases on record in the U.S. (Washington Examiner). The parasite is typically contracted through food or water contaminated with human feces.

**The conservative concern:** This outbreak underscores why government food safety protocols must be effective—not just bureaucratic theater. While the FDA and state agencies investigate, questions linger about whether current regulations actually prevent contamination or simply create compliance paperwork for producers. Colorado's agricultural sector, which supplies fresh produce nationwide, depends on consumer confidence in food safety. Overregulation without real prevention is counterproductive; underregulation invites disaster.

Republicans and industry advocates argue that transparency and traceability—not more red tape—are the real solutions. Producers need incentives to maintain rigorous standards, not punitive regulations that drive small farms out of business.
