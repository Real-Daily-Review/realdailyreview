# Trump's Legal Enforcer Ed Martin Exits DOJ to Fight Election Legal Battles

Ed Martin, the Trump administration's aggressive legal hawk who led the Justice Department's Weaponization Working Group, is leaving his post as U.S. Pardon Attorney to wage legal battles for Republicans in the 2026 midterms and 2028 presidential race.

Martin, 56, served as interim U.S. Attorney for D.C. from January through May 2025 before Trump moved him to the pardon office. His departure signals the administration's pivot toward mobilizing legal firepower outside government channels ahead of critical election cycles (Washington Examiner, New York Post).

The move underscores Trump's commitment to contesting election-related legal challenges that have dogged Republicans—a priority conservatives argue is essential after years of what they view as weaponized prosecutions and election disputes. Martin's track record leading the DOJ's weaponization probe made him a trusted figure among Trump loyalists concerned about institutional bias in federal law enforcement (Just The News).

For Colorado Republicans, Martin's exit reflects the broader national fight over election integrity and legal strategy heading into 2026—a midterm cycle where GOP control of the Senate and House will be contested. The state's Republican Party will be watching how national legal resources align with Colorado's own election challenges and ballot initiatives.
