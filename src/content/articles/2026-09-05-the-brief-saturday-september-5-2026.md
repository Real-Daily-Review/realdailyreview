# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, September 5, 2026

Colorado conservatives, the week closes with a president delivering for ranchers, a federal judge blocking election integrity, and a reminder that the left's cultural project has always come at the expense of common sense. Here's what matters this morning.

---

## Trump Cuts the Red Tape for Colorado Ranchers

President Trump signed an executive order Friday allowing farmers and ranchers to slaughter their own livestock and process their own food for sale across state lines — a direct shot at the regulatory stranglehold that has squeezed independent producers for decades. For Colorado's cattle country, this is real relief. Republicans argue that bloated federal food-processing rules have long protected big meatpackers at the expense of small ranchers. This order puts the market back in the hands of the people who actually work the land.

---

## Federal Judge Blocks USPS Mail-In Ballot Rules

A federal judge appointed by President Obama extended a block Friday on new U.S. Postal Service mail-in ballot requirements, preventing the rules from taking effect ahead of November's midterm elections. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani's ruling keeps the door open to the kind of ballot chaos that conservatives have warned about for years. Republicans argue that tightening mail-in ballot procedures is basic election hygiene — and that activist judges blocking those safeguards are doing Democrats' political work for them. Colorado, already a universal mail-ballot state, should be paying close attention.

---

## Illegal Alien Busted for Voter Fraud in Battleground State

Exclusive footage obtained by Fox News shows Homeland Security Investigations agents executing arrests as the Trump administration ramps up a nationwide crackdown on non-citizen voting ahead of the 2026 midterms. Conservatives have argued for years that lax enforcement of immigration and voter registration laws creates exactly this kind of vulnerability. Every fraudulent vote cancels out a legitimate one — and Coloradans who have watched their state's elections grow increasingly difficult to audit should see this as a warning, not a one-off.

---

## Witkoff and Kushner Head to Moscow — Skeptics Have a Point

The expected meeting between Trump envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner and Russian officials is framed as a probe for any possible movement toward a negotiated settlement to end the now four-and-a-half year war in Ukraine. Conservative foreign policy hawks are right to be cautious: Putin has shown no credible interest in a real settlement, and high-profile American visits risk handing Moscow a propaganda win without extracting meaningful concessions. Good diplomacy requires leverage — not goodwill tours.

## Gloria Steinem's Legacy: What the Left Built, and What It Cost

A sharp new piece making the rounds argues that Steinem's feminist project amounted to a wholesale rejection of womanhood — summed up in the observation that women became the men they wanted to marry. Conservatives say the cultural left spent 50 years telling women that family, motherhood, and femininity were traps — and now the data on loneliness, declining birth rates, and female dissatisfaction is the bill coming due. Colorado's families deserve a culture that respects, not demeans, the choices women actually make.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the fallout from Trump's ranching executive order — expect the regulatory lobby and big agriculture interests to push back hard in the courts. The extended block on USPS mail-in ballot rules is one to track closely as November approaches; Colorado's all-mail election system makes any national ruling on ballot procedures directly relevant to every voter in this state. And watch whether the voter fraud arrests expand — if non-citizen voting is as widespread as enforcement actions suggest, the midterms just got a lot more consequential.
