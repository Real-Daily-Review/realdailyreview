# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, August 7, 2026

Colorado conservatives head into the weekend with a full plate: federal immigration orders drawing legal fire, a Lakewood city council playing power games with its own voters, and Democrats already circling two red Colorado congressional districts. Pay attention — the left isn't taking a summer break, and neither should you.

---

## Trump Moves on Birthright Citizenship — Again

President Trump signed executive orders targeting birthright citizenship and so-called "birth tourism," doubling down on immigration enforcement even after the Supreme Court moved to protect birthright citizenship. Republicans argue the practice has been exploited for decades and that the 14th Amendment was never intended as an open invitation for non-citizens to secure automatic citizenship for their children on U.S. soil. For Colorado border communities and taxpayers already absorbing the costs of unchecked immigration, the fight over this policy is far from over.

---

## Lakewood Council Punishes Voters for Voting Wrong

After Lakewood residents used a citizens' initiative to push back on zoning policy in April's special election, the city council responded by piling new burdens onto the initiative process itself. Some residents are calling it exactly what it looks like: retaliation. This is government overreach at the local level — elected officials rigging the rules to make it harder for citizens to check their power. Colorado conservatives should be watching Lakewood closely, because what happens there can spread.

---

## Colorado School Enrollment Drops — and the Money Follows

Colorado is facing a significant enrollment decline in its public schools, and because state funding is directly tied to student headcount, that means fewer dollars flowing to districts. Rather than treating this as an opportunity to right-size bloated education bureaucracies, expect the left to use it as a pretext for more spending and tax hikes. Conservatives should demand transparency on where existing per-pupil dollars are actually going before anyone talks about backfilling a shrinking enrollment with taxpayer money.

---

## Democrats Put CO-3 and CO-4 in Their Crosshairs

The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee has added Colorado's 3rd and 4th congressional districts to its official "districts in play" list for the midterms. These are red Colorado districts — rural, energy-producing, agriculture-heavy — and Democrats are making clear they intend to compete for them. Republicans argue the DCCC's interest reflects a national strategy to flip seats that would give progressives more leverage over energy policy, gun rights, and federal land management. Colorado ranchers and energy workers should treat this as a five-alarm warning.

---

## Lakewood Zoning Fight Meets a Familiar Pattern

The Colorado Springs municipal election lawsuit — challenging the city's practice of holding elections in April — adds another front to the ongoing battle over when and how Coloradans vote. Lower-turnout April elections have long been criticized by conservatives and good-government advocates alike as a tool that benefits incumbents and insulates local officials from accountability. If the courts side with the plaintiffs, it could force higher-turnout November elections — and more accountability for city hall.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the DCCC's push into CO-3 and CO-4 — outside money is coming, and Republican incumbents will need strong grassroots support to hold those seats. Watch the Lakewood council's initiative restrictions for any legal challenges from residents; this is a test case for how far local governments can go to silence voters. And on the federal immigration orders, expect the courts to be the next battlefield — Colorado's congressional delegation should be on record about where they stand.
