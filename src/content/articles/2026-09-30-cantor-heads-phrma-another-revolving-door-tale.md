# Former GOP Leader Cantor Heads Pharma Lobby—Raising Questions About Corporate-Political Revolving Door

Eric Cantor, the ex-House Majority Leader from Virginia, has landed the top job at PhRMA, the pharmaceutical industry's most powerful lobbying outfit, according to Breitbart and the Daily Caller. The move exemplifies what conservatives often criticize: the revolving door between Capitol Hill and K Street, where politicians cash in on their government connections.

Cantor's appointment matters for Colorado. PhRMA aggressively lobbies on drug pricing, patent protections, and regulatory policy—issues that directly affect Colorado seniors, taxpayers, and state healthcare costs. The organization has consistently opposed price-negotiation measures and transparency requirements that conservatives and some Republicans support as market-driven alternatives to government price controls.

The Daily Caller frames Cantor's move as emblematic of "swamp" dynamics—a Republican leader leveraging his legislative record and political network for a lucrative private-sector perch. Critics argue this incentivizes politicians to prioritize industry interests over constituent welfare while in office.

Conservatives should watch whether Cantor's PhRMA leadership influences Republican positions on drug pricing and healthcare reform—and whether Colorado's GOP delegation maintains independence from pharmaceutical lobbying pressure on these kitchen-table issues.
