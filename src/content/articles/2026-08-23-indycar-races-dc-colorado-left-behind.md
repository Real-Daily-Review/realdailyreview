# IndyCar Roars Into D.C., But Colorado Fans Left in the Pit

IndyCar is racing through Washington, D.C., for the first time ever—but Colorado's motorsports enthusiasts won't find the action on local tracks. The "Freedom 250 Grand Prix" will tear through the National Mall on the weekend, with reigning champion Alex Palou starting from pole position and eyeing his fifth IndyCar championship (9News Denver, Just The News).

Pre-race programming kicks off at 11:30 a.m., with cars hitting the starting grid at noon and driver introductions at 12:20 p.m. The event marks a historic moment for the series, bringing high-octane racing to the nation's capital for the first time.

For Colorado, the story underscores a broader reality: major motorsports events continue to bypass the state despite its strong racing heritage and fan base. While D.C. gets its inaugural IndyCar spectacle, Colorado's track operators and racing communities remain sidelined—a missed opportunity for tourism dollars, local business revenue, and the kind of marquee events that draw national attention and investment.

The real question for Colorado conservatives and taxpayers: why isn't our state competing harder to land premium sporting events that generate economic activity without requiring massive government subsidies?
