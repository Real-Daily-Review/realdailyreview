# Colorado Farm-Labor Law Exposes Legislature's Empty Rhetoric on Worker Rights

Colorado lawmakers just proved they don't mean what they say about protecting farm workers. A new state law raising the overtime threshold for agricultural employees contradicts years of Democratic rhetoric about worker justice—and it's a cautionary tale for any Coloradan who believes politicians' promises on labor policy.

The Colorado Sun editorial board notes the law represents a step backward, undercutting legislative praise for farm workers while simultaneously weakening their legal protections. The threshold increase effectively reduces overtime eligibility for a vulnerable workforce that already faces significant wage and safety challenges in Colorado's agricultural sector.

Republicans argue that excessive overtime mandates drive up labor costs for farmers already operating on thin margins, potentially forcing automation or reduced hiring. They contend market-based wages and voluntary agreements serve workers better than rigid government mandates. Democrats counter that farm workers deserve the same overtime protections as other laborers.

The real issue: Colorado's Democratic supermajority can't square the circle between progressive labor rhetoric and business-friendly policy that protects agricultural interests. Farm workers—many of them Latino and immigrant families critical to Colorado's rural economy—are caught in the middle of partisan posturing.

Watch whether Republican legislators will use this hypocrisy to challenge future Democratic labor bills, or whether the media will hold the majority accountable for abandoning workers when it matters.
