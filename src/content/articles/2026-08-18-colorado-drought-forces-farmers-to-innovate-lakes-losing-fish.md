# Colorado's Water Crisis Deepens: Farmers Seek Drought-Resistant Crops as Lakes Die

Colorado's relentless drought is forcing hard choices—and the state's agricultural sector is fighting back with innovation rather than waiting for government rescue.

The Rye Resurgence Project is working to establish a market for drought-tolerant rye grain in the San Luis Valley, where water scarcity threatens the viability of traditional farming (CPR News). The initiative recognizes a fundamental reality: Colorado farmers cannot depend on unlimited water allocations. Instead of lobbying for more government water transfers, agricultural producers are pivoting toward crops engineered to survive on less.

Meanwhile, the drought's toll on recreation is mounting. Colorado Parks and Wildlife has lifted fishing limits at multiple lakes as water levels plummet and fisheries collapse (KDVR Fox31). Another recreational asset is slipping away—a direct hit to Colorado's outdoor economy and quality of life.

Conservatives argue this underscores why Colorado needs market-driven solutions and property-rights clarity on water, not more federal environmental restrictions or top-down mandates that strangle agricultural productivity. The state's water crisis demands innovation from farmers and ranchers, not bureaucratic micromanagement.

Watch whether state lawmakers prioritize removing regulatory barriers to drought-resistant agriculture or instead double down on restrictions that make farming less viable.
