# Denver Airport Braces for Thanksgiving Crush as Higher Costs Push Travelers to Alternatives

Denver International Airport and TSA are gearing up for one of the year's busiest travel periods, with Thanksgiving week expected to draw heavy passenger volume across all modes of transportation.

The holiday rush reflects a broader shift in traveler behavior. According to the Colorado Sun, higher gas and airline ticket prices are pushing some Coloradans toward buses and trains instead of flying or driving solo. Roads will still see peak congestion the two days immediately before Thanksgiving, KDVR reports, as families prioritize getting home over saving on fuel.

**The conservative angle:** Rising energy costs—driven partly by Biden administration policies restricting domestic oil and gas development—are hitting Colorado families' wallets during peak travel season. When fuel prices climb, working families face harder choices about holiday plans. Meanwhile, the shift toward mass transit highlights how market forces work when prices rise; consumers adapt. The question for Colorado policymakers is whether they'll support energy policies that keep fuel affordable, or continue backing restrictions that make travel costlier for ordinary Coloradans.

Republicans argue that domestic energy independence and expanded drilling on federal lands would stabilize prices. Democrats counter that climate concerns justify higher energy costs as part of the transition away from fossil fuels.
