## Tupac Shakur's Killer Finally Faces Justice — 30 Years Later

After nearly three decades, a Las Vegas jury needed just three hours to convict a former gang leader of first-degree murder in the 1996 killing of rapper Tupac Shakur. The verdict closes one of the most notorious cold cases in American criminal history. It's a reminder that the justice system, however slow, can still deliver accountability — and that violent crime has consequences, even when powerful people spend decades trying to avoid them.

## Colorado Trooper Stops Wrong-Way Driver the Hard Way

A Colorado State Patrol trooper used his own vehicle to stop a wrong-way driver on Interstate 76 on Sunday — a split-second decision that likely saved lives. That's the kind of decisive, boots-on-the-ground law enforcement Colorado taxpayers fund and depend on. ## Fort Collins Celebration Turns Deadly

A Saturday morning Tour de Fat event in Fort Collins ended in tragedy when a pickup truck crashed into multiple people near Walrus Ice Cream. Witnesses described a tree shaking from the force of impact. Two juveniles were also arrested in connection with a separate Denver shooting that occurred Friday. Coloradans deserve safe streets and public spaces — and these incidents underscore that public safety must remain a governing priority, not an afterthought to social-justice policy experiments.

## Trump Delivers the Biggest Drug Price Drop in 60 Years

The Trump administration has unlocked a dramatic prescription drug price reduction — one report cited a drop from $1,000 to $149 on a key medication — marking what supporters call the largest drug price cut in six decades. For Colorado seniors on fixed incomes, ranching families without gold-plated insurance, and small-business owners paying out of pocket, this is real, tangible relief. Republicans argue that market-oriented pressure and executive action achieved what years of Democrat price-control schemes never did.
