# Colorado Should Embrace AI Jobs, Not Regulate Them Into Oblivion

Colorado has a chance to lead on artificial intelligence—if lawmakers resist the urge to strangle the industry with red tape.

The state's AI and data center sector is generating real economic opportunity, but progressives are pushing apprenticeship mandates and regulatory frameworks that could slow job creation. According to Complete Colorado, a recent Magellan Strategies survey shows Coloradans recognize genuine reasons to bet on AI rather than panic about it.

The Colorado Sun's opinion piece advocates for AI-enabled apprenticeships, framing them as worker-training vehicles. That's reasonable—but only if participation is voluntary and driven by market demand, not government mandate. Conservatives worry that well-intentioned apprenticeship programs often become costly bureaucratic overlays that employers avoid, ultimately hurting the young workers they claim to help.

The real issue: Colorado's tech workforce is competitive. Companies are already investing in training because talent shortages demand it. Heavy-handed state regulation risks pushing data centers and AI operations to Texas, Arizona, or other business-friendly states offering lower taxes and lighter regulatory touch.

Republicans and free-market advocates argue Colorado should clear regulatory obstacles, keep taxes competitive, and let employers and workers negotiate training arrangements directly—not through government-mandated programs.

**Watch whether the legislature attempts to impose AI-specific regulations or apprenticeship requirements that could drive the industry elsewhere.**
