# Stock Market Boom Masks Real Pain for Colorado Families

Wall Street's record highs are a mirage for ordinary Coloradans struggling with mortgages, groceries, and job security. The Washington Examiner argues that aggregate economic metrics—stock prices, GDP growth—obscure the reality facing working families: stagnant wages, rising costs of living, and economic anxiety that no headline can spin away.

Conservatives contend that headline economic data favors Wall Street over Main Street, and that true prosperity requires policies rewarding local innovation and entrepreneurship rather than Wall Street speculation. Republicans argue the Biden-Harris administration's regulatory overreach and spending sprees have inflated prices while enriching financial elites disconnected from everyday Americans.

For Colorado specifically, this disconnect matters. Energy workers in the Western Slope, ranchers facing feed costs, and small-business owners in mountain towns don't feel wealthier when the S&amp;P 500 hits new records. They feel the pinch of inflation, higher property taxes, and regulatory burdens that suppress job creation outside Denver's tech corridor.

The real test: whether Colorado voters will demand pro-growth policies that lift working families, not just portfolio holders.
