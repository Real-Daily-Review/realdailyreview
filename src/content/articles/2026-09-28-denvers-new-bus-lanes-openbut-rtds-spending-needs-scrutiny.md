# Bus Lanes Open on East Colfax—But Questions Linger on RTD's Return on Investment

Denver's new bus-only lanes on East Colfax opened Sunday after two years of construction that hammered local businesses, and while merchants hope for recovery, conservatives should ask whether RTD's spending delivers real results for taxpayers footing the bill.

The Regional Transportation District's East Colfax Bus Rapid Transit project carved dedicated lanes from Broadway to Colorado Boulevard, restricting general traffic to boost bus speeds and ridership. Businesses along the corridor endured the construction gauntlet and are now betting the transit upgrade will draw customers back (KDVR Fox31, 9News).

Here's the conservative concern: RTD has a track record of cost overruns and ridership shortfalls. Before celebrating, Denver taxpayers deserve transparency on project costs, actual versus projected ridership numbers, and whether this lane configuration—which reduces road capacity for private vehicles—genuinely serves the broader commuting public or simply subsidizes a transit system that struggles with efficiency.

The bus lanes also raise a broader question about urban planning priorities: Should government restrict private transportation options to promote mass transit, or should infrastructure investment focus on what the market and commuters actually demand?

Watch whether RTD releases detailed ridership data and cost accounting within six months—and whether Denver's business community sees the promised recovery or continued struggles.
