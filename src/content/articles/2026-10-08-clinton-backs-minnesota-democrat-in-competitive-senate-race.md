# Clinton Endorsement Signals Democratic Scramble in Midwest Senate Race

Hillary Clinton's endorsement of Minnesota Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan in the U.S. Senate race underscores Democratic anxiety over holding competitive seats in purple states (Fox News Politics). Flanagan faces Republican Michele Tafoya, a former NFL player and sports broadcaster, in a matchup that could reshape the upper chamber's balance.

The endorsement highlights a broader Democratic strategy: deploy national figures to energize base voters in races where incumbents or candidates lack sufficient name recognition or momentum. Republicans counter that such endorsements often backfire, reminding voters of unpopular national Democratic leadership and failed policies—from inflation to border chaos to regulatory overreach.

For Colorado, this Minnesota race carries indirect weight. If Democrats lose Senate seats in the Midwest, the chamber shifts rightward, potentially blocking further federal land grabs, EPA overreach on water and energy, and tax-and-spend legislation that hits Colorado's energy sector and small businesses. Conversely, a Democratic hold signals continued pressure on Colorado Republicans to defend their own seats against well-funded progressive challengers.

The race also tests whether Clinton—a polarizing figure among independents and rural voters—remains an asset or liability for Democratic candidates in 2024 and beyond.
