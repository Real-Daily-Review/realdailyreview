# America's 250th Birthday Sparks Debate Over Nation's True Founding

Colorado conservatives should brace for a civics lesson wrapped in political messaging as America approaches its 250th anniversary in 2026—but not everyone agrees on what year we're actually celebrating.

National Review questions whether 1776 is the correct founding date, citing historian Willmoore Kendall's arguments that America's true birthday may differ from conventional wisdom. Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner draws inspiration from the 1876 Centennial International Exhibition in Philadelphia, where nearly 10 million visitors celebrated a century of American achievement in agriculture, machinery, mining, and industry—a model of private enterprise and innovation that conservatives say modern America has largely abandoned.

The broader conservative argument: America's founding principles—limited government, free markets, and individual achievement—built the nation into a global powerhouse. Today's progressive regulatory state and hostility toward business success (exemplified by attacks on figures like Elon Musk) represent a fundamental departure from those founding values.

The Daily Wire frames this as "America's War On Success," noting that even landmark achievements like SpaceX's IPO face ideological headwinds rather than celebration.

**Watch for:** How the Biden-Harris administration and Democratic Congress use the 250th anniversary narrative to either defend or distance themselves from progressive policies that conservatives argue betray America's founding vision.
