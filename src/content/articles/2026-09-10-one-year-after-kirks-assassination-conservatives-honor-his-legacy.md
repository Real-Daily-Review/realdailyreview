# Conservative Activist Charlie Kirk Remembered One Year After Assassination

A year after Charlie Kirk's assassination on September 10, 2025—during Turning Point USA's "American Comeback Tour" stop in Colorado—the conservative movement is honoring the activist's legacy with memorials nationwide.

Turning Point USA unveiled an 8-foot statue at its Phoenix headquarters, while the White House released a tribute video highlighting Kirk's importance to the conservative movement (Just The News). Utah's governor declared September 10 "Charlie Kirk Day," and conservative outlets across the right have called for Americans to embrace Kirk's model of public persuasion and campus activism.

National Review urged young people to adopt "his mode of public persuasion as a model for how to conduct themselves in the public square," while the Daily Caller emphasized continuing Kirk's work "one campus and one conversation at a time."

Kirk, a prolific activist and founder of Turning Point USA, had made Colorado a key stop on his tour when he was killed. His suspect's trial remains ongoing, according to 9News Denver.

The memorials underscore how Kirk's assassination galvanized conservative organizing—particularly among young voters—at a moment when the right faces sustained pressure on college campuses and in the culture.
