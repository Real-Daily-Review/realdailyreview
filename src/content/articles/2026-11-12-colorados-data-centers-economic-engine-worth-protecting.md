# Colorado's Data Centers: The Infrastructure Backbone Powering America

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are the unglamorous but essential engine driving modern commerce, and they deserve recognition—not regulatory hostility—from state policymakers.

According to Complete Colorado, these facilities represent critical infrastructure that enables everything from cloud computing to financial transactions to artificial intelligence. Yet data centers often face opposition from environmental activists and progressive regulators who view them as energy-intensive threats, despite their relatively modest footprint compared to their economic output.

The conservative case is straightforward: Colorado's data center industry creates high-wage jobs, attracts capital investment, and positions the state as a technology hub. These facilities require reliable electricity and water access—resources Colorado has in abundance. Rather than impose new restrictions or carbon mandates that would drive this economic engine elsewhere, state leaders should streamline permitting and protect property rights for data center operators.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that data centers consume significant water and electricity, raising concerns about sustainability and climate impact. They argue for stricter environmental reviews and renewable energy requirements.

The real question for Colorado taxpayers: Will state government nurture this competitive advantage, or will it regulate and tax these businesses into neighboring states? Watch for any proposed data center restrictions or new energy mandates in the 2027 legislative session.
