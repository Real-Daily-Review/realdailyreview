# Colorado Voters Face Five Constitutional Amendments—Here's What's at Stake

Colorado conservatives are pushing back hard against Democratic overreach with a slate of constitutional amendments that would protect energy development, hunting rights, and election integrity.

**Amendment 81** would require police and prosecutors to notify the U.S. Department of Homeland Security when someone charged with crimes is in the country illegally or has unconfirmed immigration status—a direct response to sanctuary policies Democrats have championed.

**Amendment 82** enshrines a right to natural gas in the state constitution, directly challenging Democratic leaders' push to phase out gas use. Conservatives argue this protects Colorado's energy independence and working families' heating costs from ideological mandates.

**Amendment 83**, backed by hunting interests and a conservative law firm, adds a constitutional right to hunt and fish—safeguarding Colorado's outdoor heritage against regulatory creep.

**Amendment 84** requires voters to write the last four digits of their Social Security number, driver's license, or ID on mail-ballot envelopes. Republicans say this strengthens election security; Democrats oppose it as a barrier to voting.

**Amendment 85** mandates ballot language be understandable to an eighth grader—a common-sense measure to prevent deliberately confusing ballot wording.

Taxpayers should watch whether Democratic-controlled state government moves to circumvent these amendments if they pass.
