# Strait of Hormuz Tensions Spike as Iran Demands "Service Fees" for Shipping

**Global energy chaos threatens Colorado's economy as hostile actors exploit shipping lanes.**

A cargo ship reported being struck by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz as Iran and Oman edge toward a deal that would allow both nations to collect "service fees" from vessels transiting the critical waterway, according to the New York Post. The arrangement amounts to a protection racket on one of the world's most vital energy corridors—a development that could ripple directly into Colorado's wallet.

The U.S. and Iran are making opposing claims about whether diplomatic talks are underway (9News Denver), but the real story is straightforward: hostile actors are weaponizing chokepoints that control global oil supplies. When Middle Eastern instability drives energy prices higher, Colorado families pay more at the pump, and energy workers—already battered by years of regulatory hostility from Denver—face further uncertainty.

Republicans argue the Biden-Harris administration's weak posture toward Iran emboldened this aggression. The Obama-era nuclear deal, which Trump withdrew from, failed to constrain Iranian ambitions. Now we're watching the consequences play out in real time.

Conservatives and Colorado taxpayers should monitor whether the administration will enforce freedom of navigation or capitulate to Iranian extortion.
