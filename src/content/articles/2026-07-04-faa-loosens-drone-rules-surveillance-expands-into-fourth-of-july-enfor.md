# Federal Drone Surveillance Expands Into Fourth of July Enforcement

The FAA's loosened drone regulations are now enabling cities and counties to deploy surveillance aircraft to police illegal fireworks during Independence Day celebrations, according to *The Daily Wire*. Under new "Drone as First Responder" (DFR) initiatives, public safety agencies across the country have accelerated access to drone-based enforcement by cutting through bureaucratic approval processes.

Conservatives should be wary of this creeping surveillance infrastructure. What starts as fireworks enforcement rarely stays limited—government agencies have a track record of mission creep, expanding surveillance tools far beyond their original purpose. Colorado counties and municipalities considering DFR programs should ask hard questions: Who monitors the monitors? What data is collected and retained? How are citizens' privacy rights protected?

The federal government's eagerness to loosen drone deployment rules while tightening citizen oversight reflects progressive governance priorities: more government eyes in the sky, fewer guardrails on state power. Republicans and civil libertarians argue that expanded drone surveillance, even for public safety, sets a dangerous precedent for warrantless monitoring of Americans exercising their constitutional rights.

Coloradans should watch whether Denver, Boulder, or other left-leaning municipalities rush to adopt DFR programs—and demand transparency on surveillance scope and data handling before it's too late.
