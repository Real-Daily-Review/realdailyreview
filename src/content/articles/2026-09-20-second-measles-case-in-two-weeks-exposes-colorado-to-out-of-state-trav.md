# Measles Exposure at Denver Transit Hub Raises Questions About Border Security and Vaccine Verification

An out-of-state traveler exposed Coloradans to measles at Union Station in Denver and in Steamboat Springs, marking the second such incident in two weeks as cases surge nationwide, according to the Colorado Sun and KDVR Fox31 Denver. The CDC is investigating the exposure.

This is the second measles case linked to out-of-state travel in Colorado within 14 days—a troubling pattern that raises hard questions about border screening and disease control at ports of entry. While Colorado health officials have identified exposure sites and are urging those who may have been affected to seek testing, conservatives argue the federal government's lax enforcement of entry protocols has left states vulnerable to preventable disease outbreaks.

Republicans and taxpayers should demand answers: Why aren't federal agencies conducting adequate health screening of incoming travelers? Colorado shouldn't bear the cost of managing outbreaks caused by failures in national border and entry-point oversight. The state health department's response is competent, but the root problem lies in Washington's refusal to enforce basic public-health protocols at airports and transit hubs.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand federal accountability—or whether this becomes another unfunded mandate dumped on state taxpayers.
