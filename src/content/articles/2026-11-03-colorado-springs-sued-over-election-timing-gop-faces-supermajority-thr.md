# Colorado Springs Faces Lawsuit Over April Election Timing—First Test of 2025 Voting Law

Colorado Springs is being sued for scheduling municipal elections in April, marking the first enforcement action since a voting law took effect in 2025, according to *Complete Colorado*. The lawsuit challenges whether the city's election timing complies with state requirements—a technical dispute that could reshape how Colorado municipalities conduct elections going forward.

Meanwhile, Republicans face a steeper climb statewide. The *Denver Post* reports that Colorado's GOP, already a deep minority in the legislature, confronts an "existential question of relevance" as Democrats threaten supermajority control. Sen. John Carson (R-Highlands Ranch) warned that losing key races could push Colorado toward "almost a one-party state"—a scenario that would give Democrats unchecked power to raise taxes, expand regulations, and advance progressive priorities without meaningful Republican pushback.

**The conservative concern is clear:** A Democratic supermajority in Denver would eliminate the legislative brake on spending, gun control measures, and environmental overreach that has already made Colorado less business-friendly than neighboring states. Republicans argue that competitive elections and divided government protect taxpayers and preserve individual liberty.

**Watch whether Colorado Springs prevails in court and whether Republicans can hold any competitive state senate seats in November.**
