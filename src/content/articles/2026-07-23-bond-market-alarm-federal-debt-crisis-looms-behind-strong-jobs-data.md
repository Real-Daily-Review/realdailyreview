# Bond Market Turmoil Signals Alarm Over U.S. Debt, Not Economic Strength

Despite strong jobs data and cooling inflation, the bond market is flashing red—and conservatives say Washington's runaway spending is the real culprit, not geopolitical tensions.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, unemployment claims have hit their lowest level in over 50 years, and core inflation has fallen to its lowest point since March, defying expectations. The International Monetary Fund has even upgraded U.S. economic growth forecasts. Yet bond yields are spiking and markets remain volatile.

The disconnect reveals what fiscal hawks have warned for years: the bond market is pricing in the unsustainable reality of federal debt. While Democrats tout strong economic headlines, conservatives argue the underlying problem—massive government spending and trillion-dollar deficits—remains unaddressed. The bond market doesn't lie; it's demanding higher yields to compensate for the risk of holding U.S. Treasury debt.

For Colorado, this matters. Rising bond yields increase borrowing costs for state and local governments, squeeze business investment, and threaten the purchasing power of retirees and savers. Energy companies and small businesses already facing regulatory headwinds will find capital even more expensive.

Watch whether Congress moves to rein in spending or continues the fiscal denial that's spooked the bond market.
