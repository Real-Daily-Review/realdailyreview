# Trump Administration Eyes Venezuelan Oil Deal as Domestic Shale Production Faces Long-Term Decline

A potential U.S.-Venezuela oil agreement could shore up American energy dominance, but it raises hard questions about property rights and free-market principles that conservatives should scrutinize carefully.

The deal hinges on accessing Venezuelan reserves as domestic shale production inevitably declines over time, according to *Just The News*. Proponents argue diverse foreign resources are essential to maintaining U.S. energy leadership globally.

But National Review flags a core conservative concern: the cynicism of negotiating with a regime that has systematically stolen American company assets. Trump has repeatedly condemned Chávez-era nationalizations—yet a Venezuela oil deal could effectively nullify American firms' legitimate claims to seized property. That's a troubling precedent for rule of law and property rights, the foundation of free markets.

The deeper tension: does energy security justify compromising on the principle that governments must respect private property? Or does abandoning expropriated American companies' claims undermine the stable legal order that made the U.S. prosperous in the first place?

For Colorado, the stakes are real. Our energy sector depends on predictable property protections and contract enforcement. Any U.S. deal normalizing Venezuelan asset seizures sends a dangerous signal about government power over private holdings—at home and abroad.

Watch whether the Trump administration demands restitution for seized American oil companies as a condition of any Venezuela agreement.
