# Colorado's 'Hail Alley' Takes Another Beating—and Taxpayers Will Foot the Cleanup Bill

Softball-sized hail hammered Colorado's Front Range and Eastern Plains for the second consecutive day, leaving a trail of destruction that will drain state and federal disaster resources while farmers and homeowners face massive uninsured losses.

The severe weather pounded communities across "Hail Alley" on June 24, with Fort Morgan enduring egg-sized hail, Akron battling flooding, and Fleming hit by a tornado, according to 9News Denver. CPR News reported witnesses describing the devastation as apocalyptic—"It sounded like everything was just breaking."

Back-to-back severe weather events expose a hard reality: Colorado's agricultural heartland and growing suburban areas remain vulnerable to climate volatility that no amount of green-energy spending can prevent. While progressive policymakers tout climate initiatives, rural Coloradans are left picking up the pieces with inadequate insurance coverage and stretched emergency budgets.

Conservatives argue that instead of pursuing costly climate regulations that burden Colorado businesses and energy workers, state leaders should focus on disaster preparedness, resilient infrastructure, and supporting farmers and ranchers directly through tax relief and streamlined recovery assistance.
