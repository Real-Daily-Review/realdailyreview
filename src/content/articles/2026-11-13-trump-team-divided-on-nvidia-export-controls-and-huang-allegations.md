# Trump Officials Split on Nvidia Chip Export Controls as Huang Controversy Swirls

The Trump administration faces an internal clash over whether Nvidia founder Jensen Huang violated export restrictions on advanced AI chips to China—a dispute that exposes competing priorities between national security hawks and Silicon Valley-friendly tech advisors.

National Review has questioned why the U.S. allows Nvidia to sell advanced chips to China at all, framing the issue as a core national security vulnerability. But David Sacks, Trump's former AI Czar and co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, dismissed espionage allegations against Huang as a "spy narrative" that is "collapsing already," according to Breitbart.

The controversy centers on Huang's relationship with a Chinese businesswoman identified as Alice Huang, with critics suggesting potential security breaches. Sacks' pushback suggests the Trump administration may be reluctant to aggressively pursue the matter—a signal that tech industry influence remains potent even in a Republican White House.

For Colorado, this matters: the state hosts significant AI and semiconductor research, and export control policy directly affects local tech firms' market access and competitiveness. Conservatives should watch whether Trump prioritizes national security over Silicon Valley lobbying on chip exports to strategic competitors.
