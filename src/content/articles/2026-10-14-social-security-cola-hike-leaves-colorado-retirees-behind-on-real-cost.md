# Social Security COLA Bump Falls Short of Inflation Reality for Colorado Retirees

The Social Security Administration announced a 2027 cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) of $75 per month—a modest bump that underscores a persistent conservative concern: government entitlement programs aren't keeping pace with real-world inflation pressures facing Colorado's seniors.

Frank Bisignano, the SSA commissioner, framed the increase as providing "greater financial stability" (Just The News). But conservatives argue the COLA formula itself is the problem. While the adjustment addresses nominal inflation, it routinely lags behind what retirees actually spend on healthcare, housing, and utilities—costs that have surged well beyond official inflation metrics in Colorado's competitive housing markets.

Republicans have long pushed for Social Security reform, including means-testing for higher earners and adjusting the COLA calculation to reflect real senior expenses rather than government-favored inflation indices. The current system, they argue, treats the program as a political tool rather than a sustainable retirement security mechanism.

For Colorado retirees living on fixed incomes, $75 monthly—roughly $900 annually—offers little relief against property tax increases, energy costs, and healthcare premiums that have climbed faster than the official COLA.

**Watch whether Republican lawmakers propose structural Social Security reforms in the next Congress, or if the COLA debate remains a political talking point without legislative action.**
