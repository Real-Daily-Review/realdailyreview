# Boulder's Climate Lawsuit Reaches Supreme Court—Conservative Legal Experts Warn It's an End-Run Around Democracy

Colorado's Boulder is bringing its eight-year legal crusade against two oil companies to the Supreme Court this term, and conservatives are sounding the alarm that the case represents an activist attempt to impose climate policy through courts rather than legislatures.

The lawsuit, detailed by CPR News, targets major oil producers in what Boulder frames as a climate accountability case. But National Review argues the real issue is far more troubling: progressive activists, unable to win majorities in elected bodies, are weaponizing the legal system to enact their preferred energy policies.

This matters to Colorado taxpayers and energy workers. If courts allow municipalities to sue energy companies over climate claims, it opens the door to cascading litigation that could chill investment in Colorado's oil and gas sector—an industry that generates substantial state revenue and employs thousands. The precedent could embolden other Colorado cities to launch similar suits, creating regulatory chaos through litigation rather than transparent legislative debate.

Conservatives argue that climate and energy policy belong in the hands of voters and their elected representatives, not unelected judges. The Supreme Court's decision will signal whether courts will become a backdoor for progressive policy goals or whether they'll respect the separation of powers.

Watch whether the Court takes the case on the merits or dismisses it on jurisdictional grounds—a ruling either way will reshape how cities can target energy companies.
