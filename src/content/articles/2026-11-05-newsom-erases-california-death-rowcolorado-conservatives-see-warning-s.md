# California Governor Erases Death Row—A Warning for Colorado on Progressive Criminal Justice

California Gov. Gavin Newsom just commuted all 548 death sentences to life without parole, a sweeping rejection of capital punishment that signals how far left-coast progressivism will go on criminal justice (Fox News Politics).

Republicans and victims' rights advocates are firing back hard. They argue Newsom's move disrespects victims' families and undermines the rule of law—overriding jury verdicts and democratic will with executive fiat. Conservatives say this is exactly the kind of soft-on-crime ideology that has fueled California's crime surge and homelessness crisis.

**Why Colorado should pay attention:** Colorado abolished capital punishment in 2020, but the state still grapples with how to balance victim advocacy, public safety, and sentencing policy. If progressive Democrats push similar commutation schemes here—or expand parole eligibility for violent offenders—Colorado taxpayers and families of victims could face the same erosion of accountability Californians are now experiencing.

The commutation also raises questions about gubernatorial overreach. When executives unilaterally rewrite sentences handed down by juries and judges, they're not reforming the system—they're circumventing it.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic leadership uses Newsom's move as a template for expanding clemency or early-release programs for violent criminals.
