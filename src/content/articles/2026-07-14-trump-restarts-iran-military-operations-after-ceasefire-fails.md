# Trump Restarts Military Operations Against Iran After Ceasefire Collapses

President Trump has formally notified Congress of resumed U.S. military operations against Iran following a breakdown in months-long ceasefire arrangements, according to a July 10 letter obtained by CBS News (The Daily Wire). Operations resumed July 7 after Iranian attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, triggering the War Powers Act notification requirement.

The escalation comes amid reported Iranian assassination attempts against Trump himself, with the regime's threats intensifying after Trump declared a prior memorandum of understanding "over" (Washington Examiner). Intelligence officials have flagged heightened Iranian hostility toward the administration.

**The Colorado angle:** While Colorado has no direct military installations involved in Iran operations, defense contractors and energy firms with Colorado ties—including those involved in Middle East logistics and energy security—could see contract shifts. Additionally, renewed Middle East tensions typically pressure oil markets, affecting Colorado consumers and the state's energy sector.

Republicans argue Trump's decisive posture deters Iranian aggression and protects American interests. Democrats have not yet issued unified statements on the renewed operations, though some progressives historically oppose military action without explicit congressional authorization.

**Watch next:** Whether Congress demands a formal war powers debate or if the administration seeks broader authorization for sustained operations.
