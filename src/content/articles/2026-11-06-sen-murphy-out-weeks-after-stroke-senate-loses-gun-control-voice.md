# Sen. Murphy Out Weeks After Stroke; Senate Loses Swing Vote on Energy, Gun Control

Connecticut Democrat Chris Murphy has been discharged from Johns Hopkins Hospital following a stroke but will miss several weeks of Senate votes, his office announced Friday. The 53-year-old senator's doctor expects a "full recovery," though Murphy suffered the stroke last week at his son's hockey game.

Murphy's absence creates a temporary gap in the Senate's narrow Democratic ranks during a critical period. The Connecticut lawmaker has been a vocal advocate for gun control measures and has opposed Republican-backed energy deregulation efforts—positions that matter for Colorado's energy sector and Second Amendment debates in Congress.

Republicans argue Murphy's absence, while unfortunate, underscores the importance of Senate efficiency and highlights how thin Democratic margins have become. Democrats counter that Murphy's recovery should proceed without political calculation.

The timing is notable: Murphy's weeks-long absence comes as the Senate prepares to tackle post-midterm legislative priorities, including energy policy and regulatory reform that conservatives have pushed aggressively. Colorado's oil and gas industry and Republican lawmakers have long opposed Murphy's environmental and gun-control agenda.

Watch whether Democrats request temporary proxy voting arrangements or whether Republicans use the absence to advance energy legislation without Democratic obstruction.
