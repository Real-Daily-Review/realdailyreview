# Russia's Plague Coverup Raises Biosecurity Red Flags for U.S.

Moscow is stonewalling the World Health Organization over a suspected pneumonic plague death at a Siberian biolab—and deleting its own public warnings to cover tracks.

Officials in Russia's Irkutsk region issued travel advisories after a scientist died at an anti-plague facility in Shelekhov over the weekend, then scrubbed the warnings within hours (New York Post). The W.H.O. has formally requested information from Russian authorities but received no response, according to a spokesperson (Breitbart).

President Trump said Tuesday the U.S. stands "willing to help" if Russia needs assistance (Fox News), a measured diplomatic gesture that underscores American concern about transparency from a geopolitical rival managing dangerous pathogens.

The incident exposes a persistent problem: Russia's opacity on biosecurity matters. When a nation with Soviet-era biological research infrastructure goes silent on potential plague outbreaks, it's not just a public health issue—it's a national security question. Americans have legitimate reasons to demand clarity on what's happening inside Russian labs, especially given Moscow's history of concealing biological incidents.

Conservatives should watch whether the Biden-Harris administration presses Russia harder on lab transparency or defaults to quiet diplomacy that lets Moscow off the hook.
