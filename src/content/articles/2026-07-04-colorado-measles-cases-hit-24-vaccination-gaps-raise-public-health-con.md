# Colorado's 24th Measles Case Highlights Vaccination Gaps as Disease Resurges

Colorado has now recorded 24 measles cases in 2026, with health officials confirming a new case in a Larimer County teenager who had not received the MMR vaccine and was likely exposed during international travel (CPR News, KDVR).

The resurgence raises questions about vaccination rates and public health preparedness. While measles is preventable through the MMR vaccine, Colorado's immunization coverage has become a point of contention. Health officials are urging residents to verify their vaccination status, particularly before international travel.

Conservatives and public health advocates generally agree that measles prevention through vaccination is sound policy—the disease poses genuine risks, especially to infants and immunocompromised individuals. However, conservatives emphasize parental choice and medical autonomy in vaccination decisions, opposing any government mandates or coercive measures. They argue that public health messaging should focus on education and access, not government overreach.

The state's 24 cases this year signal a troubling trend. Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state health officials push for expanded vaccine requirements in schools or workplaces—moves that would face significant pushback from parents and liberty-minded Coloradans who reject one-size-fits-all health mandates.
