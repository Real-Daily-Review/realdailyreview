# Americans Push Back Against Mass Surveillance Camera Networks

Washington's appetite for tracking technology has finally hit a speed bump—and Colorado should pay attention.

Both Republicans and Democrats are pumping the brakes on Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) networks, the camera systems that photograph and log every vehicle movement across America (Daily Caller). The backlash reflects growing concern over government surveillance overreach and privacy invasion—issues that resonate across the political spectrum.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters. Surveillance infrastructure expands state power and creates databases vulnerable to abuse by bureaucrats and political opponents. The fact that even Democrats are questioning these systems signals that ordinary Americans—left and right—are tired of Big Brother watching their commutes.

Republicans argue that while law enforcement tools have legitimate uses, mass surveillance networks represent exactly the kind of government overreach the Constitution was designed to prevent. The concern: once built, these systems rarely shrink, and mission creep is inevitable.

Meanwhile, Washington's fiscal recklessness continues unabated. The Washington Examiner reports that Treasury yields hit 19-year highs as bond markets punish federal borrowing binges, squeezing Americans already hammered by inflation and rising costs. Republicans must tie fiscal discipline to economic relief—cutting spending, not just talking about it.

**Watch whether Colorado's legislature resists pressure to expand ALPR networks and whether state Republicans demand real budget cuts, not accounting gimmicks.**
