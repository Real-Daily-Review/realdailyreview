# Colorado Joins Four-State Lawsuit Seeking $1.4 Trillion from Meta Over Social Media Harms

Colorado is one of four states suing Meta in what could become the costliest social media litigation ever, with potential damages reaching $1.4 trillion (CPR News). The lawsuit targets alleged harms to children from Facebook and Instagram platforms.

While the case frames itself as consumer protection, conservatives should scrutinize the underlying strategy: this represents aggressive state-level litigation that could set a dangerous precedent for government overreach into private companies' business models. Republicans argue that massive punitive damages—potentially exceeding what the company is worth—amount to backdoor regulation through the courts rather than transparent legislative process.

The suit also reflects a broader pattern of Democratic-led states using litigation as policy tool. California Gov. Gavin Newsom's administration has pioneered this approach, with activist groups now following his model into other early primary states ahead of 2028 (Washington Examiner), suggesting coordinated pressure on tech companies nationwide.

Colorado taxpayers should note: if the state wins, litigation costs and any settlement proceeds become state revenue—but the real cost is borne by Meta shareholders and, ultimately, users through reduced services or higher ad costs. The precedent matters more than the payout.
