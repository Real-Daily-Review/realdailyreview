# Michigan Democrat's Iran Record Under Fire as Student Confrontation Goes Viral

A Democratic U.S. Senate candidate in Michigan faced a pointed challenge this week over his public silence on Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps—a moment that underscores the left's struggle to articulate a coherent foreign policy on hostile regimes.

During a University of Michigan town hall, Persian student Iman Dashti confronted Abdul El-Sayed after leaked audio surfaced showing discussion of Iran's Supreme Leader Khamenei. Dashti, whose family has suffered under Iran's government, pressed El-Sayed directly: "Do you stand with us?" (Fox News, New York Post).

The exchange highlights a broader conservative critique: Democratic candidates often equivocate on America's adversaries while claiming moral clarity on domestic issues. Republicans argue that weakness on Iran emboldens the regime, destabilizes the Middle East, and betrays Iranian dissidents seeking freedom.

El-Sayed's response—or lack thereof—suggests Democrats remain divided between progressive anti-war sentiment and the reality that some regimes genuinely threaten American interests and democratic values abroad.
