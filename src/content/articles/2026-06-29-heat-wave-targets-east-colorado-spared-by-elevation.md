# Heat Wave Headed East; Colorado Likely Spared

A dangerous heat wave will pummel the central and eastern United States this week ahead of July Fourth, the National Weather Service warns, with dangerously high humidity making temperatures feel even worse—but Colorado's high-altitude geography should keep the state largely clear of the worst conditions.

The combination of rising temperatures and moisture arriving simultaneously will create what meteorologists call a "dangerous" setup for much of the country (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). Phoenix and other southwestern cities are already experiencing extreme heat, and the system is expected to spread eastward through the holiday week.

For Colorado, the good news: our elevation and drier climate typically insulate us from the worst of these Eastern Seaboard heat events. However, Front Range communities and lower elevations could see elevated temperatures and increased demand on power grids—a reminder that reliable, affordable energy infrastructure matters when weather extremes hit.

Conservatives should watch whether this weather event becomes a springboard for new climate regulations or green-energy mandates from state Democrats. History shows progressive policymakers use weather events to justify expensive, job-killing energy restrictions—even when Colorado's natural geography already protects us better than most states.
