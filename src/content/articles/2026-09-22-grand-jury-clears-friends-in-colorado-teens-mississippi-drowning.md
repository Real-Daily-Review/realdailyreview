# Grand Jury Clears Friends in Colorado Teen's Mississippi Death

A Mississippi grand jury has declined to indict anyone in the death of 18-year-old Nolan Wells of Colorado, rejecting speculation that his white friends were responsible for his July 4 drowning during a boating trip off the Mississippi Coast.

The grand jury found "the position of, the condition, time of discovery, and location of Mr. Wells' body is consistent with drowning," according to its report (Just The News). The panel explicitly rejected what it called accusations fueled by "public clamor and private malice" (The Daily Wire), pushing back against racial narratives that had circulated online and in media coverage.

Wells went missing during the boating outing and was found two days later. The grand jury's decision marks the end of the criminal investigation into his death, though his family held a news conference following the announcement (9News Denver).

The case drew national attention partly because of social media speculation about foul play, but the grand jury's findings align with the physical evidence: drowning, not criminal conduct. The decision underscores how rush-to-judgment narratives—often amplified by activist media—can obscure facts and harm innocent people before evidence is even examined.
