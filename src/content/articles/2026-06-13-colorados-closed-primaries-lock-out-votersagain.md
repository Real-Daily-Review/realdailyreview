# Colorado's Primary Chaos: Party Gatekeeping Meets Historic Unaffiliated Surge

Colorado's closed primary system is working exactly as party insiders designed it—and that's the problem. Out of 18 Republican gubernatorial candidates, only two cleared the GOP assembly hurdle (Complete Colorado), while Democrats similarly winnowed their field through their own backroom process, notably excluding heavyweights like John Hickenlooper and Michael Bennet (Complete Colorado).

Meanwhile, unaffiliated voters—now a majority of Colorado's electorate—will receive primary ballots for the first time, according to the Denver Post. That's a stunning shift: more unaffiliated ballots mailed than Democrat or Republican combined.

**The conservative case against closed primaries is straightforward:** Party assemblies let activist bases and establishment players pick nominees before general-election voters get a say. Republicans argue this produces candidates who appeal to party purists rather than swing voters—a recipe for general-election losses in purple Colorado.

Enter Courageous Colorado's push for open primaries, where all candidates appear on one ballot for all voters (9News). While the group's motives may be mixed, conservatives should recognize that closed primaries have failed to produce winning statewide candidates in recent cycles.

**Watch whether Colorado Republicans learn the lesson: nominating assembly winners who can actually win in November, or doubling down on a system that keeps losing.**
