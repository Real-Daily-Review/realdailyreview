# Fast-Moving Wildfire Forces Evacuations West of Sedalia

Colorado's wildfire season shows no mercy: a fast-moving forest fire west of Sedalia triggered mandatory evacuations Sunday evening, with residents within a two-mile radius of West Douglas Fire Station 132 ordered to leave around 5 p.m. (9News Denver).

The blaze underscores a persistent problem conservatives have flagged for years—Colorado's forests remain dangerously overgrown due to decades of mismanagement and environmental restrictions that have blocked responsible timber harvesting and forest thinning. While state and federal agencies have slowly begun addressing fuel loads, critics argue the pace remains glacial compared to the scale of the threat.

Mandatory evacuation orders affect an area spanning roughly two miles from the fire station, with residents in the immediate path facing the familiar summer nightmare of rapid wildfire spread across dry timber and brush (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

Colorado's wildfire risk has become a defining fiscal and safety challenge. Taxpayers foot the bill for massive firefighting operations, emergency response, and post-fire recovery—costs that could be reduced through aggressive forest management that environmental groups have long resisted. The state's continued reliance on reactive firefighting rather than proactive forest health measures remains a conservative talking point heading into budget season.
