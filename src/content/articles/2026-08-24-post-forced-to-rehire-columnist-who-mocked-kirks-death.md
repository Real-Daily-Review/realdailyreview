# Washington Post Forced to Rehire Columnist Who Mocked Charlie Kirk's Death

A private arbitrator has ordered the Washington Post to rehire opinion columnist Karen Attiah and pay her full back wages after the paper fired her last September for social media posts following Charlie Kirk's assassination.

Arbitrator Sarah Miller Espinosa ruled that the Post "did not have good and sufficient cause" to terminate Attiah and "violated" its labor agreement with the columnist, according to the New York Post and Washington Examiner. The Post had cited harm to the newspaper's "integrity" and violations of civility policies in its termination letter.

Attiah's posts described Kirk, the Turning Point USA co-founder, as a "white man that espoused violence," and critics say she appeared to mock public reaction to his death. The Daily Wire notes Attiah has a history of controversial statements on other issues.

The arbitration decision underscores a troubling pattern: major newsrooms struggle to enforce basic standards of professional conduct when it comes to their own staff, particularly opinion writers. The Post's inability to make the firing stick—despite clear policy violations—suggests labor agreements may shield even the most inflammatory voices from accountability.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether the Post's reinstatement of Attiah signals a broader retreat from editorial standards, or if management will attempt to enforce civility policies more consistently going forward.
