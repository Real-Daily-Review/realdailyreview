# Colorado's Digital ID App Down Ahead of Holiday Travel—State Offers No Timeline

Colorado's myColorado digital ID app has gone dark following a cybersecurity incident, leaving up to 150,000 residents unable to access the state-managed platform just as Thanksgiving travel kicks into high gear. The timing couldn't be worse: travelers relying on the app for digital identification face uncertainty, and state officials have offered no restoration timeline (Colorado Sun).

The outage compounds travel headaches already mounting from a massive coastal storm battering the eastern U.S., which has triggered over 550 flight delays and 366 cancellations as of Thursday morning (Washington Examiner). While the storm's impact is primarily East Coast, Colorado travelers heading that direction face compounded disruptions.

**The conservative concern:** This incident underscores a recurring problem with government-managed digital systems—when they fail, citizens have no backup and no recourse. The state's inability to communicate a recovery timeline raises questions about cybersecurity preparedness and whether Colorado taxpayers should be funding expansive digital ID infrastructure that collapses under pressure. Republicans have long warned that consolidating citizen data into state-managed apps creates vulnerability; this breach affecting 150,000 Coloradans vindicates those concerns.

Watch whether state lawmakers demand accountability from the agency responsible and whether this prompts a broader review of Colorado's digital infrastructure spending.
