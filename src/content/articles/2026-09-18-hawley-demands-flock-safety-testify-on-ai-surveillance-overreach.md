# AI License Plate Readers Face Senate Scrutiny Over Privacy Overreach

Senator Josh Hawley is demanding answers from surveillance tech companies about how their AI-powered cameras are tracking Americans without proper oversight. Hawley has called Flock Safety and three rival CEOs to testify before the Senate Judiciary Committee over concerns that automated license plate readers are creating a de facto national surveillance network—a conservative nightmare of government-enabled tracking without warrants or meaningful consent.

The technology captures and catalogs vehicle movements across the country, feeding data to law enforcement and private entities. Republicans argue this represents exactly the kind of unchecked surveillance expansion that the Fourth Amendment was designed to prevent. "Americans deserve answers," Hawley said, signaling bipartisan concern about privacy erosion.

For Colorado, this matters. Police departments statewide use license plate reader technology, and the state's growing tech corridor means companies like Flock have Colorado operations. Conservatives worry that without strict limits on data retention, sharing, and access, these systems become tools for government overreach—tracking citizens' lawful movements without probable cause.

Democrats have largely remained silent on the issue, though privacy advocates across the political spectrum share concerns about mass surveillance infrastructure.

Watch for whether Colorado lawmakers push for state-level restrictions on license plate reader data collection and retention before federal rules emerge.
