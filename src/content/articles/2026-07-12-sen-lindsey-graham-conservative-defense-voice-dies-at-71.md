# Senator Lindsey Graham, Republican Voice on National Security, Dies at 71

U.S. Senator Lindsey Graham of South Carolina, a stalwart conservative voice on defense and foreign policy for more than two decades, has died at 71 following a brief and sudden illness, his office announced Saturday.

Graham served 23 years in the Senate and more than three decades in politics, establishing himself as one of the Republican Party's most influential figures on national security matters. He was a close ally of President Donald Trump and maintained strong positions on military readiness and American interests abroad. Graham had recently returned from a trip to Kyiv, Ukraine, where he met with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

Trump released a statement on Truth Social, calling Graham "one of the greatest people and Senators I have ever known" and an "American Patriot" (New York Post).

Graham's death removes a significant Republican voice from the Senate at a critical moment for conservative policy on defense spending, military strategy, and America's role overseas. His influence on GOP foreign policy and his relationship with Trump shaped Republican positions on critical national security issues throughout his tenure.

Conservatives and Republicans nationwide will be assessing the implications of his passing for Senate leadership on defense matters and the broader direction of the party's national security agenda.
