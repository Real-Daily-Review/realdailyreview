# Former Mesa County Deputy Lands at ICE After Weiser Lawsuit

A former Mesa County Sheriff's deputy who faced disciplinary action and a lawsuit from Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser has landed a job at Immigration and Customs Enforcement, according to the Colorado Sun. Alexander Zwinck resigned last year amid the legal pressure after collaborating with federal immigration authorities—a move Weiser's office apparently viewed as overreach.

The case highlights the ongoing tension between Colorado's progressive state leadership and federal immigration enforcement. Weiser, a Democrat, has made opposition to ICE cooperation a signature issue, arguing that local law enforcement shouldn't assist federal immigration agents. Republicans and conservatives counter that cooperation with ICE is a legitimate law-enforcement function and that Weiser's aggressive legal posture against it undermines public safety and federal authority.

Zwinck's transition to ICE suggests the deputy found vindication—or at least employment—in the federal agency itself, effectively sidestepping Colorado's hostile legal environment for immigration enforcement.

Separately, multiple people, including Clear Creek County Sheriff's deputies, were hospitalized Thursday after a five-vehicle crash on I-70 while deputies were assisting a stranded motorist (KDVR Fox31).

Watch whether Weiser's office pursues similar legal action against other Colorado law enforcement agencies cooperating with federal immigration authorities.
