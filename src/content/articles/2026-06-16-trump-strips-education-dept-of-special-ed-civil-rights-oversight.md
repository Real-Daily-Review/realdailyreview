# Trump Administration Strips Education Department of Special Ed, Civil Rights Oversight

The Trump administration is yanking special education and civil rights enforcement out of the Department of Education—a structural overhaul that signals a fundamental rethinking of federal education bureaucracy.

Education Secretary Linda McMahon says the moves will "align federal responsibilities with agencies best positioned to support them" (Just The News). The reassignments redirect oversight to other federal departments, effectively downsizing Education's footprint.

**The conservative case:** Republicans have long argued the Education Department has become bloated and unaccountable, with civil rights enforcement often weaponized to impose progressive ideology on schools. Stripping these functions could reduce federal overreach and return education decisions to states and local districts—a core conservative principle. Fewer federal mandates mean Colorado school boards regain authority over special education implementation without Washington micromanaging.

**Democrats counter** that centralizing civil rights oversight ensures consistent protections for disabled students and minorities, warning that dispersing these functions could create enforcement gaps.

For Colorado taxpayers and parents, the key question is whether this reduces unfunded federal mandates while preserving actual services for special-needs students. Watch whether Colorado's education establishment—and the state's Democratic-controlled legislature—moves to fill any perceived gaps with state-level bureaucracy, potentially offsetting federal savings.
