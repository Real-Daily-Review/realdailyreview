# GOP Faces Voter Backlash Over AI Data Centers—and Blaming China Won't Fix It

Republicans are discovering that cheerleading for massive AI data centers may not be the winning strategy they thought. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the GOP's attempt to frame artificial intelligence development as a patriotic counter to Chinese competition is colliding with genuine voter skepticism about AI itself—skepticism that transcends party lines.

The core problem: AI has a legitimacy crisis. Voters don't trust the technology, period. Republicans banking on nationalist framing ("We must beat China!") are ignoring the real objection: many Americans simply don't want AI sprawling across their communities, regardless of geopolitical rationale.

For Colorado, this matters. The state has attracted significant data center and tech investment, and any backlash against AI infrastructure could reshape development patterns, water usage debates, and energy demand in the region. Conservative leaders championing AI as economic growth will need to address voter concerns head-on rather than deflect to foreign threats.

The lesson: you can't sell a product people fundamentally distrust by wrapping it in the flag. Republicans need a better answer than "China" if they want to keep voters on board with AI expansion in their backyards.
