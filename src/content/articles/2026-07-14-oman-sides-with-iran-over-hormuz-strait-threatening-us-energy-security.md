# Oman's Shift to Iran Threatens U.S. Energy Security and Colorado's Oil Market

Oman has abandoned its traditional balancing act between Washington and Tehran, now openly coordinating with Iran over the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint through which roughly one-third of global seaborne oil passes, according to the *Washington Examiner*. On July 11, Muscat proposed splitting Hormuz traffic into two separately controlled shipping lanes: one administered by Iran along its coast, the other by Oman near its shore.

This realignment threatens American energy interests and Colorado's economy. Tighter Iranian control over Hormuz shipping lanes could disrupt oil supplies, driving up prices at the pump and straining Colorado's energy sector and transportation costs for businesses and consumers. The proposal also signals a broader erosion of U.S. influence in the Gulf—a region critical to global energy stability.

Conservatives argue this reflects the Biden-Harris administration's failure to maintain deterrence against Iranian expansion. A stronger U.S. posture and energy independence would reduce reliance on unstable Middle Eastern shipping routes. Republicans have long pushed for expanded domestic oil and gas production—policies Colorado energy workers and landowners depend on—as a hedge against geopolitical vulnerability.

Watch for whether the Trump administration, if elected, moves to counter Iran's growing control over critical shipping infrastructure and accelerates domestic energy development.
