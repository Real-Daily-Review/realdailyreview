# Weld County Greenlights Massive Data Center—But Transparency Questions Linger

Weld County commissioners have approved a key permit for what could become Colorado's largest data center, a project that promises economic growth but has sparked legitimate concerns about government accountability and community input.

Global AI secured the permit to develop the facility on a former Carestream Health and Eastman Kodak campus (9News Denver). The project represents significant private investment and potential job creation in the region—exactly the kind of economic development conservatives typically champion.

But here's where it gets murky: CPR News reports the project has faced "community pushback over concerns about power and transparency." That's code for local residents and officials feeling shut out of the decision-making process. In a state where government overreach is already a concern, Weld County residents deserve clear answers about how much power this facility will consume, what infrastructure upgrades taxpayers might foot the bill for, and whether the permitting process followed proper public notice procedures.

Data centers are legitimate business ventures, but they're also massive infrastructure projects with real community impacts. Conservatives should support free enterprise—but not at the expense of transparent, accountable local government.

Watch whether Weld County commissioners release detailed environmental and infrastructure impact assessments before construction begins.
