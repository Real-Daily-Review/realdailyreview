# Colorado Ballot Initiatives Test Conservative Priorities on Bodily Rights, Land Spending, and Election Rules

Colorado voters face three consequential ballot measures that pit individual liberty against government expansion and special-interest spending.

**Proposition 135** centers on bodily integrity protections for minors. Conservatives argue adolescents deserve legal safeguards because judgment develops over time and some irreversible decisions warrant parental involvement and waiting periods (Complete Colorado).

**Proposition 137** would direct taxpayer dollars to land conservation—but critics say it amounts to corporate welfare for wealthy nonprofits. Complete Colorado's analysis notes that The Nature Conservancy, the measure's primary sponsor, would benefit directly, raising questions about whether Colorado taxpayers should finance activist organizations' land acquisitions.

**Election reform** gained urgency after Secretary of State Jena Griswold's primary performance exposed a structural flaw: in crowded races, a candidate can win with minority support even when voters prefer another option. Conservatives argue Colorado's system allows fractured outcomes that don't reflect genuine voter preference.

These three measures reflect a broader conservative concern: protecting individual choice and parental rights, preventing government-backed corporate favoritism, and ensuring elections reflect authentic voter will rather than procedural quirks.

Watch whether Colorado voters reject special-interest spending and affirm parental authority over minors' irreversible decisions.
