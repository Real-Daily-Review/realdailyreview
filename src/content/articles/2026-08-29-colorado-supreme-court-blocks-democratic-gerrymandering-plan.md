# Colorado High Court Blocks Democrat Gerrymandering Scheme

Colorado's Supreme Court has shut down a Democratic attempt to redraw congressional districts, keeping the state out of the national gerrymandering wars—at least for now.

According to Complete Colorado, the ruling prevents Democrats from manipulating district lines to gain partisan advantage. Republicans have long argued that gerrymandering—whether by Democrats or Republicans—undermines fair elections and voter representation. The Colorado court's decision reflects growing judicial skepticism of naked partisan mapmaking, a principle conservatives have championed across the country.

The timing matters. As CPR News reports, Colorado's 5th Congressional District race is already heating up, with Republican incumbent Jeff Crank facing Democrat Jessica Killin in El Paso County. The debate has centered on affordability, tariffs, and foreign policy—issues that will define the 2026 midterms.

For Colorado taxpayers and voters, the court's decision means district lines will remain stable heading into the next election cycle, preventing the kind of last-minute legal chaos that has plagued other states. Democrats, however, lose an opportunity to shore up their hold on competitive seats through redistricting.

**Watch for:** whether Democrats challenge the ruling or attempt new gerrymandering schemes before the 2026 election.
