# Colorado Democrats Push County Takeover While Missing Key Votes in Washington

Colorado Democrats are doubling down on a "county comeback" strategy to flip Republican-held local offices—even as their own representatives miss critical votes in Congress.

The state party has made inroads into GOP-dominated county seats, according to Complete Colorado, marking progress on a long-standing Democratic wish list to reshape local power structures across the state. But the effort raises questions about priorities: while Democrats pursue county-level gains, U.S. Rep. Brittany Pettersen (D-Colorado) was among Democrats who missed the Pete Hegseth impeachment vote in November, the Denver Post reported. Her absence—along with other Democratic no-shows—allowed Republicans to block the impeachment through a procedural motion that passed 211-204.

**The conservative concern:** Democrats are investing heavily in local takeovers that could expand government spending, regulations, and progressive policies at the county level—where they often face less organized opposition than statewide races. Meanwhile, their own representatives skip consequential votes in Washington, suggesting misaligned priorities or strategic calculation over principle.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses, this matters. County commissions control land-use decisions, tax assessments, and regulatory burdens that directly affect ranchers, developers, and small employers across the state.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado Republicans can mount effective county-level defenses in 2026 and 2028, or if Democratic gains accelerate unchecked.
