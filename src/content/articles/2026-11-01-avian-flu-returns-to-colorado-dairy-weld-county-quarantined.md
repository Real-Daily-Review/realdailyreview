# Avian Flu Returns to Colorado Dairy; State Quarantines Weld County Operation

Another avian flu case has surfaced at a Colorado dairy—this time in Weld County—marking the first detection since the 2024 outbreak that roiled the state's dairy industry and sent milk prices climbing (9News Denver). State health officials have quarantined the affected operation to contain spread, though they emphasize that pasteurized milk remains safe for consumers (Colorado Sun).

The resurgence underscores the vulnerability of Colorado's $2.7 billion dairy sector to disease outbreaks. While state officials project minimal human risk from properly processed milk, the economic hit to producers and potential supply-chain disruptions loom large. Dairy farmers already operating on thin margins face quarantine costs, lost production, and market uncertainty—exactly the kind of operational burden that drives consolidation and threatens family operations.

Republicans and agricultural advocates have long warned that heavy-handed regulatory responses to livestock disease can cripple producers without delivering proportional public health gains. The question now: will Colorado's agriculture department balance disease containment with the economic realities facing Weld County's dairy operators, or will precautionary measures tip toward overreach?

Taxpayers and dairy producers should watch whether state quarantine protocols remain science-based and time-limited, or expand into costlier restrictions that benefit no one but bureaucrats justifying their budgets.
