# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Michigan Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abdul El-Sayed met with the state's Democratic Jewish Caucus to apologize for past associations, but Republicans say the gesture rings hollow. According to Fox News, El-Sayed drew the line at distancing himself from streamer Hasan Piker, whose recent remarks blamed Jews for potential violence against them—a statement that sparked outrage.

The Washington Examiner's analysis cuts deeper: despite media claims that Democrats have "distanced themselves" from Piker's antisemitic rhetoric, only six Democrats have explicitly condemned his latest rant. That's a damning indictment of party leadership's willingness to tolerate extremism within its ranks.

Republicans argue this exposes a broader pattern: Democratic politicians offer performative apologies while refusing to sever ties with figures who traffic in antisemitic conspiracy theories and anti-American ideology. El-Sayed's refusal to dump Piker entirely suggests the apology was damage control, not genuine reckoning.

For Colorado Democrats, this national story matters. As progressive activists gain influence in state party structures, Colorado voters should ask whether their representatives will follow the same playbook—offering hollow apologies while maintaining relationships with radicals. Watch whether Colorado Democratic leaders explicitly condemn Piker's remarks or follow the national party's pattern of selective outrage.
