# Tech Outages Expose America's Crumbling Digital Infrastructure—Colorado Businesses in the Crosshairs

Recent nationwide technology outages aren't the work of foreign adversaries—they're the predictable result of decades of neglect on America's outdated digital backbone, according to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton.

Speaking on CNN's "State of the Union," Clayton attributed the disruptions to infrastructure upgrades, framing the chaos as part of "a period of technological step change" (Breitbart). The Daily Caller reports Clayton was emphatic: the incidents were "not coordinated" and "not state-sponsored."

But here's the conservative concern: while Clayton's reassurance may be technically accurate, it masks a deeper failure. America's digital infrastructure has been allowed to deteriorate through years of underinvestment and regulatory hesitation. The New York Post notes the asymmetry is stark—"AI-powered humans on offense" versus "traditional software-powered humans playing defense"—a mismatch that leaves critical systems vulnerable during routine upgrades.

For Colorado, this matters. Tech outages disrupt energy sector operations, agricultural supply chains, and small-business commerce across the state. If infrastructure this fundamental requires months of rolling disruptions to fix, taxpayers and business owners deserve answers about why government and private industry allowed conditions to deteriorate this far.

Conservatives should demand accountability: Which agencies approved this infrastructure neglect, and what's the actual timeline for hardening America's digital defenses?
