# House Passes Stopgap Funding Bill, Delays Hemp Ban—But Doesn't Address Debt

Congress punted on real fiscal reform Tuesday, passing another continuing resolution that keeps government spending on autopilot while kicking the debt crisis down the road.

The House passed a Senate-backed stopgap funding bill with bipartisan support—176 Democrats joined Republicans—that avoids a shutdown but does nothing to rein in runaway federal spending (Daily Caller). The measure also delayed a congressionally approved hemp product ban by four weeks, giving the industry until Dec. 11 to negotiate a reprieve (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative problem:** Republicans argue these continuing resolutions are fiscal theater. Rather than pass a real budget that cuts spending and addresses the $33+ trillion national debt, Congress simply extends current spending levels and postpones hard choices. The hemp delay, meanwhile, reflects industry lobbying overriding congressional intent—hardly a win for rule of law.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's hemp industry, a significant agricultural sector, gets temporary breathing room. But the larger issue affects every Colorado taxpayer: Washington's refusal to control spending means higher inflation, weaker dollar, and eventual tax increases to service debt.

**What to watch:** Whether Republicans use the Dec. 11 deadline to actually defund the hemp ban or simply extend it again—a test of whether the GOP will fight for fiscal discipline or settle for delay.
