# Social Security's Math Problem: Raising Retirement Age to 75 Gains Conservative Attention

The entitlement math is brutal, and conservatives are openly debating the unthinkable: pushing the retirement age to 75 to save Social Security from insolvency.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the baby boom generation—dubbed by some commentators the "Meals on Wheels generation"—is straining a system designed decades ago when lifespans were shorter and worker-to-retiree ratios were far healthier. The program faces a genuine solvency crisis without structural reform.

Conservatives argue that raising the retirement age is the honest policy fix. The alternative—endless tax hikes on working Coloradans or benefit cuts imposed by crisis—is worse. Republicans contend that gradually increasing the eligibility age reflects demographic reality: Americans live longer now than when Social Security launched, and the current system is unsustainable without painful choices.

The left typically resists retirement-age increases, framing them as cuts to seniors and the poor. Democrats prefer tax hikes on high earners or lifting the payroll tax cap—solutions conservatives say will slow job creation and burden Colorado's working families and small businesses.
