# Declaration's 250th Echoes Founders' Case Against Government Overreach

As America marks 250 years since the Declaration of Independence, conservatives are drawing a straight line from the Founders' grievances to today's battles over taxes, regulation, and federal power.

The economic principles animating 1776 remain strikingly relevant, according to analysis at *Breitbart*: the Founders rebelled against uncontrolled taxation, centralized trade policy, unchecked immigration authority, and runaway government growth. Those same tensions define modern conservative politics—and Colorado's own fights over energy regulation, water rights, and state spending.

A visit to Jefferson's Monticello, as covered by *National Review*, underscores how the Founders' vision of limited government and individual liberty shaped the nation. The estate serves as both museum and scholarly center, preserving the intellectual architecture of American freedom.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the parallel is unmistakable: the Declaration's indictment of distant, unaccountable power speaks directly to frustrations with federal overreach on public lands, EPA water rules, and IRS enforcement. The Founders' demand for "control of taxes" and "limiting the growth of government" remains the conservative north star.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans invoke these founding principles in the 2026 midterms—particularly on energy independence and tax relief.
