# Trump's Tariff Gamble Splits Right; Colorado Manufacturers Watch Closely

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire from within conservative ranks, even as his administration doubles down on trade barriers that could ripple through Colorado's manufacturing and agricultural sectors.

The "Liberation Day" tariffs, unveiled in April 2025, sparked immediate backlash. The *Washington Examiner* reports Trump's approval ratings slid following the announcement, which critics called "chaotic" with "absurd" justifications. Conservative skeptics worry the tariff approach repeats failed strategies without clear economic benefit.

Meanwhile, Trump's foreign policy team is charting a different course on nuclear weapons. *National Review* suggests the president should adopt Reagan's Cold War playbook—building up military strength as leverage for eventual denuclearization talks—rather than rushing into unilateral disarmament.

**The Colorado angle:** Tariffs threaten Colorado manufacturers, agricultural exporters, and energy producers who rely on global supply chains. Higher input costs could squeeze margins for businesses already navigating post-pandemic uncertainty. Farmers and ranchers face retaliatory tariffs on beef and grain exports—critical revenue streams for rural Colorado.

Conservatives are split: some back Trump's nationalist trade stance as necessary leverage against China; others warn tariffs function as hidden taxes on consumers and businesses, contradicting free-market principles.
