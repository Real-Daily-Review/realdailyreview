# Trump Administration Reaffirms Deportation Plan for Haitian Immigrants as TPS Expires

Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin doubled down Sunday on the Trump administration's plan to deport Haitian immigrants whose Temporary Protected Status (TPS) has expired, clarifying remarks that briefly suggested otherwise during a CNN appearance.

Speaking to Jake Tapper on *State of the Union*, Mullin emphasized that TPS—the "T" standing for temporary—was "never intended to be permanent" (Daily Caller). After his initial comments sparked speculation about a potential policy shift, Mullin issued a statement reaffirming deportations would proceed as scheduled for those no longer covered by the program (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that TPS has become a de facto permanent residency program, contradicting its statutory purpose. Allowing expired protections to lapse enforces the rule of law and immigration statutes as written—a core Trump administration priority.

**The left's position:** Democrats and immigration advocates typically oppose mass deportations, citing humanitarian concerns and economic contributions of long-term residents.

**Colorado angle:** Immigration enforcement directly affects Colorado's labor markets, housing costs, and public services. Deportations of TPS holders could reshape workforce dynamics in agriculture, construction, and hospitality—industries vital to Colorado's economy.
