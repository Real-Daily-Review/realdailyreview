# Colorado's Largest Active Wildfire Swells Past 89,000 Acres as Evacuations Spread

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire—now the state's largest active wildfire—has consumed more than 89,000 acres across Pueblo and Custer counties, with containment stuck at just 13 percent as of Sunday evening, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31.

The blaze has forced mandatory evacuations in Fremont County and surrounding areas southwest of Leadville, where the related Willow fire grew 900 acres overnight (Colorado Sun). About 800 firefighters are engaged in the battle, with crews hoping favorable weather—higher humidity and cloud cover—will provide the opening needed to gain ground (CPR News).

The human toll is mounting. One firefighter family lost their home entirely while continuing to battle the flames, 9News Denver reported. The fire's spread has also claimed the Ouray 100 race, a major regional event.

Conservatives and property owners should demand accountability on forest management practices. Years of federal mismanagement, environmental litigation blocking timber harvests, and underinvestment in defensible space have left Colorado's forests a tinderbox. While firefighters deserve full support, policymakers must confront whether progressive environmental policies have made these catastrophes inevitable.

Watch whether state and federal officials will finally prioritize aggressive forest thinning and fuel reduction over litigation-prone environmental reviews.
