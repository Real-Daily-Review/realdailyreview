# Colorado's Worker Shortage Deepens as State Grapples With Labor Crisis

Colorado's economy is hitting a wall—not demand, but supply: the state simply doesn't have enough workers to fill jobs across industries, according to Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City economists (CPR News). The shortage, once confined to hospitality and construction, is now spreading into healthcare, manufacturing, and professional services, threatening growth in one of the nation's fastest-expanding economies.

The problem is straightforward: Colorado's working-age population isn't keeping pace with business expansion. Conservatives argue that restrictive zoning laws, high housing costs driven by over-regulation, and state policies discouraging business investment have made Colorado less attractive to workers and employers alike. Republicans point to excessive permitting delays and labor regulations as additional headwinds.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs faces a separate legal challenge over municipal election timing. A lawsuit (Complete Colorado) targets the city's April election schedule under a 2025 voting law—the first enforcement action since the statute took effect. The case signals ongoing disputes over election administration rules in the state.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether Colorado policymakers will address root causes—zoning reform, regulatory streamlining, and pro-growth tax policy—or double down on interventions that worsen the labor crunch.
