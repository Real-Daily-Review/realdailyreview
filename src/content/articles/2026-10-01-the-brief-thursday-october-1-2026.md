# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, October 1, 2026

Colorado enters October with wolves reshaping ranch country, a DOJ investigation rattling Democrat election operations, and a media mega-merger moving toward the finish line. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Colorado Pulls Back Wolf Culling as Ranchers Pay the Price

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has announced it will stop killing wolves, citing falling adult survival rates, low pup counts, and parasite concerns. CPW says it will keep working with livestock producers on mitigation — but ranchers who've watched their herds picked off since reintroduction began know what "mitigation" usually means: paperwork, delays, and inadequate compensation. Conservatives warned from the start that wolf reintroduction was an urban environmentalist experiment imposed on rural Colorado. The bill keeps coming due, and ranchers are the ones paying it.

---

## Trump DOJ Targets Dem PAC Over Alleged Paid Voter Program

A viral undercover video showing a Texas Majority PAC employee describing a "Paid Relational Program" has triggered a full Department of Justice investigation. Democrats have long dismissed Republican concerns about organized, paid voter outreach schemes as paranoia. Now federal investigators are taking a hard look. Colorado conservatives should pay attention: if paid relational voter programs are operating in Texas, there's no reason to assume they're confined to one state heading into the 2026 midterm stretch.

---

## Paramount Settlement Clears Path for Warner Bros. Merger

A federal judge has approved a settlement between Paramount and state attorneys general, clearing the way for the Warner Bros. merger to be finalized as early as next week. Media consolidation at this scale means fewer voices, more corporate groupthink, and — given Hollywood's hard-left tilt — less ideological diversity in what Americans watch. Conservatives should watch whether the merged entity's content and news operations reflect any meaningful balance, or whether this is simply a bigger megaphone for the same progressive narrative machine.

---

## Cornell Gang Rape Investigation: Universities Can't Police Themselves

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul announced that Cornell University President Michael Kotlikoff has agreed to an independent, third-party investigation into gang rape allegations on campus. It took a governor's intervention to get a major university to agree to outside scrutiny of its own conduct. This is exactly why conservatives have argued for years that elite universities — insulated by prestige, endowments, and ideological conformity — cannot be trusted to hold themselves accountable. The question now is whether the investigation is genuinely independent or just another institutional whitewash.

## Avalanche Lock Up Bednar Through 2030-31

On a note Colorado hockey fans can actually celebrate: the Colorado Avalanche have agreed to a 4-year contract extension with head coach Jared Bednar, keeping the winningest coach in franchise history behind the bench through the 2030-31 season. In a state where good news can be hard to find amid tax hikes, regulatory overreach, and wolf attacks on livestock, we'll take it. Go Avs.
