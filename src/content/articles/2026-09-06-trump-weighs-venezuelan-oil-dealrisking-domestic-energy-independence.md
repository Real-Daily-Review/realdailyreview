# Trump Administration Eyes Venezuelan Oil Deal—But at What Cost to American Energy Independence?

The Trump administration is reportedly negotiating access to Venezuelan oil reserves, a move that raises hard questions about America's commitment to domestic energy dominance and the rule of law.

According to *Just The News*, the rationale is straightforward: U.S. shale production naturally declines over time, and diversifying foreign energy sources could help maintain American energy leadership. That's the pitch.

But *National Review* flags the contradiction. Trump has repeatedly attacked Hugo Chávez and Nicolás Maduro for seizing American company assets in Venezuela—yet now appears willing to cut deals with the same regime, effectively nullifying those claims. *National Review* argues this undercuts the "stable world order" built on property rights and rule of law that made America prosperous.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil and gas industry—already hammered by years of regulatory hostility from the Biden administration—needs clarity on energy policy. If the Trump administration prioritizes cheap Venezuelan crude over supporting domestic shale producers, it could undercut Colorado energy workers and the state's tax base.

Conservatives face a dilemma: energy independence sounds good, but not if it means abandoning American producers or legitimizing authoritarian regimes that steal from U.S. companies.

Watch whether Trump's energy team commits to maximizing domestic production *first*, or whether Venezuelan oil becomes a backdoor subsidy for cheap imports.
