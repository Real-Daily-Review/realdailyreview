# Trump's Iran Escalation Threatens Colorado Energy Costs and Markets

Renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities are pushing oil prices back above $90 per barrel, a direct hit to Colorado's economy and energy sector. President Trump has threatened additional strikes on Iran following Tehran's retaliatory attacks on regional allies, escalating tensions that threaten both Middle East stability and American energy independence (Just The News).

The spike in crude prices ripples through Colorado immediately: higher gas prices at the pump, increased costs for farmers and ranchers dependent on fuel, and pressure on the state's oil and gas industry—already navigating regulatory headwinds from Denver's progressive leadership. Colorado energy workers and consumers can ill afford another round of inflation driven by foreign conflict.

The Dow also dropped amid the uncertainty (Just The News), signaling investor concern that the escalation could derail peace efforts and destabilize global markets. Conservatives argue that strength and deterrence prevent prolonged conflict; weakness invites it. But the current trajectory—tit-for-tat strikes without clear strategic endgame—risks exactly the kind of prolonged entanglement that drains American resources and harms working families.

Watch whether Trump's administration articulates a clear exit strategy or if this becomes another open-ended Middle East commitment that keeps energy markets volatile and Colorado's economy on edge.
