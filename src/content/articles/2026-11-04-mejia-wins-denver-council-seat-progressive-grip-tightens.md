# Denver Voters Hand Progressive Activist Another City Council Seat

Denver's leftward drift continued Tuesday as progressive activist Melissa Mejía claimed victory in an at-large City Council special election, according to the *Denver Post*. Mejía's win adds another voice to a council already dominated by big-government Democrats pushing higher taxes, aggressive regulation, and expansive city spending.

The election underscores a troubling pattern for Colorado conservatives: Denver's municipal politics remain firmly in progressive hands, with little organized Republican or limited-government opposition. Mejía's campaign focused on housing affordability and social programs—code for more government intervention and taxpayer-funded initiatives that have done little to solve Denver's actual affordability crisis while driving up costs for businesses and residents.

Republicans argue that Denver's problems stem not from insufficient spending but from overregulation, zoning restrictions, and city policies that strangle housing supply and drive up development costs. Conservative critics point to the council's track record of tax hikes and regulatory expansion as proof that progressive governance has failed to deliver results.

Meanwhile, New York Republicans held Elise Stefanik's deep-red seat with Trump ally Constantino's victory, showing Republicans can still win in favorable terrain—a contrast to Denver's entrenched progressive machine.
