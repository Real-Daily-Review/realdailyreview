# Military Academies Ditch Tenure, Bases Go Off-Grid Under Hegseth's Defense Overhaul

Pete Hegseth is reshaping the military from the ground up—and Colorado should pay attention to what's coming next.

The Defense Secretary has ordered military academies to stop granting tenure to civilian faculty, part of a sweeping effort to reclaim control of military education from what conservatives view as ideologically captured institutions. According to CPR News, Hegseth is also severing ties with prestigious universities and introducing curricula centered on Western civilization—a direct challenge to progressive academic orthodoxy that has dominated officer training for years.

Simultaneously, Hegseth is ordering every military base to generate its own power, The Daily Wire reports. The move aims to make U.S. military installations "impenetrable" to attackers and reduce dependence on the civilian power grid—a national security posture that could reshape energy policy at Colorado's numerous military installations, including Fort Carson and the Air Force Academy.

Conservatives argue these reforms restore merit-based advancement and operational independence to the military. Critics on the left will likely contend that eliminating tenure threatens academic freedom and that energy independence mandates could prove costly.
