# Macy's Centennial Parade Faces First Weather Cancellation Since 1971

New York's beloved Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade—marking its 100th anniversary—could be grounded Thursday by severe weather, a scenario not seen since 1971. The Daily Wire reports that forecasters are warning of strong winds, soaking rain, and possible flooding across the Northeast, with precipitation expected to intensify Thursday afternoon. The Daily Caller notes this would be the first time in over five decades that the iconic giant character balloons face cancellation due to dangerous conditions.

NBC is banking on the parade proceeding as planned: actor Jack McBrayer (30 Rock) is set to preview the centennial celebration in an hourlong special airing Wednesday on NBC and Peacock, per 9News Denver. The network's promotional push suggests confidence the event will go forward, though meteorologists' forecasts paint a grimmer picture.

Macy's parade officials will make final decisions Thursday morning based on wind speeds and weather conditions. The giant balloons require specific atmospheric conditions to safely navigate Manhattan's streets—high winds pose a genuine safety hazard to crowds and handlers alike.

**Watch for:** Whether Macy's makes the call to proceed or postpone, and how the network pivots its centennial coverage if weather forces the first cancellation in 55 years.
