# Trump Threatens Iran Strikes, But War Distraction Risks Economic Agenda

President Trump is escalating military threats against Iran while simultaneously pushing to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but conservatives warn the escalating conflict is derailing his core economic mission (9News Denver).

The administration has threatened additional strikes on Iran as negotiations continue, signaling a hardline posture in the Middle East. However, National Review argues the Iran war is becoming a dangerous distraction—one that's consuming presidential bandwidth and political capital needed for Trump's signature economic agenda, including tariff negotiations and market stability.

**The conservative concern:** While Republicans support a strong Iran deterrent, some worry that prolonged military engagement diverts attention and resources from domestic priorities. Energy markets matter directly to Colorado—oil and gas production, refinery operations, and energy prices all hinge on Middle East stability. A prolonged conflict could spike energy costs for Colorado families and businesses already facing inflation pressures.

Conservatives also note that military escalation abroad historically expands government spending and executive power—both contrary to limited-government principles. The question isn't whether Iran deserves a firm response, but whether open-ended military engagement serves American interests better than focused diplomacy backed by credible force.

**Watch for:** Whether Trump can thread the needle—maintaining Iran deterrence while pivoting back to economic priorities that won his base.
