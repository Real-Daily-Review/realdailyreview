# Democrats Push Wage Hikes on Election Momentum—Colorado Could Be Next

Colorado taxpayers should brace for a fresh push on minimum wage hikes as Democrats leverage midterm victories to expand government mandates on employers.

Following Democratic gains in recent elections, party leaders are moving aggressively on wage floors. Pennsylvania's Josh Shapiro has signaled intent to more than double his state's minimum wage while codifying abortion rights with newfound legislative control (Fox News). The playbook is clear: use electoral wins to ram through progressive priorities before the next election cycle.

**Here's what conservatives argue:** Economists warn that massive minimum wage increases destroy entry-level jobs, particularly hurting young workers and those with fewer skills. Small businesses—already squeezed by inflation and labor shortages—face impossible payroll choices: cut hours, reduce hiring, or raise prices on Colorado families already struggling with cost of living.

Colorado's own minimum wage sits at $15.13 per hour, already above the federal floor. Democrats in the legislature have repeatedly pushed for further increases. Republicans counter that Colorado's business environment is already competitive and that wage mandates should reflect market conditions, not political ambition.

The left frames wage hikes as "worker protection." Conservatives see them as government overreach that kills opportunity for the workers they claim to help.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats introduce new wage-hike legislation in the 2025 session—and whether they target specific industries like hospitality or agriculture.**
