# House Stock Trading Ban Advances—With GOP Voter ID Rider Attached

Congress may finally crack down on its own insider trading racket, but Republicans aren't letting the moment pass without a fight over election integrity.

The House is expected to vote on the Stop Insider Trading Act, which would prohibit members of Congress and their families from buying new individual stocks while in office (Just The News). The measure has bipartisan appeal—few things unite lawmakers like preventing their colleagues from profiting off privileged information.

But here's the catch: Republican leaders added a voter ID requirement to the bill, turning a straightforward ethics measure into a proxy battle over election security. Democrats oppose voter ID mandates, arguing they suppress turnout; Republicans counter that photo identification is a basic safeguard against fraud and is supported by most Americans.

The strategic pairing highlights how polarized Congress has become. Rather than pass ethics reform on its merits, the GOP used it as leverage to advance election security measures (CPR News). Democrats now face a choice: accept voter ID rules they oppose or block a stock-trading ban that polls well with voters.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the real question is whether Congress will actually enforce ethics rules or simply create another loophole-riddled facade of accountability.

Watch whether Democrats cave on voter ID or kill the entire bill—and what that says about their true priorities on election integrity.
