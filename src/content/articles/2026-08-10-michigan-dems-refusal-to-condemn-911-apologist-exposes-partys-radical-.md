# Michigan Democrat's Radical Ties Raise Red Flags for Colorado Voters

Michigan's Democratic Senate nominee Abdul el Sayed's refusal to distance himself from a far-left streamer who defended 9/11 terrorism should alarm Colorado conservatives watching their own party's drift leftward.

El Sayed, a self-described socialist, repeatedly campaigned alongside Hasan Piker, a Twitch personality with a documented history of inflammatory rhetoric. When NBC's Kristen Welker pressed el Sayed on whether he disavowed Piker's past comments suggesting America deserved the 9/11 attacks, el Sayed essentially shrugged it off (The Daily Wire).

Republicans argue this isn't guilt by association—it's a legitimate question about judgment. Washington Examiner senior editor Peter Laffin pushed back against Democratic claims that scrutinizing these ties amounts to "Islamophobia," noting that Piker's extremist views warrant serious examination regardless of el Sayed's faith.

The pattern matters for Colorado. As national Democrats embrace increasingly radical figures and rhetoric, state party leaders face pressure to follow suit. El Sayed's unwillingness to condemn anti-American statements from his allies reveals how far the progressive wing will go to avoid alienating their activist base.

Conservatives should monitor whether Colorado Democratic candidates adopt similar evasion tactics when questioned about their own radical associations.
