# U.S. Continues Iran Strikes as Diplomacy Stalls

American military operations against Iran have now stretched into an 11th consecutive night, with ongoing strikes overshadowing efforts to resurrect a collapsed interim ceasefire deal brokered earlier this month, according to 9News Denver and The Daily Wire.

The escalating campaign reflects the Trump administration's hardline approach to Iranian aggression, rejecting what conservatives view as the failed appeasement strategy of previous administrations. Republicans argue that sustained military pressure is necessary to deter Iranian threats to U.S. interests and regional stability—particularly given Iran's history of proxy attacks and nuclear ambitions.

Democrats and diplomatic advocates counter that continued strikes risk further destabilizing the Middle East and closing off negotiation pathways, though the collapse of the ceasefire deal suggests Tehran has shown little interest in de-escalation.

For Colorado, the broader implications are significant: prolonged Middle East conflict typically drives energy prices upward, affecting gas prices at the pump and heating costs for families and businesses across the state. Colorado's oil and gas industry also faces uncertainty regarding geopolitical supply dynamics and potential regulatory responses to energy market volatility.

Conservatives should monitor whether the administration maintains this military posture or pivots back toward negotiation—and whether Colorado energy markets see relief or further pressure.
