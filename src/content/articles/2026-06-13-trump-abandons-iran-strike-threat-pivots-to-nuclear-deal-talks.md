# Trump Pivots on Iran: Deal Talk Replaces Military Threats

President Trump is abandoning public saber-rattling for behind-the-scenes negotiations with Iran, signaling a dramatic shift in leverage and strategy on nuclear containment.

After threatening Thursday morning to strike Iran "VERY HARD" and seize the critical Kharg Island oil facility, Trump reversed course by afternoon—canceling military action after Iran signaled openness to "discussions and final points" toward a deal (Washington Examiner). A senior Trump administration official told Breitbart News the president is "closing in on a final agreement" that would "actually ensure that Iran never gets a nuclear weapon," a direct rebuke to the Obama-era Iran nuclear deal conservatives have long condemned.

The whiplash reflects competing pressures: Trump's need to appear tough on Iran's regime while avoiding costly military entanglement. One Washington Examiner analysis suggests Trump's leverage may already be "spent," with the public threats-then-retreat pattern weakening his negotiating position.

**Colorado angle:** Energy markets watch Kharg Island closely. Any U.S.-Iran deal affecting global oil supplies could influence Colorado's energy sector and gas prices for state taxpayers and businesses.

Conservatives should monitor whether any final agreement actually includes the inspections and enforcement mechanisms the 2015 JCPOA lacked—or whether Trump settles for another toothless accord.
