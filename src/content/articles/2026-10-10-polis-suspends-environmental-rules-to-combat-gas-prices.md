# Polis Suspends Environmental Rules to Lower Gas Prices—But Conservatives Question the Real Fix

Governor Jared Polis has issued an executive order temporarily relaxing environmental and transportation regulations to combat Colorado's soaring fuel costs, with gas averaging $4.18 per gallon and diesel hitting $6.21 (9News Denver). Polis blamed geopolitical tensions, citing the ongoing conflict with Iran as a driver of global energy prices.

The move suspends certain state environmental policies and transportation mandates—a rare acknowledgment from a Democratic governor that regulations can burden consumers. However, conservatives argue the order is a band-aid that avoids the real problem: Colorado's own energy restrictions. Republicans contend that Polis's broader energy agenda—including opposition to oil and gas development and support for aggressive climate regulations—has constrained domestic supply and kept prices artificially high. They say temporary regulatory relief won't solve structural problems created by anti-energy policies.

Democrats counter that global market forces, not state policy, drive pump prices, and that the executive action demonstrates responsible crisis management while the administration pursues long-term clean energy transition.

For Colorado taxpayers and energy workers, the question remains: Will Polis's order extend to lifting restrictions on new oil and gas leasing on state lands, or is this purely cosmetic relief?
