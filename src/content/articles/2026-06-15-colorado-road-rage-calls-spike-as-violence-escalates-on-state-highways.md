# Colorado Roads Growing More Dangerous as Road Rage Calls Spike

Colorado State Patrol is drowning in aggressive driving complaints while violent incidents multiply on state highways—a sign that law enforcement resources are stretched thin and personal safety on Colorado roads is deteriorating.

CSP fielded more than 33,000 calls to its aggressive driving report line last year (9News Denver), a staggering volume that underscores how road rage has become routine in Colorado. The problem isn't theoretical: Boulder County saw an armed road rage shooting this week, with two arrested following the incident (KDVR Fox31), and Greeley experienced a separate weapons-related crime the same day.

Conservatives argue that Colorado's progressive criminal justice policies—including soft-on-crime sentencing and reduced enforcement priorities—have emboldened dangerous drivers. When aggressive behavior goes unpunished and violent offenders cycle quickly through the system, law-abiding Coloradans pay the price in safety and stress.

Democrats typically counter that road rage stems from broader social pressures and mental health crises, requiring treatment rather than harsher penalties. But Republicans point out that enforcement and consequences deter crime; without them, Colorado's roads become a free-fire zone.

The real issue: CSP lacks the resources and political backing to meaningfully address the problem, leaving Colorado drivers vulnerable.
