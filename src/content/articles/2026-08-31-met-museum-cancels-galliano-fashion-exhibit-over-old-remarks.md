# Met Gala Cancels Galliano Exhibit Over Decades-Old Remarks

The Metropolitan Museum of Art has scrapped a planned May 2027 exhibit honoring fashion designer John Galliano, citing controversial statements he made years ago. According to 9News Denver, the museum will replace the Costume Institute show with another exhibition, though specifics remain unclear.

The Daily Caller reports the cancellation stems from a "15-year-old drunken rant," with Galliano himself stating he "remains fully accountable for the pain caused by my words in the past."

**The conservative take:** This decision reflects the broader cultural pattern of retroactive cancellation—institutions erasing accomplished figures over old remarks, often made under personal duress, rather than evaluating their full body of work or demonstrated growth. While Galliano has apologized, the Met's choice to eliminate an entire exhibition suggests that contrition and time offer little protection against progressive activist pressure. The fashion world, like academia and corporate America, increasingly operates under a presumption that past statements—no matter how old or recanted—can justify professional erasure.

For Colorado conservatives, this serves as a cautionary tale about institutional capture: even prestigious museums now prioritize ideological conformity over artistic merit or redemption narratives.

**Watch:** Whether other cultural institutions follow suit, signaling whether this becomes standard practice or remains an outlier.
