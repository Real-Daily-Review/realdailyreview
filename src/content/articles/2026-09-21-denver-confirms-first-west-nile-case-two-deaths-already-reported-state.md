# Denver Reports First West Nile Case of 2026; Two Deaths Statewide

Colorado's mosquito season is already claiming lives—and Denver just confirmed its first West Nile virus case of the year, even as two deaths statewide have already been attributed to the virus, according to state data cited by 9News Denver.

The Denver Department of Public Health &amp; Environment confirmed the case in Denver County (KDVR Fox31), marking the start of what could be another brutal season for the disease. West Nile remains a serious public health threat across Colorado, particularly as summer heat and irrigation water create ideal breeding grounds for infected mosquitoes.

While public health agencies typically recommend basic precautions—insect repellent, protective clothing, and eliminating standing water—conservatives should scrutinize whether government health departments are using West Nile preparedness as justification for expanded surveillance, pesticide spraying programs, or regulatory overreach. Colorado's water-dependent agriculture and outdoor recreation economy make mosquito management a legitimate concern, but any response should balance public health with property rights and minimal government intrusion.

Coloradans should watch whether state or local health officials propose new emergency powers, spending initiatives, or restrictions on private land use in the name of mosquito control.
