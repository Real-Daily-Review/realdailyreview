# Colorado's Front Range Faces Double Squeeze: Tax Hikes and Fire Risk

Colorado's Front Range municipalities are squeezing residents harder than ever—and not just with wildfire danger. Combined sales tax rates have nearly doubled the number of high-tax jurisdictions, with 13 of 31 municipalities now charging above 9% in combined taxes, up from just 7 previously, according to *Complete Colorado*. That's a direct hit to household budgets already strained by inflation and housing costs.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue these cumulative tax hikes—layered across state, county, and municipal levies—are pricing working families and small businesses out of Colorado's once-affordable Front Range. Each new tax measure compounds the burden, they say, without corresponding cuts to government spending or proof the revenue solves underlying problems.

Democrats typically counter that local taxes fund essential services: schools, infrastructure, emergency response. But conservatives note that Colorado's tax burden has climbed steadily while quality-of-life metrics haven't improved proportionally—suggesting a spending problem, not a revenue problem.

Meanwhile, gusty winds and dry conditions are elevating fire danger along the Front Range this week, adding another risk to residents already managing higher costs of living.

Taxpayers should watch whether the state legislature addresses tax competitiveness or simply accepts Colorado's slide toward becoming unaffordable for the middle class.
