# Colorado's Early Fall Foliage Arrives—But Government Parking Crackdowns Loom

Colorado's fall colors are arriving a week ahead of schedule, driven by persistent drought conditions that are accelerating the state's natural display. While leaf-peepers are gearing up for peak season, Clear Creek County is preparing enforcement operations that will test visitors' patience and wallets.

According to 9News, the early arrival stems from ongoing drought stress on vegetation. The Colorado Sun notes that some areas are already showing hints of fall, though peak colors haven't yet arrived statewide.

But here's the catch: Clear Creek County is posting parking warnings on Guanella Pass and threatening fines, vehicle towing, and possible road closures during peak weekends (9News). The county's heavy-handed approach to managing fall tourism raises a familiar question—should government be regulating where Coloradans and visitors park on public mountain roads, or should market-based solutions and voluntary compliance work better?

Conservatives argue that government overreach in managing recreation areas often creates more problems than it solves. Rather than threats of fines and towing, counties could encourage carpooling, shuttle services, or private parking solutions that don't rely on enforcement bureaucracy.
