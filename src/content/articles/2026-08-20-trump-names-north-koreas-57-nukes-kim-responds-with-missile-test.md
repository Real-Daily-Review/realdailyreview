# Trump Publicly Names North Korea's Nuclear Arsenal—But Kim Responds With Missiles

President Trump broke with decades of U.S. policy by publicly disclosing that North Korea possesses 57 nuclear weapons, the first time an American official has put an exact figure on Pyongyang's stockpile (Fox News). The revelation came as part of Trump's diplomatic outreach to Kim Jong-un, including scaled-back U.S.-South Korean military exercises and offers to negotiate directly.

North Korea's response was telling: within hours, the regime launched roughly a dozen short-range ballistic missiles into the Sea of Japan, according to South Korean intelligence (Breitbart).

**The conservative pushback is sharp.** National Review argues there's "no reason to trust Kim Jong-un over our longtime ally," signaling concern that Trump's conciliatory approach may embolden rather than restrain the regime. Republicans worry that unilateral military de-escalation—without reciprocal North Korean restraint—signals weakness and undermines deterrence on the peninsula.

**For Colorado:** Any weakening of U.S. military posture affects defense contractors and military installations across the state, including Fort Carson and the aerospace sector.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump's diplomatic gambit produces verifiable denuclearization commitments or simply gives Kim cover to expand his arsenal while the U.S. stands down.
