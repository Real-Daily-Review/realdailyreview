# Congress Faces Pressure to Fund Air Traffic Control Overhaul After Nationwide Outage

A nationwide air traffic control ground stop has forced Washington to confront a decaying system that threatens Colorado's economy and travel infrastructure. Both President Trump and Senate Democrats are now demanding Congress approve funding to modernize the aging network, though their rhetoric differs sharply on blame and solutions.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has called for increased funding to prevent future disruptions. Trump is urging Congress to approve what he calls "desperately needed money" for system upgrades (Breitbart). Meanwhile, Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) pledged support for "whatever level of money is necessary" to fix the system, rejecting blame for the outage (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative concern:** Democrats have long resisted modernizing air traffic control through market-based reforms, preferring instead to throw taxpayer dollars at a bloated federal bureaucracy. Republicans argue the system needs structural reform—privatization or public-private partnerships—not just bigger budgets. Throwing money at the FAA without fixing its outdated infrastructure and workforce problems will only delay the real reckoning.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. Denver International Airport is a major hub; delays ripple through the state's economy and affect business travel, tourism, and supply chains.

**Watch whether Congress approves blank-check funding without demanding modernization reforms that reduce government control over the system.**
