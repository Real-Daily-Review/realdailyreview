# DSA's Playbook: Low-Turnout Primaries as Gateway to Power

Colorado Republicans should pay close attention to how the Democratic Socialists of America are methodically capturing local offices—because the same strategy could take root here.

According to the Washington Examiner, Pittsburgh's leftward shift didn't happen by accident. The DSA built power through "patient organizing, disciplined recruiting, and a clear-eyed focus on the races that mattered most: low-turnout primaries where entrenched power was vulnerable." A 2017 judicial race by DSA candidate Mik Pappas signaled the movement's early momentum in Pennsylvania.

The lesson for Colorado is stark: while conservatives focus on statewide and federal races, the left is quietly winning school boards, county commissions, and judgeships where voter turnout craters. These low-profile races determine who controls education policy, land-use decisions, and local spending—directly affecting Colorado families and businesses.

Republicans argue this DSA strategy exploits voter apathy in primary elections, where a disciplined minority can impose radical policies on an inattentive majority. Conservatives warn that without aggressive primary engagement at the local level, Colorado could see similar takeovers in Denver suburbs, college towns, and progressive strongholds.

Watch whether Colorado Republican Party leadership invests resources in local primary turnout and candidate recruitment—or continues ceding ground to organized leftists.
