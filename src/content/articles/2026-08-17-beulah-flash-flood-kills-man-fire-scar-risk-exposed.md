# Flash Flood Claims Life Near Beulah; Burn Scar Vulnerability Exposed

Colorado's vulnerability to catastrophic flooding in fire-scarred terrain claimed another life Sunday when a man was swept away by flash floodwaters near Beulah. According to the Fremont County Sheriff's Office, the victim was standing outside near the intersection of Highway 78 and Central Avenue when intense rainfall transformed the area into a torrent (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The fatality underscores a critical risk facing Colorado communities downslope from recent wildfires. Burn scars eliminate vegetation that normally absorbs rainfall and stabilizes soil, turning even moderate storms into deadly flash-flood events. The Aspen Acres burn scar in this region has been flagged by water managers as a particular hazard zone.

Conservatives and property owners argue that aggressive forest management—thinning overgrown timber, controlled burns, and removing dead wood—must accelerate to reduce both wildfire risk and post-fire flooding danger. Yet environmental regulations and litigation often delay or block such preventive work, leaving communities exposed.

The incident also highlights why Colorado's water infrastructure and emergency preparedness require sustained investment in early-warning systems and evacuation protocols.
