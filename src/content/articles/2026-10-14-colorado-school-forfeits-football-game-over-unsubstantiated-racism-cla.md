# Colorado School Forfeits Football Game Over Racism Allegations

A Colorado high school has punted away a chance to compete on the field, instead surrendering a football game to avoid unspecified racism claims. District 11 announced that Coronado High School will forfeit Thursday's matchup against Lutheran High, citing concerns raised before the contest (9News Denver).

The district's decision to cancel rather than play raises questions about due process and the standard of evidence required to sideline student-athletes. Conservatives argue that schools should exhaust fact-finding before yanking games off the schedule—particularly when allegations remain vague and unsubstantiated in public filings. Forfeiture sets a troubling precedent: accusation now apparently trumps investigation.

This comes as school sports nationwide face mounting pressure to address real or perceived misconduct. Yet the rush to cancel games without transparent review mirrors the broader progressive instinct to act first and ask questions later, leaving families and players in the dark.

The incident also highlights how Colorado's K-12 system handles conflict. Rather than fostering dialogue or clear accountability, District 11 chose the path of least resistance—one that denies young athletes the chance to compete and leaves the community guessing about what actually happened.
