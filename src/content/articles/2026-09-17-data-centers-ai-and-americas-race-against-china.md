# Data Centers, AI, and America's Strategic Edge Against China

The artificial intelligence race demands America build massive data centers—and fast, or cede technological dominance to Beijing. That's the core argument from national security hawks who see the current patchwork of state-by-state regulations as a self-inflicted handicap in competition with China.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, data centers represent far more than infrastructure: they're essential to military security, economic security, and political security combined. The concern is that while states impose varying environmental and land-use restrictions on data center development, China faces no such constraints and is rapidly scaling AI capabilities.

For Colorado, this matters. The state has both the power grid capacity and available land that make it attractive for data center investment—but only if regulations don't strangle projects before they start. Energy-intensive operations like AI infrastructure could mean jobs and tax revenue, or they could migrate to friendlier jurisdictions if Colorado's permitting process becomes too onerous.

Conservatives argue that excessive state-level regulation fragments American AI development and hands strategic advantage to adversaries. The counterargument—that environmental and water concerns warrant local control—reflects the broader tension between growth and sustainability that Colorado voters have wrestled with for years.
