# Denver Library Closed After Fentanyl Exposure: Another Sign of City's Public Safety Crisis

Denver's Sam Gary Branch Library shut down Wednesday after a hazmat investigation revealed exposure to a fentanyl-mixed substance that sent at least one person to the hospital. The closure underscores a growing problem: Colorado's capital city has become increasingly unsafe for residents and workers trying to use public facilities.

According to KDVR Fox31, officials discovered the narcotics-related substance during the investigation, prompting a full cleaning before the library can reopen. The incident reflects Denver's broader struggle with open drug use in public spaces—a direct consequence of progressive policies that have decriminalized drug possession and prioritized harm reduction over enforcement.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Denver's permissive approach to homelessness and drug abuse has created conditions where dangerous substances now contaminate public buildings. They point to similar incidents across the city as evidence that catch-and-release policies and underfunded law enforcement have failed residents.

City officials have not yet announced a reopening date or detailed timeline for decontamination.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether Denver's city council will finally acknowledge that their soft-on-crime approach is making public spaces uninhabitable—or double down on the same failed policies.
