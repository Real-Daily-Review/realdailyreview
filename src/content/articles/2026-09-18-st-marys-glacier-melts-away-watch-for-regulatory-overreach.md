# St. Mary's Glacier Vanishes as Colorado Faces Record Heat

Colorado's iconic St. Mary's Glacier near Idaho Springs has completely melted away this summer, marking a stark reminder of the state's extreme weather volatility—though what comes next matters far more than climate doomcasting.

The perennial snowfield, a popular hiking destination in Clear Creek County, disappeared amid record-high temperatures, according to reporting from the Colorado Sun and KDVR Fox31 Denver. Visitors expecting to see the glacier found only bare rock and water.

Here's what conservatives should note: while the left will inevitably weaponize this as proof of climate catastrophe requiring massive government intervention, the Colorado Sun itself acknowledges the critical fact—"future snowfalls can replenish the perennial snowfield." This is a natural cycle, not a permanent apocalypse. Colorado's climate has always been dynamic. Glaciers advance and retreat.

The real concern for Colorado taxpayers isn't the glacier itself—it's whether state and federal bureaucrats will use this as justification for new regulations, land-use restrictions, or energy policies that cripple Colorado's economy and energy independence. Watch closely for any push to weaponize this natural event into expanded government control over water rights, mining, or energy development in the high country.
