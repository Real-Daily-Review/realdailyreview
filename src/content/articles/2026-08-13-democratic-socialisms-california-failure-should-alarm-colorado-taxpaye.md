# Democratic Socialism's Real-World Failure Should Warn Colorado Progressives

Colorado progressives pushing higher taxes and expanded government should study California's cautionary tale: democratic socialism sounds good in theory but produces affordability crises in practice.

National Review reports that Democratic Socialists of America candidates are distancing themselves from the DSA's radical national platform, yet the mother organization refuses to retreat from its core agenda. Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner notes that California—the Left's policy laboratory—ranks dead last in affordability, despite decades of progressive governance.

Conservatives argue the pattern is clear: when the Left prioritizes "fairness" and wealth redistribution over free-market growth, the result is higher costs for working families, not relief. California's housing crisis, energy costs, and business exodus didn't happen by accident—they followed progressive tax hikes, regulatory expansion, and anti-development policies.

Colorado faces similar pressures. State Democrats have pushed tax increases and aggressive climate regulations that drive up energy and housing costs. Republicans counter that Colorado's energy workers, ranchers, and small-business owners can't afford to become the next California.

The DSA's national platform remains unchanged even as individual candidates run for cover—a reminder that progressive rhetoric often masks radical intent.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats embrace or distance themselves from California-style policies in the 2026 legislative session.**
