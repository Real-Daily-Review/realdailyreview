# Oman's Shift to Iran Threatens U.S. Energy Security—and Colorado's Wallet

Oman has abandoned its traditional balancing act between the West and Iran, openly coordinating with Tehran over the Strait of Hormuz in a move that threatens global oil markets and American energy independence. On July 11, Muscat proposed dividing Hormuz traffic into two separately controlled routes—one administered by Iran along its coast, one by Oman—effectively handing Tehran co-management of one of the world's most critical chokepoints (Washington Examiner).

The shift is a strategic win for Iran and a loss for U.S. interests. Through Oman, Iran gains legitimacy and operational control over shipping lanes that carry roughly one-third of global seaborne oil trade. For Colorado, this matters directly: instability in the Strait threatens oil prices, which ripple through energy costs for families and businesses across the state. Higher energy prices also undermine Colorado's competitive advantage in attracting energy-sector jobs and investment.

Republicans and conservatives argue the Biden-Harris administration's weak posture toward Iran—from the failed nuclear deal to sanctions relief—emboldened Tehran to pursue aggressive regional moves. The administration's retreat from energy dominance has left America vulnerable to OPEC and Iranian leverage over global supplies.

Watch whether the State Department pushes back on Oman's plan or allows Iran to consolidate control over Hormuz traffic.
