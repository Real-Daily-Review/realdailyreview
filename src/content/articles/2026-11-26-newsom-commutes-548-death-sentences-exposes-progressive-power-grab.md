# Newsom's Death-Row Commutations Expose California Hypocrisy on Crime

California Gov. Gavin Newsom just commuted the death sentences of all 548 of the state's most dangerous criminals—then turned around to attack President Trump for allegedly abusing executive power (Washington Examiner). The move exposes what conservatives see as naked political theater: Newsom positioning himself as Trump's moral opposite while wielding the same expansive executive authority he claims to oppose.

Republicans argue Newsom's mass commutations represent a reckless prioritization of progressive ideology over public safety. The governor's decision to spare murderers, rapists, and serial killers contradicts his rhetorical attacks on Trump's use of presidential power—suggesting his real objection isn't executive overreach itself, but *whose* values get advanced through it.

For Colorado, the California precedent matters. Progressive prosecutors and governors increasingly view criminal justice through an ideological lens that prioritizes inmate welfare over victim protection and community safety. If similar soft-on-crime policies gain traction in Colorado—where Democrats control the statehouse—taxpayers could face pressure to fund expanded clemency programs and death-row appeals while violent crime remains a concern in Denver and other cities.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado Democrats cite Newsom's commutations as a model for state clemency reform.
