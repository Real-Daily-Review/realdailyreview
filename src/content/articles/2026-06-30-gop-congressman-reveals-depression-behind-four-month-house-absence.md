# GOP Congressman Breaks Silence on Four-Month Absence

Rep. Tom Kean Jr. (R-NJ) ended months of speculation Tuesday by revealing he stepped back from Congress due to depression, marking a rare public disclosure of mental health struggles among sitting lawmakers.

Kean, who returned to the House floor for the first time since early March, made an emotional statement acknowledging the condition that sidelined him for nearly four months. The New Jersey Republican's absence had drawn significant attention within GOP circles, with colleagues and constituents left largely in the dark about the reasons for his extended leave.

The congressman's disclosure underscores a broader conversation about mental health in high-stress political environments. While Kean's openness may reduce stigma around depression, conservatives and taxpayers have legitimate questions about congressional accountability: Who represents constituents when members are absent for extended periods? What mechanisms exist to ensure districts receive adequate representation?

Republicans have generally supported Kean's return and his willingness to address the issue publicly. The incident raises practical concerns about House operations and constituent service—issues that matter to Colorado voters watching how their own representatives balance personal health with public duty.
