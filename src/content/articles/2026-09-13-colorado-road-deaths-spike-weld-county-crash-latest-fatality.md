# Colorado Traffic Deaths Mount as Weld County Sees Another Fatal Crash

Colorado's roads are claiming lives at an alarming rate, with at least one person killed Saturday in a head-on collision on Weld County Road 100 around 12:29 p.m., according to Colorado State Patrol (9News Denver).

The fatality is part of a troubling pattern. Investigators in a separate Loveland crash determined a driver may have lost consciousness before impact (KDVR Fox31), while a Lakewood man now faces charges after allegedly striking and killing a 17-year-old riding an e-scooter in August (KDVR Fox31).

These incidents underscore a critical question conservatives and taxpayers should demand answered: **Are Colorado's roads adequately maintained and funded?** Poor road conditions, inadequate signage, and deferred infrastructure maintenance can contribute to accidents—yet Colorado's transportation budget remains subject to political spending priorities that often favor transit schemes over basic highway safety.

Republicans have long argued that government should prioritize core functions like road maintenance before expanding into experimental programs. When drivers lose consciousness or head-on collisions occur on county roads, it raises hard questions about whether Colorado's transportation dollars are being spent wisely on proven safety measures.

Conservatives should watch whether state lawmakers will demand a transparent audit of road maintenance spending and accident data—or whether they'll use these tragedies to justify new regulations rather than fix the fundamentals.
