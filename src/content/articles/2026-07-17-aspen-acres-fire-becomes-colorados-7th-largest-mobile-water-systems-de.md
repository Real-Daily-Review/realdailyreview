# Colorado's Seventh-Largest Wildfire Tests State's Firefighting Infrastructure

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire—now the state's seventh-largest wildfire by acreage, nearing 100,000 acres—has exposed both the ingenuity and strain of the state's wildfire response system as crews deploy innovative water-supply tactics to battle the blaze.

Mobile "dip sites" equipped with large tanks are hauling water and pumping it into movable reservoirs positioned near active fire zones, allowing helicopters to refill mid-operation without returning to distant bases (CPR News). The strategy reflects how Colorado firefighters adapt on the ground, but it also underscores a hard reality: the state's water infrastructure remains stretched thin during peak fire season.

As damage assessments begin, the Red Cross is cataloging losses across affected communities, with residents facing the grim reality that "what was there before is not there" (KDVR Fox31). The fire's scale—approaching six figures in acreage—raises questions conservatives have long posed: Are Colorado's land-management policies, water allocation frameworks, and forest-thinning initiatives adequate to prevent catastrophic burns?

Republicans and energy advocates argue that aggressive forest management, responsible timber harvesting, and strategic prescribed burns—not restrictions on development or water use—offer the most effective long-term defense. Progressive environmental policies that limit logging and water availability, they contend, have left Colorado's forests dangerously overgrown and vulnerable.

Watch for legislative pushback this session on water-use restrictions and land-management funding.
