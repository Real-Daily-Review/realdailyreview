# White House Exposes China's $26B Tariff-Dodging Scheme Through Third Nations

The White House has documented what it calls the "Great Transshipment Scam"—a coordinated effort by China to route goods through over 40 lower-tariff countries to evade lawful American tariffs, costing U.S. taxpayers and businesses up to $26 billion annually (Fox News).

The scheme works by funneling Chinese-made products through third nations, obscuring their origin and allowing them to slip past tariff barriers designed to protect American workers and manufacturers. According to the White House report detailed by Just The News, the scale of potential evasion represents an economically significant threat to the integrity of the U.S. tariff system.

For Colorado, this matters directly. American manufacturers—including those in Colorado's industrial and agricultural sectors—face unfair competition from artificially cheap imports that bypass tariffs meant to level the playing field. Colorado energy and manufacturing workers depend on fair trade enforcement to keep domestic jobs competitive.

Conservatives argue this exposes the necessity of aggressive tariff enforcement and tighter customs oversight. The administration's willingness to name the scheme publicly signals a harder line on trade enforcement than previous administrations, though skeptics question whether enforcement mechanisms will actually close the loopholes or merely document them.

Watch whether Congress and the administration move to strengthen customs enforcement and penalties against transshipment hubs—or whether this remains another report gathering dust.
