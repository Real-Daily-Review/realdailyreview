# Colorado Schools Face Enrollment Cliff While Pushing Tax Hikes

Colorado school districts are doubling down on tax increases even as enrollment continues its long decline—a mismatch that should alarm taxpayers already squeezed by the state's cost-of-living crisis.

Complete Colorado reports that districts across the state face "declining enrollment for the foreseeable future," yet continue pitching higher taxes to voters under the banner of "for the kids." The logic is backwards: fewer students means less need for expanded budgets, not more justification for tax hikes.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that Colorado's education establishment refuses to right-size spending to match demographic reality. Instead of consolidating operations, cutting administrative bloat, or improving efficiency, districts reflexively demand more taxpayer money. Meanwhile, families are leaving Colorado or choosing private schools—a signal that public education isn't delivering value worth the cost.

There is one bright spot: CPR News reports that Colorado's community college system is seeing enrollment growth among older adults, driven by demand for AI and healthcare training. That's market-driven education responding to actual workforce needs—the model K-12 should emulate.

Conservatives should watch whether school boards acknowledge enrollment trends honestly or continue selling tax increases as the only solution to problems their own policies created.
