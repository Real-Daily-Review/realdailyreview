# New Deal Ghost Town Warns Against Federal Social Engineering

A West Virginia community built on Depression-era federal largesse offers a cautionary tale about government overreach that Colorado taxpayers should heed. Arthurdale, established 93 years ago as the first experimental community under Franklin Roosevelt's subsistence-homesteads program, was designed to rescue impoverished families through federal intervention and centralized planning—a model that conservatives argue exemplifies the dangers of Washington deciding how Americans should live.

The Washington Examiner's examination of Arthurdale highlights the enduring debate over whether such federal experiments represent compassionate governance or socialist social engineering. Republicans and conservatives have long warned that government-directed community development, however well-intentioned, concentrates power in bureaucratic hands, undermines individual choice, and wastes taxpayer dollars on utopian schemes that rarely deliver promised results.

The Arthurdale precedent matters for Colorado. As progressive policymakers in Denver and Washington push expanded federal housing programs, climate-mandated land-use controls, and subsidized community development initiatives, conservatives argue these echo the same top-down planning that characterized New Deal experiments. Colorado's ranchers, property owners, and energy workers have watched federal land management and regulatory overreach reshape the state for decades.

Watch for how Colorado's Republican delegation responds to any new federal housing or community development proposals—and whether state lawmakers push back against Washington's strings-attached funding.
