# EPA Moves to Scrap Biden's Costly Power Plant Emissions Rules

The Trump administration's EPA is moving to repeal Obama-era and Biden-era greenhouse gas limits on coal and natural gas power plants—a win for Colorado's energy sector and ratepayers tired of regulatory overreach.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the EPA argues the rollback will strengthen grid reliability and lower customer electricity costs. The agency is expected to announce the decision Monday at the G20 Ministerial Meeting on Energy Abundance.

Colorado's coal and natural gas industries have long chafed under federal emissions mandates that drive up compliance costs and threaten jobs in energy-producing regions. Conservatives argue these rules amount to a backdoor ban on fossil fuels, forcing utilities to retire reliable baseload power plants before viable replacements exist—destabilizing the grid and hiking bills for families and small businesses.

Environmentalists and Democrats counter that repealing emissions limits will "worsen climate change," though they offer no cost-benefit analysis of the regulatory burden on Colorado consumers and workers (*9News Denver*).

The real question: Will Colorado's Public Utilities Commission and state regulators follow the federal lead, or will they impose their own emissions mandates that undercut the Trump administration's deregulatory push?
