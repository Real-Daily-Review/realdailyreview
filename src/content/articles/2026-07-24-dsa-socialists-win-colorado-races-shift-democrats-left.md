# Democratic Socialists Gain Ground in Colorado and Beyond—But Momentum Stalls in Arizona

Colorado Democrats are watching their party shift sharply left as Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) candidates rack up wins against establishment incumbents in New York, Colorado, and Washington, D.C., according to the Washington Examiner. The DSA's primary-season surge raised expectations for another upset in Arizona this week—but the momentum broke there, with the socialist-backed challenger falling short against an incumbent Democrat.

The Colorado angle matters. DSA victories in state races signal that the party's progressive wing is consolidating power in key races, potentially pulling Democratic platforms further toward higher taxes, expanded government programs, and stricter regulations. Conservatives warn this trend threatens Colorado's business climate and threatens to accelerate the state's already-aggressive spending trajectory.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue the DSA's electoral success reflects how far the Democratic Party has drifted from centrist economics. They contend that socialist-aligned candidates openly embrace policies—wealth redistribution, government control of industries, and massive tax increases—that would devastate Colorado's energy sector, small businesses, and working families' paychecks.

The Arizona setback suggests the DSA wave may have limits, but Colorado Republicans should monitor whether socialist-backed candidates gain traction in 2024 state legislative races, where they could reshape tax and regulatory policy for years to come.
