# A Generation Removed From 9/11 Faces a Complacent America

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students born after the towers fell are learning about terrorism as history rather than lived national trauma—and conservatives warn that complacency is exactly what our enemies exploit.

CPR News reports that Denver-area schools are grappling with how to teach a generation for whom 9/11 is ancient history. The challenge is real: younger Coloradans have no memory of the security awakening that defined the early 2000s, the resolve that followed, or the stakes of counterterrorism.

But National Review sounds a sharper alarm. The outlet argues that while Americans have moved on psychologically, "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still"—suggesting that two-and-a-half decades of military engagement and homeland security spending have not eliminated the ideological threat that spawned 9/11.

Conservatives worry that generational distance breeds strategic vulnerability. When terrorism becomes a textbook topic rather than a defining national memory, the political will to maintain robust counterterrorism posture, secure borders, and strong defense spending erodes. Colorado's military installations and defense contractors depend on sustained national security commitment.

The real question: Will policymakers maintain vigilance, or will budget hawks and war-weary voters gut the defense and intelligence capabilities that have kept another major attack from U.S. soil for 25 years?
