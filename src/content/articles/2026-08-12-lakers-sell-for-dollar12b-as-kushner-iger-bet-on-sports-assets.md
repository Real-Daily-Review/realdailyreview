# Record Lakers Sale Shows Private Capital Still Flowing Despite Economic Headwinds

Josh Kushner and Bob Iger's $12 billion purchase of the Los Angeles Lakers marks another blockbuster sports franchise deal, with the price tag climbing $2 billion above Mark Walter's acquisition just one year ago (Washington Examiner). The former Disney CEO and younger brother of Jared Kushner are betting big on professional basketball's enduring appeal to wealthy investors.

The rapid appreciation—from Walter's $10 billion valuation to the new $12.5 billion price (Just The News)—reflects confidence among high-net-worth buyers that premium sports assets remain solid stores of value. For Colorado sports fans, the deal underscores how franchise valuations in major markets continue climbing while Denver's own teams command smaller price tags despite strong local support.

The transaction also signals that despite inflation concerns and market volatility, capital-rich individuals and former corporate executives retain substantial dry powder for trophy acquisitions. Iger's move from Disney's boardroom to NBA ownership illustrates the revolving door between corporate America and sports investment.

Conservatives should watch whether such mega-deals face renewed scrutiny from the Biden-Harris administration on antitrust or foreign investment grounds—or whether the focus remains on Main Street businesses facing regulatory pressure while billionaires operate in a different lane entirely.
