# Supreme Court Hands States Power Over Election Rules, Rejects Trump Immigration Push

Colorado and other states just won a major victory on election administration—but the Supreme Court's final decision day also dealt a significant blow to border enforcement.

The justices ruled that states retain authority to set their own mail-in voting deadlines, rejecting federal overreach on election procedures (National Review). This empowers Colorado lawmakers to craft voting rules without Washington dictating terms—a win for state sovereignty that conservatives have long championed.

However, the Court simultaneously rejected a core element of Trump's immigration agenda, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver. The decision dismantled a federal law enacted after Watergate that had capped certain immigration enforcement powers, limiting the administration's ability to enforce border security uniformly across the country.

The Court also upheld statewide bans on transgender athletes in sports—a significant victory for states protecting fairness in women's athletics, particularly relevant as Colorado continues debating its own sports eligibility policies.

**The split outcome reflects the Court's federalism bent:** states gain power over elections and sports policy, but lose tools for coordinated immigration enforcement. Republicans argue the immigration ruling hamstrings border security; Democrats claim it protects due process.
