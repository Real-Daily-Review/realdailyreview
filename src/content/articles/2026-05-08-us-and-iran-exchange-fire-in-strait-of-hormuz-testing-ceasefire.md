# U.S. and Iran Exchange Fire in Strait of Hormuz, Straining Month-Old Ceasefire

Three U.S. Navy destroyers intercepted Iranian attacks in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, according to U.S. Central Command, marking a serious escalation in a strategically vital waterway.

The USS Truxtun, USS Rafael Peralta, and USS Mason came under fire from Iranian fast-attack boats, missiles, and drones as they transited the strait, according to CBS News and NPR. U.S. forces struck back, targeting Iranian military launch sites responsible for the attacks, CENTCOM said.

The exchange threatens a month-old ceasefire between the nations. Iran's top military command accused the U.S. of violating the truce, while the U.S. maintains the agreement remains in effect, according to PBS NewsHour.

President Trump responded with a warning, saying the U.S. would strike Iran "a lot harder, and more violently," The Hill reported.

The incident has broader ripple effects. Iran has created a new agency to control shipping in the strait while reviewing the peace deal, PBS reported. The potential closure of the Strait of Hormuz is already driving up global shipping costs, compounding humanitarian crises—particularly in Somalia, where aid delivery expenses are escalating amid a severe hunger crisis, according to the World Food Program.
