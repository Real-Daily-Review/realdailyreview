# Trump Administration Sidesteps Nvidia Spy Scandal Ahead of Xi Meeting

The Trump administration is quietly burying what conservatives say should be a major national security reckoning: Nvidia's alleged sale of advanced AI chips to China and former CEO Jensen Huang's reported entanglement with Chinese intelligence operatives.

According to the Washington Examiner, a senior Trump official confirmed the administration will not raise the Nvidia situation during Trump's upcoming meetings with Chinese President Xi Jinping in Asia. National Review has pressed the question directly: "Why, exactly, are we allowing Nvidia to sell advanced AI chips to China?"

Conservatives argue this represents a dangerous capitulation on technology security at a moment when U.S.-China competition over artificial intelligence dominance is existential. The chip sales undermine American strategic advantage and national defense—yet the White House appears willing to let the matter drop in favor of diplomatic theater.

For Colorado, this matters: the state hosts significant semiconductor research and defense-related tech operations. If the administration won't enforce export controls on critical AI technology, it signals weakness on the tech-security framework that Colorado's innovation economy depends on.

Watch whether Congress—particularly Republican hawks on China—forces a public accounting before Trump's Asia trip concludes.
