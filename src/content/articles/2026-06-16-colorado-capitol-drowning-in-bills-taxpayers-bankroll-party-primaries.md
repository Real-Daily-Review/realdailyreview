# Colorado Capitol Drowning in Bill Overload While Taxpayers Foot the Party Bill

Colorado legislators are introducing so many bills that meaningful debate has become impossible—and taxpayers are forced to bankroll primary elections for parties they don't even support.

Complete Colorado argues the state legislature needs hard caps on bill introductions to restore deliberative lawmaking. The sheer volume of legislation churning through the Capitol creates a legislative environment where careful review and constituent input get steamrolled by procedural gridlock.

Separately, conservatives are questioning why Colorado forces taxpayers to finance primary elections for political parties. If you're an independent voter or belong to a party different from the major two, you're still picking up the tab for Democratic and Republican primaries—a subsidy that should end (Complete Colorado).

Election integrity concerns are also mounting. Rather than dismissing voter skepticism about Colorado's election system, conservatives argue the state should embrace independent audits to rebuild public confidence in results (Complete Colorado). Transparency and verification, they contend, strengthen democracy more than dismissing concerns.

These three issues—legislative overload, party-privilege funding, and election auditing—reflect a broader conservative complaint: Colorado government has grown too large, too expensive, and too insulated from taxpayer scrutiny.

Watch whether the 2027 legislative session adopts meaningful bill-introduction limits or continues the current free-for-all that favors special interests over careful governance.
