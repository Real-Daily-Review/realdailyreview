# Rural Maternity Crisis Widens as Hospitals Shutter Across America

Colorado's rural counties face a growing crisis: nearly 100 labor and delivery units have closed across 35 states in just two years, leaving more than one-third of U.S. counties without adequate maternity care (9News Denver, March of Dimes report).

The closures are hitting hardest in rural America, where expectant mothers now face longer travel times to deliver safely. Colorado's mountain and plains communities—already stretched thin by distance—stand to lose critical obstetric services that rural families depend on.

The Daily Caller notes the report identifies "unnecessary barriers to maternity care," pointing to regulatory and reimbursement obstacles that make rural hospitals uneconomical to operate. Conservatives argue that excessive federal regulations, Medicare reimbursement rates that don't cover rural costs, and liability concerns are squeezing smaller hospitals out of obstetrics entirely.

Republicans contend that Washington's one-size-fits-all healthcare mandates and low government reimbursement rates are driving rural hospital closures—the opposite of expanding access. They say deregulation and market-based solutions, not more federal intervention, will restore maternity services to underserved areas.

Colorado taxpayers and rural families should watch whether state lawmakers push for regulatory relief or demand federal action that may only compound the problem.
