# Colorado River Negotiations Collapse; States Demand Transparency

Colorado and six other states have spent years haggling over Colorado River water rights—and failed to produce a binding agreement. Now, taxpayers and lawmakers are demanding answers about what happened behind closed doors.

The negotiations, which involve Arizona, California, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah, Wyoming, and Colorado, have dragged on without public clarity on the core disputes or proposed solutions (CPR News). Republicans and water-conscious conservatives argue that opaque backroom dealing serves nobody—least of all Colorado farmers, ranchers, and Front Range municipalities dependent on reliable water allocation.

The lack of transparency raises a critical question: Are state negotiators protecting Colorado's water interests, or are they caving to pressure from California and federal bureaucrats? Colorado's agricultural economy and population growth depend on securing adequate Colorado River flows. Without public accountability, there's no way to know if state representatives are truly fighting for Colorado's share.

Meanwhile, on the campaign trail, a Douglas County Democrat running for the Colorado House was arrested for public intoxication in May (Denver Post), raising questions about candidate vetting in a swing legislative district.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's legislature demands full disclosure of Colorado River negotiation terms before any deal is ratified.
