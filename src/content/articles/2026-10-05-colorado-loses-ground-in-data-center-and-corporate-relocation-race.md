# Colorado Risks Being Left Behind in Data Center and Business Relocation Race

Colorado's business-friendly reputation is fading as Texas and Virginia aggressively court corporate relocations and data center investment—and state lawmakers aren't fighting back hard enough.

Texas is positioning itself as America's new economic powerhouse, with former House Financial Services Committee Chairman Jeb Hensarling highlighting the Texas Stock Exchange as the capstone to making the state the nation's premier destination for listing, incorporating, and headquartering businesses (Fox News). Meanwhile, Virginia has emerged as the frontrunner in the trillion-dollar artificial intelligence data center race, according to industry analysis (The Daily Wire).

Colorado once competed for this talent and capital. Today, the state's regulatory burden, energy costs, and tax environment are pushing companies elsewhere. While Virginia and Texas offer streamlined permitting, competitive power rates, and aggressive incentive packages, Colorado's progressive-dominated legislature continues piling on regulations and green-energy mandates that raise operating costs for data centers and tech firms.

The data center boom alone represents transformational economic opportunity—jobs, tax revenue, and infrastructure investment. Yet Colorado's leadership seems content to let other states win. Republicans and business advocates argue the state must cut red tape, stabilize energy prices, and offer competitive tax treatment to lure these operations.

**Watch whether Colorado's Republican minority can force a serious business-competitiveness agenda before the 2026 legislative session—or if the state cedes another generation of economic growth to Texas and Virginia.**
