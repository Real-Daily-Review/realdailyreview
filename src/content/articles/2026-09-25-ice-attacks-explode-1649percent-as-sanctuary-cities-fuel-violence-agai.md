# DHS Secretary Blames Sanctuary Cities as Attacks on ICE Officers Explode

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin is holding sanctuary politicians accountable for a staggering surge in violence against federal immigration enforcement officers—a crisis Colorado cities like Denver and Boulder have helped fuel through their own anti-ICE policies.

According to DHS data cited by Fox News and Just The News, attacks on ICE agents have skyrocketed to 647 incidents between January 2025 and September 2026, compared to just 37 during the same period under the Biden administration. That's a 1,649% increase.

Mullin directly attributes the violence to sanctuary jurisdictions that obstruct federal immigration enforcement and embolden anti-ICE activists. Republicans argue that when local politicians refuse to cooperate with ICE and shield illegal immigrants from deportation, they create lawlessness and endanger federal officers doing their jobs.

Democrats and progressive cities counter that sanctuary policies protect vulnerable immigrant communities from over-aggressive enforcement and that the Trump administration's hardline immigration stance provokes confrontation.

For Colorado, this matters: Denver's sanctuary ordinance and similar policies in Boulder have positioned the state as a haven for illegal immigration while federal agents face unprecedented danger. Taxpayers foot the bill for both the enforcement chaos and the social services drain.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature moves to strengthen or weaken sanctuary protections as the violence data becomes impossible to ignore.
