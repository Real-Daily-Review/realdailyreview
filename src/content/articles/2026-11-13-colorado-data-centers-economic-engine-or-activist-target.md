# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Wage War on Progress

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers aren't luxuries—they're the backbone of modern life, from cloud computing to AI infrastructure. Yet as Complete Colorado reports, these facilities face mounting pressure from environmental activists and progressive regulators who view them as energy hogs and climate threats.

The reality: data centers are essential infrastructure that enable everything from banking to streaming to artificial intelligence. They represent significant economic opportunity for Colorado, generating jobs and tax revenue while positioning the state as a tech hub. Republicans and free-market advocates argue that Colorado should welcome this growth, not strangle it with burdensome regulations.

The left's counterargument centers on energy consumption and water use. Progressives push for strict environmental reviews, renewable-energy mandates, and restrictions on facility expansion—policies that would drive investment to other states with friendlier business climates.

For Colorado taxpayers and workers, the stakes are clear: embrace data center development and reap the economic benefits, or cede this high-value sector to Texas, Arizona, or other competitors. The state's regulatory environment will determine whether Colorado remains a magnet for tech investment or becomes another cautionary tale of regulation-driven economic decline.
