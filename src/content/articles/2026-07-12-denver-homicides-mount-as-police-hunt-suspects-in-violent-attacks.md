# Denver's Crime Crisis Deepens as Police Struggle to Solve Violent Attacks

Denver's violent crime problem continues unabated, with police investigating multiple deadly assaults across the city in recent days—a troubling reminder that progressive leadership's soft-on-crime policies are failing to protect residents.

Denver police are seeking information on a fatal shooting that occurred Wednesday evening, while a separate incident saw a man killed in a rock attack near North Speer Boulevard and Larimer Street on Saturday, resulting in an arrest (9News Denver). The back-to-back homicides underscore a pattern of street violence plaguing Colorado's capital.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Denver's permissive approach to crime—including reduced enforcement, lenient prosecution, and insufficient support for law enforcement—has emboldened criminals and made the city less safe. They point to rising homicide rates and unsolved cases as evidence that progressive district attorneys and city leadership have prioritized ideology over public safety.

Democrats counter that violent crime is a complex issue requiring social services, mental health intervention, and addressing root causes of poverty—not simply more police presence.

For Colorado taxpayers and families, the message is clear: Denver's experiment in progressive criminal justice is producing bodies, not results. Watch whether the next mayoral election brings a shift toward public safety as a priority, or whether voters will tolerate continued disorder.
