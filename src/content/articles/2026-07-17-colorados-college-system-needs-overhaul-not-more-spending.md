# Colorado's Higher-Ed Crisis: Time to Demand Real Reform, Not More Spending

Colorado's bloated university system is pumping out debt-laden graduates while employers can't find skilled workers—a 20th-century model failing 21st-century Coloradans.

Both National Review and The Daily Wire highlight how America's college system has become a perversity factory: soaring tuition, crushing student debt, and curricula disconnected from actual job markets. Colorado taxpayers are footing the bill through subsidies to state universities while watching tuition climb anyway.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Colleges have abandoned their core mission—teaching—in favor of administrative bloat and ideological capture. Instead of throwing more tax dollars at universities, Colorado should demand accountability: tie state funding to graduation rates and employment outcomes, not enrollment numbers. Encourage trade schools and apprenticeships as legitimate alternatives. Require transparency on what degrees actually lead to jobs.

**The left's response?** More "investment" in higher education and expanded federal loan programs—which only inflates tuition further and deepens the debt trap.

Colorado families and employers deserve better. Watch whether the state legislature pushes universities to cut overhead and refocus on workforce readiness, or simply raises taxes to fund the same broken system.
