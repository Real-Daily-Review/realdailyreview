# Trump Pauses Iran Strikes, But Don't Expect Lasting Peace

President Trump canceled planned military strikes on Iran Thursday after claiming a diplomatic breakthrough, but conservatives are skeptical this cycle will end differently than before.

Trump cited Tehran's agreement to unspecified terms aimed at ending the conflict, warning Iran to "get their act together" (Just The News). However, Byron York, chief political correspondent for the Washington Examiner, noted on Fox News that Americans are witnessing a familiar pattern: Trump issues cautionary rhetoric—"Maybe it won't happen. I can't be sure, but it's coming soon"—before escalating tensions anew.

The halt in strikes represents Trump's preference for negotiation over immediate military action, a hallmark of his foreign policy approach. Yet conservatives worry the administration lacks clarity on what Iran has actually committed to, and whether Tehran will honor vague diplomatic agreements.

Republicans argue Trump's unpredictability can deter adversaries, but critics—including some conservatives—contend that repeated cycles of threats followed by negotiations signal weakness to hostile regimes.
