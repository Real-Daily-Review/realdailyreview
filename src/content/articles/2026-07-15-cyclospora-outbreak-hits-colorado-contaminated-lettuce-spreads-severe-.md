# Colorado Faces Cyclospora Outbreak as Contaminated Lettuce Spreads Illness

A parasite outbreak is hitting Colorado hard, with contaminated lettuce and salad greens linked to thousands of cases across the country causing severe gastrointestinal illness. According to KDVR Fox31 Denver, the cyclospora parasite triggers weeks of watery diarrhea and "frequent and sometimes explosive bowel movements," as the CDC confirms. Michigan health authorities have identified lettuce as a likely culprit, with cases spreading nationwide.

Colorado residents like Reba Pousma, a grad student and soccer coach, have reported debilitating stomach cramps requiring medical attention. The CDC warns that contaminated produce—particularly leafy greens—poses the greatest risk, though health officials have identified which foods remain safe for consumption.

The outbreak underscores a persistent vulnerability in America's food supply chain. While federal agencies coordinate response efforts, the incident raises questions about produce safety standards and inspection protocols that conservatives argue have grown bureaucratic without delivering results. Taxpayers foot the bill for food safety agencies that, critics contend, failed to prevent this outbreak from reaching thousands.

Coloradans should monitor local health department guidance on produce sources and watch whether federal regulators implement meaningful reforms or simply expand their budgets without accountability.
