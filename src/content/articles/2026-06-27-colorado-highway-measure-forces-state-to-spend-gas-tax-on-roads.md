# Colorado Highway Funding Measure Heads to November Ballot

Colorado voters will get a rare chance this November to actually enforce what they've already approved: a ballot measure would require state agencies to spend all motor vehicle and gas tax revenue exclusively on road repairs, blocking politicians from raiding transportation funds for other priorities.

The initiative, announced by the Secretary of State's office (Denver Post), addresses a persistent conservative complaint—that dedicated revenue streams get diverted. Republicans and taxpayer advocates argue the state has collected highway taxes for years while roads deteriorated, with money siphoned elsewhere in the budget.

Complete Colorado reports the measure represents a "guarantee" that highway funding stays on roads, framing it as a voter protection against legislative sleight-of-hand. The ballot effort reflects GOP frustration with how Colorado manages dedicated revenue, a flashpoint for conservatives who see government as chronically unable to stick to its own spending commitments.

Democrats have not yet articulated formal opposition in available reporting, though the measure implicitly challenges the legislature's budget flexibility—a power both parties have historically guarded.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this November vote signals whether the state will finally honor its own transportation commitments or continue the shell game.
