# Denver Sues to Block ICE from Polling Places—But the Real Issue Is Election Integrity vs. Immigration Enforcement

Denver and Latino advocacy groups have filed suit to keep ICE agents away from voting sites, claiming federal immigration enforcement would intimidate voters at polling places and ballot drop boxes (Colorado Sun). The lawsuit argues that armed federal agents conducting immigration arrests would interfere with "the smooth administration of elections at the local level" (Just The News).

Republicans and conservatives counter that this lawsuit conflates two separate duties: ICE's lawful authority to enforce immigration law and election officials' responsibility to secure polling places. The presence of federal law enforcement shouldn't deter *legal* voters from casting ballots—only those present illegally need concern themselves with ICE operations.

**The Colorado angle:** This directly affects Denver voters and raises questions about ballot security and who gets to vote in Colorado elections. If sanctuary-city policies prevent federal agents from operating near polling sites, it creates a de facto safe zone for illegal immigrants—undermining both immigration enforcement and election integrity.

Democrats frame this as voter intimidation; conservatives see it as an attempt to shield illegal immigrants from lawful deportation proceedings. The lawsuit reveals the left's priority: protecting illegal aliens over enforcing federal immigration law.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado courts side with Denver's sanctuary-city stance or uphold federal ICE authority—a decision that could reshape immigration enforcement statewide.
