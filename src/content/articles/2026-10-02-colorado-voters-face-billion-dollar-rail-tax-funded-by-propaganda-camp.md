# Front Range Rail Boondoggle: Colorado Voters Face Billion-Dollar Train Tab

Colorado voters are being asked to bankroll a Front Range rail project that even its own promoters must advertise with paid influencers and targeted mailers—a telltale sign the public isn't buying it on merit.

The so-called Colorado Connector would cost billions in taxpayer money for a train that ridership projections suggest few Coloradans will actually use. According to Complete Colorado, the project represents a choice between "blowing billions on a train few will ride and keeping that money in their own pockets."

What's particularly galling: millions in tax dollars are already funding a slick "education" campaign to sell voters on the idea, complete with paid Instagram influencers and targeted mailers, CPR News reports. That's not public information—that's propaganda on the taxpayer dime.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this is classic government overreach: politicians and special interests using public funds to manufacture consent for a massive spending project that benefits connected contractors and transit bureaucrats far more than ordinary Coloradans. The money would be better spent on roads, water infrastructure, or left in taxpayers' pockets entirely.

Democrats counter that rail investment is necessary for Front Range growth and emissions reduction, though they've struggled to explain why voters should trust projections from the same agencies that oversee chronically underperforming transit systems statewide.

Watch whether Colorado voters reject this tax grab at the ballot box—and whether the rail lobby tries again with an even bigger ask.
