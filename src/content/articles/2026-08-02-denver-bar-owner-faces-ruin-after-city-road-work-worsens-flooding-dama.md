# Denver Bar Owner Left Holding the Bag After City Infrastructure Fails During Storm

A Denver bar owner is facing financial ruin after heavy rainfall sent roughly 3 feet of water into the basement of his East Colfax Avenue business, causing thousands in damage and threatening the establishment's survival (KDVR Fox31). The Squire Lounge's owner blames recent road construction for exacerbating the flooding—the worst in 22 years—suggesting that city infrastructure work may have worsened drainage and left his business vulnerable (9News Denver).

This is a cautionary tale for Colorado small-business owners: when government projects go wrong, private citizens pay the price. Denver's road work appears to have created conditions that turned a heavy rain event into a catastrophic loss for a local entrepreneur. The city has yet to clarify whether it bears responsibility or will assist with recovery.

Conservatives argue that government agencies routinely prioritize project timelines and budgets over practical outcomes, leaving taxpayers and business owners to absorb the fallout. When public works damage private property, accountability should follow—not excuses.

Watch whether Denver's city government acknowledges fault, offers restitution, or leaves this business owner to fight insurance claims and rebuild alone.
