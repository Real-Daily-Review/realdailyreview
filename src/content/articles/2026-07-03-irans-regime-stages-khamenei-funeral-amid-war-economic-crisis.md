# Iran's Regime Stages Khamenei Funeral as US Weighs Next Move

America's adversaries are gathering in Tehran this weekend for a carefully choreographed show of strength—but Iran's theocracy is anything but stable underneath the pageantry.

Iran is preparing a six-day funeral spectacle for late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, killed in the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict, with militant allies and authoritarian regimes descending on the capital beginning Saturday (Washington Examiner). The regime expects millions to flood Tehran's streets in what officials hope will project power and unity, despite the country's mounting economic turmoil and internal uncertainty over succession.

The funeral timing matters. Iran's leadership is signaling continuity to domestic audiences and hostile foreign powers alike—a critical move when the regime faces war pressures, economic collapse, and questions about who controls the Revolutionary Guard. The appearance of the Guard's leader for the first time in months suggests internal jockeying over power is underway (Washington Examiner).

For American policymakers, the funeral presents both risk and opportunity. A weakened Iranian regime is less capable of threatening U.S. interests in the Middle East, but the gathering of hostile actors—and the succession uncertainty—demands careful monitoring of what comes next.

Conservatives should watch whether the Biden-Harris administration uses this moment of Iranian vulnerability to strengthen deterrence or whether it retreats into the failed diplomatic posture that emboldened Tehran for years.
