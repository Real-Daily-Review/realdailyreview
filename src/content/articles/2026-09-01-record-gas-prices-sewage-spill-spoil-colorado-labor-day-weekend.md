# Colorado Faces Double Holiday Blow: Record Gas Prices and Water Contamination

Coloradans heading out for Labor Day weekend are getting hit from both ends—at the pump and in the water.

Gas prices are expected to reach a record $4.03 per gallon over the holiday, according to 9News Denver, driven by global oil and fuel market disruptions. That's the highest Labor Day weekend price on record, meaning Colorado families will pay more to drive anywhere this long weekend.

Meanwhile, state and local agencies are warning against water contact after a massive sewage spill contaminated the Arkansas River and reached John Martin Reservoir in southern Colorado (KDVR Fox31). Officials are monitoring safety conditions ahead of the holiday as communities assess the damage.

The twin crises underscore two conservative concerns: first, energy policy that constrains domestic oil production keeps Americans dependent on global market volatility and price shocks. Second, aging infrastructure failures—like sewage systems—demand accountability and smart maintenance spending, not endless new regulations that burden taxpayers without fixing real problems.

Coloradans should watch whether state environmental regulators use this sewage incident to justify new water restrictions or agricultural limitations, or whether they focus on infrastructure repair and accountability.
