# Trump Cabinet Secretary Faces Court Challenge Over Religious Emails

A Biden-appointed federal judge has ruled that Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins likely violated the Constitution by sending Christian-themed messages to agency employees, including an Easter "He is risen" email and Christmas greetings. U.S. District Judge Trina L. Thompson of Northern California granted a preliminary injunction Thursday, blocking further religious communications from Rollins.

Conservatives argue the ruling represents judicial overreach into free speech and religious expression. Rollins, a Trump appointee, was simply sharing her faith—a right guaranteed to all Americans, they contend. The decision reflects the broader progressive assault on religious liberty in the workplace, particularly targeting conservative Christians in government.

Democrats and civil liberties groups counter that government employees have a right to a workplace free from religious coercion, and that official agency communications should remain secular to respect all faiths.

Meanwhile, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has relocated to a military base following credible Iranian assassination threats, according to Fox News. Iran has publicly mocked the move, escalating tensions with the Trump administration.

**Colorado angle:** Federal agriculture policy directly affects Colorado ranchers and farmers; this case signals how courts may constrain Trump appointees' authority over USDA operations affecting the state's agricultural economy.

**Watch:** Whether this preliminary injunction stands on appeal—and whether similar challenges target other Trump Cabinet officials' communications.
