# Colorado I-70 Winter Chaos Exposes Persistent Safety Crisis on Mountain Corridor

Colorado's I-70 corridor descended into gridlock Friday and Saturday as multiple pileups—including a 12-car wreck—snarled traffic during snowy conditions, raising hard questions about whether the state's infrastructure and driver behavior can handle Colorado winters.

The Colorado State Patrol responded to a series of multi-car collisions along the stretch, with videos capturing the intense, dangerous conditions that have become routine on I-70 during winter weather. Safety warnings followed, but critics argue the state has long known this corridor is a bottleneck that demands better planning and enforcement.

Conservatives and transportation experts point out that Colorado's I-70 remains chronically underfunded and poorly managed. While the state continues to pour money into transit projects favored by progressive urban planners, the backbone corridor connecting the Front Range to Western Slope communities—critical for commerce, energy workers, and families—gets patchwork solutions. The real issue: too many drivers ignore conditions, follow too closely, and treat a mountain pass like a suburban commute.

State officials should prioritize I-70 safety infrastructure and driver accountability over feel-good transit experiments in Denver. Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand a serious, long-term I-70 safety and capacity plan in the 2025 legislative session—or continue kicking the can.
