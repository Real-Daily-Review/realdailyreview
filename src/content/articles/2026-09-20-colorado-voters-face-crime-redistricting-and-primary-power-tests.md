# Colorado Voters Face Three Ballot Tests on Crime, Elections, and Party Power

Colorado voters will decide three consequential measures this cycle that cut to the heart of how the state handles violent crime, congressional maps, and primary elections—each with real stakes for conservative governance.

**Proposition 133** elevates sexual trafficking of minors to a Class 1 felony, making it eligible for life sentences (Complete Colorado). Conservatives argue this closes a dangerous gap in Colorado law; the measure reflects voter frustration with soft-on-crime policies that have plagued urban areas.

**Initiative 256** would ban partisan gerrymandering in congressional redistricting, imposing guardrails against map-drawing designed to favor either party (Complete Colorado). Republicans have long complained about Democratic manipulation of district lines; this measure could constrain both sides, though conservatives remain divided on whether it genuinely prevents abuse or simply locks in current Democratic advantages.

Meanwhile, **unaffiliated voter registration continues climbing** in Colorado, diluting party primary power (Complete Colorado). As party-affiliated voters skip caucuses, unaffiliateds gain leverage—a trend that could reshape which candidates reach general elections and whether establishment Republicans maintain control of nomination contests.

All three ballot items reflect deeper anxieties: criminal justice, electoral fairness, and party relevance. Conservatives should watch whether these measures actually deliver on their promises or become tools for further progressive overreach.
