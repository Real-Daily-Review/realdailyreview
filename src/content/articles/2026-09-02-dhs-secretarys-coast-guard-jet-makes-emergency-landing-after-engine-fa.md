# DHS Secretary's Coast Guard Plane Makes Emergency Landing After Engine Failure

A U.S. Coast Guard jet carrying Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin lost an engine mid-flight Wednesday and made an emergency landing near Washington, D.C., in what pilots later called a first-time experience.

The C-37A aircraft experienced right-engine failure, prompting the pilot to declare an emergency and request immediate landing at Reagan National Airport (Fox News, Washington Examiner). According to cockpit audio, the pilot calmly reported the malfunction to air traffic control.

Mullin praised the Coast Guard crew's response, posting on X that pilots executed a single-engine landing "with routine professionalism" and noted it was their first such procedure. "We could not have been in better hands," the DHS secretary said (Just The News).

The incident underscores both the skill of military aviation crews and the critical importance of maintaining federal aircraft fleets—a responsibility that falls under the Department of Homeland Security's purview. While no injuries were reported, the emergency highlights infrastructure and equipment readiness questions that Congress should examine during budget deliberations.

Conservatives should monitor whether this incident prompts scrutiny of federal aircraft maintenance protocols and whether DHS leadership will prioritize operational readiness over bureaucratic expansion.
