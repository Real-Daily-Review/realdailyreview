# U.S. and Iran Explore Deal to End Conflict

The U.S. and Iran are considering a potential agreement to end their conflict, with Pakistan expressing optimism about near-term progress. According to NPR, Pakistani Foreign Ministry spokesperson Tahir Andrabi said the country's "hope and expectation is for an agreement sooner rather than later." Tehran is currently reviewing a Washington-backed proposal, according to the Financial Times, which reports that President Donald Trump says the deal could bring the conflict to an end.

The potential agreement has already moved markets: oil prices dipped below $100 per barrel on deal hopes, the Financial Times reports.

PBS NewsHour convened experts to examine the proposal's prospects. The panel included Alan Eyre of the Middle East Institute, who served as a senior member of the Obama administration's negotiating team for the previous Iran nuclear deal, and Miad Maleki, a senior fellow at the Foundation for the Defense of Democracies.

Details of the specific proposal remain limited in available reporting. Watch for announcements from Tehran on whether it accepts the U.S. framework and any timeline for formal negotiations.
