# Trump Ditches New Air Force One at NATO Summit—Raises Questions About $400M Aircraft

President Trump opted to fly the aging Air Force One back to Washington rather than the newly acquired aircraft during his NATO summit visit, citing a desire to showcase the plane to U.S. troops stationed abroad (Fox News). Trump announced the newer jet would be positioned at Mildenhall Air Force Base in the United Kingdom for service personnel tours following the NATO gathering.

The decision sparked scrutiny, however. The Washington Examiner noted the $400 million aircraft purchase raised more questions than Trump's explanation answered, with observers questioning the logistics and reasoning behind leaving the new plane behind while returning on the older model.

The move highlights ongoing debate over presidential aircraft spending and operational decisions. While Trump has championed American military readiness and troop morale—consistent with his "America First" platform—the decision to acquire a new presidential aircraft at that price point and then not utilize it immediately invites scrutiny from fiscal conservatives concerned about Pentagon spending priorities.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether this aircraft decision becomes part of broader Pentagon budget debates in Congress, particularly as defense spending remains a flashpoint between fiscal hawks and those demanding robust military investment.
