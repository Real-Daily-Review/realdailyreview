# Biden's Memoir Flops: 21,000 First-Week Sales Trail Obama, Clinton by Miles

President Joe Biden's much-hyped memoir, *Promise Me, America*, landed with a thud—selling just 21,000 copies in its first week, according to Circana data cited by Fox News and the Washington Examiner. That's a stark contrast to Barack Obama's and Bill Clinton's presidential memoirs, which vastly outsold Biden's debut.

The book did top the New York Times nonfiction bestseller list, but the raw numbers tell a different story about Biden's political brand heading into 2024. While the White House likely hoped the memoir would energize Democrats and rehabilitate Biden's image after a rocky first term, the sales figures suggest limited enthusiasm—even among the party faithful.

For Colorado conservatives, this is a reminder that Biden's political capital continues to erode. Voters across the state who rejected his energy policies, border chaos, and inflation-driving spending spree aren't exactly lining up to buy his life story either. The memoir's weak performance underscores what Republicans have argued all along: Biden's agenda has failed to win hearts and minds.

Watch whether Biden's team attempts a media blitz to boost sales—or quietly moves on to other messaging strategies ahead of November.
