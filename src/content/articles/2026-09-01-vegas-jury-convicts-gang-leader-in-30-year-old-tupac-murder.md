# Tupac Murder Case Closes After 30 Years—But Colorado Angle Missing

A Las Vegas jury convicted Duane "Keffe D" Davis of first-degree murder for orchestrating the 1996 killing of rap icon Tupac Shakur, marking the first conviction in one of hip-hop's most notorious cold cases. After three hours of deliberation following a weekslong trial, jurors found Davis—a former high-ranking "shot caller" in an LA chapter of the Crips gang—guilty of orchestrating the drive-by shooting, though prosecutors did not accuse him of pulling the trigger (New York Post, Just The News).

Prosecutors presented testimony from 24 witnesses over nine days, with the defense calling three. According to prosecutors, Davis couldn't resist bragging about his role in Shakur's September 7, 1996 murder, a pattern that ultimately helped build their case (New York Post).

The conviction, reached nearly 30 years to the day after Shakur's death, represents a rare victory for cold-case investigators and the justice system's ability to pursue accountability across decades.
