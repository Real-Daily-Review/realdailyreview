# Colorado Voters Face New Mail Ballot ID Rules as Election Security Debate Intensifies

Colorado's all-mail voting system is about to get stricter—and the state's election integrity debate just shifted into higher gear. Initiative 362, heading to November voters, would impose new voter authentication requirements for mail ballots, tightening what critics argue has been a dangerously loose process.

Republicans and election security advocates argue Colorado's mail-ballot system has invited vulnerabilities. Complete Colorado reports that the Griswold controversy—referencing former Secretary of State Jena Griswold's troubled tenure—has made the case for reform, particularly in how candidates can win with minority support in crowded fields.

Democrats and county election officials push back hard. The Denver Post's election coverage emphasizes that Colorado county clerks have "reliably and responsibly" managed mail voting for over a decade, with nothing fundamentally broken requiring new ID mandates.

The stakes matter for Colorado taxpayers and election confidence. Tighter voter authentication could reduce fraud risk—or, Democrats warn, create unnecessary barriers. Either way, how voters decide on Initiative 362 will reshape Colorado's election administration heading into 2028.
