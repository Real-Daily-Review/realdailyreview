# Canadian Wildfire Smoke Blankets Colorado, Raising Questions About Cross-Border Air Policy

Colorado faces degraded air quality as smoke from massive Canadian wildfires drifts south, forcing residents to limit outdoor activity and raising conservative concerns about whether Washington will use the crisis to expand environmental regulations without addressing root causes.

At least 12 U.S. states are experiencing "hazardous" and "unhealthy" air conditions, according to 9News Denver, with the Great Lakes region hit hardest. Just The News reports that over a dozen Ontario wildfires—including one that burned more than 130,000 acres in Wabakimi Provincial Park north of Lake Superior—are the primary culprits.

For Colorado, the smoke adds to an already contentious wildfire season. Conservatives argue that rather than knee-jerk federal regulations, policymakers should focus on aggressive forest management, controlled burns, and removing dead timber that fuels catastrophic blazes. The left typically responds that climate change intensifies fire seasons, demanding stricter emissions controls—a framing conservatives reject as using natural disasters to justify costly regulations that harm energy workers and manufacturers.

The cross-border nature of this pollution also highlights a gap: Canada's wildfire management practices directly impact American air quality, yet federal coordination remains minimal. Republicans should demand clarity on whether the Biden administration will negotiate with Ottawa on forest stewardship or simply use the smoke as cover for domestic regulatory expansion.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for bilateral wildfire prevention agreements or allows this to become another vehicle for progressive climate mandates.
