# Gov. Polis Caught Overselling Colorado's Education Ranking—While State Grapples With School Accountability

Governor Polis is cherry-picking data to inflate Colorado's education performance, according to *Complete Colorado*, which found the governor selectively cited U.S. News rankings while downplaying metrics where the state underperforms. The selective use of statistics by public officials reveals how education claims often obscure rather than illuminate actual classroom outcomes—a problem Colorado taxpayers should demand transparency about.

Meanwhile, the state's education landscape faces deeper structural questions. *CPR News* reports that a regional education cooperative (ERBOCES) that operated a controversial "public Christian school" faces dissolution after failing to attract buyers. One board member called the lack of interest "a collective, statewide referendum" on the organization's viability, suggesting even within Colorado's education establishment, confidence in the group has collapsed.

For conservatives, these stories underscore a familiar pattern: government agencies oversell results while avoiding accountability. Colorado's education spending has grown substantially, yet the state's actual performance rankings remain middling. When officials must cherry-pick data to look good, taxpayers should ask hard questions about where their dollars are going.

Watch whether Polis's administration will acknowledge the gap between its rhetoric and Colorado's real education metrics—or continue the spin.
