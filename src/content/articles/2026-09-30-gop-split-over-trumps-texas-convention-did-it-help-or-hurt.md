# Trump's Texas Convention Gambit Splits GOP Over Campaign Impact

President Trump's September midterm convention visit to Texas has sparked an internal Republican dust-up over whether the event helped or hurt GOP candidates. Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton reportedly told allies that Trump's appearance tanked polling numbers across the board, claiming "everybody's numbers dropped" (Daily Caller). But Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC), chairman of Senate Republicans' campaign arm, pushed back hard, telling reporters Paxton was "just wrong" and insisting the convention "was a boost in the right direction" (Washington Examiner).

The disagreement exposes tension within Republican ranks just weeks before the midterms. Paxton's critique suggests Trump's star power may not automatically translate to coattails for down-ballot candidates—a concern for Colorado Republicans hoping to flip competitive House and Senate seats. Scott's defense reflects the party establishment's investment in Trump's continued political dominance.

For Colorado conservatives, the real question is whether national GOP infighting over Trump's strategy will distract from local messaging on inflation, border security, and energy policy—issues that matter far more to voters in swing districts like CO-3 and CO-8.
