# Republicans Face Slim Majorities as Record Early Voting Reshapes 2026 Landscape

Control of Congress hangs in the balance as Election Day arrives with unprecedented early voting already reshaping the electorate. Fox News reports that over 54 million ballots have been cast nationwide before polls opened, shattering previous records and signaling a highly mobilized electorate on both sides.

Republicans are defending slim majorities in both chambers—a precarious position that leaves little room for defections on key votes. For Colorado conservatives, the stakes are particularly high: a Democratic takeover of Congress could mean aggressive pushes for stricter gun regulations, expanded federal land grabs, and higher energy taxes that would hammer Colorado's oil and gas sector and rural economies.

Democrats are banking on turnout to flip seats in suburban districts and energy-producing regions where frustration over inflation and regulatory overreach has simmered. Republicans counter that their agenda—lower taxes, energy independence, and border security—resonates with working Coloradans tired of progressive governance.

The record early voting suggests neither party can take anything for granted. Turnout operations and ground game will determine whether Republicans hold the line or Democrats gain ground in purple Colorado districts.
