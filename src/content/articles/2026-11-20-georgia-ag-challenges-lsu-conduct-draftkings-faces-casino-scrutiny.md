# Georgia AG Challenges LSU Over 'Destructive' Conduct; Sports Gambling Debate Heats Up

Georgia's attorney general is calling out LSU for what he describes as a "pattern of destructive behavior," escalating scrutiny of collegiate athletics governance at a moment when state-level officials are increasingly willing to challenge institutional overreach.

The complaint, lodged with the SEC commissioner, reflects growing conservative concern about accountability in higher education—particularly when universities operate with minimal oversight and substantial taxpayer backing. Georgia AG Christopher Carr's intervention signals that state officials won't defer to conference authority when institutional conduct crosses ethical lines.

Meanwhile, the gambling debate is sharpening. Florida's attorney general is challenging DraftKings' classification of its card-game offerings as "skill-based," arguing the platform operates as an unlicensed casino. DraftKings takes $10 from each $100 pot, a house cut that looks suspiciously like casino math to regulators (Daily Caller). The company insists it's skill, not chance—a claim that will likely face legal pressure across multiple states.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's Division of Gaming and sports betting regulators should watch how Florida's challenge plays out. If DraftKings loses, Colorado's own skill-games licensing framework could face similar pressure, potentially affecting revenue streams and regulatory clarity for operators operating in the state.

**Watch:** Whether other state AGs join Georgia and Florida in challenging both institutional and gaming-industry conduct—signaling a broader shift toward state-level enforcement independence.
