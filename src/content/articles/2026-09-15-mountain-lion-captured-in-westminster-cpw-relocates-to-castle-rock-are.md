# Colorado Parks and Wildlife Relocates Mountain Lion After Westminster Sighting

A young male mountain lion lounging in a Westminster tree this week forced road closures and a rapid response from Colorado Parks and Wildlife—a reminder that suburban sprawl continues pushing wildlife into neighborhoods where families and pets face real danger.

The animal was captured and relocated west of Castle Rock, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31. The sighting late Tuesday morning triggered an immediate lockdown of the area as CPW officials worked to safely remove the big cat.

This incident underscores a growing tension in Colorado's Front Range: development keeps encroaching on wildlife habitat, yet government agencies often prioritize animal relocation over addressing the root cause—uncontrolled suburban expansion into mountain lion territory. Conservatives argue that land-use planning should prioritize property rights and economic development, but also acknowledge that coexistence requires clear boundaries and accountability when wildlife enters populated areas.

CPW's response was professional and swift, but the real question remains: how many more of these encounters will Colorado tolerate before addressing the mismatch between growth patterns and wildlife management? Taxpayers should watch whether local governments and the state coordinate on development limits in high-risk zones—or continue treating each mountain lion sighting as an isolated crisis.
