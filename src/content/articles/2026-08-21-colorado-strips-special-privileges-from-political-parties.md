# Colorado Moves to Strip Special Privileges From Political Parties—A Win for Taxpayers

Colorado is taking a rare step toward limiting government favoritism by reconsidering special recognition and privileges granted to political parties, according to *Complete Colorado*. The move reflects growing conservative concern that state government shouldn't be in the business of bankrolling or elevating partisan machinery at taxpayer expense.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives have long argued that government resources—from office space to administrative support—should serve the public interest, not party interests. When states grant official status or funding advantages to Democratic or Republican organizations, they're essentially forcing taxpayers to subsidize partisan operations, a principle that crosses ideological lines.

Democrats typically counter that party infrastructure serves legitimate civic functions, including voter registration and election administration support. However, the Colorado initiative suggests lawmakers recognize the fundamental problem: government should remain neutral between parties, not pick winners.

The practical impact on Colorado could be significant. Stripping special privileges forces parties to fund their own operations through voluntary donations rather than relying on state resources or official designation. This aligns with conservative principles of limited government and fiscal restraint.

**Watch whether other states follow Colorado's lead—and whether Democrats attempt to restore party privileges through the back door via "voter engagement" grants.**
