# Denver Mayor Pitches 'Disciplined' Budget While City Grapples With Spending Pressures

Mayor Mike Johnston is calling his 2027 budget proposal a model of fiscal restraint, claiming "disciplined budgeting and strategic investment" are fueling Denver's economic growth (9News Denver). But conservatives should scrutinize what "strategic investment" really means in a city where progressive spending has driven up costs for residents and businesses alike.

Johnston's framing masks a familiar pattern: government spending rebranded as growth strategy. Meanwhile, Councilwoman Shontel Lewis, who's running for mayor, is doubling down on progressive priorities, pledging to focus on "helping the working class and impoverished folks" (9News Denver)—code for expanded social programs and higher taxes to fund them.

For Colorado taxpayers watching Denver's trajectory, the real question isn't whether Johnston's budget sounds disciplined in a press release. It's whether Denver's government is actually cutting waste, reducing the regulatory burden on small business, or lowering the tax load on working families. The sources don't detail actual spending cuts or tax relief—only rhetoric about "investment."

**Watch for the final budget details: Does Johnston's plan include real spending reductions, or just slower growth in the rate of increase?**
