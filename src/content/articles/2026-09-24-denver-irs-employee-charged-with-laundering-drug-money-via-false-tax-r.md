# Former IRS Employee in Denver Charged With Filing False Tax Returns for Drug Dealers

A former Internal Revenue Service employee and Denver tax preparer faces a dozen federal charges for allegedly preparing fraudulent tax returns and laundering money for drug dealers, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver. The case underscores how government employees entrusted with enforcing tax law can become vectors for criminal enterprise—a reminder that the IRS itself requires serious oversight.

The charges include money laundering and filing false returns, crimes that exploit the very tax system this individual was hired to administer. Colorado taxpayers already shoulder a heavy burden funding the IRS's bloated bureaucracy; discovering that agency insiders are helping criminals dodge accountability while honest Coloradans foot the bill adds insult to injury.

The case also raises questions about vetting and internal controls at the IRS—an agency that has faced repeated scandals involving political targeting and mismanagement. Republicans have long argued the IRS wields too much power over American citizens and businesses without sufficient accountability. This Denver case provides fresh evidence that the agency's problems run deeper than policy disputes.

Conservatives should watch whether federal prosecutors pursue maximum penalties and whether Congress demands a full audit of IRS hiring and oversight practices.
