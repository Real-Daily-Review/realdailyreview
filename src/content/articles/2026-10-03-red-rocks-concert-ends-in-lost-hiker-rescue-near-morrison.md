# Teens' Red Rocks Hike Ends in Rescue—Another Reminder That Colorado's Outdoors Demands Respect

Two teenagers learned a hard lesson about Colorado's backcountry after attempting to hike back to Morrison from a Red Rocks concert: nature doesn't care about your plans. West Metro Fire Rescue responded to the lost hikers on Wednesday, highlighting a recurring problem in the Denver metro area—outdoor inexperience meeting unforgiving terrain.

The teens' decision to hike out after the show "seemed like a great idea – until it was time to find their way back," according to West Metro Fire Rescue's Facebook post. Both outlets (9News Denver and KDVR Fox31) confirmed the rescue operation, though neither provided specifics on how long the search took or what conditions the teens faced.

This incident underscores why Colorado's outdoor culture demands preparation, navigation skills, and honest self-assessment. Red Rocks sits in rugged foothill country; the trails aren't theme park paths. Too many visitors—especially younger Coloradans—treat the mountains like a backdrop for social media rather than a serious environment requiring respect and planning.

The good news: rescue crews responded and brought them home safely. The lesson: know your limits, carry a map, tell someone where you're going, and don't assume a concert venue's proximity to civilization means an easy exit.

Conservatives and outdoor enthusiasts should watch whether Colorado counties continue adequately funding search-and-rescue operations—or whether budget cuts force taxpayers to foot the bill for preventable rescues.
