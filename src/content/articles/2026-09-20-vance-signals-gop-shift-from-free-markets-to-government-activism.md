# GOP's Hamiltonian Pivot: Vance Signals End of Free-Market Orthodoxy

Vice President JD Vance has declared the Republican Party's economic philosophy is shifting decisively away from Milton Friedman's free-market tradition toward Alexander Hamilton's model of active government intervention—and he's calling it progress.

Speaking recently, Vance said American right-of-center policy is now "much more Alexander Hamilton than it is Milton Friedman," and he called that shift "obviously a good thing" (Washington Examiner). The distinction matters: Friedman's framework treated markets as the default and government action as the exception requiring justification. Hamilton's approach treats a strong central government as the engine of economic policy.

**The conservative concern:** This represents a fundamental break from decades of Republican orthodoxy. Free-market conservatives argue that government intervention, however well-intentioned, typically creates unintended consequences, stifles innovation, and wastes taxpayer dollars. For Colorado—home to energy producers, ranchers, and small businesses that depend on regulatory restraint—a GOP embrace of Hamiltonian state activism could mean more federal overreach in land use, energy development, and commerce.

The shift signals the Trump-Vance administration may pursue industrial policy, tariffs, and strategic government spending rather than deregulation and tax cuts. Colorado conservatives and business leaders should monitor whether this translates into federal mandates or spending that undercuts state sovereignty and market competition.
