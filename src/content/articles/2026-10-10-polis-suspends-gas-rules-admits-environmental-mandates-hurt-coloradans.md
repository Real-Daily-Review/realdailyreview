# Polis Suspends Environmental Rules to Lower Gas Prices—But Conservatives Question the Real Fix

Governor Jared Polis issued an executive order this week to temporarily ease environmental and transportation regulations in a bid to lower Colorado gas prices, which are averaging $4.18 per gallon for regular unleaded and $6.21 for diesel (9News Denver). Polis cited geopolitical tensions, including the ongoing conflict with Iran, as justification for the move (KDVR Fox31).

The order represents an unusual admission: Colorado's own environmental mandates are driving up fuel costs for everyday Coloradans. Conservatives argue this proves the real problem isn't global markets—it's government overreach. Rather than suspending rules temporarily, Republicans say Polis should permanently roll back the regulatory burden that makes Colorado's fuel more expensive than neighboring states.

The temporary nature of the order is telling. If easing these rules brings relief at the pump, conservatives ask, why not make the changes permanent? Polis's approach amounts to a band-aid on a self-inflicted wound: Colorado's strict fuel blends and environmental policies inflate costs for working families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation.

Democrats counter that temporary measures protect both consumers and environmental standards, but conservatives see this as regulatory theater—acknowledging the problem without fixing it.

Watch whether the legislature moves to permanently eliminate Colorado's fuel-blend mandates before the next election cycle.
