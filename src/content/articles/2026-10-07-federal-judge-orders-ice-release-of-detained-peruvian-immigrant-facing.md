# ICE Releases Detained Peruvian Immigrant After Hunger Strike—Despite Domestic Assault Allegations

Federal judges are ordering ICE to release detainees on grounds that should alarm Colorado taxpayers watching border enforcement collapse. Martin Soto, a Peruvian national held at Delaney Hall detention center in Newark, New Jersey, was released Tuesday after leading a Memorial Day weekend hunger and labor strike—despite facing domestic assault allegations, according to the Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire.

A federal judge ordered Soto's release, though details on the judicial reasoning remain unclear from available reporting. The Daily Wire notes Soto's wife, Gabriela, claimed he was "abducted" by ICE while purchasing diapers in February, framing the detention as overreach. Conservative outlets highlight the domestic violence allegations as evidence that release decisions prioritize activist pressure over public safety.

**The conservative concern:** When federal courts side with detainees leading organized strikes against immigration enforcement, it signals that judicial intervention—not immigration law—now determines who stays in custody. Republicans argue this undermines ICE's ability to enforce deportations and protect communities from individuals with criminal histories.

Colorado has absorbed significant costs from illegal immigration, from emergency room visits to law enforcement resources. As federal courts continue ordering releases, state taxpayers foot the bill for services to individuals the immigration system failed to remove.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation demands answers on how many similar releases are occurring nationwide—and whether state officials will push back on federal court decisions that effectively nullify immigration enforcement.
