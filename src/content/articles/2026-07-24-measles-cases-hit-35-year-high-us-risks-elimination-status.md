# Measles Surge Threatens U.S. Elimination Status as Cases Hit 35-Year High

The United States is on track to lose its measles elimination status for the first time since 2000, with 2026 already recording more cases than any year since 1991, according to CDC data cited by 9News Denver and Just The News.

The spike raises serious questions about public health policy and vaccine uptake across the country. While no deaths have been reported so far this year—compared to three deaths in 2025—the resurgence of a once-controlled disease signals potential gaps in immunization coverage and disease surveillance.

For Colorado families and healthcare providers, the uptick demands clarity: Are vaccination rates declining statewide? Are there specific outbreaks in Colorado communities? State health officials should provide transparent data on local measles cases and immunization rates rather than generic federal messaging.

Conservatives and public health advocates generally support voluntary vaccination programs and parental choice, but they also recognize measles as a serious threat when immunity levels drop. The key question isn't whether measles matters—it does—but whether government responses will respect individual medical decisions or veer toward mandates and overreach.

Colorado taxpayers and parents should monitor whether state health authorities propose new vaccine mandates or emergency regulations that bypass legislative oversight.
