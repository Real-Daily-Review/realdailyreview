# Denver Commits $40M in Taxpayer Funds to Host 2028 Democratic Convention

Denver is spending $40 million in public money—drawn from property and sales taxes at Union Station—to land the 2028 Democratic National Convention, according to 9News Denver. The city is betting that hosting the party's quadrennial showcase will boost downtown development and tourism.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue the city should not be subsidizing a partisan political event with taxpayer dollars. The funds come from revenue streams that could fund core services—police, roads, schools—or be returned to residents through tax relief. Hosting conventions is a private-sector function; if Democrats want to gather in Denver, party leadership and corporate sponsors should foot the bill, not working families and small-business owners.

The Denver Post reports that national Democrats are using the city as a "reality check," acknowledging that public sentiment remains skeptical of the party's direction. Yet rather than address voter concerns, Denver's Democratic leadership is spending millions to stage a convention that will largely benefit hotels, catering firms, and media operations while ordinary taxpayers absorb the cost.

**Colorado angle:** This sets a troubling precedent for how Denver allocates public resources—prioritizing partisan theater over fiscal restraint.
