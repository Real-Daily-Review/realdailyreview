# Aspen Acres Fire Devastation Exposes Colorado's Wildfire Crisis—and Government's Failure to Prevent It

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire has destroyed at least 780 structures across Pueblo and Custer counties, according to aerial analysis by Vexcel Data—a figure that dwarfs official county damage assessments still being tallied (Colorado Sun). The destruction includes homes, garages, barns, and sheds, and claimed a historic lodge that served generations of Coloradans (9News Denver).

The scale of devastation underscores a hard truth conservatives have long warned about: decades of mismanagement of Colorado's forests—driven by environmental regulations that blocked timber harvesting and fuel reduction—have created tinderboxes across the state. While Democrats and environmental groups blocked sensible forest management in the name of "preservation," dead trees and dense undergrowth accumulated, turning wildfires into catastrophic infernos.

Republicans argue that Colorado needs to reverse course: allow private landowners and timber operators to thin forests, reduce regulatory barriers to prescribed burns, and prioritize forest health over ideological environmentalism. The state's progressive approach to land management has failed Colorado families and businesses.

As damage assessments continue, taxpayers should watch whether state and federal officials finally acknowledge that prevention—not just disaster response—requires loosening the environmental regulations that created this crisis in the first place.
