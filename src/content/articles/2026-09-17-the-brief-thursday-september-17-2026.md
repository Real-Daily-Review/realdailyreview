# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, September 17, 2026

Colorado conservatives have plenty to watch today — from a Fed rate surprise that rattles markets to a data center energy fight that hits close to home for every family paying a utility bill. Let's get into it.

---

## Fed Chair Warsh Pulls a U-Turn on Rates

President Trump's own Fed pick, Kevin Warsh, has delivered a rate hike that's turning heads across Wall Street and Main Street alike. For a man appointed specifically to bring discipline to the Fed, the move is a surprising reversal — and it raises serious questions about whether the central bank is responding to economic data or political pressure. Colorado businesses and ranchers carrying variable-rate debt will feel this one. Republicans argue the Fed must stay focused on inflation, not mission creep.

---

## Douglas and Boulder Counties Eye Expanded Commissions

Pushes to expand county commissions are playing out simultaneously in Douglas and Boulder counties — two places that couldn't be more politically different. In Boulder County, an unidentified buyer has offered $42.5 million for county property, giving 213 households until Dec. 8 to submit a competing offer. Conservatives should watch whether expanding commission seats in Douglas County strengthens conservative governance or dilutes it — and whether Boulder's land deal represents responsible stewardship or another government-adjacent shuffle of taxpayer-adjacent assets.

---

## Data Centers, Power Bills, and the Fight Over Who Pays

Two fights over data center development are colliding this week. On the national level, the House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act with bipartisan support — a bill designed to stop data center energy costs from being passed along to ordinary families on their electric bills. Meanwhile, Coloradans are still asking basic questions about Project Taurus: Will it create jobs? How much power and water will it consume? Colorado taxpayers and ratepayers deserve straight answers before any government body rolls out the welcome mat for projects that could spike utility costs for working families.

---

## Polis Issues Bee Executive Order — Because That's Apparently Urgent

Governor Jared Polis — former beekeeper, current progressive executive — signed an order Wednesday directing multiple state agencies to reduce pesticide use and plant bee and butterfly habitat across Colorado. Democrats will call it environmental stewardship. Conservatives call it what it is: another layer of regulatory direction handed down from the governor's office, expanding the reach of state agencies into land and agricultural decisions that affect Colorado farmers and ranchers. Watch for compliance costs to follow.

---

## Lakewood Shooting Investigated as Possible Self-Defense

Lakewood police are investigating the death of Mark Andrew Reins, 45, as a possible act of self-defense following a shooting this week. Details remain limited as the investigation continues. This is exactly the kind of case that illustrates why Colorado's self-defense laws matter — and why conservatives must remain vigilant against any legislative effort to erode the right of law-abiding Coloradans to protect themselves and their families.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Ratepayer Protection Act as it heads to the Senate — Colorado's energy ratepayers need that protection to hold, especially as data center development accelerates across the Front Range. Watch how Douglas County's commission expansion debate unfolds; the outcome could reshape conservative governance in one of the state's most reliably red counties. And as Governor Polis continues to govern by executive order, Colorado taxpayers should ask a simple question: who authorized this, and what will it cost?
