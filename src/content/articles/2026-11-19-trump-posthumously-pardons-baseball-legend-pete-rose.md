# Trump Pardons Baseball Legend Pete Rose Posthumously

President Donald Trump has posthumously pardoned Pete Rose, baseball's all-time hits leader, clearing the slate on a 1990 federal tax evasion conviction that shadowed the Cincinnati Reds icon's final years. Rose, who died more than two years ago, pleaded guilty to tax charges decades ago—a legal cloud that kept him from baseball's Hall of Fame despite his unmatched on-field achievements.

The pardon is largely uncontroversial and reflects Trump's stated philosophy of clemency for cases he views as resolved or unjust. Rose's conviction was old, his penalty already served, and the baseball world had largely moved past the controversy in recent years, with MLB itself reinstating his eligibility.

This isn't a Colorado-specific story, but it's worth noting: Trump's pardon power—wielded aggressively during his first term and continuing now—remains a flashpoint between conservatives and the left. Republicans defend broad executive clemency as a check on prosecutorial overreach; Democrats and progressives argue it undermines the rule of law and justice. Rose's case, however, sits in the narrow middle where even skeptics of Trump's pardon authority see little harm.
