# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, November 23, 2026

Colorado conservatives head into Thanksgiving week with a Senate leadership fight in Washington, a fraud case that could put a billion dollars back in taxpayers' pockets, and a hard question about why eastern Colorado is being misrepresented in Congress. Let's get into it.

---

## Eastern Colorado Deserves Better — And the System Needs Fixing

It is, as Complete Colorado bluntly puts it, "frankly absurd" that deep-red eastern Colorado does not have a conservative representative in Congress. Lauren Boebert's defeat has renewed a serious conversation about election integrity and structural reforms that actually reflect the will of rural Colorado voters. Ranchers, energy workers, and small-business owners on the Eastern Plains didn't change — the system around them did. Republicans need to treat this as a five-alarm fire, not a post-mortem footnote.

---

## Trump Draws a Hard Line on Schumer

President Trump made clear over the weekend that a Chuck Schumer-led Senate majority would be a dead end for the conservative agenda, saying it will be "very hard" to work with the Democratic leader and predicting Schumer will "fail again." For Colorado conservatives, the Senate majority leader's identity is not an inside-baseball question — it determines whether tax relief, energy permitting reform, and border security get a floor vote or die in committee. Republicans need to hold the line.

---

## FAA Ground Delays Expose Federal Aviation Fragility

A Sunday automation system failure triggered FAA ground delays for Delta flights in and out of Atlanta's Hartsfield-Jackson Airport, rippling disruption across the national air travel network just before Thanksgiving. Colorado travelers flying through connecting hubs should plan accordingly. Conservatives have long argued that bloated federal bureaucracy and deferred infrastructure modernization — not lack of spending — are what make systems like the FAA brittle. This is another data point.

---

## DNI: Tech Outages Not a Foreign Attack — But Questions Remain

Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gaylard Clayton stated plainly that the recent wave of technology outages was "not coordinated" and "not state-sponsored." That's reassuring as far as it goes. But the fact that uncoordinated, non-state failures can cause this level of disruption to critical systems should concern every American. Colorado's defense contractors, energy infrastructure, and financial sector are all exposed to cascading tech failures. The answer isn't more federal regulation — it's private-sector resilience and accountability.

---

## $275M Solar Fraud Case: Trump DOJ Needs to Pick a Side

A False Claims Act lawsuit over Crescent Dunes — an Obama-era solar project that received a $275 million federal grant — could recover up to $1 billion for taxpayers. The problem: the Trump Justice Department is currently defending a Biden-era dismissal of the case. Colorado taxpayers, like every American, have a direct stake in whether the federal government aggressively pursues fraud against the public treasury or quietly buries it. Republicans in Congress should be demanding answers from DOJ about why this case isn't being prosecuted with full force.

---

## What to Watch This Week

Keep your eyes on the Senate majority leader fight in Washington — Trump's posture toward Schumer signals how hard the White House will push for a reliable conservative partner in the upper chamber, and that has direct consequences for every piece of the Republican agenda. On the Colorado front, watch for any follow-up from state Republicans on election reform proposals tied to the Boebert loss; the Eastern Plains cannot afford to be an afterthought heading into 2028. And if you're traveling for Thanksgiving, the FAA's weekend stumble is a reminder to build in extra time — the federal government's track record on smooth operations is not exactly a confidence-builder.
