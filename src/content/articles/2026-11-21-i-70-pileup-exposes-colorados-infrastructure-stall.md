# I-70 Shut Down Again: Colorado's Mountain Corridor Proves Why Infrastructure Matters

Colorado's critical I-70 corridor ground to a halt Tuesday after a 12-car pileup near Floyd Hill, with multiple spin-outs and slide-outs reported by Colorado State Patrol (9News Denver). The westbound closure at Vail Pass underscored a persistent problem: Colorado's main east-west artery remains vulnerable to winter weather despite decades of talk about improvements.

The incident highlights what conservatives have long argued: Colorado needs smart infrastructure investment—not the bloated, politically connected projects progressives favor. I-70 improvements require engineering solutions and private-sector efficiency, not more government bureaucracy or environmental delays that paralyze decision-making.

For Colorado businesses, commuters, and the tourism industry that depends on reliable mountain access, these closures mean real economic pain. Truckers lose hours; ski resorts lose customers; families miss connections. Yet Colorado's government has struggled to deliver timely, cost-effective upgrades while environmental reviews and regulatory hurdles multiply.

Republicans have pushed for faster I-70 expansion and modernization; Democrats and environmental groups have often prioritized transit alternatives and congestion pricing over traditional highway improvements. The result: a corridor that remains dangerously congested and weather-dependent.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature prioritizes funding for I-70 safety improvements in the next budget cycle—or whether progressive priorities again crowd out practical infrastructure fixes.
