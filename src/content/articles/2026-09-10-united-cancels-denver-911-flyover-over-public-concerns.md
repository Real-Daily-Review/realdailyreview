# United Scraps 9/11 Flyover After Backlash Over Passenger Plane, Fighter Jets

United Airlines has canceled a planned 9/11 commemoration flyover in Denver featuring a passenger jet escorted by fighter jets, citing "concerns in the community" (KDVR Fox31).

Denver's Office of Emergency Management had been preparing residents for the spectacle, which would have included coordination with military aircraft. The decision to scrap the event reflects sensitivity around large-scale aerial displays involving commercial planes—understandable given post-9/11 security protocols and public anxiety about low-flying aircraft near populated areas.

Conservatives note the irony: a tribute meant to honor those lost on 9/11 was deemed too risky or controversial to execute. The cancellation highlights how risk-averse institutions have become, often prioritizing optics over meaningful commemoration. Some argue that fear of public reaction—however overblown—shouldn't dictate how Americans remember national tragedy.

Others counter that public safety concerns and avoiding unnecessary alarm are legitimate reasons to modify plans, particularly when alternative ways to honor 9/11 exist.

The broader question for Colorado: as government and private entities become increasingly cautious about public events, are we losing the ability to conduct large-scale civic ceremonies without excessive hand-wringing?
