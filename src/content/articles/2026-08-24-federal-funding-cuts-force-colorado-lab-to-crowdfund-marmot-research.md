# Colorado Scientists Turn to 'Fat Marmot Week' as Federal Funding Dries Up

Colorado's Rocky Mountain Biological Lab is launching "Fat Marmot Week" to crowdfund research—a sign that federal science dollars are shrinking faster than alpine snowpack.

The Gothic-based lab, which has run long-term marmot studies for decades, is turning to public donations and social media contests to keep research alive, according to CPR News and the Colorado Sun. Scientists need to "drum up donations" amid what CPR describes as "federal funding cuts."

The contest—picking Colorado's "chunkiest alpine residents"—is lighthearted on the surface. But it underscores a serious problem: government science funding is unreliable. When Washington cuts research budgets, Colorado's world-class institutions scramble.

Conservatives have long argued that federal spending is bloated and unsustainable, but that doesn't mean gutting basic research that advances knowledge and benefits Colorado's outdoor economy and land management. The real issue is *how* Washington spends: too much goes to bureaucratic overhead, not fieldwork.

For Colorado taxpayers and outdoor enthusiasts, this matters. Better marmot and alpine ecosystem data helps ranchers, water managers, and wildlife officials make smarter decisions on public lands.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—Republican and Democrat alike—will push back on blanket research cuts or demand Washington prioritize science over wasteful spending.
