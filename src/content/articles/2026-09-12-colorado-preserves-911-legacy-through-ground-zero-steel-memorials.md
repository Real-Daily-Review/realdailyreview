# Colorado's 9/11 Legacy: Steel from Ground Zero Honors Fallen

A Colorado Springs man has made it his mission to place World Trade Center steel at September 11th memorials across Colorado—a tangible reminder of American resilience and sacrifice that transcends politics.

According to 9News Denver, the effort has successfully distributed Ground Zero remnants to multiple 9/11 memorials throughout the state. The initiative honors the nearly 3,000 Americans killed in the 2001 terrorist attacks and ensures that Coloradans have a direct physical connection to that defining moment in our nation's history.

Meanwhile, CPR News reports that Denver-based FEMA photographer Michael Rieger was among the first to document the devastation at Ground Zero, spending weeks photographing "the pile" in the immediate aftermath. His work provided the world its initial visual record of the attacks' scale and the heroic response that followed.

These stories underscore an enduring Colorado contribution to America's 9/11 remembrance—from firsthand documentation to ongoing stewardship of sacred materials. In an era of cultural fragmentation, such efforts remind us of shared national purpose and the valor of first responders.
