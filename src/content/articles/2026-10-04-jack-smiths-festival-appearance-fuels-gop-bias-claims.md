# Jack Smith's Festival Appearance Raises Questions About Prosecutorial Impartiality

Former Special Counsel Jack Smith attended a left-wing "Power to the People Fest" in Maryland over the weekend, where he was photographed wearing a Bruce Springsteen-inspired "No Kings" shirt—imagery widely interpreted as anti-Trump messaging (Breitbart). The event featured performances by Springsteen and other musicians and was explicitly framed as a get-out-the-vote effort.

Trump responded sharply, calling Smith "deranged" and a "crooked prosecutor" (Just The News).

**The Conservative Concern:** Republicans and Trump allies argue Smith's public appearance at a partisan political festival—complete with anti-Trump symbolism—undermines the credibility of his prosecutions and raises serious questions about judicial impartiality. If a federal prosecutor is openly campaigning at left-wing events while pursuing cases against a political opponent, conservatives contend, the appearance of bias becomes impossible to ignore. This feeds the broader Republican argument that the Justice Department has been weaponized against Trump.

Democrats have not publicly addressed the optics, but would likely argue Smith attended as a private citizen exercising free speech rights.

**Colorado Angle:** While this story centers on federal prosecution, it reflects the partisan polarization affecting Colorado's own courts and elections as Democrats and Republicans clash over judicial independence and prosecutorial conduct.
