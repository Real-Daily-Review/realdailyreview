# Leadville-Area Wildfire Swells to 4,000 Acres, Forces New Evacuations

Colorado's wildfire season is delivering exactly what years of mismanaged forests and regulatory gridlock produce: fast-moving destruction threatening mountain communities. The Willow Fire near Leadville has grown to nearly 4,000 acres, prompting fresh evacuations in Lake County as flames spread eastward across the southern edge of the burn zone (KDVR).

The rapid expansion underscores a hard truth conservatives have long pressed: Colorado's forests are choked with dead timber and dense undergrowth—the direct result of decades of fire-suppression policy and environmental lawsuits that block thinning and controlled burns. While state and federal agencies mobilize response resources, the real question is whether Colorado will finally prioritize forest health over litigation-friendly "let-it-burn" ideology.

CPR News reports the fire's growth continues along its southern perimeter, with evacuation orders expanding Sunday morning. Residents in the Leadville area face the immediate threat of losing homes and livelihoods while state officials manage the crisis reactively rather than prevent it proactively.

Conservatives argue that aggressive forest management—including timber harvesting, mechanical thinning, and prescribed burns—would reduce fuel loads and limit catastrophic wildfire spread. The environmental left has long blocked such measures in court, prioritizing litigation over lives.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature will finally pass meaningful forest-management reform or continue genuflecting to environmental groups that profit from crisis.
