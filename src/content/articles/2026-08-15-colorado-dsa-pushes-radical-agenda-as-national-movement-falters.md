# Colorado DSA Activists Push Radical Agenda While National Movement Stumbles

Colorado's Democratic Socialist Alliance is doubling down on far-left ideology even as the national DSA movement hits electoral headwinds, raising questions about whether the state's progressive wing has lost touch with mainstream voters.

According to Complete Colorado, Colorado DSA activists are embracing rhetoric and tactics reminiscent of Maoist and Bolshevik movements—language that conservatives argue reveals the true ideological roots of the organization. The comparison underscores Republican concerns that DSA-aligned politicians and activists are pushing a revolutionary agenda rather than incremental policy reform.

The timing is notable: nationally, Democratic Socialists suffered a major defeat in Wisconsin, where radical candidate Francesca Hong lost a stunning gubernatorial primary upset to moderate David Crowley (National Review). The loss suggests voters are rejecting the DSA's uncompromising approach.

Yet Colorado's DSA contingent appears undeterred. Conservatives worry that without electoral consequences, the movement will continue pressuring state Democrats to embrace higher taxes, aggressive regulation, and anti-business policies that could harm Colorado's energy sector, small businesses, and working families.

Republicans argue the Wisconsin result proves voters reject socialist economics and revolutionary posturing—a lesson Colorado Democrats should heed before the next election cycle.
