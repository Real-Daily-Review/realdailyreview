# Trump Administration Signals Religious Freedom Push in Xi Talks

President Trump is set to confront Chinese Communist Party leadership over imprisoned religious and political dissidents during bilateral talks with Chairman Xi, signaling that religious liberty will anchor the administration's diplomatic agenda alongside trade and AI negotiations.

The move reflects a broader Trump-era commitment to religious freedom at home and abroad. According to the Washington Examiner, legal advocates argue that religious liberty protections in America have reached historic strength under the current administration, with recent court victories establishing meaningful safeguards for faith-based practice and conscience rights.

The Daily Wire notes that securing the release of political and religious prisoners held by Beijing should rank among Washington's top priorities in these high-stakes talks—a position that contrasts sharply with the Obama-Biden approach, which critics argue deprioritized religious persecution in favor of climate and trade diplomacy.

For Colorado, this matters: religious liberty protections directly affect faith-based organizations, churches, and individuals navigating state and federal regulations. Colorado's progressive regulatory environment has increasingly clashed with religious conscience claims, particularly around healthcare mandates and education policy. A Trump administration that elevates religious freedom internationally may also push back against state-level overreach domestically.
