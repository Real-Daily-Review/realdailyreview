# U.S. Adds 115,000 Jobs in April as Unemployment Holds Steady

The Labor Department reported Friday that the U.S. added 115,000 jobs in April while unemployment remained flat, exceeding economist expectations, according to PBS NewsHour. The figure surpassed the Dow Jones consensus forecast of 55,000 new positions, according to CNBC.

The stronger-than-expected headline number masks underlying concerns about economic momentum. CNBC reported that the jobs report contained "several red flags for the economy," though the outlet did not detail those warnings in available excerpts.

Mohamed El-Erian, chief economic advisor at Allianz and a professor at the Wharton School of Business, discussed the report's implications with PBS NewsHour, suggesting the data offers insight into the broader economic picture beyond the headline job gains.

The April employment figures arrive as policymakers and investors monitor labor market health amid broader economic uncertainty. Watch for revisions to prior months' data and any shifts in wage growth or labor force participation that could signal whether the economy is sustaining momentum or facing headwinds.
