# RNC Challenges Colorado's Overseas Voter Residency Rule Under Griswold

Colorado's Secretary of State Jena Griswold is facing a federal lawsuit from the Republican National Committee over her office's handling of overseas voter residency requirements—a direct challenge to voting eligibility standards that could reshape how the state verifies who gets to cast ballots.

The RNC argues that Griswold's interpretation allowing residency to pass "by proxy" through a parent or spouse violates the Constitution, according to *Complete Colorado*. Republicans contend this creates a loophole permitting individuals who have never actually lived in Colorado to vote here, undermining election integrity and state sovereignty over voter qualification rules.

The lawsuit arrives amid broader scrutiny of Griswold's tenure. The *Denver Post* editorial board noted her office has been embroiled in an open-records scandal, with columnist Krista Kafer arguing that Republican challenger Michael Allen brings superior litigation expertise and a scandal-free record in public office.

For Colorado taxpayers and election officials, this case raises hard questions: Should residency mean actual, physical presence in the state? Or can family connections substitute? The stakes are real—loose residency standards could dilute the voting power of actual Colorado residents and invite legal chaos in close elections.

Watch whether Colorado courts uphold the RNC's constitutional challenge or whether Griswold's office successfully defends proxy residency rules.
