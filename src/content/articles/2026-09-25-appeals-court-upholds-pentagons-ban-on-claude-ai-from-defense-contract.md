# Pentagon Wins: Appeals Court Backs Claude AI Exclusion from Defense Supply Chain

A federal appeals court has upheld the Pentagon's decision to exclude Anthropic's Claude AI platform from the defense supply chain, dealing a significant blow to the company's government contracting ambitions.

The D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled 2-1 on Friday that Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth's March designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was justified under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 (Washington Examiner). The Pentagon had effectively blacklisted the AI company from federal procurement, citing national security concerns.

Anthropic had challenged the exclusion, but the court sided with the Defense Department's assessment. The ruling represents a win for Pentagon leadership seeking to protect sensitive defense systems and procurement from potential vulnerabilities—a concern conservatives have long emphasized regarding foreign influence and security risks in critical infrastructure.

The decision underscores the Trump administration's willingness to use existing law to tighten security vetting of technology suppliers. For Colorado, which hosts significant defense contractors and tech firms, the ruling signals that federal security standards will remain stringent for companies seeking Pentagon contracts.

Conservatives argue such supply-chain protections are essential to national security; critics contend the exclusion may stifle innovation. Watch whether other AI firms face similar designations or whether Anthropic pursues further legal remedies.
