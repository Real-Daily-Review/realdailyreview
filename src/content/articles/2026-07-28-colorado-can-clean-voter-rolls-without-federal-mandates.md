# Non-Citizen Voting Is Real—But States Don't Need Federal Mandates to Fix It

Colorado election officials need to clean voter rolls themselves without waiting for Washington to impose one-size-fits-all rules.

Non-citizen voting is no myth. New Jersey's governor has confirmed roughly 6,600 noncitizens on state voter rolls, with about 400 having actually voted, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Michigan faces similar problems. Yet Republicans pushing the federal SAVE America Act to mandate nationwide purges are overlooking a critical point: states already have the tools and constitutional authority to maintain accurate rolls without federal overreach.

Conservatives rightly argue that election integrity demands clean voter rolls. But the GOP's impulse to centralize election management contradicts limited-government principles. Colorado's election system—managed by county clerks and the Secretary of State—works best when decisions stay local, not dictated from Washington.

Democrats, predictably, claim noncitizen voting is overblown and that aggressive purges risk disenfranchising eligible voters. The left frames voter-roll maintenance as a pretext for suppression, ignoring documented cases of noncitizens casting ballots.

The real issue: Colorado and other states must act decisively on their own. Jena Griswold's office should audit rolls against federal immigration databases and DMV records without waiting for federal mandates—or federal interference.

Watch whether Colorado's Secretary of State takes proactive steps to verify citizenship before the 2024 cycle heats up.
