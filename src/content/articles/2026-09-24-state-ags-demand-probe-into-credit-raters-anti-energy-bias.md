# State AGs Take Aim at Credit Raters' Climate-Driven Bias Against Energy States

A coalition of state attorneys general is demanding a federal investigation into Moody's, Fitch, and S&amp;P for allegedly weaponizing climate and ESG criteria to downgrade fossil fuel-producing states, *Daily Caller* reports exclusively.

The move signals growing Republican pushback against what conservatives call ideological discrimination masquerading as financial analysis. These credit-rating giants wield enormous power over borrowing costs and bond markets—and if they're systematically penalizing energy-producing states, Colorado taxpayers and businesses could face higher costs for everything from infrastructure bonds to municipal debt.

Republicans argue the raters are imposing a progressive political agenda rather than objective financial assessment. By factoring in climate risk and ESG metrics, the agencies effectively punish states like Colorado that depend on responsible energy development and ranching. This isn't market discipline; it's financial coercion dressed up as climate virtue.

The AGs' investigation request targets what they see as coordinated anti-fossil-fuel bias. If credit agencies are allowed to downgrade states based on political ideology rather than actual default risk, it amounts to a backdoor carbon tax on energy-producing regions—one that bypasses voters and legislatures entirely.

**Watch for:** Whether the Trump administration's DOJ will actually pursue this investigation and whether Colorado's AG joins the coalition formally.
