# Senate Passes College Sports Bill While Dodging Fiscal Reality

The Senate just passed the Protect College Sports Act with sweeping new federal regulations on athlete compensation and transfers—a classic Washington move: solve a problem nobody asked them to solve while the national debt balloons unchecked.

The bill passed 77-22 with bipartisan support (Washington Examiner), establishing national standards for NIL (name, image, likeness) deals after court rulings upended NCAA rules. Supporters say it protects athletes and restores order to college sports.

But conservatives note the timing stinks. As the Daily Caller reports, the Senate prioritized college sports regulation while punting on the actual fiscal crisis: rising costs, the national debt, and AI policy. Republicans and Democrats both voted for the bill, but the real question is why Congress is micromanaging college athletics when Colorado families are drowning in inflation and the federal government is spending money it doesn't have.

Meanwhile, back home, national GOP money is flowing into Colorado House races—the Colorado Sun reports Republicans are defending all four GOP-held seats, including $300,000 for Lauren Boebert's district. The midterms are shaping up as a spending war.
