# Aurora Drops $23M on Farm Water Grabs—Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Aurora just committed $23 million in public money to buy water rights from two century-old farms in the Lower Arkansas Valley, according to *KDVR Fox31 Denver*. The city approved the deal Monday, allowing the Hirakata and Hanagan families to lease back their land while Aurora secures the right to divert water three out of every ten years.

**The conservative concern:** This is classic government overreach disguised as water planning. Aurora is using taxpayer dollars to acquire private property rights—essentially paying to control water that farmers have stewarded for generations. The families retain nominal use of their land, but Aurora now holds the real asset: the water itself.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** Water is the lifeblood of Eastern Colorado agriculture and the Front Range economy. When municipalities like Aurora start buying up farm water rights with public money, it signals a troubling trend: government consolidation of a finite resource that should remain in private hands or be traded through market mechanisms, not bureaucratic acquisition. This sets a precedent for other cities to follow suit, further squeezing rural water security and agricultural viability across the state.

Republicans and water-rights advocates argue that voluntary market transactions—not government spending sprees—should determine who controls Colorado's water future.

**Watch:** Whether other Front Range cities follow Aurora's playbook and how rural Colorado responds to this precedent.
