# Social Security's Day of Reckoning: Why Colorado Retirees Can't Ignore the Math

Colorado's retirees are collecting Social Security checks capped at $5,181 monthly—but the program's fiscal crisis is no longer theoretical. National Review reports that "fiscal reality interrupts everyone's Social Security fairy tales," underscoring what conservatives have long warned: the trust fund faces unsustainable obligations without structural reform.

The Washington Examiner notes that November's third round of payments went out Nov. 25 to beneficiaries born after the 21st of each month. But these payments mask a deeper problem: Social Security's trustees project the trust fund will be depleted within years, forcing automatic benefit cuts unless Congress acts.

Republicans argue that means-testing, raising the retirement age, and adjusting cost-of-living formulas are necessary to preserve the program. Democrats resist benefit reforms, preferring tax hikes on high earners—a position that ignores the program's structural insolvency.

For Colorado's 800,000-plus seniors, this matters enormously. Benefit cuts or tax increases on working Coloradans would hit both retirees and the younger workers funding their checks. The current political stalemate guarantees neither side gets its way—and taxpayers get squeezed.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for honest reform or continues kicking the can down the road.
