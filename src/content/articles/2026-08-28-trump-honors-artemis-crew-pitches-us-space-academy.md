# Trump Honors Artemis II Crew, Unveils Space Academy Push

President Trump this week received honors from the Artemis II crew and unveiled plans for a U.S. Space Academy, according to 9News Denver. The president accepted a NASA flight jacket from the astronauts during the ceremony.

The move signals Trump's continued focus on space exploration and American leadership in the sector—a priority conservatives have backed as both a national security asset and economic driver. Colorado, home to significant aerospace and defense contractors, has a direct stake in robust federal space investment and the jobs it generates.

The Space Academy proposal aligns with Trump's broader push to strengthen American competitiveness and reduce dependence on foreign space capabilities. Republicans argue that streamlined space policy and private-sector partnerships—not bloated government bureaucracies—drive innovation and cost efficiency in the aerospace industry.

Democrats have historically favored NASA's traditional structure and heavy government oversight of space programs, though the left's position on Trump's academy proposal remains unclear from available reporting.

For Colorado taxpayers and workers in the aerospace corridor, the key question is whether the Space Academy will translate into sustained funding and job creation—or remain a symbolic gesture without budget teeth.

**Watch for:** Details on the Space Academy's funding mechanism and whether Congress will appropriate dollars or simply rebrand existing programs.
