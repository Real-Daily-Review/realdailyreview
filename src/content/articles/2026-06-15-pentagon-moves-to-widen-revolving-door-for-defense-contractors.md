# Pentagon Revolving Door Threatens Defense Accountability—and Colorado Defense Contractors

The Pentagon is quietly pushing to expand the revolving door between military brass and defense contractors, potentially weakening oversight and inflating costs for taxpayers—including Colorado's defense industry workforce.

According to the Daily Caller, the Department of Defense is seeking to relax restrictions on post-government employment for military officials, a move critics say will accelerate the flow of retired generals and procurement officers directly into lucrative private-sector defense jobs. The proposal, embedded in the 2027 National Defense Authorization Act, would loosen existing cooling-off periods that currently require senior Pentagon officials to wait before joining defense firms.

Republicans and defense watchdogs argue the measure epitomizes government-industry capture: military decision-makers approve contracts, then cash in at the same contractors they once oversaw. This inflates defense spending and undermines competitive bidding—costs ultimately borne by Colorado taxpayers funding the state's robust aerospace and defense sector.

The Pentagon frames the change as necessary to retain talent. Conservatives counter that if the private sector can't attract talent without eliminating ethics guardrails, the problem isn't the rules—it's bloated defense budgets that reward cronyism over competition.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican delegation—particularly those representing districts with major defense contractors—will push back against this ethics erosion or quietly accept it.
