# Tech Outages Raise Questions About Government Readiness—and AI Risk

A cascade of system failures across airlines and banking has exposed vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure that federal officials are still scrambling to explain. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told Fox News the "homeland is secure," but acknowledged that an unnamed technology expert blamed artificial intelligence for the outages (Washington Examiner). Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called the situation a "head-scratcher," offering little reassurance to Americans whose flights were grounded and banking services disrupted.

The vague explanations from Trump administration officials underscore a broader conservative concern: government agencies lack adequate oversight of AI systems embedded in critical infrastructure, and federal cybersecurity protocols may be inadequate. Republicans have long warned that over-reliance on complex, opaque AI systems—without proper safeguards or human oversight—poses national security risks.

Colorado's aviation and financial sectors, which support thousands of jobs and billions in economic activity, depend on stable tech infrastructure. Any prolonged outages threaten the state's economy and expose gaps in federal preparedness.

Conservatives should demand that Congress hold hearings on AI governance in critical infrastructure and require transparent audits of federal cybersecurity readiness—not more regulation, but accountability for existing systems.

**Watch for:** Whether Congress demands a full briefing on what caused these outages and what safeguards are actually in place.
