# Trump's Iran Strategy Risks Colorado Gas Pump Pain Ahead of Midterms

President Trump is doubling down on military pressure against Iran's regime, but Colorado drivers and energy workers are watching gas prices climb past $4 per gallon—a political liability the White House can't ignore.

Trump ordered fresh strikes against Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps this week and told reporters the midterm elections won't change his timeline for economic strangulation of Tehran (Washington Examiner). The administration is meeting with oil refiners to discuss supply constraints, but conservatives face a hard choice: support Trump's hawkish Iran policy while absorbing pain at the pump.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that deterring Iranian aggression and protecting U.S. interests in the Middle East justifies short-term energy costs. A weakened Iran serves long-term American security and energy independence. Trump's refinery meetings signal he's aware of the political stakes.

**The risk:** Colorado's energy sector and working families depend on affordable fuel. Gas above $4 per gallon erodes Republican messaging on the economy heading into midterms—exactly when the party needs momentum. Energy workers in the state, already skeptical of Biden-era regulations, won't forgive another round of price shocks.

Watch whether Trump's refinery talks produce concrete relief or remain symbolic—and whether Colorado Republicans can hold the line on both national security and kitchen-table economics.
