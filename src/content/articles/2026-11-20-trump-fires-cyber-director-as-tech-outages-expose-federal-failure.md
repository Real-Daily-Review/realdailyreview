# White House Fires Cyber Director as Tech Outages Expose Federal Vulnerability

The Trump administration has fired National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross amid widespread technological outages that have left Americans questioning whether Washington can actually protect critical infrastructure. Kirsten A. Davies, the Pentagon's chief information officer, will assume the role in an acting capacity.

The timing raises hard questions about federal competence. Cairncross was responsible for coordinating cybersecurity policy across the entire government—a sprawling bureaucracy already bloated with overlapping agencies and unclear accountability. His removal suggests the White House believes the nation's top cyber adviser wasn't up to the job when it mattered most.

For Colorado, this matters. The state's energy sector, water systems, and financial infrastructure depend on federal cybersecurity frameworks that clearly aren't working. Colorado's tech corridor in Boulder and Denver relies on stable digital infrastructure; widespread outages threaten businesses and jobs.

Conservatives argue the real problem isn't just personnel—it's that federal cybersecurity has become another bloated, unaccountable bureaucracy. Too many agencies, too many layers, too little results. The question isn't who sits in the chair; it's whether Washington should be running cybersecurity at all, or whether private industry and state-level coordination would do the job better and faster.

Watch whether the Trump administration uses this moment to streamline federal cyber agencies or simply reshuffles the deck chairs.
