# Drone Surveillance Over July 4th: Feds Use Holiday to Expand Surveillance State

Colorado residents celebrating Independence Day this year may have unwanted company in the sky. Federal agencies are deploying drones across the country—ostensibly to catch illegal fireworks—under new FAA rules that fast-tracked "Drone as First Responder" programs by slashing bureaucratic oversight (The Daily Wire).

The move exemplifies how government uses crisis moments and holidays to normalize surveillance infrastructure. What starts as enforcement against fireworks violations can easily expand into broader monitoring of citizens exercising their rights.

**Conservatives' concern:** This represents creeping government overreach dressed up as public safety. The FAA's loosened regulations prioritize speed over accountability, letting cities and counties deploy aerial surveillance with minimal checks. For Colorado—a state with strong Second Amendment traditions and skepticism of federal overreach—this sets a troubling precedent for drone-based monitoring of lawful activity.

Republicans have long warned that emergency powers and expedited federal rules rarely sunset. Once drones are normalized overhead on July 4th, expect mission creep into other enforcement areas.

The real question: Will Colorado counties resist this federal pressure, or will they embrace surveillance tools that could eventually monitor gun ranges, hunting areas, and private property?
