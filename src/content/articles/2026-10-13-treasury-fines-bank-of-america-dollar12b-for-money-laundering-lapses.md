# Bank of America Hit With $1.2B Fine for Lax Money-Laundering Controls

The U.S. Treasury Department slapped Bank of America with a $1.21 billion penalty Tuesday for failing to enforce adequate anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance—a rare instance of federal regulators actually holding a major financial institution accountable.

According to the Washington Examiner, the violations included facilitating payments tied to Chinese money laundering networks and criminal operations along the southern border. The Treasury found that BofA's compliance systems were inadequate despite years of warnings, allowing suspicious transactions to slip through.

This is the kind of regulatory enforcement conservatives can support: targeting genuine criminal facilitation rather than punishing legitimate business activity. The fine demonstrates that strong anti-money laundering rules, when applied fairly and consistently, serve a legitimate law-enforcement purpose—particularly when foreign adversaries and border cartels exploit banking loopholes.

For Colorado, the case underscores why financial institutions serving energy companies, agricultural operations, and small businesses need clear, predictable compliance standards rather than arbitrary regulatory expansion. Banks that know the rules and follow them shouldn't face surprise enforcement actions years later.

Watch whether Treasury applies similar scrutiny to smaller regional banks serving Colorado's energy and agricultural sectors, or whether enforcement remains selective.
