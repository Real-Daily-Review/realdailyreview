# Federal Overreach on Endangered Species Threatens Colorado Property Rights and Housing

The Biden administration's expansive interpretation of the Endangered Species Act has become a backdoor tool for federal land grabs and housing restrictions that hurt ordinary Americans—and Colorado property owners are in the crosshairs.

The Fish and Wildlife Service and National Marine Fisheries Service have wielded an overly broad "harm" definition to regulate private lands far beyond Congress's original intent, according to *National Review*. That regulatory creep has real consequences: *New York Post* reporting links the ever-expanding endangered species list directly to the affordable housing crisis, as federal restrictions make development economically unfeasible on vast tracts of private property.

For Colorado, this means ranchers face federal restrictions on water use, developers can't build on land deemed habitat, and working families get priced out of homeownership. Republicans argue the agencies have weaponized environmental law to control private property without compensation or due process. The original ESA never authorized this level of federal intrusion into state land management or private ownership decisions.

Democrats counter that species protection requires aggressive enforcement, but conservatives say that's precisely the problem: unelected bureaucrats in Washington shouldn't override property rights and local economic needs in the name of environmental absolutism.

Watch for whether Colorado's Republican delegation pushes legislation to restore the ESA's original, limited scope and rein in agency authority over private lands.
