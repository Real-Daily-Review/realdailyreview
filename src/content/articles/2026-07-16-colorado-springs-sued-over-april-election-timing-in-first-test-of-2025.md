# Colorado Springs Faces Lawsuit Over April Election Timing

Colorado Springs is being sued for scheduling municipal elections in April—the first enforcement action since a 2025 voting law took effect (Complete Colorado). The lawsuit challenges whether the city's election calendar complies with state requirements, marking a significant test of the new voting statute's teeth.

The case matters because it signals whether Colorado's election rules will be enforced uniformly or ignored by local officials. Colorado Springs, as the state's second-largest city, sets a precedent. If municipalities can simply sidestep state election law without consequence, the law becomes advisory rather than binding—a problem conservatives have long warned about regarding selective enforcement of regulations.

Republicans have pushed for clearer, more consistent election procedures statewide. This lawsuit tests whether those standards stick or whether cities get a pass. The outcome will determine whether Colorado's election framework has real force or remains toothless.

Taxpayers should watch whether Colorado Springs fights the suit or complies—and whether the state attorney general's office backs enforcement or lets it slide.
