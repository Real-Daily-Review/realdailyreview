# Amendment 87 Masks a Bait-and-Switch Tax Trap for Colorado Middle Class

Colorado voters face a deceptive ballot measure this fall: Amendment 87 promises to soak the rich while cutting taxes for everyone else, but conservatives warn the math doesn't hold up—and bracket creep will eventually hammer ordinary Coloradans.

Proponents claim the measure will raise $2.7 billion annually by taxing the top 3% of earners and 5% of businesses (Colorado Sun). Democrats frame it as progressive relief for the middle class while funding state priorities.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives counter that Amendment 87 is a Trojan horse. Complete Colorado warns that bracket creep—the automatic upward drift of tax brackets with inflation—will transform this into a long-term tax increase on all Coloradans, not just the wealthy. Once the initial top-earner tax kicks in, inflation will gradually push middle-class wages into higher brackets, turning a targeted levy into a broad-based hike that erodes Colorado's competitive tax advantage.

The stakes are clear: Colorado's economy depends on attracting and retaining talent and business investment. A hidden tax increase on the middle class contradicts the measure's sales pitch and threatens the state's fiscal health.

Taxpayers should demand a detailed, honest accounting of how bracket creep will affect their own tax bills over the next decade.
