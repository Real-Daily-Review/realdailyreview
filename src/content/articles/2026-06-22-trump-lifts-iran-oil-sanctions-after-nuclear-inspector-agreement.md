# Trump Lifts Iran Oil Sanctions After Nuclear Inspector Deal

The Trump administration has temporarily lifted sanctions on Iranian oil exports in exchange for Tehran's agreement to allow international nuclear inspectors back into the country—a move that could reshape global energy markets and Colorado's oil and gas sector.

Treasury Secretary Bessent announced the two-month waiver will permit Iran to resume oil sales at market rates, ending years of forced discounts to sanctions-busting buyers (Just The News). Vice President JD Vance called the nuclear inspection agreement a "major milestone" following talks in Switzerland, describing the discussions as "challenging but productive" (Just The News).

The move signals a significant shift in Trump's Iran strategy. By conditioning sanctions relief on verifiable nuclear access, the administration argues it's securing tangible concessions rather than relying on unenforceable promises. Daily Caller notes that previous sanctions alone hadn't stopped Iranian oil sales—only forced them underground.

**The Colorado angle:** Increased Iranian oil supply could pressure global crude prices downward, affecting Colorado's oil and gas industry and state tax revenues tied to energy production. Lower energy prices also ripple through the state's economy and job markets in extraction-heavy regions.

**What to watch:** Whether Iran actually permits unfettered inspector access or uses the waiver period to rebuild leverage before negotiations resume.
