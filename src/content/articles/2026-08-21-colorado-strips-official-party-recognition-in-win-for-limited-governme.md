# Colorado Quietly Strips Official Party Recognition—A Win for Limited Government

Colorado has moved to separate state government from formal political party recognition, a rare display of constitutional restraint that conservatives should applaud. According to *Complete Colorado*, the state is eliminating special government privileges and official status granted to political parties—a "courageous" step toward keeping state machinery neutral and taxpayer-funded resources out of partisan hands.

The move reflects a core conservative principle: government should serve all citizens equally, not pick favorites among political organizations. When states grant official recognition or privileges to parties, they inevitably funnel resources, access, and legitimacy to those entities—a form of government favoritism that inflates the political machine at taxpayer expense.

Democrats and progressive activists will likely argue that party recognition serves administrative purposes. But conservatives counter that such arrangements blur the line between neutral governance and partisan politics, exactly the kind of entanglement the Founders warned against.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners tired of seeing state resources diverted to political theater, this is a straightforward win: less government picking sides, more focus on core services like roads, water, and public safety.

Watch whether other states follow Colorado's lead—and whether progressive groups push back against this common-sense separation of state and party machinery.
