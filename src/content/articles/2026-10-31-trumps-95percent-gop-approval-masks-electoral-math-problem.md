# Trump's Iron Grip on GOP Base Masks a Deeper Math Problem

President Trump commands a stunning 95% approval rating among MAGA Republicans, but that dominance may obscure a harder truth: raw enthusiasm doesn't equal electoral math (Washington Examiner). Chief political correspondent Byron York's analysis cuts through the partisan noise to examine what Trump's control of the Republican Party actually means operationally—and what it doesn't.

For Colorado Republicans, this matters. The state has trended purple in recent cycles, and internal GOP cohesion is critical to competitive statewide races. A party unified around one figure's approval ratings is powerful in primaries but can create vulnerabilities in general elections where swing voters and independents decide outcomes.

Republicans argue Trump's dominance energizes the base and keeps the party focused on core priorities: border security, energy independence, and limited government. Democrats counter that Trump's grip alienates moderate Republicans and suburban voters Colorado needs to win statewide.

The real question isn't whether Trump owns the Republican Party—he clearly does. It's whether that ownership translates into the kind of broad coalition Colorado requires to flip purple seats red in 2026.
