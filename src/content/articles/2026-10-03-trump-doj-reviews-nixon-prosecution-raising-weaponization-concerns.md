# Trump DOJ Revisits Nixon-Era Prosecutorial Conduct, Raising Questions About Federal Overreach

The Trump Justice Department is reviewing how federal prosecutors handled the Watergate investigation, signaling conservative concerns that the Nixon-era special prosecutor's office may have weaponized law enforcement against a sitting president—a practice Republicans argue has become routine against conservatives.

According to *The Daily Wire*, the DOJ is examining the conduct of the special prosecutor's office that investigated Nixon, as part of a broader effort to root out what conservatives call the "weaponization of the federal government." *The Wall Street Journal* reported on the review's scope.

*National Review* offers a more measured take, acknowledging Nixon as "a flawed but accomplished politician" while cautioning against making him a conservative role model—a distinction that underscores the right's internal debate over how to frame executive power and prosecutorial independence.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans contend that selective prosecution and expansive special counsel authority have been deployed asymmetrically against Republican presidents and allies. A fresh look at Watergate-era tactics could illuminate whether such patterns have historical precedent.

**Democrats likely counter** that the review itself represents politicization of the DOJ—using federal power to relitigate settled history rather than pursue genuine accountability.
