# House Speaker's Dark Money Floods Colorado Redistricting Battle

House Speaker Mike Johnson's PAC is dumping $250,000 into Colorado's redistricting fight—a stark reminder that Washington insiders still view our state as a prize worth buying.

The money backs Amendment 86, which would make it harder to redraw congressional districts outside the normal census-driven process. Supporters frame this as anti-gerrymandering reform. But conservatives should ask: who really benefits when Washington operatives pour quarter-million-dollar checks into state ballot measures?

According to the Colorado Sun, dark money groups are heavily financing the amendment. Meanwhile, Complete Colorado reports that voters will also decide Initiative #256, which would ban congressional redistricting intended to favor any political party—a more straightforward anti-gerrymandering approach.

The distinction matters. Amendment 86 appears designed to constrain redistricting flexibility broadly, while Initiative #256 targets partisan intent directly. Republicans argue that clear anti-partisan language is preferable to blanket restrictions that could hamstring legitimate map-drawing.

Colorado's congressional map has been contentious for years. Democrats control the state legislature and have used redistricting to their advantage. Republicans counter that federal judges, not politicians, should draw maps if partisanship is the real concern—not byzantine ballot measures funded by out-of-state power brokers.

**Watch whether Johnson's cash actually moves Colorado voters, or whether Coloradans reject Washington's attempt to rewrite our election rules from afar.**
