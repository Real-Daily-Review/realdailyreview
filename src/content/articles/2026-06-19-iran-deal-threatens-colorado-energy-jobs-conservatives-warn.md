# Biden-Era Iran Deal Leaves Colorado Energy Sector in the Cold

The Memorandum of Understanding with Iran—negotiated under the Biden administration and now facing scrutiny as a new era begins—represents a capitulation that threatens American energy independence and Colorado's oil and gas workers, conservatives argue.

National Review editors contend the deal gives "momentum to Iran" without meaningful concessions, leaving the U.S. in a weaker negotiating position (National Review). The agreement fails to address Iran's nuclear ambitions credibly or constrain its regional aggression, they argue, while potentially flooding global markets with Iranian oil—undercutting domestic producers and Colorado's energy workforce.

Republicans and energy advocates worry the deal signals weakness ahead of broader Middle East negotiations. Vice President JD Vance's handling of Iran policy during the transition period has drawn scrutiny from conservative outlets (The Daily Wire), with some questioning whether the administration adequately protected American interests.

For Colorado, the stakes are direct: cheaper Iranian oil could depress prices, squeeze margins for Colorado producers, and threaten jobs in the state's energy sector. The deal also signals the Biden administration's preference for diplomacy-first approaches that conservatives say consistently underestimate adversarial regimes.
