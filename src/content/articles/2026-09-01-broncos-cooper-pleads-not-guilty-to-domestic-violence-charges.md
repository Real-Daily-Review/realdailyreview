# Broncos' Cooper Pleads Not Guilty in Domestic Violence Case; Trial Set for November

Denver Broncos linebacker Jonathon Cooper entered a not-guilty plea Monday to domestic violence charges stemming from two June arrests, with trial scheduled for November (KDVR). Cooper was placed on the NFL's commissioner's exempt list Sunday, allowing him continued pay and team access while barred from practice and games (CPR News).

The charges relate to altercations with a woman Cooper was dating at the time of the incidents. The exempt list designation keeps him sidelined from competition but maintains his salary—a standard NFL protocol pending legal resolution.

Cooper's case arrives as the Broncos navigate a crowded linebacker rotation and prepare for the upcoming season. The November trial date means the legal proceedings will extend well into the NFL campaign, potentially affecting his availability depending on the outcome.

Conservatives and sports fans alike will be watching whether the league applies its domestic violence policies consistently and fairly, and whether the legal system reaches a just resolution based on evidence rather than public pressure.
