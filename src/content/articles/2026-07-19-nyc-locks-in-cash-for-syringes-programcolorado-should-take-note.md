# NYC Makes Cash-for-Syringes Program Permanent—A Cautionary Tale for Colorado Cities

New York City just locked in a $3 million annual program paying drug users up to $10 daily for used needles, and it's a perfect case study in progressive policy failure that Colorado municipalities should reject outright.

The Mamdani administration quietly made the pilot permanent despite fierce criticism. Conservatives and fiscal watchdogs argue the money—drawn from opioid settlement funds—should fund actual addiction treatment and recovery, not incentivize continued drug use. Critics contend the program encourages illegal drug activity rather than addressing the root crisis (Fox News, New York Post).

The logic is backward: paying people to remain active users diverts resources from rehabilitation, medication-assisted treatment, and mental health services that actually save lives. It's government-subsidized addiction masquerading as harm reduction.

**Colorado angle:** Denver and Boulder have flirted with similar "harm reduction" experiments. This NYC precedent shows what happens when cities treat symptoms instead of causes—they entrench the problem, waste taxpayer dollars, and create perverse incentives that keep people trapped in addiction rather than moving them toward recovery.

Watch whether Colorado's progressive cities attempt to import this model and how state legislators respond to protect public health funding.
