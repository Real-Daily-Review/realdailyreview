# Clinton Blasts Electoral College as 'Abomination'—While Democrats Quietly Dismantle It

Hillary Clinton's latest Netflix grievance about the Electoral College masks a more serious threat: Democrats are systematically eroding the system through the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact, which now commands 222 electoral votes—82% of the 270 needed to trigger a constitutional end-run.

In a new docuseries executive-produced by Tom Hanks, Clinton calls the Electoral College an "abomination" (Fox News). But her public complaints pale beside Democrats' legislative strategy. Virginia Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed legislation in April 2026 committing the state's 13 electoral votes to the national popular vote winner—*regardless of how Virginians actually voted* (Washington Examiner).

Republicans argue the Electoral College protects federalism and ensures smaller states retain meaningful influence. Dismantling it through interstate compacts—rather than constitutional amendment—bypasses the amendment process and concentrates power in high-population urban centers. Colorado, a swing state, could face pressure to join the compact, effectively surrendering its electoral leverage.

The compact needs just 48 more electoral votes to become binding. Conservatives should monitor which states Democrats target next—and whether Colorado's legislature faces pressure to surrender its voters' independent voice.
