# Federal Judge Blocks Trump's Birth Tourism Order—Again

A federal judge has blocked President Trump's executive order targeting "birth tourism," ruling that the directive conflicts with the Supreme Court's 14th Amendment birthright citizenship precedent. U.S. District Judge Deborah Boardman granted a preliminary injunction in a case brought by immigration advocacy groups that previously challenged Trump's initial citizenship restriction attempt (9News Denver, Just The News).

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans argue that birthright citizenship has been exploited by foreign nationals deliberately entering the U.S. to secure citizenship for their children—a practice that strains public resources and undermines immigration law. Trump's order aimed to tighten enforcement, but the judicial block frustrates efforts to close what conservatives see as a loophole in citizenship policy.

**The Legal Hurdle:** The judge's ruling hinges on the Supreme Court's existing birthright citizenship framework, leaving Trump's administration with limited immediate recourse without a higher court reversal or legislative action.

**Colorado Angle:** Immigration policy directly affects Colorado's border security concerns, labor market dynamics, and public service costs. Conservatives in the state have long pushed for stricter enforcement of immigration law and citizenship requirements.

Watch for appeals and whether Congress moves to clarify birthright citizenship through legislation—the only path forward if courts continue blocking executive action.
