# GOP Convention in Dallas Draws Fire From Conservative Critics

The Republican National Committee's inaugural midterm convention in Dallas this week is facing sharp criticism from within conservative ranks—not from the left, but from veteran GOP insiders who say it's a Trump-branded spectacle masquerading as party business.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, a longtime Republican convention observer argues the gathering is "not a convention of the whole Republican Party" despite RNC messaging to the contrary. The critic, who has attended every GOP convention since 2004, calls the event "Trumpapalooza"—a characterization suggesting the affair prioritizes Trump's political brand over genuine party deliberation and platform-building.

The complaint cuts to a core conservative tension: whether party infrastructure should serve the movement broadly or function as a vehicle for a single leader's agenda. Republicans who value institutional party strength and inclusive coalition-building worry that conflating Trump rallies with formal convention work dilutes both.

For Colorado Republicans, the optics matter. The state's GOP has struggled with unity and fundraising in recent cycles. A convention perceived as a personality-driven cash grab rather than serious party work could further alienate grassroots activists and donors skeptical of national party direction.

Watch whether Colorado delegates voice similar concerns at state-level GOP meetings, or whether the Dallas event energizes the base despite the criticism.
