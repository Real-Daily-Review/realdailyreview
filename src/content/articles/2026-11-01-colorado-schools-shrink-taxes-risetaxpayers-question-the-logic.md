# Colorado Schools Face Enrollment Cliff—Yet Taxes Keep Rising

Colorado school districts are shrinking, but taxpayers keep footing bigger bills. According to Complete Colorado, the state faces "declining enrollment for the foreseeable future," yet districts continue pushing tax hikes sold as necessary "for kids." The math doesn't add up: fewer students, same or higher spending per pupil, and no accountability for results.

Meanwhile, Colorado Community College System enrollment is shifting. CPR News reports that older adults are enrolling at higher rates, driven by AI training, healthcare job demand, and support programs. That's fine—but it raises a hard question: if K-12 enrollment is tanking statewide, why are Colorado taxpayers bankrolling ever-larger school budgets?

Republicans and conservatives argue that declining enrollment should trigger *lower* per-pupil spending and smaller tax bills, not the reverse. Instead, districts use demographic decline as cover to lock in permanent tax increases. The result: Colorado families pay more for fewer services.

School officials counter that fixed costs—buildings, transportation, administration—don't shrink proportionally with student numbers, requiring stable or increased funding to maintain quality.

Watch whether Colorado voters reject the next school tax hike, or whether the education lobby's "for the children" messaging prevails despite the enrollment data.
