# Family of Minneapolis Woman Sues Over Fatal ICE Encounter

A Colorado connection to a controversial federal immigration enforcement operation has surfaced: the family of Renee Good, killed during a January ICE raid in Minneapolis, has filed two federal lawsuits against the Trump administration and the immigration officer involved, alleging wrongful death and civil rights violations (Washington Examiner).

The January 7 shooting, captured on cellphone video, has become a flashpoint in the immigration enforcement debate. Good's partner, Becca Good, and her brother, Brent Ganger, filed the complaints in federal court (9News Denver).

**The conservative case:** Republicans and immigration enforcement advocates argue that ICE officers must have latitude to conduct enforcement operations without fear of endless litigation. They contend that federal agents face split-second decisions in the field and that lawsuits of this nature—however sympathetic the circumstances—can chill necessary enforcement of immigration law.

**The left's position:** Progressive groups and civil rights advocates argue the shooting raises serious questions about use-of-force protocols and accountability for federal agents, particularly in immigration enforcement operations that have drawn scrutiny over tactics and outcomes.

For Colorado, this case underscores the broader national debate over immigration enforcement priorities and federal agency accountability—issues that will likely resurface in future policy disputes over border security and interior enforcement.

Watch for discovery proceedings and whether this case influences Trump administration immigration enforcement policies going forward.
