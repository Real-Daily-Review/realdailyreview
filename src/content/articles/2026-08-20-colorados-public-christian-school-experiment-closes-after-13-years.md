# Colorado School Closure Raises Questions About Public-Private Education Experiments

Colorado's controversial public Christian school experiment is shutting down after 13 years, leaving questions about whether government-backed alternative education models deliver results or simply drain resources from traditional public systems.

The closure of ERBOCES (El Paso Rural Board of Cooperative Educational Services) in District 49 marks the end of what backers called "Colorado's first public Christian school"—a hybrid model that drew both praise and criticism from parents and education watchdogs. One District 49 board member defended the venture, saying critics are "Monday morning quarterbacks," but the decision to dissolve the program suggests the experiment failed to sustain itself (CPR News).

**The conservative angle:** While school choice and educational diversity matter, taxpayers deserve clarity on how public dollars were spent and whether this model wasted resources that could have strengthened core K-12 operations. The closure raises broader questions about whether Colorado should continue experimenting with unconventional public-education partnerships or refocus on fiscal discipline and proven outcomes in traditional classrooms.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives have long argued that government should stick to core competencies rather than launching experimental education ventures that ultimately fold, leaving families and budgets in limbo.

**Watch next:** Whether District 49 conducts a full financial audit of ERBOCES and whether other Colorado districts consider similar closures of underperforming alternative programs.
