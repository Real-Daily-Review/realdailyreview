# Trump Signs Iran Deal at Versailles; Oil Markets Brace for Shift

President Trump personally signed a sweeping Iran agreement at the Palace of Versailles, immediately opening U.S. oil export waivers and establishing a $300 billion development framework alongside 60-day nuclear talks (Fox News). The deal marks a dramatic foreign policy pivot that could reshape Middle East stability—and U.S. energy markets.

For Colorado, the implications cut both ways. Lower global oil prices from Iranian crude entering markets could ease pump prices for drivers, but domestic energy producers worry about margin pressure. The agreement's immediate export waivers signal the administration's willingness to use energy diplomacy as a negotiating tool, a sharp contrast to the Biden-era approach of constraining domestic production.

Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich praised the accord as a "realistic alternative" to either appeasement or another large-scale Middle East conflict (Breitbart). Conservatives argue the deal avoids the false choice between capitulation and boots-on-the-ground commitment.

Critics from the right have raised concerns about verification mechanisms and Tehran's track record, though the sources provided do not detail those objections. The 60-day nuclear negotiation window will be crucial.
