# University of Michigan Ditches Letter Grades for Freshmen—Another Coddling Experiment at Taxpayer Expense

The University of Michigan is eliminating letter grades for first-semester freshmen, claiming the move will ease a "mental health crisis" on campus. Instead of traditional A-F marks, students will receive narrative feedback, a policy the school says promotes "intrinsic motivation" and helps students "start strong."

This is grade inflation dressed up as compassion—and it signals another troubling trend in higher education: lowering standards rather than addressing root causes of student distress.

Meanwhile, a new study published in *Psychological Medicine* challenges the prevailing narrative that bullying alone drives mental health problems among LGBT students, suggesting the relationship is more complex than progressive activists claim. Researchers found "simple and compelling logic" in examining alternative explanations for elevated mental health struggles in this population.

**The Colorado angle:** State universities increasingly mirror these trends. If CU Boulder or Colorado State follow suit with grade-gutting policies, Colorado taxpayers funding these institutions should demand accountability—not participation trophies masquerading as mental health solutions.

Conservatives argue that coddling students with participation-trophy grading undermines academic rigor, inflates credentials, and leaves graduates unprepared for real-world competition. Employers won't care about "narrative feedback."
