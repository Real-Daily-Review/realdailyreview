# Colorado Shoppers Face Real Affordability Crisis Behind Fake Black Friday Discounts

Inflation and corporate shrinkflation are making Black Friday deals harder to spot—and Colorado families already squeezed by years of rising costs need to look closer before opening their wallets.

Marketing experts warn that advertised discounts often mask the real problem: prices that never came down. According to Fox News Politics, a marketing professor points out that shrinkflation and inflation obscure whether shoppers are actually saving money or just buying smaller quantities at lower sticker prices. That's cold comfort for Coloradans watching grocery bills, energy costs, and housing expenses climb year after year.

Consumer protection groups are sounding additional alarms. 9News Denver reports that holiday scammers are exploiting the shopping rush with fake discounts, tampered gift cards, and fraudulent delivery messages—adding fraud risk to an already uncertain savings landscape.

Conservatives argue that unchecked government spending and Federal Reserve money-printing fueled the inflation that created this affordability squeeze in the first place. While retailers dangle "deals," working families in Colorado are still worse off than they were three years ago, paying more for essentials regardless of what the price tag says.

Watch for whether Colorado lawmakers propose any consumer protection measures—or whether they'll simply blame retailers while ignoring the fiscal policies that triggered the inflation crisis.
