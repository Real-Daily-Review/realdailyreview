# Colorado Hits 150 Years: A State Built on Freedom, Now Facing Progressive Overreach

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives as the Centennial State celebrates a century and a half of pioneering spirit—but also confronts whether that legacy of individual liberty and limited government can survive modern progressive governance.

The state marked its 150th birthday with festivities at the Capitol, with leaders reflecting on Colorado's past and charting its future (CPR News). The Colorado Sun highlighted 150 things that "lean into Colorado's awesomeness," celebrating the state's unique character and achievements.

But here's the rub: the Colorado that built its reputation on gold rushes, ranching, and rugged individualism now faces relentless expansion of state regulation, environmental mandates that threaten energy jobs, and tax-and-spend policies that burden families and small businesses. Progressive majorities in Denver have weaponized government to reshape the state's economy and culture in ways the founders of 1876 would scarcely recognize.

Republicans and conservatives argue that honoring Colorado's 150-year legacy means returning to the principles of limited government, property rights, and economic freedom that made the state prosperous—not doubling down on the regulatory state that threatens to strangle that prosperity.

Watch closely: whether Colorado's next chapter restores limited-government principles or surrenders them entirely to progressive experimentation will define the state's next 150 years.
