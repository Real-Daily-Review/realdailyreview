# Colorado's Wildfire Season Tests State's Preparedness as Aspen Acres Fire Grows

Colorado's largest active wildfire is forcing evacuations and testing whether state land-management policies can actually protect homes and communities. The Aspen Acres fire in Custer County prompted mandatory evacuation orders this week as hot, dry conditions overwhelmed initial defenses, though heavy rain has since provided temporary relief (Colorado Sun).

Fire crews are now reinforcing perimeters around homes in southern Colorado as moderate activity continues. Multiple other fires—including Hartman Hill, Gold Mountain, Ferris, Willow, Fishhook, and Elk—are also burning across the state, straining resources and raising hard questions about forest management.

Conservatives argue that decades of federal and state restrictions on timber harvesting, prescribed burns, and active forest thinning have left Colorado's forests dangerously overgrown and primed for catastrophic wildfires. Environmentalists and progressive regulators have blocked or delayed many fuel-reduction projects, prioritizing preservation over prevention. The result: taxpayers foot massive firefighting bills while homes burn.

Republicans and property owners are demanding faster permitting for forest-thinning projects and fewer regulatory obstacles to prescribed burns—the kind of active management that reduces fuel loads before fire season arrives.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic leadership will finally embrace aggressive forest management or continue blocking the very tools that could prevent the next Aspen Acres.
