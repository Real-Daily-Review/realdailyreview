# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are being forced to finance primary elections for political parties they may not belong to or support—a government overreach that deserves scrutiny.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state has no legitimate business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties. The core complaint is straightforward: why should Colorado residents subsidize Democratic or Republican primaries when many voters are independents, unaffiliated, or members of other parties?

Conservatives argue this represents textbook government favoritism. Taxpayer dollars should not flow to partisan machinery. The arrangement effectively locks in the two-party duopoly while forcing citizens outside that system to bankroll elections they have no stake in.

*Complete Colorado* frames ending this practice as a "courageous" move—one that would genuinely separate state from party and restore fiscal fairness. The case is compelling: if you don't belong to a party, you shouldn't be forced to pay for its primary.

This touches a nerve in Colorado's independent-minded electorate. The state has a significant unaffiliated voter population that increasingly resents subsidizing partisan operations.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado lawmakers will act on this principle or continue defending the status quo as "necessary" for party operations.
