# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, September 21, 2026

Colorado conservatives, buckle up: Washington is moving fast on artificial intelligence, Denver's crime problem isn't slowing down, and Colorado Democrats are eyeing your paycheck again. Here's what matters this morning.

---

## Trump Eyes 'AI Force' and AI Czar to Lead America's Tech Future

President Trump announced plans to create a new "AI Force" and appoint an AI czar to coordinate the country's artificial intelligence strategy. For Colorado, which hosts a growing tech corridor along the Front Range and significant defense and aerospace contractors, federal leadership on AI policy matters enormously — both in terms of keeping government regulators out of the way and ensuring American dominance over China. Conservatives will want to watch whether this initiative stays lean or becomes another sprawling federal bureaucracy.

---

## Amendment 87: Colorado Democrats Want to Soak the Broncos — and You

If Amendment 87 passes, every Denver Bronco currently paying Colorado's flat 4.4% income tax rate would see that rate jump to 8.4% — nearly doubling their state tax burden. But don't be fooled into thinking this is just a fight about millionaire athletes. The Broncos are the most visible example of a broader tax hike scheme that would hammer high earners, business owners, and anyone the left decides makes "too much." Colorado's flat tax was a hard-won protection for taxpayers. Amendment 87 is a direct assault on it, and every Colorado taxpayer should be paying attention.

---

## Denver Stabbing: Juvenile Arrested as City's Crime Spiral Continues

A juvenile girl was arrested Sunday in connection with a stabbing that occurred in Denver on Saturday. The details are still emerging, but the pattern is not new: Denver's progressive criminal justice experiment — light on accountability, heavy on excuses — keeps producing predictable results. Colorado conservatives and law-and-order voters have been sounding the alarm for years. Until Denver's leadership gets serious about consequences, residents will keep paying the price.

---

## Vance's 'Hamiltonian Turn': A GOP Debate Worth Having

Vice President JD Vance made waves by declaring that Republican economic policy is now "much more Alexander Hamilton than it is Milton Friedman" — and calling that shift "obviously a good thing." That's a significant statement. Friedman's tradition championed free markets and limited government intervention; Hamilton's favored industrial policy and state-directed economic development. Conservatives who believe in free markets, deregulation, and keeping government out of the economy should engage seriously with this debate rather than ignore it. The direction the GOP takes on economics will shape policy for a generation.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on Amendment 87 as the campaign heats up — the left will dress this tax hike up as "fairness," but Colorado taxpayers should recognize it for what it is: a foot in the door to dismantle the flat tax. On the national front, watch who Trump taps as AI czar and whether the new "AI Force" comes with a lean mandate or a bloated budget. And in Denver, watch whether city leadership responds to the weekend's violence with accountability — or more of the same excuses that have defined the progressive approach to public safety.
