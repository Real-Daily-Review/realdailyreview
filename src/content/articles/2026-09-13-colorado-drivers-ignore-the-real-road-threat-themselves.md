# Distracted Driving: The Self-Inflicted Colorado Road Crisis Nobody Wants to Admit

Colorado drivers are their own worst enemy on the road—and government crash statistics aren't telling the full story.

New data cited by the Washington Examiner reveal that distracted driving crashes are significantly underreported, with millions of drivers casually scrolling playlists or checking texts at red lights while convinced their screen habit poses no real risk. The gap between perceived threats (drunk drivers, speeders) and actual behavior exposes a personal responsibility problem that regulation alone cannot fix.

Conservatives argue this is precisely where government overreach fails. Rather than piling on new phone-use bans or surveillance-heavy enforcement schemes—the progressive instinct—the real solution lies in individual accountability and driver education. Colorado already has distracted driving laws; what's missing is the cultural shift toward personal discipline.

The Left's typical response: more regulations, stricter penalties, and technology mandates. But as the data show, people know the risks and ignore them anyway. No law changes human nature.

For Colorado taxpayers and commuters, the lesson is clear: you are responsible for your safety and others'. No government mandate replaces common sense.
