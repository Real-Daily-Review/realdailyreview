# Dead Voters, Election Integrity, and Colorado's Vulnerability

California's voter roll chaos should alarm Colorado election officials—nearly 10,000 dead people remain on Los Angeles County's active rolls, according to a Public Interest Legal Foundation complaint filed with state election officials (Breitbart). While California's problem is extreme, Colorado's own voter registration database requires scrutiny to prevent similar vulnerabilities.

Election integrity matters. Republicans argue that bloated voter rolls—whether from deceased registrants, moved voters, or administrative neglect—create opportunities for fraud and undermine confidence in results. Democrats counter that dead voters rarely cast ballots and that such cases are isolated administrative errors, not evidence of systemic fraud.

Colorado has made strides cleaning its rolls, but the state's automatic voter registration system and mail-ballot-heavy elections demand constant vigilance. County clerks must actively purge records of deceased residents and those who've relocated—a task that requires resources and accountability.

Meanwhile, La Plata County faces a second flooding crisis, with thousands evacuated and residents stranded as roadways wash away (KDVR Fox31 Denver). Infrastructure failures compound disaster response.

**Watch for:** whether Colorado's Secretary of State conducts a comprehensive audit of voter roll accuracy and whether the state's election infrastructure can withstand both natural disasters and administrative lapses.
