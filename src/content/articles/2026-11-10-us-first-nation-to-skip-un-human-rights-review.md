# U.S. Breaks with UN Tradition, Skips Human Rights Review

The Trump administration made history Tuesday by becoming the first nation to completely refuse participation in a UN Human Rights Council review—a process that has operated for nearly two decades (Washington Examiner).

The move signals a sharp departure from decades of diplomatic protocol. The U.S. declined to submit to the council's universal periodic review, a standard process where member nations face scrutiny of their human rights records. By skipping the review entirely, the administration rejected what critics on the left view as an essential accountability mechanism.

Republicans and Trump officials argue the UN Human Rights Council itself lacks credibility and legitimacy. The council has long been criticized by conservatives for harboring authoritarian regimes—including China, Russia, and Saudi Arabia—while disproportionately targeting democratic nations like Israel and the U.S. From this perspective, withdrawing from a compromised process is principled, not evasive.

Democrats and international-relations advocates counter that abandoning the review damages American credibility on the global stage and surrenders moral authority on human rights advocacy.

For Colorado voters, this reflects the broader Trump administration posture toward multilateral institutions: skepticism of bodies perceived as anti-American or ineffective.
