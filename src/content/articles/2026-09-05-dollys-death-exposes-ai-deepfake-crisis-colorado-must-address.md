# Dolly Parton's Death Exposes AI Deepfake Crisis—Colorado Should Demand Legal Protections Now

Stella Parton is calling out the flood of AI-generated deepfakes and misinformation spreading online following her sister Dolly's death, highlighting a growing threat that demands legislative action before Colorado families and businesses fall victim to similar exploitation.

Stella, 77, took to social media to condemn what she called "fake AI garbage" circulating across the internet, thanking the public for genuine support while blasting the fabricated images and videos (The Daily Wire). The situation underscores how artificial intelligence can weaponize grief and celebrity for clicks and chaos—a problem that extends far beyond country music.

For Colorado, this is a wake-up call. Deepfakes threaten our election integrity, business reputation, and personal privacy. Bad actors can impersonate political candidates, business leaders, and ordinary citizens to spread disinformation or commit fraud. Colorado's legislature has largely ignored this threat while progressive states experiment with vague AI regulations that often create more problems than they solve.

Republicans and conservatives argue Colorado needs targeted, narrow legislation protecting citizens and businesses from malicious deepfakes—especially in election cycles—without handing regulators a blank check to micromanage AI development. The state should focus on criminal liability for fraudulent deepfakes, not broad restrictions that stifle innovation.

**Watch whether Colorado lawmakers introduce meaningful deepfake legislation in the 2025 session, or whether they'll punt to federal regulators while the problem metastasizes.**
