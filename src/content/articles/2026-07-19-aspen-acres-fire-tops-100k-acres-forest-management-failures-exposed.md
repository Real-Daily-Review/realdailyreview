# Aspen Acres Fire Becomes One of Colorado's Largest; Over 800 Structures Destroyed

Colorado's wildfire crisis has reached historic proportions as the Aspen Acres Fire in southern Colorado surpasses 100,000 acres burned, making it among the largest fires in state history. According to the Northern Rockies Complex Incident Management Team, the fire has destroyed 851 structures and forced widespread evacuations near Rye and San Isabel (9News Denver). As of Saturday, July 18, containment reached 61 percent with 1,611 personnel deployed to battle the blaze (KDVR Fox31).

The fire's rapid spread underscores a hard reality: Colorado's forests are tinderboxes. Conservatives have long argued that decades of forest mismanagement—excessive regulation preventing timber harvesting, fuel reduction, and controlled burns—have left the state's woodlands choked with dead trees and undergrowth. Rather than blame climate alone, Republicans point to federal and state policies that prioritize environmental litigation over practical forest stewardship.

Rain provided mixed relief across the state Friday, aiding some fires while leaving others largely unaffected (Colorado Sun). Crews continue focusing on protecting communities as hot, dry conditions persist.

Coloradans should watch whether state and federal officials finally embrace aggressive forest management and timber harvesting—or double down on the regulatory approach that helped create these catastrophic conditions.
