# Trump Admin Extends China Trade Deal, But Colorado Ag Sector Watches Soybean Promises Closely

The Trump administration has extended its trade agreement with China through January 10, rolling back restrictions on critical minerals and high-tech exports that threatened global supply chains, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced this week (Just The News).

Bessent characterized the arrangement as an "economic détente," noting that China has been "very fulsome" on soybean purchases—a critical metric for Colorado and broader U.S. agricultural interests. However, he acknowledged that Beijing has fallen short on other agricultural commitments (Breitbart).

For Colorado farmers and ranchers, the soybean gains matter. The state's agricultural economy depends on export markets, and any rollback of trade restrictions could ease pressure on commodity prices. Yet conservatives should remain skeptical: Bessent's own admission that China hasn't fully honored non-soybean agricultural deals suggests the détente remains fragile and incomplete.

The extension through mid-January also leaves uncertainty hanging over the sector. A new administration or hardline shift could upend these arrangements, leaving Colorado producers vulnerable to sudden tariff escalation.

Watch whether China actually follows through on expanded soybean purchases in the coming months—and whether the administration pursues tougher enforcement on non-agricultural commitments that benefit American workers and manufacturers.
