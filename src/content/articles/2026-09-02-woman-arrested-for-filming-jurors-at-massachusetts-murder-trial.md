# Woman Arrested for Filming Jurors at Lindsay Clancy Murder Trial

A Massachusetts court has exposed a serious breach of jury security when a 56-year-old woman was arrested for allegedly filming jurors in the Lindsay Clancy murder case. Dawn Light, who describes herself as a "crime junkie," was caught taking photos and video of jurors as they were dismissed on day four of deliberations, according to *Just The News* and *The Daily Wire*.

Light faces charges of intimidating a juror and entered a restricted courthouse area. She claims she was attempting to photograph defendant Lindsay Clancy when she unknowingly captured jurors on film—a defense that strains credibility given her deliberate entry into off-limits zones.

The incident underscores a critical vulnerability in jury protection. Jurors depend on anonymity and freedom from external pressure to render fair verdicts. When courthouse security fails to prevent unauthorized filming, it compromises the entire judicial process and exposes jurors to potential harassment or intimidation campaigns orchestrated by trial watchers, activists, or media figures.

This case raises hard questions about courthouse access, security protocols, and whether current safeguards adequately protect jurors from the social-media age's obsession with high-profile trials. Conservatives argue that robust jury protection is foundational to rule of law—and that courthouses must enforce boundaries strictly.
