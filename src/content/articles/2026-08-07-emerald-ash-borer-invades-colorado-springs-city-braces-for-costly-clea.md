# Emerald Ash Borer Invades Colorado Springs—City Faces Tree Crisis and Regulatory Headaches

An invasive beetle native to Asia has been confirmed in Colorado Springs, threatening roughly one in five city trees, according to CPR News and 9News Denver. The Emerald Ash Borer poses a significant ecological and financial challenge to Colorado's second-largest city, which now faces costly removal and replacement programs.

Colorado Springs officials say they're preparing a "proactive management plan" to combat the pest. But conservatives should ask hard questions: How much will this cost taxpayers? Will the city use this crisis to justify expanded government spending or new environmental regulations that extend beyond pest control? Invasive species management, while necessary, often becomes a Trojan horse for broader regulatory overreach.

The timing is worth noting. As Complete Colorado reported, Colorado Springs is simultaneously embroiled in an election lawsuit over municipal voting dates—the first enforcement action under Colorado's 2025 voting law. The city is juggling multiple crises that could reshape local governance and municipal budgets.

Coloradans should watch whether city leadership uses the ash borer outbreak to justify tax increases or new land-use restrictions that go beyond reasonable pest management.
