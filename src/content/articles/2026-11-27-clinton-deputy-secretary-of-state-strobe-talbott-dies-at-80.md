# Strobe Talbott, Clinton-Era Diplomat and Brookings Chief, Dies at 80

A major voice in the foreign policy establishment has passed. Strobe Talbott, who served as deputy secretary of state under Bill Clinton and later led the Brookings Institution, died at 80, according to Fox News and 9News Denver.

Talbott shaped U.S. policy toward Russia during the 1990s and spent decades as a fixture in Democratic-aligned think tanks. Before his government service, he was a Time magazine reporter—a career path that reflected his deep ties to mainstream media and progressive policy circles.

The Brookings Institution, where Talbott served as president, has long been a wellspring of center-left policy proposals, from tax-and-spend economics to expansive regulatory frameworks. Under his leadership, the think tank produced research that often aligned with Democratic priorities on climate, government spending, and international engagement.

For Colorado conservatives, Talbott's influence on foreign policy and domestic regulation matters: Brookings research has shaped federal energy policy, water management rules, and land-use decisions that directly affect Colorado ranchers, energy workers, and taxpayers. His death marks a generational shift in the Democratic foreign policy establishment.
