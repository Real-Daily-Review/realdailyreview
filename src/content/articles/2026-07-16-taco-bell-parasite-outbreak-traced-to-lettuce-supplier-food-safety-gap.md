# Taco Bell Parasite Outbreak Traced to Lettuce Supplier; Food Safety Questions Mount

A lettuce supplier has emerged as the likely source of a cyclosporiasis outbreak linked to Taco Bell, according to CDC findings reported by The Washington Post. The parasitic infection, which causes severe gastrointestinal illness, has sickened customers across Michigan, Ohio, West Virginia, and Kentucky.

The outbreak underscores a persistent vulnerability in America's food supply chain—one that regulatory agencies have struggled to prevent despite decades of oversight. While the FDA maintains inspection protocols, critics argue that centralized sourcing of produce creates cascading contamination risks that no amount of bureaucratic oversight can fully eliminate.

Republicans and food-safety advocates have long warned that consolidation in agricultural supply chains, combined with regulatory complexity that favors large producers over smaller operations, leaves consumers exposed. Some conservatives argue that market transparency and private certification standards—rather than expanded government regulation—offer better protection.

The CDC's identification of the specific farm represents a reactive, not preventive, approach: consumers already fell ill before the source was pinpointed. (9News Denver, New York Post)

Coloradans should monitor whether this incident prompts calls for new federal food-safety mandates that could burden Colorado's agricultural sector without meaningfully improving outcomes.
