# Second Rescue in a Week at Abandoned Colorado Missile Silo Raises Safety Questions

Arapahoe County deputies pulled two more people from an underground shaft early Tuesday—the second rescue at an abandoned missile silo in a week—raising urgent questions about property liability and public safety on derelict federal installations.

Last week, an 18-year-old was extracted from the same underground structure. Tuesday's rescue of a man and woman suggests the site remains dangerously accessible despite its hazards. The repeated incidents highlight a broader problem: abandoned Cold War-era infrastructure scattered across Colorado poses real risks to curious explorers and trespassers alike.

Property owners and local officials face mounting pressure to secure these sites or face liability claims when rescues become necessary. Each emergency response diverts county resources and taxpayer dollars from other priorities. (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31)

Conservatives argue that property owners—whether private or federal—must be held accountable for maintaining safe premises and preventing unauthorized access. The cost of repeated rescues ultimately falls on Colorado taxpayers. Meanwhile, the state should clarify liability rules so owners have clear incentives to fence, post warnings, or remediate hazardous conditions rather than leaving dangerous sites as de facto public attractions.
