# RNC Challenges Colorado's Overseas Voter Residency Rules Under Griswold

Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold is facing a federal lawsuit from the Republican National Committee over her office's handling of overseas voter residency requirements—a direct challenge to voting eligibility standards that could reshape how the state processes ballots from abroad.

The RNC's complaint, filed in federal court, argues that Griswold's interpretation allowing residency to pass by proxy through a parent or spouse is unconstitutional (Complete Colorado). Republicans contend the practice creates a loophole enabling individuals with no genuine Colorado ties to cast ballots in state elections, undermining election integrity and voter eligibility rules.

The lawsuit arrives amid broader scrutiny of Griswold's tenure. The Denver Post's editorial board recently highlighted her "latest scandal," noting that opponent Michael Allen brings "experience and expertise in litigation" while having "avoided scandal while in public office" (Denver Post).

**The Colorado angle:** Election administration directly affects voter confidence and ballot legitimacy. If courts side with the RNC, Colorado could tighten overseas voter verification—potentially affecting military families, expatriates, and other out-of-state residents claiming Colorado residency.

Conservatives argue that clear residency standards protect against ballot fraud and ensure only legitimate Colorado voters decide state races. Democrats have traditionally resisted tighter verification, framing it as voter suppression.

**Watch:** Whether federal courts rule on the RNC's constitutional challenge before November's election cycle.
