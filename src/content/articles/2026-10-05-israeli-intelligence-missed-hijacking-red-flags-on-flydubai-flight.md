# Flydubai Co-Pilot's Hijacking Attempt Exposes Intelligence Gaps Between U.S. Allies

A Flydubai co-pilot's failed attempt to crash a Dubai-to-Tel Aviv flight has exposed troubling gaps in intelligence-sharing between Israel and the UAE, even as both nations work to counter terrorism in the Middle East.

According to the Washington Examiner, Israeli authorities received a passenger and crew manifest from UAE officials *before* the hijacking attempt, but failed to flag any security concerns about 29-year-old co-pilot Hamam al-Hammadi. The pilot's attempted suicide dive was thwarted by crew intervention mid-flight.

The Daily Wire reports that UAE authorities are now pursuing the death penalty for al-Hammadi under Emirati law—a maximum sentence reflecting the severity of the attack.

**The conservative angle:** While Colorado has no direct stake in Middle East aviation security, this incident underscores why robust intelligence coordination among democratic allies matters. Republicans have long argued that effective counterterrorism requires real-time information-sharing and vetting protocols—not bureaucratic delays or intelligence silos. The failure to flag a known security risk before boarding raises hard questions about whether current screening procedures are sufficient.
