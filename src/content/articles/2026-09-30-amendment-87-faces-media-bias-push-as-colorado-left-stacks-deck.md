# Colorado Amendment 87 Faces Media Bias as Election Looms

Colorado's progressive establishment is using incomplete arguments and sympathetic media coverage to push Amendment 87 through the November ballot, according to conservative critics who say the state's political class is stacking the deck.

Complete Colorado reports that both Colorado media outlets and the state legislature have tilted heavily in favor of the amendment, presenting selective arguments while downplaying conservative concerns about its fiscal and regulatory implications.

The amendment battle reflects a broader pattern: when Colorado voters face consequential ballot measures, the left-leaning institutional media and Democratic-controlled statehouse coordinate messaging to shape public opinion. Republicans argue this creates an uneven playing field where taxpayers hear only one side of the story before casting votes that could expand government spending or regulatory authority.

Meanwhile, election security remains a secondary concern. The Denver Post noted that three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024 before detection, though Amendment 84—which would require Social Security numbers for mail voting—could have prevented at least some fraud. The Post framed Colorado's system as robust, but conservatives counter that even three fraudulent ballots are three too many, and that tightening verification standards is common sense.
