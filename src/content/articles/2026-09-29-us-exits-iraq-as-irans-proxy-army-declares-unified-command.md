# U.S. Withdrawal from Iraq Leaves Power Vacuum as Iran's Proxy Network Tightens

America's imminent exit from Iraq this week hands Tehran a strategic victory just as Iran's proxy militias openly declare unified command across the Middle East.

A brigadier general advising the Houthi-run Defense Ministry told Agence France-Presse on Sept. 23 that "the militias and Iran form one alliance and one body," with "joint coordination and cooperation" spanning Iran, Lebanon, and allied forces throughout the region (Washington Examiner). The admission underscores what conservatives have long warned: withdrawing U.S. forces without securing lasting stability invites Iranian expansion.

Republicans argue the Biden administration's Iraq pullout repeats the strategic blunder of the 2011 withdrawal, which created the vacuum ISIS exploited. Leaving now—as Iran's proxy network openly consolidates command—risks destabilizing Iraq, empowering anti-American forces, and threatening American interests across the Middle East.

For Colorado, this matters. Military families stationed at bases like Fort Carson depend on coherent Middle East strategy. Defense contractors and energy security tied to regional stability affect state jobs and national security posture.
