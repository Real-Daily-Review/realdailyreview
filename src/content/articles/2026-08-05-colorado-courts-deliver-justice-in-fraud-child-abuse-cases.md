# Colorado Crime Brief: Fraud and Abuse Cases Highlight Justice System at Work

Colorado's courts are delivering accountability in two serious cases that underscore the need for swift, certain punishment. A former Denver resident has been sentenced to 3.5 years in prison with millions in restitution for a multi-million-dollar fraud scheme (KDVR), while a former Fort Collins teacher faces prison time after pleading guilty to attempted sexual exploitation of a child and child abuse (9News).

These cases reflect a core conservative principle: the justice system must protect Coloradans and hold criminals accountable. Fraud schemes that bilk victims of millions and predatory behavior by those entrusted with children demand serious consequences—not soft-on-crime policies that have plagued progressive-led jurisdictions.

Colorado taxpayers should note that both cases required investigation and prosecution resources. While these convictions represent wins for law enforcement, conservatives argue that Colorado must maintain robust funding for police, prosecutors, and courts to combat rising crime, rather than diverting resources to social experiments or defunding public safety.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature continues supporting law-and-order priorities or pivots toward the progressive agenda of reducing incarceration and redirecting criminal-justice funding.
