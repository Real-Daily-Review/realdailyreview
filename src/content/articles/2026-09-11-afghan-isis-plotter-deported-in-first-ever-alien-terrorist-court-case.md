# Federal Court Deports Afghan ISIS Plotter in Landmark Terrorism Case

A 47-year-old Afghan national has become the first person deported under a specialized federal court designed to remove alien terrorists—a rare legal victory for border security and national safety that conservatives say vindicates tough immigration enforcement.

Nazira Haji Zada, living in Fort Worth, Texas, admitted to being an "alien terrorist" and was deported last month after plotting an ISIS-inspired mass shooting attack on Election Day 2024 alongside her son and son-in-law, according to the Justice Department (Fox News). The Alien Terrorist Removal Court (ATRC), created by Congress 30 years ago, had never been used until this case—underscoring how rarely such extreme vetting failures reach the deportation stage.

Conservatives argue the case demonstrates why immigration screening must be rigorous and why the U.S. cannot afford to admit individuals with terror ties or sympathies. The fact that a known terrorist operative lived undetected in America long enough to plan a coordinated attack raises hard questions about visa vetting and interior enforcement that Republicans say Democrats have systematically weakened.

Zada's son and son-in-law were previously arrested and convicted in connection with the plot (The Daily Wire). The case offers no direct Colorado angle but underscores national security gaps that affect all Americans.
