# America Marks 25 Years Since 9/11—But Still Debates the Cost of Security State

A quarter-century after the September 11 terrorist attacks, Americans are reflecting on how the nation has changed—and conservatives are asking hard questions about whether post-9/11 expansions of government power have delivered security or simply created permanent bureaucratic overreach.

Congress designated September 11 as both Patriot Day and a National Day of Service and Remembrance, though it remains a regular workday rather than a federal holiday (9News Denver). Just The News notes that America has "changed in some ways, both good and bad," with technology, media, security, and the republic itself transformed dramatically since 2001.

For Colorado conservatives, the 9/11 anniversary raises a familiar tension: the attacks justified massive expansions of federal surveillance, airport security, and intelligence gathering that persist today—often with minimal oversight and questionable effectiveness. Republicans and limited-government advocates have long argued that post-9/11 security measures, from the TSA to NSA data collection, became permanent fixtures that eroded privacy and civil liberties far beyond what genuine counterterrorism requires.

The question isn't whether America needed stronger defenses—it's whether those defenses required surrendering constitutional protections that terrorists aimed to destroy in the first place.
