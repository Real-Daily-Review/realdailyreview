# Trump's Iran Deal Reversal Draws Fire From Right and Left

President Trump's abrupt capitulation on Iran negotiations has unified critics across the political spectrum—a rare moment when National Review conservatives and Colorado Sun columnists agree the administration bungled a major foreign policy moment.

According to National Review, Trump "abandoned his previous positions in favor of the Iranian position on all the key issues of contention" entering the conflict. The president's reversal undercuts years of hardline rhetoric on Tehran's nuclear ambitions and regional aggression.

Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin argues the administration is now resorting to distraction and false claims to cover the diplomatic loss, citing Vice President JD Vance's inflammatory rhetoric about immigrants as evidence the White House lacks credible messaging.

**What conservatives say:** Republicans who backed Trump's tougher Iran stance now face a credibility gap. The reversal signals weakness to adversaries and raises questions about whether the administration can sustain any coherent foreign policy doctrine—a concern that matters to Colorado defense contractors and military families.

**The broader worry:** If Trump cannot hold the line on Iran after years of confrontation, what does that mean for his negotiating position on other fronts? Watch whether Senate Republicans demand accountability or quietly move past the reversal.
