# Jefferson County Schools Measure Barely Survives—But Voter Skepticism Is the Real Story

Colorado's second-largest school district narrowly won a $73 million tax hike after a recount, but the razor-thin margin exposes deep voter hesitation about throwing more money at education without accountability.

Jefferson County Public Schools' Proposition NN squeaked through by the slimmest of margins—just five votes out of nearly 302,000 cast, according to the Denver Post. The measure would increase property taxes to fund operations in a district already spending heavily on administration and overhead.

The Colorado Sun's analysis notes the passage was technically a "win," but the tepid support signals voters aren't convinced that more tax dollars equal better outcomes. Republicans and fiscal conservatives have long argued that Colorado schools receive substantial funding increases year after year, yet student achievement and test scores haven't kept pace. They question whether the problem is truly insufficient money or misaligned priorities and bureaucratic waste.

Democrats and education advocates counter that schools face genuine budget pressures and that property-tax-dependent funding models leave districts vulnerable. They frame spending as "investment in our future."

The real takeaway: JeffCo voters were so unconvinced they nearly rejected the measure entirely. Conservatives should watch whether the district uses this mandate to demonstrate real fiscal discipline and measurable results—or simply expands payroll as usual.
