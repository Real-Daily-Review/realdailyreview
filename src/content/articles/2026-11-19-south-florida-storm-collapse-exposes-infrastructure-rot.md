# South Florida Storms Expose Infrastructure Vulnerabilities as El Niño Intensifies

A slow-moving storm system fueled by El Niño conditions hammered South Florida this week, flooding roads, closing highway sections, and collapsing a synagogue roof—a stark reminder that even wealthy regions remain vulnerable to severe weather when infrastructure falls behind.

The deluge prompted an emergency declaration Wednesday as heavy rainfall overwhelmed drainage systems across the region (9News Denver, New York Post). While the immediate damage is localized to Florida, the incident underscores a broader concern: government-managed infrastructure often fails under stress, yet progressive policymakers reflexively demand more taxpayer spending rather than honest assessment of why existing systems crumble.

**The conservative argument:** Before greenlight­ing another round of "climate resilience" spending—code for wasteful stimulus—Florida officials must audit why current infrastructure proved inadequate. Throwing money at problems without accountability has become the default response. Conservatives argue that private-sector engineering and competitive bidding, not government bloat, deliver results.

**Colorado angle:** As Colorado faces its own water management challenges and aging infrastructure, this serves as a cautionary tale. State and local governments must prioritize maintenance and efficiency over expansion and new programs.

Watch for whether Florida's emergency response includes genuine infrastructure reform or simply becomes a vehicle for progressive climate-spending proposals.
