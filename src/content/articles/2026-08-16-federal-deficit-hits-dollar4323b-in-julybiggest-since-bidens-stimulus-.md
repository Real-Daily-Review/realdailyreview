# Federal Deficit Hits $432.3B in July—Time to Stop the Spending Spree

The federal government posted a $432.3 billion deficit in July, the largest since March 2021, and conservatives are sounding the alarm: Washington is borrowing at unsustainable levels with no pandemic or war to justify it.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the July deficit rivals the spike triggered by President Biden's COVID-19 stimulus checks—the very spending that fueled the inflation that hammered Colorado families' grocery bills and energy costs. President Trump, the *Examiner* notes, lacks the excuse of a global pandemic or declared war to explain this level of red ink.

The math is brutal. With an aging population and entitlement obligations already straining the budget, Republicans argue the federal government must confront spending discipline or face a debt crisis that will devastate future generations and crowd out private investment in Colorado's economy.

Conservatives say the lesson is clear: stimulus spending doesn't disappear—it compounds into deficits that eventually demand either massive tax hikes or inflation that erodes Coloradans' paychecks and savings. The *Examiner* warns that living beyond our means cannot continue indefinitely.
