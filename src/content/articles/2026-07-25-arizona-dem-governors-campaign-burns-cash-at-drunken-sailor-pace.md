# Arizona Dem Governor's Campaign Cash Burn Shows Why Voters Reject Progressive Spending Culture

Arizona Governor Katie Hobbs' reelection campaign is hemorrhaging money like a government agency with no budget constraints—and Republicans say it's a window into why voters are souring on Democratic fiscal recklessness.

Hobbs' operation burned through $7.4 million in the second quarter while raising just $2.64 million, according to Fox News and the New York Post. That's a spending-to-fundraising ratio that would make any Colorado small business owner wince. GOP critics argue the imbalance reveals a deeper problem: Democrats rely on wealthy donors and consultant class money rather than genuine grassroots support.

The spending strategy itself tells the story. Rather than building volunteer networks and community energy, Hobbs' campaign is pouring cash into paid media and high-priced consultants—the political equivalent of throwing money at a problem instead of solving it. Republicans argue this mirrors how Democratic governors and legislatures approach governing: spend first, ask questions later, and hope consultants can spin away the consequences.

For Colorado conservatives watching Democratic spending patterns in neighboring states, this is instructive. It's the same playbook: unsustainable budgets, reliance on consultant class expertise, and faith that enough advertising can overcome voter skepticism about fiscal management.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats adopt similar spending strategies in 2026 state races—and whether voters punish them for it.
