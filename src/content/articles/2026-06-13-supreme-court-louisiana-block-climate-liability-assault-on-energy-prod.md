# Supreme Court, Louisiana Reject Climate Lawsuit Assault on Energy Producers

Colorado's energy sector is watching as the Supreme Court and Louisiana tighten the legal noose around activist climate litigation targeting oil and gas companies. The high court recently rejected the expansive standing doctrine that would let plaintiffs sue over speculative climate theories, while Louisiana Gov. Jeff Landry signed legislation explicitly barring lawsuits claiming energy producers are liable for manmade climate change (Just The News).

The Supreme Court's decision, detailed in *National Review*, centered on who can actually sue to enforce federal law—rejecting what conservatives call "judicially invented lawsuits" and the use of legislative history to manufacture legal theories. Justice Barrett and Justice Jackson clashed sharply over the scope of such claims.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil, gas, and coal industries face relentless litigation from environmental groups seeking to hold producers liable for climate impacts. These rulings provide legal cover against the kind of expansive tort theories that could cripple energy development and cost jobs in Western Slope communities dependent on responsible energy production.

Democrats and climate activists argue such lawsuits are necessary accountability mechanisms. Republicans counter that they're a backdoor regulatory scheme designed to bankrupt energy companies without legislative approval—exactly what Colorado voters rejected when they opposed ballot measures targeting oil and gas.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature attempts to follow Louisiana's model or whether activist judges in state courts ignore these federal precedents.
