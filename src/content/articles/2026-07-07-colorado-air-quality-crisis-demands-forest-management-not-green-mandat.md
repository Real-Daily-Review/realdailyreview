# Colorado's Air Quality Crisis Demands Honest Solutions, Not Green Mandates

Denver's air quality has hit a crisis point—ranked worst in the nation and 10th globally—but Colorado's response must focus on real forest management, not ideological environmental overreach.

Wildfire smoke is the primary culprit driving air quality alerts across the metro (9News Denver). More residents are seeking medical care as smoke impacts worsen (KDVR Fox31). The health toll is real and immediate.

Here's where conservatives and the state's progressive leadership diverge sharply. **Republicans and energy advocates argue** that Colorado's decades-long fire suppression policies and resistance to responsible timber harvesting have created tinderbox conditions. They point to overgrown forests choking the state's watersheds and wildland-urban interface. **The left's answer**, however, has been to blame energy development and push aggressive climate regulations that do little to address the immediate forest health crisis.

Coloradans deserve air quality solutions grounded in science and land stewardship—thinning forests, clearing deadwood, and restoring natural fire cycles—not punitive regulations on energy producers or mandates that drive up electricity costs for families already struggling with inflation.

The state's environmental establishment must answer: where is the aggressive forest management plan to prevent the next catastrophic fire season?
