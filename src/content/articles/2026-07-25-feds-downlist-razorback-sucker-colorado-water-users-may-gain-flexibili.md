# Feds Downlist Razorback Sucker Despite Ongoing River Struggles

Federal wildlife officials have downlisted the razorback sucker from endangered to threatened status—a move that could ease regulatory burdens on Colorado River water users and energy development, but one that conservation groups say ignores the fish's precarious reality.

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service made the reclassification official, citing recovery progress. However, critics argue the designation is premature. One national wildlife non-profit told KDVR that the fish's outlook is "no better today" than when it was first declared endangered, raising questions about whether the downlisting reflects genuine recovery or bureaucratic convenience (KDVR).

For Colorado, the stakes are real. The razorback sucker listing has been used to justify water restrictions and regulatory constraints on agriculture, hydropower, and other river-dependent industries. A threatened status typically allows more flexibility than endangered designation—potentially opening room for water allocations and development decisions that stricter protections would block.

Conservatives argue the downlisting reflects sound science and recognizes that recovery efforts have worked. Environmentalists counter that the fish remains vulnerable and that weakening protections now risks unraveling decades of conservation work.

**Watch for:** whether this reclassification triggers legal challenges from environmental groups and how it reshapes Colorado River Compact negotiations and state water policy.
