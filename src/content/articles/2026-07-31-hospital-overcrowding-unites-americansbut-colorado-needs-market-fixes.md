# Hospital Overcrowding Transcends Politics—But Colorado's Healthcare Crisis Demands Market Solutions

Hospital overcrowding strips away partisan labels, uniting Americans across the political divide in shared frustration with wait times and delayed care, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The piece argues that experiences like scheduling elective surgery or finding an available bed reveal a common human vulnerability that supersedes debates over taxes, immigration, and climate policy. While the observation rings true, conservatives note that Colorado's healthcare challenges stem partly from regulatory barriers and government-imposed inefficiencies—not from too much market competition.

Republicans and free-market advocates argue that certificate-of-need laws, insurance mandates, and Medicare reimbursement caps artificially constrain hospital capacity and drive up costs. Rather than expanding government involvement in healthcare, conservatives say Colorado should remove regulatory obstacles that prevent hospitals from expanding beds, allow price transparency, and let competition drive down costs and improve service.

Democrats typically counter that healthcare is a public good requiring government oversight to ensure access and affordability—a position that has produced mixed results in states with heavy regulation.

Colorado taxpayers and patients should watch whether state policymakers respond to overcrowding with deregulation and market reforms, or with more government spending and mandates.
