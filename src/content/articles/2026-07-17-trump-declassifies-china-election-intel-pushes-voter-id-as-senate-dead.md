# Trump Declassifies Election Intel, Pushes Voter ID as Senate Faces SAVE Act Deadline

President Trump released declassified intelligence Thursday alleging China compromised voter rolls in at least 18 states, reigniting the election-security debate and putting pressure on Senate Republicans to pass the SAVE America Act before time runs out.

Trump used the intelligence release to call for mandatory voter ID and citizenship verification nationwide—measures conservatives have long championed as basic safeguards against fraud and foreign interference. According to Fox News, Trump highlighted what he termed "shocking vulnerabilities" in the declassified records.

Republicans argue that voter ID requirements and citizenship checks are commonsense protections that most democracies already enforce. Democrats typically oppose such measures, claiming they create barriers to voting, though they've offered no credible alternative to address the foreign-access concerns the declassified documents raise.

For Colorado, the timing matters: the state's mail-ballot system and voter rolls could face similar vulnerabilities if the intelligence findings are accurate. Colorado Republicans should demand answers from Secretary of State Jena Griswold on whether Chinese actors accessed state voter data and what safeguards exist.

The SAVE Act's Senate fate will determine whether election-security reform gains traction before 2026—watch whether Colorado's senators back Trump's voter ID push or side with progressive resistance to stricter verification standards.
