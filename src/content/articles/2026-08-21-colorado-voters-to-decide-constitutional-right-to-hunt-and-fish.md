# Colorado Voters Face Constitutional Hunt-and-Fish Amendment

Colorado conservatives are rallying behind a constitutional amendment that would enshrine the right to hunt and fish in the state Constitution—a direct pushback against progressive environmental groups seeking to restrict outdoor traditions. Initiative 232, brought by the conservative nonprofit Advance Colorado (per *Colorado Sun*), requires a 55 percent supermajority for passage and stands as a counterweight to liberal ballot measures this cycle, including a separate graduated income tax proposal.

Republicans argue the amendment protects Coloradans' heritage and property rights against creeping regulatory overreach from environmental activists who have repeatedly targeted hunting seasons and fishing access. The measure reflects growing concern that unelected bureaucrats and left-wing advocacy groups are using administrative power to chip away at outdoor freedoms without voter consent.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that such constitutional protections could hamstring wildlife management and conservation efforts, though *Complete Colorado* notes the amendment's specific language remains the focal point of debate.

For Colorado's hunting and fishing community—a cornerstone of rural economies and outdoor heritage—this ballot fight signals whether voters will defend traditional use rights or cede ground to progressive restrictions.
