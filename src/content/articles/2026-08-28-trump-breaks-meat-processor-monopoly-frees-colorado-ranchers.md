# Trump Moves to Break Meat-Processing Monopoly for Colorado Ranchers

President Trump is moving to let ranchers and farmers process their own livestock—a direct challenge to the stranglehold four mega-corporations hold over American meat production.

Trump accused big meat processors—Tyson, Cargill, JBS USA, and National Beef Packing Co.—of running a "nasty Monopoly" that squeezes ranchers' margins and limits their independence (Breitbart). The administration says breaking this bottleneck will give farmers and ranchers control over their own supply chains and protect their livelihoods.

For Colorado's ranching community, this matters enormously. The state's cattle industry has long chafed under processor consolidation, which limits where ranchers can sell and at what price. Allowing on-farm or regional processing could restore bargaining power and keep more revenue in rural Colorado counties.

Conservatives argue this is genuine deregulation—removing federal barriers that artificially protect corporate giants and strangle small operators. It aligns with Trump's broader push to challenge corporate consolidation and restore competition.

The move requires navigating USDA rules and food-safety regulations, which currently restrict custom processing. Watch whether Colorado legislators and the state agriculture department move to align state law with federal changes, enabling ranchers to actually exercise this new authority.
