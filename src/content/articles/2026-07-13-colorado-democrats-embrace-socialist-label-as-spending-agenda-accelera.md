# Colorado Democrat's 'Socialist' Label Masks Radical Spending Agenda

Colorado Democrats are embracing the "democratic socialist" label with increasing brazenness, and conservatives say the rhetoric masks a dangerous expansion of government spending and control. State progressive leaders like Melat Kiros are openly championing policies that treat any government expenditure as justified—a worldview that Complete Colorado argues amounts to ideological cover for unlimited taxation and bureaucratic growth.

The Colorado Sun's coverage of the Democratic primary victory suggests the party's leftward drift is now the dominant narrative heading into November. Republicans counter that voters deserve clarity: "democratic socialism" isn't a moderate compromise between capitalism and communism. It's a blueprint for higher taxes, regulatory overreach, and government micromanagement of the economy—precisely the policies that have already driven up Colorado's cost of living and made housing unaffordable for working families.

Conservatives argue that if Democrats win statewide races, Colorado taxpayers and energy workers will face aggressive climate regulations, wealth redistribution schemes, and the kind of government-first mentality that has failed in progressive strongholds nationwide. The question for November: will Colorado voters reject this agenda, or embrace it?

Watch for how Republican nominee Victor Marx frames the choice between limited government and Democratic socialism in the general election.
