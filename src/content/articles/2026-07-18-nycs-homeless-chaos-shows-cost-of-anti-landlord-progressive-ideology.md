# Progressive Housing Ideology Spirals Into Chaos—Colorado Must Learn the Lesson

New York City's descent into homelessness chaos under Mayor Zohran Mamdani's radical anti-enforcement policies offers a cautionary tale for Colorado cities flirting with similar ideology. Mamdani has effectively banned NYPD encampment sweeps and reframed evictions as "violence," allowing a 12-block homeless encampment to fester near the Intrepid Museum—fueling resident and tourist backlash (Fox News Politics). National Review reports the mayor's "Mamdani Crusade" against landlords has spiraled beyond reason, treating property rights and basic law enforcement as oppressive tools.

**The conservative argument:** When government treats evictions as moral failures rather than legal remedies for non-payment, and when homeless encampments become untouchable no-go zones, cities collapse. Property owners lose incentive to rent; neighborhoods deteriorate; public safety erodes. This isn't compassion—it's governance failure dressed in progressive rhetoric.

**Democrats counter** that criminalization of homelessness and aggressive sweeps worsen the crisis, but Mamdani's results speak louder than intentions: visible decay, safety concerns, and ordinary New Yorkers demanding change.

Colorado's Denver and Boulder have dabbled in similar permissive encampment policies. If progressive ideology transforms homelessness into an untouchable sacred cow while punishing landlords and property owners, Colorado's housing crisis will only deepen—and taxpayers will foot the bill for the cleanup.

Watch whether Colorado cities double down on Mamdani-style policies or course-correct toward enforcement and accountability.
