# Smart Ring Maker Faces Accuracy Lawsuit Over Inflated Claims

Consumer protection law is working as intended: Oura, the maker of the pricey smart rings flooding Colorado's fitness-obsessed market, is being sued for allegedly peddling sleep-tracking accuracy it can't deliver. A proposed class action filed Thursday accuses the company of marketing up to 95% accuracy in sleep-stage tracking while delivering what amounts to guesswork, according to *The Daily Wire*. Madison Surber, a California customer who paid roughly $514 for an Oura Ring 4 in May 2025, is the named plaintiff in the suit filed by Clarkson Law Firm.

The lawsuit highlights a broader problem: tech companies routinely oversell what their devices actually do. Oura's $500 rings have become popular among Colorado's wellness crowd, but if the allegations hold, customers paid premium prices for metrics that may be no better than a coin flip.

This case matters because it tests whether companies can make sweeping accuracy claims without rigorous backing—a question that extends beyond wearables to health tech broadly. Colorado consumers deserve honest marketing, not Silicon Valley hype.

Watch whether Oura settles quickly or fights the claim; either outcome will signal how seriously the company takes its accuracy promises.
