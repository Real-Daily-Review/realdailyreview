# DOJ Silent on Alaska Election Probe as Ranked-Choice Voting Muddles Senate Race

Alaska's ranked-choice voting system is creating chaos in a razor-thin Senate race, and the Justice Department isn't saying a word about ongoing election investigations.

Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan and Democrat Mary Peltola are separated by just 626 votes in first-choice ballots, with roughly 74% of votes counted (Washington Examiner). Sullivan is projecting confidence despite the narrow margin (Breitbart), but the controversial ranked-choice method—which Colorado voters rejected in 2024—means final results remain uncertain as second and third preferences are tabulated.

The DOJ has offered no updates on its Alaska election investigations, raising questions about what federal authorities have found and whether the probe will influence public confidence in the outcome (Washington Examiner). Republicans have long warned that ranked-choice voting creates opportunities for confusion and manipulation; Alaska's current deadlock illustrates exactly why conservatives oppose the system.

For Colorado, this is a cautionary tale. Progressive activists have pushed ranked-choice voting as a "reform," but Alaska's experience shows it delays results, complicates audits, and invites federal scrutiny. Colorado taxpayers should be grateful voters rejected this experiment in 2024—and remain vigilant against future attempts to impose it through ballot measures or legislative action.

Watch whether the DOJ investigation findings become public before Alaska certifies its results.
