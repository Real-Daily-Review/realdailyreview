# Biden Memoir Deal Raises Questions About Post-Presidential Profiteering

Former President Joe Biden is cashing in with a fall memoir titled *Promise Me, America*, scheduled for November release through Little, Brown and Company (Washington Examiner). The book deal represents the latest example of ex-presidents monetizing their time in office—a practice that has become standard among departing chief executives.

While memoir publishing is common, conservatives question the optics of Biden profiting from his presidency just as Americans grapple with inflation, border chaos, and energy costs his administration helped create. The timing—weeks after midterm elections—suggests a calculated effort to rebuild his political brand and influence Democratic messaging heading into 2024.

The book will likely rehash Biden's version of his presidency without the accountability voters deserve. Unlike the court of public opinion, memoir publishers don't cross-examine claims or demand evidence.

For Colorado readers: Biden's energy policies directly harmed Colorado's oil and gas workers and communities dependent on responsible energy development. His memoir will almost certainly avoid honest reckoning with those consequences.
