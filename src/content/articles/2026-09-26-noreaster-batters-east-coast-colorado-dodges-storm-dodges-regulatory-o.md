# Nor'easter Hammers East Coast; Colorado Spared—For Now

A powerful nor'easter is battering the East Coast with destructive winds, flooding, and heavy rain, leaving tens of millions in its path and knocking out power to thousands of customers across the region. According to 9News Denver, the storm is already lashing the area with strong winds and rain as conditions deteriorate further. Just The News reported that approximately 30,000 customers in New Jersey lost power early Saturday morning, with outages likely to spread as the system intensifies.

While Colorado remains outside the immediate impact zone, the storm underscores the importance of reliable energy infrastructure and grid resilience—issues increasingly strained by progressive policies favoring unreliable renewables over dispatchable power sources like natural gas and coal. Republicans argue that over-reliance on wind and solar leaves grids vulnerable during severe weather events, a concern Colorado's own grid operators have flagged as the state transitions away from traditional energy sources.

The nor'easter also highlights why Colorado's water management and emergency preparedness systems matter: eastern states' flooding demonstrates the risks of poor infrastructure planning and inadequate disaster response.

Conservatives should monitor whether the Biden administration uses this storm to push more climate regulations or green-energy mandates that further destabilize the grid—and whether Colorado's Democratic leadership follows suit with costly, unreliable energy mandates.
