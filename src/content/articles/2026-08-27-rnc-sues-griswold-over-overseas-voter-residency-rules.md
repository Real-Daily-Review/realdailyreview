# RNC Challenges Colorado's Overseas Voter Residency Rules Under Griswold

Colorado's Secretary of State Jena Griswold is facing a constitutional lawsuit from the Republican National Committee over her office's handling of overseas voter residency requirements—a direct challenge to voting eligibility standards that could reshape how the state verifies who gets a ballot.

The RNC argues that Griswold's interpretation allowing residency to pass "by proxy" through a parent or spouse violates the Constitution, effectively permitting never-resident overseas voters to cast ballots in Colorado elections (Complete Colorado). Republicans contend the rule creates a loophole that undermines election integrity and voter eligibility verification.

Griswold's office has not publicly detailed its legal defense, but the lawsuit arrives amid broader scrutiny of her tenure. The Denver Post's editorial board noted that her "latest scandal" should prompt voters to examine her opponent's record—specifically Michael Allen's litigation expertise and scandal-free public service record (Denver Post).

The case hinges on a fundamental question: what constitutes genuine Colorado residency for voting purposes? Conservatives argue that proxy residency claims weaken ballot security and dilute the voting power of actual state residents. Democrats typically defend expansive voter access rules as necessary for military families and expatriates.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado courts uphold the RNC's constitutional challenge or side with Griswold's residency interpretation—and how the ruling affects the 2026 Secretary of State race.
