# Denver Schools Seek $44M Tax Hike for Employee Raises

Denver Public Schools is asking taxpayers to approve a $44 million mill levy override in November—a direct tax increase on property owners—with more than half the haul ($25.8 million) earmarked for employee raises rather than classroom improvements.

The mill levy override, if approved, would bypass the state's revenue limits and extract additional property taxes from Denver homeowners and businesses already facing soaring housing costs and inflation. Democrats and school administrators argue teacher compensation requires investment to retain talent; Republicans and fiscal conservatives counter that DPS should prioritize spending efficiency and reallocation before asking taxpayers for more.

Colorado's property tax landscape remains contentious. DPS's approach mirrors a broader progressive strategy: when budgets tighten, seek voter approval for new taxes rather than cut overhead or consolidate administrative bloat. The district hasn't disclosed detailed spending breakdowns or demonstrated why existing revenue cannot fund raises through operational reforms.

For Colorado taxpayers, this signals another election-year push to expand government revenue. School boards across the state are watching Denver's November vote closely—a yes vote could embolden similar levy requests statewide.

**Watch whether DPS releases a full accounting of administrative costs and whether Republican-leaning precincts in Denver's suburbs turn out to oppose the measure.**
