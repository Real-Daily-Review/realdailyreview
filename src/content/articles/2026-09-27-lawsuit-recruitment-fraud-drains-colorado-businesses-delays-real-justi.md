# Civil Justice System Under Siege From Lawsuit Recruitment Fraud

Colorado businesses and taxpayers face mounting costs as the civil justice system buckles under mass-tort recruitment schemes that manufacture claims before lawsuits even begin.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the traditional premise of civil litigation—that every claim represents a real person with a genuine injury who voluntarily sought redress—is collapsing. Instead, litigation entrepreneurs are flooding the system with recruited claimants, many of whom may lack legitimate injuries or even awareness of what they're signing up for.

The problem extends beyond courtroom theatrics. When fraudulent claims clog the dockets, actual victims get delayed justice, defense costs skyrocket, and those expenses get passed to Colorado consumers and employers through higher insurance premiums and product prices. Small businesses and manufacturers face particular pressure: they can't afford the legal firepower of corporate defendants, making them easy targets for settlement demands.

Conservatives argue that lawsuit abuse reform must target the recruitment phase itself—before cases are filed—not just what happens during litigation. Without cracking down on the lawyers and marketing firms manufacturing claims, the civil justice system will continue rewarding fraud over genuine injury.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers push for transparency requirements on litigation funding and claim recruitment practices, or whether trial lawyers' lobby groups block meaningful reform.
