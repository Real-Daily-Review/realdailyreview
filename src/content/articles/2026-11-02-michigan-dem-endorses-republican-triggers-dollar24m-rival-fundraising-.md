# Michigan Democrat's Rival Endorsement Triggers Massive Fundraising Haul—And Party Fracture

A stunning intra-party revolt is exposing deep Democratic divisions. Michigan state Rep. Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over fellow Democrat Abdul El-Sayed in their competitive Senate race, triggering a $2.4 million single-day fundraising surge for El-Sayed's campaign (Fox News Politics). The endorsement signals serious Democratic reservations about El-Sayed's viability as a general-election candidate, according to National Review, which reports that Stevens is far from alone in her skepticism.

El-Sayed's campaign also reported raising $1.2 million following Stevens' defection, though the candidate's response was notably terse—reportedly hoping Stevens "doesn't return" to party circles (Daily Caller).

**The Colorado angle:** While this Michigan race sits outside our state, it underscores a broader pattern: Democrat-on-Democrat fractures over candidate quality and electability are intensifying. Colorado Democrats face similar pressures as they field candidates for statewide offices in 2026.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether this Michigan split signals a broader Democratic civil war over nominee selection—and whether Colorado Republicans can exploit similar vulnerabilities in their own races.
