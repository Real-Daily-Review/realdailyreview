# Supreme Court's Standing Doctrine Could Reshape Colorado Litigation Landscape

The Supreme Court is tightening the screws on who gets to sue whom—and Colorado businesses, property owners, and taxpayers should pay attention.

In three recent cases, the Court carefully examined the constitutional basis for filing lawsuits, signaling a stricter approach to "standing"—the legal doctrine determining whether someone has the right to bring a case (National Review). This matters in Colorado because environmental groups, activist nonprofits, and government agencies frequently use lawsuits to block energy projects, water development, and land-use decisions.

Conservatives argue that loose standing rules have enabled special interests to weaponize the courts, forcing Colorado energy companies and landowners into expensive litigation over speculative harms. A tighter standing doctrine would require plaintiffs to prove concrete, personal injury—not just ideological opposition to a project.

Meanwhile, the Court's composition remains in flux. Washington Examiner political correspondent Byron York noted that justices considering retirement should not assume Republicans will indefinitely control confirmation votes, underscoring the stakes of the 2024 and 2026 election cycles for Colorado conservatives.
