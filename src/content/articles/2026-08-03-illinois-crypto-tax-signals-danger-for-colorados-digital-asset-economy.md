# Illinois Crypto Tax Shows Why States Can't Be Trusted With New Revenue Sources

Illinois just proved why conservative taxpayers should fear any state claiming it needs "just one more tax" to balance the books. Gov. JB Pritzker (D-IL) signed a $55.9 billion budget this summer that includes the nation's first state tax specifically targeting digital asset transactions—a move that directly contradicts Congress's bipartisan push toward crypto regulatory clarity through the CLARITY Act (Washington Examiner).

The Illinois scheme is textbook progressive fiscal desperation: facing budget pressure, Democrats reached for an emerging industry rather than cutting spending. Conservatives argue this sets a dangerous precedent. Once states discover a new revenue stream, they rarely stop—and Colorado should take note. If Illinois succeeds, expect pressure on Colorado lawmakers to follow suit, potentially threatening the state's growing crypto and blockchain sector based in Denver and Boulder.

Republicans counter that targeted industry taxes kill innovation and drive businesses to friendlier jurisdictions. The crypto community already faces federal regulatory uncertainty; state-level tax raids only accelerate the exodus to Texas, Wyoming, and other low-tax havens.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats propose similar digital asset taxes in the 2025 legislative session—and whether business groups push back hard enough to stop it.**
