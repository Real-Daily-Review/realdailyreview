# Colorado's Rideshare Safety Law Takes Effect—But Federal Overreach Looms

Colorado's new rideshare driver safety standards are now in force, requiring companies to review and potentially deactivate drivers within seven business days of serious felony allegations or certain complaints (9News Denver). But the state's consumer protections face a threat from Washington: a federal bill could strip Colorado of its authority to enforce these rules, preempting state-level safeguards that Coloradans voted to strengthen.

This is classic federal overreach. Republicans and conservatives argue that states—not distant bureaucrats in D.C.—should set transportation safety standards reflecting their communities' values. Colorado's law represents local accountability; a federal mandate would centralize control and weaken protections tailored to Colorado riders and drivers.

The timing matters. As rideshare companies lobby Congress to gut state regulations nationwide, Colorado taxpayers and riders lose leverage. Weakening Colorado's standards would benefit corporate bottom lines, not safety.

Meanwhile, a federal judge rejected the Trump administration's attempt to force Colorado election officials to hand over unredacted voter rolls (Denver Post), a separate reminder that federal courts are willing to push back on executive overreach—in both directions.

Watch whether Congress moves to preempt Colorado's rideshare law and how state Republicans respond to defend Colorado's regulatory independence.
