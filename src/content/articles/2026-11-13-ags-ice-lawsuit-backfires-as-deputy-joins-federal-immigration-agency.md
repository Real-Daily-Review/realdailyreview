# Former Mesa County Deputy Lands at ICE After AG's Immigration Crackdown

Colorado's immigration enforcement saga just got messier. Former Mesa County Sheriff's Deputy Alexander Zwinck, who faced disciplinary action and a lawsuit from Attorney General Phil Weiser over ICE collaboration, has landed a job with Immigration and Customs Enforcement itself—the very agency that triggered the legal fight.

Weiser, a Democrat, sued Zwinck last year after the deputy worked with federal immigration authorities, a move the AG's office characterized as overreach. Zwinck resigned from the Mesa County Sheriff's Office rather than face the discipline. Now he's employed by ICE, the federal agency Colorado's top law enforcement officer has repeatedly criticized for aggressive tactics.

**The conservative take:** Republicans and immigration hawks argue Weiser's lawsuit exemplifies progressive obstruction of immigration enforcement. They contend federal-local cooperation on immigration is lawful and necessary—and that punishing officers for working with ICE undermines border security. Weiser's office counters that local law enforcement shouldn't be conscripted into federal immigration operations without proper oversight.

The irony is sharp: Weiser's legal pressure drove a capable immigration-focused officer straight into the federal system, potentially strengthening the very enforcement apparatus the AG opposes.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado's next administration reverses Weiser's anti-ICE stance and restores local-federal immigration cooperation.
