# Supreme Court Sides with Government Over Property Rights in Tax Foreclosure Case

The U.S. Supreme Court has ruled that state and local governments need not pay fair market value when seizing property through tax foreclosure—a decision that could embolden Colorado counties and municipalities to aggressively pursue tax-delinquent properties without compensating owners fairly.

Justice Alito's majority opinion, joined by Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett, and Jackson (Just The News), upheld the government's broad authority to foreclose and sell properties at steep discounts when owners fall behind on taxes.

**The conservative concern:** Property rights advocates argue this ruling expands government power at the expense of individual ownership. When Colorado counties foreclose on land—whether due to unpaid property taxes or other liens—owners now have even less legal recourse to recover fair compensation. This is particularly troubling for rural landowners and small business operators who may face sudden loss of assets through tax sales where the government pockets the difference between the sale price and true market value.

**The ruling's reach:** Colorado taxpayers and property owners should understand that their state and local governments now have clearer legal cover to pursue aggressive tax collection tactics. Counties can foreclose faster and cheaper, with minimal obligation to ensure owners receive equitable treatment.

Watch for Colorado property-rights groups to challenge whether state law should impose stricter fairness standards than the Constitution requires.
