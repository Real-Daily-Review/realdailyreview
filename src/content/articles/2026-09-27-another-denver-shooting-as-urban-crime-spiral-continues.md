# Denver Shooting Underscores Crime Crisis While National Incidents Pile Up

Another shooting in Denver's Five Points neighborhood Friday morning sent two people to the hospital, adding to Colorado's growing violent-crime problem even as similar incidents plague major cities nationwide. The incident follows a deadly mass shooting at a Detroit strip club early Sunday that left three dead and four wounded—a grim reminder that urban lawlessness isn't confined to the Front Range.

Republicans and conservatives argue that progressive district attorneys and soft-on-crime policies in Democratic-run cities have emboldened criminals and destabilized once-stable neighborhoods. Denver's Five Points area, long plagued by gang activity and drug trafficking, has seen repeated violence despite years of city spending on "community programs" and social services.

Colorado taxpayers should demand accountability: Why are repeat offenders back on the streets? Why has Denver's homicide rate remained stubbornly high despite record police budgets? Democrats blame "root causes" and call for more government intervention; conservatives counter that swift prosecution, certain punishment, and respect for law enforcement—not endless social spending—deter crime.

The pattern is clear across blue-run cities: permissive bail policies, reduced prosecutions, and anti-police rhetoric create a vacuum criminals exploit. Colorado voters should watch whether Denver's new district attorney will reverse the soft-touch approach that has made the city less safe.
