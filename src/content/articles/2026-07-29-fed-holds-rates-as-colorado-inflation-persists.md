# Federal Reserve Stuck in Neutral While Inflation Haunts Colorado Wallets

The Federal Reserve is expected to hold interest rates steady this week, leaving Colorado families and businesses trapped in an inflationary squeeze that shows no sign of easing.

Policymakers at the nation's central bank are reportedly "losing patience" with inflation that has remained stubbornly above their 2% target for over five years, according to 9News Denver. Yet despite that frustration, the Fed appears unlikely to raise rates when it concludes its two-day meeting Wednesday.

The Washington Examiner's analysis notes that while inflation persists above target, some economists blame "transitory factors" like elevated energy prices tied to geopolitical events rather than runaway consumer demand. That framing, however, offers little comfort to Colorado households watching grocery bills, gas prices, and housing costs remain elevated.

For Colorado's energy sector—already battered by regulatory headwinds—continued rate uncertainty complicates investment decisions and hiring. Conservatives argue the Fed's prolonged inaction, combined with years of federal overspending, created the inflation problem in the first place. Republicans contend that meaningful rate increases are necessary to restore price stability and protect savers and workers from currency erosion.

**Watch for:** whether the Fed finally acknowledges its own role in inflation and signals meaningful rate action at its next meeting.
