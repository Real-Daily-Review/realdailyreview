# Trump's AG Pick Blanche Clears Early Hurdles as Law Enforcement Backs Confirmation

Todd Blanche, Trump's nominee for attorney general, is moving smoothly through Senate confirmation with backing from law enforcement and key Republicans—a sign the administration's top law enforcement pick may avoid the partisan warfare that has plagued other Trump nominees.

The Western States Sheriffs' Association has endorsed Blanche, urging swift Senate confirmation (Fox News). Meanwhile, Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.), a Judiciary Committee member who has occasionally resisted Trump priorities, signaled he's "generally satisfied" with Blanche's qualifications and plans to meet with the nominee next week (Just The News).

Blanche, who has served as acting attorney general since Pam Bondi's departure in April, previously worked as Bondi's deputy. He's already begun confirmation meetings, including with Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-Iowa), signaling momentum toward a floor vote.

For Colorado conservatives, Blanche's confirmation matters: as AG, he'll shape federal enforcement priorities affecting everything from election integrity to Second Amendment cases to energy regulation. Law enforcement endorsement carries weight in confirmation hearings and suggests the nominee won't face the scorched-earth opposition some Trump cabinet picks have encountered.
