# Trump, Vance Split on Iran War as GOP Wrestles With Interventionism

A crack has opened in the Trump-Vance ticket over military intervention in Iran, exposing a fault line within Republican foreign policy as midterm losses mount. Vice President JD Vance has publicly blamed voter anger over the Iran conflict and oil prices for GOP electoral setbacks, while President Trump doubled down, saying he would launch the war again (Fox News). Vance, however, declined to endorse the conflict, telling reporters Friday that "the president is the one who makes the decision" (Washington Examiner)—a studied dodge that signals daylight between the two.

**The conservative divide:** Trump's hawkish stance reflects traditional Republican interventionism, while Vance represents a growing non-interventionist wing skeptical of costly foreign entanglements. Republicans argue that voter frustration over military overreach and energy costs—both tied to geopolitical instability—cost the party seats. Conservatives worry that endless wars drain resources needed at home and fuel inflation that hammers working families.

**Colorado angle:** Higher energy prices from Middle East tensions directly hit Colorado families and small businesses. Oil and gas workers in Western Colorado have particular interest in whether the GOP settles on a coherent energy and foreign policy message.

Watch whether Trump and Vance reconcile publicly or whether this fissure widens ahead of 2026.
