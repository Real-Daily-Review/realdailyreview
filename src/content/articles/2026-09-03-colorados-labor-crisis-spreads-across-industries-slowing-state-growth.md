# Colorado Worker Shortage Deepens as State Struggles to Fill Jobs Across Industries

Colorado's labor crunch is getting worse, and it's dragging down the state's economy just as employers desperately need workers.

The Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City reports that worker shortages are no longer confined to a few sectors—they're spreading across different jobs and industries throughout Colorado and neighboring states, according to CPR News. This isn't a temporary blip; it's a structural problem that economists say is actively holding back regional growth.

The causes are familiar to conservatives: years of generous unemployment benefits, remote-work migration patterns that have shifted labor dynamics, and regulatory barriers that make hiring and business expansion costly. When government makes it easier to stay home than work, and when compliance costs eat into payroll budgets, employers suffer and economic growth stalls.

Colorado's business community has been sounding the alarm for months. The state's energy sector, agriculture, construction, and hospitality industries all report unfilled positions. This matters for Colorado taxpayers: fewer workers means slower tax revenue growth, higher labor costs passed to consumers, and reduced competitiveness against other states.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers will address the real culprits—excessive regulation and welfare policies that discourage work—or simply throw more government "solutions" at the problem.
