# Libertarian Swap in SoS Race Raises Questions About Ballot Mechanics

Colorado's Libertarian Party will field a replacement secretary of state candidate after a judge allowed the late substitution, despite what the *Colorado Sun* calls a "suspicious" dropout by original nominee Alex Astley. A Denver judge ruled the swap won't delay ballot finalization for November.

Astley denied timing his withdrawal to block the party from replacing him or to benefit Republican James Wiley, according to the *Denver Post*. The move raises questions about ballot access rules and party control—issues that matter to Colorado voters watching how easily candidates can be swapped this close to Election Day.

Republicans argue such late changes create confusion and undermine the integrity of ballot procedures, while Libertarians contend they have the right to field their strongest candidate. Democrats have largely stayed quiet on the matter, though election officials must now process the substitution quickly.

The ruling sets a precedent for how Colorado courts will handle last-minute party nominee changes in the final stretch before November. Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether this decision opens the door to similar swaps in other races or whether it becomes a one-off exception.
