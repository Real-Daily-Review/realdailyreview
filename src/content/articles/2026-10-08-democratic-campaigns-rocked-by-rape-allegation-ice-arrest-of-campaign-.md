# Democratic Campaign Trail Plagued by Serious Incidents as Election Nears

Two Democratic congressional campaigns are facing serious criminal allegations just weeks before Election Day, raising questions about vetting and operational security on the left's front lines.

In Arizona, Bernadette Greene-Placentia, the Democratic nominee challenging Republican Rep. Abraham Hamadeh in the 8th Congressional District, reported to authorities that she was raped in August while repairing campaign signs in a parking lot around 2 a.m. (Washington Examiner, Daily Wire). The Maricopa County Sheriff's Office is investigating.

Meanwhile, in Pennsylvania's competitive 10th District, ICE arrested Chimaobim Adedayo Igbojekwe, a 35-year-old Nigerian national, who allegedly worked as a canvasser for Democrat Janelle Stelson's campaign (Washington Examiner, Just The News). Igbojekwe pleaded guilty to disorderly conduct after allegedly exposing himself in public. Stelson's campaign denied the association, though sources indicate he was working as a canvasser at the time of his arrest.

Neither incident directly affects Colorado, but they underscore a broader pattern: Democratic campaigns operating with minimal vetting and questionable judgment about who they hire and how they deploy volunteers. Republicans argue these incidents highlight the importance of basic security protocols and background checks—standards that should be non-negotiable for any serious campaign operation.

**Watch for:** Whether national Democratic leadership addresses campaign security standards before November.
