# Trump DOJ Reviews Nixon-Era Prosecutorial Conduct, Raising Questions About Federal Weaponization

The Trump Justice Department is reopening scrutiny of how federal prosecutors handled the Watergate investigation, signaling concern that the special prosecutor's office may have overreached against a sitting president—a move that underscores conservative arguments about weaponized federal law enforcement.

According to *The Daily Wire*, the DOJ review focuses on the conduct of the special prosecutor's office that investigated Nixon, as part of a broader effort to root out what Republicans characterize as selective prosecution of political opponents. The *Wall Street Journal* first reported the review's scope.

Conservatives argue the reexamination is warranted. *National Review* acknowledges Nixon was "flawed but accomplished," yet cautions against making him a role model—suggesting the real issue isn't Nixon's conduct, but whether the prosecutorial machinery itself operated fairly. Republicans contend that examining historical cases of alleged federal overreach strengthens guardrails against future politicization of justice.

Democrats and the left have not yet responded substantively to the review, though they're likely to argue that reopening Watergate-era decisions risks undermining institutional accountability and sets a dangerous precedent for politicizing the Justice Department itself.

**What to watch:** Whether this DOJ review produces specific findings about prosecutorial misconduct and whether it influences how federal law enforcement approaches investigations of political figures going forward.
