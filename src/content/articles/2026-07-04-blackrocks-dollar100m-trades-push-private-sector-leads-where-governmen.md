# BlackRock's $100M Workforce Bet Highlights Skilled Trades Gap—But Market, Not Government, Should Lead

BlackRock is committing $100 million to its Future Builders initiative, targeting workforce development in skilled trades as demand for electricians, welders, and other tradespeople surges (New York Post). The financial giant's move underscores a real problem: America faces a persistent shortage of workers in critical trades.

Conservatives should welcome private-sector solutions to workforce gaps—this is how markets work when government stays out of the way. BlackRock's investment reflects genuine business need, not taxpayer-funded retraining schemes that often waste money and produce mixed results.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. The state's energy sector, construction boom, and manufacturing base all depend on skilled workers. Rather than expanding government job-training bureaucracies, Colorado should remove barriers to apprenticeships and vocational education—cutting red tape, not raising taxes to fund another state program.

Meanwhile, a separate U.S.-Canada trade dispute over mushroom production (Washington Examiner) signals broader agricultural vulnerabilities. Trade conflicts can disrupt supply chains and hurt American farmers and producers.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for fair trade enforcement without resorting to protectionist overreach that harms consumers and businesses.
