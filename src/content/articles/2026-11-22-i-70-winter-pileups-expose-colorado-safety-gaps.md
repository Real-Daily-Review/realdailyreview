# I-70 Winter Pileups Expose Colorado's Chronic Road Safety Crisis

Colorado's I-70 corridor turned into a parking lot of twisted metal Friday and Saturday as multiple pileups—including a 12-car wreck—snarled traffic and raised hard questions about whether the state is doing enough to keep drivers safe on one of America's most treacherous mountain passes.

According to 9News Denver, a series of multi-car collisions erupted over the two-day stretch, with Colorado State Patrol documenting numerous spin-outs and slide-outs amid snowy conditions. The incidents prompted renewed safety warnings from authorities, but conservatives argue the real issue isn't just weather—it's infrastructure and driver accountability.

Republicans and transportation experts have long contended that Colorado needs smarter enforcement of speed limits and following distances during winter weather, not endless road-widening projects that drain the state budget. The state should prioritize traction laws and real-time driver education over expensive expansions that take years to complete and often fail to solve congestion.

Meanwhile, a separate concern emerged: Fox News reported that a breach of the National Crime Information Center (NCIC) database could compromise law enforcement safety during traffic stops if criminal records were altered. A retired FBI agent warned the hack poses serious risks to officers working I-70 stops.
