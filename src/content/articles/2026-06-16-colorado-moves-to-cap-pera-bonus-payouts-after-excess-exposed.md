# Colorado Lawmakers Target PERA Bonus Payouts After Investigation Exposes Excess

Colorado's public employee pension fund has been quietly enriching its investment staff with millions in performance bonuses—and now state lawmakers want to rein it in.

Following a Colorado Sun investigation, legislators are moving to cap bonuses at the Public Employees' Retirement Association (PERA), which has distributed substantial payouts to its investment team in recent years. The proposal reflects growing frustration over how Colorado taxpayers' retirement contributions are being spent.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that PERA's bonus structure is indefensible when the fund faces long-term solvency challenges and Colorado taxpayers already shoulder significant contribution increases. They contend that public pension administrators should operate under stricter compensation limits than private-sector firms—especially when beneficiaries are state employees and teachers whose pensions are guaranteed by taxpayers.

Democrats have not mounted a vigorous defense of the current bonus system, suggesting bipartisan concern about the optics and sustainability of the practice (CPR News, Colorado Sun).

The timing matters: PERA's unfunded liabilities have pressured state budgets for years, and any reduction in administrative excess could redirect dollars toward actual pension obligations rather than staff windfalls.

**Watch whether the legislature passes meaningful limits—or whether PERA's leadership successfully lobbies to water down the proposal.**
