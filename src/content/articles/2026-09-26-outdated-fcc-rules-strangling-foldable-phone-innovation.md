# Outdated Radiation Rules Hobble Innovation in Folding Phones

Federal regulators are using 1996 safety standards to test cutting-edge 2024 smartphones, potentially strangling innovation in the booming foldable device market, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The problem: the FCC's radiation safety guidelines were written when flip phones were science fiction. Today's dual-screen and flexible architectures—Apple's iPhone Duo, Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold, Google's Pixel Fold, and Motorola's Razr+—operate under testing protocols that don't account for how modern devices actually work.

Conservatives argue this is textbook regulatory sclerosis. Outdated federal rules written nearly three decades ago are now a drag on American tech companies competing globally. While safety matters, regulators should update standards to match current science and engineering reality—not force manufacturers to squeeze innovation into Cold War-era boxes.

The foldable phone market represents real consumer demand and American manufacturing leadership. Needless compliance costs don't protect anyone; they just hand market share to foreign competitors operating under more flexible regulatory regimes.

Colorado tech workers and consumers benefit when American companies innovate freely. Watch whether the FCC modernizes its radiation standards or continues strangling the foldable phone revolution with bureaucratic inertia.
