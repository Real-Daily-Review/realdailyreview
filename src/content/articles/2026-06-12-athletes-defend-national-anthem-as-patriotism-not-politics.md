# Athletes, Musicians Stand Firm on National Anthem Patriotism

Colorado conservatives have a fresh reminder that respect for the flag and anthem still matters to many American athletes and entertainers—and they're willing to say so publicly.

Former MLB star Jim Edmonds told the Daily Caller he would have maintained "zero tolerance" for player protests during the national anthem, underscoring a divide between old-school athletes and modern activism. Meanwhile, country singer Zac Brown is defending his decision to perform the National Anthem at an upcoming White House UFC event, framing it squarely as "patriotism, not politics" (Breitbart). Brown refused to apologize despite apparent pressure to do so.

The pushback extends to soccer: U.S. national team legend Clint Dempsey criticized USMNT coach Jesse Marsch for questioning players' patriotism, telling him to "stay in your own lane" (New York Post).

These aren't isolated moments. They reflect a broader conservative argument that standing for the anthem and flag is a baseline expectation—not a political statement. Republicans and traditionalists have long contended that using the national anthem as a protest platform disrespects the military, first responders, and the country itself.

For Colorado taxpayers and patriots, the message is clear: there's still a market for unapologetic patriotism in sports and entertainment.

Watch whether corporate sponsors and team owners continue caving to activist pressure or start backing athletes who defend the anthem.
