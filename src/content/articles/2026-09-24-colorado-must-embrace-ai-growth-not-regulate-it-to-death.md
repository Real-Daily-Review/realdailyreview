# Colorado Should Embrace AI Growth, Not Strangle It With Regulation

Colorado has a chance to lead on artificial intelligence instead of joining the progressive panic brigade—but only if lawmakers resist the urge to over-regulate the industry before it takes root.

A Magellan Strategies survey shows Coloradans are open to AI development, and for good reason. The technology promises real economic upside: job creation, innovation, and competitive advantage in a sector that doesn't require Colorado to sacrifice its natural resources or energy independence. Unlike the green-energy mandates that have already driven up electricity costs for families and businesses statewide, AI data centers can operate efficiently and attract high-wage employment.

The Colorado Sun has pitched AI-focused apprenticeships as a workforce solution, which conservatives can support—market-driven training beats government job-retraining programs every time. But the real risk isn't too little training; it's too much government interference.

Democrats and progressive activists will inevitably demand AI "guardrails," licensing schemes, and data-privacy regulations that sound reasonable until they strangle startups and hand market dominance to Big Tech incumbents who can afford compliance costs. California's regulatory overreach has already driven tech talent and investment elsewhere.

Colorado's competitive edge depends on staying business-friendly: clear property rights for data centers, reasonable utility rates, and minimal red tape. Watch whether the legislature resists calls for premature AI regulation in the 2027 session.
