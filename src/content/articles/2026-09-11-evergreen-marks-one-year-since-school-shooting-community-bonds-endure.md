# Evergreen Marks Year Since School Shooting: Community Reflects, Healing Continues

One year after the Evergreen High School shooting, the mountain community is processing grief while demonstrating the resilience that defines Colorado towns facing tragedy.

Students and neighbors gathered this week to remember the attack that devastated the small Jefferson County community. According to KDVR Fox31, many students chose to spend Sept. 10 with classmates who shared their trauma, leaning on one another rather than attending regular classes. Seventeen-year-old Ava Guyton told reporters there's "a certain quietness in the air this week"—a reflection of how deeply the shooting marked the community's collective memory.

What stands out is not political grandstanding but genuine community solidarity. Neighbors came together in the aftermath, and that bond persists a year later (KDVR Fox31). The focus remains on healing and supporting one another—the kind of organic, local response that works far better than top-down government mandates.

For Colorado conservatives, this moment underscores an important truth: when tragedy strikes, communities heal through connection and mutual support, not through rushed policy reactions or restrictions on law-abiding citizens' rights. The real answer to school safety lies in hardened facilities, trained personnel, and vigilant community members—not in expanding government authority or scapegoating constitutional rights.
