# Massive Aspen Acres Fire Ravages Southern Colorado as Containment Lags

Colorado's Aspen Acres Fire has torched nearly 94,000 acres across Pueblo County with only 15% containment, destroying or damaging more than 260 structures and leaving families—including firefighters—homeless (CPR News). The blaze has tested the resolve of volunteer fire departments who continued battling the inferno even after learning their own homes had burned, with Red Creek's fire chief and his firefighter wife losing their residence while he stayed to protect neighbors (9News).

Forecasters offered a glimmer of hope with scattered showers possible Wednesday, but hot, dry, and windy conditions are expected to return by the weekend (Colorado Sun), threatening to reignite spread across the drought-stressed landscape.

The fire underscores a hard reality for rural Colorado: volunteer fire services—the backbone of wildfire response in much of the state—operate on shoestring budgets while facing increasingly severe fire seasons. Conservatives and taxpayers should demand that state and federal land management agencies prioritize forest health and fuel reduction over environmental litigation that delays critical thinning and prescribed burns.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic leadership will use this disaster to push for new regulations and spending rather than addressing decades of federal forest mismanagement.
