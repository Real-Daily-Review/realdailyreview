# Colorado Public Health Order Forces ICE Detention Center Transparency on TB Outbreak

Colorado health officials have finally muscled through a public health order against the GEO Group's Aurora ICE detention facility after weeks of stonewalling over a tuberculosis outbreak—a stark reminder that even private contractors holding federal detainees must answer to state authorities.

According to CPR News, the standoff centered on GEO Group and ICE refusing to grant public health inspectors access to the facility or provide critical information about the TB cases. Colorado's health department had no choice but to issue a formal order to compel compliance.

Conservatives and Republicans have long criticized the federal government's handling of border security and detention operations, but this case highlights a different problem: when private contractors like GEO Group prioritize operational secrecy over public health transparency, state governments must step in. Colorado taxpayers and residents deserve assurance that communicable diseases in detention facilities aren't spreading into surrounding communities.

The order forces the facility to cooperate with contact tracing, testing, and isolation protocols—standard public health measures that should never require a legal fight.
