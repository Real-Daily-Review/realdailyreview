# Trump's Iran Deal Splits Conservative Movement on Foreign Policy

President Trump's framework agreement with Iran has exposed a fault line among conservatives: pragmatists backing the deal as a war-reduction breakthrough versus skeptics warning of unintended consequences in the Middle East.

According to the Reagan Institute Summer Survey cited by Fox News, Americans are divided—39% favor negotiated settlement while 36% back regime change. Trump's team frames the accord as historic diplomacy that lowers war risk and opens U.S.-Tehran channels. Supporters argue it represents Trump operating as a pragmatist willing to negotiate with adversaries.

Yet skeptics urge caution. The Washington Examiner's analysis warns of "unthinkable next steps" if the agreement fails to hold, while The Daily Wire notes negotiations remain preliminary—"in many ways, they have not really begun." Breitbart reports Iran's Supreme Leader backed the deal only after receiving assurances it protects Tehran's interests, raising questions about whether America secured genuine concessions.

**The Colorado angle:** This deal carries implications for energy markets and defense contractors with Colorado operations, plus broader questions about American strength projection that resonate with the state's military communities.

Conservatives should watch whether Iran honors compliance benchmarks and whether the agreement survives the next administration's scrutiny.
