# RNC Challenges Colorado's Overseas Voter Residency Rules Under Griswold

Colorado's Secretary of State Jena Griswold is facing a federal lawsuit from the Republican National Committee over her administration's handling of voter residency requirements for overseas ballots. The RNC argues that Griswold's office has unconstitutionally allowed voters who have never lived in Colorado to establish residency through proxy claims via parents or spouses—a practice the lawsuit contends violates election integrity standards (Complete Colorado).

The challenge strikes at the heart of ballot access rules. Republicans argue that residency verification exists to prevent fraud and ensure only legitimate Colorado voters cast ballots in state elections. Griswold's interpretation, they contend, guts that safeguard by allowing people with no genuine Colorado ties to vote here.

This isn't Griswold's first controversy. The Denver Post's editorial board recently noted her office has been embroiled in an open-records scandal, with columnist Krista Kafer arguing that Republican challenger Michael Allen's litigation experience and clean record present voters a stark contrast to Griswold's pattern of missteps.

The lawsuit underscores a broader conservative concern: progressive election officials using regulatory discretion to expand ballot access in ways that blur voter eligibility lines. Colorado taxpayers and election integrity advocates should watch whether federal courts side with the RNC's residency challenge—and whether it signals broader scrutiny of Griswold's election administration heading into 2026.
