# Senate Delays Blanche Confirmation; Florida AG Launches Fauci Investigation

The Senate Judiciary Committee postponed a critical vote Wednesday on Todd Blanche's confirmation as permanent Attorney General—a setback for the Trump administration's law enforcement agenda that reveals Republican concerns about executive overreach.

According to the Washington Examiner and Just The News, GOP senators worry that Blanche's Justice Department will resurrect a $1.776 billion "anti-weaponization fund" designed to investigate perceived political targeting. The agreement authorizing that fund remains in writing, senators say, raising questions about departmental independence and fiscal discipline.

Meanwhile, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier announced an investigation into Dr. Anthony Fauci following his congressional testimony Wednesday, where Fauci invoked the Fifth Amendment 111 times when questioned about COVID-19 pandemic claims. "Fauci's lack of candor to Congress is unbelievable," Uthmeier posted on X, signaling state-level accountability efforts as federal oversight stalls.

For Colorado conservatives, the Blanche delay matters: a weakened Justice Department leadership could affect enforcement of election integrity, border security, and Second Amendment protections. The Fauci investigation underscores growing Republican demands for transparency on pandemic policies that devastated Colorado's small businesses and schools.

Watch whether Senate Republicans coalesce around Blanche's confirmation or demand written assurances that the anti-weaponization fund stays dormant.
