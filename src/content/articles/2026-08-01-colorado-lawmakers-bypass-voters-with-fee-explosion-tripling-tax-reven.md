# Colorado Lawmakers Quietly Rake In Billions Through Fee Explosion While Voters Block Tax Hikes

Colorado voters have repeatedly rejected income tax increases—but state lawmakers have found an end-run around the ballot box: fees that now generate revenue exceeding three times the current income tax rate, according to *Complete Colorado*.

The fee explosion represents classic government sleight of hand. Rather than ask voters directly for more money through tax hikes, legislators have stacked on licensing fees, regulatory charges, and other levies that don't require voter approval. Conservatives argue this circumvents the democratic process and masks the true cost of government expansion. Taxpayers foot the bill either way—but without the transparency of a tax vote.

The strategy reveals a fundamental problem: when voters say no to taxes, bureaucrats find creative workarounds. Colorado's fee structure now dwarfs income tax revenue, yet most citizens remain unaware of the cumulative burden. Republicans and fiscal conservatives contend this is exactly why Colorado needs stronger constitutional limits on government revenue-raising, regardless of the mechanism.

Meanwhile, a separate *Denver Post* poll shows Colorado voters across party lines worry about AI regulation—and don't trust tech companies to police themselves. That's one bipartisan concern worth watching.

**What's next:** Watch whether Colorado Republicans push for fee-reform legislation requiring voter approval on major new charges, or whether the fee machine keeps humming unchecked.
