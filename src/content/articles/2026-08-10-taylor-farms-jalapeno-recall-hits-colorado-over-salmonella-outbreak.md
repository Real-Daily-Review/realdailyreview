# Taylor Farms Jalapeno Recall Hits Colorado Grocery Shelves Amid Salmonella Outbreak

A massive produce recall is pulling jalapeno products from Target, Walmart, and Whole Foods nationwide—including Colorado stores—after the FDA linked the peppers to a multistate salmonella outbreak sickening 345 people across 27 states, according to the Washington Examiner.

Taylor Farms, the California-based producer already under scrutiny for a cyclospora outbreak, initiated the recall after federal health officials connected the contamination to their jalapenos. Major restaurant chains including Chipotle and QDOBA have yanked the peppers from menus (Washington Examiner).

The outbreak underscores a persistent vulnerability in America's centralized produce supply chain. When one large processor fails food-safety protocols, millions of consumers across multiple states face immediate health risk. Colorado families shopping at major grocers should check their recent jalapeno purchases against the recall list and dispose of affected products.

Conservatives have long argued that heavy-handed federal regulation often fails to prevent exactly these kinds of contamination events—suggesting that market accountability and transparent traceability matter more than bureaucratic oversight. The question for Colorado taxpayers: why does the FDA's pre-market inspection regime repeatedly miss problems that only surface after Americans get sick?

Watch whether Colorado health officials demand stronger state-level produce-safety audits or whether they defer entirely to federal agencies that missed this outbreak.
