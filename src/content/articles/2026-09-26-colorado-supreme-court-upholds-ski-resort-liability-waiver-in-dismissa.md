# Colorado Supreme Court Sides with Ski Resort Over Injured Snowboarder in Waiver Case

Colorado's highest court just handed a win to Vail Resorts—and a cautionary lesson to anyone planning to sue over a ski accident. The state Supreme Court dismissed a snowboarder's case against Breckenridge Ski Resort because he signed an Epic Pass waiver *after* his snowmobile crash while the lawsuit was still pending (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

The ruling is narrow but significant: courts will enforce liability waivers even when signed during ongoing litigation. For Colorado ski resorts—a major economic engine across the state—this strengthens their legal shield against injury claims. Conservatives argue that liability waivers represent a fair allocation of risk: individuals who choose to engage in inherently dangerous activities should bear responsibility for their own safety rather than shifting costs to businesses through litigation.

The decision reflects a broader principle: freedom of contract. If you want to use a resort's slopes, you accept the terms. No government mandate forces you to sign; you're free to ski elsewhere or not at all.

This matters for Colorado's $28 billion tourism economy and the thousands of jobs tied to ski resorts. Excessive liability exposure drives up insurance costs and operational expenses—burdens ultimately passed to workers and visitors.
