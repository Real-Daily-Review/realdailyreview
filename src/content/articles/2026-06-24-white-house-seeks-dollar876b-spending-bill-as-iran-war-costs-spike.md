# White House Seeks $87.6B Spending Spree as Congress Balks at Iran War Costs

The White House is asking Congress to rubber-stamp an $87.6 billion supplemental spending bill—roughly $67 billion earmarked for Pentagon operations tied to Iran military action—even as a bipartisan majority of lawmakers has signaled opposition to further Middle East entanglement (Washington Examiner).

The Office of Management and Budget submitted the request Wednesday, bundling defense spending with farm assistance and an Ebola response package (9News Denver). Republicans and Democrats alike have expressed skepticism about expanding military commitments in the region without clear congressional authorization or exit strategy.

Conservatives argue the administration should justify every dollar of new spending, particularly when the national debt remains historically elevated. Taxpayers deserve transparency on what this money actually funds and whether it advances genuine American security interests or represents another open-ended foreign commitment. The farm assistance component, while politically popular in agricultural states like Colorado, shouldn't be used as cover for bloated Pentagon requests.

Congress should demand a detailed accounting before approving any supplemental—and reject the Washington habit of bundling unrelated spending to bypass scrutiny.

Watch whether House Republicans hold firm on demanding itemized justifications and sunset provisions before voting yes.
