# Polis Suspends Environmental Rules to Lower Gas Prices—But Conservatives Question the Real Fix

Governor Jared Polis issued an executive order this week temporarily relaxing environmental and transportation regulations to combat soaring fuel costs, citing geopolitical tensions as justification (KDVR). Colorado drivers are paying an average of $4.18 per gallon for gas and $6.21 for diesel (9News), squeezing household budgets across the state.

The move is a tacit admission that Colorado's strict environmental mandates drive up energy costs—a point conservatives have made for years. Republicans argue that permanently rolling back these regulations, rather than issuing temporary band-aids, would provide lasting relief and boost Colorado's economy. They contend that excessive environmental restrictions on fuel production and refining capacity are a root cause of price spikes, not a solution to them.

Democrats counter that the suspensions are emergency measures only, and that long-term energy policy must still prioritize climate goals. Polis's order sidesteps the larger question: whether Colorado's regulatory burden on energy development is sustainable for working families and businesses dependent on affordable fuel.

For Colorado taxpayers and small business owners already hammered by inflation, this executive action offers temporary relief—but it exposes the tension between progressive environmental policy and economic reality.

**Watch whether Polis makes these suspensions permanent or reverts to strict regulations once prices stabilize.**
