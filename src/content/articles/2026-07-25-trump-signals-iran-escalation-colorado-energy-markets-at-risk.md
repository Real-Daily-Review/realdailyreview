# Trump Weighs Iran Escalation as Ceasefire Collapses; Colorado Should Brace for Oil Volatility

President Trump is signaling readiness to expand military strikes against Iran following the collapse of a ceasefire just two weeks old, according to the Washington Examiner and Just The News. U.S. Central Command has announced daily bombing campaigns targeting Iranian capabilities in regional waterways, while Trump and Secretary of State Rubio publicly discuss broadening the operation.

**The conservative case:** Trump allies argue escalation targets hardliners obstructing genuine peace negotiations. Just The News reports both Trump and Rubio have cited difficulties engaging with current Iranian regime figures, suggesting military pressure could shift the negotiating landscape.

**What Colorado needs to watch:** Energy markets. Iran tensions historically spike oil prices—bad news for Colorado consumers already squeezed by inflation and high gas costs. Colorado's oil and gas sector, though smaller than neighboring states, remains economically significant; sustained conflict could create both opportunities and supply-chain disruptions. Additionally, any broader Middle East escalation risks drawing U.S. military resources that could affect defense contractors and veterans in Colorado.

Conservatives should monitor whether Trump's Iran strategy includes clear exit conditions and congressional authorization—or whether this becomes another open-ended military commitment financed by American taxpayers.
