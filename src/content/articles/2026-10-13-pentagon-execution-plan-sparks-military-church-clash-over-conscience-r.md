# Military Archbishop Tells Catholic Soldiers to Refuse Firing Squad Duty for Fort Hood Terrorist

The Pentagon's plan to livestream the execution of Fort Hood mass shooter Nidal Hasan has triggered a rare clash between the military establishment and the Catholic Church over conscience rights.

Archbishop Timothy Broglio, who oversees Catholic pastoral services across the U.S. military, issued a directive Monday urging Catholic service members assigned to the firing squad to request exemption from participation, according to the New York Post and Breitbart. Broglio also condemned the Pentagon's decision to broadcast the execution publicly.

**The conservative case:** Religious liberty advocates argue that forcing soldiers to participate in executions—especially livestreamed ones—violates conscience protections that should be foundational in a free military. The archbishop's intervention underscores legitimate concerns about government overreach into matters of faith and individual moral conviction.

**What the Pentagon says:** The military has not yet responded publicly to Broglio's statement, though the execution plan suggests the Defense Department views the livestream as a transparency measure for a high-profile terrorism case.

Hasan, convicted of the 2009 Fort Hood shooting that killed 13 people, has been on death row for years. The execution marks a rare capital case within the military justice system.

Conservatives and religious liberty advocates should monitor whether the Pentagon respects conscience objections or pressures soldiers to comply despite their faith commitments.
