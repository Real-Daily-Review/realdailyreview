# Bison Sculpture Theft Highlights Colorado Crime Problem

A bison sculpture created by Native youth in Denver was stolen from Pueblo overnight while touring the state—a brazen daylight theft that underscores Colorado's growing property crime crisis. According to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31, the suspect fled in a white four-door short-bed pickup truck after taking the artwork and its trailer.

The sculpture, part of an educational tour across Colorado, represents both cultural heritage and community investment. Its theft is emblematic of a larger problem: Colorado communities are increasingly vulnerable to organized theft rings targeting vehicles, trailers, and valuable property. Pueblo Police are actively investigating, but the incident raises hard questions about public safety and law enforcement resources stretched thin across the state.

Conservatives point to Colorado's catch-and-release criminal justice policies and underfunded police departments as contributing factors to rising property crime. Republicans have long argued that progressive bail reform and lenient sentencing guidelines—championed by Democratic lawmakers—embolden repeat offenders and make communities less safe. Meanwhile, law enforcement agencies struggle with recruitment and retention amid anti-police rhetoric from the left.

The theft also underscores the vulnerability of cultural and educational initiatives when basic public safety fails. Coloradans deserve neighborhoods and public spaces where property is secure and community assets are protected.

Watch for whether Pueblo Police recover the sculpture and whether this incident prompts renewed calls for tougher enforcement and bail reform from Colorado Republicans.
