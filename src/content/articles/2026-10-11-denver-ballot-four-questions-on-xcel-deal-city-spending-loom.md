# Denver Voters Face Four Ballot Questions on Xcel Deal, City Spending

Denver taxpayers will decide this November on four referred questions that could reshape how the city manages its utility franchise and budgets public money—including a contentious new Xcel Energy agreement that conservatives argue deserves scrutiny.

The Denver City Council approved referred questions 2H through 2K for the ballot, covering a revised Xcel Energy franchise agreement, budgeting changes, and related municipal matters, according to the *Denver Post*. Republicans and fiscal conservatives have raised concerns about utility franchise deals that lock in long-term rate structures and regulatory frameworks, arguing such agreements often favor corporate interests over ratepayers and limit future flexibility for cost control.

The ballot measures come as Denver grapples with rising energy costs and municipal spending pressures. Conservatives worry that bundled ballot questions—particularly those involving utility contracts—can obscure the true fiscal impact on households already facing inflation and higher property taxes.

The *Denver Post* reports the council moved these questions forward without significant public debate about alternatives, a common conservative complaint about how Denver's progressive majority handles major fiscal decisions.

Taxpayers should watch whether these referred questions include transparent cost projections and rate-impact analyses before November voting begins.
