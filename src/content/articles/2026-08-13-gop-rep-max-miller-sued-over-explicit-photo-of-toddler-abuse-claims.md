# Republican Congressman Max Miller Faces Federal Lawsuit Over Alleged Abuse, Explicit Images

A federal lawsuit filed against Ohio Republican Rep. Max Miller by his ex-wife Emily Moreno alleges he shared an explicit photograph of their 2-year-old daughter online and physically abused Moreno herself—claims that threaten to derail his reelection campaign and raise serious questions about fitness for office.

According to the complaint filed in federal court, Miller and his attorney Aaron Minc violated federal laws protecting against disclosure of intimate images when they shared a Dropbox link containing a photograph depicting the child's genitalia among other documents (New York Post). Moreno has also alleged Miller physically abused her, including by holding a gun to her head and pouring hot water on her, and sought a restraining order against the congressman last month (Just The News).

Miller has "rigorously denied" the allegations (Washington Examiner), but the lawsuit represents the latest serious challenge to his political viability. The case raises uncomfortable questions about congressional oversight and whether Republican leadership will distance themselves from a member facing such grave accusations involving child exploitation and domestic violence.

While this story centers on Ohio politics, it underscores broader concerns conservatives share about accountability: elected officials—regardless of party—must face serious legal consequences if allegations of abuse and crimes against children prove substantiated.
