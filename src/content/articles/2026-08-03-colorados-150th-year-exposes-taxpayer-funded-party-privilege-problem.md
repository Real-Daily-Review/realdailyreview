# Colorado's 150th Birthday Masks a Deeper Problem: Taxpayer-Funded Party Privilege

Colorado celebrated its sesquicentennial at the State Capitol this week with cake and fanfare, but conservatives are raising a harder question: why does state government continue forcing taxpayers to bankroll political party primaries they may not support?

As *Complete Colorado* has documented, Colorado's election system grants special government privileges to the Democratic and Republican parties—privileges funded by all taxpayers, regardless of party affiliation or lack thereof. "Colorado government has no proper business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties," the outlet argues, calling such separation "the courageous move" Colorado should make.

The complaint runs deeper than partisan squabbling. Conservatives and taxpayer advocates contend that forcing citizens to finance primary elections for parties they don't belong to violates basic principles of limited government and individual liberty. *Complete Colorado* posed the core question bluntly: "Why does Colorado government force taxpayers to finance the primaries of parties to which they do not even belong?"

Republicans and conservatives argue this represents government overreach dressed up as electoral administration. The state should either treat all political organizations equally or stop compelling taxpayers to subsidize any of them.

As Colorado marks 150 years of statehood, watch whether lawmakers move to end taxpayer funding of party primaries—a test of whether the state truly believes in limiting government power.
