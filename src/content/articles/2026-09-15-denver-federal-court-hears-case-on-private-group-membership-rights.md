# Federal Court in Denver Weighs Private Group's Right to Set Own Membership Rules

A federal appeals court in Denver is hearing arguments over whether Kappa Kappa Gamma can enforce its own membership standards—a case that pits progressive ideology against the fundamental right of private organizations to govern themselves (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

The dispute centers on whether a national sorority can maintain membership criteria without federal interference. Conservatives argue that private organizations have a constitutional right to set their own rules without government dictating who must be admitted. This principle—freedom of association—is foundational to civil society and should not be subordinated to federal mandates, no matter how sympathetically framed.

The case reflects a broader tension: as progressive activists increasingly use federal courts to override private group decisions, Americans face a choice between allowing organizations autonomy or empowering government to micromanage membership and association. For Colorado, this Denver-based proceeding could set precedent affecting how private clubs, organizations, and businesses across the state operate.

The appeals court's ruling will signal whether private groups retain meaningful control over their own affairs or whether federal courts will continue expanding government authority into spaces traditionally reserved for voluntary association.
