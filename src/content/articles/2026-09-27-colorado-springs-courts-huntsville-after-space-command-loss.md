# Colorado Springs Leaders Court Huntsville After Space Command Loss

Colorado Springs is swallowing hard and heading to Alabama to rebuild ties with Huntsville after losing the bitter fight for U.S. Space Command, according to CPR News.

The Colorado Springs chamber is sponsoring a four-day summit aimed at mending fences with the city that ultimately won the coveted federal command. The move signals pragmatism over pride—Space Command's relocation represents a significant blow to the region's defense-sector economy and prestige, but Colorado Springs leaders recognize that maintaining relationships with Huntsville could yield future opportunities for partnerships and contracts.

Meanwhile, Colorado's election landscape continues shifting. Complete Colorado reports that Colorado Springs faces a lawsuit over holding municipal elections in April—the first enforcement action since a voting law took effect in 2025. The legal challenge underscores ongoing disputes over election timing and administration across the state.

On the broader political front, unaffiliated voters are reshaping Colorado's electoral power structure. Complete Colorado notes that rising numbers of unaffiliated registrants mean party primaries increasingly decide general elections, potentially limiting choices for voters who skip caucuses but remain party-registered.
