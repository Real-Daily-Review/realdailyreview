# Nederland Pursues $125M Eldora Ski Resort Buyout—Taxpayers Should Brace for the Bill

The 1,500-person mountain town of Nederland is eyeing a $125 million purchase of Eldora Ski Resort, a deal that could transform the community—or crater it financially. According to the Colorado Sun, the plan has locals "kicking the snow tires" on what amounts to a massive municipal gamble.

Here's the conservative concern: small-town governments rarely have the expertise or financial cushion to operate ski resorts profitably. A $125 million acquisition saddles Nederland taxpayers with enormous debt, operational risk, and potential liability. If Eldora underperforms—due to poor snow years, competition from larger resorts, or management missteps—residents foot the bill.

The pitch likely centers on "community control" and "keeping profits local." But government-run businesses typically underperform private operators. Ski resort management requires sophisticated marketing, capital reinvestment, and ruthless cost discipline—not municipal bureaucracy.

Colorado Sun notes the deal "could all go downhill fast"—a fair assessment. Before Nederland commits, taxpayers deserve transparent financial projections, independent audits, and honest talk about worst-case scenarios. The town should also consider whether private operators might achieve better outcomes at lower public risk.

Watch whether Nederland's town council demands full disclosure of operating costs, debt service, and contingency reserves before the vote.
