# Federal Push to Reform Autism Therapy Billing Raises Questions About Care Standards

The Biden administration is pressuring major health insurers and medical providers to overhaul how they bill for autism treatment—a move that sounds good in theory but raises red flags about government micromanagement of healthcare delivery.

In July, the Department of Health and Human Services secured pledges from the country's largest health plans and provider groups to integrate behavioral and physical health care and adopt "evidence-based" treatment standards (Washington Examiner). The pledge also mandates new measurement and reporting requirements.

**The conservative concern:** While nobody disputes that autistic children deserve quality care, this federal push represents another layer of government-imposed standards and bureaucratic oversight. Republicans worry that Washington's one-size-fits-all approach—complete with new compliance burdens—will drive up administrative costs, reduce provider flexibility, and ultimately limit treatment options for Colorado families.

The real question: Will these mandates improve care, or simply create more paperwork and red tape that diverts resources from actual patient treatment? Colorado providers already navigate a maze of state and federal regulations; adding federal billing standards could strain smaller practices and increase costs passed to patients and taxpayers.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado health insurers comply voluntarily or face federal pressure—and whether this expands into other treatment areas.
