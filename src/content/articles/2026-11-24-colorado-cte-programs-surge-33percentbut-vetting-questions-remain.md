# Colorado's Career-Tech Programs Boom—But Oversight Questions Linger

Colorado's career and technical education (CTE) programs are expanding rapidly, with 240,000 students across 160 school districts now enrolled in over 2,000 programs—a 33% jump since 2015 and representing 46% of the state's student body (9News Denver). On the surface, that's good news: CTE offers a practical alternative to four-year degrees and addresses Colorado's skilled-labor shortage.

But the expansion raises a critical question: Are Colorado schools adequately vetting staff in these programs? The federal Department of Education recently took "unprecedented enforcement action" against a Virginia school district for failing to screen over 500 employees for child abuse or neglect (Washington Examiner). 

**Conservatives argue** that rapid program growth without rigorous personnel screening is reckless. Parents deserve assurance that every adult in Colorado classrooms—especially in hands-on technical settings—has been thoroughly vetted. The Virginia case underscores how bureaucratic negligence can endanger kids.

**Democrats and education officials** typically counter that CTE expansion reflects workforce development success and that most districts follow proper protocols. They may argue additional screening mandates impose costs on already-stretched budgets.

Colorado taxpayers should demand that state education officials confirm all 160 districts are conducting thorough background checks and abuse-history screenings before enrollment numbers climb further.
