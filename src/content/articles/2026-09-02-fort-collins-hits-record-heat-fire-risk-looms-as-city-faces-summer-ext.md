# Fort Collins Swelters Through Record Heat as City Grapples With Fire Risk

Fort Collins just endured its hottest summer on record, a reality that's sharpening focus on Colorado's vulnerability to wildfire season and the infrastructure challenges that come with extreme weather—challenges that government planning and land-management decisions will determine whether taxpayers bear the cost.

According to 9News Denver, Colorado cities including Fort Collins have recorded some of their hottest summers on record, part of a broader intensification of heat across the state. The heat spike has real consequences: KDVR Fox31 reported that a grass fire in Fort Collins' 2900 block of Arbee Lane prompted mandatory evacuations Wednesday afternoon, though the order was later canceled.

**The conservative angle:** While climate data is factual, progressives often weaponize weather events to justify expanded government regulation and green-energy mandates that drive up energy costs for Colorado families and businesses. Republicans argue that adaptation—better forest management, smarter water policy, and reliable energy infrastructure—matters far more than climate alarmism. Colorado's energy sector, which powers the grid and funds schools, shouldn't be sacrificed to progressive ideology.

Fort Collins' record heat underscores why Colorado needs robust emergency preparedness, responsible land stewardship, and energy independence—not more restrictions on oil and gas that leave the state dependent on foreign energy and vulnerable to blackouts during peak demand.
