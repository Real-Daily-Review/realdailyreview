# Denver-Area Crime Surge Strains Police Resources as Departments Face Staffing Crunch

Denver and Thornton police are stretched thin responding to serious violent crime—a hit-and-run that critically injured a pedestrian and a shooting that claimed one life—while departments struggle with officer shortages that have plagued Colorado's Front Range for years.

Denver Police are hunting for a 2003-2007 Chevrolet Silverado involved in Saturday's hit-and-run on Kalamath Street (9News Denver). Meanwhile, Thornton officers responding to an early-morning shooting saw one of their own vehicles struck by a train, adding another layer of danger to an already volatile scene (KDVR Fox31).

These incidents underscore a persistent problem conservatives have flagged: progressive cities' anti-police rhetoric and budget cuts have gutted law enforcement capacity precisely when crime demands it most. Denver and surrounding jurisdictions have faced recruitment and retention crises as officers leave for better-paying departments or exit the profession entirely.

Republicans argue that defund-the-police movements and soft-on-crime policies have emboldened criminals while leaving communities less safe. Democrats counter that investing in social services and community programs addresses root causes—though violent crime continues climbing regardless.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether city councils will finally reverse course and adequately fund police departments, or double down on failed progressive experiments.
