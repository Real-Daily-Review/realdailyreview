# Colorado Ballot Bloat: Voters Face Tax Hike, Redistricting Fights This Fall

Colorado voters are about to make their own ballots longer—and more expensive for taxpayers. Amendment 87 would change the state income tax rate, forcing voters to wade through a complex ballot question paired with a chart from a 2022 approval (9News Denver). Meanwhile, Initiative #256 would impose new "guardrails" on congressional redistricting, banning maps designed to favor either political party (Complete Colorado).

The redistricting measure appeals to good-government reformers but raises a key question: who decides what constitutes partisan intent, and will it tie Colorado's hands in future map-drawing? Republicans worry such restrictions could lock in Democratic advantages if passed at an inopportune moment.

On taxes, Amendment 87's income tax language adds another layer to an already crowded ballot—a reminder that Colorado's ballot-initiative process lets activists and special interests write policy directly, bypassing the legislature. Taxpayers foot the bill for longer ballots and the administrative burden of implementing voter-approved measures.

The state also faces a separate challenge: unaffiliated voter registration is rising, and party-affiliated voters who skip caucuses are effectively ceding primary power to the most engaged partisans (Complete Colorado). That's reshaping Colorado's political map in real time.
