# Road Rage Shooter Gets 28 Years; Colorado's Violent Crime Problem Demands Tough Sentencing

Colorado's criminal justice system delivered a rare win for public safety this week: Oscar Granados, 20, was sentenced to 28 years for attempted murder in a brazen I-25 road rage shooting in Adams County (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The sentence reflects the severity of the crime and sends a message that violent attacks on Colorado highways won't be tolerated.

Yet this case underscores a broader concern for Colorado taxpayers and commuters. Road rage incidents and highway violence continue plaguing the metro area, raising questions about whether Colorado's approach to violent crime—prosecution, sentencing, and deterrence—is sufficiently tough. Conservatives argue that consistent, serious sentences for violent felonies protect communities and discourage repeat offenders.

The 28-year sentence is appropriate, but it also highlights the cost of violent crime to the state: prosecution, incarceration, and the trauma inflicted on victims and their families. Colorado's criminal justice system must balance rehabilitation with accountability, ensuring that violent offenders face meaningful consequences that protect law-abiding citizens.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's legislature and courts maintain this tough-on-crime posture or drift toward leniency in violent cases.
