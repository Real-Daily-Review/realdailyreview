# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push hungry wildlife into residential areas at unprecedented rates, forcing a grim choice between public safety and animal welfare.

The spike reflects a cascading environmental crisis: scorched forests and parched landscapes leave bears with depleted natural food sources, driving them to raid garbage, livestock, and suburban neighborhoods. Colorado Parks and Wildlife says the euthanizations are necessary to protect residents and prevent habituation that makes bears more dangerous long-term.

Conservatives and property owners argue the state has failed to adequately manage forest health and fuel loads—decades of fire-suppression policy and environmental litigation have left Colorado's forests overgrown and vulnerable. They contend that aggressive forest management, controlled burns, and timber harvesting could reduce catastrophic wildfires and restore natural bear habitat, ultimately preventing both human-wildlife conflict and the need for mass euthanasia.

Environmental groups counter that climate change and drought are the primary drivers, not forest management alone. The left typically resists logging and fuel-reduction projects on public lands, citing ecosystem concerns.

The real issue: Colorado taxpayers are bearing the cost—in property damage, wildlife management resources, and the loss of bears—while state and federal land-management decisions remain gridlocked by regulation and litigation.
