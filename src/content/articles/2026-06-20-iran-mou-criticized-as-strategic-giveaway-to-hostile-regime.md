# Iran Deal Hands Strategic Advantage to America's Adversary, Conservatives Warn

The Trump administration's new Memorandum of Understanding with Iran has drawn sharp criticism from the right, with conservatives arguing the agreement tilts dangerously in Tehran's favor. National Review editors contend there is "not much for Americans to like" about the MOU, warning it grants momentum to a regime hostile to U.S. interests in the Middle East (National Review). 

The criticism extends to the negotiating framework itself. Trump's own track record on Iran—demonstrated in his first term—suggested tougher terms were possible. Yet this deal, conservatives argue, represents a strategic miscalculation that rewards Iranian intransigence. As National Review notes, "Iran has never won a war, yet never lost a negotiation," suggesting the administration may have repeated past mistakes in diplomatic overreach.

Vice President JD Vance's role in the decision has drawn particular scrutiny from right-leaning outlets (The Daily Wire), with questions about whether the administration adequately weighed national security implications against diplomatic optics.
