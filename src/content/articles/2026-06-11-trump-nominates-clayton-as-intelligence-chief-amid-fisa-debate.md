# Trump Taps Ex-SEC Chair Clayton for Intelligence Director Role

President Trump has nominated Jay Clayton, former Securities and Exchange Commission chairman and current U.S. Attorney, to serve as the next Director of National Intelligence, succeeding Tulsi Gabbard (Fox News, Washington Examiner).

Clayton's nomination comes as Congress grinds through contentious debates over FISA surveillance authorities and intelligence oversight—issues that have divided Republicans between those prioritizing national security tools and those demanding stricter civil liberties protections. His background in financial regulation and law enforcement suggests Trump is seeking someone with executive experience to manage the sprawling intelligence apparatus.

For Colorado, Clayton's appointment carries implications for how federal intelligence priorities may shift. The state hosts significant defense and aerospace contractors, along with critical infrastructure tied to national security. His regulatory background at the SEC also signals potential shifts in how financial oversight intersects with national security concerns—relevant to Colorado's growing fintech and investment sectors.

Republicans backing the nomination point to Clayton's executive credentials and law enforcement record. Critics on the right have raised questions about FISA reauthorization and whether intelligence leadership will respect constitutional limits on surveillance powers.

Watch whether Senate Republicans demand commitments from Clayton on FISA reform and Fourth Amendment protections before confirmation votes proceed.
