# Denver's $18M 16th Street Mall Surprise: Another Case of City Hall Keeping Taxpayers in the Dark

Denver taxpayers got blindsided again—this time by a $18 million bill for a historic brick sewer system discovered during the 16th Street Mall renovation, and city council had no idea it was coming.

The contractor unearthed the underground infrastructure, triggering delays and a hefty change order that landed squarely on the public dime. According to Denver's auditor (KDVR), the city council committee overseeing the Department of Transportation and Infrastructure wasn't informed about the cost spike before it happened—a transparency failure that should alarm anyone paying property taxes in the Mile High City.

This is textbook government mismanagement: poor planning, lack of oversight, and zero accountability until the damage is done. The auditor flagged the need for "more transparency" (9News), but transparency demands are hollow without real consequences for the bureaucrats and contractors who let this happen.

Denver has a pattern of cost overruns and surprises on major projects. The question isn't whether the city needs better communication—it's whether city leadership will actually enforce it, or if this becomes another $18 million lesson learned and forgotten.

Watch whether Denver's city council demands refunds or cost-sharing from contractors, or if taxpayers simply absorb another hit.
