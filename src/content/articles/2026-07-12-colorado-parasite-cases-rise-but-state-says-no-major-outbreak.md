# Colorado Parasite Cases Spike, But State Health Officials Say No Major Outbreak

Colorado has reported dozens of cyclosporiasis cases this year—a parasitic infection causing severe gastrointestinal illness—but state health authorities insist the cases remain isolated and unlinked to larger outbreaks plaguing other states.

According to the Colorado Sun, dozens of Coloradans have contracted the parasite, though officials characterize the situation as manageable and not part of the multi-state outbreak pattern seen elsewhere. KDVR reports that hundreds of people nationwide—and likely more—have fallen ill in recent weeks, raising questions about the scope of the problem.

The parasite, transmitted through contaminated food and water, causes acute diarrhea and other gastrointestinal symptoms. Colorado health officials have not issued emergency declarations or major public health alerts, suggesting confidence in containment.

**The conservative angle:** While government health agencies monitor the situation, Coloradans should remain skeptical of regulatory overreach in response. If state or federal agencies use this incident to justify expanded food-safety mandates or agricultural restrictions that burden Colorado farmers and producers, taxpayers should demand evidence that such rules actually prevent illness rather than simply expand bureaucratic control.

Watch whether state health officials recommend new regulations on Colorado's agricultural sector—and whether those rules are proportionate to the actual risk.
