# Trump, Fetterman Push AI Growth Over 'Doomsdaying'—But Overregulation Could Hand Edge to China

President Trump and Sen. John Fetterman (D-Pa.) are breaking ranks with progressive AI skeptics, warning that excessive U.S. caution risks ceding technological dominance to China. Trump told Michael Cohen in a recent interview that America leads the AI race "by a lot" and called current conditions "a renaissance" (Washington Examiner). Fetterman rejected what he called "AI doomsdaying," aligning with Trump's push for unfettered development rather than restrictive guardrails (Fox News).

The message is clear: both see regulatory overreach as the real threat. Republicans and pro-growth Democrats argue that heavy-handed government rules—the kind progressives have championed—could slow American innovation while Beijing races ahead without such constraints.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's tech corridor and energy sector have stakes in this debate. Aggressive AI regulation could hamper Colorado startups and increase computing costs for businesses already squeezed by state-level regulatory burdens. Meanwhile, AI's massive power demands intersect with Colorado's energy policy—another area where overregulation could prove costly.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature attempts to impose AI restrictions that mirror California's model, potentially driving tech talent and investment eastward.
