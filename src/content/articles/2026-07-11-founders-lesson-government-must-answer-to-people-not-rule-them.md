# Founders as First Watchdogs: Why Limited Government Still Matters

America's founders understood something modern progressives seem to have forgotten: government power must be audited, constrained, and answerable to the people—not the reverse.

That's the core argument emerging from conservative voices reflecting on the founders' legacy. Kelley Paul's new children's book, *Good Night, Young American*, frames the founders as "the first civil rights heroes" (Fox News), emphasizing their commitment to individual liberty against unchecked state power. The *Washington Examiner* goes further, noting that the founders "audited power" by gathering facts, naming abuses, and establishing that "government is legitimate only when it answers to the people."

For Colorado conservatives, this framing cuts to the heart of current debates over regulatory overreach, government spending, and executive power. When state and federal agencies expand without legislative oversight—whether through environmental rules, tax policy, or land-use restrictions—they violate the founders' core principle: accountability to voters, not bureaucrats.

The left, by contrast, tends to emphasize the founders' flaws and argues that modern progressive governance corrects their mistakes through expanded regulatory authority and centralized planning.
