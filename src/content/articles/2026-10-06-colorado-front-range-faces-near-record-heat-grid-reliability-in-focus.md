# Colorado Front Range Braces for Dangerous Heat Wave as Temperatures Soar 20 Degrees Above Normal

Colorado's Front Range is about to get hammered by near-record heat that could push temperatures 20 degrees above seasonal norms, with northern Colorado potentially hitting 90 degrees as early as Monday (CPR News). The scorching conditions are expected to persist through at least Tuesday, bringing hotter and drier weather across the Denver metro area and beyond (KDVR Fox31).

This heat spike arrives as Colorado faces mounting pressure on water resources and energy infrastructure. Conservatives warn that aggressive environmental regulations limiting power generation and water management—championed by progressive state lawmakers—could strain Colorado's ability to meet peak demand during extreme weather events. Republicans argue the state should prioritize reliable energy sources and flexible water policies over ideological climate mandates that hamstring practical infrastructure.

The heat also underscores why Colorado needs robust, dispatchable power generation. Renewable-only portfolios leave the grid vulnerable when demand spikes and solar output drops during evening peak hours—a reality grid operators know well.

Coloradans should watch whether state regulators and utilities can handle the load without rolling blackouts, and whether Democratic energy policies prove adequate when the heat is on.
