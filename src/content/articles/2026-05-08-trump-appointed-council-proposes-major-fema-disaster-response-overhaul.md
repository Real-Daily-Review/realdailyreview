# Trump-Appointed Council Proposes Major Overhaul of FEMA's Disaster Response Role

A council appointed by President Trump has proposed sweeping changes to FEMA's disaster relief operations, according to CBS News and NPR. The recommendations, revealed Thursday, could significantly reshape how the federal government responds to and funds disaster recovery efforts.

According to PBS NewsHour, the proposals could reduce the number of disasters that receive federal support and decrease the total amount of federal funding allocated for recovery. The council, established to reform the Federal Emergency Management Agency, presented what both outlets describe as "major changes" to the nation's primary disaster response body.

The specific details of the proposed redesign—including which disaster categories might lose federal support or how funding formulas would change—were not detailed in the available reporting. Neither the rationale behind the recommendations nor initial reactions from FEMA leadership, Congress, or disaster-affected communities were included in the source material.

The proposals represent the first formal recommendations from Trump's FEMA review effort and signal a potential shift in federal disaster policy. Watch for congressional response and whether FEMA leadership endorses or opposes the council's recommendations.
