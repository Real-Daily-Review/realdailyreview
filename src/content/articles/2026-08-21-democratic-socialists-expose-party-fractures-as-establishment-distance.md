# Democratic Socialists Expose Party's Credibility Crisis

The Democratic Party's embrace of socialist candidates is becoming an embarrassment even to its own establishment—and Colorado voters should take note of what's coming.

Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer won't commit to campaigning with Michigan socialist Abdul El-Sayed, who refuses to back Schumer as Democratic leader (Fox News). Meanwhile, Los Angeles socialist Nithya Raman drew groans from her own party's audience over a bungled answer about hiring decisions at a mayoral debate (Fox News). And a New York democratic socialist congressional candidate claimed to be "focused" on her general election campaign while touring Uruguay with fellow leftists (Breitbart).

These aren't isolated gaffes. They reveal a Democratic Party torn between its progressive base and voters who still believe in basic party loyalty and electoral seriousness. Schumer's refusal to back El-Sayed signals deep fractures; Raman's audience groans show even Democratic voters are losing patience with socialist positioning; and Avila Chevalier's Uruguay vacation while claiming election focus screams priorities misaligned with constituents.

Colorado Democrats have already pushed hard-left policies on energy, taxes, and gun control. As the national party drifts further left, expect similar credibility problems to surface in Colorado races—where moderate voters and energy workers are watching closely.
