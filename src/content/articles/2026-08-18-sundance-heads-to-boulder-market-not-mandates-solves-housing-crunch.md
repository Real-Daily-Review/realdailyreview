# Sundance Comes to Boulder—And So Do Market Incentives

CU Boulder has officially partnered with the Sundance Film Festival, which is relocating to Colorado for the first time, promising a year of programming beyond the festival itself (9News Denver). The move is already reshaping Boulder's rental market: Airbnb is offering homeowners a $200 bonus to list properties during the event, as demand for lodging surges ahead of the festival's debut (9News Denver, CPR News).

The incentive structure reveals something conservatives should appreciate—private enterprise solving a logistical problem without government mandates. Rather than Boulder imposing price controls or regulatory restrictions on short-term rentals (a progressive instinct), Airbnb is using market signals to expand supply. Homeowners get compensated; visitors find beds; the platform profits. No bureaucratic red tape required.

That said, Boulder's progressive city council has historically battled short-term rental operators over housing affordability and neighborhood character. Whether this festival-driven surge will soften that regulatory stance—or trigger a backlash from affordable-housing advocates—remains unclear.

CU's official partnership also raises questions about public university resources being directed toward entertainment programming. Taxpayers should watch whether state funds are flowing into festival operations or if this remains purely private-sector driven.
