# Trump Heads to NATO Summit With Iran Tensions, Ukraine Peace Push

President Trump arrives at the NATO summit in Ankara facing a fractured alliance—frustrated allies have denied him military bases and overflight rights needed for operations against Iran, according to Fox News. The rift underscores a core conservative concern: America's traditional alliances often prioritize their own interests over U.S. security objectives.

Meanwhile, Trump is signaling renewed confidence he can broker an end to the Ukraine war, the Washington Examiner reports. Three weeks after calling the conflict "in the rear view mirror," Trump now sees an opening for a diplomatic settlement—a stark contrast to the Biden-Harris administration's open-ended commitment to Ukrainian aid.

**The Colorado angle:** Defense contractors and energy firms tied to NATO operations watch these developments closely. A fractured alliance could reshape defense spending priorities and military base operations affecting Colorado's defense economy.

Conservatives argue that Trump's transactional approach to NATO—demanding allies meet spending commitments and contribute meaningfully to shared objectives—is long overdue. The left counters that weakening alliance cohesion invites Russian and Iranian aggression.

**Watch next:** Whether Trump can leverage NATO pressure into concrete commitments on Ukraine negotiations and Iran containment without further alienating European partners already skeptical of his foreign policy.
