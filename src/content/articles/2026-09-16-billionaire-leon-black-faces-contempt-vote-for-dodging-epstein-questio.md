# Billionaire Defies Congress on Epstein Ties; House Votes Contempt

Leon Black, the Colorado-connected financier, faces a House contempt vote after refusing to answer bipartisan questions about his payments to convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. Rather than appear for his deposition before the House Oversight Committee, Black sued to block the subpoenas—a move that triggered the contempt resolution.

The case underscores a broader conservative concern: when the powerful use litigation to dodge congressional accountability, it weakens oversight regardless of which party holds the gavel. Republicans and Democrats alike wanted answers about Black's financial ties to Epstein, signaling that some government investigations command bipartisan support.

Black's Colorado roots—his investment firm Apollo Global Management has significant state ties—make this more than a Washington scandal. Coloradans who value the rule of law and equal treatment under it should watch whether Black faces actual consequences or whether wealth and legal firepower allow him to sidestep accountability that ordinary citizens cannot.

The real question: will Congress enforce its subpoena power, or will Black's legal strategy succeed in running out the clock?
