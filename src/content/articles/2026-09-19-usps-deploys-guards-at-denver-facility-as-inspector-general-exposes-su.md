# USPS Deploys Private Guards at Denver Facility as Ballot Season Looms—While Inspector General Uncovers Widespread Survey Fraud

The U.S. Postal Service has stationed private security guards at Colorado's largest mail sorting facility in Denver ahead of the mail ballot season, citing employee safety and mail security concerns. But the timing raises fresh questions about USPS reliability just as millions of ballots will flow through the system.

The move comes as the USPS Inspector General has documented "widespread manipulation" of customer service surveys by postal workers, according to *Just The News*. The fraud created a fake 5 percent spike in consumer satisfaction metrics between 2023 and 2025—and persisted even after one cheating scheme was exposed. Union representatives at the Denver facility told *CPR News* the private guards are unnecessary, though the postal service maintains they're essential for security.

For Colorado voters and taxpayers, the optics are troubling: an agency tasked with handling ballots has just been caught systematically falsifying its own performance data. Republicans have long questioned USPS competence and security protocols around mail voting. Democrats defend the postal service as essential infrastructure.

The real concern isn't theater—it's whether an organization caught cooking the books can be trusted to safeguard the integrity of Colorado's mail ballot process.

Watch whether state election officials demand independent audits of USPS handling procedures before November.
