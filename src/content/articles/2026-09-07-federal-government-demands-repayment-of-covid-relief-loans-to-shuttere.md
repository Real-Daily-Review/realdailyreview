# Federal Government Demands Repayment of COVID Relief Loans to Businesses It Forced to Close

The federal government is now demanding repayment of Emergency Injury Disaster Loans (EIDL) it distributed to small businesses during COVID-19 lockdowns—even from owners who never fully recovered from the mandated closures Washington imposed.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the government ordered businesses shut, then lent them money to survive, and now, six years later, is collecting. The problem: these weren't ordinary commercial loans made under normal economic conditions. Government-mandated closures and operating restrictions created the crisis these loans addressed in the first place.

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** Washington created the problem, provided temporary relief, and now wants repayment from business owners still struggling with the aftermath. It's a bait-and-switch that punishes entrepreneurs for complying with government orders they had no choice but to follow.

Colorado small business owners—from Denver restaurants to mountain hospitality operators—face the same squeeze. Many took EIDL funds as a lifeline when state lockdowns shuttered their doors. If the federal government now demands full repayment from those still rebuilding, it compounds the original injustice of the closures themselves.

**Watch for Congressional action on EIDL forgiveness legislation and whether Colorado's delegation pushes relief for state business owners caught in this trap.**
