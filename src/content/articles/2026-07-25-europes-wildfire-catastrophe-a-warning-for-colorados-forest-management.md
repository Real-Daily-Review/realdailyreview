# European Wildfires Displace 250,000; Colorado Should Learn Hard Lessons on Forest Management

Massive wildfires across France and Spain have forced more than 250,000 people to evacuate their homes, with authorities bracing for dangerous wind shifts that could push flames toward major population centers like Bordeaux. The scale of the disaster—including some 70,000 evacuees in central Spain alone—has disrupted even the Tour de France, which officials shortened to redeploy security personnel to fire response efforts.

While European governments grapple with the immediate crisis, Colorado conservatives should note the cautionary tale: decades of mismanagement, environmental regulations that restrict logging and controlled burns, and failure to thin overgrown forests create tinderboxes. The same progressive policies that hamstring forest management in Europe—prioritizing "wilderness preservation" over practical land stewardship—have taken root in Colorado, leaving our state vulnerable to catastrophic fire seasons.

Republicans and conservatives argue that aggressive forest thinning, selective logging, and controlled burns are essential tools for reducing fuel loads and protecting communities. Yet environmental groups and Democratic-aligned regulators often block these measures, citing habitat concerns and climate rhetoric that ignores the reality: dead trees burn hotter and faster than managed forests.

Colorado taxpayers should watch how state and federal land managers respond to this European wake-up call—and whether they'll finally prioritize public safety over regulatory ideology.
