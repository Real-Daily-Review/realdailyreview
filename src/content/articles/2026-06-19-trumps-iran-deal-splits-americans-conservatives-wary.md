# Trump's Iran Deal Divides Americans—and Conservatives

President Trump's framework agreement with Iran is drawing cautious support from some quarters but skepticism from hardliners who worry about Tehran's true intentions. According to a Reagan Institute Summer Survey cited by Fox News, Americans are split: 39% favor a negotiated settlement, while 36% back regime change—a telling sign that the deal hasn't won over the skeptical half of the country.

Trump's team frames the agreement as a historic diplomatic breakthrough that reduces war risk and opens communication channels with Tehran. Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has backed the deal, though reportedly with reservations (Breitbart). The Washington Examiner notes supporters see it as a major achievement, but some conservatives question whether pragmatism here amounts to appeasement.

The core conservative concern: Does this agreement genuinely protect American interests and regional stability, or does it reward a regime hostile to U.S. allies and interests? Skeptics worry the deal may lack sufficient verification mechanisms and could embolden Iranian aggression elsewhere.

For Colorado, this matters insofar as Middle East stability affects energy markets and military readiness—issues touching defense contractors and oil-and-gas workers across the state.

Watch whether Congress moves to scrutinize the agreement's terms and whether Iran honors its commitments in the coming months.
