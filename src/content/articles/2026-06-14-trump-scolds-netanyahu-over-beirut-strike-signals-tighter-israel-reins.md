# Trump Rebukes Netanyahu Over Beirut Strike, Signals Shift in Israel Policy

President Trump has publicly criticized Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu over a military strike on Beirut, marking a notable departure from his first-term approach to Middle East allies. According to the Washington Examiner, Trump reportedly told Netanyahu he had "no f***ing judgment" after phoning to rebuke the strike, which Israel said was retaliation for Hezbollah missile attacks on northern Israel.

The rebuke signals Trump may be recalibrating U.S. support for Israeli military operations—a shift conservatives are watching closely. While Republicans have traditionally backed Israel's right to defend itself against terrorist threats, Trump's direct criticism suggests he wants tighter control over escalation decisions that could drag America into broader regional conflict.

The incident underscores a tension within conservative foreign policy: supporting Israel's security while avoiding open-ended commitments to every military decision. Trump's "America First" doctrine prioritizes U.S. interests over being pulled into Middle East conflicts, even by traditional allies.

For Colorado voters concerned about military spending and entanglement abroad, this signals Trump intends to be a more transactional partner—demanding allies exercise restraint rather than writing blank checks.

Watch whether Trump's criticism leads to formal conditions on U.S. military aid to Israel or signals a broader recalibration of American commitments in the region.
