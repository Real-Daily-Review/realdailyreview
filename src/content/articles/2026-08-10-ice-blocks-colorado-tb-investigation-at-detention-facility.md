# ICE Stonewalls Colorado Health Officials on TB Case at Detention Facility

Federal immigration authorities have stopped cooperating with Adams County public health investigators probing an active tuberculosis case confirmed in June at a local detention facility, according to CPR News. The lack of transparency from ICE has left Colorado officials unable to conduct proper disease tracking and community notification—a serious breach of basic public health protocol that puts local residents at risk.

Public health officials held a town hall to field community questions, but without ICE's cooperation, they cannot determine exposure scope or identify potentially infected individuals. Republicans and conservatives argue this exemplifies federal overreach and lack of accountability: when Washington agencies operate detention facilities, they must comply with state and local health laws, not ignore them.

The silence also raises questions about what ICE knows and isn't sharing. Colorado taxpayers fund local public health infrastructure; when federal agencies undermine that system, it wastes resources and erodes trust in government institutions meant to protect citizens.

Democrats typically frame immigration enforcement as a federal prerogative, but even progressive public health advocates recognize that disease doesn't respect jurisdictional boundaries. Coloradans deserve answers: what's the full scope of TB exposure, and why is ICE blocking investigation?

Watch whether Colorado's health department escalates this to state Attorney General or Congress for enforcement action.
