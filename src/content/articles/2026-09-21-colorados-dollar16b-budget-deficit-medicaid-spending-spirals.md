# Colorado Faces $1.6B Budget Hole as Medicaid Costs Spiral Out of Control

Colorado lawmakers are staring down a $1.6 billion state budget deficit in fiscal year 2027-28, driven by runaway Medicaid spending that threatens core government functions and taxpayer wallets.

According to the Colorado Sun, healthcare costs are the culprit behind the widening shortfall. Complete Colorado reports that Medicaid overspending alone could hit $443 million in FY 2026-27 if legislators fail to act decisively.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Colorado expanded Medicaid under the Affordable Care Act, and the program has become a fiscal albatross. Rather than controlling costs through market reforms, managed competition, or eligibility tightening, state Democrats have allowed the program to balloon unchecked. Conservatives argue that means-testing, work requirements, and competitive bidding among providers could rein in costs without gutting coverage—but those reforms face fierce resistance from the left.

The deficit looms as Colorado faces competing demands: education funding, transportation, and debt service all compete for shrinking dollars. Taxpayers will ultimately foot the bill through either tax hikes, service cuts, or both.

**Watch whether the legislature pursues real Medicaid reform or simply raises taxes to paper over the problem.**
