# Boulder Valley Schools Face Closure Wave as Enrollment Plummets

Colorado's largest school district outside Denver is shuttering schools it can no longer afford to operate—a hard reckoning that progressive education policies and demographic shifts have made unavoidable.

The Boulder Valley School District board voted Tuesday to close four schools (sources differ on whether a fifth consolidation counts as a closure) as enrollment continues its decline. The decision sparked outrage from parents and educators at a packed board meeting, but the math is unforgiving: fewer students means fewer tuition dollars and unsustainable overhead.

This is what happens when districts expand spending during boom years without adjusting for reality. Colorado's Front Range has seen population shifts away from traditional school-age families, and Boulder Valley—despite its affluent reputation—cannot escape demographic gravity. The closures will disrupt communities and force consolidations that families didn't choose.

Meanwhile, the broader education establishment continues pushing costly initiatives (curriculum overhauls, DEI programs, administrative bloat) that drain resources from classrooms. Virginia's Fairfax County Schools recently faced scrutiny over ideologically-driven 9/11 teaching guidance, a reminder that progressive priorities often crowd out fiscal discipline.
