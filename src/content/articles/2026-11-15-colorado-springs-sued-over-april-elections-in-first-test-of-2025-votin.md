# Colorado Springs Faces Lawsuit Over Election Timing—First Test of 2025 Voting Law

Colorado Springs is being sued for scheduling municipal elections in April, marking the first enforcement action under a 2025 voting law that appears to mandate different election timing (Complete Colorado). The lawsuit signals that Colorado's election rules—ostensibly designed to standardize voting procedures—are now being litigated over their practical application.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that expanding election regulations, even when framed as "security" or "fairness," create compliance headaches for local governments and open the door to litigation over implementation details. The Colorado Sun's recent essay celebrating "bipartisan teams" ensuring voting security glosses over the real-world friction: cities now face legal challenges simply for scheduling elections, raising questions about whether the 2025 law was clearly written or workable for Colorado municipalities.

**The conservative concern:** Election laws should be straightforward enough that local officials can follow them without lawsuits. Vague or overly prescriptive voting statutes burden taxpayers with legal fees and administrative confusion—exactly the kind of regulatory creep conservatives oppose.

**What to watch:** Whether Colorado Springs prevails or settles, expect more municipalities to challenge the 2025 law's election-timing requirements, forcing lawmakers to clarify or repeal unclear provisions.
