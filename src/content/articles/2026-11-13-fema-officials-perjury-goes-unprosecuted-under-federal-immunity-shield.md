# FEMA Official's Perjury Exposed—But Justice Department Won't Prosecute

A FEMA official caught lying under oath about her law school attendance illustrates how federal bureaucrats operate with near-total immunity from accountability. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the official swore in a September 29 deposition that she attended law school as a "night student" while employed at the agency. School records subpoenaed two weeks later proved she attended day classes in fall 2018 and spring 2019, earning her degree from the institution's "day division." The official ran FEMA's Continuous Improvement Program—a position overseeing federal spending and operations.

The Justice Department has declined to prosecute, citing the Westfall Act, a federal law that shields government employees from personal liability for actions taken within the scope of employment. Conservatives argue this creates a two-tiered justice system where ordinary Americans face perjury charges while federal workers lie with impunity. The *Examiner* notes the loophole effectively grants whistleblower-style immunity to any bureaucrat willing to commit fraud under oath.

For Colorado taxpayers funding FEMA operations and disaster relief, this case underscores why government accountability remains elusive. Watch whether Congress moves to close the Westfall Act loophole or if federal agencies continue sheltering dishonest officials.
