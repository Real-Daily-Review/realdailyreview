# Trump Administration Fractures Over Policy as 2028 Jockeying Begins

President Trump's inner circle is splintering over major policy questions, with Vice President Vance and Defense Secretary Hegseth signaling divergent positions on key issues—a dangerous split for any administration, but especially one eyeing re-election messaging.

National Review reports a "Trump-Vance fissure" on an unspecified but "hugely important policy question," noting that disagreement at the VP level poses particular peril for White House cohesion. Separately, Trump's recent amnesty overtures to Democrats have drawn skepticism from conservatives; National Review argues "it's hard to see how there'd be any deal in the offing" given Democratic resistance to serious border enforcement.

Meanwhile, Defense Secretary Hegseth is already positioning himself for a 2028 presidential run, according to the Washington Examiner. Defending himself against Rep. Thomas Massie's impeachment effort, Hegseth cast himself as Trump's "devoted servant"—while simultaneously signaling independent ambitions. The move amounts to needling Vice President Vance, who many assume is Trump's preferred successor.

For Colorado conservatives, this infighting matters: a weakened Trump administration struggles to defend energy development, Second Amendment rights, and water rights from progressive overreach. Watch whether this internal discord emboldens Democrats to push harder on regulatory fronts where Trump appointees should be united.
