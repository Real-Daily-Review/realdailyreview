# Colorado DSA Movement Faces Backlash as Voters Reject Far-Left Agenda

Colorado's Democratic Socialist Alliance is discovering what Wisconsin voters already know: radical ideology doesn't win elections. After a stunning primary upset in Wisconsin—where DSA-backed chef Francesca Hong lost badly to moderate Democrat David Crowley for governor (National Review)—Colorado conservatives are watching closely as the state's own socialist faction doubles down on what Complete Colorado describes as "vengeful dogma" echoing Bolshevik rhetoric.

The DSA's influence in Colorado politics has grown in recent years, but the Wisconsin result signals voter fatigue with the movement's maximalist demands: wealth confiscation, energy sector decimation, and expansive government control over markets and private enterprise. Republicans argue these policies would devastate Colorado's energy economy, burden taxpayers with unsustainable spending, and drive businesses to friendlier states.

Democrats counter that DSA activism pushes their party leftward on climate and inequality—issues they claim resonate with younger voters. But the Wisconsin primary suggests moderates still control the party's electoral machinery when it matters.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the takeaway is clear: the radical fringe remains unpopular at the ballot box, but watch whether state Democratic leadership distances itself from DSA demands or capitulates to appease the activist base heading into 2026.
