# Trump's Iran Deal Divides GOP Faith Leaders, Raises Colorado Defense Concerns

Trump's proposed Iran memorandum is fracturing evangelical Republican support—a warning sign for a coalition the GOP needs heading into 2026.

According to Fox News, evangelical leaders are split on the Trump-Iran agreement, with some backing military posturing while others argue Israel faces betrayal. The Washington Examiner counters that the deal should be viewed as long-term economic strategy benefiting both nations and the Middle East, despite recent setbacks and renewed tensions.

**The conservative divide matters for Colorado.** Defense contractors and military installations across the state—from Peterson Space Force Base to Buckley Air Force Base—depend on clear, unwavering Middle East policy. Evangelical voters, a core GOP constituency in rural Colorado counties, are watching whether Trump prioritizes Israeli security or diplomatic normalization with Iran.

Republicans arguing *for* the deal frame it as pragmatic economic development. Those opposing it—particularly pro-Israel evangelicals—see it as appeasement that weakens America's strongest regional ally. The fracture exposes a real tension: can Trump hold both the defense-hawk wing and the deal-making wing of his coalition?

Colorado conservatives and defense-sector workers should monitor whether this Iran agreement survives Senate scrutiny and whether evangelical defections cost Republicans support in 2026 primaries and general elections.
