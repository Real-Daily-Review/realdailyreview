# Massachusetts Murder Trial Exposes Troubling Shift in How America Treats Violent Crime

A Massachusetts woman on trial for strangling her three children is drawing hundreds of supporters who argue mental illness should excuse homicide—a dangerous precedent that threatens accountability and public safety.

Lindsay Clancy's defense doesn't dispute she killed her children. Instead, her lawyers argue postpartum psychosis should eliminate criminal responsibility entirely. Hundreds of women gathered outside Plymouth Superior Court in coordinated pink vigils, some holding infants, to signal sympathy for the defendant (9News Denver, The Daily Wire).

The case has spawned a troubling social-media phenomenon. TikTok users have embraced "same, Lindsay" trends, and online sleuths peddle alternative narratives divorced from facts. As The Daily Wire noted, the obsession reflects how internet culture now shapes criminal justice perception—often divorced from reality.

**The conservative concern:** Excusing violent crime based on mental-health claims, however sympathetic, erodes the rule of law. Accountability for taking innocent lives—especially children—cannot hinge on subjective mental-state arguments that become infinitely expandable. Victims' families deserve justice, not social-media activism that romanticizes murder.

Colorado taxpayers and families should watch whether this trial influences how courts handle insanity defenses and whether sympathetic narratives begin replacing evidence-based verdicts in high-profile cases.
