# Supreme Court Allows Trump's White House Ballroom Work to Proceed—For Now

Chief Justice John Roberts has temporarily halted the legal obstruction of President Trump's $400 million White House modernization project, allowing construction on the East Wing ballroom to continue while the Supreme Court considers the case on its merits.

Roberts signed an emergency administrative stay Friday, clearing the way for work to resume after what conservatives view as a predictable delay tactic from progressive groups. The National Trust for Historic Preservation and allied activists had sought to block the project, claiming it threatens the building's architectural integrity—a familiar refrain from the regulatory left whenever a Republican president wants to get anything done.

The stay is temporary. The Supreme Court will weigh the full appeal, meaning this fight isn't over. Expect the usual suspects to argue that any modernization of federal property requires endless environmental reviews, community input sessions, and deference to self-appointed "preservation" boards—all code for government gridlock.

For Colorado conservatives, this is a reminder: even when Republicans control the White House and courts, progressive litigation and regulatory capture can still bog down basic infrastructure work. The same obstruction tactics used against Trump's ballroom get deployed against energy projects, water development, and land management across the West.

Watch whether the Supreme Court rules on the merits before the next legal filing deadline—or whether this becomes another indefinite holding pattern.
