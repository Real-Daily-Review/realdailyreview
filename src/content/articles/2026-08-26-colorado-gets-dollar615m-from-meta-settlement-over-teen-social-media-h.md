# Colorado Pockets $615M From Meta Settlement, But Questions Linger on Tech Accountability

Meta agreed to pay nearly $17 billion across 29 states—including Colorado's $615 million share—to settle lawsuits alleging Facebook and Instagram exploited teen users and fueled social media addiction (Colorado Sun, CPR News). The settlement avoids trial and CEO Mark Zuckerberg's testimony before a jury in California federal court.

Under the deal, Meta must implement usage limits, nighttime access blocks for teenagers, improved age verification, and enhanced parental controls (Just The News, The Daily Wire).

**The conservative angle:** While the settlement represents a rare bipartisan win against Big Tech overreach, skeptics question whether government-negotiated payouts actually protect kids or simply enrich state coffers. Colorado's $615 million windfall raises an immediate question: Will lawmakers spend it responsibly, or treat it as a blank check for new programs? Republicans should demand transparency on how these funds are deployed—and whether they address the real problem: parental responsibility and market competition, not government mandates on private platforms.

The settlement also sets a precedent for state-level tech regulation that could expand government's role in controlling online content and user experience—territory conservatives have traditionally opposed.
