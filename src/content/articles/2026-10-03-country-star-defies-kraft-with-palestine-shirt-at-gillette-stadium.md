# Country Star's Palestine Shirt at Kraft Stadium Reignites Free Speech Debate

Zach Bryan wore a "Free Palestine" shirt during a concert at Robert Kraft's Gillette Stadium Friday, directly challenging the billionaire Patriots owner after he reportedly pressured Ed Sheeran to drop Macklemore from his tour over antisemitic remarks (Daily Caller, Daily Wire).

Bryan's shirt also featured an "Appeal to Heaven" pine tree—a Revolutionary War symbol—suggesting he framed the gesture as a free speech statement. "You're free to disagree with me," Bryan reportedly said, according to the Daily Caller.

The incident follows Macklemore's September removal from Sheeran's tour, which Kraft's influence allegedly triggered. Conservatives and free speech advocates argue the Macklemore incident raised legitimate questions about private venue owners' authority to police speech—even offensive speech—versus the principle of open discourse. The left counters that venues have the right to curate events and that platforming antisemitism crosses a line.

For Colorado: This touches on broader debates over free expression, private property rights, and cultural gatekeeping that resonate with conservative voters skeptical of corporate censorship and progressive speech policing.
