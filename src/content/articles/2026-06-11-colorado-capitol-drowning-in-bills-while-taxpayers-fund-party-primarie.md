# Colorado Capitol Drowning in Bill Overload While Taxpayers Foot the Party Primary Bill

Colorado legislators are introducing so many bills that the Capitol can barely function—and taxpayers are footing the bill for party primaries they didn't choose to support.

Complete Colorado argues the state needs hard limits on bill introductions to restore legislative focus and efficiency. The sheer volume of legislation clogs the process, dilutes debate, and forces staff to work unsustainable hours processing measures that often go nowhere.

Separately, conservatives are questioning why Colorado forces all taxpayers to finance primary elections for political parties, including those voters don't belong to or support. Complete Colorado frames this as a subsidy that violates fiscal fairness: why should an independent voter or Republican fund Democratic primary operations?

Election integrity concerns are also mounting. Complete Colorado's Jon Caldara argues that independent audits of Colorado elections would strengthen public confidence rather than dismiss voter skepticism. The state's current audit structure, critics say, lacks the independence needed to convince skeptical voters the system is trustworthy.

These three issues—legislative bloat, party-privilege funding, and election audit transparency—reflect a broader conservative complaint: Colorado government has grown too large, too expensive, and too insulated from accountability.

Watch whether the 2027 legislative session adopts meaningful bill-introduction caps and whether lawmakers move toward truly independent election audits.
