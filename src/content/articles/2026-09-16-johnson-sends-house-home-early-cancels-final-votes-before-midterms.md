# House Speaker Cancels Final Votes Before Midterms—Lawmakers Head Home to Campaign

Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) is shutting down the House voting schedule before the midterm elections, sending Republicans and Democrats alike home to campaign in their districts. According to the *Washington Examiner*, Johnson framed the early adjournment as an opportunity for members to highlight the GOP's legislative record back home—a move that also conveniently clears the calendar of potentially controversial votes ahead of November's elections.

The timing raises questions about what legislative priorities are being shelved. By canceling the final voting day, Johnson avoids forcing members to cast difficult votes on contentious issues that could be weaponized by opponents in tight races. For Colorado Republicans running in competitive districts, the move offers breathing room to focus on local messaging without Washington drama.

Democrats will likely argue the cancellation represents legislative gridlock and Republican unwillingness to tackle pressing issues. Republicans counter that members should be in their districts selling accomplishments directly to voters rather than grinding through procedural votes in a lame-duck atmosphere.

Conservatives should watch whether Johnson uses the recess to consolidate GOP messaging on inflation, border security, and energy policy—or whether the early exit signals weakness heading into what could be a pivotal election cycle for Colorado's congressional delegation.
