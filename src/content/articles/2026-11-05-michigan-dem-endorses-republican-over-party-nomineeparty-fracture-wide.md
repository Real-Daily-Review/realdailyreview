# Michigan Democrat's Cross-Party Endorsement Signals Deep Party Fracture Over Radical Candidate

A sitting Democratic congresswoman's endorsement of a Republican Senate nominee in Michigan reveals serious doubts within her own party about the Democratic winner—a stark warning for Colorado Democrats banking on party unity.

Rep. Haley Stevens (D-MI) backed Republican Mike Rogers over Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, who ultimately won the race. "I don't regret it," Stevens said, acknowledging the political cost of her decision (Washington Examiner). National Review reports that Stevens is far from alone—multiple Democrats harbor "serious reservations" about El-Sayed, suggesting the party's leftward drift is fracturing even establishment figures.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters: it demonstrates that progressive candidates can alienate moderate Democrats, creating openings for Republicans in purple districts. When Democrats nominate ideologically extreme candidates, even party insiders jump ship.

Republicans argue El-Sayed's victory despite intra-party defection proves Democrats are willing to nominate candidates their own moderates won't support. Stevens' refusal to regret her choice signals she views El-Sayed as genuinely unacceptable—a damning verdict from within.

Colorado Republicans should watch whether similar fractures emerge in 2026 state races, particularly if progressive nominees threaten to alienate swing-district Democrats.
