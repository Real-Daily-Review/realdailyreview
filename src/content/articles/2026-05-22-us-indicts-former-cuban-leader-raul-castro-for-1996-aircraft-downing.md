# U.S. Indicts Raúl Castro for 1996 Civilian Aircraft Downing

The U.S. Department of Justice has charged former Cuban leader Raúl Castro with murder and other crimes for his alleged role in the 1996 downing of two civilian aircraft, according to BBC World.

The indictment has sparked diplomatic pushback and public demonstrations. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum protested the charges at a Friday press conference, arguing the incident occurred too long ago to warrant prosecution now, according to the Washington Examiner. Meanwhile, thousands of Cubans rallied outside the U.S. embassy in Havana to show support for Castro and protest the indictment, Al Jazeera reported.

The case centers on the shooting down of two civilian planes, which killed multiple people. The timing of the indictment—nearly three decades after the incident—has become a focal point of criticism from regional leaders who question the rationale for pursuing charges at this stage.

Cuban-Americans hold varied views on the indictment, with some viewing it as long-overdue accountability while others see it through different lenses, according to BBC reporting on community sentiment.
