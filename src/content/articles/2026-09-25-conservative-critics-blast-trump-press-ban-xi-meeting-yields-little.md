# Trump Press Ban Draws Conservative Criticism; Xi Meeting Yields Few Details

**National Review and other right-leaning outlets are pushing back on Trump's decision to ban certain press outlets, arguing it undermines free speech principles that conservatives claim to champion.**

The Trump administration's press restrictions have drawn rare criticism from the right. National Review editors argue the ban "violates the spirit of a free press" and contend Trump would be "better served by confronting reporters directly" rather than excluding them (National Review). The critique reflects a conservative tension: defending First Amendment protections even when media coverage is hostile.

Meanwhile, Trump's meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping produced minimal substantive details. Both leaders remained vague on discussions, though Trump indicated talks included rebranding artificial intelligence as "super intelligence" (Just The News). The lack of clarity on trade, Taiwan, or other strategic issues leaves questions about what, if anything, was actually negotiated.

**Colorado angle:** Energy and trade policy outcomes from U.S.-China relations directly affect Colorado's oil, gas, and agricultural sectors. Vague diplomatic statements offer no clarity on tariffs or market access that matter to state exporters and energy producers.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump reverses the press ban—a move that would align rhetoric on free markets and free speech with actual policy.
