# Trump Administration Signals North Korea Engagement as Beijing and Moscow Tighten Grip on Pyongyang

The Trump administration is moving toward direct talks with Kim Jong-un even as North Korea remains locked firmly within the China-Russia axis, raising questions about whether diplomatic engagement can yield meaningful concessions from a regime that has little incentive to negotiate.

According to *Just The News*, Trump has signaled willingness to meet with Kim Jong-un, a move that follows the administration's decision to downscale planned military exercises with South Korea. Trump attributed the scaling back partly to Seoul's reluctance to support U.S. operations in Iran.

*National Review* warns that Kim Jong-un "is not our friend" and that his regime remains "firmly within the China and Russia axis"—a structural reality that constrains any bilateral breakthrough. Meanwhile, *Washington Examiner* opinion argues that North Korea actually fears genuine peace with the South, since the regime's survival depends on perpetual tension and isolation.

**The conservative concern:** Engagement without leverage rarely produces results. If the administration reduces military readiness or exercises without securing verifiable denuclearization commitments, it risks signaling weakness to both Pyongyang and Beijing. South Korea's security—and by extension, regional stability—depends on credible deterrence, not unilateral concessions.

Watch whether the administration conditions any talks on concrete nuclear rollback or whether it accepts the regime's current arsenal as a fait accompli.
