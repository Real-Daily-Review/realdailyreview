# Data Center Backlash Gains Steam, But Colorado Should Weigh Real Costs Against Growth

Colorado communities are watching the national backlash against AI data centers intensify—and the state needs to think hard before joining the anti-growth chorus.

Texas Gov. Greg Abbott recently called for prohibiting AI data centers in rural neighborhoods, signaling how quickly the issue has moved from industry circles into mainstream politics ahead of midterm elections (Washington Examiner). The backlash is real, but National Review notes it hasn't yet translated into measurable political consequences for politicians supporting the projects.

Conservatives argue the anti-data-center movement is misdirected. The Washington Examiner's analysis contends critics are targeting the wrong culprit—blaming infrastructure development rather than addressing legitimate concerns about power consumption, water use, and land impact through smarter regulation.

For Colorado, the stakes are concrete. Data centers represent jobs, tax revenue, and economic diversification for rural counties. But they also demand massive electricity and water resources in a state already managing drought and competing water claims. Energy costs could spike if data center demand strains the grid, hitting Colorado families and businesses.

Republicans and conservatives should resist reflexive opposition to development while demanding transparency: What are the actual environmental costs? Will communities see real tax benefits? Are power and water agreements sustainable?

Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to restrict data centers or craft smart incentive structures that protect taxpayers while attracting responsible investment.
