# Major Mudslides Close Roads at Black Canyon, Strand Visitors

Colorado's Black Canyon of the Gunnison National Park is dealing with the aftermath of severe mudslides that have shut down key access routes and left visitors temporarily stranded. Debris from drainage systems above the roadway buried sections of the main road, campground, and picnic areas under six feet of boulders, rocks, and mud, according to 9News Denver. At the nearby Curecanti National Recreation Area, the National Park Service reported that 21 people were temporarily stranded at the bottom of East Portal Road; most were evacuated by midnight, KDVR reported.

The closures highlight infrastructure vulnerabilities at federally managed recreation areas that Colorado residents and tourists depend on. While the Park Service managed the immediate evacuation competently, conservatives should question whether adequate maintenance budgets—and whether federal land management itself—are sufficient to prevent such disruptions. Deferred maintenance at national parks remains a chronic problem nationwide, often blamed on underfunding and bureaucratic inefficiency.

The mudslides also underscore climate and weather unpredictability in Colorado's high country, where drainage systems and road engineering must contend with intense runoff events. Local economies depending on park access—campgrounds, lodges, guide services—face real costs when roads close unexpectedly.

Watch for Park Service statements on road reopening timelines and whether Colorado lawmakers push for increased federal infrastructure investment in national recreation areas.
