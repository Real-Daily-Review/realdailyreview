# Man Pleads Guilty in Political Assassination of Minnesota Lawmaker

A Colorado connection to political violence: Vance Boelter has pleaded guilty to murdering Minnesota House Speaker Melissa Hortman and her husband, Mark, in what authorities describe as a targeted political assassination.

Boelter admitted in federal court to the June 2025 killings and also to shooting another Minnesota lawmaker and their spouse. Under a plea agreement, he avoided the death penalty—a decision that raises questions about federal prosecution standards in cases of premeditated political murder.

The case underscores a troubling national trend: violence targeting elected officials, regardless of party. While the mainstream media has often downplayed or selectively covered political violence depending on the perpetrator's ideology, conservatives rightly note that *all* political assassination attempts represent an existential threat to democratic governance and the rule of law.

Republicans and conservatives argue that such cases demand swift, certain justice and that plea agreements in assassination cases warrant public scrutiny. The focus should remain on prosecuting violent criminals to the fullest extent, not on using high-profile cases to advance partisan narratives about which political movements are "dangerous."

Colorado taxpayers and gun owners should watch whether federal authorities use this case to push for expanded restrictions on firearms or due process—or whether they focus narrowly on prosecuting the individual criminal.
