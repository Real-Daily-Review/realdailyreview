# America's Founding Creed Still Stands—But Only If We Defend It

As America marks 250 years since the Declaration of Independence, conservatives are reminding citizens that the founding document's principles of individual liberty and limited government remain under constant assault from progressive overreach.

The Declaration articulates a shared American creed that has sustained the nation through centuries of challenge, according to *National Review*. But that endurance isn't automatic. The founding principles—unalienable rights, consent of the governed, and resistance to tyranny—require active defense against those who would expand government power at the expense of individual freedom.

*National Review* also notes the Declaration carries legal weight beyond symbolic value, with real consequences for how American law should be interpreted and applied. Meanwhile, the *Washington Examiner* frames the Declaration as "the greatest public affairs campaign in American history," highlighting how the Founders translated principle into action—a lesson modern conservatives say applies to today's fights against regulatory bloat, tax expansion, and executive overreach.

For Colorado specifically, this matters: defenders of Second Amendment rights, property rights, and energy independence invoke founding principles against state and federal restrictions. The question conservatives pose is whether elected officials will honor the Declaration's vision or continue dismantling it through bureaucratic creep.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature and governor invoke founding principles when confronting the next round of progressive spending and regulatory proposals.
