# Colorado's Water Crisis: Urban Sprawl Draining Rural Livelihoods

Colorado's Front Range cities are quietly engineering one of the state's most consequential land-use disasters—systematically buying up water rights from the Lower Arkansas River Valley to fuel sprawl, leaving farmland permanently dry and rural communities hollowed out.

A new website documenting these transfers reveals the scale of the problem. As CPR News reports, water advocates are asking the hard question: "As we permanently dry up more farmland to fuel urban growth, are we building a better Colorado for our grandchildren or trading away the very things that make Colorado worth calling home?"

This is crony capitalism dressed up as progress. City governments and developers engineer water transfers that enrich a few landowners while destroying agricultural heritage, rural tax bases, and the open spaces that define Colorado. The permanent loss of farmland means permanent loss of jobs, property tax revenue for rural counties, and the ranching culture that built this state.

Conservatives argue this reflects a broader failure: government should protect water rights and agricultural land use through clear property law and market mechanisms, not allow cities to simply buy their way out of growth management. The real solution isn't transferring scarcity—it's controlling urban sprawl through zoning discipline and fiscal responsibility.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature will impose meaningful restrictions on inter-basin water transfers or continue letting Front Range metros cannibalize rural Colorado.
