# New York AG Debate Exposes James's Out-of-Touch Priorities

New York Democratic Attorney General Letitia James stumbled badly in Thursday's debate when asked straightforward questions about her reading habits and professional heroes—revealing how far removed she is from everyday concerns facing New York families.

When pressed to name a former attorney general she admired, James went silent for several seconds, unable to produce an answer. Her Republican challenger, by contrast, delivered what *Fox News* called a "zinger for the ages," demonstrating command and quick thinking.

The contrast sharpened on another question: James cited "Unthinkable," a January 6 memoir by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), as her recent read. Her GOP rival named a children's book she reads to her 2-year-old—a grounded, relatable answer that underscored James's preoccupation with Trump-era grievances over the bread-and-butter issues New Yorkers actually care about (*The Daily Wire* reported).

Republicans argue James exemplifies the progressive AG playbook: weaponize the office against political enemies rather than focus on crime, public safety, and economic opportunity. While James has pursued high-profile cases against Trump and allies, New York continues grappling with rising crime, inflation, and business exodus.

Conservatives should watch whether James's debate performance dents her standing heading into Election Day—and whether New York voters are ready to reject activist AGs who prioritize partisan crusades over constituent service.
