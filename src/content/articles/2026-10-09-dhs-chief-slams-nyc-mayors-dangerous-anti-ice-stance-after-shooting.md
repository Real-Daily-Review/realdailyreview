# DHS Chief Blasts NYC Mayor's Anti-ICE Rhetoric as 'Dangerous' After Shooting

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin is calling out New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani for inflammatory anti-ICE rhetoric following an Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer's shooting of a Dominican national with alleged gang ties in the city.

Mullin accused Mamdani of "putting criminals ahead of public safety" (Washington Examiner), arguing the mayor's words are "dangerous and incite hostility" (Washington Examiner). The DHS chief's rebuke underscores the widening chasm between federal immigration enforcement and progressive city leadership hostile to ICE operations.

Republicans argue that anti-law-enforcement rhetoric from Democratic mayors undermines public safety and emboldens criminal elements. They contend that ICE agents deserve support when executing their duties to protect Americans from criminal aliens. Democrats and progressive activists counter that ICE operations disproportionately target immigrant communities and that accountability for officer-involved incidents is essential.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado cities, including Denver, have adopted sanctuary policies limiting ICE cooperation. This clash between federal enforcement and local progressive politics mirrors tensions playing out in Colorado's Front Range, where Democratic leadership has resisted immigration enforcement while crime and gang activity remain concerns for taxpayers and law enforcement.
