# AI Safety Warnings Spark Debate Over Industry Self-Regulation vs. Government Control

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is sounding the alarm about artificial intelligence risks—but his call for the industry to "slow down" is raising questions about whether tech companies should police themselves or face federal mandates.

Amodei warned that AI could be capable within six to 12 months of leading coordinated cyberattacks that could compromise the entire internet, among other catastrophic scenarios (9News Denver). He's arguing the AI industry needs time to develop adequate safety measures before pushing forward.

The New York Post editorial board agrees the risk is real—but rejects the implicit case for government intervention. "If Amodei and [OpenAI's Sam] Altman think their work is running into risky territory, they can slow down without need for federal action—and should" (New York Post). The conservative argument: tech leaders have every incentive to prevent disasters that would destroy their own companies and industries. Regulatory overreach could simply hand control to Washington bureaucrats with no skin in the game.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts growing AI research and tech infrastructure; heavy-handed federal AI regulation could chill innovation and investment here while competitors in less-regulated jurisdictions race ahead.
