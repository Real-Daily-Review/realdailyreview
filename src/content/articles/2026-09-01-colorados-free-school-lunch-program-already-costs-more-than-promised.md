# Colorado's 'Free' School Lunch Program Already Bleeding Money—and Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Colorado's universally subsidized school lunch program is proving what conservatives warned: government "free" initiatives cost far more than advertised. According to Complete Colorado, the program required additional revenue almost immediately after launch, contradicting Democratic claims of fiscal sustainability.

The timing is brutal. As school districts across Colorado now ask voters to approve millions in mill levy overrides this November (Denver Post), taxpayers are already stretched thin by the cost-of-living crisis. Districts are competing for voter attention on crowded ballots while pitching tax hikes—even as the state's lunch subsidy experiment drains resources faster than promised.

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** Progressive policymakers sold universal school meals as a "free" benefit funded by existing budgets. Reality: it wasn't. Now districts must return to voters cap-in-hand, asking for higher property taxes to cover both the lunch program's shortfall *and* basic operations.

Democrats counter that child nutrition is a public good worth the investment, but Republicans argue the state should have been honest about costs upfront instead of disguising a tax-and-spend agenda as a freebie.

**Taxpayers should watch whether districts disclose the lunch program's actual cost when pitching November mill levies—or quietly bury it in the fine print.**
