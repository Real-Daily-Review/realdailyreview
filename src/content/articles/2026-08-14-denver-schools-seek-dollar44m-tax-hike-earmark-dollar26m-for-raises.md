# Denver Schools Seek $44M Tax Hike as Taxpayers Foot Bill for Employee Raises

Denver Public Schools is asking voters to approve a $44 million mill levy override this November—a direct tax increase on Colorado property owners. The Denver Post reports that DPS plans to spend $25.8 million of that haul on employee raises, with the remainder going to unspecified operational costs.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that Colorado schools already consume substantial tax dollars and that DPS should prioritize spending efficiency over new levies. They point out that mill levy overrides bypass the normal budget process and shift the burden squarely onto homeowners and business property taxpayers—many already squeezed by inflation and rising housing costs.

DPS officials counter that competitive salaries are necessary to attract and retain quality educators in a tight labor market. The district frames the override as essential to student outcomes.

The timing matters: this comes as National Review highlights ongoing concerns about government overreach in classrooms, raising questions about how schools spend existing resources and whether new money will genuinely improve education or simply expand bureaucracy.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether DPS provides detailed breakdowns of how the $44 million will be allocated and whether the district faces pushback from property owners already burdened by other local tax measures.
