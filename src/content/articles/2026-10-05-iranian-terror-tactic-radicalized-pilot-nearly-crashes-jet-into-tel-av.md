# Flydubai Hijacking Exposes New Iranian Terror Tactic—and Aviation Security Gaps

A thwarted hijacking attempt aboard a Dubai-bound commercial flight has exposed a chilling new terror strategy potentially orchestrated by Iran's regime, raising urgent questions about aviation security that should concern every American traveler.

Omani co-pilot Hamam al-Hammami, 29, allegedly attempted to crash Flydubai flight FZ1073 into Tel Aviv airport, telling investigators he had a "dream" to execute the attack (New York Post). Al-Hammami now faces the death penalty. The Daily Wire reports the incident has sparked concerns among Israeli counterterrorism experts that Iran may be deploying a new tactic: radicalized aviation personnel with insider access to aircraft.

Investigators uncovered deeply disturbing social media posts from al-Hammami celebrating violence against women and promoting extremist ideology—posts made while he studied aviation management remotely at a British university. The troubling gap: a radicalized individual with flight credentials and cockpit access nearly succeeded in mass murder.

**The conservative concern:** This hijacking underscores why vetting procedures for foreign aviation personnel must tighten immediately. The U.S. and allies cannot afford security theater while hostile regimes recruit operatives into critical infrastructure roles. Colorado's commercial aviation hubs—Denver International chief among them—depend on robust personnel screening.
