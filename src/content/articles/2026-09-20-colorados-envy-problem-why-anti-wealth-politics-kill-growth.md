# Colorado's Wealth-Creation Problem: When Envy Replaces Ambition

Colorado's progressive political establishment has embraced the German model of viewing successful entrepreneurs as targets rather than role models—and the economic consequences are becoming impossible to ignore.

A *Washington Examiner* analysis comparing 12 countries found a stark correlation: nations that celebrate wealth creation outpace those that treat the rich as scapegoats. Vietnam, which views entrepreneurs as visionaries driving growth, is pulling ahead economically. Germany, where social resentment toward the wealthy runs deep, lags behind.

The lesson for Colorado is uncomfortable but clear. As Democrats push higher taxes on businesses and earners—framing wealth as something to be redistributed rather than created—they're importing the German playbook. Colorado's income tax hikes, corporate minimum taxes, and anti-business regulatory climate send a message: success here is suspect.

Conservatives argue that when government treats job creators as villains, capital and talent flee. Entrepreneurs choose Texas, Florida, and other states where ambition isn't punished. Colorado loses the very people who build companies, create jobs, and fund schools through economic growth rather than government extraction.

The left counters that higher taxes on the wealthy fund essential services and reduce inequality. But the data suggests that approach sacrifices long-term prosperity for short-term redistribution.

Watch whether Colorado's business exodus accelerates as progressive tax policy deepens—and whether voters eventually demand a shift back toward pro-growth leadership.
