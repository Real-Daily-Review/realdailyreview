# Montana's Citizens United Challenge Signals Populist Revolt Against Big Money in Politics

Montana Republicans are moving to overturn Citizens United, the landmark Supreme Court ruling that unleashed unlimited corporate and union spending in elections—a stunning break from national GOP orthodoxy that could reshape campaign finance across red states.

According to the Washington Examiner, grassroots advocates in Trump-friendly Montana are leveraging the state's unique political heritage to restrict money in politics, even as conservative legal doctrine has long defended Citizens United as a free-speech victory. The initiative reflects deep frustration among populist conservatives who see unlimited corporate spending drowning out working-class voices, regardless of party affiliation.

**The conservative divide is real.** Free-market purists argue Citizens United protects political speech and corporate participation in the democratic process. But populist conservatives counter that Wall Street and corporate interests use unlimited donations to undermine Main Street priorities—a tension now playing out in a state Trump won by nearly 20 points.

For Colorado, this matters: if Montana succeeds in crafting a legal workaround to Citizens United, other red states may follow, potentially reshaping how campaigns are funded nationwide and forcing Colorado Republicans to choose between corporate allies and populist voters demanding campaign finance limits.
