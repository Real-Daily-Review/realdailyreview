# McDonald's Apple Pie Return Overshadows Real Colorado GOP Governor Race

While McDonald's celebrates America's 250th birthday with a fried apple pie comeback, Colorado Republicans are locked in a serious primary fight over who can actually govern the state.

Sen. Barb Kirkmeyer brings three decades of executive and legislative experience—she's helped write state budgets, led an executive branch, and spent years in local government. But in a GOP primary hungry for outsider energy, that resume cuts both ways. Kirkmeyer's establishment credentials could energize fiscal conservatives who want proven budget discipline, or alienate voters tired of career politicians (Denver Post).

Meanwhile, Victor Marx is running an unconventional campaign that has drawn scrutiny—and occasional incredulity—over his background claims. Marx says he's comfortable with the scrutiny and that belief in his story doesn't matter to his candidacy (Denver Post).

The contrast is stark: Kirkmeyer offers tested governance; Marx offers disruption. Colorado Republicans must decide whether they want someone who's actually managed state finances or someone promising to shake up the system entirely.

Watch whether Kirkmeyer's budget-writing experience becomes her calling card or a liability in a primary that may reward anti-establishment fervor over traditional qualifications.
