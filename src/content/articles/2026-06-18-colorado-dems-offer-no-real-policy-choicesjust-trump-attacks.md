# Colorado Democrats Offer Voters Little Choice on Policy—Just Trump Talk

Colorado's Democratic gubernatorial race has devolved into a Trump-focused slugfest with virtually no daylight between the two leading candidates on substantive issues. U.S. Senator Michael Bennet and Attorney General Phil Weiser, the frontrunners for the Democratic nomination, agree on housing, taxes, and most policy matters—leaving voters searching for meaningful differences (Colorado Sun). Instead, both are banking on anti-Trump messaging to separate themselves.

The race is tightening enough that Bennet recently loaned his campaign $1 million, signaling concern about momentum (Colorado Sun). A debate dust-up—with accusations that Weiser backed out—suggests the campaign is intensifying, though the underlying policy contrasts remain thin.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, this matters: neither candidate is offering a genuine alternative to progressive governance. Bennet leans on his record as an elected official and government insider (Denver Post), while Weiser brings his tenure as Attorney General—both representing the Democratic establishment that has driven Colorado's regulatory expansion and tax burden upward.

Republicans should watch whether either candidate breaks ranks on energy policy, water rights, or Second Amendment issues before the general election—or whether Colorado Democrats remain locked in lockstep on the left's agenda.
