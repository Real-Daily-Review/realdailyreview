# The Hyde Amendment at 50: Taxpayers Still Protected From Abortion Funding

For half a century, the Hyde Amendment has done what Washington rarely does—protect taxpayers from funding policies they oppose on moral grounds. Passed Sept. 30, 1976, the provision bars federal dollars from financing elective abortions through Medicaid, a principle that has enjoyed broad bipartisan support since its inception under Rep. Henry Hyde (R-IL).

The amendment's 50-year run represents a rare legislative victory for fiscal and social conservatives: Americans who object to abortion are not compelled to subsidize it through their tax dollars. As *The Daily Wire* notes, Congress embedded a straightforward principle into law—one that resonated across party lines at the time.

Yet the amendment faces mounting pressure from the progressive left, which increasingly views taxpayer-funded abortion as a "right" rather than a policy choice. Democrats have repeatedly attempted to strip the Hyde Amendment from appropriations bills, framing opposition as a matter of "reproductive justice" rather than what it is: forcing Americans to finance procedures they find morally objectionable.

For Colorado taxpayers and voters who believe government should respect conscience rights, the Hyde Amendment's survival matters. The question ahead: Will Congress defend this protection, or will progressive activists succeed in erasing it?
