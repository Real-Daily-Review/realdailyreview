# Second Lady Usha Vance Brushes Off Democrat's Identity Attack

Michigan Democratic Senate nominee Abdul el Sayed's attempt to weaponize Second Lady Usha Vance's Indian heritage against Vice President JD Vance backfired spectacularly—and revealed the left's shrinking playbook of personal attacks.

El Sayed, running for Senate in a state Republicans are targeting, took a swipe at Usha Vance's background during a campaign rally. Rather than dignify the identity-politics gambit, the Second Lady responded with grace, telling reporters she has always felt "loved and embraced" by her husband's family (Washington Examiner).

The New York Post characterized el Sayed's move as a "racist attack," noting that Usha Vance's measured response stood in sharp contrast to the candidate's divisive rhetoric.

The incident underscores a broader Democratic vulnerability heading into 2026: when policy arguments fail, progressives resort to personal and racial attacks. El Sayed's stumble in Michigan—a state where Republicans see pickup opportunities—demonstrates how that strategy alienates swing voters who care about inflation, border security, and jobs, not identity grievances.

Conservatives should watch whether el Sayed's misstep becomes a template for how Republicans frame Democratic Senate candidates nationwide as out-of-touch identity warriors.
