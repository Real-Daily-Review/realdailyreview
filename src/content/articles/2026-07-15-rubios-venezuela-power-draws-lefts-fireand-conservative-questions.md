# Marco Rubio's Venezuela Power Play Draws Left's Fire—and Raises Questions About Executive Overreach

Secretary of State Marco Rubio's aggressive hand in Venezuela policy—from sanctions architecture to oil revenue management—has triggered predictable outrage from the Left, but conservatives should ask harder questions about executive scope creep, even when the cause is sound.

According to *The Daily Wire*, Rubio is effectively directing Venezuelan policy from Washington, maintaining regular contact with the country's acting leadership and shaping how oil revenues flow. *National Review* frames this as proof that progressives view the State Department as a threat to their agenda—which is partly true. But the real story is more complicated.

**Republicans argue** that Rubio's Venezuela stance reflects necessary American resolve against socialist regimes and protects U.S. interests in the Western Hemisphere. His sanctions regime has teeth, and his influence over Venezuelan oil markets affects global energy prices—relevant to Colorado's energy sector and gas prices at the pump.

**The Left claims** Rubio is operating beyond proper constitutional bounds, wielding executive power without adequate congressional oversight.

Both sides have a point. Conservatives should champion Rubio's anti-socialist foreign policy while remaining vigilant against the precedent of unilateral executive power—a tool the next Democratic administration could weaponize domestically.

Watch whether Congress demands clarity on Rubio's Venezuela authority and whether Colorado Republicans push back on executive overreach, even when ideologically aligned.
