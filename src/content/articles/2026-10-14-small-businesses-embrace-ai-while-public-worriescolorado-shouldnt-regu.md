# Small Business Owners Turn to AI Despite Public Skepticism

While Americans fret over artificial intelligence's societal risks, practical business owners in Ohio and Iowa are already deploying the technology to solve real problems—and finding genuine relief from administrative burden.

April Polk, a small retailer in Columbus, discovered this firsthand when her assistant used AI to complete a grant application that would have otherwise consumed hours of her time. For small-business operators already stretched thin managing payroll, inventory, and customer service, AI tools are becoming force multipliers that free up resources for growth rather than paperwork.

The gap between public anxiety and business-world adoption reveals something important: while progressive activists and media outlets warn of AI's existential dangers, entrepreneurs are quietly using the technology to compete more effectively and reduce costs. This is capitalism working—businesses adopting tools that improve efficiency without waiting for government permission or regulation.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's small-business community, from Front Range retailers to mountain-town operators, faces the same administrative burden. If Colorado policymakers rush to regulate AI before understanding its practical benefits, they risk handicapping local entrepreneurs while their counterparts in freer states gain competitive advantage.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats propose AI regulation that could slow adoption by small businesses—or whether the state embraces a lighter regulatory touch that lets market forces drive responsible innovation.
