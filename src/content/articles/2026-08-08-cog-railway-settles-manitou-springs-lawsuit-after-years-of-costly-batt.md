# Pikes Peak Cog Railway Settles With Manitou Springs After Years of Costly Legal Battle

Colorado's Pikes Peak Cog Railway and Manitou Springs have ended a protracted lawsuit that exposed how government desperation for cash can trap private businesses in endless litigation.

The dispute centered on a 2018 settlement where the railway paid the city $1.25 million to help plug a municipal budget hole (CPR News). What should have been a straightforward transaction instead spiraled into years of courtroom costs and uncertainty—a cautionary tale of how local governments weaponize private enterprise to solve their own fiscal mismanagement.

The railway's willingness to settle reflects the brutal calculus facing Colorado businesses: fighting government in court often costs more than capitulating, even when the business has legitimate grievances. Manitou Springs' initial demand for a bailout payment should have raised red flags about the city's budgeting discipline. Instead, taxpayers watched as both sides burned resources on legal fees.

This case illustrates why Colorado businesses remain skeptical of government partnerships and why regulatory overreach drives companies to relocate. When cities treat profitable private enterprises as piggy banks for budget crises, they signal that property rights and contract certainty matter less than political expediency.

Watch whether Manitou Springs has learned to balance its budget without shaking down local businesses—or whether this settlement merely postpones the next fiscal crisis.
