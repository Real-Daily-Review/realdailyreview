# Alaska's Ballot Chaos: Two Dan Sullivans Will Confuse Voters in November Senate Race

Alaska's ranked-choice voting system just handed Democrats a gift—and Republicans a headache that could cost them a Senate seat.

Both Dan Sullivan candidates advanced from Tuesday's all-party primary to November's general election, meaning Alaskan voters will face two identically named options on the ballot. Sen. Dan Sullivan, the Republican incumbent backed by President Trump, will compete against Dan J. Sullivan Jr., a retired schoolteacher running on the Republican ticket—a move Republicans argue is designed to split the GOP vote and hand the seat to Democrat Mary Peltola (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

Trump endorsed the incumbent Sullivan and called the other candidate's bid "an attempt to trick voters." Dan J. Sullivan has denied the accusation, but the damage is done: ranked-choice voting means a confused electorate could easily split conservative votes between the two Sullivans, allowing Peltola to win with a plurality.

This is what happens when states abandon straightforward primary systems for experimental voting mechanics. Alaska's ranked-choice process, which advances the top four candidates regardless of party, created the perfect storm for ballot confusion and Democratic advantage.

Conservatives should watch whether Alaska's Republican Party challenges the ballot design or whether voter education efforts can prevent the split that hands Peltola a Senate seat on a technicality.
