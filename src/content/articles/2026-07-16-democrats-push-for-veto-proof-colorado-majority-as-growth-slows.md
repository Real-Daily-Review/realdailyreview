# Colorado Democrats Eye Veto-Proof Majority as Population Growth Stalls

Colorado's political landscape is shifting—and not in conservatives' favor. Democrats are positioning themselves to expand their legislative supermajority, potentially locking Republicans out of meaningful input on taxes, spending, and regulation for years to come (Complete Colorado).

The threat arrives as Colorado's once-unstoppable population boom hits a wall. After drawing hundreds of thousands of residents from other states between the 1990s and 2020, Colorado's growth is slowing as the Midwest and South gain ground (CPR News). That demographic shift matters: fewer new arrivals means fewer swing voters and less pressure on progressive legislators to moderate their agenda.

Republicans argue a veto-proof Democratic majority would accelerate Colorado's transformation into a high-tax, heavily regulated state—threatening the business-friendly environment that once made the state a magnet for growth. Conservatives worry about unchecked spending on progressive priorities, expanded gun restrictions, and aggressive climate mandates that could further damage Colorado's energy sector and rural economy.

The timing is critical: with population growth slowing, Colorado's tax base may not expand fast enough to fund Democratic spending ambitions without hiking rates on existing residents and businesses.

Watch whether Republicans can recruit candidates competitive enough to hold ground in suburban districts—or whether Democrats cement control of state government for the next decade.
