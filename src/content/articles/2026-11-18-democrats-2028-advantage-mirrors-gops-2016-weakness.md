# Democrats Hold Structural Edge Heading Into 2028, But History Suggests Vulnerability

Democrats enter the 2028 presidential race with commanding congressional majorities—259 House members and 56 senators—mirroring Republicans' position before 2016, according to the *Washington Examiner*. But the parallel cuts both ways.

Republicans argue the analogy reveals Democrats' weakness, not strength. In 2016, despite controlling Congress, the GOP faced a fractured primary and an unpopular frontrunner. Democrats now face similar headwinds: an aging party establishment, deep divisions over foreign policy and immigration, and a restless base questioning whether their agenda has delivered results.

The structural advantage—controlling the Senate and House—matters for judicial confirmations and blocking Republican legislation. But it hasn't insulated Democrats from midterm losses or prevented the erosion of working-class support in key regions.

For Colorado specifically, Democrats' Senate control affects judicial appointments and energy regulation—two issues where the state's oil, gas, and mining sectors face ongoing pressure from the Biden-Harris administration's environmental agenda. Republicans note that despite Democratic congressional strength, Colorado voters have grown skeptical of progressive overreach on energy and water policy.

Conservatives should watch whether Democrats can consolidate their base around a 2028 nominee or whether internal fractures—similar to 2016's Republican chaos—create an opening for Republican gains in purple states like Colorado.
