# Iran's Currency Collapses as U.S. Tightens Economic Screws

The incoming Trump administration is signaling serious intent to cripple Iran's economy through maximum-pressure sanctions, with Tehran's rial hitting a record low of 2.02 million to the dollar Monday as Washington prepared to announce what officials called an "economic D-Day" (9News Denver, Washington Examiner).

The currency collapse reflects years of U.S. sanctions and a naval blockade already strangling Iran's economy. The new round of sanctions represents a sharp departure from the Biden administration's approach of diplomatic engagement and sanctions relief—a strategy conservatives argued emboldened Iranian aggression across the Middle East.

Republicans have long maintained that economic pressure, not negotiation from weakness, is the only language Tehran understands. The Trump team's willingness to deploy financial weaponry aggressively signals a return to deterrence-based foreign policy after four years of what the right views as appeasement.

For Colorado, the geopolitical implications matter: stability in the Middle East affects energy markets and gas prices at the pump. A weakened Iran also reduces threats to American interests and allies like Israel, which Colorado's pro-defense conservatives support.

Watch whether the incoming administration follows through with the threatened sanctions package and how aggressively it enforces them against foreign banks and companies doing business with Tehran.
