# Colorado GOP Eyes Ballot Wins as Bottoms Enters Governor's Race

Three Colorado ballot measures are shaping up as potential Republican victories, with party strategists spotting openings on issues that energize conservative voters—particularly around girls' sports and transgender policy. Meanwhile, state Rep. Scott Bottoms (R-Colorado Springs), a two-term legislator, announced his gubernatorial bid, telling the *Denver Post* he believes God called him to politics despite admitting he "hates going to the Capitol every day."

According to *Complete Colorado*, GOP operatives see the ballot measures as a chance to mobilize base voters frustrated with Democratic-led progressive policies. Party chairman Krista Wadhams expressed bewilderment at Democratic support for transgender athletes competing in women's sports, signaling this remains a flashpoint issue for Colorado Republicans heading into the election cycle.

Bottoms' entry into the governor's race adds a social-conservative voice to what could be a crowded Republican primary. His faith-driven motivation reflects the values-based politics that have animated GOP organizing in Colorado Springs and beyond.

Conservatives should watch whether these ballot measures gain traction with independent voters and whether Bottoms can translate grassroots energy into statewide viability against an incumbent Democratic administration.
