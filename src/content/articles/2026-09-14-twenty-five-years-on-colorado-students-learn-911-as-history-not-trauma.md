# A Generation Removed From 9/11 Faces a Complacent America

A quarter-century after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students born after 2001 are learning about terrorism as history rather than lived national trauma—and conservatives warn that fading collective memory is exactly what our enemies want.

CPR News reports that Denver-area schools are grappling with how to teach the attacks to students with no memory of that day. Meanwhile, National Review argues the real danger isn't forgetting 9/11 itself—it's losing sight of the ideological threat that motivated it. The outlet warns that "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still," suggesting that while the immediate terror threat has diminished, the underlying conflict persists largely unaddressed in American discourse.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts significant military installations, defense contractors, and intelligence operations. A generation disconnected from the stakes of counterterrorism and national security could reshape how voters approach defense spending, border security, and foreign policy—issues where conservatives and progressives diverge sharply.

Republicans argue that robust counterterrorism education and vigilance remain essential; the left often frames such concerns as Islamophobic or warmongering. That gap in understanding could prove costly.

Watch whether Colorado schools adopt curricula that honestly address the ideological roots of terrorism or retreat into sanitized, politically correct versions of history.
