# Trump Declassifies Election Intel; China Allegedly Breached 18 State Voter Systems

Colorado election officials need to answer whether this state's voter rolls were among those compromised by Chinese actors, according to newly declassified intelligence released by the Trump White House.

President Trump released declassified documents Thursday evening detailing what he called "shocking vulnerabilities" in American election infrastructure (Fox News). The records indicate China "compromised" at least 18 state voter rolls, according to the declassified materials (Breitbart).

Trump used the disclosure to push for the SAVE America Act, which would mandate voter ID and citizenship verification—measures conservatives have long championed as essential safeguards against fraud and foreign interference. Republicans argue such protections are basic election security; Democrats have historically resisted ID requirements, claiming they suppress voter turnout.

The timing matters: the SAVE Act faces a Senate vote with a ticking clock. For Colorado, the question is immediate: Did foreign adversaries access Colorado's voter database? State election officials must provide transparency on any breaches, remediation steps taken, and whether voter rolls were compromised.

Conservatives say this declassified intelligence vindicated their warnings about election vulnerabilities and proves the case for voter ID laws. The left will likely argue the threat is overblown or that ID requirements remain unnecessary.

**Watch for:** Colorado Secretary of State's response on whether this state's systems were targeted and what safeguards are now in place.
