# Big Pharma Pushes Privacy-Threatening Changes to Federal Drug Discount Program

Pharmaceutical giants are quietly lobbying to weaken a federal drug discount program—and the collateral damage could be Colorado patients' medical records.

The 340B program, which requires drug makers to offer steep discounts to hospitals and safety-net providers serving low-income Americans, has cut into Big Pharma's profits. Rather than accept lower margins, major drug companies are now pushing policy changes that would require expanded data sharing on patient prescriptions and medical histories, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The catch: opening those records to broader scrutiny creates security vulnerabilities that hackers could exploit. Patient privacy advocates warn that the push prioritizes corporate profit protection over Americans' sensitive health information.

Conservatives face a genuine tension here. Free-market advocates typically oppose heavy-handed price controls on pharmaceuticals—the 340B program itself is a form of government mandate. But they also oppose corporate lobbying that trades public safety for private gain. The real problem: when government creates distorted markets through price controls, companies respond by seeking regulatory workarounds that often harm consumers.

Colorado patients and taxpayers should watch whether state legislators or federal representatives push back against data-sharing requirements that lack robust privacy safeguards—or whether Big Pharma's lobbying dollars win the day.
