# Turkey Arms Deal Raises Red Flags for U.S. Security

The Biden administration's consideration of F-35 fighter jet sales to Turkey has triggered sharp pushback from national security conservatives who argue the move would hand advanced American military technology to an unreliable NATO ally with a troubling track record.

National Review warns that selling F-35s to Turkey would "needlessly jeopardize American security and service personnel," citing Ankara's history of hedging its bets between Washington and adversaries. The Washington Examiner's editorial board goes further, questioning whether Turkey qualifies as a "great ally" at all—pointing to the country's past flirtation with joining conflicts against Israel and, by extension, U.S. interests.

Republicans argue that Turkey's NATO membership doesn't guarantee loyalty. The country has purchased Russian air defense systems, maintained ambiguous relations with Iran, and shown willingness to pursue its own regional agenda regardless of American interests. Selling cutting-edge fighter jets to such a partner, conservatives contend, amounts to reckless technology transfer that could eventually compromise U.S. military advantage.

The Biden administration hasn't publicly detailed its rationale, but proponents of the sale likely argue it strengthens NATO cohesion and keeps Turkey anchored to the West. Conservatives reject this logic as naive.
