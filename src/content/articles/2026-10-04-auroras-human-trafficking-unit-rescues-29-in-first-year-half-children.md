# Aurora PD's Human Trafficking Unit Shows Why Dedicated Law Enforcement Matters

Aurora police have recovered 29 human trafficking survivors in the first year of their dedicated Human Trafficking Unit—more than half of them children—demonstrating what focused, resourced law enforcement can accomplish against one of Colorado's most brutal crimes.

The unit isn't necessarily uncovering a spike in trafficking activity, according to police (CPR News). Rather, dedicated investigators with specialized training are identifying ongoing exploitation that might otherwise slip through the cracks of generalist detective work. That's the conservative case for smart, targeted policing: give cops the tools and focus they need, and they'll protect vulnerable people.

This matters for Colorado. Human trafficking networks operate across state lines and exploit our communities' most defenseless residents. While progressive activists often focus on defunding police or redirecting resources, this Aurora unit shows the opposite approach works: dedicated, professional law enforcement saves lives.

The 29 recoveries in year one (KDVR Fox31) represent real people—especially children—pulled from exploitation. That's measurable impact.

Conservatives should watch whether the state legislature and Denver-area city councils continue funding specialized units like this, or whether budget pressures and anti-police sentiment squeeze resources back toward generalist models that miss trafficking networks entirely.
