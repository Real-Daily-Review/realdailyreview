# Senate Confirms Trump's AG Pick Blanche in Razor-Thin Vote—Now Faces Fauci Test

Todd Blanche's confirmation as attorney general in a 50-49 Senate vote Saturday marks a narrow victory for Trump, but the real battle looms: whether the new AG will prosecute the contempt-of-Congress charge against Anthony Fauci or face Democratic accusations of weaponization.

Republicans Collins (Maine) and Murkowski (Alaska) broke ranks to join Democrats in opposition, leaving no margin for error. Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), whose vote proved decisive, defended the call as "a gradation of gray"—acknowledging the stakes but backing Blanche anyway (Washington Examiner).

The Fauci contempt vote now tests Blanche's independence. Democrats warn that prosecuting the former NIH director would signal political revenge, while Republicans argue Fauci defied lawful congressional subpoenas. Fox News notes the move will define whether Trump's DOJ operates as an instrument of presidential will or maintains institutional integrity.

For Colorado conservatives, the confirmation matters: a Blanche-led DOJ could reshape enforcement priorities on energy regulation, Second Amendment cases, and federal land disputes—areas where the previous administration's regulatory overreach frustrated state interests.

Watch whether Blanche pursues the Fauci case and how aggressively his DOJ tackles progressive regulatory agencies that have hamstrung Colorado's energy and water sectors.
