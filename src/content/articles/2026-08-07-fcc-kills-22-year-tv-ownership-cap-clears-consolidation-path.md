# FCC Kills TV Ownership Cap, Clearing Path for Consolidation

The Federal Communications Commission has voted to eliminate the 39% broadcast television ownership limit, a regulatory restraint that has stood for nearly two decades. The move, reported by 9News Denver, removes a barrier to media consolidation that conservatives have long argued stifles competition and innovation in the broadcasting industry.

The 39% cap was set in 2004, according to Just The News, when Congress raised a previous 35% threshold from the 1990s. Proponents of deregulation argue the rule is outdated in an era of streaming, digital platforms, and fragmented media consumption—that it artificially constrains companies while viewers have more content options than ever.

The decision will likely face legal challenges, 9News reports, as progressive groups and some lawmakers oppose further media consolidation. Democrats and media-watchdog organizations typically argue that removing ownership limits concentrates editorial power and reduces local news diversity, particularly in smaller markets.

For Colorado, the practical impact remains unclear: the state's major broadcasters operate within existing ownership structures, though the rule change could reshape the regional media landscape if larger national chains acquire additional stations.

Conservatives should monitor whether this deregulation actually spurs competition or simply accelerates consolidation without meaningful benefits to viewers or local news operations.
