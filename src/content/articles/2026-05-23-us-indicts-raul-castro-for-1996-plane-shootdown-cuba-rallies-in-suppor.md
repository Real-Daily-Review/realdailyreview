# Cuba Rallies Behind Castro as US Pursues Cold War-Era Indictment

The United States has indicted former Cuban President Raul Castro over the 1996 shootdown of a civilian aircraft, prompting thousands to demonstrate outside the US embassy in Havana in his defense (according to Al Jazeera English).

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum joined the criticism, arguing the timing was inappropriate. "This happened 30 years ago," Sheinbaum said at a Friday press conference, questioning why the US pursued charges so long after the incident (per Washington Examiner).

The indictment centers on the downing of an unarmed civilian plane, an event that strained US-Cuba relations during the Cold War era. The rallies in Havana reflect Castro's continued influence in Cuban politics and society, despite his retirement from formal power in 2008.

The move represents a rare instance of the US pursuing criminal charges against a former foreign leader for actions taken decades earlier. It underscores ongoing tensions between Washington and Havana, even as diplomatic relations have shifted over the past two decades.

Watch for whether the indictment advances to trial or faces diplomatic complications given Castro's age and Cuba's resistance to US jurisdiction.
