# NYC Mayor's 9/11 Pen Gift to Al Qaeda Defense Lawyer Sparks Outrage

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed a ceremonial 9/11 remembrance order, then handed the symbolic pen to an aide who represented a convicted al Qaeda member—a move that has drawn sharp criticism from conservatives and 9/11 families alike.

The aide in question, Ramzi Kassem, served as a defense lawyer for a jihadist convicted in connection with al Qaeda operations. Conservatives argue the gesture is deeply offensive to the nearly 3,000 Americans killed on September 11th and sends a troubling message about where progressive leadership's sympathies lie. Critics call the move tone-deaf at best, morally indefensible at worst.

Mamdani's office has not provided a substantive explanation for the decision, which has fueled speculation about the mayor's judgment and values. The incident underscores a broader conservative concern: that progressive politicians increasingly prioritize ideological commitments to criminal defense and anti-American narratives over respect for victims of terrorism and national security.

While this story centers on New York, it reflects the same progressive worldview gaining ground in Colorado cities like Denver and Boulder—where soft-on-crime policies and anti-law-enforcement sentiment have reshaped local governance.
