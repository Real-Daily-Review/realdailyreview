# Biden Admin's Hormuz Power Grab Faces Constitutional Pushback

The Biden administration's attempt to unilaterally impose tolls on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has triggered sharp conservative backlash over executive overreach—and rightly so.

National Review argues flatly that the president lacks constitutional authority to levy tolls on international waters, a power that belongs squarely with Congress under the Constitution's Commerce Clause. Republicans contend the move represents precisely the kind of unchecked executive power conservatives have warned against for years.

Meanwhile, shipping data tells a different story than Tehran's bluster. Breitbart reports that traffic through the disputed waterway has actually *increased* since U.S.-Iran talks resumed in Switzerland, directly contradicting Iranian claims of closure.

Former President Trump seized on the opening, announcing the Strait remains open after Iran agreed to renewed nuclear inspections—what he called "nuclear honesty." Trump criticized media outlets for downplaying the significance of Iran's commitment to inspector access.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy prices and supply chains matter to Colorado consumers and businesses. Any disruption to global oil markets ripples through state gas pumps and shipping costs. More broadly, this episode underscores why constitutional limits on executive power matter—even when a president claims good intentions.

Watch whether Congress challenges the toll authority or if courts intervene before implementation.
