# Trump's Press Ban Draws Fire From Right and Left—But for Different Reasons

Donald Trump's restrictions on press access are backfiring, drawing criticism from both conservative and progressive outlets—though for starkly different reasons.

The Colorado Sun's Mike Littwin compares Trump's media strategy to foreign policy failures, arguing the "war on the press" is a strategic disaster. Meanwhile, National Review—a flagship conservative publication—breaks ranks, urging Trump to rescind the press ban entirely, calling it "a violation of the spirit of a free press."

**The conservative case against the ban is straightforward:** restricting press access undermines the First Amendment principles conservatives claim to defend and hands critics a legitimate grievance. National Review argues Trump would be "better served by confronting reporters directly" rather than stonewalling—a more effective political strategy that doesn't cede the moral high ground on free speech.

The broader tension here matters for Colorado voters who value constitutional limits on government power. If conservatives argue government shouldn't overreach on Second Amendment rights or regulatory authority, the same principle applies to press freedom—even when dealing with hostile media.

**Watch whether GOP leadership distances itself from the ban or doubles down, signaling whether constitutional conservatism or tribal loyalty wins the day.**
