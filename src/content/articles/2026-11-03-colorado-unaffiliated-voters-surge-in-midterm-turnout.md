# Colorado Unaffiliated Voters Surge to Polls as Midterms Heat Up

Colorado's unaffiliated voters are returning ballots at higher-than-normal rates ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, according to the Secretary of State's office, signaling robust early turnout across the state (CPR News). The surge reflects a competitive midterm environment with control of Congress on the line for the final two years of President Trump's administration.

Republicans argue that strong turnout—particularly among independent voters—could benefit GOP candidates running on economic messaging and opposition to progressive overreach. Democrats counter that disapproval of Trump and economic concerns favor their candidates, though some acknowledge competitive races (Daily Caller).

**The Colorado angle:** Unaffiliated voters now represent a decisive swing bloc in statewide races. How these independent voters break—on taxes, energy policy, gun rights, and border security—will likely determine whether Colorado remains purple or shifts red in key congressional and state contests.

Conservatives should watch whether unaffiliated voters prioritize fiscal restraint and limited government over progressive spending agendas, and whether Colorado Republicans can capitalize on turnout momentum to flip or hold competitive seats.
