# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Michigan Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abdul El-Sayed met with the state's Democratic Jewish Caucus to apologize for past associations, but Republicans say the gesture rings hollow. According to Fox News, El-Sayed refused to fully distance himself from streamer Hasan Piker, drawing criticism from GOP rival Mike Rogers, who called the apology a "sham."

The Washington Examiner's analysis goes further, noting that despite media claims of Democratic distancing from Piker—who has made antisemitic remarks and expressed anti-American views—only six Democrats have explicitly condemned his latest statements. The outlet argues the party's response has been performative rather than substantive.

**The Colorado angle:** While this drama unfolds in Michigan, Colorado Democrats should take note. The party's tolerance for fringe voices and reluctance to enforce real consequences for antisemitism and extremism weakens its credibility with mainstream voters, including Jewish Americans who've historically supported Democratic causes. When party leaders offer "apologies" without genuine accountability, voters notice.

Republicans argue Democrats have a credibility problem: they claim to oppose antisemitism and extremism while maintaining ties to figures who embody both. Until that changes, expect Jewish voters—and independents concerned about party standards—to remain skeptical.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado Democratic leaders will face similar pressure to distance themselves from controversial figures and rhetoric within their own coalition.
