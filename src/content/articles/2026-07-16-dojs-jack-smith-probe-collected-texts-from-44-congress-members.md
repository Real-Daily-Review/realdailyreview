# DOJ's Jack Smith Probe Caught Collecting Texts From 44 Congressional Members—Even a Democrat Smells a Rat

The Justice Department's sprawling investigation under special counsel Jack Smith swept up text messages from 44 members of Congress and White House staff, Senate Judiciary Committee documents revealed this week—and the overreach is so brazen that even a veteran Democratic senator is crying foul.

Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) broke ranks to acknowledge that Republicans are "rightly concerned" about possible improper access to emails during the probe (Fox News Politics). The Senate Judiciary Committee's Tuesday release exposed the dragnet collection, raising hard questions about prosecutorial boundaries and Fourth Amendment protections.

Republicans have long warned that Smith's investigation represents the kind of unchecked federal power that threatens individual liberty and congressional independence. The revelation that DOJ operatives vacuumed up communications between elected officials and their staff—without apparent warrant specificity—vindicates those concerns. This isn't a narrow, surgical investigation; it's a fishing expedition with a badge.

Coloradans should care: federal overreach that targets Congress today can target state legislatures, local officials, and ordinary citizens tomorrow. When government agencies operate without meaningful restraint, nobody's rights are safe.

Watch whether Senate Republicans demand accountability hearings and whether the DOJ faces real consequences for the scope of this collection.
