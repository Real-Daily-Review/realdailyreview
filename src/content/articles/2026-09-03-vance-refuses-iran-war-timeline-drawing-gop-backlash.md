# Vance Doubles Down on Iran Conflict Without Timeline—Drawing Fire From Right

Vice President JD Vance is refusing to set an end date for U.S. military operations against Iran, citing ongoing threats to the Strait of Hormuz and a hardened stance against Tehran diplomacy (Fox News Politics). Vance told reporters Thursday that the administration has pivoted from negotiation to sustained military pressure—but stopped short of calling the escalating conflict a formal war.

The position is drawing criticism from an unexpected quarter: Republican hawks. Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) blasted Vance's semantic dodge, calling it an "insult to the men and women fighting" (Washington Examiner). Massie's rebuke highlights a fault line within the GOP between those demanding clarity on military commitment and those favoring operational flexibility.

**The Colorado angle:** Extended Middle East operations strain defense budgets and military recruitment—concerns for Colorado Springs' Fort Carson and Peterson Space Force Base, which rely on clear strategic direction and stable funding.

Conservatives are split on the Iran strategy. Some argue indefinite conflict without congressional declaration violates constitutional war powers; others support sustained pressure to protect energy markets and regional allies. The lack of a defined exit strategy troubles fiscal hawks worried about open-ended spending.

**Watch:** Whether Congress demands a formal war authorization or budget vote on Iran operations—a test of Republican resolve on constitutional limits.
