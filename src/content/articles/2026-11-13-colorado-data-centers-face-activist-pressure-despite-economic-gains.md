# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Plot Restrictions

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are becoming targets for progressive activists who see them as energy hogs, even as these facilities quietly power the internet, cloud computing, and AI infrastructure that modern life depends on. According to *Complete Colorado*, these centers "are not the enemy of modern life. They are what makes it run"—yet environmental groups and some local officials are pushing for stricter regulations and higher energy costs that could cripple Colorado's competitive advantage in the sector.

The stakes are real for Colorado taxpayers and workers. Data centers generate substantial tax revenue, create high-wage jobs, and position the state as a critical node in America's digital backbone. Conservatives argue that strangling these facilities with new regulations or punitive energy mandates would be economic self-sabotage, handing competitive advantage to other states while killing jobs.

Democrats and green activists counter that data centers consume significant electricity and water—resources they say should be rationed in the name of climate policy. They're pushing for tighter environmental controls regardless of economic cost.

Colorado voters and policymakers should watch closely for any legislative push to impose data-center-specific regulations or energy taxes that could drive these operations—and their jobs—out of state.
