# Fed Holds Rates Steady as Trump's Pick Warsh Takes Second Meeting at the Helm

Trump's Federal Reserve chairman Kevin Warsh kept interest rates unchanged at 3.5–3.75 percent this week, marking his second decision since replacing Jerome Powell—whom Trump had repeatedly criticized for resisting rate cuts. The Federal Open Market Committee voted 9 to 3 to maintain the status quo, according to reporting from *The Daily Wire* and *Breitbart*, though the vote revealed internal division on the central bank's monetary policy direction.

The decision came after what *The Daily Wire* described as "one of the most closely watched and unpredictable meetings for the central bank this year." Warsh's appointment signaled Trump's intent to reshape Fed leadership around his preference for lower rates—a shift conservatives have long demanded to stimulate economic growth and reduce the debt service burden on federal spending.

For Colorado, stable rates offer some relief to homebuyers and small business owners battered by years of rate hikes, though mortgage costs remain elevated. Energy companies and agricultural operations dependent on credit lines will watch closely whether the Fed eventually pivots toward cuts that could ease borrowing costs.

**Watch next:** Whether Warsh's Fed moves toward rate cuts in coming months—a key test of whether Trump's monetary policy vision gains traction or faces resistance from the three dissenters.
