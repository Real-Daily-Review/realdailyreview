# University Bias Against Christian Groups Raises Free Speech Alarm

A lawsuit alleges that a public university has systematically discriminated against Christian student organizations while granting preferential treatment to Muslim groups—a stark example of government-funded institutions weaponizing bureaucratic rules against religious liberty.

According to *Just The News*, campus chapters of national Christian organizations claim they face onerous restrictions: mandatory permission slips to pray, approval requirements for routine activities like social gatherings, and selective enforcement of recognition policies. Meanwhile, the suit contends, Muslim student groups operate without comparable scrutiny, enjoying full recognition privileges and worship freedoms.

This case exemplifies a broader pattern conservatives have documented for years: progressive administrators using vague "nondiscrimination" policies as cudgels against Christian expression while extending deference to minority religions. The double standard is indefensible.

Republicans and free-speech advocates argue that public universities—funded by taxpayers of all faiths—cannot constitutionally favor one religion over another or burden Christian groups with compliance burdens imposed nowhere else. The First Amendment protects religious exercise equally.

Democrats and university administrators typically counter that such policies aim to ensure "inclusive" campus environments and prevent discrimination, though critics say that framing masks selective enforcement.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state university systems here adopt similar discriminatory policies—and demand transparency on how student religious groups are treated.
