# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Wednesday, July 29, 2026

Colorado woke up to flash flood emergencies, a brewing election-law fight, and fresh evidence that the state's political class still hasn't learned its lessons from the Griswold debacle. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Flash Floods Hammer Southern Colorado, Hwy 78 Shut Down

The first Flash Flood Emergency in Colorado since last August hit Pueblo County Tuesday, forcing the closure of Highway 78 at Beulah Hill. This is exactly the kind of natural disaster that demands local emergency response — not a federal bureaucracy — and Colorado's rural communities deserve infrastructure funding that actually reaches them instead of getting swallowed by Denver-driven budget priorities. Watch road conditions closely if you're in the Wet Mountains corridor.

---

## Griswold Fiasco Exposes Colorado's Election Integrity Problem

The 2026 primaries delivered the second-highest primary turnout since 2010 — and yet the Griswold era leaves Colorado's election administration with a credibility deficit that turnout numbers alone can't fix. Republicans and conservatives have long argued that Colorado's election oversight has been more focused on partisan convenience than transparent administration. The fiasco makes the case plainly: it's time for structural reform, stronger accountability, and an election chief Coloradans can actually trust.

---

## Colorado Springs Sued Over April Municipal Elections

Colorado Springs is facing a lawsuit over its practice of holding municipal elections in April, with the legal challenge raising questions about voter access and election timing. Conservatives should note that election-schedule fights are rarely just procedural — they're often a vehicle for the left to reshape who votes and when, in ways that favor progressive turnout operations. Colorado Springs voters, not activist litigators, should decide how their city runs its elections.

---

## Trump Administration Babies and the Colorado School District Standoff

At least one Colorado school district is pushing back against Trump administration rules, claiming federal policy conflicts with Colorado law. Democrats and the education establishment will frame this as protecting children — but conservatives argue federal overreach into local classrooms is exactly what the administration should be pushing back against, and Colorado districts that use "state law" as a shield to resist federal accountability deserve scrutiny from parents and taxpayers alike.

---

## Wicked Weather Batters Boulder County

Hail and a mudslide struck parts of Boulder County Tuesday, adding to a summer that's already tested Colorado's Front Range communities. Meanwhile, a two-decade-old criminally negligent homicide case finally reached a guilty plea — a reminder that justice delayed is justice denied, and that Colorado's courts owe victims' families resolution, not endless procedural limbo.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado Springs election lawsuit — if activist courts start dictating municipal election calendars, no local government is safe from the next round of progressive legal engineering. The Griswold fallout isn't over either; with turnout up and trust down, the pressure for real election reform at the Capitol will only grow heading into the 2027 legislative session. And as flash floods continue to threaten rural Colorado roads and communities, watch whether state emergency resources actually reach the people who need them — or get tied up in bureaucratic red tape.
