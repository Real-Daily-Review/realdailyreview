# Cañon City's Water Crisis: Drought Exposes Infrastructure Vulnerability

Colorado's water crisis just got personal—Cañon City residents are now questioning whether their tap water is safe to drink, as algae blooms foul the Arkansas River supply during severe drought conditions (Colorado Sun, CPR News).

Low flows in the Arkansas River have allowed algae to infiltrate Cañon City's water system, producing foul-smelling and bad-tasting water that has alarmed residents. Water officials insist testing shows the supply remains safe, but the incident underscores a hard truth: Colorado's aging water infrastructure is increasingly vulnerable to drought stress.

This is precisely what conservatives have warned about for years. While environmental groups push to restrict water development and dam construction in the name of "ecosystem protection," Colorado communities face real consequences—contaminated drinking water—when flows drop. The state's refusal to invest adequately in water storage and treatment infrastructure, hamstrung by green regulations and litigation, leaves towns like Cañon City exposed.

Republicans and water advocates argue Colorado needs more storage capacity, not fewer dams. The left's obsession with "freeing" rivers has left municipalities scrambling to manage basic water quality.

Water officials say testing confirms safety, but Cañon City residents deserve transparency and a long-term fix—not assurances while their water smells like a stagnant pond.

Watch whether state water officials propose real infrastructure upgrades or simply blame drought while blocking new development.
