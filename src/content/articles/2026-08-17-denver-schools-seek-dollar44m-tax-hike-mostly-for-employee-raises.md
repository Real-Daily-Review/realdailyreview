# Denver Schools Seek $44M Tax Hike to Fund Employee Raises

Denver Public Schools is asking voters to approve a $44 million mill levy override this November—a direct tax increase on property owners—with $25.8 million earmarked for employee raises, according to the *Denver Post*. The remaining funds would support unspecified operational needs.

**The conservative case against:** Taxpayers already fund public education through substantial state and local appropriations. A new mill levy override amounts to a backdoor tax hike on homeowners and businesses already squeezed by inflation and rising property values. Republicans argue schools should prioritize fiscal discipline and reallocate existing budgets rather than demand more from taxpayers. The focus on employee compensation over classroom outcomes raises questions about whether additional spending actually improves student achievement or simply expands the education bureaucracy.

**The broader context:** This comes as national debates over school governance intensify. A Nebraska lawsuit highlighted in *National Review* challenges government speech monopolies in classrooms, arguing schools shouldn't function as "fiefdoms of government speech" divorced from parental input.

Colorado voters have rejected multiple education tax increases in recent years, signaling taxpayer fatigue. DPS will need to make a compelling case that this levy directly benefits students—not just payroll.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado voters approve the override and what it signals about taxpayer appetite for education spending amid broader cost-of-living concerns.
