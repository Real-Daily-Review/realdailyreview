# Kushner's Hamas Gambit Raises Conservative Concerns Over Terror Legitimacy

Jared Kushner's rare direct meeting with Hamas representatives has ignited sharp criticism from the right over whether engaging terrorist organizations undermines U.S. security interests and Israeli trust.

According to *Just The News*, Kushner pressed Hamas for specifics on a purported disarmament plan for Gaza, warning that Israel remains skeptical of any Hamas commitment to demilitarization. *National Review* countered that legitimizing terrorist groups—regardless of stated intentions—sets a dangerous precedent and weakens the negotiating position of democratic allies.

The core conservative objection is straightforward: Hamas remains designated a terrorist organization by the U.S. State Department. Republicans argue that direct talks without preconditions—particularly on disarmament—signal weakness and reward an entity committed to Israel's destruction. The group's track record of breaking agreements and using civilian infrastructure for weapons storage fuels skepticism that any promise of demilitarization is credible.

For Colorado, this matters insofar as Middle East policy shapes energy markets, national security posture, and America's standing with regional partners. Destabilizing negotiations or perceived appeasement of terror groups can ripple through oil prices and defense spending that affects Colorado's economy and military installations.

Watch whether the Biden-Harris administration formally endorses this diplomatic track or distances itself from Kushner's approach—a tell on whether the administration views terror negotiations as legitimate statecraft.
