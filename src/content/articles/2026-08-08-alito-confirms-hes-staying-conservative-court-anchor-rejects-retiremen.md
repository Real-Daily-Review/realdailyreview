# Alito Staying Put: Conservative Court Anchor Rejects Retirement Rumors

Justice Samuel Alito has definitively shut down months of speculation about his retirement, telling the Wall Street Journal he intends to serve "another term" on the Supreme Court. The 76-year-old justice's statement comes after NPR published and then retracted a premature retirement announcement, fueling fresh chatter about his future on the bench.

For Colorado conservatives, Alito's commitment matters. The justice has been a reliable originalist voice on Second Amendment rights, religious liberty, and limiting federal regulatory overreach—issues that directly affect gun owners, faith-based organizations, and energy developers across the state. His presence on the Court has proven crucial in defending constitutional limits on government power, from *Dobbs* to recent decisions constraining the administrative state.

The retirement speculation had circulated for years, intensifying recently as Democrats and their media allies hoped for a conservative vacancy they could exploit. Alito's firm declaration removes that uncertainty and signals continuity for constitutional conservatives who depend on the Court to check progressive regulatory expansion.

Conservatives should watch whether the left intensifies pressure on other aging justices—particularly Chief Justice John Roberts, whose recent decisions have disappointed the right on major cases.
