# Small Businesses Need Real Access to Courts—Not Just Theoretical Rights

Colorado's entrepreneurs and small-business owners face a rigged fight when taking on well-funded corporate opponents or government agencies: they often can't afford the legal battle, even when they have a legitimate claim.

The Washington Examiner argues that access to justice requires more than constitutional guarantees on paper. For much of American history, lawmakers and courts recognized that individuals and small businesses need *practical* ways to protect their rights against far better-funded adversaries. Without litigation funding mechanisms, the right to sue becomes meaningless for those without deep pockets.

This matters in Colorado, where family ranchers, energy companies, and Main Street businesses regularly clash with government regulators and larger competitors. When a small operator can't afford a lawsuit—even a winnable one—regulatory overreach and corporate bullying go unchecked.

Conservatives argue that removing barriers to litigation for ordinary citizens and small firms is a pro-market, pro-liberty position. It levels the playing field without requiring government handouts; it simply ensures that legal rights are enforceable for everyone, not just the wealthy.

Watch for Colorado legislative efforts to expand litigation-funding options and remove restrictions that keep small businesses out of court.
