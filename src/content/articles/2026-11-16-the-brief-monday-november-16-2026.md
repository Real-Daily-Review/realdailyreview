# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, November 16, 2026

Colorado taxpayers are waking up to a Front Range that's getting harder to afford and a state government that keeps making it worse. From punishing tax hikes to regulatory schemes that chase away capital, the left's grip on Colorado policy is costing real people real money — and this week, the bills are coming due.

---

## Front Range Tax Hikes Are Squeezing Coloradans Dry

Local governments across the Front Range have been quietly stacking tax hikes on top of each other, and the numbers are damning. Of the 31 municipalities in the district, 7 areas already charged a combined sales tax rate above 9% — now that number has jumped to 13. That's not economic stewardship; that's government picking the pockets of working Coloradans and small business owners who are already fighting inflation. Every percentage point added to the sales tax is a direct hit on families trying to make ends meet along the Front Range.

## Colorado's Data Center Moratorium Is Telling Capital to Get Lost

Progressive activists and sympathetic local officials pushing moratoriums on data center development may feel good about it — but the market doesn't care about their feelings. As Complete Colorado lays out plainly: what the moratorium movement accomplishes is telling capital to go somewhere else, and capital listens. Colorado should be competing aggressively for the high-paying jobs and tax base that data centers bring. Instead, the regulatory reflex is driving investment to Texas, Arizona, and beyond. This is what government overreach costs — in jobs, in growth, and in Colorado's economic future.

## SCOTUS Sends Transgender Inmate Case Back to 10th Circuit

The Supreme Court issued a "grant, vacate and remand" order Monday in a case involving a transgender inmate in El Paso County, sending it back to the 10th Circuit for reconsideration. While not a final ruling on the merits, conservatives should watch this closely — the 10th Circuit covers Colorado, and its decisions on inmate accommodation claims set precedent that affects how Colorado county sheriffs and the state Department of Corrections manage facilities and spend taxpayer dollars. The left will continue using the courts to expand inmate entitlements; conservatives need to stay engaged at every level.

## Nvidia's China Problem Lands in Trump's Lap

Why is Nvidia still selling advanced AI chips to China? That's the question conservatives and national security hawks are pressing the Trump administration to answer. The concern is legitimate: advanced semiconductors fuel military AI development, and China is not a friend. For Colorado, which hosts a growing defense-tech and aerospace corridor along the Front Range, the integrity of U.S. export controls matters directly. Republicans argue that economic relationships with adversaries must be subordinated to national security — full stop.
