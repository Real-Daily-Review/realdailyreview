# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, November 7, 2026

The blue wave may have crashed ashore this week, but Coloradans — as they've done before — showed they're not ready to hand the left a blank check. Here's what you need to know this morning.

---

## Houston Jumps the Gun on ICE — And Texas Pays the Price

Houston Mayor John Whitmire has barred city police from notifying ICE during nonviolent stops, defying the order of defeated Republican Gov. Greg Abbott. This is sanctuary-city policy by another name, and it's a preview of what emboldened Democrats plan to do everywhere they hold power. For Colorado conservatives, the warning is clear: a blue wave doesn't just change who holds office — it changes who gets protected and who gets ignored, starting with federal immigration law.

---

## Colorado's Gas System Is Being Managed Toward the Grave

The Colorado Public Utilities Commission has tightened its grip on Xcel Energy's natural gas system, putting it on what amounts to a policy-driven retirement timeline. This isn't market forces at work — it's regulators in Denver deciding that Coloradans should freeze out of natural gas whether they want to or not. Colorado ranchers, rural homeowners, and working families who depend on affordable, reliable natural gas heat should be paying close attention. This is what the energy transition looks like up close: bureaucrats pulling the plug.

---

## Coloradans Voted Democrat — Then Voted Against Democratic Ideas

Complete Colorado flags a pattern that should give conservatives both hope and a headache: Colorado voters elected Democrats up and down the ballot this week, then used citizen initiatives to reject the very policies those Democrats champion. It's a split-screen electorate — one that wants a Democratic name on the door but keeps bolting it shut against progressive governance. Republicans need to learn how to speak to those ticket-splitters, because the initiatives won't always be there to save the day.

---

## Democrats Eye the Electoral College — While the Votes Are Still Warm

Fresh off their midterm blowout, Democrats are already floating plans to gut the Electoral College. The left claims the current system is unfair; conservatives argue it is the single most important structural protection small and rural states — like much of Colorado outside the Front Range — have against being permanently governed by coastal population centers. If Democrats move on this, Colorado's Eastern Plains, Western Slope, and rural communities lose whatever remaining leverage they have in presidential elections. Watch this one closely.

---

## AOC 2028: The Left's Next Headache

Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez is being talked up as a 2028 presidential contender, but even with this week's Democratic gains, she faces real headwinds. The left claims she represents the future; Republicans argue she represents a brand of democratic socialism that has already proven toxic in swing districts. For Colorado, the question is whether the national Democratic Party lurches further left on spending, energy, and gun control — or whether the ticket-splitters described above force some restraint.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado PUC and how aggressively regulators move to accelerate Xcel's natural gas timeline — that fight will define energy costs and reliability for hundreds of thousands of Colorado households. Watch whether Denver-area Democrats, emboldened by Tuesday's results, move to preempt local citizen initiatives like the ones that just saved Lakewood residents from their own city council. And nationally, any Democratic push to dismantle the Electoral College deserves immediate, organized opposition from Colorado's Republican leadership — the clock starts now.
