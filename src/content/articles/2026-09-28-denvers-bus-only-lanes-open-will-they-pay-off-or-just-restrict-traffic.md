# Denver's Bus-Only Lanes: Another Transit Gamble on Colfax

Denver's new bus-only lanes on East Colfax opened this week after two years of construction that hammered local businesses—and conservatives should ask hard questions about whether the payoff justifies the pain.

The Regional Transportation District (RTD) completed the East Colfax Bus Rapid Transit project Sunday, opening dedicated bus lanes from Broadway to Colorado Boulevard. Businesses along the corridor are hopeful the lanes will finally drive foot traffic and sales after enduring years of disruption (KDVR Fox31).

Here's the conservative concern: Denver has spent heavily on transit infrastructure while traffic congestion worsens citywide and RTD's operational performance remains spotty. Bus-only lanes reduce road capacity for regular commuters and delivery vehicles—a hidden tax on drivers and small businesses that rely on vehicle access. The real question isn't whether bus ridership increases; it's whether Denver taxpayers get genuine return on investment or simply subsidize transit riders while gridlock spreads elsewhere.

Supporters argue the lanes will boost transit use and economic activity. Republicans and fiscal conservatives counter that dedicated bus infrastructure often underperforms projections, that two-year construction timelines suggest poor planning, and that Colorado's car-dependent sprawl demands smarter solutions than lane restrictions.

Watch whether RTD releases honest ridership and economic impact data—or buries disappointing numbers under feel-good messaging.
