# Pentagon Scrambles to Rebuild Munitions Stockpiles After Iran Operations Drain Supplies

Recent military operations against Iran have exposed a hard truth: America's advanced missile and munitions inventories are dangerously depleted. The Pentagon is now pushing defense contractors to ramp up production of critical weapons systems, according to memos obtained by 9News Denver and Just The News.

The Trump administration is working to accelerate manufacturing of high-end interceptors and explore mass-produced alternatives to rebuild stockpiles stretched thin by sustained operations. While the Pentagon disputes the severity of supply concerns, it's simultaneously demanding faster production timelines from contractors.

**The Colorado angle matters here.** Defense manufacturing and aerospace production represent significant economic activity across the state—from Colorado Springs to the Denver metro. Increased weapons production could mean job growth and contract opportunities for Colorado-based defense firms, though it also signals the military-industrial base has been allowed to atrophy under years of underinvestment.

Conservatives argue that maintaining robust munitions capacity is essential to deterring adversaries and protecting American interests abroad. The depletion underscores the cost of delayed military modernization and the folly of assuming peace dividends that never materialize.

Watch whether Congress approves supplemental defense spending to fund this production surge—and whether Colorado contractors get their fair share of the work.
