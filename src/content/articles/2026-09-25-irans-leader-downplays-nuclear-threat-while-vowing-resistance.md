# Iran's Leader Downplays Nuclear Threat While Vowing Continued Resistance

Iran's president is playing word games while his regime races toward nuclear capability—a threat that demands clarity from Washington, not Tehran's spin.

During a Thursday Fox News interview, Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian claimed his country has no "systematic relationship" with the Houthis and Hezbollah, despite well-documented Iranian support for both terror groups (Washington Examiner). He also dismissed Iran's stockpile of enriched uranium as "not a big deal," insisting Iran doesn't want nuclear weapons "at all"—a claim contradicted by years of covert uranium enrichment and ballistic missile development.

Pezeshkian vowed Iran would "keep resisting" U.S. military pressure and "find ways" to counter American actions (Breitbart). Translation: the regime intends to continue its destabilizing activities across the Middle East.
