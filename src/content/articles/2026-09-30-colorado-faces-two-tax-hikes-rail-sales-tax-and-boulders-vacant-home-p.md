# Colorado Voters Face Two Tax Hikes This Fall—One Statewide, One in Boulder

Colorado Front Range voters are being asked to approve a sales tax increase to fund passenger rail expansion, while Boulder residents face a separate ballot measure targeting second-home owners with a punitive annual tax.

**The statewide push:** Issue 7A would raise sales taxes by roughly 33 cents per $100 in purchases to expand Denver-Fort Collins passenger train service and extend routes to Pueblo (Colorado Sun). Supporters frame this as transit infrastructure; conservatives argue it's another tax burden on already-squeezed Colorado families and businesses, with no guarantee of ridership or fiscal discipline.

**The Boulder experiment:** The city is proposing an annual tax of at least $4,000 on homes occupied 183 days or less per year (Complete Colorado). Proponents claim it addresses housing shortages; Republicans counter that it's government overreach that punishes property owners and could discourage investment in Colorado real estate.

Both measures reflect the left's preference for tax hikes over spending restraint. Issue 7A hits working Coloradans at the register; Boulder's vacant-home tax sets a dangerous precedent for government micromanaging private property use.

Conservatives should watch whether these measures gain traction—and whether other Colorado cities follow Boulder's lead in weaponizing the tax code against unpopular property owners.
