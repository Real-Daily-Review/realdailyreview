# Polling Collapse in Democratic Primaries Exposes Industry-Wide Credibility Crisis

Pollsters spectacularly whiffed on Wisconsin and Michigan Democratic primaries this week, raising serious questions about whether Americans can trust pre-election surveys at all—especially heading into 2026.

State Rep. Francesca Hong entered Wisconsin's gubernatorial primary as the "clear favorite" according to polls, yet underperformed significantly (Washington Examiner). Similar misses plagued Michigan's Democratic contests. The Daily Caller reports that "polling primaries is generally very difficult," but that's cold comfort to voters and campaigns relying on supposedly scientific predictions.

The polling failures stemmed from late-breaking voter shifts, unexpected turnout patterns, and the industry's chronic inability to reach decisive voters—problems the Examiner notes are "harder-to-solve" than simple methodology tweaks. Translation: pollsters don't know who's actually going to show up or what they'll do.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters enormously. If national pollsters can't read Democratic primary voters in neighboring states, their predictions about Colorado's 2026 Senate race, gubernatorial contest, and ballot measures deserve heavy skepticism. Campaigns, media outlets, and donors have wasted resources chasing phantom leads based on flawed data.

Watch whether Colorado pollsters acknowledge these failures and adjust their methodologies—or whether they'll keep peddling unreliable numbers to the highest bidder.
