# Colorado Connection Thin in Clancy Mistrial, But Jury Integrity Questions Loom

A Massachusetts mother's defense team is demanding judicial scrutiny of a lone juror who blocked acquittal in a high-profile case—raising questions about jury deliberation standards that could ripple through Colorado courtrooms.

Lindsay Clancy's attorney filed a motion Friday seeking investigation into the holdout juror who refused to join 11 others in voting to acquit her in the deaths of her three young children (9News Denver, The Daily Wire). The case ended in mistrial when the jury deadlocked, unable to reach unanimity.

Defense counsel Kevin Reddington is pushing the judge to preserve records from all 12 jurors, signaling concern that one juror may have acted improperly or with bias during deliberations. The motion reflects a broader conservative concern: jury systems depend on reasoned deliberation, not obstruction by a single actor with an agenda.

While the Clancy case itself is Massachusetts-based, Colorado courts face identical jury dynamics. State judges and attorneys regularly confront hung juries and questions about juror conduct. If courts allow single jurors to derail verdicts through bad-faith holdouts, it undermines the jury system's legitimacy and burdens defendants with retrials.
