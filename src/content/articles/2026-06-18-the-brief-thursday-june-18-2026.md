## Iran Deal Signed — Trump Ends the War, Opens the Strait

The U.S. and Iran have digitally re-signed an agreement ending hostilities and reopening the Strait of Hormuz, with negotiations over Tehran's nuclear program set to resume. The deal largely restores pre-war conditions. Vice President JD Vance is pushing back hard against critics who say this echoes Obama's disastrous JCPOA, arguing the leverage dynamic is fundamentally reversed — carrots and sticks have switched hands. For Colorado's energy sector, a reopened Strait means stabilized global oil flows, which matters to every producer on the Western Slope.

## Vance vs. The View — And He Won

Joy Behar — yes, that Joy Behar — called Vice President JD Vance "intelligent" after his appearance on The View and suggested he should run for president. That's not a typo. Vance walked into hostile territory and walked out with a compliment from one of the left's most reliable voices. Conservatives should take note: when Republicans engage directly and confidently instead of avoiding unfriendly rooms, they win the exchange. ## Colorado's Governor's Race: Two Democrats, Zero Daylight

A new look at the Democratic primary for Colorado governor finds that Michael Bennet and Phil Weiser agree on nearly everything — housing, taxes, the policy agenda. Their campaign contrast is almost entirely built around who can better fight Donald Trump. That tells you everything about where the Colorado Democratic Party is in 2026: no serious policy vision, just opposition theater. Colorado taxpayers and small business owners deserve a debate about spending, regulation, and energy policy — not a contest over who hates Washington more.

## Colorado's Own Jenny Simpson Collapses at Track Event

Former University of Colorado standout and American Olympic track and field medalist Jenny Simpson collapsed with no pulse at a recent event in a frightening scene. Simpson won four NCAA championships as a Buff before becoming one of America's most decorated middle-distance runners. The details of her condition are still developing, but Coloradans across the state are pulling for one of their own. The Buffs claim her, and so does the whole state.
