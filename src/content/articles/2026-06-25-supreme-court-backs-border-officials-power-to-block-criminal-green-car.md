# Supreme Court Backs Border Officials' Power to Block Criminal Green Card Holders

The Supreme Court handed a win to immigration enforcement this week, ruling that border officials can restrict entry to green card holders suspected of serious crimes—a decision that tightens DHS authority without requiring proof of conviction first.

In a split decision, the Court determined that the standard for keeping criminal immigrants out of the nation is now lower than before (Washington Examiner). Conservatives argue this clarifies federal power to protect Americans from foreign nationals with serious criminal histories, closing a gap that had allowed suspected offenders to challenge deportation or entry restrictions in court.

The ruling addresses a fundamental question about government authority: where does the power to file lawsuits originate, and who gets to exercise it? (National Review). For Colorado, this matters—border security and immigration enforcement directly affect state resources, law enforcement capacity, and public safety in border-adjacent regions.

Republicans see this as overdue reform of a broken system. Democrats and immigration advocates typically counter that lowering evidentiary standards risks due-process violations and separates families without full judicial review.

**Watch for:** Whether Congress moves to codify these standards into statute or whether the Biden-Harris administration appeals for narrower DHS authority.
