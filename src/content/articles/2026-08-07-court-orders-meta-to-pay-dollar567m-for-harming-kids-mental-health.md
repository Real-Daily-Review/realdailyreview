# Meta Ordered to Pay $567M for Harming Kids—But Big Tech's Real Problem Remains Unaccountable

A court has ordered Meta to pay $567 million after jurors found the social media giant knowingly harmed children's mental health and concealed what it knew about child exploitation on its platforms, according to 9News Denver.

The verdict reflects what conservatives have long argued: Big Tech companies prioritize engagement and profit over child safety, then hide the damage. Meta's algorithms are designed to addict young users, and the company buried internal research showing the harm.

However, conservatives remain skeptical that a fine—however large—solves the core problem. The real issue isn't just Meta's misconduct; it's the lack of meaningful accountability mechanisms and parental control. A $567 million penalty is a rounding error for a company with Meta's market cap. What's needed is transparency, parental rights to monitor children's accounts, and genuine consequences for knowingly exploiting minors.

Colorado parents should note: their kids are using these platforms daily, and Meta's business model treats them as products to be monetized. The company's "safety" promises have repeatedly proven hollow.

Conservatives should watch whether this verdict prompts legislative action—or whether Big Tech simply writes the check and continues business as usual.
