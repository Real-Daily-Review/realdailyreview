# Venezuela Earthquake Death Toll Surpasses 5,000

A humanitarian catastrophe is unfolding in Venezuela, where two powerful earthquakes last month have killed over 5,000 people—a stark reminder of the regime's inability to protect its citizens or manage disaster response.

Venezuela's interim government reported late Friday that the death toll reached 5,069, with 16,740 injured, according to 9News Denver and the Washington Examiner. Recovery and debris-clearing operations continue more than a week after the June 24 earthquakes struck the country.

The disaster underscores the broader collapse of Venezuela's infrastructure and governance under socialist mismanagement. The Maduro regime's incompetence in disaster preparedness, emergency response, and basic public safety has compounded the human toll. While international aid efforts proceed, Venezuela's political instability and economic dysfunction—hallmarks of decades of left-wing governance—have hampered relief operations.

For Colorado, this serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of unchecked government expansion and central planning. The Venezuelan model of state control over energy, resources, and the economy has left the nation unable to respond effectively even to natural disasters.
