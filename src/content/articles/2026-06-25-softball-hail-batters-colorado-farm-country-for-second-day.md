# Severe Hail Batters Colorado's Agricultural Heartland—Again

Colorado's Eastern Plains and Front Range took a brutal pounding from softball-sized hail for the second consecutive day, leaving farmers and ranchers facing crop devastation and property damage that could strain already-tight agricultural margins.

Fort Morgan residents reported hail the size of eggs, while Akron dealt with flooding and Fleming experienced tornado damage, according to 9News Denver. CPR News documented softball-sized hail across "Hail Alley" on the Front Range and Eastern Plains, with one resident describing the assault: "It sounded like everything was just breaking."

Back-to-back severe weather events hit Colorado's most productive agricultural regions during a critical growing season. Farmers in these areas—already contending with water scarcity, regulatory pressure on groundwater use, and volatile commodity prices—now face potential total crop loss. The damage compounds existing economic headwinds for rural Colorado communities that depend on agriculture and ranching.

While severe weather is part of Colorado's climate reality, the timing and intensity underscore why agricultural producers need regulatory relief and tax incentives to recover and invest in resilience. Federal crop insurance will help some operators, but deductibles and coverage gaps leave many exposed.

Conservatives should watch whether state and federal disaster declarations follow, and whether relief efforts avoid the typical progressive impulse to layer new environmental restrictions on farmers already struggling to survive.
