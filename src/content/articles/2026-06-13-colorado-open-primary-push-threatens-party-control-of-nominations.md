# Colorado Primary Fight: Open-Ballot Push Challenges Party Control

A new political campaign is demanding Colorado scrap its closed primary system and let all voters choose from a single ballot of all candidates, regardless of party affiliation. The group Courageous Colorado is pushing the reform as a challenge to what it views as excessive party gatekeeping (9News Denver).

The timing is pointed. Colorado Republicans just winnowed 18 gubernatorial candidates down to two through their assembly process (Complete Colorado), while prominent Democrats like John Hickenlooper and Michael Bennet notably bypassed the Democratic assembly altogether (Complete Colorado). 

**The conservative case against open primaries is straightforward:** closed primaries let party members—not the general electorate—decide which candidates best represent their platform and values. Forcing all candidates onto one ballot dilutes party identity, empowers moderate-leaning voters to pick nominees, and hands general-election advantages to candidates who appeal to the broadest middle rather than those committed to core conservative principles. Republicans argue the assembly process ensures nominees reflect actual party membership, not media-driven popularity contests.

Open primaries sound democratic in theory. In practice, conservatives warn they weaken party coherence and favor establishment candidates over principled conservatives willing to challenge the status quo.

**Watch whether Colorado Republicans and Democrats unite to defend the current system—or whether one party sees tactical advantage in opening the field.**
