# St. Mary's Glacier Vanishes: Colorado's Climate Reality Check

Colorado's iconic St. Mary's Glacier has melted away entirely for the first time in over a century, leaving hikers near Idaho Springs with an unexpected landscape (Colorado Sun). The perennial snowfield, a fixture of the high country for generations, succumbed to record summer heat and faster melt rates than snowfall can replenish.

The disappearance marks a stark moment for Colorado's water and outdoor recreation economy. While climate advocates will cite this as evidence demanding aggressive carbon restrictions, conservatives note that Colorado's natural systems have always been dynamic—glaciers advance and retreat across geological timescales. The real policy question isn't whether to impose economy-crushing emissions mandates, but how Colorado adapts: investing in water storage, forest management to reduce catastrophic wildfires that accelerate snowmelt, and responsible energy development that keeps power affordable as demand rises.

Visitors to Clear Creek County are already discovering the change firsthand (KDVR Fox31), turning what was a reliable alpine destination into a cautionary tale about Colorado's changing climate. Future snowfalls *can* rebuild the glacier, but that depends on winter precipitation patterns—not government policy.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether state Democrats use this moment to justify new environmental regulations or carbon taxes that would burden Colorado's energy sector and working families.
