# Medicare Proton Therapy Cuts Threaten Cancer Patients, Warn Providers

Federal reimbursement cuts to proton therapy—a precision cancer treatment—are forcing medical centers to choose between patient care and financial survival, according to providers quoted in the *Washington Examiner*.

Proton therapy delivers radiation directly to tumors while sparing surrounding healthy tissue, making it especially valuable for pediatric cancer patients. One Southwest Florida center highlighted the case of an 11-year-old with brain cancer who needed six weeks of daily treatment. The precision approach protects developing brain tissue that conventional radiation would damage.

Medicare's new reimbursement formula cuts payments by roughly 30 percent, providers say. That math doesn't work for specialized cancer centers already operating on thin margins. The result: fewer facilities offering the treatment, longer patient wait times, and reduced access for seniors and Medicare beneficiaries nationwide.

**The conservative concern:** Government price-fixing in healthcare—even well-intentioned—creates shortages and reduces innovation. When Washington dictates reimbursement rates without regard to actual treatment costs, providers exit the market. Patients lose access to cutting-edge care. This is regulatory overreach masquerading as cost control.

Colorado cancer patients on Medicare should watch whether state lawmakers push back on federal reimbursement policies or whether they'll accept Washington's one-size-fits-all approach to healthcare pricing.
