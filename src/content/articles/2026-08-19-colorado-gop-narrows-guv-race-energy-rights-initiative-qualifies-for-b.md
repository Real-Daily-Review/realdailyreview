# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary to just two candidates after the state assembly, a sharp culling that reflects party discipline—or, critics might say, establishment gatekeeping. Only two of 18 hopefuls cleared the assembly hurdle, according to *Complete Colorado*, forcing the broader field into a competitive primary that will test whether grassroots conservatives can challenge the party's preferred picks.

Meanwhile, Colorado voters will weigh Initiative 177 this fall, a ballot measure that would enshrine a constitutional "right to natural gas" championed by Advance Colorado. The initiative qualified for the ballot via the Colorado Secretary of State's office, setting up a direct clash over energy policy and state authority.

The natural gas measure matters enormously for Colorado's economy and working families. Conservatives argue that enshrining energy rights protects Colorado's oil and gas industry—a major employer and tax revenue source—from regulatory strangulation by progressive legislatures and unelected bureaucrats. The left counters that such constitutional protections lock in fossil fuels and hamper climate goals. Expect heavy spending on both sides.

**Watch:** whether the two assembly-approved GOP candidates can unite the broader conservative base, and whether Initiative 177 survives a well-funded progressive opposition campaign targeting Colorado's energy workers and taxpayers.
