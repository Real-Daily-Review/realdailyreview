# Westminster Trail Safety Crisis: Motorized Vehicles Endanger Cyclists, Raise Questions About Enforcement

Colorado's popular recreational trails are becoming danger zones as motorized vehicles illegally operate on paths designed for pedestrians and non-motorized users. FOX31 Denver documented motorized vehicles traveling the Big Dry Creek Trail in Westminster after receiving complaints from frequent trail users concerned about rider safety.

The incident raises a critical question: who enforces trail regulations, and why are violators operating with apparent impunity on public pathways? Trail systems across Colorado depend on clear rules and consistent enforcement to protect users—cyclists, runners, and families—from reckless operators.

Westminster and other Front Range municipalities must clarify which agencies have jurisdiction over trail violations and ensure adequate patrols. Without enforcement teeth, posted rules become suggestions, and safety becomes a secondary concern.

Conservatives argue that government's primary responsibility is protecting public safety and property rights—including the right of law-abiding citizens to use trails without hazard. When enforcement fails, residents lose confidence in local government's basic competence.

The broader Colorado angle: as trail usage explodes across the state, municipalities must choose between reactive complaints and proactive management. Taxpayers fund these recreational assets; they deserve protection from abuse.

Watch whether Westminster city officials commit to visible enforcement and publish violation data—or whether this becomes another example of government managing public resources by complaint rather than by design.
