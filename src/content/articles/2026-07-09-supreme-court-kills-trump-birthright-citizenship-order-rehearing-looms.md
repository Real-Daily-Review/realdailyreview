# Supreme Court Blocks Trump's Birthright Citizenship Order; Legal Fight Continues

The Supreme Court has rejected President Trump's executive order targeting birthright citizenship, dealing a significant blow to his immigration agenda and leaving Colorado's border security concerns unresolved.

Trump's legal team announced it will immediately seek a rehearing, calling the ruling "insane" (Washington Examiner). The majority of justices found the executive order unconstitutional, citing 128 years of precedent under the 14th Amendment. National Review notes that overturning such longstanding stare decisis requires more than a close case and divided dissenters—a high bar the Court determined had not been met.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans contend that birthright citizenship as currently interpreted incentivizes illegal immigration and strains public resources. Trump's order aimed to redefine citizenship rules for children born to non-citizen parents, a policy conservatives argue would deter illegal border crossings and reduce unfunded liabilities on states like Colorado, which bears significant costs for emergency services, education, and social programs serving undocumented populations.

**Democrats counter** that the 14th Amendment's text is clear and that unilateral executive action on citizenship violates constitutional separation of powers.

For Colorado taxpayers and border communities, the ruling means continued pressure on state budgets absorbing federal immigration policy failures. Watch whether Trump's legal team can craft a narrower rehearing petition that might gain traction with the Court's conservative majority.
