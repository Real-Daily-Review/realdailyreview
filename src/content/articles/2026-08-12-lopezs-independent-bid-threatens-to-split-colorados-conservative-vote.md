# Colorado's Independent Governor Race Just Got Complicated

Greg Lopez, a former Republican congressman and repeat gubernatorial candidate, has officially qualified for Colorado's 2026 governor ballot as an independent—a move that could fracture the conservative vote and hand the race to the Democratic incumbent.

Lopez's independent bid mirrors a national trend: Arizona's Democratic Governor Katie Hobbs selected former Republican John Giles as her running mate (Daily Caller), signaling how both parties are now courting cross-party candidates to appear moderate ahead of 2026.

Republicans argue Lopez's candidacy risks splitting the anti-incumbent vote. Victor Marx, the GOP's likely nominee, faces a crowded field where Lopez could siphon conservative and independent voters frustrated with both major parties. In a three-way race, that's a recipe for Democratic victory—especially in a state trending purple.

Democrats will frame Lopez's independent status as proof that Republicans are fractious and unstable. Conservatives counter that Lopez's repeat runs and party-switching reflect personal ambition over principle, not principled disagreement.

For Colorado taxpayers, the stakes are clear: a divided right-of-center vote could lock in four more years of Democratic control over state spending, energy policy, and regulatory expansion.

Watch whether Lopez's polling forces Marx to sharpen his message on taxes, water rights, and energy development—or whether the conservative base consolidates behind one candidate before November 2026.
