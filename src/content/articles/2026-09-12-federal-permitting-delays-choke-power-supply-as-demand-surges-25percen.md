# Federal Red Tape Is Strangling America's Power Supply—and Colorado Pays the Price

Washington's Byzantine permitting rules are blocking the power generation America desperately needs while demand surges 25% over the next decade, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Domestic manufacturing expansion, artificial intelligence, and vehicle electrification are straining a grid designed for the 20th century—yet federal bureaucracy keeps new capacity offline.

The problem hits Colorado hard. Energy workers, manufacturers, and data centers need reliable, affordable power to compete. Instead, they face years of regulatory delays that push projects to friendlier states. Republicans argue that streamlined permitting and interconnection reform are essential to meet demand without sacrificing reliability or affordability. The Biden-Harris administration's approach—prioritizing environmental reviews over grid urgency—has left America with insufficient generation capacity and rising electricity costs.

Conservatives say the solution is clear: cut permitting timelines, reduce environmental litigation opportunities, and let energy companies build. Colorado's economy depends on competitive power costs. Without reform, manufacturers and tech firms will relocate to states with faster, smarter energy policy.

Watch whether Congress acts on permitting reform before the next administration takes office—and whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for rules that let Colorado energy producers meet national demand.
