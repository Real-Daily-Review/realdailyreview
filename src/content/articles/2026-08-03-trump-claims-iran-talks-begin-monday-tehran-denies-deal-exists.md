# Trump Claims Iran Talks Set to Begin—Tehran Denies It

President Trump announced Sunday that negotiations with Iran would restart Monday to address nuclear weapons and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, claiming a deal is "imminent" after he postponed what he called a potentially massive U.S. military strike (Breitbart). The move reflects Trump's stated preference for diplomacy over escalation, backed by allied pressure to hold off on strikes.

But Iran's government immediately rejected Trump's account, flatly denying that any talks were scheduled or planned (New York Post). The contradiction raises questions about whether either side is genuinely committed to the negotiating table or simply positioning for domestic and international audiences.

For Colorado, the stakes are real: instability in the Middle East and disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz directly affect energy prices at the pump and heating costs for families and businesses across the state. Any deal—or failure to reach one—will ripple through Colorado's economy and energy sector.

Conservatives argue that Trump's willingness to use military force as leverage, combined with his pivot to talks, represents strength-through-strength diplomacy. Critics counter that mixed signals and Iranian denials suggest the administration's negotiating position may be weaker than claimed.

Watch whether Iran actually shows up Monday and what concessions, if any, the U.S. offers on sanctions relief.
