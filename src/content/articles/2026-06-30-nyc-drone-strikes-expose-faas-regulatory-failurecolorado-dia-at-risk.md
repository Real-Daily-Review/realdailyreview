# Drone Collisions Near NYC Airports Expose Dangerous Regulatory Vacuum

Federal regulators have allowed drone proliferation to outpace safety enforcement, and two near-catastrophic incidents at New York airports in a single day prove it. A JetBlue Airbus A321 struck a drone while landing at JFK after an overnight flight from Las Vegas, and hours later a helicopter pilot reported a near-miss with a remote-controlled aircraft (9News Denver, New York Post).

These aren't isolated incidents—they're symptoms of the FAA's failure to establish and enforce clear airspace rules as consumer drones have become ubiquitous. Republicans and aviation safety advocates argue the agency has been too slow to implement meaningful restrictions on where and how drones can operate near commercial flight corridors.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's busy airspace—including Denver International Airport, one of the nation's busiest—faces identical risks. DIA handles over 60 million passengers annually; a drone strike at altitude could be catastrophic.

Democrats and drone-industry advocates typically resist stricter regulations, citing innovation and commerce concerns. But conservatives argue public safety must come first: no amount of tech-sector growth justifies risking passenger lives.

The FAA must clarify enforcement authority and establish enforceable no-fly zones around all major airports—or Congress should mandate it.
