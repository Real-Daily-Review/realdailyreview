# Real Daily Review — The Brief
**Friday, May 8, 2026**

---

Friday opens with cascading crises on multiple fronts: a volcanic disaster in Indonesia, a cyberattack disrupting American classrooms, and a federal court rebuke of the administration's cuts to academic funding.

---

## Indonesia's Mount Dukono Eruption Kills Three, Leaves 10 Missing

An eruption of Mount Dukono on Indonesia's eastern Halmahera island has killed three hikers — including two foreign nationals — and left 10 others missing, authorities say. The early-morning eruption sent an ash cloud miles into the air. Search and rescue operations are ongoing in difficult terrain, with the missing hikers' whereabouts still unknown.

---

## Cyberattack Takes Down Canvas Learning Platform Nationwide

A cyberattack has knocked the Canvas learning management system offline, disrupting thousands of schools and universities across the United States. Canvas is widely used by K-12 districts and higher-education institutions to manage coursework, assignments, and communications. No group has claimed responsibility, and the scope of the breach or the timeline for restoration has not been publicly confirmed.

---

## Federal Judge Rules DOGE Humanities Grant Cuts "Unlawful"

A federal judge has found that the Trump administration's elimination of humanities grants — carried out through DOGE — was unlawful. Three major scholarly organizations had challenged the cuts in court. The ruling adds to a growing body of legal setbacks for the administration's effort to reduce federal funding for academic and cultural programs.

---

## FEMA Review Council Recommends Major Agency Overhaul

A council appointed by President Trump has proposed sweeping changes to FEMA's disaster relief operations. The recommendations call for significant restructuring of how the agency responds to disasters. The proposal comes amid ongoing debate over federal versus state responsibility for emergency management, a tension that has intensified following recent large-scale natural disasters.

---

## Two Australian Women Charged With Crimes Against Humanity Over IS Links

Two Australian women with alleged ties to the Islamic State have been charged with crimes against humanity following their return from Syria. A third woman was separately charged with joining a terrorist organization. The cases mark a significant step in Australia's legal efforts to prosecute citizens who traveled to conflict zones to join extremist groups.

---

## What to Watch

Search-and-rescue efforts on Mount Dukono will be the immediate focus as Indonesian authorities work to locate the 10 missing hikers, with weather and ash conditions likely to affect operations. On the legal front, the humanities-grants ruling will be closely watched for whether the administration appeals or signals a broader policy shift on DOGE-directed cuts. In the U.K., final results from Thursday's local council and devolved-government elections will clarify the scale of Labour's losses and the pressure on Prime Minister Starmer's leadership.
