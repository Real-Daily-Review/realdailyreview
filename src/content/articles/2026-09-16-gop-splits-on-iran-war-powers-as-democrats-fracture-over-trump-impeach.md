# House Republicans Break Ranks on Iran War Powers; Democrats Split on Trump Impeachment

Bipartisan fractures are widening on Capitol Hill as seven House Republicans joined Democrats to pass a war powers resolution constraining Trump's military engagement in Iran beyond a 60-day window (Fox News Politics). The move signals GOP resistance to executive war-making authority—a position conservatives have long championed when opposing Democratic presidents, though application here proves inconsistent.

Meanwhile, 64 House Democrats, including Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, voted with Republicans to table an impeachment attempt against Trump (Just The News), exposing deep divisions within the Democratic caucus over whether removal efforts serve electoral strategy or constitutional duty.

Republicans are already sharpening messaging on healthcare, with National Review urging the party to campaign aggressively against Medicare for All, warning voters of "crippling tax hikes" and disrupted access for hundreds of millions.

For Colorado conservatives, these votes matter: the Iran resolution constrains executive power (a principle worth defending consistently), while Democratic fractures on impeachment suggest the left's Trump obsession is politically exhausted. Colorado's Republican delegation should clarify whether they prioritize constitutional limits on war powers equally under all administrations—or risk appearing partisan.
