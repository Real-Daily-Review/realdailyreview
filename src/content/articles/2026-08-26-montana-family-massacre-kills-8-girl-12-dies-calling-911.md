# Montana Family Massacre Kills 8, Including Four Children

A man opened fire on his own family during a Sunday dinner gathering in Billings, Montana, killing eight relatives across four generations—from a great-grandmother in her 90s to a 7-year-old girl, according to 9News Denver. The suspect, identified as Alan Smith, fired on the blended family assembled at a home; a 12-year-old girl, Charlotte Ghekiere, attempted to call 911 to alert authorities but was killed in the attack, the New York Post reports.

The tragedy underscores a hard reality: no gun law stops a determined killer bent on murdering his own family. While progressives inevitably call for more restrictions, the facts remain: background checks wouldn't have prevented this; red-flag laws depend on someone reporting concerns beforehand; and existing prohibitions on felons owning firearms are already on the books. This was a failure of family intervention and law enforcement awareness, not a failure of Second Amendment rights.

For Colorado, the case is a grim reminder that mass violence often stems from personal grievance and mental health crises—not from legal gun ownership by law-abiding citizens. Coloradans should resist the reflexive push for new gun controls and instead demand better mental health resources and threat assessment protocols.
