# Nation's Civic Fabric Fraying as America 250 Looms

America's 250th birthday arrives amid a crisis of confidence: two in five Americans doubt the republic will survive another quarter-century as a unified nation, according to recent polling cited by the *Washington Examiner*. The threat isn't external—it's internal decay of shared civic purpose and strategic restraint.

The numbers are sobering. Two-thirds of Democrats now view socialism more favorably than capitalism, signaling a fundamental ideological fracture that extends far beyond typical partisan disagreement. For Colorado conservatives, this reflects the broader leftward drift of Democratic leadership on spending, regulation, and the role of government—priorities that directly shape state policy on energy development, water rights, and tax burdens on working families.

Republicans argue the nation's founding principles of limited government and individual liberty have been systematically eroded by progressive expansion of federal power. Without a return to constitutional restraint and shared commitment to the republic's core values, they warn, institutional collapse becomes plausible.

Meanwhile, the Trump administration marked America 250 celebrations with a test flight of a new presidential aircraft, underscoring the ceremonial aspects of the milestone even as deeper structural questions loom.
