# FDA Backtracks on Colorado Lettuce Blame, but Damage to Local Producer Lingers

The FDA quietly retracted its claim that lettuce from Taylor Farms' Colorado Springs warehouse caused a five-state cyclospora outbreak—but the agency's initial false positive has already hammered a major Colorado agricultural operation. According to the Colorado Sun, lab testing produced a "false positive" that wrongly implicated the produce company, yet the agency's initial accusation spread faster than the correction.

This is exactly the kind of regulatory overreach conservatives warn about. A single government lab error triggered immediate reputational damage to a Colorado business and likely cost the company sales and customer confidence. The FDA's sloppy testing—and slow retraction—underscores why heavy-handed food safety bureaucracy often does more harm than good without delivering proportional safety gains.

Meanwhile, 9News reports the cyclospora outbreak itself continues widening across multiple states, suggesting the real source remains unidentified. That's the real problem: the FDA blamed the wrong target, wasted investigative resources, and left Colorado's agricultural sector vulnerable to collateral damage from federal bungling.

Colorado taxpayers and ranchers should watch whether Taylor Farms pursues damages against the FDA for the false accusation—and whether Congress finally demands accountability for agency negligence that harms American businesses.
