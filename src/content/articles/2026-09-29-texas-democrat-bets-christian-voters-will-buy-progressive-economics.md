# Colorado Conservatives Should Watch Texas Senate Race—It's a Test of Progressive Faith Politics

A Texas Democrat is betting that Christian voters will embrace progressive economics if wrapped in religious language. James Talarico, the Democratic candidate challenging Republican Ken Paxton for Texas's U.S. Senate seat, is running as a self-styled Christian progressive—arguing that Democratic policies on spending, regulation, and redistribution align with Gospel values.

National Review and The Daily Wire both flag the strategy as unconvincing. National Review notes the convenient coincidence: "Jesus agrees with the Senate candidate about important planks of the Democratic platform circa 2026." The Daily Wire similarly scrutinizes Talarico's attempt to claim Christian authority for left-wing positions, calling it "The Gospel According To James Talarico"—suggesting the framing is more political theater than theological argument.

Conservatives argue this reflects a broader Democratic playbook: repackaging big-government policies as moral imperatives to win back religious voters lost in recent cycles. Republicans counter that Christian stewardship means personal charity and limited government—not government-mandated wealth redistribution.

**Colorado angle:** If Talarico's "Christian progressive" messaging gains traction in Texas, expect Colorado Democrats to test similar appeals to evangelical and Catholic voters in 2026 state races and the presidential contest.

**Watch:** Whether Talarico's faith-based progressive framing moves the needle with Texas voters—or whether it signals desperation in a party struggling to reclaim religious America.
