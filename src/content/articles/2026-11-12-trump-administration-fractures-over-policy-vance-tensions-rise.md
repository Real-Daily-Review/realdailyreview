# Trump Administration Fractures Over Policy, Foreign Relations

President Trump's inner circle is splintering over major policy questions, signaling rough waters ahead for his second term agenda.

Reports indicate tension between Trump and Vice President JD Vance on a "hugely important policy question," according to *National Review*, with the disagreement creating particular vulnerability for Vance in his new role. Meanwhile, Trump's recent amnesty discussions with Democrats have drawn skepticism from conservatives, who argue (per *National Review*) that "it's hard to see how there'd be any deal in the offing" given fundamental GOP-Democrat divisions on immigration enforcement.

Internationally, Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu reportedly had a heated phone conversation about Iran policy last weekend, *Washington Examiner* reports. While the story has been downplayed in recent days, the troubled exchange underscores friction in a key alliance.

**The Colorado angle:** Border security and immigration enforcement remain top concerns for Colorado Republicans and taxpayers wary of federal overreach and unfunded mandates. Any amnesty deal would likely face fierce GOP resistance in Congress.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump's internal disagreements—particularly with Vance—harden into public splits that weaken GOP messaging on immigration, foreign policy, and executive authority heading into 2026 midterms.
