# Colorado Sees Uptick in Cyclosporiasis Cases, But State Health Officials Say No Outbreak Risk

Colorado has recorded 178 cases of cyclosporiasis—a parasitic infection causing severe gastrointestinal illness—but state health authorities say there's no imminent outbreak threat, according to the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (9News Denver).

The parasite, which causes what sources describe as acute diarrheal illness, typically infects produce contaminated by human feces, usually through irrigation or washing with tainted water (9News Denver). Most of Colorado's cases are tied to international travel rather than local transmission, the state health department told the Colorado Sun.

Federal health officials are investigating a broader outbreak pattern across multiple states, though specific Colorado-origin cases remain limited. The distinction matters: travel-related cases don't signal systemic food-safety failures in Colorado's agricultural or retail supply chains.

Conservatives should note that food-safety oversight involves multiple layers of federal and state regulation. While cyclosporiasis is rare in the U.S., the incident underscores why agricultural producers and food handlers need clear, predictable rules—not expanding bureaucratic mandates that drive up costs without measurable safety gains. Colorado's farming and food-processing sectors operate under strict FDA and state guidelines; policymakers should resist using isolated cases to justify new regulatory burdens on producers.
