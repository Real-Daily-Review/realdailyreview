# Conservative Colorado Honors Ed Feulner's Half-Century of Institution-Building

Ed Feulner, the towering figure who built the Heritage Foundation into America's most influential conservative think tank, was remembered last week at Colorado Christian University as the architect of the modern conservative movement—not a headline chaser, but an institution builder.

Former Vice President Mike Pence keynoted the William L. Armstrong Award dinner at CCU, celebrating Feulner's five decades of intellectual leadership. Pence's framing was telling: "While others chased headlines, Ed Feulner built institutions." That distinction matters. Feulner understood that lasting political change requires durable organizations, rigorous policy work, and long-term commitment to principle—not viral moments or cable-news theatrics.

The Heritage Foundation, which Feulner led, has shaped conservative policy across Republican administrations and remains a powerhouse in battles over regulation, spending, and constitutional governance. For Colorado conservatives, Feulner's legacy underscores a crucial lesson: winning the culture and the policy arena requires sustained institutional investment, not just electoral victories.

As the conservative movement navigates internal debates over direction and strategy, Feulner's example—building think tanks, training policy experts, publishing rigorous research—offers a roadmap for the next generation of movement leaders.

Watch for how Colorado's conservative institutions leverage Feulner's legacy to shape state and national policy debates ahead.
