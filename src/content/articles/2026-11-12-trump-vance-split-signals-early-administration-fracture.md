# Trump Administration Fractures Over Key Policy Splits

The Trump-Vance administration is already showing cracks over major policy disagreements that could undermine conservative priorities heading into 2025.

Vice President JD Vance finds himself at odds with President Trump on what National Review describes as "a hugely important policy question"—a dangerous position for any VP. The specifics matter: when a president and vice president diverge on core issues, it signals internal disarray to Congress, allies, and adversaries alike.

Adding to the friction, Trump's recent amnesty overtures to Democrats have drawn skepticism from the right. National Review notes it's "hard to see how there'd be any deal in the offing with Democrats," suggesting the White House may be chasing a compromise that abandons conservative immigration principles without securing Democratic support in return.

Meanwhile, reports of a heated phone call between Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu over Iran policy (per the Washington Examiner) reveal tension on the international stage just weeks into the administration.

For Colorado conservatives, these internal divisions matter: unclear White House direction on immigration, energy policy, and foreign affairs creates uncertainty for businesses, ranchers, and workers counting on consistent leadership. A fractured administration struggles to push back against progressive overreach at the state level.

**Watch whether Trump and Vance can resolve their differences publicly—or whether this becomes a full-blown rift that weakens conservative leverage in Congress.**
