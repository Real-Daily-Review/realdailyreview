# Conservative Money Floods Colorado to Block Steep Tax Hike on Earners

Millions in untraceable funding is pouring into Colorado to oppose Amendment 87, a graduated income tax that would hammer earners above $500,000 and businesses—and conservatives aren't apologizing for it.

The measure, projected to generate $2 billion annually, represents exactly the kind of class-warfare tax policy Colorado voters should reject, according to opponents (Colorado Sun, CPR News). Complete Colorado frames it bluntly: "class envy disguised as tax policy," designed to sell a tax increase on working families as something else entirely.

The spending battle is heating up as legal questions mount. A voter lawsuit challenges the Secretary of State's ballot certification of Amendment 87 over signature validity concerns, adding uncertainty to the measure's status (KDVR Fox31).

**The Colorado angle:** This isn't abstract politics. Amendment 87 would directly target Colorado's entrepreneurs, small-business owners, and high-earning professionals—the very people driving job creation and tax revenue. A $2 billion annual tax grab would reshape Colorado's competitive advantage in the Mountain West and likely trigger capital flight to lower-tax states.

Democrats frame the measure as necessary revenue for schools and infrastructure. Republicans counter it's a dangerous precedent that punishes success and will ultimately shrink the tax base.

Watch whether the signature challenge derails Amendment 87 before November—or whether Colorado voters reject it outright.
