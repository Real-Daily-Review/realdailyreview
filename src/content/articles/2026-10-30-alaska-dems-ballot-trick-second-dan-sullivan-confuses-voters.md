# Alaska Democrats' Ballot Trick Exposes Desperation as Same-Name Challenger Confuses Voters

Alaska Democrats are resorting to a transparently dirty tactic: placing a second Dan Sullivan on the ballot to siphon votes from Republican Senator Dan Sullivan and hand the seat to their preferred candidate. According to Fox News Politics, Alaska's Division of Elections reported roughly 58,000 early ballots cast while the same-name independent challenger competed for attention—nearly one in 10 Alaskans voting early.

The scheme is so blatant that even left-leaning voices are calling it out. Hosts on ABC's "The View" blasted the move as a "shady tactic" designed to confuse voters, per Breitbart—a rare moment of bipartisan agreement that ballot manipulation is corrosive to democracy.

Republicans argue this stunt reveals how far Democrats will go when they can't win on policy. Rather than make their case to Alaskan voters on energy development, resource management, or fiscal responsibility, they're banking on voter confusion and electoral chicanery.

**The Colorado angle:** While Alaska's ballot chaos unfolds 2,000 miles away, Colorado Republicans should watch closely. As Democrats control state elections here, similar ballot-engineering tactics could emerge in tight races—especially in competitive congressional districts where vote-splitting could decide outcomes.
