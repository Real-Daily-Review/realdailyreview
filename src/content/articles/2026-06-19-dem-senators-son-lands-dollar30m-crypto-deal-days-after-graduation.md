# Dem Senator's Son Lands $30M Crypto Startup Deal—Timing Raises Eyebrows

A 22-year-old with no industry experience shouldn't be able to raise $30 million for a financial startup, but the son of a sitting U.S. senator apparently can.

Theodore Gillibrand, son of Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand (D-NY), has secured $30 million in funding for a derivatives trading platform valued at $300 million—less than a week after graduating college, according to *The Daily Wire* and *Just The News*. The timing is raising red flags among conservatives watching Washington's revolving door of influence and family connections.

The optics are particularly sharp because Gillibrand herself has positioned herself as one of crypto's most influential allies in Congress, giving her son's venture immediate credibility in an industry hungry for regulatory clarity. Republicans argue this exemplifies how political power translates into private wealth for the connected class—a textbook case of insider advantage that ordinary entrepreneurs never enjoy.

While the sources don't detail any explicit quid pro quo or legal violation, conservatives note the pattern: a senator with regulatory influence over financial institutions, a son with zero track record, and a massive capital raise that defies market logic. It's the kind of deal that would face intense scrutiny if a Republican senator's child were involved.
