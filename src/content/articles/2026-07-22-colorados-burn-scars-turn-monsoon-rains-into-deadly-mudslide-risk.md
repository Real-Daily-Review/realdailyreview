# Colorado Faces Dangerous Flood and Mudslide Season as Burn Scars Amplify Storm Risk

Colorado's wildfire legacy is turning monsoon rains into a public safety crisis—and state officials need to explain why forest management hasn't kept pace with the threat.

Heavy rainfall moving through Colorado this week has triggered Flood Watches across western regions as monsoon moisture collides with drought conditions and, more critically, the state's extensive burn scars (CPR News). The combination is deadly: hard-baked soil in fire-scorched areas cannot absorb water, forcing runoff into roads and communities with little warning.

The danger is immediate. A motorcyclist was swept off Highway 145 into the San Miguel River after a series of mudslides closed the route (9News), underscoring how quickly these events turn fatal. Denver and the Front Range face increased storm chances as well (9News).

Conservatives and taxpayers should demand accountability here. Years of environmental litigation and regulatory gridlock have prevented aggressive forest thinning and prescribed burns—the proven tools for reducing wildfire severity and post-fire flooding risk. Instead, Colorado communities now bear the cost in emergency response, infrastructure damage, and lost lives.

The state's water and land-use agencies must prioritize forest health over litigation delays. Watch whether Governor Polis's administration accelerates forest management permits or continues deferring to environmental groups that oppose active intervention.
