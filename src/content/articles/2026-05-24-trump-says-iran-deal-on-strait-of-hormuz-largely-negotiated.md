# Trump Says Iran Deal on Strait of Hormuz 'Largely Negotiated'

President Trump said Saturday that a deal with Iran, including reopening the Strait of Hormuz, has been "largely negotiated" after calls with regional allies, according to PBS NewsHour. The announcement comes as Americans face Memorial Day weekend gas prices at their highest level in nearly four years, according to The Hill.

A fragile ceasefire has been in place since April 8, punctuated by skirmishes as the U.S. and Iran have maneuvered over control of the critical waterway, CNBC reported. Qatar and Pakistan have served as mediators in the talks, with Pakistan recently holding what it described as "highly productive" discussions in Tehran, according to Al Jazeera.

Tehran has signaled progress on negotiations but indicated that nuclear weapons—a key sticking point in previous talks—are not part of an initial framework being discussed, the BBC reported. Trump said the deal will be announced soon.

The standoff has had real human costs: a missile strike on the oil tanker Skylight at the start of the conflict left at least one crew member missing, the BBC documented.

Watch for the formal announcement of any agreement and whether nuclear issues become part of broader negotiations.
