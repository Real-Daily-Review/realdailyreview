# ATF's Secret Gun Registry Exposes Dangerous Federal Overreach While AI Boom Roars Ahead

A congressional witness has accused the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives of defying Congress by maintaining a massive database of 1.4 billion records identifying which Americans own which guns—a direct challenge to Second Amendment rights and federal law. Meanwhile, AI startup Anthropic raised a record $81 billion in its stock market debut (Breitbart), pricing shares at $83 each and valuing the company behind the Claude chatbot at $1.5 trillion, signaling explosive growth in the tech sector.

The two stories underscore a fundamental tension: while American innovation thrives in free markets, federal agencies continue building surveillance infrastructure that threatens constitutional liberties. Republicans and gun-rights advocates argue the ATF's registry violates the Firearm Owners' Protection Act of 1986, which explicitly prohibits a centralized gun registry. The agency has long denied maintaining such a database, making the congressional testimony a bombshell.

For Colorado gun owners and Second Amendment advocates, this revelation demands immediate action. The state's outdoor heritage and hunting traditions depend on privacy protections that the ATF appears to be systematically dismantling.
