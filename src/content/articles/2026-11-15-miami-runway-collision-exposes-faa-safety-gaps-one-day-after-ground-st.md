# Miami Airport Collision Raises Questions About FAA Safety Systems

A UPS cargo plane and Copa Airlines passenger jet collided on the tarmac at Miami International Airport Saturday night, injuring two people, just hours after the FAA lifted a nationwide ground stop triggered by a telecommunications failure.

The collision occurred around 9:15 p.m. while both aircraft were taxiing (9News Denver, Washington Examiner). The FAA is investigating the incident.

The timing is troubling. One day earlier, a communications system failure had forced the FAA to ground all U.S. flights for hours—a stark reminder that America's air traffic control infrastructure remains vulnerable to cascading failures. Conservatives have long argued that the FAA's aging systems and bloated bureaucracy create safety risks while hampering efficiency. Rather than modernizing technology and streamlining operations, the agency continues to expand regulations and staffing without delivering proportional safety gains.

Colorado's aviation industry—from Denver International Airport operations to regional carriers—depends on reliable, efficient air traffic control. When the FAA stumbles, it ripples through Colorado's economy and tourism sector.

The good news: only minor injuries were reported, and both planes were moving slowly during taxiing. The bad news: these incidents expose systemic vulnerabilities that demand real reform, not more government spending on failed bureaucracies.

Watch whether Congress demands accountability from FAA leadership or simply throws more taxpayer dollars at the problem.
