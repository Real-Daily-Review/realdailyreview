# Boulder Violence Spree Raises Questions About Public Safety, Mental Health Response

A series of violent incidents in Boulder this week—including a fatal boulder collapse that killed one man and a knife attack on Pearl Street Mall that injured two others—has put a spotlight on the city's ability to protect residents and visitors in public spaces.

In the first incident, a man died after a roughly 1,000-pound boulder fell on him while he walked with a coworker on an Arkansas River trail; his companion sustained minor injuries (Daily Caller). Days later, Boulder police arrested a man who attacked two people with a knife on Pearl Street Mall, one of the city's most trafficked commercial areas. Police say the suspect deliberately chose the crowded location "to hurt people" (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

Conservatives argue that Boulder's progressive approach to criminal justice—including reduced police funding and emphasis on social workers over traditional law enforcement—leaves residents vulnerable. The Pearl Street attack raises hard questions about whether the city's mental health intervention model can prevent violence before it happens, or whether more robust police presence and enforcement are necessary.

Boulder's left-leaning city council has championed alternatives to traditional policing, but these incidents suggest that strategy may not adequately address violent crime or public safety threats.

Watch whether Boulder voters and city leadership demand a reassessment of public safety priorities—or double down on the current approach.
