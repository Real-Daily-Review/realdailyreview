# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, November 13, 2026

Colorado conservatives head into the weekend with a Democratic Party in open civil war, an immigration enforcement story with a Colorado twist, and a reminder that the left never stops organizing — even when it's losing.

---

## Kansas Goes Blue Again — And the GOP Should Take Note

Democratic state Sen. Cindy Holscher defeated Trump-backed Republican Ty Masterson to keep the Kansas governor's mansion in Democratic hands. Republicans argue this is a wake-up call: candidate quality and coalition-building matter, even in red-leaning states. Colorado conservatives know this lesson well — the state has drifted left in part because the GOP failed to field candidates who could hold the suburbs. Kansas should not be next. Watch your bench, Republicans.

---

## Mesa County Deputy Disciplined for ICE Cooperation? He's Now Working for ICE.

Former Mesa County Sheriff's Deputy Alexander Zwinck — who resigned after facing disciplinary action and a lawsuit from Democratic Attorney General Phil Weiser for collaborating with federal immigration authorities — has landed a job at ICE. Conservatives say this is exactly the right outcome: a law enforcement officer penalized for doing the right thing finds his footing with the agency that actually wants the job done. Weiser's pursuit of Zwinck looks less like justice and more like political intimidation.

---

## Schumer's Caucus Fractures After Historic Losses

Chuck Schumer is fighting to hold onto his Senate Democratic leadership role as Bernie Sanders, Elissa Slotkin, and four newly elected senators reject his bid. The Democratic Party is splitting between its radical progressive base and whatever remains of its establishment wing. For Colorado conservatives, this is good news in the short term — a fractured opposition is a less effective one. But don't get comfortable: the left's internal fights tend to produce more extreme winners, not more reasonable ones.

---

## AOC Eyes 2028 — and She's Already Building the Machine

Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez has tapped the strategist behind Bernie Sanders' record-breaking fundraising operation as progressive groups push her toward a 2028 presidential run. The left isn't licking its wounds — it's organizing. Colorado conservatives should pay close attention: AOC's brand of democratic socialism, green energy mandates, and federal spending expansion would hit Colorado's energy sector, ranching communities, and small businesses hard. The 2028 race starts now, whether Republicans are ready or not.

---

## Democrats Threaten Impeachment — DOJ Says No Ongoing Investigations

White House counsel Todd Blanche confirmed there are no ongoing investigations into the Democrats' impeachment targets — Howard Lutnick, Stephen Miller, Tom Homan, and Marco Rubio — as House Democrats float threats of impeachment proceedings and congressional hearings come January. Republicans argue this is pure political theater from a minority party with no real power and no real agenda. Conservatives say the American people already rendered their verdict in November 2024. Democrats should govern where they can and stop the grandstanding.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on Attorney General Phil Weiser's immigration enforcement posture — the Zwinck case shows Colorado's Democratic leadership is willing to punish local law enforcement for cooperating with federal authorities, and that fight is far from over. Watch the Democratic leadership battle in the Senate: whoever emerges will set the opposition's tone heading into the 2028 cycle, and a Sanders-aligned caucus means more pressure on Colorado's Democratic delegation to go hard left. And as the weekend storm rolls in across the Western Slope, spare a thought for the families of the four firefighters killed in the Knowles Fire in Mesa County — a reminder of the real costs borne by the men and women who protect Colorado's land and communities.
