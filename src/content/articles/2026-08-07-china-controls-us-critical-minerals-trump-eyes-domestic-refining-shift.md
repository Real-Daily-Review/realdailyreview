# Critical Minerals Refining: America's Dangerous Dependence on China

Colorado's economic future depends on reversing Washington's surrender of critical minerals refining to Beijing.

The U.S. faces a strategic vulnerability: China dominates the refining of minerals essential to military hardware, vehicles, data centers, and power infrastructure—a chokepoint that threatens national security and economic independence. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the industrial capacity to transform raw minerals into usable materials has largely migrated overseas, leaving America dependent on hostile actors for technologies that power everything from military vehicles to hospital backup power systems.

Republicans and Trump administration officials argue the solution requires aggressive domestic investment in refining capacity and streamlined permitting for mining and processing operations. They contend that regulatory barriers and environmental restrictions have crippled American mineral independence, forcing reliance on China's state-directed industrial machine.

The left typically counters with environmental and land-use concerns, resisting expanded mining and refining on public lands and in sensitive ecosystems—a position that has effectively outsourced critical infrastructure to adversaries.

For Colorado, this matters directly: the state holds significant mineral deposits and has the industrial base to become a refining hub, creating high-wage jobs while reclaiming supply-chain security from Beijing. Yet state and federal red tape continues to block development.
