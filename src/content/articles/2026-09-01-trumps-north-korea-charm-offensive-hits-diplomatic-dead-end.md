# Trump's North Korea Gambit Faces Cold Reality as Kim Jong Un Ignores Diplomatic Overtures

President Trump's hopes for another summit with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un are running into a wall of silence—and that's a problem for U.S. credibility in the region.

Trump has signaled he wants to meet with Kim again this year, building on the 2018 Singapore summit and 2019 Vietnam talks. But Kim appears to be ghosting the president, according to the Washington Examiner. The North Korean leader has shown no interest in resuming negotiations, even as Trump ordered the Pentagon to scale back joint military exercises with South Korea—a significant concession meant to sweeten the diplomatic pitch.

The concern: Kim Jong Un isn't the same negotiator Trump faced in 2018. He's hardened his position, expanded his nuclear arsenal, and appears content to let Trump chase him rather than engage. By pulling back military readiness to facilitate talks that Kim won't attend, the administration risks signaling weakness to Beijing and Seoul while undermining the U.S.-South Korea alliance that's critical to regional stability.

Conservatives worry that unilateral military stand-downs without reciprocal North Korean movement reward bad behavior and undercut deterrence—the very foundation of peace through strength.

Watch whether the administration recalibrates its approach or doubles down on personal diplomacy at the expense of allied security commitments.
