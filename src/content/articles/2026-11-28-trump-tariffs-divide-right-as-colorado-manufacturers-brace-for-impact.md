# Trump's Tariff Gamble Divides Right; Colorado Manufacturers Face Uncertainty

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire even from conservative allies, with critics arguing the approach mirrors failed protectionist experiments rather than Reagan-era strength.

The "Liberation Day" tariffs, unveiled in April 2025, sparked immediate backlash. *Washington Examiner* opinion writers contend the announcement was "chaotic" with "absurd" justifications, noting Trump's approval ratings slid following the rollout. The concern: repeating protectionist mistakes without clear economic rationale.

**The conservative divide:** National Review argues Trump should adopt Reagan's Cold War playbook—building military strength as leverage for negotiation—but questions whether tariffs serve that disciplined strategy. Meanwhile, Trump defenders maintain tariffs pressure adversaries and protect American manufacturing.

**Colorado angle:** Manufacturers across the state—from defense contractors to agricultural equipment makers—face margin pressure if tariff costs ripple through supply chains. Colorado's export-dependent sectors, particularly energy and agriculture, could see retaliatory tariffs on key markets.

Republicans generally support Trump's nationalist posture on trade, but establishment conservatives worry tariffs without clear endgame damage growth and consumer purchasing power. Democrats predictably oppose tariffs as inflationary.

**Watch:** Whether Trump clarifies tariff conditions and timelines—or doubles down without measurable progress—will determine if this splits the GOP or rallies conservatives around a coherent America First trade doctrine.
