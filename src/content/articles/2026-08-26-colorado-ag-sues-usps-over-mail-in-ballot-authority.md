# Colorado AG Joins 25 States Suing USPS Over Mail-In Ballot Power Grab

Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser is leading a multistate lawsuit to block the U.S. Postal Service from unilaterally regulating mail-in voting—a move that puts the state's top Democrat directly at odds with federal overreach, even as conservatives warn the USPS rule threatens election integrity.

Weiser joined 25 other states in federal court challenging a new USPS regulation that grants the agency authority to set mail-voting rules across states (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The lawsuit argues the rule exceeds executive authority and conflicts with federal law, according to the Washington Examiner.

The case reflects a rare alignment: Democrats typically defend mail-in voting expansion, but they're objecting here to *federal* agency power encroaching on *state* election authority. Conservatives argue the USPS rule creates opportunities for ballot mishandling and delays that undermine election security—concerns that have animated Republican election-integrity efforts nationwide.

Colorado's involvement is significant. The state has relied heavily on mail-in voting since 2013, making USPS operational standards directly relevant to Coloradans' ballots. Republicans and election-integrity advocates should scrutinize whether this lawsuit genuinely protects state sovereignty or simply repositions state-level mail-voting expansion beyond federal scrutiny.

Watch whether the federal court sides with the states and whether Colorado Republicans demand transparency on how USPS rule changes affect ballot security in 2024.
