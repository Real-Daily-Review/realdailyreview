# Fourth of July Surveillance Drones: Government Eyes in the Sky Over Holiday Celebrations

President Trump hosted America's 250th birthday celebration on the National Mall Saturday, with his keynote address rescheduled to 11 p.m. ET after weather forced an evacuation (Breitbart). But while fireworks lit the sky, federal agencies deployed a less visible presence: surveillance drones.

Public safety agencies nationwide are using new FAA rules to launch "Drone as First Responder" programs during Fourth of July celebrations, ostensibly to spot illegal fireworks (The Daily Wire). The FAA loosened regulations last year, allowing cities and counties to fast-track drone deployments by cutting bureaucratic review—a move conservatives should view with skepticism.

**The conservative concern is clear:** expanded drone surveillance, even for public safety, sets a troubling precedent for government monitoring of Americans exercising their freedoms. What starts as illegal-fireworks enforcement can easily become routine surveillance infrastructure. Colorado communities hosting Independence Day events should ask hard questions: Are local officials signing onto federal drone programs? What data is collected, stored, and shared? Who oversees it?

The Biden-era FAA's deregulatory move on drones—ironically framed as cutting red tape—actually accelerates government's surveillance capabilities. That's the opposite of the limited government conservatives demand.

Watch whether Colorado cities adopt these drone programs and what privacy safeguards, if any, they demand in return.
