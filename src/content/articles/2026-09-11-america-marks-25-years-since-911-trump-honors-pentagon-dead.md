# Nation Marks 25 Years Since 9/11 as Trump Honors Pentagon Victims

America paused Tuesday to remember the 184 lives lost at the Pentagon on September 11, 2001—a quarter-century after the terror attacks reshaped national security policy and American life itself.

President Trump attended the Pentagon memorial ceremony at 9:30 a.m., joining Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and First Lady Melania Trump to commemorate the anniversary (Washington Examiner). The president delivered remarks at the Defense Department facility, shifting from an initially planned appearance in New York to focus on the Pentagon attack specifically.

Parallel ceremonies unfolded across the nation. Ground Zero in New York City hosted its own 9/11 memorial event (Just The News), while 9News Denver carried live coverage of the Pentagon observance for Colorado viewers.

The 9/11 attacks fundamentally altered American counterterrorism doctrine, defense spending, and federal surveillance authority—changes that remain contentious among conservatives who debate the proper balance between security and civil liberties. Two decades of post-9/11 wars and homeland security expansion have cost trillions in taxpayer dollars, a reality that shapes ongoing debates over government scope and military commitments.

For Colorado, the attacks reinforced the state's role in national defense through installations like NORAD at Cheyenne Mountain and Peterson Space Force Base in Colorado Springs.

Conservatives should monitor whether 9/11 remembrance translates into renewed scrutiny of federal security overreach and wasteful defense contracting.
