# Colorado Primary Chaos: Unaffiliated Voters and Progressive Money Reshape State Elections

Colorado's primary system is increasingly divorced from reality—and from the voters who actually decide general elections. Growing numbers of unaffiliated voters are locked out of meaningful participation in party primaries, even as those contests determine which candidates Colorado will face in November (Complete Colorado). Meanwhile, progressive dark-money groups are pouring millions into Democratic statehouse races, successfully unseating moderate incumbents and pushing the party further left (Denver Post).

Republicans and conservatives argue this two-track system is fundamentally broken. Party-registered voters who skip caucuses surrender their voice to activists, while the state's 1.3 million unaffiliated voters—nearly a third of the electorate—watch from the sidelines as primaries select nominees they'll later choose between. The result: Colorado's legislature increasingly reflects the preferences of primary activists, not general-election voters.

Democrats counter that primary engagement is a matter of choice and that their candidates won fair contests. But the influx of outside spending to push sitting Democrats out from the left raises questions about who actually controls the party's direction.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the stakes are clear: a statehouse dominated by primary-selected progressives means higher taxes, more regulation, and fewer checks on government spending.

Watch whether Republicans exploit this primary-versus-general divide to recruit unaffiliated candidates and challenge the Democratic supermajority in swing districts.
