# Gold Mountain Fire Explodes to 18,000 Acres; Western Slope Communities Face Evacuation

Colorado's Western Slope is battling a catastrophic wildfire that underscores the state's chronic failure to manage public forests responsibly. The Gold Mountain fire has scorched more than 18,000 acres near Ouray, forcing new evacuations as flames make a northeast run (CPR News). One Ouray business owner and father-to-be told 9News Denver he's watching his livelihood burn while hoping for survival—a story repeated across fire-ravaged Colorado communities year after year.

Conservatives have long argued that federal and state mismanagement of forest lands—choking them with dead timber, blocking logging, and restricting prescribed burns—creates tinderboxes that threaten lives and property. Instead of addressing root causes, Democratic-led Colorado continues to pour resources into climate narratives while ignoring practical forest health solutions that would reduce fuel loads and prevent catastrophic burns.

The Gold Mountain fire exemplifies why Colorado needs aggressive forest thinning, timber harvesting on public lands, and fewer regulatory barriers to property defense. Ouray residents and Western Slope businesses shouldn't be sacrificed on the altar of environmental ideology.

**Watch whether state and federal officials finally commit to science-based forest management—or double down on the failed policies that created this disaster.**
