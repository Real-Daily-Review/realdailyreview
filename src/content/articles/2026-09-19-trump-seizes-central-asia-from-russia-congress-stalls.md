# Trump's Central Asia Pivot Stalls in Congress While Putin's Grip Weakens

The Trump administration has seized a historic opportunity to displace Russian influence across Central Asia, but Congress is dragging its feet on the diplomatic and trade moves needed to lock in the gains.

Russia's war in Ukraine has already hollowed out Moscow's regional dominance—a strategic windfall no American policy could have manufactured alone. The White House moved fast. In August 2025, Washington brokered the Armenia-Azerbaijan conflict, positioning the U.S. as the region's new power broker. But Congress hasn't followed suit, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The stall matters. Without congressional action on trade policy and regional engagement, the U.S. risks ceding back to Russia what Ukraine's resistance has already cost Moscow. Republicans argue that hesitation now wastes a rare geopolitical opening and leaves American interests vulnerable to Chinese and Russian competition in a strategically vital region.

For Colorado, this plays into broader questions about American energy independence and supply-chain resilience. Central Asia holds critical minerals and energy resources; a U.S.-led regional order could strengthen domestic alternatives to hostile-nation suppliers.

Watch whether Congress moves on Jackson-Vanik repeal and Central Asia trade frameworks before the window closes.
