# Federal Judge Allows Trump's UFC Event at White House Despite Last-Minute Legal Challenge

A federal judge has cleared the way for a UFC fight card to proceed on the White House South Lawn this weekend, rejecting an emergency motion to block the event. According to 9News Denver and Just The News, the judge ruled that plaintiffs lacked legal standing and filed their challenge too late to warrant intervention. The Department of Justice argued that halting the event at the last minute would make it impossible to arrange alternative logistics.

The ruling underscores a broader pattern: courts increasingly skeptical of eleventh-hour legal maneuvers designed to derail executive decisions. The Trump administration's use of White House grounds for the event—a departure from tradition—drew opposition, but the judiciary found no basis to second-guess the decision.

For Colorado conservatives, this signals judicial restraint on executive authority. Republicans have long complained that courts weaponize procedural delays to block conservative initiatives; this ruling suggests some judges recognize when litigation becomes weaponized delay tactics.

The decision also reflects the administration's willingness to test boundaries on how federal property can be used, a posture that will likely face further legal scrutiny as Trump's second term unfolds.
