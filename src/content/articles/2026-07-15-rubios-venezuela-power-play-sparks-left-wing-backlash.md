# Rubio's Venezuela Role Draws Left-Wing Fire—But Conservatives See Smart Statecraft

Marco Rubio's aggressive Venezuela policy is drawing fierce criticism from the left, which views the Secretary of State's hands-on approach to sanctions and oil-revenue management as overreach (National Review). Democrats and progressive activists argue Rubio is wielding executive power beyond traditional State Department bounds.

Conservatives counter that Rubio's strategy—including direct oversight of sanctions enforcement and regular contact with Venezuela's opposition leadership—represents exactly the kind of clear-eyed, anti-communist foreign policy the Trump administration promised. The Daily Wire reports Rubio is effectively shaping Venezuela policy from Washington, a posture that energizes the GOP base but alarms the progressive wing.

For Colorado, this matters: energy markets respond to Venezuela policy shifts, and oil-and-gas workers here have a stake in how U.S. sanctions affect global crude supplies and prices. Rubio's hardline stance could support domestic energy production—a win for Colorado's energy sector and workers—but also risks volatility if markets react sharply.

The left's pushback reveals deeper anxiety about Rubio's influence over Latin American policy writ large. Conservatives should watch whether Democrats attempt legislative constraints on executive Venezuela authority or whether Rubio's approach becomes a 2026 campaign flashpoint.
