# America's Founding Principles: A Conservative Call to Return to First Principles

Colorado conservatives should remember that our nation was founded on a radical idea: that government exists to protect individual rights, not to expand its own power.

As Americans mark the 250th anniversary of the nation's founding, the Washington Examiner argues that the Founders' core insight—that humans possess inalienable rights preceding government—stands as a rebuke to modern progressive governance. Unlike nations built through conquest and centralized authority, America was explicitly designed around the principle that government's sole legitimate purpose is protecting those pre-existing rights.

For Coloradans, this principle has direct implications. It challenges the regulatory state that constrains energy development, water rights disputes, and Second Amendment protections. It questions whether government should dictate land use, control water allocation, or restrict individual liberty in the name of progressive causes.

Conservatives argue that modern government—federal, state, and local—has inverted the Founders' design: it now claims authority to grant rights conditionally, regulate behavior extensively, and expand its reach into domains the Constitution reserves to individuals and states. The Left counters that modern governance requires active government to address market failures and protect vulnerable populations.

The question for Colorado voters: Does your state government protect your rights, or does it increasingly restrict them?
