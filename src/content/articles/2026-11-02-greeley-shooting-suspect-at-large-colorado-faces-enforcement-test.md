# Colorado Halloween Violence Underscores Need for Enforcement, Not More Gun Restrictions

A 20-year-old suspect remains at large after shooting two people—killing one—at a Halloween party near Greeley, according to the Weld County Sheriff's Office (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The shooting is the latest in a series of Halloween-weekend violence incidents, including a fatal shooting at a California Spirit Halloween store that left one woman dead and several injured.

Colorado conservatives argue these incidents highlight a critical gap: enforcing existing gun laws and prosecuting violent criminals, not expanding regulations that burden law-abiding gun owners. The suspect in the Greeley case was already subject to law enforcement scrutiny—the real question is why he remains at large and what resources Weld County has to apprehend him.

Democrats typically respond to such violence by calling for stricter gun laws, universal background checks, and red-flag confiscation measures. Republicans counter that Colorado already has background checks and that focusing on criminal prosecution and mental health intervention—rather than restricting Second Amendment rights—is the proven path to public safety.

The Greeley shooting also raises questions about law enforcement capacity in rural Weld County, where resources are often stretched thin. Taxpayers should watch whether state or federal resources are deployed to assist in the manhunt and whether Colorado lawmakers use this incident to justify new gun restrictions rather than supporting enforcement efforts.
