# Colorado Expands Mail Voting While Primary System Draws Fire From Centrists

Governor Jared Polis has signed legislation extending Colorado's mail-voting window by a week, allowing voters until the day after Election Day to receive and return ballots—a move Democrats frame as protecting the state against federal interference. Meanwhile, former health-care executive Kent Thiry is warning that Colorado's primary system has become captive to ideological factions, creating what he calls a "tyranny of the minority" that doesn't reflect mainstream voters (Complete Colorado).

The mail-voting expansion, effective November, represents another step in Colorado's shift toward universal mail balloting. Democrats argue the extended window insulates the state from federal election restrictions; Republicans counter that mail-voting expansion increases administrative complexity and ballot-handling risks without addressing voter access—Colorado already leads the nation in mail-voting participation.

Thiry's critique cuts across party lines. He argues that primary elections dominated by ideological activists produce general-election candidates who don't represent the median voter, a concern that resonates with Colorado moderates frustrated by polarization in both parties.
