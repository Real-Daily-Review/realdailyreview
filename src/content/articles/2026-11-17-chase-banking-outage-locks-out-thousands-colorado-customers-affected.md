# Major Banks Hit by Software Glitch; Colorado Customers Among Those Locked Out

A significant software bug crippled online banking for Chase and other major U.S. banks Monday, leaving thousands of customers unable to access accounts—a stark reminder of how dependent Americans have become on digital financial infrastructure with minimal regulatory safeguards.

Chase blamed the outage on a "software bug" (KDVR Fox31, 9News). According to The Daily Wire, Downdetector recorded more than 2,500 user reports involving Chase at noon Monday, with 39% reporting account balance and transaction issues, 32% experiencing web login failures, and 16% unable to access other services.

Colorado customers relying on Chase and competitor banks faced real disruption. While Chase said transactions returned to normal by day's end, the incident exposes a vulnerability: major financial institutions operate with insufficient redundancy and oversight.

**The conservative concern:** This outage underscores why government should focus on *enforcing existing cybersecurity standards* rather than expanding regulatory burden on banks. Republicans argue that heavy-handed compliance mandates often distract institutions from core operational resilience. The real issue isn't more rules—it's accountability for the ones already on the books.

Coloradans should watch whether regulators demand meaningful transparency from Chase on root causes and prevention measures, or whether this becomes another excuse for progressive lawmakers to demand new federal banking controls that ultimately raise costs for Colorado consumers and small businesses.
