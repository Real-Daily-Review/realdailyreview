# Colorado Should Reject Surveillance-State Overreach While Washington Drowns in Debt

Colorado conservatives face a two-front battle: stopping government surveillance creep at home while demanding Washington stop its reckless borrowing binge that's crushing working families.

Across the country, states are pumping the brakes on Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) networks—camera systems that track every vehicle movement—according to the Daily Caller. Republicans and Democrats alike are questioning whether mass surveillance infrastructure serves public safety or simply enables government overreach. Colorado should scrutinize any expansion of ALPR deployment in our state, particularly in Denver and along I-25, where such systems could track law-abiding citizens without warrant or cause.

Meanwhile, Washington's fiscal recklessness is hitting Coloradans in the wallet. The Washington Examiner reports that Treasury yields have surged to 19-year highs, signaling bond-market alarm over federal borrowing. Americans already squeezed by rising living costs face another squeeze: higher mortgage rates and inflation driven by Washington's spending addiction. Republicans must make fiscal responsibility central to their platform, not just rhetoric.

Colorado taxpayers and small-business owners can't afford Washington's debt spiral or state-level surveillance expansion. Both represent government overreach—one financial, one invasive.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers propose new ALPR funding or surveillance initiatives in the 2026 session.
