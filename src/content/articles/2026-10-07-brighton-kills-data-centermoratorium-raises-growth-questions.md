# Brighton Halts Data Center Push—For Now

Brighton's city council has voted to impose a one-year moratorium on data centers, effectively killing a controversial 155-acre project after the developer withdrew its application (9News Denver). The move marks a local victory for residents opposed to the facility, but raises questions about whether Colorado communities are blocking economic opportunity out of fear.

The Silo application, which had drawn fierce neighborhood opposition, was withdrawn hours before the council vote (KDVR Fox31). Supporters of the project argued it would bring jobs and tax revenue to the Adams County city. Opponents worried about water consumption, traffic, and industrial sprawl in a residential area.

**The conservative concern:** While protecting neighborhood character matters, one-year moratoriums can signal to businesses that a community is hostile to development and investment. Data centers represent real economic activity and employment—the kind of growth Colorado needs to remain competitive. If every city blocks projects reflexively, companies will simply relocate to more business-friendly jurisdictions.

Brighton's pause gives the city time to craft sensible zoning rules rather than blanket bans. But conservatives should watch whether the moratorium becomes permanent or whether other Front Range communities follow suit, potentially ceding Colorado's data center market to Texas, Arizona, or other states with fewer regulatory obstacles.
