# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—If Regulators Don't Kill It

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are the unglamorous backbone of modern life, yet they face mounting pressure from environmental regulators and progressive activists who treat them as enemies of sustainability. These facilities—which process everything from cloud computing to AI training—generate substantial tax revenue and high-paying jobs across the state, according to *Complete Colorado*. Yet the industry operates under constant threat from new regulations and anti-growth sentiment.

Republicans and industry advocates argue that data centers are essential infrastructure deserving of streamlined permitting and tax certainty. The facilities consume energy, yes, but Colorado's diversified power grid—including natural gas and renewables—can support responsible expansion. Progressives counter that data centers strain water resources and carbon emissions, demanding stricter environmental reviews and higher operational costs.

For Colorado taxpayers and workers, the stakes are clear: data centers represent a rare, high-value economic engine that doesn't require mining or agriculture. They employ skilled technicians, engineers, and support staff while generating property and sales tax revenue that funds schools and roads.

The real question: Will Colorado's regulatory environment remain business-friendly enough to keep these operations competitive, or will Sacramento-style overreach drive them to Texas and Arizona?
