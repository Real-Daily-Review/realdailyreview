# Diesel Prices Spike Past Biden Era Levels as Ukraine War Strains Global Energy Markets

Colorado truckers and agricultural operators are facing diesel costs that now exceed even the peak Biden years, a painful reminder that energy policy extends far beyond the Oval Office. Energy Secretary Chris Wright attributed the surge primarily to Ukraine's destruction of Russian refineries, disrupting global supply (Washington Examiner). The Daily Caller reports diesel prices have climbed higher than their Biden-era highs, squeezing margins for Colorado's transportation and farming sectors already battered by regulatory costs.

Republicans argue that while geopolitical shocks matter, years of anti-fossil-fuel policies—from permitting delays to refinery underinvestment—left America's energy infrastructure fragile and dependent on global volatility. Wright's acknowledgment of the Ukraine factor underscores conservatives' broader point: energy independence requires domestic production capacity, not wishful thinking about renewables alone.

For Colorado, where trucking, agriculture, and energy development anchor rural economies, elevated diesel costs ripple through supply chains and ranch operations. Every cent per gallon compounds into real losses for small businesses already navigating inflation and regulatory burden.

Watch whether Congress acts to streamline refinery permitting and expand domestic crude production—or whether energy policy remains hostage to progressive climate ideology and international crises.
