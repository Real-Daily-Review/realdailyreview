# Colorado Republicans Eye 2028 as National Party Resets After Midterm Losses

Republicans are already mapping out the 2028 presidential field after midterm setbacks handed Democrats significant gains, with the party's Colorado contingent watching closely as potential candidates position themselves for the next race.

The Washington Examiner notes that this cycle's Democratic wave may mark both the end of one presidential contest and the beginning of another, with the scale of Republican losses forcing the party to recalibrate its 2028 strategy. Meanwhile, National Review examines whether Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez could emerge as a Democratic frontrunner, though the outlet suggests she faces considerable headwinds despite the left's recent electoral success.

For Colorado conservatives, the 2028 landscape matters: the state has become a perennial battleground, and early positioning by Republican candidates will signal whether the party plans to compete aggressively here or cede ground to Democrats. Colorado's 10 electoral votes and competitive Senate races make it a proving ground for national GOP strategy.

Republicans argue the party must learn from recent defeats and nominate candidates with proven appeal to independent voters and suburban families—demographics that have drifted left in Colorado. Democrats counter that their momentum reflects voter rejection of Republican policies on abortion and democracy itself.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans coalesce around a 2028 frontrunner or splinter into competing camps, signaling the party's seriousness about reclaiming the state.
