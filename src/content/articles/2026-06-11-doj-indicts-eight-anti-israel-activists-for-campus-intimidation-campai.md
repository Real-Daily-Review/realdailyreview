# Federal Indictment of Anti-Israel Activists at University of Michigan Signals DOJ Crackdown on Campus Intimidation

The Department of Justice has indicted eight anti-Israel protesters at the University of Michigan for allegedly orchestrating a coordinated campaign of threats and intimidation targeting university officials and a local Jewish nonprofit. According to the Washington Examiner, the defendants engaged in disruptive "autonomous actions" designed to pressure the university into severing ties with Israel following Hamas's October 7, 2023 attack.

U.S. Attorney Jerome F. Gorgon Jr. emphasized the principle at stake: "In America, we rule by law not by fear." The indictment alleges the conspirators threatened university officials in a systematic effort to coerce institutional policy changes.

**The Conservative Take:** Republicans and law-and-order conservatives argue this prosecution vindicates their warnings about campus radicalism and the erosion of free speech through intimidation. They contend that progressive universities have tolerated—and in some cases enabled—anti-Israel activism that crosses from protest into criminal harassment. The indictment demonstrates that federal law still applies to those who use threats to suppress dissent, even on college campuses where such behavior has often gone unchecked.

Democrats have largely remained silent on the specifics, though campus free-speech advocates may argue the line between protected activism and criminal threat-making requires careful scrutiny.
