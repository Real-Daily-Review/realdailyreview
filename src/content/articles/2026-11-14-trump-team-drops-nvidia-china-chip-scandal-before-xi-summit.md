# Trump Administration Sidesteps Nvidia Chip-Export Scandal Ahead of Xi Meeting

The Trump administration is quietly shelving scrutiny of Nvidia's advanced AI chip sales to China—a move that raises hard questions about whether national security takes a back seat to diplomatic convenience.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, a senior administration official confirmed Friday that the alleged Chinese spy scandal involving former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang will not be discussed when President Trump meets with Chinese President Xi Jinping next week during his Asia trip. The *National Review* has pressed the question directly: "Why, exactly, are we allowing Nvidia to sell advanced AI chips to China?"

The silence is conspicuous. Conservatives argue that unrestricted AI chip exports to a strategic adversary undermine U.S. technological dominance and national defense. Advanced semiconductors are critical infrastructure—selling them to Beijing amounts to arming a competitor while claiming to compete.

For Colorado, this matters: the state hosts significant aerospace, defense, and tech sectors that depend on American technological superiority and secure supply chains. If the administration deprioritizes enforcement against corporate transfers of sensitive tech to China, it weakens the competitive edge Colorado's defense and innovation economy relies on.

Republicans and national security hawks will be watching whether Trump's team pursues the Nvidia matter or lets it fade into diplomatic expediency.
