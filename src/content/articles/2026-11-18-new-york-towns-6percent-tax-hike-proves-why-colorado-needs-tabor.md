# New York Town's 6% Tax Hike Shows Why Colorado Must Guard Against Runaway Government Spending

A cautionary tale from New York's Babylon shows what happens when government unions and elected officials run unchecked: residents watched helplessly as their town proposed a nearly 6% tax increase while handing the supervisor a 29% raise (Washington Examiner).

The Nov. 5 town meeting drew more than two dozen angry residents—a sign that even deep-blue New York is hitting taxpayer limits. Babylon's budget would again breach the state's 2% tax cap, a restriction that exists precisely because governments default to spending more when given the chance.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's Taxpayer Bill of Rights (TABOR) has protected residents from similar raids on household budgets since 1992. While progressives constantly attack TABOR as "restrictive," Babylon's debacle proves why spending caps matter. Without them, government unions negotiate sweetheart deals, supervisors pocket raises, and working families foot the bill.

Republicans argue that public-sector compensation packages—especially when negotiated behind closed doors—drain resources from core services and burden taxpayers who lack similar bargaining power. Democrats counter that competitive wages attract quality public servants, but Babylon residents clearly disagree when raises outpace inflation while their own wages stagnate.

Colorado conservatives should watch for any legislative push to weaken TABOR or expand government union authority—the playbook is identical coast to coast.
