# Trump's Iran Move Draws Rubio Defense—But Colorado Should Ask: What's the Cost?

Secretary of State Marco Rubio defended President Trump's decision to initiate conflict with Iran on Friday, arguing the administration was elected to "do the hard things," according to the *Washington Examiner*. Speaking at Mar-a-Lago, Rubio framed the move as fulfilling Trump's mandate, with Vice President JD Vance issuing a supporting statement after midterm elections.

Republicans argue Trump campaigned on a strong foreign policy stance and restoring American deterrence abroad. The *Daily Wire* noted Rubio reminded voters why they elected Trump two years ago, positioning the Iran decision as consistent with that platform.

**The Colorado angle matters here.** While national security is legitimate, Colorado taxpayers should demand transparency on costs. Military operations abroad don't come cheap—and they often fund bloated Pentagon budgets that conservatives claim waste resources. Colorado's defense contractors and military installations benefit from spending, but so do federal deficits that ultimately burden Colorado families.

Democrats and progressive critics will likely argue military escalation risks broader regional conflict and diverts resources from domestic priorities. Conservatives counter that weakness invites aggression and that American strength abroad protects prosperity at home.

**Watch for:** Congressional budget hearings on Iran-related military spending and whether Colorado's Republican delegation demands fiscal accountability alongside security measures.
