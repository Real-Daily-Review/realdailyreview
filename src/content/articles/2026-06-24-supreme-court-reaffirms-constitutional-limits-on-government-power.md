# Constitution Still Stands: Supreme Court Reaffirms Limits on Government Power

The Supreme Court's final decisions of the term deliver a bracing reminder that America's constitutional guardrails still work—and that liberty depends on enforcing them, not eroding them through regulatory overreach.

As *National Review* notes, the Court's rulings on tariffs and other major cases underscore a fundamental principle: the Constitution exists to say "no" to unchecked government power. That matters enormously for Colorado. Whether the issue is federal land grabs, EPA water regulations, or Washington's appetite for controlling energy policy, Coloradans depend on courts willing to enforce constitutional limits rather than rubber-stamp progressive expansions of federal authority.

The broader lesson is equally vital. *National Review* warns that "freedom is still always just one generation from extinction." That's not hyperbole—it's a call to vigilance. Every election, every regulatory battle, every court appointment shapes whether future Coloradans inherit a republic of limited government or a sprawling administrative state.

The Supreme Court's willingness to check executive and legislative overreach offers hope. But the fight doesn't end in June. Conservatives must remain engaged in defending constitutional limits at every level—state, local, and federal.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation and state leaders will push back against federal overreach in energy, water, and land management in the next session.
