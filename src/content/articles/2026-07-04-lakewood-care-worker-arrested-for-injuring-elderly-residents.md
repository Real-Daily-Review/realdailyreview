# Colorado Assisted Living Facility Worker Arrested After Injuring Elderly Residents

A Lakewood employee at an assisted living facility has been arrested for allegedly causing serious injuries to two elderly residents, including a 100-year-old memory care patient whose skin was torn from elbow to wrist (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The arrest occurred in June, within the employee's first 30 days on the job.

The incident underscores a critical vulnerability in Colorado's long-term care sector: inadequate vetting, training, and oversight of workers handling our state's most vulnerable citizens. As Colorado's population ages and demand for care facilities grows, taxpayers and families must demand stronger background check protocols, mandatory training standards, and facility accountability measures.

This case also raises questions about hiring practices at assisted living centers statewide. Conservative advocates argue that market-driven accountability—where facilities compete on safety records and families choose providers based on transparent performance data—works better than expanding government bureaucracy. However, basic safeguards against hiring workers with red flags remain essential.

Colorado families deserve confidence that their elderly relatives are protected. Watch whether state regulators strengthen facility oversight requirements or whether the industry voluntarily implements tighter hiring standards to restore public trust.
