# Texas Democrat's Pay-to-Play Problem Raises Red Flags for Colorado Voters

A Texas Democratic Senate candidate drew hefty paychecks from a consulting firm while his government agency awarded that same firm millions in contracts—a textbook pay-to-play arrangement that should alarm Colorado taxpayers watching their own state's revolving door between government and special interests.

James Talarico, running for Texas Senate, collected substantial salary payments from MAYA Consulting while overseeing the Texas Education Agency, which simultaneously steered lucrative contracts to the firm (Fox News). Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, his Republican opponent, accused Talarico of abusing his office to enrich himself and his employer (Just The News). Talarico's campaign pushed back against the "blatant corruption" allegation, but the optics are damaging.

This case exemplifies why conservatives oppose the unchecked power of government bureaucrats. When officials control contract awards and procurement decisions, conflicts of interest become inevitable—especially when personal financial gain is on the line. Colorado has seen similar controversies involving state employees and outside consulting arrangements; voters deserve transparency about who profits from government contracts.

Republicans argue this is precisely why government agencies should be smaller, budgets tighter, and procurement processes more transparent and competitive. Democrats typically counter that such arrangements are legal if disclosed, though that defense rings hollow when millions flow to connected firms.

Watch whether Colorado media and state ethics officials apply the same scrutiny to similar arrangements in our own government.
