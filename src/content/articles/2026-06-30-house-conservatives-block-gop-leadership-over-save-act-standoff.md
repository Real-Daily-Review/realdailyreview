# House Conservatives Block GOP Leadership Over SAVE Act, Forcing Early Recess

House conservatives are holding firm against Republican leadership, refusing to advance the National Defense Authorization Act until the SAVE America Act gets a standalone vote—and it's working. More than a dozen GOP hardliners sustained their blockade Tuesday, derailing floor action and forcing Speaker Johnson to call an early recess after a rules vote collapsed (Daily Caller).

The standoff centers on what conservatives see as a matter of principle: the SAVE Act, which addresses election integrity and government accountability, deserves its own floor debate rather than being buried in a larger defense bill. Republicans who voted against advancing the NDAA made their position clear—they won't rubber-stamp leadership priorities without getting a direct vote on their priorities first.

This is classic intra-GOP tension between establishment Republicans eager to move legislation and conservative hardliners demanding accountability and transparency. Democrats are watching the chaos unfold, though they've remained largely silent on the substance of either bill.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the question is whether this blockade signals a genuine shift toward fiscal restraint and constitutional governance—or merely theater. The early House recess means less legislative time to address spending, regulatory overreach, and other priorities that matter to Colorado families and businesses.

Watch whether the SAVE Act gets its standalone vote when the House returns, and whether conservatives maintain their leverage or fold under leadership pressure.
