# Dry Creek Fire Forces Evacuations in Garfield County

Colorado's tinder-dry conditions are fueling rapid wildfire spread, with the Dry Creek Fire south of Rifle forcing mandatory evacuations Tuesday afternoon. The blaze has consumed nearly 288 acres and destroyed at least two structures in Garfield County, prompting emergency crews to deploy two large air tankers, two helicopters, and an Air Attack resource to contain the fast-moving flames.

Residents south of the Rifle Garfield County Airport were ordered to evacuate as fire crews battled the spreading inferno in conditions described as dangerously dry. The speed of the fire's growth underscores Colorado's ongoing vulnerability to catastrophic wildfires—a reality that should refocus state leadership on forest management and fuel reduction rather than ideological environmental policies that restrict timber harvesting and controlled burns.

Conservatives have long argued that decades of federal and state overregulation of public lands, combined with environmental litigation blocking sensible forest management, have created tinderbox conditions across Colorado's mountains and foothills. Proper thinning, selective logging, and prescribed burns—not restrictions on development—are the proven tools to reduce wildfire risk and protect communities.

The state should prioritize removing regulatory barriers to aggressive forest management on both public and private land.
