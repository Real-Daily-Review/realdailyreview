# Trump Secures Historic Venezuela Oil Deal—Energy Boost for America, Relief for Colorado Energy Workers

President Donald Trump announced Friday that the United States has secured majority control of more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil reserves—a move conservatives say could reshape American energy independence and lower gas prices without costing taxpayers a dime.

The Washington Examiner and Daily Wire report Trump touted the agreement as the "biggest oil deal in world history," struck after the U.S. backed the ouster of Venezuelan dictator Nicolás Maduro in January. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and War Secretary Pete Hegseth will oversee the arrangement.

For Colorado, the implications are significant. A surge in domestic oil supply could stabilize energy markets, supporting the state's oil and gas sector—an industry under relentless regulatory assault from Denver progressives. Lower energy costs ripple through Colorado's economy, benefiting manufacturers, ranchers, and working families already squeezed by inflation.

Conservatives argue this deal exemplifies Trump's "America First" energy strategy: secure resources, reduce dependence on hostile regimes, and unlock prosperity without government handouts. It directly counters the Biden-Harris administration's war on fossil fuels and Colorado Democrats' push toward forced energy transitions.

The left will likely attack the deal as imperialist overreach, but Republicans counter that energy security is national security—and that affordable American oil beats OPEC manipulation every time.
