# Colorado Should Embrace AI Skills Training, Not Regulate It Into Oblivion

Colorado has a chance to lead on artificial intelligence—by letting the private sector train workers, not by imposing heavy-handed government mandates that kill innovation.

The Colorado Sun recently pitched state-backed AI apprenticeships as a path forward, arguing young workers need "solid supervision" and "clear standards" in AI-enabled workplaces. That's the progressive playbook: identify a trend, then regulate it.

But Complete Colorado points to a Magellan Strategies survey showing Colorado voters are ready to move past AI panic. The real opportunity isn't government-designed apprenticeships—it's removing barriers so employers can build their own training pipelines. Companies racing to hire AI talent have every incentive to develop skilled workers fast and cheap. They don't need state bureaucrats writing curriculum.

Colorado's energy sector, data centers, and tech hubs are already attracting major investment. Overregulation and government-mandated training programs would only drive those jobs elsewhere. Republicans argue the state should focus on education basics—math, coding, critical thinking—and let employers handle specialized training.

The conservative case is straightforward: Colorado workers win when businesses compete for talent and innovate freely. Government-run apprenticeships sound nice but cost taxpayers money and slow adaptation in a fast-moving field.

Watch whether Democratic lawmakers push for state-funded AI training programs that would expand government payroll and regulatory reach.
