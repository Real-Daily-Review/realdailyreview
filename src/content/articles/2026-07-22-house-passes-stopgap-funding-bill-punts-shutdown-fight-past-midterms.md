# House Punts Shutdown Fight Past Election, Avoiding Campaign-Season Chaos

Republicans just bought themselves breathing room—and Democrats a free pass to spend without accountability until after November.

The House passed a temporary government funding bill on a mostly party-line 220-205 vote (9News Denver), kicking the shutdown showdown to after the midterms. Speaker Mike Johnson's gambit: avoid a fiscal crisis during campaign season that could tank GOP candidates (Fox News Politics).

Here's the conservative problem: Congress is once again punting its basic constitutional duty to fund government responsibly. Instead of forcing Democrats to justify their spending priorities *before* voters head to the polls, Republicans have handed the Senate—and the Biden administration—a blank check through the election. No leverage. No debate. No accountability.

Colorado taxpayers should care because this pattern enables the federal spending spree that inflates prices, weakens the dollar, and funds progressive initiatives without scrutiny. When government funding battles get delayed past elections, the public never gets a clear choice on spending priorities.

Republicans argue they avoided a shutdown that would hurt the economy and their midterm message. Fair point. But the cost is real: another continuing resolution means no serious budget reform, no cuts, and no real fiscal discipline until after voters decide.

Watch whether Senate Democrats use this window to slip through spending riders that bypass normal appropriations review.
