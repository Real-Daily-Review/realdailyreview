## Hantavirus Outbreak: Race to Trace Passengers Across 12 Countries

About 12 countries have been linked to a hantavirus outbreak aboard the MV Hondius, with the World Health Organization confirming at least five cases. Health authorities are working to locate and monitor passengers who traveled on the vessel. The outbreak has triggered an international contact-tracing effort spanning multiple continents, underscoring how quickly a shipborne illness can scatter across borders before it is identified.

## Tenerife Braces for Hondius Arrival Amid Local Concern

Residents and officials on the Spanish island of Tenerife have expressed anger and resignation ahead of the MV Hondius's imminent arrival, with some warning the docking poses a health risk to the local population. The ship's approach puts Spanish authorities in the difficult position of balancing port obligations against public-health precautions, and the situation is being closely watched as a test case for how European ports handle vessels linked to active disease outbreaks.

## David Attenborough Turns 100

Sir David Attenborough marks his 100th birthday today, with the BBC hosting a celebration at the Royal Albert Hall and cinemas screening his nature films. Despite the global outpouring of tributes, Attenborough has long maintained that the animals — not the narrator — should be the focus of his work. His career spans decades of landmark natural-history television and has made him one of the most recognized voices in broadcasting worldwide.

## Iran Nuclear Talks: U.S. Awaits Tehran's Response

The United States says it expects Iran's reply to a proposed peace framework, as reports of clashes in the Strait of Hormuz add tension to the diplomatic track. Separately, Israel killed 31 people in southern Lebanon on Friday, including a rescue worker, according to Lebanon's National News Agency — a development that complicates the broader regional picture as negotiators push for progress on the Iran file.

## Canada: School Site of Deadly Mass Shooting to Be Demolished

Officials have announced that Tumbler Ridge Secondary School — the site of a February mass shooting in which nine people were killed and dozens more injured — will be torn down. The decision reflects the scale of trauma the attack inflicted on the small British Columbia community. The shooting ranks among the deadliest in Canadian history, and the demolition announcement comes as the community continues to grieve and rebuild.
