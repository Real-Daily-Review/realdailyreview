# Colorado Universities Jump on AI Bandwagon While Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Colorado's higher education establishment is embracing artificial intelligence with state-sponsored fanfare, even as questions linger about whether taxpayer-funded universities should be doubling down on unproven tech rather than core academics.

Six Colorado colleges and universities have earned the state's new "AI Engaged Campus Designation," according to CPR News, marking the first cohort to receive the honor for intentional AI integration into their operations and curricula. The designation reflects a broader push across higher ed to position institutions as innovation leaders in the AI space.

But conservatives raise a familiar concern: Colorado's universities already struggle with bloated administrative costs, ideological imbalance in faculty hiring, and mission creep away from rigorous instruction in math, science, and the humanities. Pouring resources into AI initiatives—however trendy—risks further diverting funds from undergraduate education and workforce readiness in fields where Colorado actually needs workers.

Republicans argue that state universities should focus on fiscal discipline and academic fundamentals before chasing the latest Silicon Valley obsession. The Washington Examiner has noted the broader political scramble around AI branding, suggesting that much of the enthusiasm may be more marketing than substance.

**Watch whether Colorado's legislature demands transparency on how these AI designations translate to actual student outcomes and taxpayer return-on-investment.**
