# FBI Crime Database Breached as Federal Cybersecurity Failures Mount

Federal agencies are hemorrhaging sensitive data to hackers, and Colorado law enforcement—which relies on the FBI's National Crime Information Center for real-time criminal records—faces potential operational disruption.

The FBI's nationwide criminal information database was compromised in a cyberattack, CNN and Fox News reported, marking the second major federal breach in months. Hackers accessed the National Crime Information Center, a critical tool for police departments nationwide, including Colorado agencies tracking suspects, warrants, and stolen property. The FBI is still probing the entry point and scope of the breach.

This isn't isolated. The U.S. Department of Agriculture system that processes farmer payments was also hacked recently, knocking offline loan, payment, and crop insurance applications—a direct hit to Colorado's agricultural economy and rural communities dependent on timely USDA disbursements.

Republicans argue these breaches expose the incompetence of federal cybersecurity infrastructure and underscore why government agencies cannot be trusted with sensitive data. The breaches raise urgent questions about how Colorado law enforcement will operate if critical databases remain compromised and whether federal systems protecting agricultural payments can be relied upon.

President Trump suggested the FBI breach could reveal information about past investigations, though details remain scarce.
