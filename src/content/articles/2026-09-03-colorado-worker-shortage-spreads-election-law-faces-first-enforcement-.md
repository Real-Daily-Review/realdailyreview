# Colorado's Worker Shortage Deepens as State Grapples With Election Law Enforcement

Colorado's labor crisis is spreading across industries and threatening economic growth, according to Federal Reserve economists (CPR News), even as the state navigates new complications in its voting system.

The Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City reports that worker shortages are no longer confined to specific sectors—they're now constraining growth across Colorado and neighboring states. This matters for Colorado taxpayers and businesses: fewer workers mean slower job creation, reduced tax revenue, and competitive disadvantages for employers trying to expand operations or fill critical roles in energy, construction, agriculture, and other sectors.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs faces a lawsuit over municipal election timing under a 2025 voting law now being enforced for the first time (Complete Colorado). The city held April municipal elections, triggering the legal challenge—raising questions about whether Colorado's election rules are creating compliance headaches for local governments.

On the political front, unaffiliated voter registration continues climbing in Colorado, with primary elections increasingly deciding general outcomes (Complete Colorado). Party-affiliated voters who skip caucuses are effectively ceding power to unaffiliated voters in general elections—a structural shift reshaping Colorado politics.
