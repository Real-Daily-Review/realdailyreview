# Trump's 50% Canada Tariffs Loom as Last-Minute Talks Stall

President Trump's threatened 50% tariffs on Canadian imports are set to hit at midnight Wednesday unless negotiators reach an 11th-hour deal—a move that could ripple through Colorado's energy and agricultural sectors.

The U.S. and Canada are in frantic last-minute negotiations (9News Denver) to avert the tariffs, which target roughly $20 billion in Canadian goods. Notably, these levies would apply even to products shielded under the U.S.-Mexico-Canada trade agreement, according to *Just The News*, signaling Trump's willingness to override existing trade frameworks.

**The conservative case:** Trump supporters argue tariffs are necessary leverage to force Canada into fairer trade terms and address border security concerns. Conservatives see this as using economic pressure to renegotiate deals that have favored foreign competitors at American workers' expense.

**The concern:** Colorado businesses reliant on Canadian energy, timber, and cross-border supply chains face immediate cost pressures. Ranchers and manufacturers dependent on integrated North American production could see input costs spike.

Canada's Finance Minister Chrystia Freeland has engaged Trump directly, but the midnight deadline leaves little room for compromise.

Watch whether Trump follows through on the tariff threat or uses it as negotiating theater—and whether Colorado's business community feels the pain before any deal materializes.
