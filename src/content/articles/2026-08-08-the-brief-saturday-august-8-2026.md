# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, August 8, 2026

Colorado wakes up to a weekend where federal power, judicial stability, and local government accountability are all in play — and conservatives have plenty of reason to stay sharp.

---

## Enes Freedom Calls the WNBA's Bluff

Former NBA player Enes Kanter Freedom declared himself a prospect for the 2027 WNBA Draft Friday, daring the league to explain on the record how its eligibility rules would disqualify him. Freedom — a vocal conservative activist and American citizen — is forcing a conversation the left-leaning sports media would rather avoid: if biological males can compete in women's sports, what exactly is the WNBA's legal and moral basis for exclusion? Colorado parents and female athletes who've watched this debate play out in high school gyms and swimming pools across the state know the answer matters.

---

## Aurora ICE Facility TB Case: Colorado Health Officials Still Locked Out

Adams County health authorities remain blocked from investigating a tuberculosis case at the GEO Group's Aurora ICE detention facility, and officials say they are unlikely to pursue further legal action at this time, pointing to an ongoing lawsuit. This is a straightforward public health and rule-of-law problem — and it cuts both ways. Colorado taxpayers and communities surrounding the Aurora facility deserve answers, and no private contractor or federal bureaucracy should be able to stonewall legitimate public health oversight. Watch whether the Polis administration applies any real pressure here, or lets this quietly disappear.

---

## Pentagon Pulls Frank Kendall's Security Clearance

The Department of Defense revoked former Air Force Secretary Frank Kendall's security clearance and eligibility for sensitive positions following his public comments about Air Force One. Republicans argue the revocation reflects the administration's broader effort to hold former officials accountable for conduct and public statements that cross clear lines. The left will frame this as political retaliation — but conservatives note that security clearances are a privilege, not a lifetime entitlement, and that former officials who use their credentialed status as a platform for political attacks should expect consequences.

---

## Alito: I'm Not Going Anywhere

Justice Samuel Alito put retirement rumors to rest Friday, saying flatly, "I'm here for another term" — a direct rebuke to months of speculation, including an erroneous NPR report that prematurely announced his departure and required a published editor's correction. For Colorado conservatives and the broader originalist legal movement, Alito's continued presence on the Court is significant. He remains one of the most reliable votes for constitutional limits on federal power, Second Amendment rights, and religious liberty — all issues with direct stakes for Coloradans fighting progressive overreach from Denver and Washington alike.

---

## Broncos Take a Hit: Henningsen Out for the Season

Denver Broncos lineman Matt Henningsen reportedly suffered a season-ending Achilles tear during a 1-on-1 drill, with doctors continuing to evaluate but the injury appearing serious. Henningsen, a veteran who appeared in 34 games across the 2022-23 seasons, is a real loss for a Broncos offensive line that can't afford attrition heading into the regular season. Colorado football fans have reason to temper their preseason optimism — depth on the line will be tested early.

---

## What to Watch

Justice Alito's confirmation that he's staying put should prompt Colorado conservatives to keep pressure on their U.S. senators to remain vigilant about any future Court nominations — the left will not stop pushing for a transformed judiciary. On the Aurora ICE facility TB case, watch whether Adams County health officials find the spine to escalate or whether bureaucratic inertia wins. And as the WNBA eligibility story gains traction nationally, Colorado's own ongoing fights over women's sports fairness in schools and athletics will be the real proving ground for whether anyone in state government is willing to draw a clear line.
