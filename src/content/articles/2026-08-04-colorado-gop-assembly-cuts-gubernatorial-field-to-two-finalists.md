# Colorado GOP Narrows Field in Contentious Primary Assembly

Colorado Republicans faced a brutal winnowing at their state assembly this week: of 18 gubernatorial candidates, only two cleared the delegate threshold to advance to the primary ballot. The result reflects deep fractures within the state party over direction, messaging, and who can actually win statewide in a purple state trending blue.

The assembly process—where party delegates rather than voters select nominees—has long been controversial. Republicans argue it ensures ideological consistency and prevents establishment capture; critics counter it silences grassroots voices and produces nominees out of step with general-election voters. In Colorado, where Democrats control the governor's mansion and both chambers of the legislature, the stakes are high.

The two survivors now face a primary gauntlet in a state where Republican registration has slipped and suburban Denver voters have drifted left. Colorado conservatives will be watching whether the assembly's picks can rebuild the coalition that once made this a reliably red state—or whether internal GOP divisions hand Democrats another easy cycle.

Taxpayers should monitor whether the eventual Republican nominee can articulate a clear case against Democratic spending, regulation, and energy policy that resonates beyond the base.
