# MS-13 Suspect Leaves U.S. — But Not to Where Trump Wanted

A Salvadoran national at the center of a high-profile deportation fight is voluntarily leaving the United States for Spain this week, according to the Washington Examiner and Breitbart. Kilmar Abrego Garcia, whose case drew national scrutiny during the Trump administration's immigration crackdown, reached a deal with federal authorities allowing him to depart on a commercial flight rather than face formal deportation proceedings.

The twist: Garcia is heading to Spain, not back to El Salvador—a detail conservatives say undercuts the administration's immigration enforcement message. Breitbart reports that Senate Democrat Chris Van Hollen, a candidate for Senate Democratic leadership, praised the departure as a "victory," suggesting Democrats view the voluntary-exit arrangement as a win over Trump's harder-line stance.

Republicans argue the case illustrates the complications of immigration enforcement when foreign nationals exploit legal loopholes and third-country options. Conservatives say allowing Garcia to choose his destination—rather than enforcing direct deportation—sets a problematic precedent for future cases.

For Colorado, the broader question remains: how aggressively will federal authorities pursue immigration enforcement in sanctuary-friendly jurisdictions like Denver and Boulder?

Watch whether the Trump administration tightens rules on voluntary departures and third-country placements in future cases.
