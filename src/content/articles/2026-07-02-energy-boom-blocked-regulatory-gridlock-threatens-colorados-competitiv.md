# America's Energy Boom Stalled by Regulatory Gridlock—Colorado Caught in the Crosshairs

America is poised for its biggest energy expansion since World War II, driven by artificial intelligence and data center construction, yet regulatory paralysis is strangling the nation's ability to deliver it. According to the *Washington Examiner*, more than 2,000 gigawatts of proposed generation capacity sits stuck in permitting limbo across the country—a self-inflicted wound that threatens American competitiveness and prosperity.

The core problem: a maze of permits, lawsuits, and regulatory delays that systematically empower obstructionists over builders. Whether it's natural gas pipelines, transmission lines, or critical mineral mines, infrastructure projects face years of litigation and bureaucratic friction before breaking ground.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. The state's energy sector—oil, gas, coal, and renewables—depends on streamlined permitting and transmission infrastructure to remain competitive. Regulatory gridlock doesn't just delay projects; it kills jobs, raises energy costs for families and businesses, and cedes American energy leadership to foreign competitors.

Conservatives argue the solution is straightforward: streamline permitting timelines, cap litigation delays, and trust market forces and state regulators rather than federal bureaucrats. The left's approach—endless environmental reviews and litigation rights—has become a veto mechanism disguised as caution.
