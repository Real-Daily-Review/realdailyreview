# Ex-ICE Guard Faces Prosecution; Prop 137 Draws Conservative Fire Over Taxpayer Funding

A Colorado judge has ruled there's sufficient evidence to prosecute Brandon Booth, an ex-ICE detention center guard accused of shooting toward female protesters who allegedly called him a "race traitor" and threatened to locate guards' children, according to the Colorado Sun. The case moves forward despite Booth's claims to police about his motivations.

Meanwhile, conservatives are mounting opposition to Colorado's Proposition 137, which would direct taxpayer dollars toward land conservation efforts. Critics argue the measure amounts to corporate welfare for wealthy nonprofits—particularly The Nature Conservancy, the ballot initiative's primary sponsor (Complete Colorado). Rather than letting private conservation groups fund their own missions, Coloradans would be forced to subsidize them through government spending.

The Prop 137 fight reflects a broader conservative concern: government shouldn't pick winners and losers among special interests, no matter how environmentally friendly they claim to be. If land conservation is genuinely valuable, market forces and voluntary donations should fund it—not mandatory taxpayer extraction.

The Booth prosecution raises separate questions about protest dynamics and self-defense claims that will likely divide legal observers.

Taxpayers should watch whether Prop 137 gains traction in November and whether similar "public-private" conservation schemes gain momentum statewide.
