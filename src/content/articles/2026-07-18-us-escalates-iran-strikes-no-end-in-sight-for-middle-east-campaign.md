# U.S. Intensifies Iran Campaign as Middle East Conflict Escalates

The U.S. military has conducted seven consecutive nights of strikes against Iran, with no clear off-ramp in sight—raising questions about mission scope and long-term strategy in a region where American commitments have a habit of metastasizing into open-ended deployments.

According to U.S. Central Command, strikes continued through July 17, targeting infrastructure including bridges and water treatment facilities (9News Denver). President Trump addressed the nation in a primetime speech, insisting the campaign is proceeding as intended (9News Denver), though the Washington Examiner reports CENTCOM has offered no timeline for de-escalation.

**The conservative concern:** Sustained military operations without clearly defined exit criteria or congressional authorization raise red flags for taxpayers footing the bill. Republicans have long warned against nation-building and indefinite Middle East commitments that drain resources from domestic priorities—including Colorado's infrastructure, border security, and energy independence. The lack of transparency about objectives and duration mirrors past conflicts that consumed billions while delivering murky results.

Democrats typically frame such operations through a humanitarian or containment lens, but conservatives argue the burden of proof should rest on demonstrating how escalating strikes advance measurable American interests rather than entangling us deeper in regional proxy wars.

**Watch for:** Congressional pushback on war powers and demands for a formal authorization or exit strategy.
