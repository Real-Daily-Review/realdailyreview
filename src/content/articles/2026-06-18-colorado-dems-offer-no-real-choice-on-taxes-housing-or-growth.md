# Colorado Democrats Offer Voters Little Choice on Policy Substance

Colorado's Democratic gubernatorial primary has devolved into a Trump-focused slugfest with virtually no daylight between the two frontrunners on the issues that matter to taxpayers. U.S. Sen. Michael Bennet and Attorney General Phil Weiser are so aligned on housing, taxes, and economic policy that they've abandoned substantive debate in favor of attacking President Trump (Colorado Sun). The race appears tighter than Democrats want to admit—Bennet recently loaned his campaign $1 million, signaling internal concern about momentum (Colorado Sun).

Bennet, leaning on his long record as an elected official and government bureaucrat (Denver Post), represents continuity with Colorado's progressive establishment. Weiser, the sitting AG, offers no meaningful alternative vision. Republicans argue this primary exposes the left's policy bankruptcy: when two Democrats can't find genuine differences on governing Colorado, it's because they're reading from the same big-government playbook.

The lack of debate over taxes and housing is particularly telling. Colorado families and small businesses are struggling with cost-of-living pressures and regulatory burdens—issues neither Democrat appears willing to address honestly.

Watch whether the eventual Democratic nominee commits to specific tax relief or housing deregulation, or whether they'll simply pivot to Trump attacks in the general election.
