# Trump Seeks to Vacate New York Conviction as Cohen Recants

President Trump is moving to overturn his Manhattan conviction after key witness Michael Cohen recanted testimony in the case brought by DA Alvin Bragg, signaling a potential legal opening for the president to dismiss what conservatives view as a politically motivated prosecution.

Trump announced Friday he plans to ask New York to drop "any remnant" of the case stemming from the 2023 indictment over allegedly falsified business records related to hush-money payments (Washington Examiner). The recantation by Cohen—Trump's former fixer and prosecution star witness—undermines the DA's core narrative, though Trump's legal path to vacating the conviction remains uncertain (Just The News).

Conservatives have long characterized the Bragg case as a weaponized prosecution designed to damage Trump politically. The recantation bolsters that argument: if the prosecution's linchpin witness now contradicts his own testimony, the case's evidentiary foundation crumbles. Republicans argue this exemplifies the left's use of the courts as a political tool.

Democrats and Bragg's office have not yet responded publicly to Cohen's recantation or Trump's request, but the move sets up a legal battle over whether New York will voluntarily dismiss charges or whether Trump must pursue appellate remedies.
