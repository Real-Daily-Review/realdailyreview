# Rubio's Western Defense Draws Conservative Scrutiny Over Historical Framing

Secretary of State Marco Rubio's recent address on Western civilization and values—delivered against the backdrop of the Acropolis—aims to rally confidence in American institutions against authoritarian rivals like China and Russia. But fellow conservatives are questioning whether his framing adequately reckons with the West's actual historical record.

National Review argues that defending Western values requires honest acknowledgment of past hubris and mistakes by those who built our institutions, not simply reassertion of their superiority. The Washington Examiner similarly contends Rubio misses critical nuances in how the West should present itself to the world.

The debate reflects a broader conservative divide: should America's case rest on ideological confidence in liberal democracy and free markets, or on humbler recognition that Western achievements coexist with real failures? For Colorado voters skeptical of government overreach and progressive revisionism alike, the question matters—it shapes how America argues for limited government and individual liberty against socialist and authoritarian alternatives.

Watch whether this conservative-on-conservative critique influences how the State Department frames U.S. foreign policy toward China and Russia going forward.
