# Woman Shot at Aurora ICE Facility Returns to Protest Despite Suspect's Claims

A woman wounded in a shooting outside Aurora's ICE detention facility has resumed protesting there, rejecting the suspect's account that he fired only to intimidate demonstrators. According to a new affidavit obtained by KDVR, the shooter claimed he intentionally pulled the trigger but never meant to hit anyone—a defense that rings hollow to the victim, who stated bluntly: "Because somebody was hurt by the words that I was saying to him, that doesn't give you the right to shoot somebody."

The incident underscores the volatile environment surrounding immigration enforcement operations in Colorado. Aurora's ICE facility has become a flashpoint for activist protests, and this shooting—whether characterized as intentional or reckless—demonstrates the real-world dangers when political tensions escalate into violence.

Conservatives and Second Amendment advocates face a delicate position here: while defending gun rights, they must also acknowledge that shooting at protesters, regardless of intent, crosses a legal and moral line. The suspect's claim that he was merely trying to scare people rather than harm them strains credibility and undermines legitimate self-defense arguments.

Watch whether prosecutors pursue charges that reflect the actual harm caused, and whether the incident becomes a flashpoint for further restrictions on lawful protest or firearm rights around federal facilities.
