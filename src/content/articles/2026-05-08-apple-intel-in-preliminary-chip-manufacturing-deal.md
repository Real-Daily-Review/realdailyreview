# Apple, Intel in Preliminary Chip Deal

Apple and Intel have reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to manufacture chips for Apple hardware, according to The Wall Street Journal, marking a significant reversal after Apple's well-publicized shift to in-house Apple Silicon processors.

The deal's scope remains unclear—WSJ reports it is "still unclear" which Apple products would receive Intel chips. Neither company has confirmed the arrangement; Intel spokesperson Kiana Ducey declined to comment, and Apple did not respond to requests for comment.

The potential partnership comes as Intel undergoes leadership and financial restructuring. The chipmaker appointed Lip-Bu Tan as CEO in March 2025, and the U.S. government acquired a 10 percent stake in August, according to The Verge. Intel, Samsung, and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company are the only three firms globally capable of producing the most advanced chips required for AI applications, per CNBC.

Intel shares surged on the news, with analysts viewing the deal as a potential strategic pivot for the company's manufacturing operations.

Watch for official confirmation from either company and details on which product lines—if any—will use Intel-manufactured components.
