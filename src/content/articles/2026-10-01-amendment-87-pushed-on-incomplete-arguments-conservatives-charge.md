# Colorado's Amendment 87 Faces Skepticism Over Incomplete Arguments

Colorado's media establishment and state legislature are pushing Amendment 87 with what conservatives argue are incomplete and misleading claims, according to analysis from Complete Colorado.

The amendment has drawn scrutiny from right-leaning outlets questioning the framing presented to voters. Complete Colorado contends that proponents are relying on selective arguments rather than full disclosure of the measure's implications—a common conservative complaint about ballot initiatives backed by government and media allies.

Meanwhile, election security remains a live issue in Colorado. The Denver Post reported that three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024 after passing signature verification, though both were eventually caught. The cases involved Mesa County residents who claimed they were testing the system's vulnerabilities. While the Post frames Colorado's voting infrastructure as robust—citing "many checks and balances"—the incidents underscore that no system is foolproof.

Republicans and conservatives have long pushed for stronger ballot security measures, including voter ID requirements and stricter signature verification protocols. Amendment 84, which would require Social Security numbers on ballots, emerged partly from these concerns, though Democrats argue such measures create unnecessary barriers to voting.

**Watch for:** how Amendment 87 performs at the ballot box and whether Colorado voters reject the incomplete arguments conservatives say dominate the pro-amendment messaging.
