# Boulder PD Eyes New DNA Tech to Crack JonBenét Case After Three Decades

Thirty years after six-year-old JonBenét Ramsey was murdered in her Boulder home, investigators say emerging DNA technology could finally crack Colorado's most infamous cold case—but they're not ready to deploy it yet.

According to the Colorado Sun, Boulder police acknowledge that cutting-edge DNA techniques hold promise for identifying the killer. However, KDVR reports that law enforcement says the technology isn't sufficiently validated for investigative use in this case.

The hesitation reflects a legitimate concern: premature reliance on unproven forensic methods could contaminate the investigation or produce false leads, wasting resources and potentially closing doors on viable evidence. Conservatives and law-and-order advocates generally support rigorous, science-based investigative standards—rushing to judgment or deploying untested tools serves neither justice nor the Ramsey family.

Boulder PD's cautious approach is defensible, though the three-decade stall tests public patience. The case has haunted Colorado's reputation and remains a symbol of investigative frustration. If validated DNA technology becomes available and reliable, deploying it promptly should be non-negotiable.

Watch whether Boulder police commit to a concrete timeline for adopting new DNA methods, or whether bureaucratic caution continues to delay closure for a family and community that deserve answers.
