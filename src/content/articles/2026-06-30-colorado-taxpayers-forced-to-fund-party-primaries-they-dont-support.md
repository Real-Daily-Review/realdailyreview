# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are bankrolling primary elections for political parties—whether they belong to them or not, and whether they agree with them or not.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state government has no legitimate business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties, yet it continues to force taxpayers to finance party primaries. The conservative outlet argues this amounts to compelled subsidy of partisan machinery, a practice fundamentally at odds with limited government and individual liberty.

The core complaint is straightforward: Why should a Colorado voter who is unaffiliated, or who belongs to a party different from the major two, be forced to pay for Democratic or Republican primary elections? *Complete Colorado* frames this as a "courageous" move toward separating state from party—a principle conservatives have long championed.

This touches on a broader conservative argument about government overreach. Republicans and limited-government advocates contend that the state should not be in the business of subsidizing partisan political machinery at all. Parties are private organizations; let them fund their own elections.

The question now is whether Colorado lawmakers will act on this principle or continue the status quo of taxpayer-financed party privilege.

Watch whether any legislative effort emerges to end state funding of party primaries—and whether Democrats will defend the current system or concede the point.
