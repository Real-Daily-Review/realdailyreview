# Michigan Republican Sees Opening as Democrats Nominate Far-Left Candidate

Michigan Republicans smell blood in the water after Democrats nominated a far-left primary winner who alienated the party's moderate wing.

GOP Senate nominee Mike Rogers is actively courting disaffected Democratic voters following Abdul El-Sayed's narrow primary victory, according to Fox News and The Daily Wire. Rogers released a video Friday pitching himself to moderate Democrats who feel abandoned by their party's leftward lurch. The play targets roughly 728,000 voters who backed Rep. Haley Stevens (D-MI)—a more centrist alternative—over El-Sayed in the primary.

Rogers' strategy reflects a broader Republican calculation: El-Sayed's victory signals the Democratic base has moved so far left that it's leaving room for Republicans to poach swing voters and disaffected moderates. The Daily Wire characterized El-Sayed's win as "divisive," suggesting the nominee represents a faction rather than party consensus.

This dynamic matters for Colorado. As national Democrats continue drifting toward progressive orthodoxy on energy, taxes, and regulation, Colorado Republicans may find similar openings among voters exhausted by one-party rule in Denver. If Michigan's moderate Democrats are genuinely up for grabs, Colorado's independent-minded voters—particularly in energy-dependent regions and suburban areas—could prove equally persuadable.

Watch whether Rogers' outreach actually converts Democratic primary voters, or whether party loyalty ultimately prevails.
