# Boulder's $34M Sundance Gamble Backfiring as Lodging Costs Soar

Boulder's taxpayer-funded bet to land the Sundance Film Festival is already showing cracks, with accommodation prices spiraling far beyond what city officials promised.

The city committed $34 million in public money to bring the prestigious festival from Park City, Utah—betting that Boulder could offer cheaper lodging and a more accessible event. That pitch has collapsed. Homes in Boulder are now listed at over $5,000 per night, undercutting the original value proposition that was supposed to justify the massive public investment (CPR News, KDVR Fox31).

The price surge raises a hard question: Did Boulder overpay for a festival that's pricing out the very attendees it aimed to attract? When Park City hosted Sundance, lodging competed with ski tourism. Boulder promised relief from that squeeze. Instead, property owners are capitalizing on the festival's prestige, and the city's $34 million subsidy appears to have simply inflated local prices without delivering the promised affordability.

This is a cautionary tale for Colorado communities considering similar "economic development" schemes. Government spending rarely produces the outcomes politicians promise, and taxpayers often foot the bill for private-sector windfalls.

Watch whether Boulder's city council acknowledges the failed assumptions behind this deal or doubles down with more public money to "fix" the problem they created.
