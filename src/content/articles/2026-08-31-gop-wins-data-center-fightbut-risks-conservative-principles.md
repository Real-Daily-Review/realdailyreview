# Republicans Win Data Center Battles—But Risk Losing Conservative Principles in the Process

Republicans are scoring political victories against data center expansion across the country, but conservatives may be surrendering free-market principles in the fight, warns the *Washington Examiner*.

The pattern is clear: GOP candidates are running *to the left* of Democratic incumbents on data centers, demanding development freezes and stricter regulations. In Pennsylvania, the Republican gubernatorial candidate is positioning himself as tougher on data center projects than the sitting governor—a striking reversal of typical ideological lines.

The danger, according to conservative analysis, is that Republicans are embracing the very regulatory overreach and government intervention they typically oppose. By weaponizing data center opposition at the ballot box, the Right risks normalizing the precedent that government should micromanage private development and energy infrastructure decisions.

For Colorado, this matters. The state has attracted significant data center investment, and if the national GOP trend accelerates toward blanket opposition rather than case-by-case market analysis, Colorado Republicans may face pressure to adopt similar anti-development stances—potentially harming the state's economy and energy sector.

Conservatives should watch whether this data center populism metastasizes into broader attacks on private infrastructure investment and whether Colorado's GOP leadership resists the temptation to out-regulate Democrats for short-term political gain.
