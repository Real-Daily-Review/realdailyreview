# Federal Health Agencies Under Fire for Loose Death Counts and Vaccine Accountability

Pennsylvania's measles outbreak has exposed troubling gaps in how federal health bureaucrats track and report disease deaths—raising questions about data integrity that should concern Colorado taxpayers footing the bill for these agencies.

State officials claimed two measles-associated deaths in Lancaster County last week, but the CDC declined to include them in its weekly outbreak update, citing an ongoing investigation (Washington Examiner). The move highlights a pattern: health agencies make sweeping public claims about vaccine-preventable deaths, then quietly walk back the certainty when pressed for evidence.

Just The News reports the Pennsylvania governor has faced criticism across the political spectrum for overstating what the data actually shows and withholding information about deaths attributed to being unvaccinated. Meanwhile, the FDA has allowed Pfizer to sidestep commitments from its last COVID vaccine approval—another sign federal regulators are letting pharmaceutical companies off the hook.

Republicans and conservatives argue this loose accounting undermines public trust in health institutions. When agencies blur the line between "associated with" and "caused by," they weaponize statistics to justify one-size-fits-all mandates that ignore individual risk profiles and natural immunity.

Colorado families deserve transparent, evidence-based health guidance—not bureaucratic hand-waving. Watch whether Colorado's health department demands the same accountability standards from federal partners that it imposes on state hospitals and clinics.
