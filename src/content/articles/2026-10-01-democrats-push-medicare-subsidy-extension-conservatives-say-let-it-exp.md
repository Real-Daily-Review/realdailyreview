# Congress Should Let Medicare Subsidy Expire, Not Extend Wasteful Spending

Democrats are pushing Congress to extend a temporary Medicare Part D premium stabilization program beyond its 2026 expiration date—another bid to mask government spending with temporary price controls that ultimately burden taxpayers.

The program, created during the prescription drug benefit's overhaul, shields seniors from sharp premium increases. But according to the *Washington Examiner*, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services now says Part D insurers have sufficient experience to operate without the subsidy. Democrats argue the extension protects seniors from sticker shock. Conservatives counter that extending the program simply delays inevitable costs while adding to federal debt—and that insurers have proven they can manage premiums without government props.

The real issue: Democrats want to claim credit for "affordable" prescriptions while hiding the true cost from voters. Republicans argue Congress should let the program sunset as scheduled, allowing market forces and insurer competition to determine sustainable pricing rather than creating permanent entitlements disguised as temporary relief.

For Colorado seniors and taxpayers, this is a test of whether Washington will finally restrain spending or keep kicking the debt can down the road.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democratic members—backs the extension or joins Republicans in letting the subsidy expire on schedule.
