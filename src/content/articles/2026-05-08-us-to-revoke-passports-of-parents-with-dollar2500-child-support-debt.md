# US to Revoke Passports of Parents with Child Support Debt

The State Department will revoke passports of Americans with outstanding child support obligations exceeding $2,500, according to new federal policy.

The Department of Health and Human Services will notify the State Department of all parents with past-due payments above that threshold, triggering passport revocation for those who hold valid documents, PBS NewsHour reports. The policy aims to compel payment of child support arrears.

The threshold of $2,500 applies to cases where parents have failed to meet court-ordered payment obligations. According to BBC World, the measure could affect a significant population of Americans with unpaid child support debt.

The revocation process will target existing passport holders in the affected group, effectively preventing them from international travel until their child support obligations are resolved or fall below the threshold.

The policy represents an escalation in enforcement mechanisms available to states and the federal government to collect overdue child support payments. It joins existing tools such as wage garnishment, tax refund interception, and driver's license suspension.

Watch for implementation details on how parents will be notified before revocation and what appeal or payment plan options may be available.
