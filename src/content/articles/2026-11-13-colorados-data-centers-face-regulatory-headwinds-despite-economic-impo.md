# Colorado's Data Centers Power the Digital Economy—But Regulators Keep Throwing Up Roadblocks

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are critical infrastructure powering modern life, yet state and local regulators continue imposing restrictions that threaten this vital industry, according to Complete Colorado.

Data centers drive everything from cloud computing to artificial intelligence—the backbone of the digital economy. Colorado's geographic advantages, reliable power grid, and tech talent make the state a natural hub. Yet progressive policymakers often treat these facilities as environmental liabilities rather than economic assets.

Conservatives argue that excessive regulation—whether through energy mandates, water restrictions, or zoning obstacles—risks pushing data center investment to neighboring states with friendlier business climates. Colorado's competitive edge depends on keeping regulatory barriers low and letting market forces guide development.

The stakes are real for Colorado taxpayers and workers. Data centers create high-wage jobs, generate substantial tax revenue, and anchor the state's tech economy. Strangling this sector through overregulation serves no one—least of all working Coloradans who benefit from the jobs and tax base these companies provide.

Watch whether the Colorado legislature moves to streamline permitting for data center expansion or, conversely, whether environmental groups push new restrictions that could chill investment.
