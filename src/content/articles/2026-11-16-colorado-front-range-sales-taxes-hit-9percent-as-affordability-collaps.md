# Colorado Front Range Sales Taxes Double Down on Affordability Crisis

Colorado's Front Range municipalities are pricing out working families faster than ever. According to *Complete Colorado*, the number of areas charging combined sales tax rates above 9% has nearly doubled—from 7 of 31 municipalities in the district to 13—hammering residents already squeezed by inflation and housing costs.

This tax creep reflects a broader pattern: government keeps spending, and taxpayers keep paying. Democrats and local officials argue these levies fund essential services like schools, transit, and infrastructure. But conservatives counter that municipalities are addicted to tax hikes instead of controlling costs and eliminating waste. When combined sales taxes exceed 9%, Colorado families lose purchasing power on groceries, gas, and goods—money that could stay in their pockets or fuel local business growth.

The *Washington Examiner* notes the national parallel: since the Affordable Care Act's 2010 passage, federal healthcare spending jumped from $765 billion to $1.5 trillion annually, yet average family premiums nearly doubled to $2,360 a month. More regulation, more spending—less affordability.

Colorado's Front Range is becoming a cautionary tale: tax hikes without spending discipline don't solve problems; they create them. Taxpayers should watch whether Republican state lawmakers push back on local tax-and-spend momentum or cave to the pressure.
