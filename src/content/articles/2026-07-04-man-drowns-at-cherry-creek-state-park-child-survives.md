# Man Drowns at Cherry Creek State Park; Child Survives

Colorado Parks and Wildlife is investigating a drowning at Cherry Creek State Park after a man fell off a paddleboard and failed to resurface, leaving questions about water safety oversight at one of the state's busiest recreation areas.

According to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31, the incident occurred around 3:49 p.m. when a man and child were paddleboarding just south of the swim beach. The child made it back to shore, but the man did not survive, CPW said.

The tragedy underscores a persistent concern among Colorado conservatives: whether state parks agencies adequately staff lifeguard stations and enforce basic water-safety protocols—or whether budget constraints and bureaucratic priorities have left gaps in public safety. Cherry Creek State Park draws thousands of visitors annually, yet drowning prevention remains inconsistently resourced across Colorado's park system.

CPW has not yet released details on whether lifeguards were present, water conditions, or whether the paddleboarders were wearing life jackets—information critical to understanding whether this was a preventable tragedy or an unavoidable accident.

Conservatives and taxpayers should demand transparency from CPW on staffing levels, safety equipment availability, and what systemic changes—if any—will prevent similar incidents.
