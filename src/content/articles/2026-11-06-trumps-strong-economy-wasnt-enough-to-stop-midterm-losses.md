# Trump's Strong Economy Couldn't Prevent Midterm Losses—Here's Why

Strong job growth and a roaring economy weren't enough to save Republicans from midterm losses, raising hard questions about messaging and voter sentiment heading into 2026.

In October, U.S. employers added 165,000 jobs—nearly triple the 59,000 expected—and unemployment dropped, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data cited by The Daily Wire. By any traditional measure, the economy was performing well as voters headed to the polls. Yet Republicans still faced significant midterm headwinds focused on affordability and cost-of-living concerns.

National Review and conservative outlets are now asking what went wrong. The Daily Caller reports tension within Trump's own base, with the former president appearing to mock supporters over stock market volatility—a move that has angered key voters already frustrated by inflation's lingering effects on their wallets.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado voters, like Americans nationwide, have felt the squeeze of higher prices on groceries, energy, and housing despite headline job numbers. Republicans must reckon with why economic data didn't translate to electoral gains in a state where energy workers and small-business owners have been hit hard by progressive policies.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether the GOP will refocus its 2026 messaging on real purchasing power and cost-of-living relief—or continue relying on statistics that don't match voters' lived experience.
