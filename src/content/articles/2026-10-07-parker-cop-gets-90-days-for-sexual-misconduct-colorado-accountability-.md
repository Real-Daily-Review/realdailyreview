# Colorado Law Enforcement Officer Sentenced for Sexual Misconduct; School Counselor Also Convicted

A former Parker police sergeant has been handed a lenient sentence that should alarm Colorado taxpayers concerned about accountability in government. Troy Brienzo received just 90 days in jail and four years probation for unlawful sexual conduct by a peace officer and first-degree official misconduct involving two adult victims he encountered through a youth law enforcement career program, according to KDVR Fox31.

The sentence underscores a troubling pattern: Colorado's criminal justice system appears reluctant to impose serious consequences on public employees who abuse their authority. Separately, a former Fort Collins school counselor, Janell Edsall, was sentenced to 60 months of sex offender intensive probation with no contact restrictions for attempted sexual exploitation of a child (9News Denver).

Both cases involve individuals positioned to mentor and influence young Coloradans—precisely the positions requiring the strictest vetting and accountability. When law enforcement and school officials betray that trust, the public loses confidence in institutions meant to protect children and enforce the law.

**What conservatives and taxpayers should watch:** Whether Colorado lawmakers will tighten sentencing guidelines for public employees convicted of sexual misconduct and abuse of authority, and whether police departments strengthen background checks and oversight of youth mentorship programs.
