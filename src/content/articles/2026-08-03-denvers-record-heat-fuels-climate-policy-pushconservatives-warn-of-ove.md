# Denver Swelters Through Second-Hottest July on Record—But Don't Expect Climate Alarmists to Let Facts Get in the Way

Denver is experiencing genuine heat this summer, with July 2026 ranking as the second-hottest on record and the Rockies' home opener hitting the second-highest first-pitch temperature in Coors Field history (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). Downtown and Denver International Airport both topped 100 degrees again Sunday, marking the city's fourth triple-digit day of the season.

The data is real. What matters now is how Colorado's political class responds.

Conservatives should brace for the inevitable: progressive activists and their media allies will weaponize these temperatures to justify expanded government control over energy policy, land use, and transportation. They'll demand more regulations, higher taxes on energy producers, and restrictions on Colorado's oil and gas industry—all in the name of "climate action."

Republicans counter that Colorado's economy depends on responsible energy development and that unilateral regulatory overreach here won't cool the planet while it *will* cost jobs and drive up utility bills for working families. They point out that weather events, however hot, don't prove causation—and that adaptation beats economic self-sabotage.

Watch whether Denver's city council and state legislators use this summer's heat wave to justify new climate mandates that could hobble Colorado's energy sector and raise costs for taxpayers already squeezed by inflation.
