# Ukraine Breaks Trump's Energy Ceasefire, Resumes Russian Refinery Strikes

President Trump's unilateral energy ceasefire in Ukraine just collapsed—and it reveals the limits of American leverage over Kyiv without congressional oversight or clear strategic goals.

Ukrainian forces resumed attacks on Russian oil refineries this week, striking Rosneft's Ryazan facility southeast of Moscow, just days after Trump declared a pause in such operations (Washington Examiner). Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky accused Russia of violating the truce by continuing attacks on Ukraine's power grid, and he leveled a sharper charge: that the U.S. betrayed Kyiv by withdrawing critical defense support (Just The News).

**The conservative concern:** Trump's ceasefire announcement lacked teeth. Russia never agreed to it, Ukraine ignored it, and the U.S. has no enforcement mechanism—yet American taxpayers are still footing the bill for Ukraine's war effort. Republicans argue this underscores why Congress, not the executive branch alone, must authorize and oversee foreign military aid. Without clear strategic objectives and exit ramps, the U.S. risks open-ended entanglement in a European conflict.

Zelensky's complaint about American "betrayal" also signals that Kyiv expects unlimited U.S. support regardless of diplomatic realities—a dangerous expectation for American fiscal hawks watching defense spending spiral.

**Watch whether Congress demands accountability for Ukraine aid and whether Trump's team develops an actual negotiating strategy, not just symbolic ceasefires.**
