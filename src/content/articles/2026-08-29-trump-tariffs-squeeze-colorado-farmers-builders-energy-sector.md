# Trump's Tariff Gamble Threatens Colorado Energy and Agriculture

President Trump's escalating trade war—now targeting Canadian imports with 50% tariffs on lumber, agricultural products, and electronics—risks real pain for Colorado's economy, even as conservatives debate whether protectionism serves American interests.

The tariffs hit Colorado where it counts. Lumber tariffs threaten construction costs and housing affordability across the state. Agricultural tariffs endanger Colorado farmers and ranchers who depend on cross-border trade and Canadian markets. Energy development, already hamstrung by Biden-era regulations, faces added uncertainty as supply chains tighten and input costs rise.

Republicans are split. Trump's base argues tariffs protect American workers and manufacturing from unfair Chinese and Canadian competition. Critics, including many free-market conservatives, contend that broad tariffs function as a tax on American consumers and businesses—exactly the kind of government-imposed economic distortion conservatives typically oppose (Washington Examiner). National Review has called the approach "absurd," warning that trade wars create losers among America's own producers and workers.

For Colorado, the real question is whether tariff revenue or jobs gained in protected sectors offset the costs borne by farmers, builders, and energy companies dependent on efficient supply chains and open markets.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly those in agriculture and energy districts—pushes back against tariffs that harm their constituents' bottom lines.
