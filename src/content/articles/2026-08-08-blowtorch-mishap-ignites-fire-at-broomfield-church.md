# Blowtorch Weed-Burning Goes Wrong at Broomfield Church

A preventable fire at a Broomfield church Friday morning is a stark reminder of why property owners need to think twice before DIY fire hazard solutions—and why Colorado's dry conditions demand common sense, not shortcuts.

Beautiful Savior Church near West 120th Avenue and Main Street caught fire around 9:20 a.m. when someone attempting to burn weeds with a blowtorch lost control of the flames (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The misuse of an open flame in Colorado's arid climate turned a routine maintenance task into a fire department emergency.

The incident underscores a broader reality: Colorado's fire season runs long and hot, and even small mistakes can spiral into major property damage. While the church's property damage appears contained, the broader lesson cuts deeper—government can't regulate common sense, but Coloradans can exercise it.

This is precisely why property rights matter: landowners should be free to maintain their own property without unnecessary bureaucratic interference. But that freedom comes with responsibility. In a state where wildfire risk is real and growing, residents need to understand the consequences of their choices.

Watch whether Broomfield or the state uses this incident to justify new weed-burning restrictions or fire-prevention mandates that could burden homeowners and small businesses.
