# Generation Uninterested: High School Seniors Rejecting Marriage and Family

A generational shift is underway—high school seniors are increasingly uninterested in marriage and parenthood, marking a departure from decades of unfulfilled family desires that characterized the Baby Bust.

According to the Washington Examiner, birth rates have declined for over 18 years, but until now, the decline reflected unmet demand for children. That's changing. Today's teenagers are actively rejecting family formation itself, not simply postponing it due to economic constraints.

The cultural implications run deep. Conservatives have long warned that progressive ideology—emphasizing individual autonomy over community bonds and family stability—would hollow out the institutions that anchor American society. This data suggests those warnings are materializing among the youngest voters.

Colorado families and taxpayers should take note: declining birth rates strain pension systems, reduce the workforce base, and shrink the tax base needed to fund government obligations. States betting on immigration to offset population decline are gambling with cultural cohesion and labor market dynamics.

The question for conservatives isn't whether to reverse this trend through coercion—that's antithetical to liberty—but whether to articulate why family formation, marriage, and parenthood remain sources of meaning, stability, and prosperity that no government program can replicate.

Watch whether Colorado policymakers acknowledge this demographic reality when crafting education and workforce policy over the next budget cycle.
