# Trump's Prisoner Diplomacy: A Transactional Win for Freedom

The Trump administration has demonstrated that American leverage can still extract real concessions for political prisoners held by authoritarian regimes—a stark contrast to years of rhetorical hand-wringing from the diplomatic establishment.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, President Trump's envoys secured the release of dozens of political prisoners from Belarus after direct engagement with Alexander Lukashenko, with sanctions relief on the Belarusian state airline serving as the negotiating currency. The *National Review* frames Pastor Ezra Jin's case as proof that "American power can still be leveraged to advance human freedom."

The approach is transactional and unapologetic. Rather than endless multilateral committees or toothless UN resolutions, Trump's team identified what authoritarian leaders want—sanctions relief, diplomatic recognition—and used it to extract tangible results: freed prisoners, not press releases.

Critics on the left typically argue that sanctions relief legitimizes bad actors. Republicans counter that securing the freedom of political prisoners justifies pragmatic dealmaking; ideology matters less than outcomes for those languishing in cells.

The model has already spread: Georgian oligarchs are reportedly taking notes on how to leverage prisoner releases for geopolitical gain, the *Examiner* reports.

**Watch:** Whether this transactional approach becomes standard U.S. policy or remains ad-hoc depends on whether future administrations maintain Trump's willingness to negotiate directly with adversaries.
