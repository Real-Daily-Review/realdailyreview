# Colorado's Seventh-Largest Wildfire Sparked by Tree on Power Line—Raising Questions About Forest Management

A tree falling on a power line ignited Colorado's Aspen Acres fire on June 29 in Custer County, destroying 850 structures and scorching over 102,000 acres, state investigators confirmed this week. The blaze became the state's seventh-largest wildfire on record.

The fire's origin points to a familiar problem: overgrown forests and dead timber near power infrastructure. According to 9News Denver and the Colorado Sun, the tree that triggered the ignition fell just 200 feet west of Highway 165—terrain typical of Colorado's densely forested southern regions where decades of fire suppression have left dangerous fuel loads.

CPR News notes the Aspen Acres fire burned through areas scorched by six previous blazes, underscoring that wildfire scars alone don't prevent new fires. Conservatives argue this demonstrates the urgent need for aggressive forest thinning, controlled burns, and timber removal—policies often blocked by environmental regulations and litigation that prioritize pristine wilderness over public safety.

The incident also raises accountability questions for utility companies and state forest management agencies. Republicans contend Colorado's regulatory framework makes it too costly and time-consuming for private landowners and power companies to conduct preventive forest maintenance.

Watch for whether state regulators will streamline permitting for forest management projects or continue letting red tape fuel the next catastrophe.
