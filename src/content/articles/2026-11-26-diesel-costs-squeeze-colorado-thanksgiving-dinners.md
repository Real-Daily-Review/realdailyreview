# Thanksgiving Price Surge Hits Colorado Families as Diesel Costs Squeeze Food Supply

Colorado families face steeper Thanksgiving bills this year as diesel-driven transportation costs push up prices on meat, dairy, and fresh produce heading into the holiday (Fox News Politics). The culprit: elevated fuel expenses rippling through the supply chain, making holiday staples noticeably more expensive at checkout.

An economist quoted by Fox News suggests brand flexibility—switching to store labels or less premium cuts—can help shoppers trim costs without sacrificing the meal. But that's cold comfort for Coloradans already squeezed by inflation and higher energy prices, issues conservatives argue stem partly from Biden-era energy policies that have constrained domestic fuel production and driven up transportation costs nationwide.

Republicans point out that Colorado's energy sector—oil, gas, and refining—directly affects diesel availability and pricing. When federal policy restricts domestic energy development, it tightens supply and raises costs for everything from groceries to heating bills. Democrats counter that energy transition investments will lower long-term costs, though that argument rings hollow for families paying more at the grocery store today.

The broader lesson: when government policies constrain energy supply, working families pay the price first—and hardest.
