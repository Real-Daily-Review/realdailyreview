# Colorado Democrats Face Reckoning Over Radical Endorsements

Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed's months-long campaign partnership with antisemitic Twitch streamer Hasan Piker—who has praised Hamas and claimed "America deserved 9/11"—is forcing a hard question for the left: how far will Democratic candidates go for online activist support?

The damage control began after national outrage erupted over the alliance. El-Sayed's team attempted distance from Piker, but the streamer himself torpedoed that strategy, *The Daily Wire* reports, bragging that El-Sayed is "faking moderation to scam voters" and insisting the candidate's core positions haven't shifted. *National Review* documents that Piker was no casual endorser—he actively campaigned alongside El-Sayed when his extremist views were already well known.

**The conservative argument:** This isn't about one Michigan race. *Washington Examiner* opinion argues every Democrat in a competitive contest should answer directly: do you align with Piker's radicalism or not? The episode exposes how Democratic campaigns court the militant left while maintaining plausible deniability.

Colorado Democrats banking on youth turnout and progressive energy should take note. If national party figures are willing to embrace figures who celebrate terrorism and antisemitism for electoral gain, Colorado voters deserve clarity on where local Democrats draw the line—or whether they draw one at all.

Watch whether Colorado Democratic candidates distance themselves from similar radical endorsements or double down on the strategy.
