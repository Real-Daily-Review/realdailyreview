# Preventable Tragedies Expose Failures in Criminal Justice, Border Enforcement

Colorado and the nation are confronting two preventable deaths that indict systemic failures in law enforcement and immigration enforcement alike. In Broomfield, a family grieves after police say a man with prior domestic violence allegations killed their daughter—raising hard questions about why repeat offenders remain free to kill. Meanwhile, a North Carolina case spotlights a three-time deported illegal immigrant allegedly responsible for a fatal crash, underscoring the consequences of failed deportation enforcement (Fox News).

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** Both cases reveal government's core failure—protecting citizens from known threats. When domestic abusers with prior allegations remain on the street, or when illegal immigrants deported three times re-enter and kill, the system has broken its most basic duty. Republicans argue that catch-and-release policies, lenient sentencing, and inadequate immigration enforcement create preventable bloodshed.

Democrats typically counter that isolated cases don't justify sweeping policy changes, and that systemic solutions require more resources and rehabilitation programs—arguments conservatives reject as soft on crime and borders.

For Colorado, the Broomfield case hits home: families deserve a criminal justice system that takes repeat domestic violence seriously, and law enforcement must act decisively on prior allegations.

**Watch whether Colorado lawmakers respond with tougher penalties for repeat offenders or retreat into progressive platitudes about "root causes."**
