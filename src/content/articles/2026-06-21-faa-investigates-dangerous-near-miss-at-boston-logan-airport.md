# FAA Probes Near-Miss at Boston Logan as Safety Questions Mount

A Delta flight was forced to abort its landing Saturday at Boston Logan International Airport to avoid colliding with an American Airlines plane departing from an intersecting runway, according to the FAA investigation now underway (9News Denver, New York Post).

The close call raises serious questions about air traffic control coordination and runway management at one of the nation's busiest hubs. While the FAA hasn't yet detailed what went wrong, the incident underscores the critical importance of robust safety protocols—and the risks when federal agencies fail to enforce them consistently.

For Colorado travelers and businesses relying on commercial aviation, this serves as a reminder that FAA oversight directly affects the safety of flights in and out of Denver International Airport. Colorado's growing aerospace and aviation sector depends on a well-functioning national air traffic system. Any breakdown in coordination or communication between ground control and pilots poses real risks to passengers and crew.

The FAA must conduct a thorough investigation and implement corrective measures immediately. Taxpayers fund the FAA to prevent exactly these kinds of incidents—not to investigate them after the fact.

Watch for the FAA's full incident report and whether it reveals systemic failures in Boston Logan's operations or broader air traffic control deficiencies that could affect Colorado airports.
