# Data Center Backlash Shows Risks of Chasing AI Jobs Without Clear Rules

Pennsylvania's sudden turn against data centers—once hailed as economic saviors—is a cautionary tale for Colorado as the state courts AI and tech investment. According to the Washington Examiner, what began as enthusiasm for reopening shuttered facilities has devolved into local opposition, with data centers now cast as "public enemy No. 1" in some communities.

The shift reflects a broader pattern: communities embrace job-creation promises, then face pushback over power consumption, water usage, property tax impacts, and land use changes. For Colorado—already balancing energy policy, water scarcity, and tech-sector ambitions—the lesson is stark: attract investment without transparent local input and zoning clarity, and you risk the same backlash.

Conservatives argue that clear, predictable regulatory frameworks and property-rights protections are essential to attracting and keeping major infrastructure projects. When local governments allow anti-development sentiment to override economic opportunity, they cede competitive advantage to states with business-friendly policies. Colorado's energy sector and water-dependent economy make this especially relevant: data centers demand both, and without upfront community engagement and honest cost-benefit analysis, political opposition can kill deals overnight.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature and local governments establish clear data-center permitting standards before the next wave of AI investment pitches arrive.
