# Trump's Acting AG Moves Toward Confirmation by Killing $1.8B IRS Settlement

Todd Blanche, Trump's acting attorney general, cleared a major hurdle toward Senate confirmation by agreeing to rescind a $1.8 billion settlement deal the administration had struck with the IRS—a move that satisfies GOP holdouts and aligns with conservative demands to rein in federal overreach.

According to Fox News, Blanche met with Republican senators who had balked at the settlement terms, which critics argued would have expanded IRS enforcement authority and spending without proper congressional oversight. The Daily Caller reports Blanche reached a deal with GOP holdouts including key senators, paving the way for a confirmation vote.

Conservatives have long viewed the IRS as a tool of government weaponization—a concern sharpened after the agency's targeting of conservative groups during the Obama administration. By killing the settlement, Blanche signals a commitment to reining in the tax agency and preventing what Republicans see as unchecked bureaucratic power.

For Colorado taxpayers and small-business owners, the move matters: a more aggressive IRS could mean higher audit rates and tighter scrutiny of deductions. Conversely, a scaled-back agency means less federal harassment of individuals and enterprises.

Watch whether Blanche's confirmation vote succeeds and whether his Justice Department follows through on investigating alleged IRS misconduct under previous administrations.
