# Justice Alito Ends Retirement Speculation: 'I'm Here for Another Term'

Supreme Court Justice Samuel Alito has definitively shut down months of speculation about his retirement, telling the Wall Street Journal he intends to serve another term on the bench. The 76-year-old conservative jurist's statement comes after an embarrassing NPR retraction of a premature retirement announcement by reporter Nina Totenberg—a blunder that only amplified the rumor mill.

For Colorado conservatives, Alito's commitment matters. The justice authored the Dobbs decision overturning Roe v. Wade, a ruling that returned abortion regulation to the states—a win for limited government and federalism that Colorado's left has spent two years trying to undo through ballot measures and legislative overreach. Alito's continued presence on the Court preserves the constitutional originalist majority that has also defended Second Amendment rights and constrained regulatory agency power.

Republicans have long worried about losing conservative justices to retirement during a Democratic presidency. Alito's firm statement removes that uncertainty, at least for now, and signals stability in the Court's ideological composition heading into the next election cycle.

Conservatives should monitor whether the left renews pressure for court-packing legislation or constitutional amendments—their likely response to losing the Dobbs fight.
