# Colorado's Worker Shortage Is Strangling Growth—While Election Law Enforcement Finally Kicks In

Colorado's economy is hitting a wall: the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City reports that a persistent labor shortage is now constraining growth across multiple industries and job sectors throughout Colorado and neighboring states, limiting what businesses can produce and hire (CPR News). The shortage isn't confined to one field anymore—it's spreading.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs is facing the state's first enforcement action under the 2025 voting law, sued for scheduling municipal elections in April rather than the state-mandated November timeframe (Complete Colorado). The lawsuit signals that election law compliance is no longer theoretical.

**The conservative case:** Labor shortages reflect years of government policies that discourage work—extended unemployment benefits, regulatory red tape that strangles small business hiring, and wage-suppressing immigration restrictions that limit the labor pool. Colorado businesses need workers *now*, not bureaucratic solutions. On elections, conservatives have long argued that off-cycle voting depresses turnout and invites manipulation; the 2025 law's November-only requirement was designed to restore integrity and participation.

**What to watch:** Whether Colorado Springs capitulates or fights the lawsuit—and whether other municipalities face similar enforcement, signaling real teeth in the state's election law.
