# Colorado Water Officials Duck While Arizona Leads on River Cuts

Colorado's top water negotiator is sitting on the sidelines while Arizona actually negotiates real compromises on Colorado River usage, according to the Colorado Sun. As western states face historic drought and overallocation, Arizona is putting forward concrete proposals to curtail water consumption—but Colorado officials remain passive, unwilling to make the hard choices they're demanding of others.

The problem is clear: Colorado can't expect other states to sacrifice if state leadership won't lead by example. Water is Colorado's lifeblood. Agriculture, energy production, and Front Range municipalities all depend on reliable Colorado River allocations. Yet instead of proactive negotiation, Colorado's approach amounts to hoping the problem solves itself.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Colorado needs aggressive water leadership—not defensive posturing. The state should be at the table proposing solutions that protect Colorado's interests while acknowledging western realities. Sitting idle while Arizona moves forward risks Colorado getting steamrolled in negotiations that will determine water availability for decades.

The Denver Post's recent commentary on the Republican gubernatorial primary (noting Victor Marx's primary lead) underscores broader frustration: Colorado voters want leaders who actually *do* things, not manage decline.

Watch whether Colorado's water officials finally engage in serious negotiations—or continue ceding leverage to more aggressive states.
