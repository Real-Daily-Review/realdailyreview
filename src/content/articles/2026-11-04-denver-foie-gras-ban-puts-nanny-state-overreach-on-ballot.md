# Denver Foie Gras Ban Puts Nanny-State Overreach on Ballot

Denver voters are being asked to micromanage what restaurants can sell—and how food is prepared—through Initiated Ordinance 311, which would ban foie gras sales and the production methods behind them (Complete Colorado). It's a textbook case of progressive government deciding it knows better than consumers and chefs what belongs on a dinner plate.

The measure reflects a broader pattern of left-wing ballot initiatives targeting personal choice and business operations. While California voters rejected stricter voter ID requirements on Proposition 39 (New York Post), Colorado progressives are busy expanding government control over commerce and culinary decisions—hardly the limited-government approach conservatives advocate.

Meanwhile, Denver voters approved a mill levy override for schools, while similar measures failed in Jefferson and Douglas counties (Denver Post). The mixed results suggest taxpayers remain divided on education spending, even as Denver's tax-and-spend coalition pushes forward.

**The Colorado angle:** If Denver's foie gras ban passes, it signals the city council's willingness to regulate business practices on ideological grounds—a warning sign for Colorado's broader economy and the principle that markets, not government mandates, should determine what consumers buy.

**Watch next:** Whether this ordinance passes and sets a precedent for other Denver-area food and agricultural restrictions.
