# Colorado Taxpayers Face $10M Bill for Assassination Suspect's Defense

Colorado taxpayers are staring down a potential $10 million legal bill to defend Tyler Robinson, the 23-year-old accused of assassinating conservative activist Charlie Kirk, as his defense team wages a scorched-earth challenge to every aspect of the prosecution's case.

According to *The Daily Wire*, Robinson's lawyers are systematically contesting evidence and procedures in pretrial hearings—a strategy that could stretch the case out for months or longer. Multiple legal experts told *The New York Post* that this approach, while Robinson's constitutional right, will rack up substantial public defender costs as the case grinds through Colorado courts.

*National Review* reports that prosecutors have already presented a "mountain of evidence" during pretrial hearings that undermines conspiracy theories circulating among some conservative circles about the Kirk case. The prosecution's case appears strong, yet the defense strategy prioritizes exhausting every legal avenue—a legitimate tactic, but one with real fiscal consequences for state coffers already stretched thin.

For Colorado conservatives, the irony stings: taxpayer dollars funding an aggressive defense against charges in a case targeting one of the right's most prominent voices. Republicans argue that while due process matters, courts should also consider expediting cases where evidence is overwhelming to avoid unnecessary public expense.
