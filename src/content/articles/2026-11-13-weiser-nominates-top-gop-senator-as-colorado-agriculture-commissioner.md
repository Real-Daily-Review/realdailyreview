# Colorado Governor-Elect Taps Top Republican for Agriculture Post

Phil Weiser's incoming administration is signaling bipartisan outreach by nominating Cleave Simpson, the state Senate's top Republican, as Colorado's next agriculture commissioner, according to the *Denver Post*. The move suggests Weiser intends to work across the aisle on issues affecting Colorado's ranching and farming communities.

Simpson's nomination matters for Colorado conservatives and agricultural interests. Agriculture remains vital to the state's economy and rural communities, and having a Republican lead the department could provide a counterweight to progressive environmental regulations that often burden farmers and ranchers. Republicans have consistently argued that overregulation—particularly around water use, land management, and energy development—threatens Colorado's agricultural heritage and rural livelihoods.

Weiser, a Democrat, is also filling other cabinet slots with Denver officials, signaling a more urban-focused administration overall. Conservatives should watch whether Simpson can genuinely influence policy on issues like water rights, grazing permits, and resistance to federal land grabs—or whether he becomes a token appointment with limited authority.

The real test: Will Simpson's appointment translate into tangible protection for Colorado ranchers against regulatory overreach, or will progressive policies still dominate the governor's office?
