# Colorado Marks 150 Years: A State Built on Freedom, Now Facing Progressive Overreach

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives as the state celebrates a frontier legacy of self-reliance and limited government—values increasingly at odds with Denver's expanding regulatory state.

The Colorado Sun and CPR News both covered statewide festivities at the Capitol, where leaders reflected on the state's past and future. The celebrations highlighted Colorado's unique character: a state born from pioneers who valued independence, mining, ranching, and resource development.

Yet the milestone arrives amid tension. Colorado's founding principles—individual liberty, free enterprise, and limited government—clash with the progressive policies now dominating state government. Democrats control both chambers of the legislature and the governor's office, pushing higher taxes, aggressive environmental regulations, and gun restrictions that many Coloradans view as betrayals of the state's frontier ethos.

Republicans and conservatives argue that honoring Colorado's 150-year legacy means returning to the limited-government principles that built the state. They point to overregulation of energy development, water restrictions harming agriculture, and tax burdens that threaten Colorado's competitive edge.

The state's founders would likely recognize the irony: a sesquicentennial celebration of freedom occurring under an increasingly activist government.
