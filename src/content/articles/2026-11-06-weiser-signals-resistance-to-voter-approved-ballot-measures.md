# Colorado Voters Back Three Ballot Measures—But Weiser Signals Delay Tactics

Colorado voters approved three ballot measures backed by Protect Kids Colorado, but the incoming Democratic governor is already signaling resistance to implementation.

Gov.-elect Phil Weiser told the Denver Post the measures—which address transgender acceptance and fentanyl penalties—will require "a long, hard look" before the state acts on them. Translation: expect legal challenges, regulatory delays, and bureaucratic obstruction from a Democratic administration hostile to the will of voters.

Protect Kids Colorado declared victory as the measures passed, even as Colorado Democrats cemented supermajorities in both the House and Senate (Complete Colorado). That legislative dominance gives Democrats the power to water down or undermine voter-approved policies through the budget process, regulatory interpretation, or legislative "fixes."

Conservatives argue this is textbook Democratic overreach: when voters reject progressive orthodoxy at the ballot box, the left simply refuses to implement the results. Weiser's pre-emptive skepticism suggests Colorado taxpayers and parents should expect years of litigation and regulatory foot-dragging rather than prompt execution of democratically approved policy.

Watch whether Weiser's administration actually implements these measures or uses state agencies to delay, rewrite, or effectively nullify them through regulation—a test of whether Colorado elections still mean anything.
