# Florida Primary Tests Antisemitism Tolerance—Colorado Should Watch Its Own Backyard

Florida's Tuesday primaries will reveal whether antisemitic rhetoric and anti-Israel extremism gain traction among Democratic and Republican voters alike, a warning sign for Colorado's own political landscape.

The races pit pro-Israel incumbents against challengers from ideological fringes on both sides (Washington Examiner). President Trump has endorsed Sydney Gruters in District 16 and Catalina Lauf in District 19, signaling the GOP's preference for mainstream candidates (Breitbart). Meanwhile, Democrats face pressure from hard-left activists pushing anti-Israel positions within their party.

The stakes matter beyond Florida. Colorado's Jewish communities are already grappling with how antisemitism plays out in local politics and activism. A Denver Post op-ed highlighted the disconnect between public debate and lived experience—noting that mainstream outlets often frame antisemitism without consulting actual Jewish voices (Denver Post).

Conservatives argue that antisemitism has no place in either party and that pro-Israel positions should remain bipartisan common ground. The Left's tolerance for anti-Israel rhetoric, particularly within progressive activist circles, reflects a troubling blind spot on the left wing of the Democratic Party.

Colorado Republicans and pro-Israel advocates should monitor whether similar extremist challenges emerge in state and local races heading into 2026.
