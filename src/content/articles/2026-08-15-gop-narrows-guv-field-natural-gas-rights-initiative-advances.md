# Colorado Republicans Narrow Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their gubernatorial field to two candidates advancing from the state assembly, signaling a competitive primary ahead—while voters will simultaneously decide whether to enshrine a constitutional "right to natural gas" in November.

The assembly process filtered 18 Republican gubernatorial hopefuls down to two finalists, according to *Complete Colorado*. The move reflects the party's effort to consolidate support around viable general-election candidates in a state where Democrats hold statewide office.

Separately, Initiative 177—backed by Advance Colorado—has qualified for the ballot, *Denver Post* reports. The measure would constitutionally protect Colorado's natural gas industry, a direct response to progressive efforts to phase out fossil fuels and restrict energy development.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and energy advocates argue that enshrining natural gas rights protects Colorado's economy, energy independence, and the jobs of thousands of workers in oil and gas. The left's regulatory assault on hydrocarbon development threatens both household energy costs and state revenue.

**Democrats counter** that the initiative locks in fossil fuel expansion and undermines climate commitments.

Colorado taxpayers and energy workers should watch whether Initiative 177 gains traction—and whether the GOP's two nominated candidates can articulate a pro-energy, pro-growth message in a state increasingly hostile to fossil fuels.
