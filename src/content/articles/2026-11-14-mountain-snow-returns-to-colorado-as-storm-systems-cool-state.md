# Mountain Snow, Cooler Temps Return to Colorado This Weekend

Colorado's brief warm spell is about to break as a parade of storm systems moves into the state, bringing the first meaningful mountain snow and fall-like temperatures back to the high country.

The San Juan Mountains are already picking up 1-2 inches of snow, with a Winter Weather Advisory in effect above 10,000 feet, according to KDVR Fox31. The storms will keep most precipitation south of I-70, meaning Denver and the Front Range will see mostly cloudy skies and cooler—but not snowy—conditions through the weekend.

This weather shift marks a return to more typical late-season patterns after an unusually warm stretch. For Colorado's water-dependent agriculture and ski resort operators, the early snow is welcome news; the state's snowpack directly feeds reservoirs and irrigation systems critical to ranching and farming operations across the state.

The cooler temperatures and moisture also arrive as Colorado heads into fire season transition—early snow in the high country can help reduce wildfire risk heading into fall and winter.

Coloradans should watch whether these storm systems develop into a sustained pattern that delivers the consistent precipitation the state needs to shore up water supplies and snowpack for next year.
