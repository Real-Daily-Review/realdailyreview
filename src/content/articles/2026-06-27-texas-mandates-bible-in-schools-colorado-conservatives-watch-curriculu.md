# Texas Bible Reading Mandate Reignites School Curriculum Wars—Colorado Should Pay Attention

Texas just became the first state in recent history to require Bible stories in K-12 public school curricula, and the move is already sparking a national debate over what belongs in classrooms.

The Texas State Board of Education, controlled by Republicans, voted Friday to mandate Bible passages as part of required reading lists. Supporters argue the Bible is a foundational text for understanding Western literature, history, and law—and that state law permits the curriculum choice. Critics claim it violates the First Amendment and amounts to government-sponsored religion in public schools.

The decision matters for Colorado. As progressive school boards in Denver and Boulder push their own ideological curricula—from gender ideology to critical race theory—conservatives here face a mirror question: *Should public schools teach contested moral and religious frameworks at all?* Republicans counter that secular progressive values are already embedded in many curricula, making the playing field uneven.

Colorado parents and taxpayers should watch whether this Texas precedent emboldens Republican-led school boards in red counties to challenge progressive curriculum mandates, or whether legal challenges force a Supreme Court showdown on First Amendment limits in public education.
