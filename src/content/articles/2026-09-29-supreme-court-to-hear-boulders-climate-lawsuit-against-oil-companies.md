# Supreme Court to Hear Boulder's Radical Climate Lawsuit Against Oil Companies

Boulder's attempt to weaponize the courts against energy producers just cleared a major hurdle—Justice Alito's recusal removes one potential skeptic from the bench.

The Supreme Court will hear oral arguments this fall on whether Boulder can sue oil companies directly for climate damages, a case that could open the floodgates to municipal litigation against the energy sector nationwide. According to CPR News, Justice Alito has recused himself from the case, which targets fossil fuel producers over alleged climate-related harms.

Conservatives argue this lawsuit represents exactly the kind of judicial overreach progressives champion: using courts to accomplish what they cannot through legislation. Rather than debate energy policy through democratic processes, Boulder seeks to impose massive liability on companies that legally produce the fuel America depends on. If the Court allows such suits, energy companies—and ultimately Colorado consumers and workers—face ruinous legal exposure.

The case arrives as Colorado's energy sector remains under sustained regulatory pressure from state Democrats. A ruling favoring Boulder would embolden similar lawsuits in Colorado and beyond, threatening jobs in oil, gas, and coal regions already struggling under state-imposed restrictions.

Watch whether the remaining justices signal openness to this novel theory of corporate liability—and whether Colorado's Republican leadership speaks up for the workers and taxpayers who depend on affordable energy.
