# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Abdul El-Sayed's carefully staged apology tour with Michigan's Democratic Jewish Caucus won't solve the left's deepening antisemitism crisis. The Michigan Democrat met with Jewish leaders to walk back his association with Hasan Piker, a streamer known for anti-American rhetoric and inflammatory remarks about Jewish communities—but El-Sayed refused to fully distance himself from Piker, drawing Republican criticism that the gesture amounts to damage control rather than principle.

Republicans argue the real problem runs deeper: Democrats have largely failed to meaningfully condemn Piker's rhetoric. According to the Washington Examiner, only six Democrats have explicitly spoken out against Piker's latest antisemitic comments blaming Jews for potential violence against them. That's a damning indictment of party leadership's willingness to tolerate extremism within its ranks.

Fox News reports that Mike Rogers, a Republican, called El-Sayed's apology a "sham"—a fair assessment when the candidate won't actually break with the offending figure.

For Colorado voters watching national Democratic politics, this illustrates a broader pattern: progressive tolerance for antisemitism when it comes wrapped in anti-Israel or anti-American messaging. It's a warning sign about where the party's moral compass points when ideology meets identity politics.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic establishment distances itself from similar rhetoric, or follows the national party's lead in looking the other way.
