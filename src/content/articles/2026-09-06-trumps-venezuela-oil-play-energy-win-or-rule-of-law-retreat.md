# Trump's Venezuela Oil Deal: Energy Realpolitik or Dangerous Precedent?

The Trump administration is reportedly negotiating access to Venezuelan oil reserves—a move that could shore up American energy dominance but raises hard questions about property rights and rule of law.

According to *Just The News*, the deal could help secure U.S. energy leadership if domestic shale production declines over time. Diverse foreign resources, the outlet notes, would backstop America's position as a world energy leader.

But conservatives are split. *National Review* argues the move carries troubling cynicism: Trump has repeatedly attacked Venezuela's Chávez regime for seizing American company assets, yet now appears willing to negotiate with the same government—potentially legitimizing the very theft he condemned. The magazine warns that abandoning support for stable international order and rule of law undermines the legal framework that made America prosperous.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil and gas industry depends on predictable property rights and contract enforcement. Any signal that the U.S. government will negotiate with regimes that steal American assets—or that property claims are negotiable based on geopolitical convenience—threatens investor confidence in domestic energy development and could chill future capital investment in Colorado shale operations.

Conservatives should watch whether this deal includes compensation for American companies or sets a precedent for abandoning property-rights protections abroad.
