# Federal Tax Hike on Young Workers Would Deepen Entitlement Crisis, Conservatives Warn

A bipartisan proposal to raise taxes on working-age Americans threatens to worsen the nation's unsustainable entitlement spending spiral, according to conservative analysis.

Sens. Bernie Moreno (R-OH) and Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) are backing a tax increase targeting younger workers—precisely the demographic already bearing the heaviest burden of federal transfer payments to seniors. The *Washington Examiner* notes that the federal government redirects trillions annually from working-age, fertile people to retirees, a dynamic that has contributed to America's declining fertility rate.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans and fiscal hawks argue that raising taxes on young earners compounds an already broken system. Rather than address runaway entitlement spending—Social Security and Medicare consume roughly half the federal budget—lawmakers are choosing to squeeze the very workers needed to sustain these programs long-term. This approach ignores the core problem: unsustainable benefit structures and demographic decline.

**What's at Stake for Colorado:** Young Colorado workers and families already face federal tax burdens that limit their ability to save, invest, and build wealth. Higher levies would reduce disposable income in a state where cost-of-living pressures are mounting.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republicans—will oppose this tax increase or allow it to advance without meaningful resistance.
