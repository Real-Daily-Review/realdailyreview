# Gaza "Peace Deal" Hinges on Hamas Following Through—History Suggests Skepticism

A new Gaza ceasefire framework has emerged, with Hamas formally agreeing to dismantle its military wing for the first time since 2007, according to reporting from the New York Post. The "Board of Peace" has unveiled a 15-point plan aimed at ending the conflict that erupted in late 2023 when Hamas forces stormed Israeli border towns, prompting Israel's counter-invasion (Just The News).

But conservatives and Middle East analysts are right to pump the brakes. National Review notes the hard truth about such agreements: "phase 2 never comes." The skepticism is warranted. Hamas has a decades-long track record of using ceasefires to rearm, regroup, and prepare for the next round of violence. A formal pledge to disarm means little without ironclad verification mechanisms—and the current framework raises five critical questions about implementation that remain unanswered (New York Post).

For American taxpayers, the real concern is whether the U.S. will be pressured into funding reconstruction or security guarantees that amount to rewarding terrorist organizations. The Biden-Harris administration's track record of appeasing hostile actors abroad doesn't inspire confidence.

**Watch whether Congress demands strict verification protocols and refuses blank-check aid commitments before any "phase 2" materialize.**
