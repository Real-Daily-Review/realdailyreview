# Colorado Welfare Work Requirements Face Fresh Progressive Assault

Colorado's welfare system—built on the principle that able-bodied adults should work—is under renewed attack from the left, which wants to weaken work requirements and expand benefits without accountability.

Work-oriented welfare reform has proven successful since the 1990s, reducing dependency and encouraging self-sufficiency. But progressives continue targeting these safeguards, according to *National Review*. The left argues that work requirements are too harsh; conservatives counter that welfare's primary purpose should be temporary relief, not permanent subsidy.

*The Daily Wire* warns that modern welfare programs have created perverse incentives: work is disincentivized, families remain trapped in poverty cycles, and taxpayers foot an ever-growing bill. Republicans argue that genuine compassion means helping people become independent, not comfortable on government checks.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. State welfare spending directly impacts tax burdens on working families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation and regulatory costs. Weakening work requirements would expand the welfare rolls, increasing state expenditures and potentially raising taxes on the very workers the system should encourage.

Democrats will likely frame stricter requirements as "cruel"; conservatives should counter that dependency is the real cruelty—it traps people in poverty while bankrupting the state.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats push to eliminate or soften work requirements in the next legislative session.
