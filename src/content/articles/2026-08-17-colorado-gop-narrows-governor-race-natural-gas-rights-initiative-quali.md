# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary to just two candidates through the state assembly process, tightening the field ahead of the general election. Meanwhile, voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution via Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado and approved by the Secretary of State's office.

The dual developments signal competing priorities within Colorado's center-right coalition. Republicans are consolidating around establishment-vetted candidates, while energy advocates are taking direct action to protect Colorado's natural gas industry from regulatory encroachment—a critical economic driver for the state and a key concern for rural Colorado communities and workers.

Initiative 177 reflects conservative frustration with progressive efforts to phase out natural gas in favor of all-electric mandates. Democrats and environmental groups have pushed aggressive timelines for electrification in buildings and transportation, threatening jobs in Colorado's energy sector and raising costs for households and businesses. Conservatives argue that enshrining energy choice in the constitution prevents future legislatures from imposing unilateral bans.

The initiative's success or failure will reveal whether Colorado voters prioritize energy independence and economic freedom over the progressive climate agenda—a bellwether for 2026 statewide races.

Watch whether Democrats mount a serious campaign against Initiative 177 or attempt to reframe it as an "oil and gas giveaway."
