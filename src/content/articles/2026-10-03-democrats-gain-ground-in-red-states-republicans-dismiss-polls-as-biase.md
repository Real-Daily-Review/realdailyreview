# Democrats Show Strength in Red States, but Republicans Dismiss Polls as Unreliable

A New York Times/Siena College poll has Democrats tied or ahead in several traditionally Republican Senate races—Texas, Alaska, Ohio, and Kansas—signaling potential Democratic gains as midterm elections approach, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The polling snapshot suggests Democrats are competitive in states President Trump carried decisively in 2024, tightening what could become a closer-than-expected Senate map. For Colorado voters watching national trends, this underscores how volatile the political environment has become heading into the midterms.

Republicans, however, dismiss such polling as unreliable. Conservative analysts argue that historical polling errors, particularly in Trump-era elections, consistently underestimate Republican performance. The *Washington Examiner's* opinion section features commentary predicting a Republican sweep in both chambers, arguing that underlying fundamentals—inflation, border security concerns, and voter dissatisfaction with Democratic governance—favor the GOP despite what surveys suggest.

The core dispute: Democrats point to polling showing competitive races in red territory; Republicans counter that polls systematically miss their voters and that real-world conditions favor a Republican wave.

For Coloradans, the national Senate picture matters directly—Colorado's own Senate race will reflect broader momentum, and control of the chamber affects energy policy, water rights, and federal land management that impact the state's economy and way of life.

Watch whether Colorado polling aligns with or diverges from national trends as Election Day nears.
