# Democrat Senator's Son Lands $30M Crypto Deal Days After College Graduation

When a 22-year-old with zero industry experience raises $30 million for a financial startup, it's fair to ask whether political connections matter more than merit.

Theodore Gillibrand, son of Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand (D-NY), secured the funding for a derivatives exchange less than a week after graduating college, according to *Just The News* and *The Daily Wire*. The startup is already valued at $300 million.

The timing raises eyebrows. Gillibrand chairs a Senate subcommittee overseeing financial institutions and has positioned herself as one of Washington's most influential allies to the cryptocurrency industry—a sector hungry for favorable regulation. Republicans and watchdogs argue this arrangement exemplifies how political families leverage government access for private gain, regardless of qualifications.

Democrats have not publicly addressed the optics. Supporters might contend that venture capital decisions are made on merit and market potential, not family ties.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this story underscores a broader conservative concern: when government officials wield regulatory power over industries where their families profit, conflicts of interest become inevitable. The lack of transparency around how a recent college graduate attracted such massive capital—and from whom—demands scrutiny.

Watch whether Congress demands disclosure of the investors backing this venture and whether Gillibrand recuses herself from cryptocurrency legislation.
