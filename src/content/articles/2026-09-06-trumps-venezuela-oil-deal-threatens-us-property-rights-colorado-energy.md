# Trump's Venezuela Oil Play Risks American Credibility—and Colorado's Energy Future

A potential U.S.-Venezuela oil deal under Trump threatens to undermine decades of American commitment to property rights and rule of law, while raising hard questions about domestic energy strategy that directly affect Colorado's oil and gas workers.

National Review flags the core conservative concern: Trump's willingness to negotiate Venezuelan oil access contradicts his own rhetoric about Chávez-era theft of American company assets. The cynicism cuts both ways—abandoning American firms' legitimate claims abroad while potentially weakening incentives for domestic production signals weakness on property rights, a cornerstone of free-market capitalism.

Just The News argues the deal could hedge against declining shale production, noting that American wells deplete over time and diverse foreign resources might sustain energy dominance. But here's the Colorado angle: Colorado's oil and gas sector—which supports thousands of jobs and generates substantial tax revenue—depends on stable, predictable policy favoring domestic extraction. Chasing Venezuelan crude while regulatory pressure mounts on U.S. producers sends the wrong signal to investors and workers betting their livelihoods on American energy independence.

National Review's broader point sticks: a stable world order built on rule of law made America prosperous. Abandoning that principle for short-term oil deals weakens both American credibility and the legal framework protecting Colorado energy companies.

Watch whether the Trump administration clarifies its actual domestic energy priorities—or whether Venezuela becomes a distraction from defending American shale.
