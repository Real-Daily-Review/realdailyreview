# Colorado's Worker Shortage Deepens as State Grapples With Labor-Market Headwinds

Colorado's labor shortage is no longer a post-pandemic blip—it's a structural problem dragging down the state's economy, according to Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City economists (CPR News). The shortage is spreading across industries and job categories, signaling that Colorado businesses face sustained difficulty filling positions even as unemployment remains historically low.

The implications are serious. Without adequate workers, Colorado employers cannot expand, productivity stalls, and wage pressures mount—ultimately hitting consumers and taxpayers. Manufacturing, construction, hospitality, and healthcare sectors are all feeling the pinch.

Conservatives point to Colorado's regulatory environment and tax burden as contributing factors. High housing costs, driven partly by restrictive zoning and environmental regulations, price out workers. Meanwhile, generous unemployment benefits and work-disincentive policies—legacy programs from pandemic-era expansions—may still be suppressing labor-force participation in some demographics.

Republicans argue the state needs to focus on workforce development, reduce regulatory barriers to business expansion, and ensure tax policy doesn't discourage work. The left typically blames "wage stagnation" and calls for more government training programs and wage mandates—approaches conservatives say worsen the shortage by raising employer costs.
