# Colorado Roads Growing Hostile as Aggressive Driving Calls Spike

Colorado State Patrol is sounding the alarm over a surge in dangerous driving behavior, with more than 33,000 calls logged to its aggressive driving report line last year, according to 9News Denver. The spike reflects a troubling trend of road rage incidents across the state, including a recent shooting in Boulder County that left two people arrested (KDVR Fox31).

The data underscores a law-and-order challenge that conservative Coloradans have long warned about: when personal civility erodes and enforcement appears inconsistent, public safety suffers. Road rage isn't merely a traffic inconvenience—it's a symptom of broader social dysfunction that demands serious attention from law enforcement and community standards alike.

CSP's reporting line offers Coloradans a way to document reckless behavior, but the volume of complaints suggests the problem is outpacing response capacity. Republicans and law-and-order advocates argue that consistent enforcement, swift prosecution, and a cultural reset around personal responsibility are essential to reversing the trend.

Meanwhile, separate incidents—including a toy gun bank robbery in Greeley (KDVR Fox31)—highlight how crime continues to test Colorado communities in unexpected ways.
