# Colorado Republicans Eye 2028 Field as National Party Regroups After Midterm Losses

Republicans are already mapping out 2028 presidential contenders after midterm setbacks that have scrambled the GOP primary landscape, according to analysis from the Washington Examiner and National Review.

The midterm results, while disappointing for Republicans nationally, have reset expectations for the next presidential cycle. The Washington Examiner notes that the scale of Republican losses has effectively ended some 2028 candidacies before they began, while simultaneously opening the field for new contenders. Meanwhile, National Review questions whether progressive figures like Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez—despite Democratic gains this cycle—face structural headwinds that could limit their viability in a 2028 general election matchup.

For Colorado Republicans, the 2028 race matters considerably. The state has trended purple in recent cycles, and any GOP nominee will need to appeal to independent voters and suburban moderates who've drifted left on cultural issues while remaining skeptical of big-government economics. Colorado's energy sector, water rights, and Second Amendment voters represent a crucial Republican coalition that the next nominee must energize without alienating the state's growing urban centers.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado Republicans coalesce around a candidate who can defend free-market energy policy and limited government—or whether national GOP fracturing leaves the state vulnerable to Democratic consolidation in 2028.
