# Cornell Gang Rape Case Reopened; Hochul Taps AG James as Special Prosecutor

New York Governor Kathy Hochul has appointed Attorney General Letitia James as special prosecutor in a reopened gang rape case at Cornell University, signaling lost confidence in the local district attorney's handling of the matter.

The case centers on allegations filed by a woman—identified as "Jane Doe"—who sued in September, nearly two years after the alleged incident, naming seven individuals. Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten revived the prosecution based on new evidence the alleged victim submitted.

Hochul stated that newly surfaced information "raised serious questions about how this case was handled" and that she no longer trusts the DA's office to prosecute it fairly (Just The News). The appointment of James, a prominent progressive prosecutor, underscores the governor's determination to pursue the case aggressively.

While the case itself is a New York matter, it reflects broader concerns conservatives raise about campus sexual assault adjudication: the politicization of prosecutorial decisions and the appointment of ideologically aligned prosecutors rather than letting established legal channels work. The two-year delay between the alleged incident and lawsuit filing also raises questions about evidence preservation and the reliability of delayed allegations—issues central to due process debates on college campuses nationwide.

Watch whether James's appointment leads to swift prosecution or becomes another example of high-profile cases weaponized for political gain.
