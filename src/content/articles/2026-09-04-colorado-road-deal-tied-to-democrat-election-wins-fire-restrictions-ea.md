# Western Colorado Fire Restrictions Eased; Road-Funding Deal Hinges on Democrat Election Wins

Recent rainfall across the Western Slope has given fire officials enough breathing room to downgrade restrictions, allowing contained campfires in much of the region (CPR News). But while one crisis eases, Colorado taxpayers face a larger one: backers of a road-funding ballot measure have agreed to pull their initiative after state officials—led by Democratic governor candidate Phil Weiser—promised a legislative funding package (Denver Post).

Here's the catch: that promised road money depends entirely on Democrats winning November elections. If Republicans gain ground in the statehouse, the deal evaporates, leaving Colorado's crumbling infrastructure unfunded and voters who trusted the agreement out of luck.

Conservatives argue this is classic Democratic bait-and-switch: kill a ballot measure by promising legislative action contingent on partisan victories. It's a gamble that shifts road funding from voter-approved ballot language to backroom Democratic deals—exactly the kind of government overreach that erodes public trust.

The real question for Colorado taxpayers: Will November's election results actually deliver the road funding that was promised, or will this become another unfunded commitment buried in the next budget crisis?
