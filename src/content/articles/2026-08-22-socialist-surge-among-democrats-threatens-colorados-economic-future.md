# Democrats' Socialism Problem Spreads—Colorado Should Take Note

The Democratic Party's embrace of socialist ideology is no longer fringe—it's mainstream. A UC Berkeley poll found that 58% of California Democrats now view socialism favorably, even as party leaders like House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries publicly reject the Democratic Socialists of America agenda (Fox News). National Review reports Democrats are nominating openly socialist candidates in traditionally conservative states, signaling the party's leftward lurch has real electoral consequences.

For Colorado, this matters. The state's Democratic establishment has already moved left on energy policy, taxes, and regulation. If national Democrats continue normalizing socialism, Colorado voters—particularly in swing counties and rural areas—will face candidates pushing higher taxes, aggressive climate mandates, and expanded government control over land and resources.

Republicans argue the data exposes a fundamental contradiction: Democratic leaders denounce socialism while their base increasingly embraces it. Conservatives say this ideological drift will alienate working-class voters and energize Republican turnout in purple regions.

The disconnect between Democratic messaging and voter sentiment creates an opening for Republicans to make the case that Colorado's prosperity depends on free markets, not government-directed economies.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats distance themselves from national socialism rhetoric—or double down on it heading into 2026.
