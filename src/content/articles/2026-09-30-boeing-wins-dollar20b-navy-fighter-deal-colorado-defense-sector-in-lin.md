# Boeing Wins $20B Navy Fighter Contract—Colorado Defense Sector Watches Closely

Colorado's aerospace and defense corridor just got a major boost: Boeing has landed a $20 billion-plus Navy contract for the F/A-XX, a sixth-generation carrier-based fighter designed to replace aging F/A-18 Super Hornets and EA-18G Growlers (Fox News). This marks Boeing's second sixth-gen fighter win, following its 2025 selection for the Air Force's F-47 program (New York Post).

For Colorado, the implications matter. The state hosts significant defense contracting operations, subcontractors, and skilled manufacturing jobs tied to fighter jet production and avionics. A major Boeing program typically cascades work through regional supply chains—from parts fabrication to engineering support.

The contract underscores the Pentagon's commitment to maintaining air superiority against near-peer competitors, particularly China. Conservatives argue robust defense spending on cutting-edge platforms protects American interests and deters aggression—a stark contrast to progressive calls for defense budget cuts.

However, watchdog groups across the political spectrum have scrutinized Boeing's cost overruns and schedule delays on previous programs. Taxpayers should monitor whether this contract includes firm fixed-price incentives or cost-plus structures that could balloon federal spending.

**Watch for:** Boeing's subcontractor announcements and whether Colorado firms secure meaningful work on F/A-XX development and production.
