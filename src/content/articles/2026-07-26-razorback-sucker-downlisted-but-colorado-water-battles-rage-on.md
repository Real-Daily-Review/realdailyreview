# Feds Downlist Razorback Sucker, but Colorado Water Wars Aren't Over

Federal wildlife officials have downlisted the razorback sucker from endangered to threatened status—a move that sounds like progress but masks deeper questions about Colorado's water management and regulatory overreach.

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service made the call based on population recovery in Arizona rivers, according to CPR News. But the reclassification is already drawing fire. One national wildlife nonprofit told KDVR Fox31 Denver that the fish's outlook is "no better today" than when it was first declared endangered, raising questions about whether the downlisting reflects genuine recovery or bureaucratic box-checking.

Here's what matters for Colorado: the razorback sucker has been a flashpoint in Colorado River water disputes for decades. Environmental regulations tied to the species have constrained water deliveries and agricultural use across the West. Conservatives argue that endangered species protections, while well-intentioned, often prioritize fish over farmers, ranchers, and municipalities dependent on reliable water supplies.

The downlisting could ease some regulatory pressure on Colorado water users—but don't expect the environmental lobby to accept this quietly. Expect renewed litigation and calls for re-listing if populations dip.

Watch whether Colorado agricultural groups and water districts use this momentum to challenge other species-based water restrictions on the Colorado River.
