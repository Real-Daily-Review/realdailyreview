# America's Trades Crisis: Colorado Must Act on Skills Gap

Mike Rowe's two-decade crusade to fix America's skilled-trades shortage is finally gaining traction—and Colorado can't afford to ignore it.

The skills gap in manufacturing, construction, and energy sectors has become a genuine national security and economic problem, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Rowe has warned that the widening chasm threatens U.S. competitiveness in auto, steel, and critical industries. His America Workforce Academy represents a market-driven alternative to the college-at-all-costs mentality that has left millions of good-paying trades jobs unfilled.

For Colorado specifically, this matters enormously. The state's energy sector, construction boom, and manufacturing base all depend on skilled workers—electricians, welders, HVAC technicians, and equipment operators. Yet Colorado schools continue pushing four-year degrees over apprenticeships and vocational training, leaving employers desperate and wages artificially inflated.

Conservatives argue that government should get out of the way and let industry-led apprenticeship programs and private training academies flourish. Rather than more federal workforce mandates or taxpayer-funded retraining schemes, the answer is removing regulatory barriers to apprenticeships and letting employers hire and train directly.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's legislature will remove licensing and regulatory obstacles to apprenticeships, or double down on traditional education mandates that ignore market demand.
