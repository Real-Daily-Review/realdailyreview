# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, November 14, 2026

Colorado taxpayers are getting squeezed from every direction — higher taxes at home, reckless policy in Washington, and a Democratic Party tearing itself apart in public. Here's what you need to know this morning.

---

## Front Range Tax Burden Keeps Climbing

Seven Front Range municipalities already carried combined sales tax rates above 9% — now that number has jumped to 13, according to new reporting. That's not economic growth; that's government feeding itself on the backs of working Coloradans. Every percentage point added to the sales tax is a direct hit on families, small businesses, and the working-class communities progressives claim to champion. Colorado conservatives should be asking their local elected officials one simple question: when does it stop?

---

## SCOTUS Sends Transgender Inmate Case Back to 10th Circuit

The Supreme Court issued a "grant, vacate and remand" order in a case involving a transgender inmate in El Paso County, kicking it back to the 10th Circuit for reconsideration. Conservatives say the courts have long been weaponized to force taxpayer-funded accommodations for inmates based on gender ideology, and the 10th Circuit — which covers Colorado — will now have another shot at getting this right. Colorado sheriffs and county officials should be watching this one closely.

---

## Nvidia's China Problem Lands in Trump's Lap

Two outlets are raising the pointed question: why is Nvidia still selling advanced AI chips to China? Republicans argue that advanced semiconductor technology in Chinese hands is a direct national security threat — one that could accelerate Beijing's military and surveillance capabilities. For Colorado, home to a significant defense and aerospace sector anchored along the Front Range, the stakes are real. The Trump administration needs clear answers and clearer policy, not corporate lobbying dressed up as free trade.

---

## Jeffries Heckled as Democrats' Civil War Goes Public

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries got an earful at the "What's Next" Democratic conference, as activists demanding a Green New Deal and an arms embargo on Israel erupted with shouts of "Traitor!" The left is eating itself, and the fractures are getting harder to paper over. For Colorado conservatives, this is a useful reminder of what the Democratic coalition actually looks like beneath the polished messaging — radical climate activists and foreign policy absolutists pulling the party further from the mainstream.

---

## Barry Morphew Back in the News — and Back in Trouble

Barry Morphew, whose first-degree murder case in Alamosa County has already made national headlines, has now pleaded guilty to careless driving in a Denver hit-and-run. Worse, a review of GPS data from the August crash reportedly showed he violated bond conditions tied to his murder case. Colorado courts set those conditions for a reason. If the reporting holds, this raises serious questions about whether the system is adequately enforcing the terms it sets — and who bears responsibility when it doesn't.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eye on the Front Range tax story — with 13 municipalities now above the 9% combined sales tax threshold, the pressure on Colorado's cost of living is becoming impossible to ignore heading into the 2027 legislative session. Watch the 10th Circuit on the El Paso County transgender inmate remand; Colorado's federal courts have a mixed record on these cases, and the ruling will set precedent across the region. And in Washington, the Nvidia-China chip question isn't going away — Colorado's defense community needs the Trump administration to draw a hard line before the next technology transfer becomes tomorrow's security breach.
