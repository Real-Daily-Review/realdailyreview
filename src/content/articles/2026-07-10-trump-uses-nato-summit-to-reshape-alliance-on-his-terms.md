# NATO Summit Signals Trump's Conditional Support—But Colorado Defense Contractors Should Brace for Uncertainty

President Trump used this week's NATO summit in Ankara to green-light Ukraine's domestic production of Patriot missile components while signaling openness to Turkey's F-35 fighter jet program reinstatement, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The moves suggest Trump remains willing to leverage NATO partnerships—but on his terms.

Conservatives see the approach as pragmatic: Trump is extracting commitments from allies while avoiding open-ended entanglements. The *National Review* notes NATO "survived Trump" and still has work ahead, implying the alliance remains functional but requires constant renegotiation.

**The Colorado angle matters here.** Lockheed Martin's Grand Junction facility manufactures F-35 components; any shift in Turkey's participation or broader F-35 production could ripple through Colorado's defense economy and skilled manufacturing jobs. Similarly, Patriot system work touches Colorado defense contractors.

Republicans argue Trump's transactional approach prevents wasteful spending and forces allies to shoulder fair burden-sharing. Critics worry unpredictability undermines alliance cohesion, though conservatives counter that decades of predictable NATO policy produced complacency and freeloading.

**Watch whether Trump's F-35 decision accelerates Turkish reintegration—and whether Colorado's defense sector sees new contracts or production shifts as a result.**
