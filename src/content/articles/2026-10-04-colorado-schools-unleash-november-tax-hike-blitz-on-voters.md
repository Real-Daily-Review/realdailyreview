# Jefferson County, Denver, Douglas County Hit Voters With School Tax Hikes

Colorado taxpayers are facing a coordinated assault of education mill levy overrides this November, with Jefferson County residents particularly burdened by multiple tax measures hitting the ballot simultaneously.

In Lakewood and Wheat Ridge, voters confront three new sales taxes at once, according to Complete Colorado. Meanwhile, Denver Public Schools is asking voters to approve a nearly $44 million mill levy override through Ballot Issue 4A, while Douglas County schools are seeking $54 million through Ballot Issue 5A.

**The conservative argument:** These measures represent exactly the kind of tax-and-spend governance that erodes Colorado's economic competitiveness. Rather than demanding fiscal discipline and efficiency from bloated school bureaucracies, districts reflexively turn to taxpayers for more money. Conservatives argue that schools should prioritize existing resources, cut administrative overhead, and demonstrate measurable results before asking families already squeezed by inflation to shoulder higher property and sales taxes.

School districts counter that funding gaps require voter approval to maintain services and competitive teacher salaries—a position Democrats typically support as necessary investment in education.

**What's at stake:** These measures will directly hit Colorado homeowners and small businesses already dealing with rising costs. Approval would lock in permanent tax increases for years.

Watch whether Douglas County's $54 million ask and Denver's $44 million override pass—their success will likely trigger a wave of similar measures statewide.
