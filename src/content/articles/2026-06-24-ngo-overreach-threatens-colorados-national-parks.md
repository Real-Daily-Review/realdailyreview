# National Parks Face NGO Overreach—Colorado Should Demand Transparency

Colorado's national parks and monuments risk becoming platforms for progressive ideology rather than American heritage, thanks to unchecked influence from left-leaning nonprofits embedded in the National Park Service.

The Washington Examiner warns that NGOs have gained outsized control over how parks interpret and present U.S. history—often downplaying the nation's founding principles of liberty, justice, and representative government in favor of revisionist narratives. These unelected organizations shape visitor experiences and educational programming without public accountability or congressional oversight.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. Rocky Mountain National Park, Great Sand Dunes, and other iconic sites attract millions annually and drive tourism revenue. When NGOs dictate interpretive messaging, they're essentially rewriting how visitors—especially schoolchildren—understand American exceptionalism and Colorado's own pioneering heritage.

Conservatives argue the Park Service should prioritize historical accuracy and celebrate America's achievements, not amplify activist agendas. The left counters that modern interpretation requires acknowledging historical complexities, but that argument ignores the fundamental problem: unelected nonprofits shouldn't control public lands.

The Trump administration should audit NGO contracts with the Park Service, demand transparency in grant-making, and restore parks to their core mission—preserving natural beauty and honest history.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation demands a full accounting of NGO influence in the state's federal parks.
