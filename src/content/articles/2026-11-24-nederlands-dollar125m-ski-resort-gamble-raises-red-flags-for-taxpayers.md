# Nederland Eyes $125M Eldora Ski Resort Buyout—Taxpayers Should Ask Hard Questions

A small mountain town is betting $125 million of public money on a ski resort acquisition that could leave Colorado taxpayers holding the bag if the deal goes sideways.

The 1,500 residents of Nederland are pursuing a purchase of Eldora Ski Resort, according to the *Colorado Sun*. While proponents frame this as community ownership and economic control, conservatives should scrutinize the fundamentals: Where is the $125 million coming from? What happens if operating costs balloon or visitation drops? Who absorbs losses?

Government-run ski operations have a spotty track record nationwide. Once a municipality takes the helm, operational inefficiencies, union labor costs, and political decision-making often replace market discipline. Private operators answer to shareholders and profit margins; government operators answer to budget cycles and political pressure—a recipe for cost overruns and subsidy requests.

Nederland's pitch likely emphasizes local control and keeping revenue in-house. But ski resorts are capital-intensive, weather-dependent businesses. A harsh winter, economic downturn, or shift in tourism patterns could crater revenues while fixed costs remain locked in.

Taxpayers across Colorado should watch whether this deal requires state or federal funding, and demand full transparency on the town's financial projections and exit strategy if operations underperform.
