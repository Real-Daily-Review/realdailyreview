# Colorado's Progressive Tax Gamble May Backfire on Democrats

Colorado voters narrowly approved Amendment 87, replacing the state's flat income tax with a six-bracket progressive system—a move Democrats pitched as soaking the rich but that may ultimately shrink state revenue, according to conservative analysts.

The amendment, which eliminated Colorado's uniform tax rate in favor of graduated brackets, exemplifies the left's faith that higher rates on top earners will fund government expansion. But *Complete Colorado* reports an ironic outcome: the progressive structure could eventually produce *less* tax revenue than the flat system it replaced—an unintended silver lining for taxpayers tired of funding Denver's regulatory appetite.

The vote reflected broader national resistance to progressive taxation. *National Review* notes that voters across multiple states rejected wealth and property tax hikes, signaling taxpayer fatigue with government revenue grabs disguised as "fairness."

Republicans warned that Colorado's new progressive brackets would invite capital flight and discourage business investment in a state already burdened by regulatory overreach. The flat tax had provided predictability; the progressive model introduces complexity and creates perverse incentives for high earners to relocate—precisely the dynamic that could hollow out the tax base Democrats hoped to expand.

Watch for revenue projections over the next fiscal cycle: if collections fall short of Democratic forecasts, it will vindicate conservative warnings that punitive tax policy drives earners and businesses elsewhere.
