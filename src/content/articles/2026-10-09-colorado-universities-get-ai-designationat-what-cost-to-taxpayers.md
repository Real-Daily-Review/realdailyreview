# Colorado Universities Chase AI Designation While Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Colorado's higher-education establishment is embracing artificial intelligence with state-backed fanfare, as six colleges and universities earned a new "AI Engaged Campus Designation" (CPR News). The honor recognizes intentional AI deployment across instruction and research—a move universities tout as forward-thinking innovation.

But here's what conservatives should question: Who's paying for this expansion, and what's the actual return on investment for Colorado taxpayers already burdened by rising tuition and administrative bloat?

Colorado's universities have long been laboratories for progressive policy experiments. Adding AI infrastructure and training programs sounds cutting-edge, but it typically means more spending, more bureaucracy, and more justification for higher student costs and state subsidies. The designation itself—a feel-good credential with no clear accountability metrics—exemplifies how higher ed uses branding to dodge hard questions about fiscal discipline.

Republicans have rightly criticized academia's bloated administrative overhead and ideological mission creep. Adding AI initiatives without transparent cost-benefit analysis and measurable outcomes for students and employers only deepens that concern.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature demands detailed spending reports and employment outcomes before funneling more taxpayer dollars into these AI programs.
