# Front Range Voters Face Major Rail Tax Hike as RTD Struggles

Colorado's Front Range Passenger Rail District is asking voters to approve a hefty new sales tax to fund the "Colorado Connector" commuter rail project—even as the state's existing RTD system continues to underperform (Complete Colorado). The ballot measure would impose a 0.333% sales and use tax estimated to generate $295 million annually, according to Complete Colorado.

The timing is awkward. Colorado's track record with government-run transit is poor: RTD, the region's flagship commuter rail operator, has become a cautionary tale of mismanagement and cost overruns. Conservatives argue that throwing another tax at voters before fixing existing failures is fiscally reckless and rewards bureaucratic incompetence.

**Republicans counter** that Colorado taxpayers are already overtaxed and that rail projects should compete for funding through competitive bidding and private investment—not automatic sales tax hikes. They point out that voters have recently rejected progressive tax schemes, signaling voter fatigue with new levies (Complete Colorado).

The measure comes as Colorado voters also weigh a separate ballot initiative to cap the state income tax rate, reflecting broader voter skepticism toward tax expansion.

**Conservatives should watch** whether Front Range voters reject this tax hike or whether rail-district marketing overcomes voter skepticism about government transit competence.
