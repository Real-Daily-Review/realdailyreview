# Texas Bible Curriculum Move Reignites School Content Wars—Colorado Should Pay Attention

Texas just became the first state in recent history to mandate Bible stories in K-12 public school curricula, and the move is already sparking the culture-war debate that could reshape education policy nationwide—including in Colorado.

The Texas State Board of Education, controlled by Republicans, voted Friday to approve the reading list. Supporters argue Bible passages belong in literature and history instruction as foundational texts to Western civilization and American law. The Washington Examiner's opinion section notes that state law and legal precedent allow the mandate, and critics' First Amendment concerns don't hold water under existing jurisprudence.

Opponents will inevitably cry foul, claiming the move violates church-state separation and imposes religious instruction on public school students. Expect the usual suspects—progressive education groups and Democratic lawmakers—to argue this is Christian nationalism masquerading as curriculum.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's education establishment leans left, and Denver's school board has resisted conservative curriculum pushes for years. If Texas's Bible mandate survives legal challenge, Colorado Republicans will face pressure to pursue similar policies—or defend why our public schools shouldn't teach foundational Western texts.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican legislators introduce comparable curriculum bills in the 2025 session, and whether the state's progressive education bureaucracy fights back.
