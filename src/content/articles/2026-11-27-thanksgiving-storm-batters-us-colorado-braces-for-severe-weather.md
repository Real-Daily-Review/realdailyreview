# Thanksgiving Storm Batters Nation; Colorado in Path of Severe Weather

Thanksgiving travel and holiday plans across America face disruption as heavy rain, high winds, and flooding sweep both coasts and the interior—with Colorado in the storm's trajectory. The system knocked out power to nearly a million customers and prompted the first-ever postponement of Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade (KDVR), while torrential downpours flooded East Coast communities (9News).

The timing couldn't be worse for holiday travelers already facing inflation-driven fuel and airfare costs. Coloradans heading to the mountains or across state lines should prepare for dangerous road conditions, potential power outages, and flight delays.

While severe weather is a natural occurrence, conservatives note that disaster preparedness and response—not federal climate mandates—should guide state and local planning. Colorado's energy grid, already strained by progressive policies favoring unreliable renewables over dispatchable power, faces added pressure during peak demand periods like holiday storms. Reliable baseload generation from natural gas and coal remains essential when weather threatens grid stability.

Residents should monitor National Weather Service alerts and ensure backup power supplies and emergency supplies are stocked before conditions deteriorate.
