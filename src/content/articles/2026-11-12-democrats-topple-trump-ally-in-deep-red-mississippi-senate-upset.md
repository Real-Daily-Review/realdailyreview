# Democrats Pull Off Stunning Mississippi Upset; GOP Senate Majority at Risk

Senate Republicans face a stunning setback as Democrat Scott Colom defeats incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in deep-red Mississippi—a state President Trump won with nearly 61% of the vote just two years ago. Colom secured 49.2% to Hyde-Smith's 48.7%, according to the Associated Press call at 5:58 p.m. EST (Fox News, Washington Examiner).

The loss marks one of the 2026 midterm cycle's biggest upsets and signals potential trouble for the GOP heading into the general election. Republicans argue the defeat reflects broader voter fatigue and messaging failures, while Democrats claim it demonstrates growing momentum against Trump-backed candidates in traditionally conservative strongholds.

**The Colorado angle:** While Mississippi's Senate race doesn't directly affect Colorado policy, the national trend matters. If Republicans lose Senate seats in red states, Colorado Democrats gain leverage on energy regulation, gun control, and federal land-use decisions that directly impact Colorado ranchers, energy workers, and gun owners. A narrower GOP Senate majority could embolden progressive overreach on Western water rights and mineral development.
