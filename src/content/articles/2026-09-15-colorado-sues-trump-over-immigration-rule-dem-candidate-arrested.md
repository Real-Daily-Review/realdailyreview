# Colorado Sues Trump on Immigration Rule; Democrat Candidate Arrested

Colorado's Democratic leadership is joining a multi-state lawsuit against the Trump administration's "public charge" rule, which restricts green cards and entry for immigrants likely to use public benefits. The rule, enforced by federal immigration authorities, aims to ensure immigrants are self-sufficient and don't burden taxpayers.

Democrats argue the policy is discriminatory and will separate families. Colorado Attorney General Phil Weissberg (D) claims the rule violates due process and harms the state's economy and workforce. Republicans counter that enforcing immigration law and protecting public resources for citizens is constitutional and fiscally responsible—Colorado taxpayers already shoulder substantial costs for emergency services, education, and welfare benefits regardless of immigration status.

Meanwhile, a Lone Tree Democrat running for Colorado's House of Representatives was arrested in May for public intoxication, according to Texas court records. The arrest raises questions about candidate vetting in a competitive Douglas County race.

The lawsuit reflects Colorado's progressive tilt on immigration policy, but conservatives should watch whether federal courts uphold the public charge rule—and whether Colorado voters in swing districts like Douglas County will tolerate sanctuary-state litigation funded by their tax dollars.
