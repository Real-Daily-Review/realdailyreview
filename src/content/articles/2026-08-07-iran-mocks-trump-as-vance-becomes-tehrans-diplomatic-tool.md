# Trump's Iran Gambit Backfires as Vance Becomes Tehran's Useful Fool

Vice President JD Vance has become an unwitting asset for Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, according to conservative critics examining the ceasefire memorandum of understanding brokered under Trump's watch. The Washington Examiner argues that Trump—who famously learned hard lessons in New York real estate about being deceived—has allowed himself to be "fooled once" by Tehran's negotiators, who reportedly "played his vice president like an Iranian flute."

Iran's top nuclear negotiator has openly mocked the Trump administration's approach, the Daily Wire reports, even as Iranian citizens take to the streets demanding an end to ayatollah rule. The disconnect is stark: while ordinary Iranians cry out for regime change, the U.S. appears to be legitimizing the very government they oppose.

For Colorado, this matters. A weakened American posture toward Iran threatens energy markets and regional stability—concerns that ripple through Colorado's oil and gas sector and defense-dependent communities. Conservatives argue that appeasement of hostile regimes invites further aggression and undermines American credibility with allies.
