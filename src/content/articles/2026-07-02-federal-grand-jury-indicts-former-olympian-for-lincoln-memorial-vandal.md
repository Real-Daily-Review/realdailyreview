# Federal Grand Jury Indicts Former Olympian for Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool Vandalism

A D.C. grand jury has indicted former U.S. Olympic canoeist David Hearn on a felony destruction of property charge stemming from alleged vandalism at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, according to the Washington Examiner. Hearn faces up to 10 years in prison if convicted.

President Trump previously flagged the incident as part of what he characterized as a broader pattern of federal authorities making "multiple arrests" for Reflecting Pool vandalism (9News Denver). The Daily Caller reported the indictment describes "a violent effort, to rip up the sealant," suggesting deliberate damage to the monument's infrastructure.

The case underscores ongoing tensions over monument security and protest activity in the nation's capital. While Democrats have often downplayed or contextualized vandalism as political expression, Republicans and conservatives argue that destruction of federal property—especially national monuments—represents lawlessness that must be prosecuted vigorously. The felony charge here reflects that prosecutorial stance.

For Colorado taxpayers and citizens, the case serves as a reminder of how federal resources are deployed to protect and maintain the nation's most iconic sites. Conservatives should monitor whether D.C. prosecutors apply similar rigor to all vandalism cases regardless of the perpetrator's political leanings.
