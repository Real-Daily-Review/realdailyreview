# Colorado's Minimum Wage Hike Arrives Again—And the Data Shows Mixed Results

Colorado's minimum wage is climbing higher in 2027, continuing an automatic inflation adjustment that keeps ratcheting up labor costs for small businesses across the state.

The increase, mandated by the state constitution, has delivered measurable wage gains for the lowest-paid food workers, according to data cited by the *Colorado Sun*. But the broader picture is murkier: the pay bump hasn't translated into proportional gains for most other workers, and Denver's inflation sits at 3.9%—a reminder that wage floors don't automatically lift all boats.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that Colorado's automatic minimum wage escalator, enshrined in the state constitution, removes legislative flexibility and ignores local economic conditions. Small businesses in rural Colorado face different pressures than Denver employers, yet both operate under the same mandated floor. The *Colorado Sun* also reported that CU is cutting its job growth forecasts, raising questions about whether higher labor costs are dampening hiring.

The state's first organized casino and other economic developments offer some offsetting bright spots, but the minimum wage trajectory remains a headwind for employers already navigating inflation and tight margins.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers attempt to revisit the constitutional provision—or whether business groups push for carve-outs tied to regional economic conditions.
