# New York's Spending Spree Shows Why Colorado Must Guard Against Progressive Excess

New York City's latest fiscal fiasco offers a cautionary tale for Colorado taxpayers watching their own government expand. The Mamdani administration just extended a $1.86 billion contract with hotels to shelter the homeless—a three-year commitment that exemplifies how progressive cities throw money at problems without solving them (New York Post). Meanwhile, confusion over a new pied-à-terre tax on second homes spiraled so badly that 900,000 properties landed on a supplemental roll, yet only 17,000 homeowners received actual surcharge letters, forcing officials to extend exemption deadlines (Fox News).

Republicans argue these twin disasters reveal the core problem: progressive governance prioritizes spending and regulation over results. A $1.86 billion homeless shelter contract doesn't address root causes—addiction, mental illness, job scarcity—it simply warehouses people in hotels while the city bleeds money. The pied-à-terre tax, designed to punish wealth, became so poorly executed it punished bureaucratic incompetence instead.

Colorado conservatives should note the pattern. As Denver and Boulder lean further left, similar schemes—from vacant property taxes to expanded social spending—gain traction. Without clear metrics, sunset clauses, and fiscal discipline, Colorado risks becoming another cautionary tale.

Watch whether Colorado's Democrat-controlled legislature proposes similar wealth-targeting taxes or open-ended homeless spending contracts in the 2025 session.
