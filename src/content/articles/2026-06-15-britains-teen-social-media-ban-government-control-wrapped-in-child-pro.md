# Britain's Social Media Ban for Teens Signals Government Overreach—and a Warning for America

Britain's move to ban children under 16 from social media platforms like TikTok, YouTube, and Snapchat is government control masquerading as child protection.

Prime Minister Keir Starmer announced the ban will take effect early next year, with legislation introduced before Christmas (9News Denver). The UK joins Australia in tightening tech regulation, but conservatives warn the real danger lies beneath the surface.

Freedom advocates argue the ban opens the door to invasive digital ID systems and "papers please" internet restrictions for adults (Breitbart). Implementing age verification at scale requires government surveillance infrastructure that will inevitably expand beyond protecting minors—a classic case of government mission creep.

While protecting children from social media's harms is legitimate, the mechanism matters. Conservatives question whether a blanket ban is the right tool when parental controls and market competition offer less intrusive alternatives. The precedent is troubling: once governments claim authority to police online access for one age group, expanding that power to monitor all citizens becomes the next logical step.

Colorado parents and taxpayers should watch whether progressive lawmakers here cite Britain's ban as justification for similar legislation—and whether any proposed rules include the digital ID infrastructure that freedom advocates fear.
