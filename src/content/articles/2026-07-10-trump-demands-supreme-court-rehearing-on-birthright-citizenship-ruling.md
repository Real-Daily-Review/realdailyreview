# Supreme Court Birthright Citizenship Ruling Leaves Trump Vowing Immediate Rehearing

President Trump is refusing to accept the Supreme Court's decision on birthright citizenship, announcing he will "immediately" petition for a rehearing and calling the ruling a "miscarriage of justice" (The Daily Wire). The Court's decision upheld 128 years of precedent establishing automatic citizenship for children born on U.S. soil, regardless of parental immigration status.

**The Conservative Case:** Trump and his allies argue the ruling threatens American sovereignty and immigration law enforcement. Conservatives contend that birthright citizenship, as currently interpreted, incentivizes illegal immigration and creates perverse incentives for pregnant women to cross the border specifically to secure citizenship for their children. They say the Constitution's 14th Amendment language is ambiguous enough to warrant reconsideration, and that stare decisis—judicial deference to precedent—shouldn't be absolute when foundational constitutional questions are at stake.

**The Counterargument:** Legal scholars and the Court majority argue that overturning 128 years of settled law requires extraordinary justification beyond a close case and divided dissenters (National Review). They contend the precedent is well-established and disrupting it would create chaos in citizenship records and create a vulnerable underclass.

**Colorado Angle:** Immigration policy directly affects Colorado's labor markets, education costs, and border-state concerns. The state's business community and agricultural sector have competing interests in this debate.

Watch whether Trump's rehearing petition gains traction or whether the Court declines to revisit the issue.
