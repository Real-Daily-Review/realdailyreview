# Colorado Local Governments Push Surveillance and Regulatory Overreach While Dodging Accountability

El Paso County commissioners just handed a major win to Buc-ee's by fast-tracking zoning approval without public hearings—a 4-1 vote that reclassified the massive travel center as a "convenience store" to bypass normal review (CPR News). Meanwhile, Colorado communities are quietly expanding surveillance infrastructure. Summit County activated Flock license plate-reading cameras before shutting them down for 30 days after public backlash and commissioner concerns about how the data was being used (KDVR). Not to be outdone, Kersey installed permanent speed cameras on County Road 49, launching a 30-day warning period before issuing citations (9News).

**The conservative concern:** Government agencies are expanding surveillance tools and regulatory shortcuts without transparent public process. Flock cameras raise Fourth Amendment questions about warrantless tracking; speed cameras are revenue generators masquerading as safety measures. The Buc-ee's decision, while pro-development, bypassed the public input process that keeps government accountable.

Coloradans should demand answers: What data are these cameras collecting, who accesses it, and how long is it retained? Speed cameras and license-plate readers are tools for government overreach—not public safety.

**Watch whether Colorado legislators move to restrict Flock deployment and demand transparency on camera data retention.**
