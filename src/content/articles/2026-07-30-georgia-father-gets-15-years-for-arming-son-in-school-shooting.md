# Father of Georgia School Shooter Sentenced to 15 Years for Providing Weapon

A Georgia father's decision to gift his teenage son a rifle has landed him 15 years in federal prison—a case that gun-rights advocates say highlights parental negligence rather than a Second Amendment failure.

Colin Gray, 55, was convicted of second-degree murder and involuntary manslaughter after his son, Colt Gray, then 16, used the weapon to kill four people at Apalachee High School (Washington Examiner, Just The News). The younger Gray was sentenced to life without parole days before his father's sentencing.

The case presents a thorny distinction for conservatives: while Second Amendment advocates fiercely defend gun ownership rights, they also emphasize that responsible ownership includes keeping firearms secure from minors and monitoring a child's mental state. Gray's decision to give his son a rifle—reportedly as a Christmas gift—without apparent safeguards or awareness of warning signs crosses from rights exercise into negligent parenting.

Colorado gun owners and Second Amendment defenders should note this case doesn't challenge constitutional carry or permit laws; it underscores the legal and moral duty parents bear when introducing firearms into a home. The conviction rests on parental failure, not gun availability.
