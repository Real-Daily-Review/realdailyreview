# Omar Fraud Saga Won't Die—But Constitutional Reality Limits Trump's Options

Minnesota Democrat Ilhan Omar faces mounting scrutiny over alleged ties to individuals involved in a massive fraud scheme, but President Trump's calls for her resignation bump against a constitutional wall: only the House can remove its own members.

Fox News reports Trump has demanded Omar resign, yet the Constitution vests expulsion power exclusively in Congress. That hasn't stopped Republicans from pressing the issue. Minnesota State Rep. Kristin Robbins told The Daily Wire that Omar was "thick as thieves" with people caught up in the fraud scandal, describing her as "one fed-up Republican" demanding accountability (The Daily Wire).

The New York Times recently exposed another twist in the Omar controversy, reigniting questions about her judgment and associations—though specifics of the alleged fraud remain under investigation.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue Omar's credibility is compromised by her proximity to fraudsters and that House Democrats should launch a serious ethics review. Taxpayers deserve representatives with clean associations and sound judgment.

**What's next:** Watch whether House leadership—currently Republican-controlled—pursues formal ethics proceedings or whether this becomes another partisan flashpoint heading into 2024.
