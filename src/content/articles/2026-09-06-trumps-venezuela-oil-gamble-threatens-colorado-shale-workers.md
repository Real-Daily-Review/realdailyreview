# Trump's Venezuela Oil Deal: A Gamble on Foreign Energy Over Domestic Shale

Trump's reported pursuit of Venezuelan oil access signals a troubling retreat from American energy independence—exactly when Colorado's shale producers need Washington's backing, not distraction.

The deal reportedly aims to secure crude supplies as domestic shale wells naturally decline over time, according to *Just The News*. Conservatives argue this approach inverts sound energy policy: rather than double down on proven American reserves and the workers who extract them, the administration is chasing unstable foreign sources.

National Review editors raise a sharper concern: the cynicism of the move. Trump has repeatedly attacked Venezuela's regime for seizing American company assets—yet now appears willing to cut a transactional deal with the same government, undermining the rule-of-law principles that made America prosperous in the first place.

For Colorado, the stakes are direct. Colorado's oil and gas workers, already facing regulatory headwinds from the Biden years, need a president committed to domestic production dominance, not hedging bets on Venezuelan crude. Shale decline is manageable through investment and innovation; outsourcing energy security to Caracas is not.

Republicans counter that energy diversity matters; Democrats would likely oppose any Venezuela deal on human-rights grounds. The real question: will Trump prioritize Colorado's energy workers or geopolitical shortcuts?

Watch whether this deal advances—and whether Colorado Republicans demand domestic-first energy policy in return.
