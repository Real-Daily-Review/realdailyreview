# Missouri Flooding Devastates Homes; National Guard Mobilizes Rescue

Historic rainfall—12 inches in just 12 hours—hammered southeastern Missouri, overwhelming rivers and destroying homes and vehicles across the region. Army National Guard troops deployed eight Blackhawk helicopters Friday to rescue hundreds of young campers and stranded residents from rooftops and rising waters, according to Breitbart and 9News Denver.

The catastrophic flooding underscores why Colorado water management matters. While Missouri grapples with uncontrolled runoff, Colorado's water infrastructure—dams, reservoirs, and flood-control systems—protects taxpayers and property. Yet progressive environmental groups routinely block dam maintenance and new water-storage projects in the name of "river restoration," leaving Colorado vulnerable to similar disasters.

The rescue operation, while impressive, also highlights the limits of government response. First responders saved lives, but prevention through robust infrastructure investment would have spared families the trauma and economic loss. Conservatives argue that states must prioritize engineering solutions—not regulatory restrictions—to manage extreme weather.

Colorado should watch whether this Missouri disaster prompts state lawmakers to fast-track water-infrastructure upgrades or whether environmental activists will again block critical projects that protect communities and preserve water supplies for agriculture and energy development.
