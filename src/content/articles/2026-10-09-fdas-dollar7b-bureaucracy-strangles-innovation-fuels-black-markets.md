# FDA's $7 Billion Bureaucracy Strangles Innovation, Fuels Black Markets

The FDA's bloated $7 billion budget is doing what government agencies do best: creating problems while claiming to solve them.

A September 2026 Government Accountability Office report, highlighted by the *Washington Examiner*, exposes how the Food and Drug Administration operates as an "unaccountable bureaucratic black box" that burdens taxpayers and leaves businesses guessing about regulatory requirements. The result? Delayed product approvals, frustrated entrepreneurs, and thriving black markets filling the void the FDA leaves behind.

While the agency has greenlit some reduced-risk products recently, conservatives argue the fundamental problem persists: an unelected bureaucracy with minimal accountability, bloated budgets, and glacial approval timelines that punish American innovation and push consumers toward unregulated alternatives.

For Colorado, this matters. State biotech firms, supplement manufacturers, and health-product companies face the same FDA delays and compliance costs as their counterparts nationwide—expenses that get passed to Colorado consumers or drive businesses to relocate to less-regulated jurisdictions.

Republicans and free-market advocates say the fix is clear: slash FDA red tape, demand transparency in approval processes, and let markets—not bureaucrats—determine which products consumers trust.

Watch whether Congress acts on GAO recommendations to streamline FDA operations or if the agency continues its pattern of regulatory expansion masquerading as public health.
