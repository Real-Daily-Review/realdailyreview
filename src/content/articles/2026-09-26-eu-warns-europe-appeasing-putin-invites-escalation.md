# EU's Top Diplomat Warns Europe: Don't Appease Putin

Europe's chief diplomat is sounding the alarm that Russia's sabotage and intimidation campaign is designed to fracture Western support for Ukraine—and that capitulation will only invite escalation.

Kaja Kallas, the EU's top foreign policy official, warned that Moscow's strategy of destabilization aims to weaken European resolve (Fox News). Her message: give Putin an inch, and he'll take a mile.

Meanwhile, at the United Nations, Russia and China are openly coordinating against American interests. During Saturday's General Assembly speeches, both nations sided with Iran against the Trump administration's policies and criticized U.S. operations related to Cuba (Washington Examiner). The alignment signals a hardening axis of authoritarian powers willing to challenge U.S. leadership on the world stage.

**The Colorado angle:** As energy prices and geopolitical stability affect Colorado's oil and gas sector, and as defense spending impacts Colorado Springs and northern Colorado military installations, these international power plays matter locally. A weakened West invites higher energy costs and defense uncertainty.

Republicans argue that appeasement emboldens adversaries and that strength—not negotiation from weakness—deters aggression. Democrats typically emphasize diplomacy and de-escalation, though support for Ukraine remains bipartisan.

**Watch:** Whether the Biden-Harris administration will match Kallas's hardline rhetoric with actual policy, or continue signaling openness to negotiated settlements that could undermine Ukraine.
