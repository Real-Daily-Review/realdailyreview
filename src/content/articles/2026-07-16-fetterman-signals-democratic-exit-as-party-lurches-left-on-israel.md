# Fetterman's Party Loyalty Cracking as Dems Distance Themselves

Pennsylvania Sen. John Fetterman is signaling daylight between himself and the Democratic Party, revealing that an official anti-Israel stance would be his breaking point—a telling admission about the progressive wing's growing influence over party orthodoxy.

According to a Quinnipiac University poll cited by the *Washington Examiner*, over half of Pennsylvania voters across party lines believe Fetterman should leave the Democrats entirely. The numbers are striking: 57% of Democrats, 55% of Republicans, and 46% of independents want him gone. The *Washington Examiner* reports that even Democrats appear ready to show him the door.

Fetterman's comments underscore a deeper fracture within the party. His willingness to name Israel as a non-negotiable issue suggests the Democratic base has drifted so far left on foreign policy that a moderate senator must publicly defend his position. Republicans see this as validation of their argument that the Democratic Party has been captured by its progressive wing, leaving little room for centrists.

For Colorado Republicans, this is instructive: it shows how primary pressure from activist bases can radicalize parties and alienate mainstream voters. As Colorado Democrats face similar pressures on energy, gun control, and spending, watch whether moderate Democrats here follow Fetterman's lead or capitulate to the progressive agenda.
