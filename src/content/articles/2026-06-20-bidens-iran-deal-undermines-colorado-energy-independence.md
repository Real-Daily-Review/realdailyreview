# Biden-Era Iran Deal Leaves Colorado Energy Sector in the Cold

The Obama-Biden administration's new Memorandum of Understanding with Iran hands leverage to a hostile regime while undercutting American energy independence—a direct blow to Colorado's oil and gas workers already reeling from years of federal overreach.

National Review warns the MOU is fundamentally lopsided, with Iran gaining negotiating momentum despite never winning a military conflict (National Review). The deal effectively legitimizes Iranian nuclear ambitions while weakening U.S. leverage, critics argue—the latest in a pattern of Democratic appeasement that undermines American interests abroad.

**The Colorado angle:** This agreement threatens domestic energy production at a critical moment. Colorado's energy sector—responsible for thousands of jobs and billions in state revenue—faces continued pressure from Biden-Harris policies favoring renewable mandates and oil-and-gas restrictions. A weakened U.S. position on Iran emboldens OPEC coordination, depressing American crude prices and undercutting the economic case for domestic drilling.

Republicans and conservatives argue the deal repeats the failures of the 2015 Iran nuclear accord, which enriched Tehran while leaving America weaker. They contend the administration should instead prioritize energy independence through domestic production—a position that directly benefits Colorado's economy and workers.

**Watch:** Whether the incoming administration reverses this MOU and restores pro-energy policies that unlock Colorado's vast reserves and create good-paying jobs without government picking winners.
