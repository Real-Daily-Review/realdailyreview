# DU Sends Students to Sundance on Taxpayer Dime

Denver University is spending resources to send 24 enrolled students—plus 16 more applicants—to the Sundance Film Festival as part of a new class offering, according to 9News Denver. The university frames the trip as an educational opportunity tied to coursework.

Conservatives should ask hard questions about how DU justifies this expenditure when Colorado families struggle with tuition costs and state funding pressures. While film education has merit, subsidizing out-of-state festival trips raises the familiar problem: universities expanding amenities and experiences that drive up the cost of attendance for ordinary students. DU's tuition already ranks among the highest in Colorado; adding expensive field trips doesn't lighten the load on families or taxpayers supporting higher education.

The Sundance Film Festival itself—headquartered in Utah and steeped in progressive Hollywood culture—isn't inherently controversial. But the broader question remains: should universities prioritize affordable, accessible education, or premium experiences for select students?
