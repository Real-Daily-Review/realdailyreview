# Trump Tariffs Get Court Green Light—Colorado Manufacturers in the Crosshairs

A federal appeals court has temporarily allowed President Trump's 10% global tariffs to remain in effect while legal challenges work through the courts, handing the administration a procedural win even as the underlying trade policy faces sustained legal fire.

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit ruled Thursday that Trump's tariff regime—imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974—can proceed for now, despite a lower court striking down the policy last month (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

**The conservative case for tariffs:** Trump administration officials argue the levies protect American manufacturing and reduce trade deficits. Supporters say targeted tariffs can level the playing field against unfair foreign competition.

**The opposition:** Business groups and free-market conservatives worry tariffs raise costs for American manufacturers and consumers. Colorado's agricultural exporters, tech firms, and outdoor equipment makers—heavy users of imported components—face margin pressure if tariff costs stick around.

The ruling is temporary; the legal battle continues. Colorado companies dependent on global supply chains should prepare for prolonged uncertainty: tariffs could stay, disappear, or shift targets as courts decide the merits and Congress potentially reasserts its trade authority.

Watch whether the full Federal Circuit or Supreme Court takes up the case—and whether Colorado's business community mobilizes politically to challenge tariff expansion.
