# Colorado DSA Movement Faces National Headwinds as Radical Wing Stumbles

Colorado's Democratic Socialist Alliance is pushing an increasingly radical agenda just as the movement hits a credibility wall nationwide. A stunning primary upset in Wisconsin—where radical DSA-backed candidate Francesca Hong lost her gubernatorial bid to moderate Democrat David Crowley—signals voter rejection of the far-left's most extreme platform planks (National Review).

Complete Colorado reports that Colorado's DSA chapter is channeling rhetoric eerily reminiscent of Bolshevik revolutionary dogma, with what observers call "vengeful" ideological messaging that mirrors historical communist movements. The group's push for wealth redistribution, government control of key industries, and anti-capitalist positioning has gained traction in certain Denver and Boulder precincts—but the Wisconsin result suggests even Democratic primary voters are drawing a line.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue DSA policies—higher taxes, expanded government, restrictions on business and energy development—would cripple Colorado's economy and drive out job creators. Conservatives worry that while national momentum may be shifting, Colorado's progressive supermajority in the legislature remains vulnerable to DSA pressure on taxes, gun control, and land-use regulation.

**What's next:** Watch whether Colorado Democrats distance themselves from DSA rhetoric ahead of 2026, or double down on radical messaging and risk repeating Wisconsin's lesson.
