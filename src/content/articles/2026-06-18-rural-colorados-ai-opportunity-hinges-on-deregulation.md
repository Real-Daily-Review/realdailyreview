# Rural Colorado Could Power AI Boom—If Regulators Get Out of the Way

Colorado's rural communities face a stark choice: become energy powerhouses for the artificial intelligence revolution, or watch another economic opportunity slip away to overregulation.

The Washington Examiner argues that rural America has endured decades of progressive hostility toward traditional industries. Coal towns, oil and gas regions, and agricultural communities were told to "learn to code" as their livelihoods were systematically regulated out of existence through litigation and cultural condemnation. Now, data centers powering AI demand massive, reliable electricity—exactly what rural Colorado can provide.

The opportunity is real. Colorado's energy infrastructure, grid capacity, and available land make rural counties natural candidates for data center development. But conservatives warn that the same regulatory mindset that killed coal and constrained oil and gas could strangle this chance too.

Republicans argue rural Colorado deserves a fair shot at economic renewal without facing new environmental restrictions, permitting delays, or local opposition fueled by anti-energy ideology. The state's energy workers and rural taxpayers shouldn't be forced to choose between their heritage industries and their future.

Watch whether Colorado regulators and local governments embrace data center development or impose the same barriers that have crippled rural prosperity for two decades.
