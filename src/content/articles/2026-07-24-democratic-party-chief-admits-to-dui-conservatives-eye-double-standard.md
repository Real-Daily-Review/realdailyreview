# Massachusetts Democratic Party Chief Admits to DUI; Colorado Republicans Note Party's Selective Accountability

Steve Kerrigan, chairman of the Massachusetts Democratic Party, admitted to sufficient facts in a June drunken driving case after registering 0.089 on a breathalyzer—just above the state's 0.08% legal limit—during a Southborough traffic stop (Fox News Politics). His license was suspended for 45 days following the failed test (New York Post).

The admission comes as Democrats nationwide push stricter drunk-driving enforcement and traffic safety measures. Republicans note the irony: while the left champions aggressive law-and-order policies on transportation and public safety, prominent Democratic officials often escape serious consequences for similar violations.

Colorado conservatives should watch how national media covers this case compared to similar incidents involving Republican officials. The disparity in coverage and accountability—or lack thereof—reveals the double standard many see in progressive politics. When Democratic leaders face legal trouble, the narrative often emphasizes "mistakes" and "learning moments." Republicans face calls for resignation.

This case underscores a broader conservative argument: progressive elites demand strict rules for ordinary Americans while exempting themselves from real accountability. Kerrigan's 45-day suspension is notably light for an admission of impaired driving—a standard that wouldn't apply equally to a Colorado construction worker or rancher facing the same charge.

Watch whether Massachusetts Democrats demand Kerrigan's resignation or quietly move forward.
