# Boulder County Highway Crash Kills One; Heat-Damaged Roads Raise Safety Questions

A three-vehicle collision on Highway 119 in Boulder County Wednesday evening claimed one life and hospitalized another, raising fresh concerns about road conditions as extreme heat buckles pavement across the region.

Colorado State Patrol reported that a pickup truck, SUV, and minivan collided near milepost 34 on the highway. One person was killed and another transported to a hospital, though specific identities and injury details remain under investigation.

The crash comes as Boulder County roads face mounting stress from high temperatures causing pavement buckling on multiple routes (KDVR). While authorities have not yet determined whether road conditions contributed to Wednesday's collision, the timing underscores a broader infrastructure challenge: Colorado's aging highway system faces accelerating wear from both climate stress and deferred maintenance.

Conservatives and taxpayers should scrutinize whether state transportation budgets adequately prioritize road safety over pet projects. Colorado's gas tax and vehicle registration fees fund CDOT operations, yet buckling pavement and hazardous conditions persist—suggesting either misallocation of resources or insufficient funding transparency. The state must prove it's using transportation dollars efficiently before asking voters for more.

Watch for CDOT's official findings on the Highway 119 crash and whether the agency accelerates pavement repairs statewide.
