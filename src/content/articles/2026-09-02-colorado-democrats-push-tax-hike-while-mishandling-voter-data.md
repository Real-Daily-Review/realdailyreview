# Colorado Voters Face Tax Hike Ballot Measure While State Mishandles Voter Data

Colorado Democrats are pushing Initiative 195 this November—a constitutional amendment imposing a graduated income tax on earnings above $500,000, effectively raising taxes on high earners and corporations. Republicans argue the measure would drive successful Coloradans and businesses to lower-tax states, shrinking the tax base and ultimately harming working families through reduced economic growth and job creation. The flat 4.4% rate has kept Colorado competitive; Democrats counter that the wealthy should pay more to fund schools and services.

Meanwhile, Democratic Secretary of State Jena Griswold's office bungled voter data security. A website update exposed confidential profiles of 59 Colorado voters—a breach Griswold said she "sincerely regrets" (9News Denver). The incident raises questions about government competence managing sensitive citizen information, especially as Democrats expand state control over elections and now seek broader taxing authority.

Conservatives worry Initiative 195 signals a shift toward California-style progressive taxation that punishes success and funds bigger government. Combined with the voter-data mishap, the pattern suggests Democratic leadership prioritizes revenue extraction over protecting taxpayer privacy and prosperity.

Watch whether Republicans effectively message that Colorado's economic edge depends on keeping taxes low and government lean—and whether voters trust this administration with more power over their wallets.
