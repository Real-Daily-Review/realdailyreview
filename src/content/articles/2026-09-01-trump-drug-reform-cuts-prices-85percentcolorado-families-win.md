# Trump's Drug Price Reform Delivers Real Relief to Colorado Families

Trump administration action on prescription drug pricing is delivering tangible savings that bypass the usual government-spending playbook—and Colorado families are feeling it in their wallets.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, specific medications have plummeted in cost, with one example dropping from $1,000 to $149, marking what the outlet calls "the biggest drug price drop in 60 years." The reform targets pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), the middlemen who have long inflated costs while patients and taxpayers absorbed the hit.

This matters for Colorado because working families across the state—from Denver to rural counties—have faced the same brutal choice: buy medicine or pay rent. Unlike progressive proposals that rely on government price controls or expanded bureaucracy, this approach uses market pressure and transparency to force actual competition.

Conservatives argue this demonstrates how deregulation and targeting rent-seeking intermediaries can achieve what decades of government programs promised but failed to deliver. The reform doesn't expand the federal health apparatus; it removes barriers that allowed PBMs to extract hidden profits at patients' expense.

Democrats have historically pushed price-control schemes that conservatives warn distort markets and discourage drug innovation. This market-based alternative sidesteps that trap entirely.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democratic members—will acknowledge these savings or continue pushing their own regulatory agenda regardless of results.
