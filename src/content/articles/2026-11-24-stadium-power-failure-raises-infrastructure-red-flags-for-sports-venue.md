# Northwest Stadium Lights Fail During Bengals-Commanders Game; Commanders Win Anyway

A power failure at Northwest Stadium during Monday night's NFL matchup between the Cincinnati Bengals and Washington Commanders caused a brief delay in the third quarter, raising questions about the facility's infrastructure reliability. The lights went dark mid-game, forcing officials to pause play while crews restored power.

Despite the electrical hiccup, the Commanders capitalized on the momentum shift, with rookie quarterback Jayden Daniels orchestrating a stunning 64-yard drive in just 34 seconds to set up Drew Stevens' game-winning 26-yard field goal as time expired (New York Post).

The incident underscores a broader concern about aging stadium infrastructure across the country—a problem that could resonate with Colorado sports fans and taxpayers. If similar venues in the region face comparable issues, the costs of upgrades or replacements could fall on local governments and taxpayers already stretched thin by bloated municipal budgets.

While this particular failure occurred out of state, it's a reminder that public funding for sports facilities often comes with hidden long-term maintenance costs that politicians downplay during the initial pitch phase. Colorado taxpayers should remain vigilant about any stadium expansion or renovation proposals that promise "economic development" without transparent accounting of infrastructure upkeep expenses.

Watch for whether Northwest Stadium's ownership acknowledges the root cause and commits to specific, funded repairs—or quietly hopes the story fades.
