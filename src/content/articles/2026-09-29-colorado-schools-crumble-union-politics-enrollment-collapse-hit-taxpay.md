# Colorado School Districts Face Dual Crises: Union Politics and Enrollment Collapse

Colorado's public school systems are imploding under the weight of progressive labor politics and demographic collapse—and taxpayers are footing the bill for both.

In District 11 (Colorado Springs), the school board voted to reinstate teacher Angelica Givler-Viers after she was fired over pro-union social media posts (CPR News). The reinstatement signals how union activism now trumps administrative authority in Colorado classrooms—a win for organized labor that conservatives argue prioritizes ideology over merit and accountability.

Meanwhile, the Poudre School District north of Denver is recommending closure or consolidation of 11 schools, including three mountain facilities, as enrollment craters (KDVR Fox31). Mountain families are mobilizing to fight the closures, but the real culprit is clear: declining student populations and the fiscal mismanagement that follows when districts can't right-size operations.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue these crises stem from years of bloated administrative overhead, unsustainable union contracts, and progressive policies that have made Colorado schools less competitive. Democrats counter that school funding shortfalls reflect insufficient state investment.

The closures will devastate rural mountain communities already struggling with population loss. Conservatives should demand transparency on how much District 11 spent defending the firing and reinstatement battle—money that could have gone to classrooms.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers will finally address the structural cost drivers crushing district budgets, or simply throw more taxpayer money at the problem.
