# State's Digital ID App Down Before Holiday Travel Rush

Colorado's myColorado digital ID app has crashed at the worst possible time—right before Thanksgiving travel, leaving up to 150,000 residents potentially exposed to a cybersecurity breach with no restoration timeline in sight (Colorado Sun). Travelers are now urged to carry physical IDs through airports as the state scrambles to contain the damage.

This is exactly what conservatives warned about: centralized government digital systems create massive security vulnerabilities while stripping citizens of privacy and control. The myColorado app was sold as convenient and modern—the future of state ID. Instead, it's become a cautionary tale about government incompetence managing sensitive personal data.

The timing couldn't be worse. Thanksgiving air travel is expected to hit a 16-year high, with nearly 418,000 flights scheduled between Nov. 23 and Dec. 1 (New York Post). Coloradans relying on the app for airport security now face delays and frustration during peak travel season.

Republicans have long argued that government should stick to core functions—not experiment with untested digital platforms that put citizens' data at risk. This breach vindicates that skepticism. The state needs to explain exactly what data was compromised, who accessed it, and why there's no contingency plan for critical infrastructure.

Watch for the state's full disclosure on the breach scope and whether Colorado lawmakers demand accountability—or simply throw more taxpayer money at a failed system.
