# Arkansas River Sewage Disaster Exposes Colorado Infrastructure Neglect

A 4.25 million gallon raw sewage spill into the Arkansas River is the latest reminder that Colorado's aging water infrastructure—and the government agencies managing it—are failing taxpayers and agricultural users alike.

The spill, reported by both the Colorado Sun and CPR News, has raised urgent questions about food safety for downstream cantaloupe and pepper growers, river cleanup timelines, and E. coli contamination. But the real issue is preventable: Colorado's water systems are crumbling under decades of deferred maintenance and bureaucratic mismanagement.

Conservatives argue that bloated government agencies tasked with infrastructure oversight have consistently prioritized environmental regulations over basic maintenance and modernization. Instead of fixing pipes, they've spent resources on compliance theater and expanding their own budgets.

The spill threatens Colorado's agricultural economy—a cornerstone of rural livelihoods—and demonstrates why private-sector efficiency and accountability must replace government monopolies on critical infrastructure. Taxpayers deserve answers on how this happened, who's responsible, and why maintenance budgets have been neglected.

Watch for whether state and local officials demand real accountability or simply issue apologies and move on to the next crisis.
