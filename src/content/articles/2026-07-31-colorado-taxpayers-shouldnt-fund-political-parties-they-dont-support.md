# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado government is compelling taxpayers to finance primary elections for political parties—even when those taxpayers don't belong to either major party. That's the core complaint from conservatives at Complete Colorado, who argue the state has no business granting special recognition or privileges to Democratic and Republican organizations on the public dime.

The case is straightforward: Why should an independent voter, Libertarian, or Green Party member subsidize Democratic or Republican primaries? Complete Colorado frames this as a matter of principle—separating state from party is "courageous" governance that respects taxpayer liberty and limits government favoritism.

Republicans and conservatives argue this arrangement violates basic fairness. Taxpayers shouldn't be forced to finance political machinery they actively oppose or refuse to join. It's a form of compelled speech through taxation, they contend, and it props up the two-party duopoly at public expense.

Democrats have not publicly challenged this framing in the available sources, leaving the conservative critique largely unopposed in the current debate.

The Colorado angle cuts deep: state taxpayers are funding a system that excludes them. As the state celebrates its 150th birthday this weekend, conservatives should press lawmakers to end this political subsidy and let parties fund their own primaries.

**Watch for legislative action in the 2027 session on bills that would eliminate taxpayer financing of party primaries.**
