# Target Caves to Outrage Over Halloween Costume—Another Corporate Capitulation

Target has yanked a Halloween clown costume from shelves after social-media activists claimed its pose mimicked Jim Crow-era racial caricatures. The retailer issued a groveling apology, saying "we got this wrong" (9News Denver). Just The News reports the costume featured a model with one leg raised in a waving gesture that critics linked to offensive historical depictions.

This is corporate America's playbook: design a product, sell it, then fold instantly when the outrage mob mobilizes. Target's rapid retreat signals how thoroughly progressive activists have colonized corporate decision-making—even when the offense is manufactured or debatable.

**The conservative concern:** Retailers increasingly outsource editorial judgment to Twitter mobs rather than making independent business calls. That's not principled corporate governance; it's cowardice dressed as corporate responsibility. When companies abandon backbone over speculative racial readings of a clown pose, they're signaling that no product, no marketing choice, no creative decision is safe from retroactive condemnation.

Colorado consumers should ask whether they want to shop at retailers that prioritize appeasement over common sense.

Watch whether Target faces pressure to implement "diversity training" or hire sensitivity consultants to prevent future "missteps"—the typical corporate penance that follows these capitulations.
