# NASA's Swift Telescope Headed for Fiery Reentry—Another Government Space Project Stumbles

A $320 million NASA telescope is about to become space junk after a rescue mission failed to save it from atmospheric reentry. The Swift Observatory, a 1½-ton aging satellite, is expected to plummet to Earth on or around Friday, with most of it burning up during descent, according to the U.S. Space Force (KDVR Fox31).

The telescope, which has been in orbit since 2004, lost its ability to maintain altitude after its propulsion system degraded. NASA attempted a rescue maneuver but ultimately abandoned efforts to salvage the aging instrument.

While the reentry poses minimal risk to populated areas—most debris will incinerate—conservatives should ask hard questions about government space spending. NASA's aging fleet of satellites and telescopes represents decades of federal investment with limited accountability for maintenance and end-of-life planning. Taxpayers funded this mission; the agency should have had a coherent strategy for deorbiting it safely rather than letting it drift into an uncontrolled fall.

Colorado aerospace contractors and space industry workers have a stake in how NASA manages its assets and contracts. Poor stewardship of billion-dollar programs undermines confidence in government space initiatives and raises costs for private industry trying to compete fairly.

Watch for NASA's official investigation into what went wrong—and whether the agency will tighten oversight of aging satellite maintenance before the next costly failure.
