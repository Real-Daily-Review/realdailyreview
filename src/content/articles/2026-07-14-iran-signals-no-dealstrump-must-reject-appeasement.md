# Iran Signals It Won't Play Ball—Trump Must Listen

Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf just declared that the era of one-sided deals is finished, signaling Tehran views negotiations as leverage, not reconciliation. His statement came with a 60-day safe-passage offer for commercial vessels—a classic negotiating tactic designed to extract concessions while appearing reasonable.

The message is clear: Iran hasn't changed its stripes. While the Biden-Harris administration spent years coaxing Tehran back to the negotiating table, Iranian leadership openly mocked the very concept of balanced agreements. Conservatives argue this vindicates skepticism of appeasement-style diplomacy and underscores why the Trump administration's maximum-pressure approach—including sanctions and military deterrence—remains the only language Tehran understands.

For Colorado, this matters. Energy markets respond to Middle East tensions. Oil and gas producers here benefit from stable, predictable pricing that comes from American strength abroad, not weakness at the negotiating table. Iranian nuclear ambitions and regional aggression directly affect energy costs for Colorado families and businesses.

Republicans and conservatives say Trump must reject any deal that doesn't permanently dismantle Iran's nuclear program and constrain its regional militancy—not temporary agreements that reward bad behavior.

Watch whether the incoming administration holds firm on sanctions and refuses to legitimize Iranian negotiating theater.
