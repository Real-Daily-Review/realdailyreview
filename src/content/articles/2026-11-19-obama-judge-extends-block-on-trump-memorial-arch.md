# Federal Judge Extends Block on Trump Memorial Arch

An Obama-appointed federal judge is using the courts to delay President Trump's proposed memorial arch in Washington, D.C., extending a temporary restraining order for another 14 days.

U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan, who issued the initial block, has now extended the order until December 3 or until she rules on a preliminary injunction request, according to the Washington Examiner and New York Post. The restraining order was set to expire Thursday but will remain in place as litigation continues.

Republicans argue this is another example of activist judges weaponizing the courts to obstruct the Trump administration's agenda. The delay keeps the memorial project in legal limbo while opponents mount their challenge—a familiar tactic that conservatives say reflects the judiciary's progressive overreach.

The arch project itself has drawn opposition from various groups, though the specific grounds for the legal challenge remain part of ongoing litigation. Chutkan's decisions will determine whether the project can move forward or faces prolonged court battles.

Conservatives should watch whether Chutkan rules on the preliminary injunction before December 3, and whether the Trump administration appeals any adverse ruling to higher courts.
