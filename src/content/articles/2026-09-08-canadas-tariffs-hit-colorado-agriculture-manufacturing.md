# Canada's Retaliatory Tariffs Hit US Goods—Colorado Manufacturers and Ranchers in the Crosshairs

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on $20 billion of U.S. goods took effect Tuesday, escalating the trade war and threatening Colorado's agriculture, manufacturing, and energy sectors.

The counter-tariffs, ranging from 15% to 50%, target steel, furniture, clothing, and electronics (New York Post). They follow President Trump's August 22 decision to impose a 50% tariff on Canadian goods after stalled negotiations.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado ranchers and agricultural exporters face direct exposure. Canadian tariffs on U.S. beef, grains, and processed foods will raise costs for Canadian buyers and risk market share to competitors. Colorado manufacturers dependent on cross-border supply chains—particularly in aerospace and industrial equipment—now face higher input costs and retaliatory duties on exports northward.

Republicans argue tariffs are necessary leverage to force fairer trade terms and protect domestic workers. Democrats and free-market conservatives counter that tit-for-tat tariff escalation raises prices for American consumers and businesses, hurts export-dependent Colorado industries, and risks recession.

The Washington Examiner reports negotiations between the two countries have stalled, with no clear off-ramp in sight.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republican representatives—will pressure the administration to resume talks before tariffs permanently damage agricultural and manufacturing relationships with Canada.
