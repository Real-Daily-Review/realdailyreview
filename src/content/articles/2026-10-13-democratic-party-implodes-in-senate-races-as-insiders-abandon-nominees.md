# Democratic Chaos in Senate Races Signals Weakness Heading Into Election

Party insiders are abandoning their own nominees as Democrats scramble to hold Senate seats in competitive races. Montana's Democratic Party chairwoman, Shannon O'Brien, resigned Monday and endorsed an independent candidate over her party's official Senate pick, signaling deep fractures within the state party (Washington Examiner). Meanwhile, in Maine, a Democratic Super PAC's ad campaign targeting GOP Sen. Susan Collins' Democratic challenger Troy Jackson misfired—literally running ads in Portland, Oregon instead of Maine (Just The News), suggesting organizational chaos on the left.

Republicans argue these stumbles reveal Democratic vulnerability heading into the election. When party leadership won't back their own nominees and campaign infrastructure fails at basic execution, it undercuts Democratic messaging and morale.

**The Colorado angle:** While these races unfold in Montana and Maine, Colorado Democrats face similar headwinds. Any erosion of Democratic turnout or enthusiasm in neighboring states could ripple westward, particularly if Colorado Republicans capitalize on messaging about Democratic dysfunction and failed governance.

**What to watch:** Whether Democratic infighting spreads to Colorado's 2024 Senate race and whether Republican candidates can exploit these national party fractures to energize Colorado voters tired of progressive overreach.
