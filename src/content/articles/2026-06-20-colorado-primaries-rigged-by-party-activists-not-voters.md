# Colorado's Primary Problem: Party Activists, Not Voters, Are Picking Winners

Colorado's primary system is letting a small slice of party activists—not the broader electorate—decide which candidates advance, and unaffiliated voters are increasingly sidelined by the rules.

According to Complete Colorado, party-registered voters who skip caucuses are "simply limiting their options under the current rules." The problem runs deeper: ideological factions within both parties dominate primaries, shaping outcomes that don't reflect most Colorado voters' preferences. Kent Thiry has warned of a "tyranny of the minority" in Colorado elections, where primary activists wield outsized power.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and reform-minded observers argue the current system empowers the most ideologically rigid activists while freezing out moderates and unaffiliated voters—roughly 40% of Colorado's electorate. This narrows the field of viable candidates and can produce nominees misaligned with general-election voters. Conservatives also point to election integrity concerns; Complete Colorado has called for independent audits, arguing that "we don't save democracy by telling skeptical voters to shut up, but by making the system more trustworthy."

**The stakes:** Colorado's primary rules directly shape who represents the state in Congress, the legislature, and statewide office. A system dominated by party insiders rather than voters weakens Republican competitiveness in general elections and undermines public confidence in the process.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers move to reform primary rules or expand audit transparency—and whether unaffiliated voters demand a seat at the table.
