# DOJ Cracks Down on California's Noncitizen Voter Problem—Colorado Should Take Note

The Justice Department is threatening to sue California over noncitizens on voter rolls, demanding the state review suspected illegal voters within 48 hours (Just The News). The move signals a federal crackdown on election integrity that conservatives have long demanded.

California's voter registration system has allowed noncitizens to receive election mail ballots—a vulnerability that undermines the fundamental principle of one citizen, one vote. Republicans argue this is exactly the kind of lax election administration that fuels voter fraud concerns and erodes public confidence in elections.

**The Colorado angle matters here.** While Colorado has tightened voter ID requirements in recent years, the state's mail-ballot system remains vulnerable to similar problems. Conservatives worry that Colorado's automatic voter registration and same-day registration policies could enable noncitizens to vote if election officials don't verify citizenship status rigorously. Democrats counter that such fraud is rare and that stricter ID rules suppress legitimate voters.

The Justice Department's aggressive stance—threatening litigation and setting hard deadlines—represents a shift toward federal enforcement of election integrity standards. This could pressure other states, including Colorado, to audit their voter rolls more thoroughly.

**Watch whether Colorado election officials voluntarily conduct a citizenship audit or resist federal pressure to do so.**
