# Missouri's Redistricting Chaos Shows Why Courts Shouldn't Referee Elections

Justice Brett Kavanaugh's refusal to lift a block on Missouri's revised congressional map has left the state in legal limbo weeks before November—a cautionary tale for Colorado voters watching judicial overreach into election administration.

Missouri Republicans drew a new map favoring their party 7-1 and used it successfully in primaries, but a federal judge halted it before the general election, forcing the state to revert to the old 6-2 Republican map (Fox News Politics). Kavanaugh declined to grant an emergency stay, leaving Missouri stuck between competing court orders (Just The News).

**The conservative argument:** This mess exemplifies why unelected judges shouldn't micromanage redistricting. States have constitutional authority over their own maps. Courts inserting themselves into election timing creates chaos, uncertainty, and undermines voter confidence. Republicans note the map was legally drawn and passed through proper channels—yet judicial second-guessing now punishes the state's voters and election officials.

**Democrats counter** that the revised map was an extreme partisan gerrymander that violated voting rights protections.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's own redistricting battles have landed in court repeatedly. This Missouri precedent shows how judicial intervention can drag out disputes right up to Election Day, potentially affecting Colorado's 2024 races if similar challenges resurface.

Watch whether the Supreme Court takes a full case on redistricting timing—or whether states will start insulating their maps from last-minute judicial intervention.
