# Colorado Moves to Strip State Perks From Political Parties

Colorado is taking a rare stand against government favoritism by ending special state recognition and privileges granted to political parties—a move conservatives say corrects decades of improper entanglement between state apparatus and partisan machinery.

According to Complete Colorado, the state has historically granted official status and benefits to major parties, a practice that blurs the line between neutral government and political patronage. Republicans and conservatives argue this represents government overreach dressed up as tradition. "Colorado government has no proper business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties," the outlet notes, framing the shift as a necessary separation of state from party machinery.

The move reflects a broader conservative principle: government should remain neutral and impartial, not subsidize or elevate any faction. For Colorado taxpayers and voters, this means state resources—funding, office space, official designation—won't flow preferentially to Democratic or Republican operations.

Democrats have not yet publicly detailed their objection, though the left typically defends such arrangements as administrative convenience or historical practice.

This development matters because it tests whether Colorado will actually enforce equal treatment under law, or whether bureaucratic inertia and partisan convenience will reassert themselves.
