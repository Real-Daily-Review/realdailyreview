# Colorado Fruit Growers Face Ruin After April Freeze; State Must Cut Red Tape to Aid Recovery

An April freeze wiped out fruit crops across Colorado's North Fork Valley, leaving growers like the Kropp family's First Fruits facing total losses (Colorado Sun). Western Slope orchardists are now scrambling to survive through business pivots and cost-cutting—exactly the kind of resilience government should support, not hinder.

This disaster underscores a hard truth: Colorado agriculture operates on razor-thin margins. When nature strikes, farmers need fast relief, not bureaucratic delays. Republicans and conservatives argue that state and federal disaster assistance should be streamlined and free of red tape, allowing growers to rebuild quickly without drowning in paperwork. The left's typical response—more regulation and subsidy programs—often creates new compliance burdens that small family farms can't afford.

Meanwhile, Colorado faces a separate but related crisis: soaring mortgage rates are pricing residents out of homeownership, while the state's power grid strains under demand (9News). These challenges demand market-driven solutions—deregulation of housing development, streamlined permitting for energy infrastructure, and tax relief for struggling families—not more government "solutions" that inflate costs further.

The North Fork Valley freeze is a reminder that Colorado's economy depends on agriculture, energy, and affordable housing. Government's job is to get out of the way.

**Watch for:** Whether state lawmakers push disaster relief that actually reaches farmers, or bury aid under regulatory requirements.
