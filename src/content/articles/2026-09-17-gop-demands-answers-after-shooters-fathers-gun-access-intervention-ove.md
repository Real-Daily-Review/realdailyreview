# GOP Blindsided by New Details on Trump Butler Shooter—Including Father's Failed Gun-Access Intervention

Republican lawmakers are demanding answers after learning that Thomas Matthew Crooks' father had actively tried to block his son's firearm access months before the July assassination attempt on Trump in Butler, Pennsylvania. According to Fox News and Just The News, Matthew Crooks sought ways to prevent his son from accessing guns as early as May 2024—roughly two months before the shooting—yet federal authorities failed to alert the task force investigating the incident that they had interviewed Crooks' family members.

The revelations have left GOP lawmakers questioning how the Secret Service and FBI missed critical warning signs. "Awfully strange," one lawmaker told Fox News, underscoring Republican concerns about intelligence failures and coordination breakdowns between federal agencies.

The new details raise hard questions about government competence: if Crooks' own father was alarmed enough to intervene, why weren't federal agencies connecting those dots? Republicans argue this points to systemic failures in how the federal government handles threat assessment and inter-agency communication—issues that directly affect public safety and national security.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether congressional Republicans push for accountability hearings and whether the Biden-Harris administration's handling of the investigation becomes a campaign issue heading into November.
