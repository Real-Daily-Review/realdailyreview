# Cambridge's DEI Disaster Offers a Cautionary Tale for Colorado Universities

Elite institutions' obsession with diversity credentials over merit is collapsing under its own weight—and Colorado's universities should take note before they follow the same path.

Cambridge University's implosion over now-former Professor Jason Arday, who became the institution's youngest Black professor at 36 before being exposed for fabricated credentials and academic inadequacies, exposes the rot at the heart of DEI-driven hiring. National Review reports that Cambridge was "willing to look the other way" when it came to Arday's "manifest inadequacies and deceptions for DEI reasons." The Washington Examiner notes the irony: Arday has done more to discredit diversity initiatives than any meritocratic critic could have managed.

The lesson for Colorado is stark. Universities like CU Boulder and Colorado State have aggressively pursued DEI mandates and hiring quotas in recent years, often at the expense of rigorous academic standards. When institutions prioritize demographic boxes over qualifications, they inevitably hire unqualified people, damage their reputations, and—most importantly—shortchange students paying tuition.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that DEI is fundamentally anti-merit and corrosive to institutional excellence. Cambridge's self-inflicted wound proves the point.

Colorado taxpayers and parents should demand that state universities commit publicly to merit-based hiring and transparent hiring criteria—before another Arday situation embarrasses our institutions and wastes public dollars.
