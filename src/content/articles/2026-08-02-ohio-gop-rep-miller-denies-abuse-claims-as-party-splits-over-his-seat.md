# Ohio GOP Rep. Max Miller Denies Abuse Allegations as Party Fractures Over His Future

Rep. Max Miller (R-OH) took to social media Sunday to deny domestic abuse allegations leveled by his ex-wife, Emily Moreno, claiming the accusations have "no evidence" and were "already investigated" (Just The News). Miller said he has "nothing to hide" (Fox News), though he cited Moreno's "significant mental health challenges" in his defense (Washington Examiner).

The controversy has split Ohio Republicans. Sen. Bernie Moreno—whose daughter Emily is Miller's ex-wife—broke ranks Friday, calling for Miller's resignation and saying he needs "professional help" (New York Post). The House Ethics Committee has refused to comment on whether it will investigate (Daily Caller).

**The Colorado angle:** This intra-GOP fracture mirrors tensions conservatives face nationwide between loyalty to party and accountability standards. Colorado Republicans watching their Ohio counterparts should note how public pressure and family involvement can override party unity—a lesson relevant as state GOP leaders navigate their own disciplinary decisions.

**What to watch:** Whether the House Ethics Committee opens a formal investigation and whether other GOP members follow Moreno's lead in calling for Miller's ouster.
