# E-Bike Crashes Raise Safety Questions as Denver Streets Grow More Congested

Denver's roads are becoming a patchwork of competing traffic modes, and the results are increasingly dangerous. A series of e-bike incidents—including a crash on Interstate 70 that sent one rider to the hospital and a fatal collision involving a Fort Collins man days after his father's death—underscore a growing problem: Colorado's cities lack clear safety standards for electric bikes sharing space with cars and trucks.

The I-70 crash (9News Denver) closed westbound lanes as Denver Police investigated a collision between a motorist and e-bike rider. Days earlier, a Fort Collins man died in a separate e-bike crash, compounding tragedy for a family already grieving (9News Denver).

**The conservative concern:** While progressives champion e-bikes as a climate solution, they've rushed to integrate them into urban infrastructure without adequate safety regulations, enforcement, or driver education. Colorado taxpayers are footing the bill for bike lanes and infrastructure while accident liability remains murky.

Republicans and safety advocates argue that before expanding e-bike programs, cities must establish clear rules of the road, require safety equipment and licensing, and ensure adequate separation between bikes and vehicle traffic. Denver's piecemeal approach—mixing high-speed corridors with untested bike infrastructure—prioritizes ideology over public safety.
