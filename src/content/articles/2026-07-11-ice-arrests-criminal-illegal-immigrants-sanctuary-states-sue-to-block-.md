# ICE Cracks Down on Criminal Illegal Immigrants as Sanctuary States Fight Back

The federal government is finally enforcing immigration law against serious criminals—and progressive states are suing to stop it.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement arrested illegal immigrants convicted of attempted murder, rape, and child sex crimes in targeted operations across multiple states this week (Fox News). Among those deported was an illegal alien convicted of repeatedly sexually abusing a child, whom Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz had previously pardoned (Breitbart).

The crackdown has exposed the sanctuary-state rebellion. The Department of Justice sued Maryland over its Community Trust Act, accusing the state of an "active and deliberate effort" to block ICE detainers and prevent deportations—a direct violation of federal supremacy (Fox News). Meanwhile, Mexico's government vowed legal action after ICE fatally shot an illegal migrant who allegedly attempted to ram an agent with a car during an operation.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's own sanctuary policies in Denver and Boulder have shielded illegal immigrants from federal deportation efforts. If the DOJ prevails against Maryland, Colorado cities could face similar legal pressure to comply with ICE operations—or face federal consequences.

Conservatives argue that sanctuary laws endanger public safety by protecting criminals. Democrats counter that such policies protect vulnerable immigrant communities from overzealous enforcement.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic leadership will double down on sanctuary protections or quietly distance themselves as the political cost rises.
