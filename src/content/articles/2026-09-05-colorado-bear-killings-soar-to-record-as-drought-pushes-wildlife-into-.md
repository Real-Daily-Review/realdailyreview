# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfires Drive Wildlife Into Suburbs

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears so far this year—more than triple last year's total—as prolonged drought and wildfire conditions push hungry wildlife into populated areas at unprecedented rates, according to the Colorado Sun.

The spike reflects a cascading environmental problem: water scarcity and fire damage are decimating natural food sources in the high country, forcing bears downslope into residential neighborhoods where they raid garbage, livestock, and bird feeders. Once habituated to human food, bears become dangerous and are typically destroyed.

**The conservative angle:** This underscores why Colorado needs responsible land management—including timber thinning, forest maintenance, and controlled burns—rather than locking up public lands. Environmentalist resistance to active forest management has left Colorado's wilderness overgrown and vulnerable to catastrophic fire and drought. The result: dead bears, endangered communities, and wasted wildlife.

Republicans and outdoor conservatives argue that preventing these tragedies requires letting landowners and state agencies manage forests aggressively, not restricting access or banning logging. Democrats and environmental groups counter that climate change is the root cause and demand more protected wilderness.

**Watch this:** Whether Colorado's next legislature funds aggressive forest restoration or continues environmental restrictions that leave forests tinderboxes—and bears homeless.
