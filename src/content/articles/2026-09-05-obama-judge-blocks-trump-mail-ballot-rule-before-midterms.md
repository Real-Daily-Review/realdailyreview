# Federal Judge Blocks Trump's Mail-Ballot Rule Ahead of November Elections

An Obama-appointed federal judge has torpedoed the Trump administration's attempt to tighten U.S. Postal Service mail-in ballot procedures just weeks before the midterms, throwing election administration into uncertainty across the country—including Colorado, which relies heavily on mail voting.

U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani issued a preliminary injunction Friday blocking portions of the USPS rule developed under Trump's executive order, preventing enforcement for November's elections (Washington Examiner, The Daily Wire).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the new USPS procedures were designed to strengthen ballot security and ensure timely mail delivery—reasonable safeguards for election integrity. The Trump administration framed the rules as operational improvements to prevent delays and lost ballots. Blocking them, conservatives contend, removes commonsense protections and leaves mail-voting vulnerable to the very problems the rules aimed to fix.

**The left's position:** Challengers to the rule—backed by voting-rights groups—argued the new procedures would burden states and voters, creating unnecessary obstacles to mail-in balloting.

For Colorado, which conducts elections almost entirely by mail, this ruling means continued uncertainty over federal ballot-handling standards heading into a critical election cycle. Coloradans should watch whether the Trump administration appeals or whether states move to adopt their own ballot-security measures independently.
