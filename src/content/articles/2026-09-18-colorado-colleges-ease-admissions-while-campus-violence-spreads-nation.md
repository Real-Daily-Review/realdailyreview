# Colorado College Admissions Get Easier, But Campus Safety Remains a Concern

Colorado State University Pueblo and Colorado School of Mines are streamlining admissions processes for state high school students, reducing bureaucratic friction in getting qualified Coloradans into higher education (CPR News). The moves aim to make college access less cumbersome—a straightforward efficiency gain.

Yet the timing underscores a darker reality on American campuses. Recent mob assaults on college students, including a caught-on-camera beating near York College of Pennsylvania that resulted in criminal charges against three juveniles (New York Post), highlight how campus safety has deteriorated. While Colorado institutions work to welcome students, they must also grapple with the lawlessness plaguing other campuses nationwide.

Conservatives argue that streamlined admissions are fine, but only if paired with genuine campus security and accountability for violent crime. Colorado's universities should prioritize student safety as aggressively as they pursue enrollment efficiency. The state's higher-ed institutions must ensure that easier admission doesn't come at the cost of campus order and protection for Colorado's young people.

Watch whether CSU Pueblo and Mines implement corresponding security upgrades to match their admissions expansion.
