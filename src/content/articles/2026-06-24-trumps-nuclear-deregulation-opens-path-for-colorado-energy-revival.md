# Trump's Nuclear Push Opens Door for American Energy Independence

The Trump administration's deregulation agenda is finally unlocking America's dormant nuclear sector—and Colorado should pay attention to what comes next.

Valar Atomics, a nuclear startup operating in Utah, recently became the first Department of Energy-authorized reactor built outside a national laboratory to reach "criticality," according to the *Washington Examiner*. The milestone marks a tangible win for Trump's stated goal of rebuilding the U.S. nuclear industrial base as a cornerstone of energy independence.

For Colorado, this matters. The state has a long history in nuclear development and uranium production. Advanced reactor technology could reshape the energy landscape across the Mountain West, offering reliable baseload power without the carbon footprint that progressives claim to oppose—yet often block through regulatory obstruction.

The real test now is whether federal permitting stays streamlined. Conservatives argue that decades of regulatory red tape strangled American nuclear innovation while China and Russia raced ahead. Removing those barriers, not subsidies, is the path forward.

Taxpayers and energy advocates should watch whether Colorado lawmakers and the state Public Utilities Commission embrace advanced reactor development or whether environmental groups succeed in throwing up new obstacles.
