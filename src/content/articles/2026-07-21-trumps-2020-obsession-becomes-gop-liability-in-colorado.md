# Trump's Election Obsession Threatens GOP Momentum in Colorado

Former President Donald Trump's continued focus on 2020 election fraud claims is becoming a liability for Republicans heading into the fall campaign—and Colorado voters are noticing the contradiction.

Trump has repeatedly claimed mail-in ballots and voting machines are rigged to favor Democrats, yet he hasn't made similar accusations when Republicans win elections, including his own 2016 victory. The inconsistency is raising eyebrows among Colorado voters, according to letters published in the *Denver Post*.

National Review, a conservative outlet, warns that reviving "widely unpopular" stolen-election rhetoric won't help Republicans this cycle. The criticism underscores a growing divide: while many conservatives support Trump's policies, his relentless focus on 2020 grievances threatens to overshadow GOP messaging on inflation, government spending, and energy policy—issues that matter to Colorado taxpayers and workers.

For Colorado Republicans, the stakes are real. The state has become increasingly purple, and dwelling on election disputes from six years ago distracts from winning arguments about fiscal responsibility, regulatory overreach, and protecting Colorado's energy sector from federal overreach.

**Watch whether Colorado GOP candidates distance themselves from Trump's election claims or double down—a telling sign of where the party stands heading into 2026.**
