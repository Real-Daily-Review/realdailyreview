# Ex-Republican Leader Cantor Joins Big Pharma Lobby—Classic Washington Revolving Door

Eric Cantor, the former House Majority Leader, has landed the top job at PhRMA, the pharmaceutical industry's most powerful lobbying outfit—a move that crystallizes everything conservatives despise about Washington's revolving door.

Cantor's appointment as president and CEO of the Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America signals how establishment Republicans cash in after leaving office, trading legislative access for seven-figure compensation packages. The Daily Caller and Breitbart both flagged the move as emblematic of "swamp" behavior: a onetime GOP power player now positioned to lobby his former colleagues on behalf of an industry that has fought tooth-and-nail against price transparency and market competition.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, this matters. PhRMA's lobbying directly shapes federal drug pricing policy, Medicare negotiations, and patent protections that inflate medication costs. Cantor's insider status means Big Pharma gains another channel to block reforms that would inject real market competition into pharmaceutical markets—exactly the kind of crony capitalism that infuriates limited-government Republicans.

Conservatives have long argued that the pharmaceutical industry uses regulatory capture and lobbying muscle to prevent price competition and protect monopoly profits. Cantor's hire underscores why term limits and strict revolving-door restrictions remain essential reforms.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly any members claiming to champion "Main Street over Wall Street"—accepts PhRMA donations or meets with Cantor's team without public disclosure.
