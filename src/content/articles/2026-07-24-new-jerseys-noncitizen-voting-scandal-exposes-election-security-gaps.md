# New Jersey's Noncitizen Voting Fiasco Raises Election Integrity Alarms

New Jersey's admission that at least 400 noncitizens illegally voted in recent elections exposes the real-world danger of lax voter registration controls—a warning Colorado election officials should heed closely.

Democratic Gov. Mikie Sherrill acknowledged Tuesday that her state improperly registered nearly 7,000 noncitizens between June 2023 and June 2024, resulting in illegal votes cast in state elections. Sherrill called the situation "unacceptable," though she downplayed its significance as "not a problem" in her view (Washington Examiner).

Republicans argue this scandal validates their long-standing concerns about election security and the need for strict voter ID requirements and citizenship verification. Colorado conservatives have pushed similar safeguards here, facing Democratic resistance to what the left dismisses as "voter suppression." The New Jersey case demonstrates that without rigorous vetting, election rolls become vulnerable to fraud—whether through negligence or design.

Democrats typically counter that noncitizen voting is rare and that strict ID laws disenfranchise eligible voters. Yet New Jersey's own numbers—400+ illegal votes from a single registration blunder—suggest the problem is neither theoretical nor trivial.

Colorado taxpayers and election integrity advocates should watch whether state election officials tighten citizenship verification procedures and whether the legislature acts to prevent similar registration failures here.
