# Colorado Salmonella Outbreak Tied to Jalapeños at Major Chains

Colorado health officials have linked a salmonella outbreak affecting 110 state residents to contaminated jalapeños served at Chipotle and Qdoba locations, according to CPR News and the Colorado Sun. Both restaurant chains have since pulled the peppers from their menus, and state health authorities say there is no ongoing risk to diners at these establishments.

The outbreak underscores a persistent vulnerability in America's food supply chain—one that regulatory agencies have struggled to prevent despite decades of oversight. While the chains' swift response to remove the product is commendable, conservatives rightly question whether the FDA's Byzantine inspection regime actually protects consumers or simply creates compliance theater that burdens smaller producers while larger corporations absorb costs and move on.

Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for outbreak investigations and public health response. The state's health department will need resources to trace the contamination source—likely imported produce—raising questions about supply-chain transparency and whether domestic agriculture should receive stronger market incentives over cheaper foreign alternatives.

Health officials have declared the immediate risk contained, but Colorado consumers should demand clearer labeling of produce origin and stronger liability frameworks that incentivize food safety without expanding government bureaucracy.

Watch whether state lawmakers use this incident to justify new food-safety regulations that ultimately protect big chains while squeezing local producers and farmers' markets.
