# Trump Pushes Data Centers While Colorado Faces Energy, Water Trade-offs

President Trump is doubling down on data center development as a path to prosperity, declaring communities that reject them will become "backwards and poor" (New York Post). But the pitch glosses over real concerns that matter to Colorado taxpayers and ratepayers.

Data centers promise jobs and tax revenue. Trump frames opposition as backward thinking. Yet polling shows most Americans oppose them in their neighborhoods, and conservatives have legitimate questions about the trade-offs.

**The Colorado angle:** Data centers demand massive amounts of electricity and water—two resources Colorado already manages carefully. If facilities land here, they could drive up power costs for families and businesses, strain water supplies during drought, and burden local infrastructure. Republicans argue the state should demand genuine cost-benefit analysis before green-lighting projects, not rubber-stamp them based on national cheerleading.

The Daily Caller notes Trump's claims about prosperity lack supporting data. Conservatives say that's the real issue: show Coloradans the numbers. What are actual job creation figures? Who bears infrastructure costs? Will ratepayers subsidize private data center profits?

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado's Public Utilities Commission demands transparent energy-impact studies and whether Republican legislators push for local control over siting decisions rather than state-level mandates favoring developers.
