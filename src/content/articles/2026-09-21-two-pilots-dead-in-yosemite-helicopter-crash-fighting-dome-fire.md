# Two Pilots Killed Fighting California Wildfire; Safety Questions Loom

A helicopter crashed Sunday while battling the Dome Fire near Yosemite National Park in California, killing both pilots, the FAA confirmed Monday. The aircraft went down in Mariposa County while the fire—which ignited last Tuesday near Wawona Dome—continues to draw over 500 crisis responders.

Details surrounding the crash remain unclear, but the incident underscores the inherent risks firefighting crews face during wildfire suppression operations. The Dome Fire is one of several major blazes consuming California resources as the state grapples with seasonal fire season.

For Colorado, the tragedy serves as a stark reminder of wildfire dangers facing our own state. Colorado firefighters and aerial crews regularly battle similar conditions, particularly during peak summer months. The crash raises questions about equipment maintenance, pilot training standards, and whether federal agencies adequately resource and support the men and women on the front lines of fire suppression.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that California's restrictive environmental regulations and failure to maintain forests through responsible timber management and controlled burns have worsened wildfire conditions—a lesson Colorado policymakers should heed as they balance environmental mandates with practical forest stewardship.

Watch for federal investigations into the crash's cause and any resulting changes to wildfire aviation protocols affecting Colorado operations.
