# Trump's Iran Deal Collapse Sparks Conservative Criticism

President Trump's abrupt reversal on Iran negotiations has drawn sharp rebuke from both left and right, with conservatives questioning whether the administration abandoned core positions in a hastily brokered truce agreement.

National Review reports that Trump capitulated on key sticking points, adopting Iranian positions across major points of contention. The criticism cuts deeper than typical partisan sniping: even right-leaning outlets argue the president surrendered leverage without securing meaningful concessions, undermining the hardline stance that defined his first term's "maximum pressure" strategy.

Democrats and their media allies, meanwhile, seize on the reversal as evidence of chaos and incompetence. Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin frames the deal as a product of desperation, citing Vice President JD Vance's inflammatory rhetoric about Haitian immigrants as symptomatic of an administration resorting to distraction and falsehood.

**The conservative case:** Republicans who backed Trump's 2015 Iran deal withdrawal now face an uncomfortable question—did this administration trade away negotiating strength for a political win? Conservatives argue any Iran agreement must include ironclad verification mechanisms and sunset clauses, not capitulation.

**What taxpayers should watch:** Whether Congress demands transparency on the deal's terms and whether energy markets—critical to Colorado's economy—react to renewed Iranian oil production.
