# Colorado Boating Emergencies Highlight Need for Personal Responsibility and Private Rescue

Two separate water emergencies across Colorado this week underscore why outdoor recreation demands individual preparedness—not more government mandates.

Mesa County Search and Rescue teams worked through the night to evacuate 120 boaters and six dogs from the Colorado River during the Snyder fire, according to CPR News. Meanwhile, nine people were rescued by fellow boaters after their vessel capsized at Chatfield State Park near Denver, KDVR reported.

Both incidents reveal a troubling pattern: recreationalists venturing onto Colorado's waterways without adequate safety planning, forcing taxpayer-funded rescue teams into dangerous overnight operations. The Snyder fire evacuation alone tied up county resources that could have been deployed elsewhere.

Conservatives argue these incidents demonstrate why personal responsibility—proper life jackets, safety briefings, weather awareness, and trip planning—must come before expanding state water-safety bureaucracy. Colorado doesn't need new regulations; it needs boaters who respect the risks.

The private boaters who rescued the Chatfield group showed what civic-minded citizens accomplish without government intervention. That's the model worth promoting.

Watch whether Mesa County or state officials use these rescues to justify new water-safety regulations or funding requests that burden Colorado's already-stretched outdoor recreation infrastructure.
