# Supreme Court Faces Competing Overreach in Trump Cases

The Supreme Court is wading into two high-stakes Trump disputes where judicial overreach meets executive authority—and Colorado voters should care about how the justices resolve the collision between constitutional limits and partisan litigation.

In one case, the Court is examining Trump's mail-in ballot order against a district judge's sweeping countermand (National Review). Both sides, conservatives note, have stretched their authority: Trump's directive and the lower court's blocking order both raise serious separation-of-powers questions. The justices must thread a narrow needle without endorsing either overreach.

Separately, Trump is asking the Supreme Court to overturn an $83 million judgment to E. Jean Carroll, invoking the Court's 2024 presidential immunity decision (Just The News). Trump argues that ruling shields him from liability in the case. Democrats will predictably frame this as Trump escaping accountability; Republicans counter that the immunity doctrine protects executive function from weaponized civil suits.

For Colorado, these cases matter: they test whether courts will respect constitutional limits on their own power or continue expanding judicial reach into executive and electoral decisions. The outcomes will shape how future administrations—of either party—operate.

**Watch whether the Court actually constrains lower courts' authority or gives them a pass while limiting Trump.**
