# Flawed Surveillance Tech Snares Innocent Colorado Woman—Lawsuit Exposes Risks of Automated Policing

A Colorado woman is suing Flock Safety, Denver police, and an officer after license plate camera data falsely flagged her as a package thief, according to 9News Denver. Chrisanna Elser was accused based on the surveillance company's technology, which matched her vehicle to a theft scene—a claim later proven wrong. She filed suit a year after police showed up at her door with the faulty evidence.

This case highlights a critical conservative concern: government and private surveillance systems can destroy lives before due process kicks in. Automated license plate readers, increasingly deployed across Colorado cities, generate mountains of data that police rely on—often without adequate verification protocols. When a private vendor's algorithm flags you as a suspect, the burden falls on the innocent to prove their innocence, not on authorities to prove guilt.

Republicans and civil libertarians have long warned that surveillance infrastructure, however well-intentioned, creates opportunities for error and abuse. This lawsuit underscores why Colorado lawmakers should demand transparency and accountability standards before expanding these systems further.

Watch whether Denver and other Colorado police departments will tighten verification procedures before acting on automated camera data—or whether they'll double down on the technology regardless of collateral damage to innocent citizens.
