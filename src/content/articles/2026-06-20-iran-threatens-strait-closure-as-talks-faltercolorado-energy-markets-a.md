# Iran Threatens Strait of Hormuz Closure as Negotiations Collapse—Colorado Energy Markets Should Brace

Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps announced Saturday it is closing the Strait of Hormuz again, escalating tensions just as the regime's diplomatic team heads to Switzerland for talks with the United States. The closure comes amid alleged ceasefire violations in Lebanon fighting, which resumed for a second consecutive day despite both sides agreeing to terms.

Vice President JD Vance disputed Iran's claim, saying there is no evidence the strait has actually been shut down (New York Post). The Iranian foreign ministry simultaneously announced its negotiating team was departing for Switzerland, signaling Tehran is playing both confrontation and diplomacy simultaneously.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy markets nationwide—including Colorado's oil and gas sector—face potential price volatility if Iran follows through on threats to block one of the world's most critical shipping lanes. A genuine closure would disrupt global crude supplies and spike prices at the pump and for heating fuel, directly hitting Colorado households and energy workers already navigating regulatory headwinds from state Democrats.

Republicans argue the Biden-Harris administration's weak posture toward Iran—including the failed interim agreement—has emboldened the regime to use economic coercion as negotiating leverage. Conservatives warn that appeasement invites further aggression.

**Watch:** Whether Iran actually enforces the closure and how U.S. energy markets respond—Colorado taxpayers should monitor gas prices and demand state leaders prioritize domestic energy independence over green mandates.
