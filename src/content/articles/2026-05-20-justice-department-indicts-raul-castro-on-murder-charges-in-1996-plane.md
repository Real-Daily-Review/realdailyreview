# Raúl Castro Indicted on Murder Charges in 1996 Shootdown Case

The Justice Department indicted former Cuban President Raúl Castro on Wednesday on murder and conspiracy charges stemming from Cuba's 1996 shootdown of two civilian planes operated by Brothers to the Rescue, a Florida-based humanitarian group founded by Cuban exile José Basulto.

Castro and five others were indicted by a grand jury in the Southern District of Florida, according to The Hill. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the charges in Miami "in conjunction with a ceremony to honor the victims," the outlet reported.

Brothers to the Rescue, according to PBS NewsHour, aimed to help Cuban refugees in the Florida Straits by dropping supplies from small planes and alerting the U.S. Coast Guard. The 1996 incident, which occurred three decades ago, significantly inflamed U.S.-Cuba relations, CBS News noted.

The timing of the indictment coincides with Cuba's Independence Day and comes amid President Trump's stated threat to invade the island, according to The Hill. The move represents an escalation in pressure on the Cuban government as the Trump administration takes office.
