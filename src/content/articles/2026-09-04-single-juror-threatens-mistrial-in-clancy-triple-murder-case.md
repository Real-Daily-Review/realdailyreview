# Hung Jury Looms in Clancy Trial as Judge Questions Holdout Juror

A Massachusetts mother accused of killing her three children faces a potential mistrial after a single juror has twice signaled the jury cannot reach unanimous agreement. Lindsay Clancy's trial has stretched into its seventh day of deliberations, with Judge William Sullivan taking the rare step of questioning the holdout juror Friday morning to determine whether a verdict remains possible.

The case has drawn comparisons to the 2001 Andrea Yates infanticide trial. George Parnham, Yates' defense attorney, told 9News Denver the cases share "significant similarities"—both involving mothers accused of killing their children under circumstances raising questions about mental state and criminal responsibility.

Conservatives and legal observers have watched such cases closely as they intersect with questions about mental illness defenses, parental responsibility, and the jury system's ability to reach justice. A mistrial would force prosecutors to decide whether to retry Clancy, prolonging an already emotionally taxing case for all involved.

The judge's decision to interview the juror directly—a step rarely taken—suggests the court is exploring whether deliberations can continue productively or whether a new trial is inevitable.
