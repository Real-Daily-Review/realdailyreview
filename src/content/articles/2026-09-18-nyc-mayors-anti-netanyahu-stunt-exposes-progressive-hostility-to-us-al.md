# NYC Mayor's Anti-Netanyahu Stance Exposes Progressive Hostility to U.S. Ally

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's attempt to arrest Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu during his U.N. General Assembly visit reveals how far progressive politicians will go to appease anti-Israel activists—even at the cost of diplomatic relations with America's closest Middle East ally.

Mamdani initially signaled he would pursue Netanyahu's arrest on war crimes allegations, prompting Israel's U.N. Ambassador Danny Danon to fire back, calling for *Mamdani's* arrest instead (Washington Examiner). The mayor has since walked back his more inflammatory rhetoric (Fox News), but the damage to U.S.-Israel relations and American credibility is real.

This isn't a Colorado story yet—but it matters. Progressive activists are emboldened by Democratic leaders who prioritize ideological purity over strategic alliances. If similar anti-Israel sentiment gains traction in Colorado's Democratic establishment, it could reshape how our state's representatives vote on Middle East policy, foreign aid, and defense spending.

Conservatives argue that undermining Netanyahu—whatever one's views on Israeli policy—weakens America's position globally and signals to adversaries that the U.S. won't stand by its partners. The left frames this as moral clarity; Republicans see it as naive and dangerous.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic delegation distances itself from Mamdani's position or stays silent.
