# Colorado High Court Blocks Democratic Gerrymandering Scheme

Colorado's Supreme Court has shut down a Democratic-backed redistricting plan, keeping the state out of the national gerrymandering wars—at least for now. The ruling prevents Democrats from redrawing congressional maps outside the normal decennial cycle, a move conservatives argue would have handed the party unfair electoral advantages.

Republicans and conservative groups have long warned that gerrymandering—whether by Democrats or Republicans—undermines representative government and voter confidence. The Colorado court's decision reflects judicial skepticism toward mid-decade map manipulation, a tactic that has roiled politics nationwide. Texas Republicans faced similar criticism for aggressive redistricting before the 2020 census (Denver Post), though Democrats have pursued comparable strategies in other states.

For Colorado, the ruling preserves the current district lines through the next census, protecting the state's competitive balance in congressional races. Colorado voters and taxpayers benefit when electoral maps are drawn fairly and predictably, not redrawn every election cycle to benefit one party.

The decision also signals the court's willingness to police partisan overreach—a principle that should apply equally to both parties. Conservatives should watch whether Democrats attempt end-runs around this ruling or push for constitutional amendments to weaken redistricting protections.
