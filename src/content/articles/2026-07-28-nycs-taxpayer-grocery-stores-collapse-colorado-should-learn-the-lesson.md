# Government Grocery Stores: A Cautionary Tale for Colorado Taxpayers

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's taxpayer-funded municipal grocery store experiment is unraveling—and Colorado should take note before progressive politicians pitch similar schemes here.

Economists are shredding Mamdani's central claim that government-run stores can deliver 30% savings to shoppers. According to Fox News, experts call the promise an "illusion" that simply shifts costs from customers to taxpayers footing the bill. The Washington Examiner reports that an immigrant-led business coalition is preparing to sue over the stores, arguing they unfairly undercut private grocers—many minority-owned—that have long served their communities.

This is textbook progressive overreach: government bureaucrats claiming they can run retail better than the market, then discovering they can't. The "fatal economics," as Breitbart notes, don't disappear because politicians wish them away—they get buried in budget deficits and business failures.

Colorado conservatives should recognize the pattern. When Denver or state lawmakers face food access complaints, the instinct will be to propose government solutions. But Mamdani's debacle proves that subsidizing government stores doesn't lower costs; it hides them. Private grocers, competition, and market incentives work. Government ownership doesn't.

Watch whether any Colorado Democrat introduces similar "public grocery" language in the 2025 legislative session—and be ready to cite New York's costly failure.
