# Secret Service Rolls Out New Badge While Accountability Questions Linger

The Secret Service unveiled a shiny new unified badge this week—a first in 161 years—but the agency still hasn't answered for the catastrophic security failures that nearly cost a former president his life.

According to a watchdog report cited by Fox News, the Secret Service missed 102 radio transmissions about Thomas Crooks on a roof with a long gun before shots were fired at the Butler rally. That's not a minor oversight. That's a systemic breakdown in the most basic duty of presidential protection.

Meanwhile, the agency is debuting a redesigned insignia badge manufactured by Blackinton, a Massachusetts firm, featuring a raised star and circular shield with blue highlights meant to "represent unity" (Washington Examiner). Republicans and conservatives argue the timing is tone-deaf: Americans don't need a prettier badge. They need accountability, leadership overhaul, and proof the Secret Service can actually do its job.

The badge rollout feels like bureaucratic theater while serious questions remain unanswered. How did 102 radio transmissions get missed? Who failed to act? What systemic changes will prevent the next failure?

Coloradans and taxpayers nationwide should watch whether Congress demands real reforms—or settles for a new logo and business as usual.
