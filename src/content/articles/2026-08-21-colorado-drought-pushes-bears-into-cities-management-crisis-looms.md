# Colorado Bears Invading Cities as Drought Worsens—Wildlife Management Faces Test

Prolonged drought is forcing Colorado's black bear population into urban areas at an alarming rate, creating dangerous encounters in kitchens, cars, and college campuses across the state (CPR News). The crisis exposes a hard reality: when government environmental policies prioritize wilderness restrictions over active forest management and water infrastructure, wildlife and residents both suffer.

Bears desperate for food are no longer staying in the backcountry. They're ransacking suburban homes, raiding college campuses, and forcing residents to live behind bear-proof barriers in their own neighborhoods. This isn't a wildlife success story—it's a management failure.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans and wildlife experts argue that Colorado's overly restrictive land-use policies and underinvestment in water infrastructure have worsened drought conditions. Thinning forests to reduce fire risk and improve water retention, along with sensible dam and reservoir projects, could ease both the drought and wildlife pressure. Instead, progressive environmental groups have blocked or delayed such projects for years.

Colorado Parks and Wildlife faces mounting pressure to manage the crisis, but without addressing the root causes—water scarcity and forest density—the problem will only intensify.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado's legislature will finally approve water infrastructure and forest management projects, or continue blocking them in deference to environmental activists.
