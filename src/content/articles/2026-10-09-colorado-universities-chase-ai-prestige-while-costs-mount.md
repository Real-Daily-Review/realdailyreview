# Colorado Universities Chase AI Prestige While Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Colorado's higher-education establishment is racing to rebrand itself as "AI-forward," with six state colleges and universities earning a new "AI Engaged Campus Designation" from unnamed state officials (CPR News). The move signals yet another expensive pivot toward trendy technology—one that will inevitably require new spending, new hires, and new administrative overhead.

Conservatives should ask hard questions: Who's paying for this? What's the actual return on investment for Colorado taxpayers? And why does higher education keep chasing shiny new initiatives instead of controlling costs and improving graduation rates?

The designation itself—while presented as an honor—represents the kind of credential inflation that plagues academia. Universities compete for prestige rankings and state funding by adopting whatever buzzword dominates the moment. Yesterday it was "sustainability." Today it's artificial intelligence. Tomorrow, who knows?

Colorado's universities already struggle with affordability and bloated administrative costs. Before celebrating AI initiatives, legislators and trustees should demand transparency: How much will this cost? Will tuition rise? Will it actually benefit students, or just pad résumés in the provost's office?

**Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand a cost-benefit analysis before throwing more taxpayer money at higher-ed's latest fad.**
