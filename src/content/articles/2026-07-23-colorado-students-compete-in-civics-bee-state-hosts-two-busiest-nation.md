# Colorado Students Compete in Civics Bee as State Draws Record Park Traffic

Colorado's top middle schoolers will compete Friday in the National Civics Bee State Finals, a competition that tests knowledge of American government and constitutional principles—skills increasingly rare in public education. The event streams live Friday and airs Saturday on KTVD Channel 20 and 9NEWS.

Meanwhile, a new study confirms Colorado's outsized draw for outdoor recreation: the state is home to two of the nation's busiest national parks, underscoring the economic and infrastructure pressures on Colorado's public lands and gateway communities.

The civics competition reflects growing concern among conservatives about civics literacy in schools. Republicans have long argued that progressive curricula prioritize ideology over foundational American history and constitutional knowledge. The National Civics Bee offers a refreshing counterweight—emphasizing the Constitution, separation of powers, and civic responsibility rather than contemporary political grievances.

The national parks finding highlights another conservative concern: federal land management and the strain of overuse on Colorado ecosystems and local resources. While parks drive tourism dollars, conservatives argue that proper stewardship requires adequate funding, maintenance, and sometimes limits on access—not just federal control without accountability.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado's education system will expand civics requirements and whether state leaders will push the federal government for better park management and local revenue-sharing.
