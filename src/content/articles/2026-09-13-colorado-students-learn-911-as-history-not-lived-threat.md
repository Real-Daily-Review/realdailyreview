# A Generation Removed From 9/11 Faces a Threat It Never Knew

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, a generation of Colorado students born after 2001 now encounters the lessons of that day as history rather than lived trauma—even as conservative analysts warn that ideological threats persist unchecked.

CPR News reports that Denver-area schools are grappling with how to teach 9/11 to students with no memory of the event. The attacks that once unified the nation and defined national security policy have receded into textbooks, leaving younger Coloradans to understand counterterrorism and homeland defense as abstract concepts rather than urgent imperatives.

But National Review argues the real danger isn't historical distance—it's complacency. The outlet contends that "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still," suggesting that while Americans have moved past the immediate terror of 2001, the ideological and geopolitical threats that motivated those attacks remain active and underestimated.

For Colorado conservatives, the stakes are clear: a generation unprepared by direct experience may lack the vigilance necessary to recognize and resist ongoing threats to American security. As schools decide how to frame 9/11 for students who never knew a pre-attack America, the question becomes whether education will emphasize the enduring nature of the threat or treat it as a closed historical chapter.

Watch whether Colorado educators adopt curricula that honestly assess current terrorism threats or sanitize the lesson into feel-good multiculturalism.
