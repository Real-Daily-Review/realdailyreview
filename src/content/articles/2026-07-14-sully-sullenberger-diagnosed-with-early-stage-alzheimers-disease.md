# American Hero Sully Sullenberger Diagnosed With Early-Stage Alzheimer's

Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger, the retired airline captain who safely landed a crippled U.S. Airways jet on the Hudson River in 2009 and saved 155 lives, announced Tuesday he has been diagnosed with early-stage Alzheimer's disease.

The 75-year-old revealed the diagnosis in a statement on his website, noting that "for now, this means a name may not come easily to me, I forget a [lot]," according to reporting from *The Daily Wire* and the *Washington Examiner*. Sullenberger's calm professionalism during the January 2009 emergency landing—when both engines failed shortly after takeoff from LaGuardia—became a defining moment of American resilience and earned him the nickname "Hero of the Hudson."

The diagnosis marks a sobering turn for a man whose steady hand and quick thinking under catastrophic pressure saved an entire aircraft. Sullenberger has remained active in public life since his retirement, speaking on aviation safety and other matters of national importance.

His announcement comes as Alzheimer's disease continues to affect millions of Americans, raising questions about healthcare resources and support systems for aging citizens and their families.
