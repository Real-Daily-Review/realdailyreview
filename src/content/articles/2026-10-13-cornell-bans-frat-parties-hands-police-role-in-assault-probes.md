# Cornell Suspends Frat Parties After Sexual Assault Case Resurfaces

Cornell University has banned on-campus fraternity parties for the rest of the academic year and handed state police a role in investigating campus assault reports—a move that raises questions about whether administrative overreach or genuine safety concerns should drive campus policy.

The suspension follows a resurfaced 2024 sexual assault case in which a woman filed a Jane Doe civil suit against unnamed defendants last month (Washington Examiner). Cornell President Michael Kotlikoff framed the ban as temporary, saying the university needs time to decide "whether and how" fraternities can "safely" continue (Washington Examiner).

The decision reflects a broader pattern: when high-profile incidents occur, universities often resort to blanket restrictions rather than targeted accountability. Conservatives argue that collective punishment of student organizations—banning all fraternity parties rather than addressing specific perpetrators—punishes the innocent and expands administrative authority over student life. Involving state police in campus investigations, meanwhile, could represent either appropriate accountability or mission creep, depending on whether campus bureaucracies have proven themselves unable or unwilling to handle serious crimes.

The real test: whether Cornell uses this pause to reform its actual investigative processes or simply doubles down on restrictions that treat all fraternities as guilty until proven safe.
