# Colorado's Primary Problem: Unaffiliateds and Progressive Money Reshaping State Elections

Colorado's primary system is increasingly deciding general elections—and unaffiliated voters are sitting them out while progressive dark money floods Democratic races unchecked.

According to Complete Colorado, party-registered voters who skip caucuses are limiting their own options under current rules, yet Colorado's growing unaffiliated population has little say in which candidates advance. Meanwhile, the Denver Post reports that progressive statehouse candidates raced to victory in metro Denver Democratic primaries despite—or perhaps because of—millions in dark-money opposition spending that failed to stop them.

The pattern is clear: Democrats are using primary contests to nominate increasingly left-wing candidates, then coasting to general-election wins in safely blue districts. Republicans and conservatives argue this represents a fundamental problem with Colorado's caucus-and-primary hybrid system, which effectively hands candidate selection to the most ideologically committed activists while excluding unaffiliated voters and discouraging moderate participation.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this means the statehouse is drifting further left on taxes, regulation, and energy policy—with little electoral accountability until November, when many races are already decided.

Watch whether Republicans and reform-minded Coloradans push for primary-system changes that would give unaffiliated voters a real voice before the 2028 cycle.
