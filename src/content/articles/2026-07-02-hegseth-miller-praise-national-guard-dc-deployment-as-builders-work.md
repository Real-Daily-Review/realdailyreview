# National Guard Praised for DC Deployment; Miller Touts 'Builders' Over 'Destroyers'

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and White House official Stephen Miller visited Washington's Meridian Hill Park Thursday to honor National Guard troops deployed to the nation's capital, signaling the administration's commitment to restoring order in federal spaces. Miller told approximately 250 troops that "civilization only exists because of people like you," framing the deployment as essential work by "builders" rather than "destroyers" (Daily Caller).

Hegseth praised the Guard's mission despite what he called "ingrate" protesters and brutal heat conditions, according to the Washington Examiner. The visit underscores the administration's messaging that National Guard deployment represents stability and civic responsibility—a sharp contrast to progressive critics who have characterized such deployments as militarization of civilian spaces.

The Colorado angle: While the deployment centers on DC, it reflects broader national security priorities that affect how federal resources are allocated and how the administration views civil order. Colorado's National Guard units could face similar deployment requests if domestic unrest spreads westward.

Republicans frame the Guard's work as essential infrastructure maintenance and public safety. Democrats and progressive groups argue such deployments risk normalizing military presence in civilian areas and suppress legitimate protest.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether this deployment model expands to other cities or becomes a template for federal intervention in state capitals.
