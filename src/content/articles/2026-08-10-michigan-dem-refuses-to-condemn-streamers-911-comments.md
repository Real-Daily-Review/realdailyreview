# Michigan Democrat's Radical Ties Raise Red Flags for Colorado Conservatives

Michigan's Democratic Senate nominee Abdul el Sayed's refusal to distance himself from a far-left streamer who defended 9/11 should alarm anyone concerned about the left's direction—including Colorado voters watching their own party drift further left.

El Sayed, described by The Daily Wire as "socialist-aligned," appeared on NBC's "Meet the Press" and dodged questions about disavowing Hasan Piker's inflammatory past remarks suggesting America deserved the 9/11 attacks. Rather than condemn Piker's extremism, el Sayed shrugged it off—despite repeatedly campaigning alongside the Twitch personality.

Washington Examiner senior editor Peter Laffin defended Republican scrutiny of the relationship, arguing it's entirely legitimate to question el Sayed's judgment. "It isn't Islamophobia to say that Hasan Piker, the live streamer," raises serious concerns, Laffin wrote, pushing back against Democratic attempts to weaponize identity politics to shield their candidate from accountability.

This pattern—radical allies, evasive responses, socialist positioning—mirrors the ideological capture Colorado Democrats have undergone. From energy policy to gun control to spending priorities, the state party increasingly echoes the far-left fringe rather than mainstream Colorado values.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic establishment distances itself from el Sayed's approach or doubles down on defending radical allies as "misunderstood."
