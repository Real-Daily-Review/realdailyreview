# Energy Crisis Averted—But U.S. Reserves Are Running on Empty

A Middle East ceasefire is holding back the worst-case energy scenario, but America's strategic cushion is nearly gone.

Double blockades on the Strait of Hormuz created the world's largest energy supply disruption in years, yet the U.S. avoided the predicted global crisis—thanks largely to robust strategic petroleum reserves and domestic fracking, according to *Just The News*. However, those reserves have been "severely depleted" in the process.

The temporary reprieve masks a hard reality: American energy independence remains fragile. Conservatives argue that reliance on Middle East stability and drawn-down reserves underscores why the Biden-Harris administration's war on domestic oil and natural gas production was reckless. Colorado's energy sector—which supports thousands of jobs and state revenue—depends on policies that encourage, not obstruct, American energy development.

The ceasefire between the U.S. and Iran is "under threat," *9News Denver* reports, with fighting persisting in Lebanon. If hostilities resume and blockades return, the U.S. will lack the strategic buffer that just saved the global economy from shock.

Taxpayers and energy workers should watch whether the incoming administration reverses course on domestic energy production—or whether America remains vulnerable to the next disruption.
