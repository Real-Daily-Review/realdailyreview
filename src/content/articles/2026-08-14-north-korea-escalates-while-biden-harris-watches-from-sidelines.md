# North Korea's Escalation Exposes Biden-Harris Weakness on Foreign Policy

The Biden-Harris administration's passivity on North Korea has emboldened Pyongyang to deepen military ties with Russia while American attention drifts elsewhere. According to National Review, North Korea is "further entrenching itself in Russia's war," with significant troop commitments now underway in Europe. The Washington Examiner notes that repeated missile launches—most recently on August 6—have become so routine that South Korean society no longer treats them as urgent threats, a dangerous normalization born of perceived American indifference.

This matters for Colorado. While foreign policy may seem distant, weakness abroad invites adversaries to test American resolve, potentially drawing the U.S. into costlier conflicts later. Defense contractors and energy security tied to geopolitical stability affect Colorado's economy and jobs.

Republicans argue the administration squandered opportunities to contain North Korea through strength and clear deterrence. Instead, serial provocations now go largely unheeded—a pattern that undermines U.S. credibility with allies like South Korea and Japan while signaling to China and Russia that aggression carries minimal cost.

Conservatives should watch whether the next administration reverses this posture with concrete deterrence measures and whether Colorado's defense and aerospace sectors benefit from renewed strategic focus on Indo-Pacific security.
