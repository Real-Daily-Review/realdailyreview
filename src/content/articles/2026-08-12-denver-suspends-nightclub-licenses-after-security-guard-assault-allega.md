# Denver Nightclub Licenses Suspended After Security Guard Assault Allegations

Denver's Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection has suspended the liquor and cabaret licenses of Olympus Discotheque following serious allegations against a security guard, according to reporting from KDVR Fox31 Denver and 9News Denver.

The guard stands accused of sexually assaulting an underage customer at gunpoint, prompting swift action by city authorities. The suspension represents standard regulatory enforcement when venues fail to maintain adequate safety protocols and vet their security personnel properly.

The case underscores a critical point conservatives emphasize: private businesses bear direct responsibility for protecting patrons and enforcing their own standards. When venues cut corners on security screening or fail to monitor staff conduct, they face consequences—as they should. Market forces and licensing enforcement work together here; customers vote with their feet, and regulators step in when public safety is genuinely threatened.

Denver's response demonstrates that government licensing can serve a legitimate protective function, though conservatives remain skeptical of regulatory mission creep beyond core safety issues. The real question is whether the city's licensing regime focuses narrowly on genuine public safety or expands into social engineering.

Watch whether the city uses this incident to justify broader regulatory expansion into nightlife operations, or whether it stays focused on the specific security failures that enabled this alleged crime.
