# Corporate Cartels Are Squeezing Colorado Doctors—and Your Healthcare Choices

Colorado's independent physicians are disappearing into massive insurance and hospital conglomerates, stripping patients of real choice and doctors of autonomy.

According to the Washington Examiner, the illusion of doctor-patient decision-making has collapsed. Corporate bean-counters thousands of miles away—not the physician in the white coat—now dictate treatment decisions at local clinics. This vertical integration of insurance, hospital systems, and provider networks creates what conservatives describe as a corporate cartel that prioritizes profit margins over patient care.

For Colorado, this trend threatens the state's remaining independent practices and rural healthcare access. When decisions flow through distant corporate hierarchies rather than local doctors who know their patients, care quality suffers and costs rise. Conservatives argue this consolidation is the predictable result of decades of government regulation that favored large, compliant systems over small practitioners.

Republicans point out that federal healthcare mandates and insurance regulations have made it nearly impossible for independent doctors to compete. Rather than fix the regulatory burden, progressives have allowed—even encouraged—consolidation under the guise of "efficiency." The result: less competition, fewer choices, higher costs, and doctors who answer to corporate overlords instead of patients.

Colorado taxpayers and patients should watch whether state legislators will push back against further consolidation or continue enabling the corporate takeover of medicine.
