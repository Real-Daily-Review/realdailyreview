# Texas Bible Curriculum Move Reignites Church-State Clash—Colorado Parents Should Pay Attention

Texas just made Bible stories required reading in public schools, and the decision exposes a widening divide over what belongs in American classrooms.

The Texas State Board of Education approved the curriculum change Friday, making biblical passages mandatory material for students across a state educating roughly one in 10 American schoolchildren. The Washington Examiner notes that legal precedent and state law permit the move, despite critics' First Amendment objections.

**Here's the conservative case:** Supporters argue that Bible literacy is essential to understanding Western literature, history, and law. The Examiner frames it as a reasonable educational standard, not religious imposition. Republicans counter that secular curricula have systematically stripped Judeo-Christian foundations from schools while promoting progressive ideology unchallenged.

**The left's objection:** Critics claim mandatory Bible instruction violates church-state separation and pressures students of non-Christian faiths. They argue public schools should remain neutral on religion.

**The Colorado angle:** This matters here. As progressive school boards in Denver and Boulder push gender ideology and DEI curricula without parental consent, conservatives ask: why is Bible literacy deemed unconstitutional while activist curricula face minimal scrutiny? Colorado parents should watch whether similar curriculum battles emerge locally—and whether courts apply the same First Amendment standards consistently.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic-controlled legislature attempts to preempt similar moves through state law.
