# Colorado School Districts Push $177M in Tax Hikes While Dodging Accountability

Colorado school districts are asking taxpayers to foot a $177 million bill even as enrollment shrinks and spending priorities raise red flags among conservatives.

Jefferson County Schools is seeking a $133 million tax increase (Complete Colorado), while Denver Public Schools wants voters to approve a $44 million mill levy override in November. The Denver Post reports DPS plans to spend $25.8 million of that windfall on employee raises—a priority that troubles fiscal hawks who question whether districts are managing existing budgets responsibly.

The timing is particularly galling in Jeffco's case. The district is currently in federal litigation over its transgender-student policies, yet school leaders are asking voters for more money rather than demonstrating fiscal discipline or policy clarity. Republicans argue that before asking taxpayers for additional revenue, districts should prove they're spending current dollars wisely and addressing legitimate community concerns about curriculum and governance.

Conservatives counter that Colorado families are already stretched thin by inflation and property taxes. They question whether these districts have exhausted efficiency measures, cut administrative overhead, or prioritized classroom instruction over bureaucratic expansion.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado voters approve these levies and whether school boards face accountability for how they spend the new revenue.
