# Colorado's Data Centers: The Infrastructure Backbone Powering the Digital Economy

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are critical infrastructure that powers modern life—not a threat to it, according to industry advocates and conservatives pushing back against anti-development sentiment.

Data centers represent a massive economic engine for the state, supporting high-wage jobs and attracting tech investment while using Colorado's natural advantages: reliable power grids, cool climate, and geographic diversity. Yet progressive activists and some local governments have increasingly targeted these facilities with restrictive zoning, environmental complaints, and energy-use scrutiny.

Republicans and business leaders argue that blocking or heavily regulating data centers would cripple Colorado's competitive edge in the digital economy and cost the state thousands of jobs and tax revenue. The facilities are essential infrastructure—as vital to modern commerce as highways or power plants—not luxury amenities.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that data centers consume enormous amounts of electricity and water, straining Colorado's already-stressed resources and contributing to climate concerns. They demand stricter environmental reviews and local control over siting decisions.

The real issue: Colorado taxpayers and workers benefit from a thriving data center sector, but progressive regulation threatens to push that investment to friendlier states like Texas or Arizona.
