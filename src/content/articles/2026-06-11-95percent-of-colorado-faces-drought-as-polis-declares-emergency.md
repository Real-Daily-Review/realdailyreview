# Colorado's Drought Crisis Deepens as 95% of State Faces Water Emergency

Governor Jared Polis declared a statewide drought emergency last week as conditions deteriorated sharply across Colorado. According to the National Integrated Drought Information System, nearly 95% of the state now faces drought conditions (KDVR), marking a significant setback in water availability that threatens ranching operations, agricultural productivity, and municipal water supplies.

The drought emergency underscores a critical reality for Colorado: water scarcity is not a distant problem—it's here. Ranching families across the state are praying for rain as livestock operations face mounting pressure from depleted water sources and parched pastures (CPR News). For Colorado's agricultural economy and rural communities, this is an existential challenge.

Conservatives and taxpayers should scrutinize how state government responds. While drought is a natural phenomenon, the question becomes whether Polis's administration will resist the urge to impose new regulations or water-use mandates that punish productive landowners and ranchers. Colorado's water challenges demand market-based solutions and cooperation with the federal government—not bureaucratic overreach that strangles agriculture and energy development.

Watch whether the state uses this emergency to justify expanded government control over water rights or to push through progressive environmental policies that further restrict Colorado's water and energy sectors.
