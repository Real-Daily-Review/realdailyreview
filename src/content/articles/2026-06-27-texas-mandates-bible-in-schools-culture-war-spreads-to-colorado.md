# Texas Bible Curriculum Sparks National Education Culture War—Colorado Should Pay Attention

Texas just became the first state in recent memory to mandate Bible stories in K-12 public school curricula, and the move is already igniting a fierce national debate over what belongs in classrooms.

The Texas State Board of Education, controlled by Republicans, voted Friday to approve the reading list. Supporters argue the Bible is foundational to Western literature, history, and law—and that teaching it doesn't violate the First Amendment. The *Washington Examiner* notes legal precedent supports Texas's authority to include biblical texts as literature and historical documents.

Critics immediately cried foul, claiming the mandate violates church-state separation. The left frames it as religious imposition in public schools.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's education establishment leans progressive, and this Texas move will likely fuel demands here to resist similar "religious curriculum" initiatives—even as conservatives argue that understanding biblical literacy is essential to understanding American founding documents, literature, and law. Expect Colorado Democrats to use Texas as a cautionary tale to block any similar proposals.

Republicans counter that secular progressives have already infused ideology into curricula through DEI programs and gender-focused materials; Bible literacy is simply intellectual balance.

Watch whether Colorado's State Board of Education faces pressure to take an explicit stance against Bible-in-schools policies—and whether conservative board members push back.
