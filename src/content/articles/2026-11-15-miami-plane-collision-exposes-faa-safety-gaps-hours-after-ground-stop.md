# Miami Tarmac Collision Raises Fresh Questions About FAA Safety Systems

A UPS cargo plane and Copa Airlines passenger jet collided on the tarmac at Miami International Airport Saturday night, injuring two people, raising concerns about airport safety protocols just hours after a nationwide ground stop crippled U.S. aviation.

The collision occurred around 9:15 p.m. while both aircraft were taxiing, according to the Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire. The incident came less than 24 hours after a telecommunications failure forced the FAA to ground all U.S. flights—a stark reminder of how dependent modern aviation is on aging infrastructure and centralized federal systems.

The FAA is investigating. Two people suffered minor injuries.

For Colorado travelers and businesses relying on air cargo and passenger service, the back-to-back disruptions underscore a troubling pattern: the federal government's monopoly control over airspace and safety systems leaves little room for redundancy or private-sector alternatives. When the FAA's systems fail—whether through technical glitches or operational lapses—there's nowhere else to turn.

Conservatives argue that over-regulation and federal bloat have left the aviation system vulnerable. The question now is whether the FAA will tighten ground-control procedures or whether Colorado's traveling public and freight operators will face more disruptions from a system that lacks competitive pressure to innovate.
