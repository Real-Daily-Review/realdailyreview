# AOC's 2028 Ambitions Face Steep Odds as GOP Eyes Comeback

Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez's potential 2028 presidential run confronts serious headwinds despite Democratic gains this election cycle, according to National Review, while Republicans are already positioning for their own comeback contest.

The left has been emboldened by recent electoral victories, but conservatives note that AOC—a self-described democratic socialist from New York—carries significant baggage in a general election. National Review points out the structural challenges facing any far-left candidate in a presidential race, where swing voters and working-class Democrats have repeatedly rejected radical economic agendas.

Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner reports that Republicans are treating the midterm results not as a Democratic mandate but as the opening bell for 2028. GOP strategists argue the scale of Democratic underperformance in key races signals voter fatigue with progressive overreach on spending, inflation, and regulatory expansion—issues that resonate across Colorado's energy sector, ranching communities, and small-business corridors.

For Colorado conservatives, the takeaway is clear: Democrats are doubling down on their most ideological voices while Republicans consolidate around candidates offering fiscal restraint and market-friendly policies. The question isn't whether AOC can win—it's whether the Democratic Party will nominate someone even further left.

Watch whether Colorado's moderate Democrats distance themselves from national progressive figures ahead of 2028, or embrace the party's leftward tilt on taxes and energy policy.
