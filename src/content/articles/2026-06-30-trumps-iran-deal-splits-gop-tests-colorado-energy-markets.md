# Trump's Iran Deal Splits GOP, Leaves Colorado Energy Sector Watching

Trump's proposed Iran memorandum of understanding has fractured his evangelical base and raised questions about Middle East strategy—but Colorado's oil and gas industry is paying close attention to what sanctions relief could mean for global energy markets.

Evangelical leaders remain divided (Fox News reports), with some backing military posturing while others worry Israel faces abandonment. Meanwhile, conservative analysts argue the deal merits a longer view. A Washington Examiner op-ed frames the memorandum as an "economic development strategy" that could stabilize regional markets and benefit U.S. interests long-term—a counterweight to concerns the administration is moving too fast on normalization.

Republicans broadly support Trump's foreign policy instincts but warn against repeating Obama-era mistakes on Iran verification and enforcement. The core conservative argument: any deal must guarantee inspections, sunset clauses, and ironclad consequences for violations.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. Sanctions relief could flood global oil markets, pressuring domestic producers already navigating regulatory headwinds under Biden-era restrictions. Energy workers and shareholders have skin in the game—cheaper foreign crude undercuts Colorado's competitive advantage.

Watch whether the Trump administration ties sanctions relief to concrete concessions on nuclear capability and regional proxy activity, or whether political pressure from the faith coalition forces a harder line.
