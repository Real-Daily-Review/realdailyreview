# Federal Wealth Tax Would Hand IRS Sweeping Power Over Private Assets

A proposed federal wealth tax isn't primarily a revenue grab—it's a blueprint for unprecedented government intrusion into Americans' personal finances, conservatives warn.

The debate centers on IRS authority, not just tax rates. Under a wealth tax regime, the federal government would gain expansive power to scrutinize, value, and challenge what citizens own, according to analysis in the *Washington Examiner*. This represents a fundamental shift in the relationship between taxpayers and the tax agency.

Conservatives argue the real danger lies in the mechanism: the IRS would need to assess the value of illiquid assets—private businesses, real estate, art, family farms—annually. That opens the door to arbitrary valuations, endless audits, and forced asset sales to cover tax bills. Colorado ranchers and small-business owners would face particular exposure, as family operations often tie up wealth in land and equipment that's difficult to value and impossible to liquidate quickly without destroying the business.

The left frames wealth taxes as fairness measures targeting the ultra-rich. Republicans counter that such schemes inevitably expand downward, ensnare the middle class, and weaponize the IRS as a tool of political control rather than revenue collection.

Watch whether Congress moves to introduce wealth-tax legislation and how Colorado's delegation responds—especially given the state's energy sector and agricultural interests.
