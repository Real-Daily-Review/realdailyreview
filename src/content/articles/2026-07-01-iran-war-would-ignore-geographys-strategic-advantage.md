# U.S. Military Overreach in Iran Would Ignore Hard Geographic Reality

A potential U.S. war against Iran to secure Persian Gulf oil and gas would ignore a fundamental strategic truth: geography beats military hardware every time.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, Iran's mountainous terrain dominates the northern Persian Gulf shore, including the critical Strait of Hormuz chokepoint. That geographic advantage means any American military campaign would face severe logistical constraints and vulnerability to Iranian counterattacks—regardless of U.S. firepower superiority.

For Colorado, this matters directly. Energy independence and stable global oil markets affect gas prices at the pump and heating costs for families and ranchers across the state. A prolonged conflict in the Persian Gulf would disrupt energy supplies and spike prices, hitting Colorado's working families and small businesses hardest.

Conservatives argue that military interventions driven by resource control—rather than direct national security threats—often backfire strategically and drain the Treasury. The lesson: before committing American lives and dollars abroad, policymakers must reckon with terrain, logistics, and the limits of military power.

Watch whether Congress demands a serious cost-benefit analysis before any Iran escalation reaches a vote.
