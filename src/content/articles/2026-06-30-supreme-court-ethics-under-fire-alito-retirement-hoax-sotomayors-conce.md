# Supreme Court Ethics Questions Mount as Justices' Financial Disclosures Reveal Lavish Gifts

A pair of recent Supreme Court controversies is raising fresh questions about judicial ethics and transparency—issues that should concern Colorado taxpayers watching how the nation's highest court operates.

NPR accidentally published and then retracted a story Tuesday claiming Justice Samuel Alito was retiring, sparking confusion across political circles before the outlet corrected course (9News Denver). Meanwhile, newly released financial disclosures reveal Justice Sonia Sotomayor received over $4,000 in concert tickets from Bad Bunny's record label during a Puerto Rico trip, according to Fox News Politics and the Daily Caller.

Conservatives argue the concert-ticket disclosure raises legitimate ethics questions: Should Supreme Court justices accept high-value gifts from entertainment companies with potential interests before the Court? Republicans have long pushed for stricter judicial ethics rules and greater transparency, contending that even the appearance of impropriety undermines public confidence in the judiciary.

Democrats typically counter that justices operate under different ethics standards than lower courts and that isolated gifts don't constitute conflicts of interest.

The NPR retraction, meanwhile, highlights media credibility problems that conservatives have flagged repeatedly—sloppy fact-checking with national implications.

**Watch for:** Whether Congress moves to impose binding ethics codes on Supreme Court justices, a debate that could reshape judicial independence arguments.
