# GOP Foreign Policy Divide Threatens Party Unity as Trump Weighs In

Donald Trump's grip on the Republican Party faces its biggest test yet: a fundamental split over whether America should keep fighting endless wars abroad or pull back from global military entanglement.

Hawks within GOP ranks argue the U.S. must continue confronting adversaries by force to protect national interests and allies. Restrainers counter that decades of inconclusive wars have drained resources better spent at home—a position gaining traction among Trump's base.

According to The Daily Wire, Trump has emerged as the unlikely glue holding these warring factions together, at least for now. But the tension reveals deeper fractures in conservative thinking about America's role in the world.

The Denver Post notes that Trump's brand of "religious, conservative populism" has reshaped the party's identity, though the foreign policy schism suggests that coalition may not hold indefinitely. For Colorado Republicans—many of whom have military ties and defense industry jobs—this debate carries real stakes. Defense spending and military readiness directly affect Colorado's economy and workforce.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump can maintain party discipline on foreign policy heading into 2026, or whether restrainers gain enough ground to force a genuine reckoning over America's military commitments.
