# Tina Peters Visits Trump After Polis Commutation, Fueling Election Integrity Debate

Colorado's former Mesa County Clerk Tina Peters visited the White House this week to thank President Trump for her release, according to a Trump Truth Social post stating Peters "just came to the White House to thank me for getting her released from prison in Colorado" (9News Denver).

Peters was convicted in 2022 on charges related to her handling of election equipment and voting system access. Governor Jared Polis commuted her sentence last year—a move that sparked immediate backlash from Democrats and election security advocates who viewed her case as central to Colorado's 2020 election integrity concerns.

Republicans and conservatives have seized on the commutation inconsistency: Complete Colorado notes that if Democrats truly believed Polis' action represented a "moral collapse," they would have objected equally to his other commutations. The outlet frames the episode as emblematic of selective outrage from the left.

The Peters case remains a flashpoint in Colorado's ongoing election security debate. Conservatives argue her prosecution reflected legitimate concerns about election administration safeguards, while Democrats contend her actions undermined public confidence in voting systems.
