# Final Pre-Midterm Inflation Report Shows Prices Still Climbing for Colorado Families

Inflation ticked up to 3.7% year-over-year in September—the last consumer price index report before midterm elections—according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, signaling that kitchen-table costs remain a drag on voter sentiment heading into November (Washington Examiner, Daily Wire).

The month-to-month increase of 0.6% from August to September slightly exceeded analyst expectations of a 0.2-point bump. Gasoline prices surged 9.1% in September alone, a particular pinch for Colorado drivers and rural families already hammered by energy costs (Just The News).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that sustained inflation reflects the Biden administration's reckless spending agenda and regulatory overreach on energy production. Colorado energy workers and small-business owners have watched their purchasing power erode while Washington doubles down on policies that restrict domestic oil and gas development—the very sector that powers Colorado's economy and state budget.

Democrats counter that inflation is a global phenomenon tied to pandemic disruptions and supply-chain chaos, not domestic policy alone.

The timing is brutal for the party in power. Voters head to the polls in weeks still feeling the squeeze at the pump and grocery store—a reality no spin can erase.
