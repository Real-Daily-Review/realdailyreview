# Government Grocery Stores: Another Progressive Experiment Destined to Fail

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's plan to open government-owned grocery stores—complete with ID requirements for purchases—is the latest example of progressive overreach that will collapse under its own contradictions, conservatives argue.

Mamdani's scheme, pitched as a solution to food deserts and high prices, would create taxpayer-funded stores requiring identification at checkout. The irony is sharp: Democrats demand ID for nothing else, yet suddenly want "papers" for groceries. Former Los Angeles mayoral candidate Spencer Pratt called the plan "disingenuous" and highlighted the hypocrisy—demanding ID to vote is "bigotry," but demanding it to buy government-subsidized arugula is progressive policy (Washington Examiner).

National Review notes these stores are "likely to become hubs of disorder," suggesting Mamdani's model will struggle operationally before it ever threatens private bodegas or supermarkets.

**The Colorado angle:** While this targets New York, it signals how progressives nationwide view government's role in markets. Colorado's business community should watch whether similar proposals gain traction in Democratic-controlled legislatures—especially as inflation and cost-of-living complaints mount.

Conservatives say the real solution is removing regulatory barriers that drive up food costs, not replacing markets with government bureaucracy.
