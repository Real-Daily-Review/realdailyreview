# U.S. Climate and AI Restrictions Hand Competitive Edge to China

America's self-imposed regulatory constraints are handing Beijing a strategic advantage in energy, manufacturing, and artificial intelligence—while Colorado's energy workers and tech sector watch from the sidelines.

A coordinated legal and policy network is systematically constraining American energy production and industrial capacity in the name of climate advocacy, according to *National Review*. Meanwhile, the Biden administration's heavy-handed approach to AI regulation is backfiring spectacularly. When the U.S. government ordered Anthropic to suspend access to advanced AI models for foreign nationals in June, China's Zhipu responded days later by releasing GLM-5.2, an open-weight system anyone could download and deploy locally, the *Washington Examiner* reports.

The pattern is clear: Washington imposes restrictions on American companies and energy development under progressive policy mandates, while Beijing operates without such handicaps. Colorado's energy sector—already battered by federal overreach on oil and gas leasing—faces yet another headwind as domestic innovation stalls.

Republicans argue these restrictions cripple American competitiveness without slowing Chinese advancement one bit. The left counters that climate and national-security regulations are necessary, but offers no answer for why unilateral American restraint leaves Beijing stronger.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation will demand a strategic review of how federal climate and tech policy affects American workers and innovation.
