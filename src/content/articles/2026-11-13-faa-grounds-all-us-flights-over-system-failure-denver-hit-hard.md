# FAA Grounds All U.S. Flights Over System Failure—Colorado Travel Disrupted

A nationwide FAA ground stop Friday afternoon halted all departing flights across the United States, including from Denver International Airport, citing "extraordinary circumstances" and safety concerns. The Washington Examiner reported the halt stemmed from a "rolling frequency outage" affecting air traffic control facilities that monitor planes in flight, with two FAA employees confirming the disruption moments after the order was imposed.

The Federal Aviation Administration allowed inbound international flights to be managed on a case-by-case basis, but all departures remained grounded until further notice (9News Denver, New York Post). The outage disrupted travel for thousands of Colorado passengers and highlighted vulnerabilities in critical federal infrastructure.

**The conservative concern:** This incident exposes how government monopoly over air traffic control—a system that has resisted modernization for decades—leaves the entire economy hostage to federal system failures. Republicans have long argued for private-sector involvement and competitive alternatives in aviation infrastructure. The FAA's aging technology and bureaucratic resistance to innovation created a single point of failure affecting millions of Americans and billions in economic activity.

Conservatives should watch whether Congress demands accountability from FAA leadership and whether this crisis finally opens the door to NextGen modernization and private-sector competition in air traffic management.
