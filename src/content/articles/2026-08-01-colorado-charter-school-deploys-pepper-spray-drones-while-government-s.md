# Colorado Charter School Turns to Drones to Solve What Government Schools Won't: Protecting Kids

A Colorado charter school is taking school safety into its own hands—literally—by installing pepper-spraying drones to counter active shooter threats, a move that exposes the failure of government-run schools to adequately protect students.

John Adams Academy plans to deploy the drone system at a cost of about $200,000 over four years, making it the only school in Colorado using this technology (9News Denver). The charter school's decision underscores a hard truth: when government bureaucracies drag their feet on security upgrades, innovative private institutions step up.

Charter schools operate with leaner budgets and less red tape than traditional public districts, allowing them to experiment with unconventional solutions. While the pepper-spray drone approach is novel—and will certainly draw scrutiny from the usual suspects—it reflects the kind of creative problem-solving that thrives outside the government education monopoly.

Conservatives have long argued that school choice and charter school expansion give parents real alternatives when public systems fail to prioritize safety. This case proves the point: when accountability matters and bureaucratic inertia doesn't slow you down, schools find answers.

Watch whether Colorado's traditional public school districts adopt similar security innovations—or whether union-friendly administrators and state education officials continue blocking the kind of decisive action parents demand.
