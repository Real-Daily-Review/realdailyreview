# Colorado's Digital ID App Down Ahead of Holiday Travel—State Still Silent on Timeline

Colorado's myColorado digital ID app sits offline just days before peak Thanksgiving travel, leaving up to 150,000 residents potentially exposed after a cybersecurity incident the state has yet to fully explain. The outage compounds travel chaos already triggered by a massive coastal storm battering the East Coast, with over 550 flights delayed and 366 canceled as of Thursday morning.

The timing is brutal: Coloradans relying on the app for mobile driver's licenses and ID verification face uncertainty heading into one of the year's heaviest travel periods. State officials have offered no restoration timeline, raising questions about government IT infrastructure and data security protocols that conservatives have long flagged as vulnerabilities of centralized digital systems.

Republicans argue that over-reliance on government-managed digital platforms—especially for essential identity documents—creates unnecessary risk. They contend that traditional physical IDs remain the more reliable, privacy-respecting alternative and that state agencies consistently underestimate cybersecurity threats.

Democrats counter that digital services improve convenience and that isolated incidents don't justify abandoning modernization efforts.

The incident underscores a broader conservative concern: when government controls critical infrastructure, failures cascade directly onto taxpayers with no recourse.
