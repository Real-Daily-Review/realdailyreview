# Trump's Tariff Gambit Threatens Colorado Energy, Agriculture, and Auto Supply

President Trump's new 50% tariffs on Canadian imports risk hitting Colorado's economy where it matters most: energy, timber, and agricultural trade. The Washington Examiner reports that trade negotiations with Canada broke down last week, prompting Trump to impose steep levies on lumber, agricultural products, and consumer electronics—categories that directly affect Colorado ranchers, timber operators, and manufacturers dependent on cross-border supply chains.

Republicans backing the tariff strategy argue it protects American workers and forces better trade terms. But conservatives and business groups warn the policy could backfire. National Review calls the approach "absurd," and the Examiner notes Trump's populist economic doctrine faces a "reality check" as tariffs ripple through the Midwest auto industry and beyond.

For Colorado specifically, the tariffs threaten:
- **Agriculture**: Ranchers and grain producers relying on Canadian markets face retaliation
- **Energy**: Oil and gas operations dependent on Canadian equipment and cross-border logistics
- **Supply chains**: Manufacturers in Denver and the Front Range tied to North American production networks

The real test comes next: whether Trump negotiates a better deal or doubles down, and whether Colorado's export-dependent sectors absorb the pain or see relief.
