# Colorado Primary Season Heats Up as Unaffiliated Voters Reshape State Elections

Colorado's primary system is quietly handing power to unaffiliated voters—and that's a problem for party discipline, conservatives warn.

Six states held primaries on Aug. 11 (9News Denver), but Colorado's structural challenge runs deeper. The state's growing unaffiliated voter bloc is reshaping which candidates survive to November, according to Complete Colorado. Party-registered voters who skip caucuses effectively forfeit their say in candidate selection, leaving the field to those willing to show up—or to unaffiliated voters in general elections who face no primary constraints.

On the Republican side, Colorado's statewide primary winnowed 18 gubernatorial candidates to just two through the assembly process (Complete Colorado), illustrating how the caucus-to-primary pipeline filters candidates before general voters weigh in.

**The conservative concern:** When unaffiliated voters dominate, candidates chase the center rather than build durable party coalitions. Republicans who skip caucuses hand nomination power to the most engaged—or most ideologically driven—activists. That can produce nominees out of step with general-election Colorado, or it can empower candidates who don't answer to party principles.

Colorado taxpayers and gun owners should watch whether the 2026 cycle produces nominees willing to fight for limited government and Second Amendment protections, or whether primary fragmentation yields another round of moderate-to-left nominees in a purple state.
