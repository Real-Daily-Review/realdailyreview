# Front Range Rail Tax Faces Ballot Chaos and Conservative Backlash

Colorado voters in 13 counties are being asked to approve a new sales tax for the Front Range Passenger Rail project—but election officials bungled the ballot itself, printing Issue 7A for voters outside the rail district who shouldn't be voting on it at all (9News Denver).

The rail proposal would siphon billions from Colorado taxpayers to fund a train that conservatives argue few will actually ride. Complete Colorado laid out 10 reasons to reject the measure, framing it as a choice between "blowing billions on a train" or letting Coloradans keep their own money.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives oppose the tax hike on principle: it expands government spending on a project with questionable ridership projections and diverts sales-tax revenue that could stay in local economies or reduce the tax burden on families and small businesses. The ballot printing error compounds voter frustration—if election officials can't get the basics right, how can taxpayers trust them to oversee a multi-billion-dollar rail authority?

Democrats and rail advocates argue the project addresses Front Range congestion and positions Colorado for sustainable growth, though those arguments carry less weight with voters already skeptical of government transit boondoggles.

Watch whether Colorado election officials correct the ballot error before Election Day and whether the printing failure tanks voter confidence in Issue 7A.
