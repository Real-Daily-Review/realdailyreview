# California's Tax-Credit Gamble Won't Stop the Exodus—and Colorado Should Take Note

Governor Gavin Newsom just signed a scaled-down film and TV tax credit in California, admitting what conservatives have long argued: you can't bribe businesses to stay in a hostile regulatory and tax environment. The program launched "considerably smaller than supporters originally envisioned" (New York Post), a tacit acknowledgment that even Hollywood subsidies can't overcome California's punitive cost structure.

This matters for Colorado. While our state has attracted production companies fleeing California's chaos, we risk repeating the same mistake if we chase industry with targeted tax credits instead of broad, competitive tax policy. Newsom's half-measure proves the point: handpicked corporate welfare doesn't work. Lower overall taxes and lighter regulation do.

Separately, Newsom signed election bills creating new felony penalties for ballot seizure and restricting law enforcement near polling places (Washington Examiner). Republicans argue these measures are election theater—designed to preempt federal election oversight while leaving California's own ballot-access problems unaddressed.

Colorado conservatives should watch whether our legislature gets tempted by California-style corporate tax credits or election-security theater instead of pursuing genuine tax reform and transparent election administration.
