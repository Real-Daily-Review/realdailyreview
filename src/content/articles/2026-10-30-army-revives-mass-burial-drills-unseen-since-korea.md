# Army Revives Mass Burial Drills for First Time Since Korea—What's the Pentagon Planning?

The U.S. Army conducted a rare large-scale exercise at Fort Bragg testing mass burial capabilities not deployed since the Korean War, signaling Pentagon planners are preparing for a potential conflict involving catastrophic casualties on a scale Americans haven't faced in seven decades.

According to the Washington Examiner, the exercise "served as a critical test" of logistics and procedures the military has largely shelved since air superiority and modern medical evacuation reduced battlefield deaths in recent conflicts. The Daily Caller reports the Army is now "bringing rapid burial training at stomach-turning rate not seen since Korean War."

The drills reflect a strategic shift: military leadership appears to be gaming out peer-conflict scenarios—likely against China or Russia—where American technological advantages might not prevent mass casualties. This isn't routine training; it's contingency planning for a worst-case outcome.

**The Colorado angle:** Fort Carson and other Colorado military installations could be mobilized for similar exercises or surge operations. Coloradans should understand what battlefield assumptions the Pentagon is now operating under.

**What to watch:** Whether the Pentagon publicly explains the strategic rationale for these drills and whether Congress demands clarity on what conflict scenarios justify this level of casualty preparation.
