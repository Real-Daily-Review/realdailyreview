# Colorado Woman Sues Over Warrantless Flock Camera Tracking by Police

A Denver woman is challenging law enforcement's use of automated license plate readers without a warrant—a case that could reshape how Colorado police deploy surveillance technology. Chrisanna Elser alleges Columbine Valley police violated her constitutional rights when a sergeant cited Flock Safety camera footage to falsely accuse her of stealing a $25 package from a porch, according to the Colorado Sun.

The lawsuit centers on a core conservative principle: government power must be constrained by the Fourth Amendment. Elser was never charged, but the warrantless tracking raised red flags about how police departments are deploying surveillance infrastructure without judicial oversight or probable cause.

Conservatives have long warned that expanding government surveillance—even in the name of public safety—erodes individual liberty. This case illustrates the danger: automated systems can generate false leads, damage reputations, and chill lawful behavior. Colorado taxpayers should ask hard questions about which police departments are using Flock cameras, under what policies, and whether warrants are actually required before officers access footage.

The broader issue: as technology outpaces law, Colorado legislators and local officials must decide whether to rein in warrantless tracking or let surveillance expand unchecked.
