# Trump's Burden-Sharing Push Raises Stakes for U.S. Energy Independence

President Trump is demanding allies pay more for their own defense—but conservatives warn against trading away American energy strength to sweeten the deal.

The Pentagon's recent $22.9 billion Tomahawk production contract and the Navy's redeployment of the USS George Washington underscore the real cost of maintaining global security commitments. Trump's push for burden-sharing with South Korea and other allies is sound policy, conservatives argue—wealthy nations should fund their own defense rather than rely on American taxpayers to subsidize their security.

But here's the catch: the Washington Examiner warns that cutting American domestic energy production is "the wrong currency" for these negotiations. As Trump negotiates with allies, policymakers must resist pressure to sacrifice Colorado's oil and gas industry or restrict federal leasing on Western lands as a bargaining chip.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. Energy development funds schools, roads, and local economies across the state. Surrendering domestic drilling capacity to appease international partners would weaken American leverage, increase dependence on hostile regimes, and gut Colorado's energy sector—all while making the U.S. less secure.

**Watch for:** whether Trump's trade negotiations include any concessions on federal energy leasing or domestic drilling permits that could harm Colorado's economy.
