# British Labour's New PM Shows Why Big Government Fails

Andy Burnham has become Britain's seventh prime minister in a decade—a cautionary tale for Colorado voters watching progressive governance implode.

Burnham, the former Greater Manchester mayor, replaced Keir Starmer after Starmer's resignation Monday (Just The News). The rapid turnover reflects Labour's inability to deliver on its promises, a pattern conservatives say mirrors what happens when left-wing governments prioritize ideology over results.

National Review warns that Burnham brings "inexplicable nostalgia for the business-starving policies of the 1970s"—the era of stagflation, union militancy, and economic stagnation that crippled Britain for years. His track record as Manchester mayor offers little comfort: the city saw continued decline despite heavy government intervention and spending.

The broader lesson: when governments expand their reach into business, energy, and the economy, instability follows. Britain's seven prime ministers in ten years reflects the chaos of perpetual policy reversals and failed progressive experiments. Colorado's own struggles with regulatory overreach, energy policy confusion, and business-unfriendly governance echo similar patterns.

Conservatives argue Burnham's ascent proves that redistributionist economics and heavy-handed regulation don't solve problems—they create them, destroying investor confidence and economic growth.

Watch whether Burnham's government doubles down on anti-business policies or pivots toward pro-growth reform—and whether Colorado's Democratic leadership takes notes or learns the lesson.
