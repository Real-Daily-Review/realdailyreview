# Colorado Families Crushed by Soaring Electric Bills as Regulators Scramble

Colorado households are abandoning their power at alarming rates, with utility disconnects spiking 40 percent as families fall behind on bills averaging $750 in arrears.

Energy Outreach Colorado is now burning through roughly $1 million weekly just to keep the lights on for struggling residents—a staggering subsidy that masks the real problem: runaway electricity costs that regulators have failed to control. The Colorado Public Utilities Commission has launched a probe into the disconnects, but critics argue the agency should be examining why rates have climbed so steeply in the first place.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives point to years of aggressive renewable energy mandates and grid modernization costs that utilities have passed directly to ratepayers. Colorado's push toward 100 percent clean electricity by 2040 requires massive infrastructure spending—expenses that don't disappear; they simply get billed to families already struggling with inflation.

The Left frames rising disconnects as a poverty crisis requiring more government aid. Conservatives counter that the real solution is reining in regulatory costs that drive rates up, not expanding welfare programs that treat the symptom while ignoring the disease.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether the PUC addresses the root cause—excessive regulation and mandates—or simply recommends more ratepayer-funded assistance programs.
