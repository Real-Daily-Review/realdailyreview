# California's Waymo Ban Exposes Self-Driving Hype—Colorado Should Pump the Brakes on Regulation

California regulators have banned Waymo's driverless cars from freeways after multiple close calls in construction zones, forcing the company to pause freeway operations nationwide (Breitbart). The move reveals what skeptics have long warned: autonomous vehicle technology remains unproven in real-world conditions, and government mandates won't fix engineering failures.

Meanwhile, Denver city leaders are demanding more regulatory authority over self-driving cars, claiming state law handcuffs their ability to enforce rules after incidents (KDVR Fox31 Denver). This is where conservatives should draw a line.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Colorado doesn't need another layer of municipal regulation stacked atop state oversight. Denver's frustration reflects a deeper problem—government agencies chasing solutions to problems the private sector should solve through liability and market discipline. If Waymo's cars are unsafe, customers won't use them, and the company faces lawsuits. That's accountability.

Adding Denver's regulatory apparatus won't make the technology safer; it will slow innovation and create compliance costs that benefit established players while crushing startups. Colorado's economy thrives when businesses can experiment and fail without bureaucratic permission slips.

**Watch whether Colorado lawmakers resist Denver's push for local control and keep self-driving regulation light—or cave to the regulatory impulse.**
