# Colorado Election Security Amendment Faces Media Bias, Conservative Critics Say

Colorado's Amendment 87 is being pushed through with "incomplete arguments" from a sympathetic media and legislature, according to conservative watchdogs at Complete Colorado, who argue the ballot measure lacks transparent public debate on its actual costs and scope.

The amendment has drawn scrutiny as voters weigh competing election-related proposals. Amendment 84, which would require Social Security numbers on mail ballots, gained attention after three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024—all from Mesa County residents attempting to "prove" election fraud existed, according to the Denver Post. While the Post noted Colorado's voting system has "many checks and balances," conservatives argue tighter ID requirements remain necessary to prevent future vulnerabilities.

Republicans contend that election integrity measures like Amendment 84 protect Colorado voters' confidence in results, while Democrats and election officials typically resist ID expansions, citing low fraud rates and concerns about voter access.

Complete Colorado's criticism centers on what it calls media and government alignment in favor of Amendment 87 without full public scrutiny of its implications—a common conservative complaint about how Colorado's left-leaning press covers ballot measures.

Coloradans should watch whether conservative groups successfully counter the narrative on Amendment 87 before November's vote.
