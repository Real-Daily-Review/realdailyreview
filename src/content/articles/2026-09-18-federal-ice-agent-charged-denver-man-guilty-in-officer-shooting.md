# Federal Agent Faces Charges Over Minneapolis Shooting; Denver Man Pleads Guilty to Cop Shooting

A federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement agent has pleaded not guilty to lying to the FBI about his role in a Minneapolis shooting during Operation Metro Surge, marking the first prosecution of a federal immigration officer for actions tied to that enforcement operation (Just The News). Meanwhile, in Colorado, a Denver man pleaded guilty Friday to attempted first-degree murder and other charges after shooting at officers, facing up to 80 years in prison (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

The Minneapolis case raises questions about federal law enforcement accountability and the Justice Department's willingness to prosecute its own agents. The Denver case underscores Colorado's ongoing struggle with violent crime against law enforcement—a reality that should inform debates over defunding police or weakening officer protections.

Conservatives argue that federal agencies must maintain integrity and that officers who violate the law should face consequences, but also that the Justice Department should not weaponize prosecutions against agents conducting legitimate enforcement operations. The Denver guilty plea demonstrates that Colorado's courts are holding violent offenders accountable, a contrast to progressive jurisdictions that have moved to reduce sentences or bail requirements.
