# Democrats Hold Congressional Edge Heading Into 2028, But History Suggests Trouble Ahead

Democrats enter the 2028 presidential cycle with commanding congressional majorities—259 House seats and 56 Senate seats—mirroring the Republican advantage Republicans held in 2016, according to the *Washington Examiner*. But the parallel cuts both ways.

The structural setup inverts 2016's dynamic: then, Republicans controlled Congress while nominating an outsider; now, Democrats hold the legislative branch as they prepare their presidential ticket. Historically, the party holding Congress during a presidential transition faces headwinds. Voters often punish the majority party for legislative records and accumulated policy fatigue.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters. Democrats' congressional dominance has enabled aggressive spending, regulatory expansion, and progressive priorities—from energy mandates to gun-control measures that affect Colorado's energy sector and Second Amendment rights. A 2028 Republican resurgence could reverse course on federal overreach that impacts Colorado's economy and individual liberty.

The question for Coloradans: will Democratic control of Congress become a liability in 2028, or will they consolidate power before the next election cycle shifts the balance?
