# Convicted Felon Charged With Starting Washington Wildfire; Criminal Justice System Failure Raises Questions

A 37-year-old Arizona man with a violent criminal history—convicted of manslaughter and aggravated assault—has been charged with first-degree arson for intentionally starting the Old Trails Fire near Spokane, Washington, which burned approximately 3,600 acres (The Daily Wire, 9News Denver).

Aaron Farinacci's arrest underscores a troubling pattern: a repeat violent offender remained free to allegedly cause massive property damage and ecological harm. The case raises hard questions about criminal justice priorities and whether dangerous individuals are adequately monitored or incarcerated.

**The Colorado angle:** Western wildfires directly threaten Colorado's forests, watersheds, and communities. Arson-caused fires strain already-stretched firefighting resources and divert attention from natural fire management. Coloradans depend on neighboring states' law enforcement to keep dangerous criminals contained—not roaming free to torch thousands of acres.

Conservatives argue that soft-on-crime policies and inadequate criminal supervision enable repeat offenders to commit catastrophic crimes. When violent felons walk free and cause environmental devastation, it's not a failure of the justice system alone—it's a failure to protect public safety and private property.
