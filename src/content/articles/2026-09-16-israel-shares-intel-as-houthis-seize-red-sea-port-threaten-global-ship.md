# Israel Shares Intel with Saudi Arabia as Iran-Backed Houthis Tighten Grip on Red Sea

U.S. Middle East strategy is fracturing as Iranian-backed Houthis consolidate control of Yemen's Red Sea coast, forcing Israel and Saudi Arabia into closer intelligence-sharing while Washington fumbles a coherent response.

According to Israeli reports cited by Breitbart, Israel has begun feeding real-time intelligence to Saudi Arabia through U.S. Central Command as Riyadh battles an escalating Houthi offensive. The Iranian proxy group has captured the strategic Red Sea port of Mokha—its most consequential victory since seizing Yemen's capital Sanaa over a decade ago—and now threatens the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a chokepoint for global shipping and energy transit.

The Washington Examiner argues that containment requires recognizing breakaway states Somaliland and South Arabia to fragment Houthi territorial control and disrupt Iranian logistics networks. Without decisive action, analysts warn, the group will continue weaponizing the Red Sea against commercial shipping and regional allies.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy markets and supply-chain stability matter directly to Colorado's economy. Houthi attacks on shipping drive up fuel and goods costs for Colorado families and businesses. Sustained Red Sea disruption could pressure oil prices and inflation—issues hitting working Coloradans hard.

**Watch next:** Whether the Biden-Harris administration commits to a coherent Yemen strategy or continues ad-hoc responses that leave Israel and Saudi Arabia to manage the crisis alone.
