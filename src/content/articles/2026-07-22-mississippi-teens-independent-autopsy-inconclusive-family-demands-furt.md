# Independent Autopsy in Mississippi Teen Death Yields No Clear Answers

An independent autopsy in the death of 18-year-old Nolan Wells, a Mississippi college football player who went missing after a July 4 boat trip, has returned inconclusive results, according to reports from 9News Denver and The Daily Wire. Wells' body was discovered two days after he became separated from friends during the outing.

The family, represented by civil rights attorney Ben Crump, is now calling for further investigation into the circumstances surrounding the teen's death. The inconclusive findings mean the cause of death remains unclear, leaving questions about what happened during the boating incident.

While this case has no direct Colorado angle, it underscores a broader concern conservatives raise about high-profile cases: the rush to judgment and politicization of tragedy by activist attorneys and media before facts are established. The reliance on independent autopsies—rather than letting official investigations proceed—often reflects distrust in institutions, a sentiment many Coloradans share regarding government competence.

Conservatives should monitor whether this case becomes another flashpoint for racial narratives before evidence is complete, and whether media coverage remains fact-based or devolves into speculation.
