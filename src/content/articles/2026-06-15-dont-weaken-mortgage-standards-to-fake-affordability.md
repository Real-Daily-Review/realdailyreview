# Housing Policy Brief

Colorado homebuyers could face riskier mortgages if federal regulators weaken lending standards in the name of affordability.

The Washington Examiner warns against regulatory shortcuts that would loosen mortgage underwriting requirements. The concern: policymakers eager to lower borrowing costs may compromise the credit-vetting safeguards that protect both lenders and borrowers from predatory lending cycles.

Historically, homeownership expanded dramatically between 1940 and 1960—from 44% to 62%—through legitimate economic growth and returning servicemembers gaining stable employment, not regulatory gimmicks. That model worked because it rested on sound lending practices and borrower capacity to repay.

Conservatives argue that easing lending standards repeats mistakes that preceded the 2008 financial crisis: lowering down payments, relaxing income verification, or ignoring debt-to-income ratios sounds compassionate but ultimately harms the very borrowers it claims to help. When mortgages fail, families lose homes and communities destabilize.

Colorado's housing affordability crisis is real—but the answer isn't looser rules that invite defaults. It's removing regulatory barriers to new construction, cutting permitting delays, and letting supply respond to demand. Taxpayers should demand that any federal housing policy prioritize sustainable lending over political theater.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes back against any Biden or future administration moves to weaken mortgage underwriting standards.
