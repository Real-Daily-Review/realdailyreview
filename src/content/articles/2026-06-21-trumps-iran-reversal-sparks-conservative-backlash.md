# Trump's Iran Deal Reversal Draws Fire From Right and Left

President Trump's abrupt capitulation on Iran negotiations has triggered rare bipartisan criticism, with conservatives questioning whether the administration abandoned core positions to declare a quick diplomatic win.

According to *National Review*, Trump reversed his previous stance on key contentious issues, effectively adopting the Iranian negotiating position wholesale. The conservative outlet calls it a "prompt and utter humiliation" for an administration that campaigned on strength in foreign policy.

Meanwhile, *Colorado Sun* columnist Mike Littwin argues the White House is now relying on misleading claims to sell the deal, citing Vice President JD Vance's recent false statements about the agreement's terms.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans and right-leaning analysts contend Trump surrendered leverage without securing meaningful concessions—a stark reversal from his 2016 "America First" messaging on Iran. They worry the deal leaves regional allies (particularly Israel) exposed and fails to address ballistic missile development or uranium enrichment timelines.

**The Left's Critique:** Progressive outlets echo concerns about capitulation but frame it as proof Trump lacks diplomatic skill, contrasting unfavorably with the 2015 JCPOA.

For Colorado taxpayers and energy interests, Iran policy matters: sanctions relief could flood global oil markets, affecting domestic energy prices and Colorado's oil and gas sector competitiveness.

Watch whether Congress moves to block the deal or demand transparency on negotiation terms.
