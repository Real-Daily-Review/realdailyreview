# Gold Mountain Fire Spreads Near Ouray as Colorado Faces Dangerous Fire Season

Colorado's wildfire season is intensifying, with the Gold Mountain Fire burning northeast of Ouray and threatening communities across the state as dangerous fire weather persists. According to KDVR, firefighters have achieved only 3% containment despite aggressive efforts, underscoring the scale of the challenge facing the region.

The fire continues to grow in the San Juan Mountains area, though CPR News reports no new evacuations have been ordered at this time. Officials warn the wildfire remains a significant threat to nearby communities and infrastructure.

Colorado faces a critical vulnerability: decades of forest mismanagement—including restrictions on timber harvesting and controlled burns—have left the state's forests dense with fuel. Conservative energy and land-use advocates argue that overly restrictive environmental regulations have prevented the active forest management necessary to reduce catastrophic wildfire risk. Timber harvesting and strategic prescribed burns, they contend, are essential tools that state and federal agencies have underutilized.

The Gold Mountain Fire also highlights Colorado's water security concerns. Wildfires threaten watersheds that supply drinking water and irrigation to Front Range communities and agricultural operations across the state.

Taxpayers should watch whether state officials will finally embrace aggressive forest management policies or continue relying on reactive firefighting at enormous public expense.
