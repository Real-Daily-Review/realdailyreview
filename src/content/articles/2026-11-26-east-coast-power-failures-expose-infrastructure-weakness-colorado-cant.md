# East Coast Power Failures Expose Infrastructure Vulnerability on Thanksgiving

Severe storms knocked out power at two water filtration plants serving nearly 2 million residents in Maryland suburbs outside Washington, D.C., forcing authorities to issue emergency water conservation orders on Thanksgiving (Washington Examiner). The disruption highlights a critical vulnerability: aging infrastructure dependent on fragile power grids that fail under weather stress.

This matters for Colorado. Our state's water systems—already strained by drought, population growth, and competing claims between agriculture, cities, and environmental mandates—depend similarly on aging infrastructure. If East Coast utilities can't keep the lights on during a storm, what happens when Colorado's grid faces a summer heat wave or winter blizzard?

Conservatives argue that instead of pursuing aggressive climate regulations that drive up energy costs and reliability, policymakers should prioritize hardening the electrical grid and modernizing water infrastructure. Colorado's push toward renewable energy, while well-intentioned, risks further straining grid stability without adequate baseload generation—a lesson the Maryland outage underscores.

The Left typically frames infrastructure failures as proof we need more federal spending and green-energy mandates. Republicans counter that we need smarter investment in resilience, not ideological energy policy that sacrifices reliability.

**Watch whether Colorado regulators learn from this: Will they demand grid hardening and reliable power sources, or double down on renewables-only mandates?**
