# Denver Faces Another Day of Near-Record Heat as Unseasonable Warmth Persists

Denver is bracing for another day of near-record temperatures this week, with highs climbing into the upper 80s across the Front Range (KDVR Fox31). The unseasonably warm stretch has meteorologists watching for possible record-breaking heat, though 9News reports a cooler shift should arrive by the weekend.

The persistent warmth underscores Colorado's volatile spring weather patterns—a reminder that energy demand for cooling could spike unexpectedly, putting pressure on the state's power grid as it transitions away from reliable coal generation. Conservatives have long warned that Colorado's aggressive renewable energy mandates, including the 2019 clean energy law, risk grid reliability during peak demand periods without adequate baseload capacity.

While this week's heat is a weather event, not a policy crisis, it highlights why Colorado needs dependable energy infrastructure. As the state phases out coal plants ahead of schedule to meet progressive climate targets, grid operators face tighter margins during heat waves—exactly when Coloradans need reliable, affordable power most.

The cooler pattern expected by weekend should provide temporary relief, but the underlying tension between aggressive decarbonization timelines and grid stability remains unresolved.
