# Congress Punts $1.8 Trillion Spending Fight Past Election Day—Again

Congress has once again chosen political convenience over fiscal responsibility, kicking a $1.8 trillion spending battle to December and leaving Colorado taxpayers in limbo.

On September 1, the House voted 370–48 to pass a continuing resolution funding the federal government through December 11, after the Senate cleared it 90–6 weeks earlier (Washington Examiner). The move avoids a pre-election showdown over actual budget priorities—but that's precisely the problem.

**What conservatives argue:** This is governance by avoidance. A continuing resolution isn't a budget; it's a placeholder that locks in current spending levels and punts hard choices to after voters have spoken. Republicans and fiscal hawks say Congress should pass real budgets that reflect priorities and allow meaningful debate over federal spending. Instead, lawmakers from both parties prefer the political cover of a stopgap measure.

The timing is transparent: Democrats and Republicans alike want to avoid a spending fight that might hurt their midterm chances. Twenty-nine Democrats and 19 Republicans voted against the measure, but the overwhelming majorities in both chambers show how comfortable the establishment is with kicking the can.

For Colorado, this means continued uncertainty over federal land management, water policy, and energy development—all tied to annual appropriations Congress refuses to finalize.

Watch whether Republicans, if they gain control of the House, actually demand a real budget process in 2023 or fall into the same trap.
