# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push starving animals into residential areas across the state. The surge in human-bear encounters reflects a cascade of environmental pressures: water scarcity is decimating natural food sources, while wildfires are destroying habitat and forcing bears to forage in backyards and garbage cans from the Front Range to the Western Slope.

CPW officials say the killings are a last resort, but conservatives and property owners argue the state has failed to address root causes. Ranchers and rural Coloradans point to decades of forest mismanagement, water policy gridlock, and resistance to controlled burns as contributors to the crisis. Some also question whether aggressive bear relocation efforts have been adequately funded or prioritized before resorting to lethal removal.

The record cull underscores a hard truth: Colorado's natural resources—water, timber, wildlife habitat—are under strain from both climate pressures and policy choices. Taxpayers are footing the bill for emergency wildlife management while underlying land and water governance remains contentious.

Watch whether state lawmakers will fund preventive habitat restoration and forest management, or continue cycling through reactive bear killings each drought season.
