# Denver's $18M 16th Street Mall Surprise: Another Case of Government Opacity Costing Taxpayers

Denver taxpayers got blindsided again—this time by a historic brick sewer system nobody told them about until the bill came due.

During renovation of the 16th Street Mall, contractors discovered an underground brick sewer that forced a redesign and added $18 million to the project cost (9News Denver). The kicker: Denver's auditor found that the city council committee overseeing the Department of Transportation and Infrastructure **didn't even know about the change order** before it was approved.

This is textbook government dysfunction. A major cost overrun happened without proper oversight or transparency. The auditor's office flagged the lack of communication between city departments and elected officials—meaning taxpayers footed an $18 million bill for work nobody had properly vetted or disclosed upfront.

Conservatives argue this exemplifies why government-run infrastructure projects routinely spiral over budget: poor planning, inadequate due diligence, and zero accountability until the damage is done. When private contractors miss something this significant, they eat the cost. When Denver does it, you do.

The auditor called for "more transparency," but that's bureaucratic speak for "we need better processes." What taxpayers need is actual consequences for the officials who approved $18 million without knowing what they were approving.

Watch whether Denver's leadership implements real oversight reforms—or just issues another hollow promise.
