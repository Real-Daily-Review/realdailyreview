# Colorado Moves to Strip State Perks From Political Parties—A Win for Taxpayers

Colorado is quietly dismantling a system that has handed government resources and special privileges to Democratic and Republican party machinery, a move conservatives say restores proper separation between state and faction.

According to Complete Colorado, the state is moving to eliminate official recognition and taxpayer-funded benefits that have long flowed to party organizations. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this represents a necessary correction: government should remain neutral, not subsidize partisan operations or grant one party leverage over state resources.

The shift reflects growing frustration across the political spectrum that state government has become entangled with party infrastructure—a problem that wastes taxpayer dollars and corrupts the principle that public resources belong to all Coloradans, not Democratic or Republican operatives.

Democrats have not publicly objected to the move, suggesting even the left recognizes the optics problem of state-funded partisanship. Complete Colorado frames the change as "courageous," acknowledging it breaks from decades of bipartisan backscratching.

For Colorado taxpayers, this means fewer dollars flowing to party committees and more resources theoretically available for core government functions. Conservatives should monitor whether the state actually redirects savings or simply finds new ways to spend them.
