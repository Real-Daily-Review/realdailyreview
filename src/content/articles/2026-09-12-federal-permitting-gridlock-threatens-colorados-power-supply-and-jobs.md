# America Has the Power—Washington Won't Let Colorado Use It

Federal permitting gridlock is strangling America's electricity supply just as demand surges, threatening Colorado's competitive edge in manufacturing and energy development.

The U.S. faces a 25% increase in power demand over the next decade, driven by domestic manufacturing growth, artificial intelligence expansion, and electrification of heating and transportation, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Yet Washington's byzantine permitting process is blocking the grid upgrades and new generation capacity needed to meet that demand.

For Colorado, this is a direct hit. The state's energy sector—coal, natural gas, and increasingly renewables—depends on efficient grid interconnection and permitting timelines measured in months, not years. Delays mean lost jobs, delayed projects, and higher electricity costs for families and businesses already squeezed by progressive energy mandates.

Conservatives argue that permitting reform is essential: streamline environmental reviews, set hard deadlines for approvals, and let market forces drive energy development rather than bureaucratic whim. The left's approach—layering more environmental reviews and expanding regulatory authority—only deepens the bottleneck while pretending to serve climate goals.

Colorado taxpayers and energy workers should watch whether state lawmakers demand that Washington fix federal permitting, or whether they'll accept more federal control over Colorado's power future.
