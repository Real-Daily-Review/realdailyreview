# Denver Heat Wave Persists; Upper 90s Expected Through Week

Colorado's Front Range is bracing for another scorcher as a statewide heat wave settles in with no immediate relief in sight. The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for the Denver area, with afternoon highs climbing into the upper 90s and approaching record territory through the week, according to 9News and KDVR Fox31.

The persistent heat is already testing Colorado's outdoor culture. Dr. Joseph Ponce warns that active Coloradans—hikers, runners, construction workers—often underestimate the dangers of prolonged heat exposure and fail to take adequate breaks, risking heat-related illness (9News).

For Colorado's energy sector and grid operators, sustained high temperatures mean peak demand for air conditioning and electricity. This underscores the importance of reliable, affordable power generation—a reality that should inform state policymakers' energy decisions, particularly as Colorado continues to phase out coal plants and rely increasingly on intermittent renewables.

Residents should monitor local forecasts, stay hydrated, and limit outdoor exertion during peak afternoon hours. Employers in construction, agriculture, and outdoor industries should enforce heat protocols to protect workers.

Watch for whether Colorado's grid experiences strain or rolling blackout risks during this heat event—a metric conservatives should track as the state pursues aggressive decarbonization targets.
