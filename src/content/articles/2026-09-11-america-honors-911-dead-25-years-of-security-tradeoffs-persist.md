# America Marks 25 Years Since 9/11: A Quarter-Century of Changed Security, Vigilance

America paused this week to remember the 184 lives lost at the Pentagon and thousands more across the nation on September 11, 2001—a day that fundamentally reshaped American security policy, military posture, and the balance between liberty and safety that conservatives continue to debate today.

Ceremonies at the Pentagon and Ground Zero in New York drew national leaders, including President Trump, to honor the fallen and reflect on two and a half decades of counterterrorism operations, homeland defense investments, and the ongoing cost of vigilance (9News Denver, Just The News).

For Colorado, the 9/11 legacy remains tangible: enhanced airport security protocols at Denver International, military readiness at Fort Carson and other installations, and the persistent question of how to protect Americans without surrendering constitutional freedoms to unchecked government surveillance. Conservatives have long argued that post-9/11 security measures—from the PATRIOT Act to NSA programs—often overreached, trading privacy for promised safety without delivering proportional results.

The 25-year mark invites hard questions: Have we struck the right balance between security and liberty? Have federal security bureaucracies grown too large and unaccountable? Colorado taxpayers and defense workers should watch whether policymakers use this anniversary to justify further expansions of government power or to recalibrate toward constitutional limits.
