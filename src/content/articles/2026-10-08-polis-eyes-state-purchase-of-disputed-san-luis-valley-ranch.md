# State Considers Buying Into San Luis Valley Land Dispute; Conservatives Question Price Tag

Gov. Jared Polis has tapped former U.S. Sen. Ken Salazar to broker a solution to the long-running battle over Cielo Vista Ranch in Costilla County, with a state report now recommending Colorado purchase part of the disputed property (Colorado Sun). The move marks another instance of government stepping in to resolve a private land dispute—raising immediate questions about how much taxpayer money will be spent and whether market-based solutions were exhausted first.

Republicans and property-rights advocates argue that Colorado's appetite for land acquisition, particularly in rural areas, often inflates prices and diverts public funds from core services. They contend that private negotiation, mediation, or court resolution should precede state buyouts. The San Luis Valley, already home to competing water and agricultural interests, doesn't need state ownership complicating future land-use decisions, conservatives say.

Polis's administration frames the purchase as conflict resolution in a region where ranching and conservation interests clash. But taxpayers deserve transparency: what's the asking price, how was it determined, and what happens to the land once the state owns it? Will it become another government-managed property requiring ongoing maintenance and staff?

Watch whether the Polis administration discloses the full acquisition cost and timeline before committing Colorado dollars.
