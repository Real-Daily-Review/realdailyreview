# Trump Presses Iran for Compensation Over Decades of American Deaths

The Trump administration is taking a hardline stance that Tehran should pay reparations for Americans killed by Iranian proxies over the past 50 years—a demand that signals Washington won't soft-pedal accountability in future negotiations.

President Trump announced Monday that compensation for victims' families will become a non-negotiable element of all U.S.-Iran talks going forward, particularly regarding attacks by Iranian-backed forces using roadside bombs and other means (Washington Examiner). The demand comes as tensions remain elevated following joint U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iranian targets in late February.

Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi fired back, refusing direct engagement with Washington until the U.S. addresses Tehran's own demands—chiefly regarding the Strait of Hormuz dispute (Breitbart). The standoff underscores how far apart the two sides remain.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the U.S. has been too accommodating to Iranian aggression for decades. Demanding compensation for American lives lost to Iranian proxies—from Iraq to Lebanon—is basic justice and deterrence. Weakness invites further attacks.

**Iran's position:** Tehran claims the U.S. must first meet its demands before any direct talks resume, suggesting both sides are dug in.
