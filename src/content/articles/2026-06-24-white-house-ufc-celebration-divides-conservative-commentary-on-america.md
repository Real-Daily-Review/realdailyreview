# White House Embraces Unconventional America 250 Celebration—And Conservatives Are Split

The White House's decision to host a UFC cage match on the lawn as part of America's 250th birthday celebration has sparked debate among right-leaning commentators about what patriotic commemoration should look like.

The Washington Examiner's editorial board initially bristled at the optics, noting the instinctive reaction to call it "tacky"—a cage fight as the centerpiece of Independence Day festivities seemed incongruous. But the outlet ultimately embraced the unconventional approach, arguing that America's strength lies in its freedom to celebrate messily and without pretense, even if it offends refined sensibilities.

National Review took a broader constitutional view, framing America's 250th as a moment to reaffirm that liberty depends on limits—particularly as the Supreme Court's recent decisions on tariffs and other matters underscore the Constitution's enduring role as a check on unchecked power.

The celebration reflects deeper conservative questions: Should patriotic observance be solemn and traditional, or can it embrace popular culture and spectacle? Conservatives generally agree the milestone should emphasize constitutional restraint and individual freedom over government expansion, but disagree on tone.

**What to watch:** Whether the Biden administration uses the 250th to push progressive policy initiatives or genuinely focuses on constitutional principles—a telling indicator of where Democrats see America's future heading.
