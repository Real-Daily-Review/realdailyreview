# Colorado Expands Mail Voting While Primary System Draws Fire Over Ideological Gatekeeping

Gov. Jared Polis has signed legislation extending Colorado's mail-voting window by a week—a move Democrats frame as voter access but Republicans warn could invite election vulnerabilities and federal scrutiny. Starting in November, voters will have extra time to receive and return mail-in ballots, part of what the *Denver Post* reports is an effort to "insulate the state against federal interference."

Meanwhile, prominent Colorado moderate Kent Thiry is raising alarms about the opposite problem: primary elections dominated by ideological factions that don't represent most voters. According to *Complete Colorado*, Thiry warns of a "tyranny of the minority" in which party primaries are shaped by activist wings, producing general-election candidates out of step with mainstream Colorado.

The tension is revealing. Democrats are betting on expanded mail voting to protect turnout; Republicans counter that looser ballot procedures and extended timelines create security gaps and administrative chaos. Conservatives argue Colorado's primary system already tilts toward ideological purists, and that adding a week to mail voting—without tighter chain-of-custody rules—compounds the problem.

For Colorado taxpayers and election officials, the real question is whether convenience outweighs security. Watch whether the GOP-controlled legislature pushes back on Polis's voting expansion or demands stricter ballot-tracking measures in return.
