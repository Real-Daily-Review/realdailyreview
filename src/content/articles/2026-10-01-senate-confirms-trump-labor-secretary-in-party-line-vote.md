# Senate Confirms Trump's Labor Secretary Pick in Party-Line Vote

Trump's pick to lead the Labor Department cleared the Senate on a narrow 47-41 party-line vote, with Keith Sonderling winning confirmation after serving as acting secretary since April. Sonderling brings career experience across multiple roles within the department, positioning him to reshape labor policy from the Trump administration's pro-business, deregulation-focused perspective.

For Colorado, the confirmation matters. Sonderling's Labor Department will influence how federal wage rules, workplace regulations, and labor enforcement affect Colorado businesses, construction firms, and energy operators already burdened by state-level progressive labor mandates. Republicans argue that rolling back Biden-era labor regulations—including aggressive OSHA enforcement and union-friendly interpretations of labor law—will free up employers to hire and invest. Democrats counter that weakening labor protections will harm workers and erode workplace safety standards.

The narrow confirmation underscores the Senate's razor-thin Republican majority and signals continued partisan warfare over labor policy. Colorado's two Democratic senators, Michael Bennet and John Hickenlooper, almost certainly opposed the nomination, reflecting the state's leftward drift on labor issues despite its business-friendly reputation.

Watch whether Sonderling moves quickly to roll back Biden-era labor rules that Colorado businesses have had to navigate—and whether state Democrats push back with their own regulatory tightening.
