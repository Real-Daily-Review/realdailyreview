# UK Social Media Ban Won't Cross the Atlantic—And That's Good News for American Liberty

Britain's new ban on social media for under-16s won't translate to America, and conservatives should be relieved. Prime Minister Keir Starmer's sweeping restriction on platforms like TikTok, Instagram, and Snapchat for minors represents the kind of paternalistic government overreach that the First Amendment was designed to prevent, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The UK model assumes government knows best how to manage children's digital lives—a premise fundamentally at odds with American constitutional principles protecting free speech and parental authority. While progressives may eye Britain's approach as a template for regulating Big Tech, such legislation would face insurmountable legal challenges in U.S. courts.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Parents, not politicians, should decide what their children access online. Heavy-handed federal bans risk creating precedent for broader speech restrictions and empower bureaucrats to define acceptable content. Colorado families deserve the freedom to make their own choices without Washington or Denver dictating terms.

The real solution isn't prohibition—it's transparency, parental tools, and market competition that incentivizes platforms to police themselves. Government mandates inevitably expand beyond their stated purpose.

Watch whether Democratic lawmakers attempt to import Britain's model into U.S. legislation—a telling indicator of how far the left will push to regulate speech in the name of child safety.
