# Trump Sidesteps Nvidia Spy Scandal in Xi Talks—But Questions Linger on AI Exports

President Trump will dodge the Jensen Huang Chinese espionage matter entirely during next week's meetings with Xi Jinping, according to a senior administration official cited by the *Washington Examiner*. The move raises eyebrows among conservatives who argue the real issue—why the U.S. allows Nvidia to export advanced AI chips to China at all—remains unresolved.

The *National Review* frames the core problem bluntly: American companies are shipping cutting-edge technology to a strategic adversary while a former CEO faces allegations tied to Chinese intelligence. Republicans and national security hawks say this represents a catastrophic failure of export controls and corporate accountability.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. The state hosts significant tech infrastructure and talent pipelines feeding companies like Nvidia. If U.S. policy on AI exports remains muddled—prioritizing diplomatic niceties over security—Colorado's tech sector could face tighter federal restrictions down the road, or worse, reputational damage if Chinese espionage ties deepen.

The administration's silence on Huang suggests either diplomatic calculation or avoidance of uncomfortable questions about its own vetting of corporate leadership. Conservatives should watch whether Congress demands hearings on Nvidia's China dealings and whether Trump's team tightens AI export rules or simply lets the scandal fade.
