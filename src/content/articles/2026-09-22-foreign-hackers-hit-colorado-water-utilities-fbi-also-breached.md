# Foreign Hackers Target Colorado Water Infrastructure; FBI Also Breached

Colorado's water security just got a wake-up call—and it's not alone. Two privately owned Colorado water utilities fell victim to foreign hackers in August, according to the governor's office, with the identity of the attackers still murky (KDVR Fox31 Denver). Meanwhile, the notorious ShinyHunters hacking group claimed responsibility for breaching the FBI itself, stealing employee data after the bureau publicly detailed its methods and discouraged ransom payments (New York Post).

The Colorado water breaches underscore a critical vulnerability: critical infrastructure—especially in a water-scarce state where every drop matters—remains exposed to sophisticated foreign adversaries. Conservatives argue that hardening cybersecurity defenses for essential services should be a top priority, not an afterthought buried in bureaucratic red tape.

The FBI breach adds insult to injury. The agency's public posturing against ransoms apparently made it a target, raising uncomfortable questions about whether federal law enforcement's own operational security matches its swagger. If hackers can penetrate the FBI's systems, what does that say about protecting Colorado's water systems from state-level threats?

Republicans and taxpayers should demand immediate answers: What data was compromised at those Colorado utilities? Were customer records exposed? And what concrete steps are being taken to prevent the next breach?

Watch for whether state officials demand federal cybersecurity standards for Colorado's water infrastructure—and whether those mandates come with unfunded compliance costs passed to ratepayers.
