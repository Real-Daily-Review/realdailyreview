# Colorado Wildfire Season Rages as Forest Management Debate Intensifies

Colorado's wildfire season is exploding—and conservatives say decades of misguided federal forest policy are partly to blame.

Multiple fires are currently burning across the state (9News Denver), with conditions worsening as summer peaks. But the real story isn't just the flames—it's the fuel load choking Colorado's forests.

National Review argues that "ideology and short-term thinking" in federal forest management have created tinderboxes across the West. Conservatives point to overly restrictive environmental regulations that have blocked thinning operations, prescribed burns, and timber harvesting—the very tools that reduce catastrophic wildfire risk. When the federal government controls vast swaths of Colorado land, it dictates management policy, often prioritizing environmental litigation over practical forest health.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that climate change is the primary driver, and that aggressive logging and development worsen conditions. They argue for stricter protections and carbon-reduction measures.

For Colorado taxpayers and property owners, the stakes are existential: homes lost, air quality decimated, and emergency response costs soaring—all while federal bureaucrats debate ideology instead of clearing dead trees and undergrowth.
