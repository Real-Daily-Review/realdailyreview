# Berlin Pride Attack Highlights Failures in Deportation and Detention Policy

A vehicle attack at a Berlin LGBTQ+ pride march killed at least one person and injured others—and the suspect is a 21-year-old Islamist previously detained on terror charges, raising hard questions about why he remained in Germany.

According to Breitbart, the suspect, identified as Abdul Ballut, had been previously detained in Germany on terror offenses, including alleged support for ISIS. Police shut down the event and evacuated attendees after the attack (Just The News).

**The Conservative Angle:** This incident exposes a recurring failure across Western Europe: known security threats remain free to operate. Germany's immigration and detention policies have repeatedly allowed individuals with documented terror links to remain in the country—a pattern that should alarm any government serious about protecting its citizens.

Republicans and conservatives argue that nations must enforce strict deportation standards for foreign nationals with terror connections and maintain robust detention policies for those who pose clear security risks. Soft-on-security policies, they contend, prioritize political correctness over public safety.

Democrats typically resist hardline deportation measures, citing humanitarian concerns and due-process protections—but this case demonstrates the real-world cost of that approach.
