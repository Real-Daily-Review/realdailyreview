# Trump Pushes Ukraine Settlement as War Grinds On—Colorado Should Watch Energy Fallout

Trump is openly pressuring Zelenskyy to negotiate an end to the Russia-Ukraine war, citing staggering casualty figures while simultaneously approving Ukraine's domestic Patriot missile production (Just The News). The mixed signals—pushing for peace talks while greenlighting weapons manufacturing—underscore the administration's conflicting approach to the conflict.

What matters to Colorado: Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries have already spiked U.S. oil prices, directly hitting Colorado energy workers and consumers at the pump. A prolonged war keeps global energy markets volatile; a rushed settlement could leave Ukraine weakened and Russia emboldened to threaten broader energy infrastructure.

Republicans argue that endless U.S. military aid to Ukraine drains American resources without a clear exit strategy. Democrats counter that abandoning Ukraine invites Russian aggression and destabilizes NATO allies. Conservatives worry that Trump's dealmaking instincts, while well-intentioned, may sacrifice Ukrainian sovereignty for a headline-grabbing truce that collapses within months.

Daily aerial strikes continue—at least seven killed Saturday (Breitbart)—suggesting neither side is ready for serious negotiations despite Trump's pressure.

**Watch whether Trump's energy-truce proposal gains traction: a Russia-Ukraine ceasefire tied to oil market stability could reshape U.S. energy policy and Colorado's oil and gas sector.**
