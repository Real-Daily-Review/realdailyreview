# California's $34M School Building Sits Empty—a Warning for Colorado's Fiscal Future

California's Los Angeles County is now sitting on a $34 million abandoned school building, a cautionary tale of government waste that Colorado taxpayers should heed closely.

What began as a charter high school campus serving hundreds of students has devolved into a crumbling monument to poor planning. As school-age enrollment declined across Los Angeles, the facility could never break even—yet the public money was already spent. Today the building stands vacant, damaged by holes in walls, water intrusion, and fire marks, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The lesson here cuts straight to Colorado's core: when government commits massive capital to projects without sustainable enrollment or demand projections, taxpayers foot the bill for decades. Colorado school districts have faced similar enrollment volatility, especially post-pandemic, yet continue pursuing costly facility expansions and bond measures.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this California debacle underscores why education spending must be tied to real enrollment data and why charter schools—while theoretically market-driven—still require rigorous financial oversight. Democrats typically counter that adequate public funding prevents such deterioration, but the *Examiner* reporting suggests the problem wasn't insufficient money; it was misallocation.

Colorado voters should demand transparency on how local school districts project enrollment and justify new construction before approving the next bond measure.
