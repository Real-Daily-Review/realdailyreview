# Trump's Iran Deal Draws Fire From Right and Left Over Capitulation Claims

President Trump faces withering criticism from both conservative and progressive outlets over a newly brokered Iran truce agreement, with critics across the spectrum accusing him of abandoning core negotiating positions.

National Review argues Trump "abandoned his previous positions in favor of the Iranian position on all the key issues of contention" (National Review). The Colorado Sun's Mike Littwin frames the deal as evidence Trump "has only one card to play — lie," citing Vice President JD Vance's public statements as examples of misleading claims about the agreement's terms.

Conservatives and Republicans who backed Trump's hardline Iran stance now face a credibility test: the president campaigned on strength against Tehran and rejected the Obama-era nuclear deal as a capitulation. This reversal undercuts that messaging heading into 2026 midterms, when Colorado Republicans will need to energize voters skeptical of foreign policy retreats.

The specifics of what Trump conceded remain contested. Both outlets suggest the administration is spinning the agreement's actual terms to voters—a damaging admission for a president who built his brand on straight talk and deal-making prowess.
