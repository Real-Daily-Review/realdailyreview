# Colorado Voters Face Crucial Energy Choice on Amendment 82

Colorado faces a ballot showdown over whether to constitutionally protect natural gas—a direct rebuke to progressive activists bent on eliminating affordable home heating and cooking fuel.

Amendment 82 would enshrine Coloradans' right to use natural gas in the state constitution. Supporters argue the measure is essential protection against a coordinated left-wing campaign to ban natural gas appliances and infrastructure. Complete Colorado warns that banning natural gas—"the real intent of opponents"—would spike energy costs and damage Colorado's economy, hitting working families and businesses hardest.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that the amendment locks in fossil fuel dependence and blocks Colorado's path to clean energy. They frame the debate around climate goals and air quality, not affordability.

The stakes are concrete: natural gas heats Colorado homes, powers water heaters, and fuels stoves across the state. Progressive cities and states have already moved to restrict or ban gas hookups in new construction. Colorado's energy-dependent economy—and the jobs tied to natural gas production and distribution—hang in the balance.

Conservatives argue this amendment is necessary insurance against regulatory overreach that would force Coloradans onto all-electric systems at massive cost.

Watch whether the amendment gains traction with rural and suburban voters who depend on affordable, reliable natural gas.
