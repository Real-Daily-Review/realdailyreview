# USS Abraham Lincoln Crew Struggles After 260-Day Deployment Raise Military Readiness Questions

The USS Abraham Lincoln has been at sea for more than 260 days without port call, and while CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper praised the carrier's leadership for prioritizing mental health, reports of crew struggles underscore growing concerns about extended deployments and sailor welfare.

Cooper visited the vessel Sunday to tout the crew's "resilience," but acknowledged that "not all is perfect" aboard the ship (Washington Examiner). The extended deployment has drawn attention to alleged mental health issues among sailors and reports of deteriorating conditions on the carrier, which has played a key role in U.S. Central Command operations.

The situation highlights a broader military readiness problem: when sailors are pushed to their limits for months without respite, morale and operational effectiveness suffer. Conservatives have long argued that military readiness depends on proper funding, adequate personnel, and realistic deployment schedules—not just rhetoric about resilience.

The Navy must balance global commitments with the welfare of its personnel. Extended deployments without adequate port calls strain crews and risk long-term retention of skilled sailors the military desperately needs.

Watch whether Congress demands accountability on Navy deployment schedules and whether the service branch commits to concrete changes in crew rotation and rest protocols.
