# Colorado Family Grieves as Impaired Driving Claims Another Life

A Greeley family is mourning after a fatal crash claimed two lives, reigniting urgent questions about Colorado's enforcement of impaired-driving laws and border security failures that allow dangerous criminals back onto American roads.

According to KDVR Fox31 Denver, the grieving daughter of one victim is urging drivers to reconsider getting behind the wheel while impaired. The tragedy underscores a preventable loss that Colorado law enforcement and prosecutors must address head-on.

The incident mirrors a broader pattern highlighted by The Daily Wire: a previously deported illegal immigrant was accused of killing a young father while driving drunk in a stolen vehicle on a North Texas highway in September. That case—involving Alexander Nunez, 26, who struck a vehicle carrying Kenan Ellis and two cousins—exposes a compounding failure: both lax immigration enforcement and inadequate consequences for repeat offenders.

**Conservatives argue** that Colorado and federal authorities must strengthen DUI enforcement, ensure stolen-vehicle recovery systems work, and enforce immigration law to keep previously deported criminals out of the country. Allowing repeat offenders—whether drunk drivers or deportees—back on Colorado streets is a failure of government's most basic duty: protecting citizens.
