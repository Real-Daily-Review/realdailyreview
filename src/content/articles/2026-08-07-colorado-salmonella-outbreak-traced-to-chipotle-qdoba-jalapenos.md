# Colorado Salmonella Outbreak Tied to Jalapeños at Major Chains

A salmonella outbreak linked to jalapeño peppers at Chipotle and Qdoba has sickened over 100 Coloradans, exposing how quickly foodborne illness can spread through popular restaurant chains.

Colorado health officials traced the outbreak to jalapeños served at both chains, according to reporting from CPR News and the Colorado Sun. KDVR reports that Colorado recorded a spike of salmonella cases in July—more than double the monthly average—with a significant portion tied to the contaminated peppers. Both restaurants have since pulled the product from their menus.

The good news: health officials say there is no ongoing risk from eating at these establishments now that the peppers have been removed.

This incident underscores a broader concern for Colorado consumers and business owners alike. While the chains responded quickly, the outbreak highlights vulnerabilities in food supply chains and the importance of rigorous vendor oversight. Restaurants operating on thin margins face real costs when suppliers fail quality checks—costs that ultimately hit consumers through higher prices or reduced menu options.

Colorado's food safety system caught this outbreak, but conservatives should ask harder questions: Are current FDA and state inspection protocols sufficient to prevent contamination upstream, or are we relying too heavily on reactive measures after people get sick?
