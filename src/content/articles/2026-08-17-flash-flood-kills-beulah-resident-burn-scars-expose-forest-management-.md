# Flash Flood Kills Beulah Resident; Burn Scars Remain Catastrophic Liability

Colorado's failure to manage forest health has turned wildfire burn scars into death traps. An 80-year-old Beulah resident, Henry R. Humphreys, was killed Sunday when flash flooding swept through the Pueblo County area after just 1 to 1½ inches of rain fell on terrain scorched by the Aspen Acres fire, which destroyed over 300 homes earlier this summer (Colorado Sun, KDVR Fox31 Denver).

Burned hillsides lack vegetation to absorb water and stabilize soil. The result: predictable, preventable disasters. Conservatives have long warned that decades of federal mismanagement—blocking timber harvests, resisting controlled burns, and prioritizing environmental litigation over forest stewardship—leaves Colorado communities defenseless against cascading catastrophes.

The Coroner's Office is investigating the incident. More rain is forecast this week across Colorado burn scars, putting additional lives and property at risk.

This tragedy underscores what happens when ideology trumps land management. Taxpayers foot the bill for disaster response, rebuilding, and litigation—while bureaucrats in Denver and Washington debate forest policy from air-conditioned offices. Colorado needs aggressive fuel reduction, not more environmental restrictions.

Watch whether state and federal officials finally prioritize forest health over green activism in the post-fire recovery effort.
