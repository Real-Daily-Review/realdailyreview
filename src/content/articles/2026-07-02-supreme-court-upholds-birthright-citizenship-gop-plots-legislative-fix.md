# Supreme Court Upholds Birthright Citizenship; Conservatives Push Legislative Workaround

The Supreme Court has rejected efforts to overturn birthright citizenship, dealing a blow to immigration hawks who argue the practice incentivizes illegal border crossings and exploits the Fourteenth Amendment. ICE responded by surging enforcement operations, making over 10,000 arrests in five days (Fox News), signaling the administration's pivot toward executive action where courts blocked statutory change.

Republicans aren't accepting defeat. Rep. Andy Ogles introduced the Anchors Away Act to restrict birthright citizenship for children of non-citizens and temporary visitors—a legislative end-run around the Court's decision (Fox News). National Review argues Congress retains power to curtail "the worst aspects" of the ruling, suggesting statutory limits remain viable.

Conservative outlets are scathing. The Daily Wire called the decision "easily the worst" in modern times, likening birthright citizenship to a "giant game show" where illegal aliens game the system. Yet Trump told Breitbart the Slaughter decision—granting presidents power to fire independent agency staff—"more than made up" for the birthright setback, signaling he's moving forward on executive power expansion.

**Colorado angle:** Immigration enforcement and border security resonate with rural and suburban Colorado voters concerned about labor market impacts and public services strain.

**Watch:** Whether the Anchors Away Act gains traction in Congress or if the administration pursues narrower executive restrictions on birthright benefits.
