# Colorado Ballot Crowded With Tax Hikes, Election Security Measure

Colorado voters face a November ballot packed with tax increases—and a rare win for election integrity.

School districts across the state are seeking hundreds of millions in new property tax obligations through mill levy overrides (Complete Colorado), while the Yampa Valley transit authority is pushing a sales tax hike to fund basic operations. Meanwhile, the Denver Post reports a graduated income tax hike targeting high earners has cleared the ballot threshold.

The tax assault comes during a cost-of-living crisis, forcing districts to compete for voter attention on an already crowded ballot.

On the bright side for conservatives: Initiative 362, which adds voter ID requirements to Colorado's all-mail voting system, has made the November ballot. The measure represents a rare pushback against the state's mail-voting-first model—a system Republicans have long criticized as vulnerable to fraud and ballot harvesting.

A Libertarian secretary of state candidate's late withdrawal (Denver Post) has also cleared the way for Republican James Wiley to appear on the ballot, potentially strengthening GOP prospects in that race.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado voters reject these tax hikes or approve them—signaling appetite for more government spending even amid economic strain.
