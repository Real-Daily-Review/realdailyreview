# Federal Judge Blocks Trump DOJ Subpoena of Minnesota Governor in Immigration Probe

A federal judge has quashed the Trump administration's effort to subpoena Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz and other state officials as part of an immigration enforcement investigation, dealing a setback to the administration's push to hold Democratic-led states accountable for obstructing federal immigration operations.

U.S. District Judge Patrick Schiltz issued a scathing 29-page ruling (Washington Examiner) characterizing the Justice Department's subpoenas as harassment and retaliation. The DOJ had sought records from Walz's office regarding Minnesota's cooperation—or lack thereof—with federal immigration enforcement during operations in the Minneapolis-St. Paul area.

The Trump administration argues that sanctuary-state policies and official obstruction of immigration enforcement undermine federal law and border security. The administration has signaled it intends to hold states accountable when they impede federal agents (Just The News).

The judge's decision reflects judicial skepticism of the DOJ's investigative scope and methods, even as the administration pursues a broader agenda of enforcing immigration law more aggressively.
