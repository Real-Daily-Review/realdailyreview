# Trump's Iran Deal Collapse Sparks Conservative Criticism

President Trump's sudden reversal on Iran negotiations has drawn sharp rebukes from both the right and center-left, with critics arguing he abandoned core positions to secure a face-saving agreement. National Review reports that Trump capitulated on "all the key issues of contention" in the dispute, reversing his previous hardline stance. Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin characterizes the deal as built on falsehoods, citing Vice President JD Vance's inflammatory rhetoric about the agreement as symptomatic of the administration's credibility problem.

Conservatives are divided. National Review's assessment suggests Trump weakened America's negotiating posture by caving to Iranian demands rather than maintaining pressure. The right had expected the administration to hold firm on nonproliferation safeguards and regional security concerns—positions Trump had championed before talks collapsed.

For Colorado, the Iran deal's terms could affect energy markets and oil prices at the pump, given Colorado's energy sector ties to global crude dynamics. Any agreement limiting Iranian sanctions relief could stabilize markets; conversely, a weak deal might embolden Tehran and destabilize the Middle East, raising energy costs for Colorado families and businesses.
