# Douglas County's Firefighting Helicopter Proves Smart Government Spending Works

Douglas County's decision to deploy its own dedicated firefighting helicopter is delivering real results when Colorado needs them most. The county now launches the aircraft for every confirmed fire, regardless of size, and crews credit the chopper with containing recent wildfires that could have spread rapidly across the dry landscape (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

Unlike most Colorado counties that depend on state and federal resources, Douglas County made the upfront investment in its own helitack crew—the only county-owned operation of its kind in the state. During this year's hot, dry conditions, that decision has paid dividends: faster response times, more aggressive containment, and fewer acres lost to uncontrolled burns.

This is government spending done right. Rather than bloating bureaucracy or funding pet projects, Douglas County invested in infrastructure that directly protects lives and property. The helicopter reduces response times and gives firefighters tools to stop fires before they explode into the catastrophic wildfires that have devastated other parts of Colorado and the West.

The model raises a question for other Colorado counties: Is a dedicated firefighting asset worth the capital investment? With wildfire season intensifying, taxpayers should watch whether other counties follow Douglas's lead—or whether state and federal agencies finally get serious about forest management and fuel reduction instead of relying on reactive firefighting.
