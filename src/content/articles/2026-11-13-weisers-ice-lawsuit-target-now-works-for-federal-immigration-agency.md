# Former Mesa County Deputy Lands at ICE After Weiser Lawsuit

A former Mesa County Sheriff's deputy disciplined for ICE collaboration has now joined the federal agency itself—a move that underscores the left's hostility toward immigration enforcement and border security cooperation.

Alexander Zwinck resigned last year after facing disciplinary action and a lawsuit from Attorney General Phil Weiser, according to the Colorado Sun. Weiser, a Democrat, had targeted Zwinck for working with federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement—the same agency progressives routinely attack as heavy-handed and discriminatory.

Conservatives argue that cooperation between local law enforcement and ICE is essential for public safety and enforcing federal immigration law. Republicans counter that Weiser's aggressive legal posture against officers assisting federal immigration authorities reflects the broader progressive resistance to immigration enforcement, even when it protects Colorado communities.

Zwinck's transition to ICE employment suggests that despite local political pressure, federal immigration work remains a viable career path for law enforcement professionals. The question for Colorado taxpayers: Will Weiser's office continue pursuing legal action against officers who cooperate with federal immigration authorities, or will this case signal a shift in the AG's approach to immigration enforcement?

Watch whether Weiser's office appeals or settles the Zwinck matter—and whether other Colorado sheriffs face similar pressure for ICE collaboration.
