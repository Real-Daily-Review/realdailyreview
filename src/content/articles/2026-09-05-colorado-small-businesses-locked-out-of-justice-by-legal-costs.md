# Colorado Small Businesses Need Real Access to Justice—Not Just Legal Theory

Colorado entrepreneurs face a rigged fight when taking on deep-pocketed corporations or government agencies: they can't afford the legal battle, even when they're right. According to the *Washington Examiner*, access to justice requires more than constitutional guarantees—it demands practical tools for individuals and small businesses to protect their rights against far better-funded opponents.

The problem is acute for Colorado's energy sector, agricultural operations, and family-owned manufacturers. When a rancher challenges federal land-use rules or a small oil-and-gas operator fights regulatory overreach, legal costs can exceed six figures before trial. Without litigation funding mechanisms, many Coloradans abandon legitimate claims simply because they can't afford the fight.

Conservatives argue that removing barriers to litigation funding—whether through third-party financing or contingency-fee reforms—levels the playing field without expanding government. It's not about creating new entitlements; it's about letting individuals and businesses exercise rights they already possess.

The left often opposes litigation funding, claiming it encourages frivolous suits, but that's backwards: Colorado's courts already have mechanisms to dismiss baseless claims. The real barrier isn't too many lawsuits—it's too few justice seekers who can afford to pursue legitimate ones.
