# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed's carefully staged apology to Jewish Democrats won't solve the party's deepening credibility crisis on antisemitism. El-Sayed met with Michigan's Democratic Jewish Caucus to walk back remarks, but stopped short of distancing himself from Hasan Piker, the streamer accused of antisemitic rhetoric and anti-American extremism (Fox News Politics). 

The problem runs deeper than one candidate's damage control. According to the Washington Examiner, only six Democrats have explicitly condemned Piker's latest antisemitic rant—a damning indictment of party-wide silence. The Examiner notes Piker has been credibly described as "America-hating," "Mao-loving," and a "terrorist-cheererer," yet most Democratic leaders have offered no public rebuke.

This matters for Colorado. As the state's Democratic establishment tightens its grip on state politics, voters should ask whether tolerance for radical voices and antisemitic rhetoric will become normalized here too. The pattern is clear: when confronted, Democrats offer theatrical apologies while maintaining relationships with extremists.

Republicans argue this reveals the Left's hollow commitment to fighting antisemitism when it conflicts with ideological solidarity. The silence from national Democratic leaders is deafening.

**Watch whether Colorado Democratic leaders—from Gov. Polis on down—will be asked to take a clear stand on antisemitism and extremism, or whether they'll follow the national party's lead of performative gestures and strategic silence.**
