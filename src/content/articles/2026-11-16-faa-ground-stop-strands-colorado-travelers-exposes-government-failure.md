# FAA Meltdown Grounds Colorado Flights; System Failures Raise Questions About Government Competence

A nationwide FAA ground stop Friday night left Denver International Airport and travelers across Colorado stranded, with hundreds of flights delayed or canceled before operations resumed Saturday. The outage, blamed on a telecommunications failure, grounded aircraft for over four hours and exposed what conservatives argue is yet another example of government infrastructure mismanagement.

By Monday, major airlines had largely recovered—the Daily Wire reports delays and cancellations plummeted to 8,681 delays and 547 cancellations, down sharply from Sunday's 26,440 delays and 2,860 cancellations. But the damage to Colorado's travel-dependent economy and tourism sector was real.

**The conservative concern:** Republicans and taxpayers are asking hard questions about FAA readiness and redundancy. The nation's air traffic control system, operated by a federal bureaucracy, failed to prevent a single telecom outage from paralyzing the entire country. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin attributed the stop to the telecommunications failure, but conservatives argue this highlights why government monopolies on critical infrastructure are dangerous—private sector competition would have built in failsafes.

For Colorado, DIA is a major economic engine. Weekend travel chaos rippled through hotels, rental car companies, and restaurants statewide.
