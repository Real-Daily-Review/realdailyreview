# Colorado GOP Picks Unconventional Nominee in Tight Governor's Race

Colorado Republicans have nominated Victor Marx, a nonprofit leader and first-time candidate, as their gubernatorial standard-bearer—a choice that signals the party's appetite for outsider politics even as the primary remained extraordinarily close. Marx edged out state Sen. Barb Kirkmeyer by roughly 2,000 votes in a race that stayed too tight to call for days while voters corrected deficient ballots, according to the Denver Post.

The outcome reflects a dramatic winnowing: of 18 Republican gubernatorial candidates who competed at the state assembly, only two advanced to the primary ballot, per Complete Colorado. That narrow funnel underscores how Colorado's GOP assembly process—designed to elevate consensus candidates—produced a contentious result anyway.

Marx's victory comes despite what the Denver Post describes as a "controversial personal history" and an "unorthodox campaign," suggesting he ran against the establishment favorite Kirkmeyer, a veteran legislator. Republicans backing Marx appear to have prioritized fresh blood and outsider credibility over legislative experience—a bet that could energize the base or prove risky against a Democratic incumbent in a purple state.

Conservatives should watch whether Marx can consolidate GOP support and articulate a clear contrast on taxes, energy policy, and government overreach heading into the general election.
