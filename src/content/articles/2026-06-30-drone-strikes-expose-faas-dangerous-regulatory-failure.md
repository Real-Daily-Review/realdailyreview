# Drone Strikes on Aircraft Expose Dangerous Regulatory Vacuum in Airspace

Federal regulators have allowed recreational drone proliferation to create a clear and present danger to commercial aviation, with a JetBlue Airbus A321 struck by a drone while landing at JFK and a helicopter pilot reporting a near-miss hours later the same day (9News Denver, New York Post).

The incidents underscore what conservatives have long warned: the FAA's permissive drone licensing regime prioritizes tech-industry expansion over public safety. While the agency has issued tens of thousands of recreational drone permits, enforcement remains spotty and penalties insufficient to deter reckless operators.

Republicans and aviation safety advocates argue the FAA must tighten airspace restrictions near major airports and impose meaningful penalties—including criminal charges—for operators who endanger manned aircraft. The current regulatory framework treats drones as toys rather than potential weapons, they contend.

Democrats and drone-industry backers counter that blanket restrictions stifle innovation and that most operators follow rules responsibly. They emphasize the incidents remain rare relative to total drone flights.

For Colorado, the concern extends beyond Denver International Airport. As drone delivery services and commercial operators expand across the state, lax federal oversight could invite similar near-misses over populated areas and critical infrastructure.

Watch whether Congress demands FAA reform or if regulators tighten enforcement without legislative action.
