# Government Grocery Stores Face Legal Challenge Over Unfair Competition

New York City's experiment with taxpayer-funded grocery stores is colliding with reality: small business owners say government-run competition is crushing them.

A coalition of immigrant-owned grocers and bodega operators filed suit against Mayor Zohran Mamdani's plan to open city-owned stores with artificially discounted prices, arguing the scheme violates antitrust law and amounts to predatory pricing (Washington Examiner). The Multicultural Business Coalition, led by Kenneth Roldan, says the subsidized stores will undercut independent operators who built their businesses without government handouts.

The plan's math is worse than it looks. Fox News reports NYC taxpayers could pay twice—once through direct subsidies for the government stores, and again through grants to independent grocers meant to help them "compete" against the city's own operations. It's corporate welfare masquerading as food justice.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Government shouldn't use taxpayer dollars to undercut private businesses. When politicians can't solve a problem through free markets, they create a bigger one by throwing public money at it. Small business owners—many immigrants who built their enterprises from scratch—shouldn't be sacrificed on the altar of progressive policy theater.

**Colorado conservatives should watch whether similar schemes gain traction here.** Expect progressive activists to pitch "community-owned" or "municipal" alternatives to private enterprise in Colorado's cities—and demand scrutiny of the true costs before taxpayers foot the bill.
