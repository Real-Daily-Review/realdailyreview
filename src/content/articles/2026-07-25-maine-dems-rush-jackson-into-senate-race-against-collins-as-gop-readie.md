# Maine Democrats Scramble With Last-Minute Senate Nominee as Collins Race Tightens

Maine Democrats have crowned Troy Jackson, a fifth-generation logger and former state Senate president, as their emergency replacement for imploded nominee Graham Platner—betting a fresh face can unseat Republican incumbent Susan Collins in a race now rated a toss-up.

Jackson, selected Saturday at a state convention in Bangor, immediately pivots to a hard-left platform: Medicare for all and abolishing ICE. Republicans aren't waiting. The GOP Leadership Fund has already vowed a $42 million assault, with operatives warning Jackson carries baggage eerily similar to Platner's collapse—citing what they describe as "volatile personality" and "anger issues with women" (Daily Caller).

The 101-day sprint to November reveals Democratic desperation. Platner's sudden withdrawal left Maine's Senate race in chaos, and Jackson's nomination represents a Hail Mary to salvage a seat Democrats see as winnable. Fox News reports the race remains genuinely competitive, though Collins has proven resilient in Maine's purple terrain.

For Colorado conservatives, this is a cautionary tale: even in competitive states, radical progressive platforms—socialized medicine, open-borders rhetoric—struggle when scrutinized. Watch whether Jackson's Medicare-for-all messaging and ICE abolition rhetoric gain traction or become anchors in a state where working-class voters still value border security and fiscal restraint.
