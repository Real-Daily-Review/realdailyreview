# British PM Burnham's Anti-Business Agenda Should Concern Colorado Energy Sector

Andy Burnham has become Britain's new prime minister after Keir Starmer's resignation, and his track record signals trouble for free-market economies on both sides of the Atlantic—including Colorado's energy interests.

National Review warns that Burnham brings "inexplicable nostalgia for the business-starving policies of the 1970s," a red flag for anyone who remembers stagflation and economic stagnation. His appointment matters to Colorado conservatives because Britain's regulatory direction often foreshadows progressive policy experiments Washington Democrats try to import stateside.

Just The News reports Burnham met with King Charles III to formally assume office after Starmer's Monday resignation. The Washington Examiner notes Burnham is already strategizing how to manage relations with the Trump administration—a sign he understands the ideological gulf between his interventionist approach and America's incoming pro-growth leadership.

Conservatives argue that Burnham's 1970s-style statism—higher taxes, stricter business regulation, and government-directed industrial policy—will strangle British enterprise and serve as a cautionary tale for Colorado voters watching similar proposals bubble up in Democratic circles locally.

The real test: whether Burnham's failures abroad embolden or chill progressive activists pushing similar policies in Colorado's legislature.
