# Former House Speaker Dennis Hastert, GOP's Longest-Serving Speaker, Dead at 84

Dennis Hastert, who held the House speakership longer than any Republican in U.S. history, has died at 84 from natural causes at his Illinois home, according to the Kendall County Coroner's Office.

Hastert represented Illinois's 14th District from 1987 until his 2007 resignation and served eight years as Speaker—a tenure that made him one of the most powerful Republicans of his era. Yet his legacy remains complicated: he became the highest-ranking U.S. official ever imprisoned for federal crimes, serving time on bank fraud charges tied to hush-money payments (Fox News).

His fall from power stands as a cautionary tale for the Republican establishment. Once a fixture of GOP leadership during the Bush years, Hastert's criminal conviction underscored how personal misconduct can obliterate even the most senior political careers—a lesson that resonates as Republicans navigate questions about character and accountability in their own ranks.

Conservatives have long debated how to reckon with figures whose legislative records diverge sharply from their personal conduct. Hastert's death closes a chapter on a complicated Republican figure whose speakership achievements are now inseparable from his criminal past.
