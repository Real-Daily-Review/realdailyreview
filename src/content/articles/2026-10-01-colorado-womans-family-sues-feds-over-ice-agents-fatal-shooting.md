# Colorado Woman's Family Sues Federal Government Over ICE Agent's Fatal Shooting

A Colorado resident's death at the hands of a federal immigration officer has sparked dual lawsuits against the U.S. government—raising hard questions about accountability when government agents use lethal force.

Renee Good, who lived in Colorado, was shot and killed by an ICE agent during immigration enforcement operations in Minneapolis. Her partner, Becca Good, and brother, Brent Ganger, have now filed two separate lawsuits: one alleging wrongful death and another charging a conspiracy to interfere with civil rights (Just The News, 9News Denver).

The case underscores a conservative concern about federal overreach and insufficient accountability mechanisms within immigration enforcement. When unelected bureaucrats wield deadly force with minimal transparency or consequence, taxpayers foot the bill for litigation while families seek justice. Republicans have long argued that federal agencies require stronger oversight and that officers—regardless of agency—must face genuine consequences for misconduct.

The lawsuits name the U.S. government and ICE officers directly, forcing the federal government to defend its agents' conduct in court. Whether the suits succeed or fail, Colorado families watching this case will note that federal power, once exercised, is difficult to constrain.

Watch whether the Biden administration settles or fights these claims—and what that decision reveals about how seriously it takes accountability within its own agencies.
