# Inflation Eases, but Colorado Families Still Paying More Than Before Biden's Energy Chaos

Consumer prices rose just 3.4% year-over-year in July—down from 3.5% in June—as gas, prescription drugs, and grocery costs declined, according to the Department of Labor (Breitbart). The monthly increase was a modest 0.1%, suggesting inflation's grip is loosening after months of elevated pressure (9News Denver).

But here's the catch: prices remain stubbornly higher than they were before the Iran war began in February, meaning Colorado families are still paying more at the pump and grocery store than they were months ago. For energy-dependent Colorado, the cooling gas prices offer some relief—but only after years of Biden-Harris administration policies that restricted domestic oil and gas development, driving up costs in the first place.

Conservatives argue the administration's anti-fossil-fuel agenda and regulatory overreach created the inflationary environment that squeezed working families. While Democrats will claim credit for the recent cooling trend, Republicans point out that inflation never should have spiked this high if energy policy had prioritized American production instead of green mandates.

The real test: whether this cooling trend holds through the fall election season, or whether geopolitical shocks and continued government spending reignite price pressures that hurt Colorado's working class and fixed-income seniors.
