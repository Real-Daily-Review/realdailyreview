# Federal Justice System Moves to Try Haitian President's Assassins on U.S. Soil

The Department of Justice is bringing 18 suspects stateside to face trial for the 2021 assassination of Haitian President Jovenel Moïse, marking a significant assertion of federal jurisdiction in a high-profile international crime.

According to the Washington Examiner, the suspects are being transported to the United States via military plane after 30 individuals were indicted in connection with Moïse's killing. U.S. Attorney Jason Reding Quiñones (Southern District of Florida) stated the DOJ will "follow the evidence wherever it leads," signaling a commitment to prosecuting the case through the American criminal justice system rather than leaving it to Haiti's notoriously weak institutions (Just The News).

**The conservative angle:** This move reflects appropriate federal authority—prosecuting serious international crimes that touch U.S. interests and security. However, it also underscores the broader instability in Haiti, a failed state that has become a source of migration pressure on America's southern border and a haven for criminal networks. Republicans have long argued that border security and immigration enforcement require addressing root causes of instability in the Western Hemisphere, not simply managing the symptoms at the U.S. line.

Conservatives should monitor whether this prosecution becomes a model for holding foreign actors accountable, or whether the Biden administration uses it as political theater while ignoring the larger Haiti crisis fueling illegal migration.
