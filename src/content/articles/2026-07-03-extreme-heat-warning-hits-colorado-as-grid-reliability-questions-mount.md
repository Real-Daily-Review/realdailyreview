# Colorado Braces for Extreme Heat as Nation Celebrates Independence Day

A dangerous heat wave is bearing down on Colorado and 22 other states this Fourth of July weekend, with the National Weather Service placing seven states entirely under extreme heat warnings (9News Denver). While Americans prepare holiday celebrations, Coloradans should expect dangerous temperatures that could strain the power grid, spike energy costs, and pose real risks to outdoor workers and vulnerable populations across the state.

The timing coincides with the nation's 250th anniversary celebrations. Former President Trump is set to commemorate the occasion at Mount Rushmore and the National Mall's "Salute to America" event (Fox News), while New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani will deliver remarks on the state's founding role ahead of the holiday (Washington Examiner).

For Colorado specifically, the extreme heat warning underscores ongoing concerns about grid reliability during peak demand periods—a challenge compounded by the state's aggressive transition away from reliable baseload power. Energy costs could spike as air conditioning demand surges, hitting Colorado families and small businesses already facing inflationary pressures.

Conservatives argue that Colorado's rush to shutter coal plants and restrict natural gas development leaves the state vulnerable to exactly these scenarios, while the left insists renewable energy expansion is the long-term solution.
