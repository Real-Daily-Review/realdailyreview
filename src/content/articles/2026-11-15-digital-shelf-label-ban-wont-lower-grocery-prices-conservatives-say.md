# Digital Shelf Label Ban Won't Fix Grocery Prices—Just Retail Efficiency

Colorado grocers and consumers shouldn't expect relief from a proposed ban on digital shelf labels, which would eliminate a cost-cutting technology without addressing the real drivers of inflation.

Some lawmakers are pushing to restrict electronic price displays at retail stores, framing the move as consumer protection. But conservatives argue the ban misses the mark entirely. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the actual culprits behind rising grocery costs are inflation, tariffs, and geopolitical instability—not the technology retailers use to manage inventory and adjust prices efficiently.

Digital shelf labels allow stores to reduce labor costs and streamline operations, benefits that ultimately help keep prices competitive. Banning them would force retailers to rely on manual price changes, increasing overhead without lowering what shoppers pay at checkout.

For Colorado's grocery chains and small retailers already squeezed by supply-chain disruptions and labor shortages, this regulation would add unnecessary compliance costs. Those expenses don't disappear—they get passed to consumers.

Republicans and free-market advocates say Congress should focus on the real inflation drivers: monetary policy, energy costs, and trade barriers. Banning retail technology is feel-good regulation that solves nothing while making business harder.
