# Banking Outages Expose Fragility of Digital Financial Infrastructure

Thousands of Americans—including Colorado customers—lost access to online banking Monday as major U.S. banks experienced widespread outages, raising hard questions about the resilience of critical financial systems.

Chase bore the brunt of the disruption, with Downdetector recording over 2,500 user reports at peak (The Daily Wire), while customers at other major institutions also reported login failures and inability to view account balances. According to 9News Denver, the technical failures affected "several major U.S. banks," though the full scope and cause remain unclear.

The timing is troubling. The outages coincided with separate failures at the FAA and Microsoft, suggesting either cascading infrastructure vulnerabilities or a broader systemic weakness in how America's digital backbone is managed and protected (The Daily Wire).

For Colorado families and small-business owners dependent on reliable access to their accounts—especially those managing payroll, bill payments, or emergency funds—Monday's blackout was more than an inconvenience. It exposed a critical gap: banks and tech platforms have grown so complex and interconnected that a single failure can ripple across the entire financial system.

Conservatives should demand transparency: What failed? Why? And what safeguards exist to prevent the next outage from freezing millions of Americans out of their own money?

Watch whether Congress demands accountability from Chase and other banks on cybersecurity and redundancy protocols.
