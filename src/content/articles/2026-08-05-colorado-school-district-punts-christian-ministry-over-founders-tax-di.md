# Colorado School District Boots Christian Ministry Over Founder's Tax Stance

A Colorado school district has terminated a Christian student ministry's access to released-time Bible instruction, citing the founder's opposition to a tax increase and his religious views—a move that raises serious First Amendment concerns for conservatives.

According to *Just The News*, the superintendent told the school board months before the cutoff that community members objected to the founder's Facebook posts and his "particular interpretation of the Bible." The timing and stated rationale suggest viewpoint discrimination rather than legitimate educational policy.

The incident mirrors a broader pattern: progressive-controlled institutions using regulatory power to punish dissent on fiscal issues. Colorado taxpayers and parents should ask hard questions about whether public schools are weaponizing access policies against citizens who dare oppose tax hikes.

Secretary of State Jena Griswold, meanwhile, celebrated a federal court dismissing the Trump administration's lawsuit seeking access to Colorado voter rolls (CPR News). Griswold framed it as protecting "sensitive voter information"—but Republicans argue election integrity requires scrutiny of voter registration accuracy.

Both stories underscore how Colorado's Democratic leadership uses government machinery to suppress conservative speech and limit Republican oversight.
