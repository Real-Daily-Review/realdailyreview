## Feds Eyed for Transmission Project That'll Hit Your Wallet

A Colorado legislator is asking the USDA to intervene in a 28-mile electricity transmission project — and the reason conservatives should pay close attention is buried in the fine print: most of the cost gets passed directly to ratepayers. That means Colorado families and businesses foot the bill for infrastructure decisions made by regulators and utilities, not the free market. Republicans argue that when government-backed projects shift costs onto consumers without meaningful accountability, it's Coloradans who get stuck holding the tab.

## East Colfax Bus Lanes Open — But at What Cost?

Denver businesses along East Colfax are celebrating the opening of new bus-only lanes after enduring two years of construction disruption. Good news for those who survived — but conservatives should ask the harder question: who decided that dedicating scarce road lanes exclusively to buses was worth grinding a commercial corridor to a halt for two years? Small business owners along Colfax didn't choose that tradeoff. Government planners did it for them. The ribbon-cutting is nice; the lost revenue during construction was real.
