# Trump Official Warns of 'Fraudfluencers' Exploiting Social Media to Recruit Government Benefit Scammers

A Trump administration labor official is sounding the alarm on a brazen new fraud racket: social media "fraudfluencers" recruiting Americans into government benefit schemes, according to Fox News.

Department of Labor Inspector General Anthony D'Esposito issued the stark warning about coordinated scams targeting federal programs. These operators use social platforms to normalize and recruit participants into what amounts to organized theft from taxpayers.

The scheme represents a growing headache for the Trump administration as it attempts to restore integrity to federal benefit systems. Conservatives have long argued that lax oversight and inadequate fraud detection in government programs drain resources meant for legitimate recipients—a particular concern in Colorado, where state and federal benefit programs serve hundreds of thousands of residents.

Republicans view aggressive fraud prosecution as essential to restoring public trust in government assistance while protecting working Coloradans footing the bill. The administration's focus on "fraudfluencers" signals a tougher posture on benefit abuse than the previous approach.

The warning underscores how digital platforms have become vectors for organized crime against federal coffers. Taxpayers should watch whether the DOL Inspector General's office follows through with criminal referrals and prosecutions of the social media operators orchestrating these schemes.
