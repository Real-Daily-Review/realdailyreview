# Colorado's Data Centers Power the Digital Economy—But Regulators Keep Circling

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are critical infrastructure for the modern economy, yet they remain targets for progressive regulation and environmental scrutiny. According to *Complete Colorado*, these facilities—which process everything from cloud computing to AI workloads—are "what makes" modern life function, not obstacles to it.

The conservative case is straightforward: data centers generate jobs, tax revenue, and economic growth while enabling the digital services Coloradans depend on daily. They require reliable, affordable electricity—a reality that puts them at odds with Colorado's aggressive renewable mandates and grid-reliability concerns.

Democrats and environmental groups, however, argue data centers consume excessive water and energy, contributing to Colorado's drought stress and carbon emissions. Regulators have increasingly scrutinized power consumption and water usage, with some local governments imposing new permitting hurdles.

The tension reflects a broader Colorado divide: economic growth versus environmental activism. As *Complete Colorado* notes, dismissing data centers as "the enemy" ignores their essential role in powering everything from telehealth to financial services that Coloradans use daily.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado's Public Utilities Commission and state legislature prioritize grid reliability and economic competitiveness or double down on restrictions that could push data-center investment to Texas and other business-friendly states.
