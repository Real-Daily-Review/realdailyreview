# Jefferson County Schools Seek $133M Tax Hike as District Shrinks

Jefferson County schools are asking voters to approve a $133 million tax increase in November—a significant ask from a district that's actually losing enrollment. The Jeffco school board voted unanimously Thursday to place two measures on the ballot, with the pitch centered on teacher pay raises and facility repairs (CPR News).

The timing is notable: Jeffco is currently in federal litigation over its transgender-student policies, raising questions about priorities as the district seeks more taxpayer dollars while enrollment declines. Complete Colorado reports the district is simultaneously asking for a massive tax hike even as student numbers shrink—a pattern that troubles conservatives who argue schools should first demonstrate fiscal discipline and enrollment-based budgeting before seeking new revenue.

Republicans and taxpayer advocates typically counter that districts should trim administrative overhead, consolidate facilities matching actual enrollment, and prove stewardship of existing funds before returning to voters for more. Teacher compensation is legitimate, but not at the expense of fiscal responsibility or without addressing why per-pupil spending hasn't improved outcomes.

Denver Public Schools is also on the ballot in November with a $44 million mill levy override, with $25.8 million earmarked for employee raises (Denver Post)—suggesting a coordinated push across Colorado's largest districts.

**Watch for voter turnout and approval rates: if Colorado taxpayers reject these measures, it signals real pushback against education spending without reform.**
