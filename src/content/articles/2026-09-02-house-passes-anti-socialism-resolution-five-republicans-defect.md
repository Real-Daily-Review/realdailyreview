# House Republicans Pass Anti-Socialism Resolution Despite Handful of GOP Defections

The House passed a resolution condemning socialism and the Democratic Socialists of America platform Tuesday—a rare moment of ideological clarity in Congress, though five Republicans broke ranks to oppose the procedural rule allowing the vote.

The resolution passed 220-192-2, with eight Democrats joining Republicans in support (Just The News). The measure calls lawmakers to "condemn and denounce socialism in all its forms" and oppose socialist policy implementation in the U.S.

The defections matter. According to the Washington Examiner, Reps. Andy Harris (R-MD), Clay Higgins (R-LA), Ralph Norman (R-SC), and others voted against the rule—a procedural move that signals deeper fractures within GOP ranks on messaging priorities.

For Colorado conservatives, this vote reflects a national divide over whether Republicans will aggressively counter the left's socialist drift or retreat into procedural timidity. Colorado has seen progressive policies reshape its tax code, energy sector, and gun laws over the past decade—all justified through left-wing economic frameworks. A House resolution is symbolic; what matters is whether Republicans will translate anti-socialism rhetoric into actual legislative resistance to the regulatory state and spending expansions that Colorado taxpayers fund.
