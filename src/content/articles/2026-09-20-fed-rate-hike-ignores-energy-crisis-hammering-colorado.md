# Fed Rate Hike Masks Real Problem: Energy Inflation Hammering Colorado Families

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh's latest quarter-point rate increase ignores the elephant in the room—skyrocketing energy costs that are crushing Colorado households and businesses.

While the Fed points to overall August inflation of 3.4% and core inflation of 2.4%, the real story is buried in the details: energy prices jumped 16.3% year-over-year, with gasoline up 27.4% (Washington Examiner). Gasoline alone accounted for more than one-third of the inflation spike, yet the Fed's blunt-instrument rate hike treats all inflation equally.

Conservatives argue this approach is backward. As National Review notes, rising prices are not an inevitable byproduct of a strong economy—they reflect policy failures upstream. Colorado ranchers, truckers, and working families already squeezed by inflation at the pump don't need higher borrowing costs; they need energy policy that increases supply and lowers costs.

The real culprit? Years of anti-energy regulation and underinvestment in domestic production. Colorado's energy sector, which generates billions in state revenue and supports thousands of jobs, has been hamstrung by federal overreach. Rate hikes won't fix that; unleashing American energy production will.

Taxpayers should watch whether the Fed's next moves acknowledge energy as the primary inflation driver—or whether Warsh continues treating the symptom while ignoring the disease.
