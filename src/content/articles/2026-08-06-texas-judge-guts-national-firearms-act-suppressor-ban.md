# Federal Judge Strikes Down Decades-Old Suppressor Restrictions in Major Second Amendment Win

A federal judge in Texas has dealt a decisive blow to one of America's most outdated gun regulations, ruling that the National Firearms Act's restrictions on suppressors and certain short-barreled firearms are unconstitutional.

U.S. District Judge James Hendrix found that Congress lost its constitutional authority to enforce these provisions after eliminating the tax that originally justified the restrictions nearly a century ago. "The regulatory provisions must be enjoined," the judge ruled, according to the Daily Caller.

**The Colorado angle:** This decision matters for Colorado gun owners who have long chafed under federal red tape that treats suppressors like contraband. Second Amendment advocates argue these devices reduce hearing damage and noise pollution—practical benefits that have nothing to do with criminal activity. The ruling removes a regulatory relic that treated suppressors as if they were mob-era weapons rather than hearing protection.

Republicans and gun-rights groups hail the decision as a vindication of constitutional limits on federal power. The left typically argues such regulations protect public safety, though critics note the suppressor ban has survived decades without evidence it prevents crime.

**Watch next:** Whether the Biden-Harris administration appeals this ruling or whether other federal courts follow suit in striking down similar outdated firearms restrictions.
