# Ship Attacked in Hormuz as Trump Asserts U.S. Control, Iran Escalates Threats

Trump's aggressive posture on the Strait of Hormuz is being tested as Iranian threats turn kinetic.

An unidentified projectile struck a commercial vessel in the Strait of Hormuz early Tuesday, damaging the engine room and killing a crew member, according to 9News Denver. The attack comes as a 60-day peace negotiation window expires—and as President Trump has doubled down on declaring the strategic waterway U.S. territory, asserting Washington maintains "total control" of the passage (Breitbart).

Iran has threatened military action to break what it calls a U.S. naval blockade and signaled a shift toward a more offensive posture. The incident underscores the fragility of Trump's deterrence strategy in one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints.

**The Colorado angle:** Disruptions to Middle Eastern oil flow drive energy prices higher—directly hitting Colorado consumers at the pump and raising costs for state businesses and agriculture. Energy sector workers in Colorado also face uncertainty as geopolitical volatility affects global markets.

Conservatives argue Trump's clear show of force and willingness to assert U.S. interests deters further Iranian aggression. Critics worry escalation could spiral into broader conflict.

Watch whether Iran sustains attacks or whether Trump's naval presence forces de-escalation—and how oil prices respond.
