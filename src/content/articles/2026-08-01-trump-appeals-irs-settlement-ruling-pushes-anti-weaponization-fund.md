# Trump Fights Court Ruling on IRS 'Anti-Weaponization' Fund

The Trump administration is appealing a federal judge's decision blocking a $10 billion lawsuit settlement designed to compensate alleged victims of IRS political targeting—a move that underscores the ongoing legal battle over government agency accountability.

U.S. District Judge Kathleen Williams ruled the lawsuit was brought for an "improper purpose" and questioned whether Congress had authorized using settlement funds to create an "anti-weaponization" initiative (Washington Examiner, Just The News). The Trump administration filed to appeal the ruling Friday.

Trump has signaled he'll pursue legislative action if needed. According to Just The News, the president said he would push for an "anti-weaponization bill" if GOP senators block attorney general nominee Pam Bondi's confirmation—currently facing holdouts from Cornyn and Tillis.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans contend the IRS weaponized its enforcement authority against conservative groups and political opponents during the Obama-Biden years. An anti-weaponization fund or legislation would provide restitution and establish guardrails against future agency abuse. Conservatives view this as essential accountability for government overreach.

**Democrats counter** that the lawsuit lacks merit and that creating such a fund circumvents congressional appropriations authority.

**Watch:** Whether Senate Republicans unite behind either Bondi's confirmation or standalone anti-weaponization legislation—a test of GOP resolve on restraining federal agencies.
