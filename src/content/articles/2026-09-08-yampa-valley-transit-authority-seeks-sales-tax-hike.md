# Yampa Valley Transit Authority Pushes Sales Tax Hike on November Ballot

A fledgling transit authority in northwest Colorado is asking voters to approve a new sales tax to fund basic operations—the latest example of government agencies turning to tax increases rather than efficiency reforms.

The Yampa Valley transit authority, still in its startup phase, is sending a sales tax measure to the November ballot (Complete Colorado). The move reflects a familiar pattern: new government entities immediately seeking revenue rather than proving they can operate within existing budgets.

Conservatives argue that transit authorities nationwide routinely underestimate costs and overestimate ridership, then return for tax hikes when projections fail. Before Yampa Valley voters approve new taxes, they should demand detailed operational plans, realistic ridership forecasts, and accountability measures—not blank checks.

The timing matters for Colorado's energy-dependent northwest region. Routt County and surrounding areas depend on oil and gas jobs and tourism; new taxes on sales hit workers and small businesses directly. Voters should ask whether this transit system serves genuine community need or represents another layer of government bureaucracy.

Watch whether the Yampa Valley authority releases transparent cost projections and ridership assumptions before November—or whether it expects voters to fund operations on faith alone.
