# Bipartisan Housing Bill Passes, But Conservatives Question Whether It Actually Fixes Affordability

President Trump and Senator Elizabeth Warren's unlikely alliance produced the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act—a rare bipartisan moment that sailed through Congress. But conservatives are pumping the brakes.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the bill, despite its cross-party backing, may be more political theater than real relief for Colorado families priced out of the market. The concern: the legislation focuses on construction standards and regulatory tweaks rather than addressing the root causes of housing scarcity—zoning restrictions, permitting delays, and government-imposed building codes that inflate costs.

Republicans and conservatives argue that true affordability requires gutting local zoning laws that prevent new housing supply and cutting red tape that makes construction prohibitively expensive. The ROAD Act's approach, they contend, tinkers around the edges while leaving the regulatory architecture intact.

For Colorado specifically, where Denver's median home price has soared and mountain communities face acute housing shortages, the distinction matters. State and local governments have resisted aggressive zoning reform, and this federal bill doesn't force their hand.

Taxpayers and housing advocates should watch whether Colorado's legislature uses this moment to pursue genuine deregulation—or settles for the federal government's incremental approach.
