# Six Korean War Soldiers Identified After 70 Years; Nation Honors the Fallen

America's commitment to bringing home its war dead has finally borne fruit for six families who waited seven decades for answers. The Defense POW/MIA Office (DPAA) has identified six U.S. soldiers missing from the Korean War, including two teenagers who died as prisoners of war or in combat, according to Fox News and the New York Post.

The identifications represent a hard-won victory for military families and the dedicated personnel at DPAA who continue the painstaking work of accounting for America's missing servicemen. Among those identified were young men who never made it home—a sobering reminder of the cost of the Korean conflict, which claimed roughly 37,000 American lives and left thousands unaccounted for.

The DPAA's success in these identifications underscores the importance of sustained, well-funded efforts to honor our military obligations. For conservative Coloradans who value service and sacrifice, these identifications affirm that America does not abandon its warriors, even when decades pass.

The families of these six soldiers can finally lay their loved ones to rest with dignity and closure—a fundamental duty the nation owes to those who gave everything.
