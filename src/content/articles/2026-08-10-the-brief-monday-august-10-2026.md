# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, August 10, 2026

Colorado's government class never takes a summer break — and neither do the forces in Washington shaping your taxes, your rights, and your community. Here's what matters this Monday morning.

---

## Lakewood Council Punishes Voters for Using Their Own Initiative Rights

Lakewood residents who exercised their right to petition government through a citizens' initiative on zoning are now facing new procedural burdens piled on by the city council — and some locals are calling it exactly what it looks like: retaliation. The April special election vote on zoning apparently stung the council enough to make future citizen-led efforts harder. This is the playbook of local government everywhere: when voters don't deliver the "right" answer, make it more expensive and complicated to ask the question again. Colorado conservatives should be watching every city council in the state for this brand of quiet power grab.

---

## Trump Names Will Scharf as Next White House Counsel

President Trump tapped White House Staff Secretary Will Scharf to replace David Warrington as White House Counsel starting in September. Scharf is one of the lawyers credited with securing Trump's landmark Supreme Court immunity victory — a ruling with lasting implications for executive power and the limits of politically motivated prosecutions. For Colorado conservatives who watched the left weaponize the legal system against Trump for years, putting a proven legal fighter in the West Wing's top law job is a signal that the administration intends to stay on offense.

---

## Michigan Democrats Nominate a Senate Candidate With Radical Ties — and the Media Yawns

Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic nominee in Michigan's U.S. Senate race, is working overtime to distance himself from far-left streamer Hasan Piker — a man who has suggested America deserved the 9/11 attacks. Republican Peter Laffin said plainly that questioning El-Sayed's ties to Piker is not Islamophobic; it's basic political vetting. Democrats counter that the scrutiny is unfair. Conservatives say: if a Republican Senate candidate had ties to anyone who cheered 9/11, it would be the only story on every network. Colorado voters should note how the national Democratic Party continues to normalize its radical fringe.

## Statue of Liberty Boat Tragedy: Woman and Baby Dead

A 22-foot Bayliner speedboat capsized near Liberty Island around 10:25 p.m. Saturday, killing a woman and a baby, according to the U.S. Coast Guard. The investigation is ongoing. No political angle needed here — this is a tragedy, full stop. Our thoughts are with the families.

## Ben Jones, 'Dukes of Hazzard' Star and Congressman, Dead at 84

Ben Jones — best known as Cooter from "The Dukes of Hazzard" and later as a U.S. congressman from Georgia — died Sunday after a major heart attack, his wife announced. Jones was a rare figure: a Democrat who represented rural, working-class Americans at a time when that still meant something different than it does today, and a man who never forgot where he came from. He was 84. Rest in peace.

Keep your eyes on Lakewood — if the council's new initiative burdens survive legal or political challenge, expect other Front Range cities run by progressive majorities to copy the tactic. And as the Michigan Senate race heats up, watch whether Colorado's own Democratic candidates are pressed on their ties to the party's increasingly radical national base — or whether the state's media gives them the same free pass the national press is handing El-Sayed.
