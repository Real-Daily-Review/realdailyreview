# California's Nanny-State Booster Seat Law Shows Why Colorado Must Guard Against Regulatory Creep

California just proved that progressive governments never stop micromanaging family decisions—now they're mandating booster seats for teenagers up to age 16 unless kids pass an arbitrary "seat belt fit test."

Assemblymember Lori Wilson's bill requires minors to meet five criteria before graduating out of booster seats or sitting in front seats, according to Fox News Politics. Violators face $500 fines, turning parents into lawbreakers for letting their 15-year-old ride normally.

Republicans and conservatives argue this exemplifies the progressive impulse to replace parental judgment with government diktat. Parents—not Sacramento bureaucrats—have always decided when their children are ready for adult seating. The law treats teenagers like toddlers and assumes California knows child development better than families do.

Democrats claim the measure improves safety, but conservatives counter that this is regulatory mission creep dressed up as child protection. It's the same playbook: identify a sympathetic concern, ignore parental authority, impose one-size-fits-all rules, and fine non-compliance.

**Colorado angle:** While California leads the charge, Colorado lawmakers should resist importing this paternalism. Colorado families don't need Denver telling them when their teenagers can sit in front seats.

Watch whether Colorado legislators propose copycat legislation—and whether gun owners and homeschoolers recognize this as the same government-knows-best mentality threatening their freedoms.
