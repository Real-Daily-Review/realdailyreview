# Major Media Merger Stalls as California AG Blocks Paramount-Warner Bros. Deal

California's aggressive antitrust crusade is freezing one of Hollywood's biggest consolidation plays. Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery agreed Friday to delay their merger for up to 10 months while a federal court examines whether the deal violates antitrust law—a lawsuit spearheaded by California Attorney General Rob Bonta (Washington Examiner). The companies won't close the transaction until at least five days after the lawsuit resolves or June 1, 2027, whichever comes first (Just The News).

A federal judge had already issued a temporary restraining order blocking the deal, and that order was extended Thursday (Breitbart), giving Bonta's office more runway to argue the merger would harm competition in media and entertainment.

**The conservative concern:** California's attorney general is using antitrust enforcement as a cudgel against business consolidation, raising questions about whether litigation is driven by genuine competition concerns or progressive hostility toward large corporations. The delay imposes real costs on shareholders and management while the case grinds through courts.

Colorado investors and media consumers should monitor whether this precedent emboldens other state AGs—including Colorado's—to weaponize antitrust law against routine business deals.
