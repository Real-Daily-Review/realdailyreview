# One Illinois Lottery Winner Takes Home $1 Billion—A Reminder That Government Gambling Schemes Prey on Hope, Not Opportunity

An Illinois resident won the Powerball jackpot worth $1.04 billion this week, though the lump sum value before taxes came to $433.1 million (9News Denver). The odds of winning: 1 in 292,201,338 (New York Post).

Here's what conservatives should remember: state lotteries are regressive taxes on people who can least afford them. While the Illinois winner beat astronomical odds, millions of ordinary Americans—many in Colorado—spend money they don't have chasing a dream that will never materialize. States market these games aggressively, knowing full well that lower-income households spend a disproportionate share of their earnings on tickets.

Colorado's lottery generates revenue for parks and education, but that's government-speak for "we'd rather fund priorities through gambling than honest taxation or spending discipline." It's easier to sell lottery tickets than to cut bloated budgets or lower taxes.

The real story isn't the one-in-300-million winner. It's the hundreds of millions in tickets sold to people who needed that money for rent, groceries, or savings.

Conservatives should ask: why does Colorado continue marketing a game rigged against working families when the state could simply cut spending and let people keep their own money?
