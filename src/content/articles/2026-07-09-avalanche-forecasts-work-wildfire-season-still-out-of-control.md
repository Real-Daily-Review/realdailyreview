# Colorado's Avalanche Forecasting Works—But Wildfire Season Rages On

Colorado's avalanche warning system is doing its job: a record-breaking snow cycle this season produced virtually no deaths, with 92% of backcountry travelers adjusting plans based on forecasts from the Colorado Avalanche Information Center, according to the *Colorado Sun*. That's effective public safety messaging working as intended.

The good news ends there. The Ferris Fire has now burned over 60,000 acres and ranks among Colorado's 10 largest wildfires in state history—the second such mega-fire this summer alone, *9News* reports. While avalanche forecasting relies on voluntary compliance and sound science, wildfire management involves federal land policy, forest management practices, and resource allocation that conservatives argue have been hamstrung by environmental restrictions and underfunding of preventive thinning.

The contrast is instructive: when government provides clear, actionable information without mandates, Coloradans respond responsibly. When federal agencies manage forests under layers of environmental litigation and bureaucratic caution, catastrophic fires follow. Republicans have long argued that active forest management—including controlled burns and selective logging—prevents the tinderbox conditions that fuel these megafires.

Colorado taxpayers and property owners in the wildland-urban interface should watch whether state and federal officials finally embrace aggressive forest thinning or continue blaming weather while fires consume record acreage.
