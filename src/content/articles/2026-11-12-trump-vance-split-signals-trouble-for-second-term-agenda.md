# Trump-Vance Split on Major Policy Signals Trouble for Second Term

President Trump and Vice President Vance are diverging on a "hugely important policy question," according to National Review, a fissure that threatens to undermine the administration's ability to execute its agenda during a critical window.

The disagreement comes as Trump signals openness to amnesty negotiations with Democrats—a move conservatives view with deep skepticism. National Review notes it's "hard to see how there'd be any deal in the offing with Democrats," reflecting Republican concerns that any compromise on immigration would betray Trump's core campaign promise to enforce the border and deport illegal aliens.

For Colorado, where illegal immigration strains schools, hospitals, and law enforcement budgets, any softening on deportations or legalization would directly affect state and local taxpayers already bearing the cost of sanctuary policies in Denver and Boulder.

The Trump-Vance rift also extends to international affairs. Reports of a heated phone call between Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu over Iran policy (Washington Examiner) suggest the administration is struggling to maintain unified messaging on critical foreign-policy fronts.

Conservatives should watch whether Vance—positioned as the administration's immigration hardliner—can rein in Trump's dealmaking instincts or whether the VP becomes a figurehead sidelined on core issues.
