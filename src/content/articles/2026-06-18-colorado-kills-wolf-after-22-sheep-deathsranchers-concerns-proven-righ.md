# Colorado Parks and Wildlife Kills Wolf After Livestock Predation—Ranchers' Concerns Vindicated

Colorado Parks and Wildlife killed a wolf this week after the animal allegedly killed 22 sheep, vindicating ranchers' long-standing warnings about predation costs when wolves roam near working lands.

The wolf, born in 2024, had become a symbol in Colorado's intensifying divide over predator reintroduction. Environmental activists—some calling the animal "Freedom" or "Lucky"—celebrated its survival as a win for wildlife restoration. One supporter even commissioned a ballet about the wolf (Colorado Sun). But for ranchers and rural Coloradans, the kill count told a different story: wolves don't coexist peacefully with livestock operations.

Colorado Parks and Wildlife's decision to remove the animal reflects the hard reality conservatives have long argued: predator reintroduction policies, however well-intentioned, impose real costs on agricultural communities. When a single wolf kills 22 sheep—a substantial loss for any operation—the burden falls on families, not on the urban progressives cheering from Denver.

Republicans and ranching advocates have consistently warned that wolf management requires balancing wildlife interests with the livelihoods of those who steward Colorado's land. This killing underscores that balance cannot be ignored indefinitely.

Watch whether Colorado Parks and Wildlife faces pressure to adopt stricter lethal-removal protocols or, conversely, whether environmental groups push for expanded protections that further constrain ranchers' ability to protect their herds.
