# Frontier Airlines Adds First Class—Colorado's Budget Carrier Bets on Premium Comfort

Denver-based Frontier Airlines is betting that even budget-conscious flyers will pay for comfort, rolling out new first-class seating and extra-legroom cabins starting March 2027.

The ultra-low-cost carrier, headquartered in the Denver metro area, announced the cabin reconfiguration Tuesday as part of a broader push to compete with legacy carriers on amenities. The new tiers will include enhanced first-class seating, extra-legroom options, and in-seat power—features that have become table stakes for airlines chasing higher-margin passengers (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

For Colorado travelers and the Denver International Airport ecosystem, this matters: Frontier is one of DIA's largest carriers and a major employer in the region. The move signals confidence in post-pandemic demand and reflects competitive pressure to capture premium leisure and business travel.

The reconfiguration also suggests Frontier sees room to boost revenue per seat without abandoning its low-cost model—a smart play in an economy where consumers are increasingly willing to trade up for comfort, even if they won't pay full legacy-carrier prices.

Watch whether this cabin refresh translates to actual profitability gains or simply cannibalizes existing bookings.
