# Bear Attack Closes Jefferson County Park as Wildlife Encroachment Worsens

Colorado Parks and Wildlife shut down Apex Park in Jefferson County after a bear attacked a hiker Sunday, marking the state's first reported bear attack of 2026—a warning sign that Colorado's wildlife management challenges are intensifying.

The woman was hiking when a bear grabbed her backpack and made contact with her leg, according to CPW reports (9News Denver). Officials cite a dry, warm winter as the culprit: bears are ranging farther into neighborhoods and recreational areas searching for food, forcing closures that restrict Coloradans' access to public lands.

This incident underscores a broader problem conservatives have long flagged—that Colorado's expanding bear population and habitat overlap with human activity demand smarter management, not just reactive closures. While environmental groups often resist culling or population controls, residents and outdoor enthusiasts pay the price in lost access and safety risks.

The closure is temporary, but the pattern is not. As bear sightings climb and climate conditions shift wildlife behavior, Colorado faces a choice: proactive wildlife management that balances conservation with public safety, or continued band-aid responses that shrink recreational opportunity for taxpayers who fund these lands.
