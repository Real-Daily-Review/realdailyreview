# Trump Meets Iranian Officials as Tensions Escalate

President Trump is simultaneously threatening military action and opening diplomatic channels with Iran, signaling a high-stakes gambit to either force Tehran to the negotiating table or prepare for conflict.

At the United Nations, Trump warned he may be forced to choose between a deal or the "annihilation" of Iran (Just The News). Hours later, U.S. officials met with an Iranian delegation for three hours in New York City, according to reports. Trump defended his confrontational posture at the U.N., arguing he has shown "fortitude" while other leaders have "equivocated" on Iran policy (9News Denver).

The dual approach—maximum pressure rhetoric paired with back-channel talks—reflects Trump's negotiating style: create leverage through credible military threat, then use diplomacy to extract concessions. Republicans argue this strategy worked during Trump's first term, when the administration withdrew from the Iran nuclear deal and imposed sanctions that constrained Tehran's regional ambitions.

Democrats and foreign policy critics counter that military threats undermine diplomatic efforts and risk miscalculation. They argue Trump's unpredictability destabilizes global markets and alliances.
