# Supreme Court Backs Trump's Power to Fire Rogue Regulators—A Win for Presidential Authority

The Supreme Court has handed President Trump a major victory, upholding his authority to fire FTC Commissioner Rebecca Slaughter and reasserting presidential control over the executive branch (9News Denver). The ruling strikes down decades-old restrictions that had insulated federal regulators from removal, a shift conservatives have long championed as essential to reining in unelected bureaucrats.

Republicans argue the decision restores proper constitutional balance. For years, independent agencies have operated with near-immunity from presidential oversight, allowing career bureaucrats to impose regulations without meaningful accountability to voters. Trump's legal team contended that such independence contradicts the Constitution's vesting of executive power in the president.

Democrats counter that agency independence protects regulatory expertise from political manipulation and ensures consistent enforcement of laws like consumer protection and antitrust rules—though they offer no answer to the conservative point that unelected officials shouldn't wield unchecked power over American businesses and citizens.

The Colorado angle matters here: federal regulators have wielded outsized influence over energy development, water rights, and land use across the state. Weakening their insulation from presidential accountability could accelerate permitting for oil, gas, and mining projects that Colorado's economy depends on.

Watch whether Trump uses this authority to reshape the FTC and other agencies that have blocked Colorado energy projects or imposed costly compliance burdens on state businesses.
