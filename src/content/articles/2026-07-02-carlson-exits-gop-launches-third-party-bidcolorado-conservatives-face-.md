# Tucker Carlson Breaks With GOP, Vows to Build Third Party

Tucker Carlson's announcement that he's abandoning the Republican Party to build a third political force signals deepening fractures on the right—and raises questions about whether Colorado's conservative voters will follow.

In an interview with the Columbia Journalism Review, Carlson said he plans to "help build a third party" after leaving the GOP last month, citing frustration with what he calls a "lock-step" political establishment (Washington Examiner). "There should be a good-faith effort to figure out what benefits the country," he told the outlet, arguing that "what really matters is war and finance" (Daily Caller).

Carlson's move reflects broader conservative discontent with Republican leadership on spending, foreign policy, and economic priorities. His departure could splinter right-leaning voters in Colorado—a state where Republicans have already struggled to compete statewide in recent cycles. A viable third-party challenger could further divide the anti-establishment conservative vote, potentially benefiting Democrats in close races.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the real question is whether Carlson's new party will offer a genuine alternative focused on fiscal restraint and limited government, or whether it becomes a protest vehicle that weakens conservative influence in state and federal elections.
