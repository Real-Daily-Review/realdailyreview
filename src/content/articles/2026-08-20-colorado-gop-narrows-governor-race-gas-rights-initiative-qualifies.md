# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary to just two candidates after the state assembly, a sharp culling that reflects the party's effort to consolidate behind a unified nominee. Of 18 initial contenders, only two cleared the assembly threshold—a dramatic filtering that signals GOP determination to avoid a fractured primary heading into the general election.

Meanwhile, Colorado voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution. Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado, qualified for the ballot, according to the Colorado Secretary of State's office. The measure reflects conservative and energy-sector concerns that Colorado's regulatory environment—shaped by Democratic majorities in the legislature and progressive local governments—increasingly threatens reliable, affordable natural gas access for households and businesses.

Democrats and environmental groups argue the initiative would lock in fossil fuel dependence and undermine Colorado's clean energy transition. Republicans counter that energy reliability and affordability matter more than ideological climate mandates, and that Colorado families and manufacturers shouldn't be forced into costlier alternatives.
