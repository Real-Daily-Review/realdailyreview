# U.S. Wins 2031 Women's World Cup Hosting Rights—Colorado Could Bear Infrastructure Costs

FIFA has awarded the United States hosting duties for the 2031 Women's World Cup, with Mexico, Costa Rica, and Jamaica as co-hosts (New York Post). The decision, announced Monday in Zurich, means American cities—potentially including Denver or other Colorado venues—will shoulder significant public infrastructure and security expenses for the tournament.

While the economic development angle appeals to local boosters, conservatives should scrutinize how much taxpayer money Colorado and host cities will commit. Major sporting events routinely require stadium upgrades, transportation improvements, and security spending that often exceed initial projections, leaving local governments holding the bill long after the tournament ends.

The U.S. last hosted a World Cup in 1994. England will host the 2035 Women's World Cup, marking its first hosting duty since 1966 (9News Denver).

Coloradans should watch whether state and local officials negotiate hard on cost-sharing with FIFA and the U.S. Soccer Federation—or simply rubber-stamp another corporate-welfare sports deal that shifts expenses to taxpayers while private interests pocket the gains.
