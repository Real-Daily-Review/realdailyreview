# California's Squatter Mess Is a Warning for Colorado Property Owners

California's descent into legalized property theft should alarm every Colorado homeowner who believes the government exists to protect private property rights—not abolish them.

The family of Przemyslaw Jeziorski, a murdered UC Berkeley professor, is locked in a losing court battle to evict squatters from his home. California's permissive squatter laws have made it nearly impossible for the family to reclaim the property, according to the Washington Examiner. The state's policies effectively prioritize the "rights" of illegal occupants over legitimate owners—a cautionary tale of progressive governance run amok.

Conservatives argue that California's approach represents government abandonment of its core duty: protecting property rights and the rule of law. When courts side with trespassers over families, the incentive structure collapses. Squatters multiply. Property values plummet. Neighborhoods decay.

Colorado hasn't adopted California's extreme squatter protections, but the state's Democratic legislature has shown appetite for tenant-friendly policies that chip away at landlord and property owner authority. As housing costs rise and progressive activists push for expanded "occupancy rights," Colorado conservatives should watch closely whether similar proposals gain traction in Denver.

The lesson is stark: once government decides property rights are negotiable, owners lose everything.
