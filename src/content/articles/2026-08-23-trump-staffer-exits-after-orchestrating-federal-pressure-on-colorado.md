# White House Staffer Exits After Targeting Colorado Over Tina Peters Case

A Trump administration official has left her job following revelations that she orchestrated a federal pressure campaign against Colorado—apparently to advance the president's push to free Tina Peters, the Mesa County clerk convicted of election interference.

The staffer sent an email directing five federal agencies to a meeting where they were asked to discuss "immediate actions" they could take "with respect to Colorado," according to the Denver Post. The timing and context—coinciding with Trump's efforts to intervene in Peters' case—raised red flags about weaponizing federal resources for political purposes.

Republicans and Trump allies have argued Peters was prosecuted unfairly, but the incident exposes a troubling dynamic: using federal grant leverage and agency authority to pressure a state over a single criminal case. That's precisely the kind of executive overreach conservatives typically condemn—regardless of which party wields it.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses should be alarmed. Federal grants and support shouldn't become bargaining chips in political disputes. The staffer's departure doesn't erase the question: how many other Colorado-targeted directives remain in motion, and what's the full scope of federal agencies' involvement?

Watch whether Congress demands transparency on what those five agencies actually discussed and what "immediate actions" were proposed.
