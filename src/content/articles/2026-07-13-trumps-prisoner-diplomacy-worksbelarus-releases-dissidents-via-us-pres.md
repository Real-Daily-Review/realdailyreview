# U.S. Leverage on Political Prisoners Works—Trump's Dealmaking Proves It

American diplomatic pressure and strategic negotiations can still free political prisoners abroad, a reality underscored by the Trump administration's success in Belarus and its ripple effects globally.

Pastor Ezra Jin's case, highlighted by *National Review*, demonstrates that U.S. power remains a potent tool for advancing human freedom when deployed deliberately. More concretely, the *Washington Examiner* reports that Trump's envoys secured the release of dozens of political prisoners from Belarus after direct engagement with President Alexander Lukashenko—a transaction paired with sanctions relief on Belarusian state airline Belavia.

The approach reveals a transactional clarity: the administration identified what authoritarian regimes value and leveraged it. That lesson, the *Examiner* notes, has already traveled to Georgia, where oligarchs are taking notes on the model.

Conservatives argue this represents principled realpolitik—using America's economic and diplomatic weight to protect dissidents without the performative grandstanding that often accompanies progressive human rights rhetoric. The contrast is sharp: results over rhetoric, deals over declarations.

The strategy raises questions about consistency and scalability across other regimes holding political prisoners, particularly in China and Iran.

Watch whether the administration applies this dealmaking framework systematically to other authoritarian states—or whether it remains selective.
