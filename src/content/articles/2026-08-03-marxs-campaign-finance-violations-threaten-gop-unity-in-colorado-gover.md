# Colorado GOP Governor's Race Fractures as Marx Faces Donation Violations, Lopez Enters as Independent

Victor Marx's Republican gubernatorial bid is already under siege—facing campaign finance violations while a splinter independent candidate threatens to divide the conservative vote in 2026.

Colorado election officials allege Marx's campaign accepted more than $78,000 in improper donations from 111 people, exceeding individual contribution limits of $1,450 each, according to the Denver Post. That's 197 violations the secretary of state's office is now investigating—a serious compliance failure for a candidate positioning himself as an outsider reformer.

Meanwhile, Greg Lopez, a former Republican congressman and repeat gubernatorial candidate, has qualified for the ballot as an independent, the Denver Post reports. Lopez's entry complicates Marx's path considerably: in a three-way race against the Democratic nominee, an independent siphoning conservative votes could hand the governorship to the left.

Marx announced George Markert, a fellow Marine, as his lieutenant governor pick, emphasizing both candidates' non-politician credentials. "He's a proven leader who has spent his life accomplishing missions," Marx said on radio.

The timing is brutal. Republicans need unity to reclaim the governor's office and reverse Colorado's leftward drift on taxes, regulation, and energy policy. Instead, they're watching their frontrunner stumble on basic campaign finance compliance while a rival fractures the conservative base.

Watch whether the FEC violations force Marx to withdraw or whether Colorado Republicans can consolidate behind a single nominee before the primary.
