# Massive Aspen Acres Fire Rages Out of Control as Utility Faces Negligence Suit

Colorado's largest active wildfire—the nearly 100,000-acre Aspen Acres blaze south of Pueblo—continues to threaten thousands of homes and has displaced at least 3,000 residents, with containment stuck at just 36% despite brutal firefighting efforts (Colorado Sun).

The fire remains a "serious threat" to the town of Rye as hot, dry conditions persist across southern Colorado. Multiple other fires—Gold Mountain, Willow, Ferris, Fishhook, Green Ridge, and Elk—are also burning statewide, straining resources and personnel (Colorado Sun, CPR News).

Now comes accountability: San Isabel Electric Association faces a negligence lawsuit alleging that a fallen tree on one of its power lines ignited the catastrophic fire. A witness reportedly observed the downed line before the blaze erupted (KDVR Fox31). If proven, the utility's failure to maintain its infrastructure could expose it to massive liability—and raise hard questions about whether Colorado's electric cooperatives are adequately inspecting and maintaining equipment in fire-prone areas.

Conservatives and taxpayers should demand answers: Did San Isabel Electric cut corners on maintenance? And what regulatory oversight exists to prevent utilities from creating fire hazards on Colorado's drying landscape?

Watch for the lawsuit's discovery phase and whether state regulators launch their own investigation into utility infrastructure standards.
