# Colorado Faces Twin Wildfire Crisis as Gold Mountain and Willow Fires Force Evacuations

Colorado is battling two major wildfires simultaneously, with mandatory evacuations now in effect across mountain communities as fire season intensifies ahead of peak summer months.

The Gold Mountain fire southeast of Montrose has scorched more than 27,000 acres with just 3% containment as of Monday morning, according to CPR News, forcing Gunnison County residents to flee their homes. Meanwhile, the Willow Fire near Leadville has burned over 4,100 acres and prompted evacuations affecting more than 400 residents in Lake County, with containment remaining low (9News Denver).

These fires underscore a persistent problem: Colorado's forests remain tinderboxes due to decades of mismanagement and environmental restrictions that have blocked aggressive thinning and timber harvesting. Conservatives argue that overly restrictive environmental regulations and litigation by green groups have prevented the forest management practices necessary to reduce fuel loads and wildfire risk. Instead of letting markets and private landowners manage forests efficiently, federal and state policies have tied hands and left communities vulnerable.

The dual crisis also strains state emergency resources and threatens tourism-dependent mountain economies during peak season—costs that ultimately fall on Colorado taxpayers through disaster response and recovery spending.
