# Colorado Should Embrace AI Skills Training, Not Regulate It Into Oblivion

Colorado has a chance to lead on artificial intelligence—by letting the private sector train workers, not by imposing heavy-handed government mandates that kill innovation.

The Colorado Sun's recent op-ed pushes state-sponsored AI apprenticeships, framing them as a way to give young workers "real work, under solid supervision, against clear standards." But conservatives argue that government-designed training programs inevitably become bloated, bureaucratic, and slow to adapt to fast-moving tech markets.

Complete Colorado counters that Colorado should "ease off the artificial intelligence panic pedal" altogether. A Magellan Strategies survey shows Coloradans are ready to bet on AI and data centers—the engines of future economic growth. Republicans point out that Colorado's energy abundance and tech talent make the state a natural hub for AI development, but only if regulators don't strangle it with red tape.

The real question: Will Colorado trust businesses and workers to figure out training on their own, or will lawmakers create another layer of government oversight? Private companies already invest heavily in workforce development because skilled workers drive profits. Heavy-handed state apprenticeship programs risk crowding out those market-driven solutions.

Watch whether the legislature resists the urge to regulate AI development and instead focuses on removing barriers to business growth and private training investment.
