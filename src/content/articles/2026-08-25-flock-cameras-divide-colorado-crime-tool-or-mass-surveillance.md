# Flock Cameras: Law Enforcement Tool or Mass Surveillance? Colorado Cops and Privacy Hawks Clash

License plate reader cameras are becoming a flashpoint between law enforcement efficiency and Fourth Amendment concerns—and Colorado communities are caught in the middle.

Flock Safety's automated license plate readers, which photograph and catalog vehicle plates en masse, have won backing from police departments nationwide seeking faster crime-solving tools. National Review argues the technology is a "valuable asset" for law enforcement and warns that rolling back deployment would handicap cops fighting serious crimes (National Review).

But conservatives and civil libertarians are sounding alarms. These cameras create permanent databases of Americans' movements without warrants or individualized suspicion—a mass surveillance infrastructure that contradicts limited-government principles. Republicans and privacy advocates worry about mission creep: today's crime-fighting tool becomes tomorrow's tool for tracking political opponents, monitoring gun owners, or enforcing unpopular regulations.

Colorado law enforcement agencies have quietly adopted Flock systems in several jurisdictions. The question facing Colorado taxpayers and gun owners: Do we want our movements logged and stored indefinitely by government databases, even if cops say it helps solve crimes?

**Watch for:** Colorado legislative pushback in 2025 requiring warrants for plate reader data access and limiting retention periods—a rare area where privacy-minded conservatives and progressive civil libertarians might actually agree.
