# Trump-Canada Trade Spat Risks Colorado's Energy and Agricultural Ties

President Trump's escalating trade tensions with Canada threaten Colorado's cross-border energy partnerships and agricultural exports, as Ontario Premier Doug Ford fired back with profanity after Trump mocked him on social media.

Ford told CP24 he wouldn't tolerate Trump's personal attacks, telling the president to "kiss my a--" after Trump compared him unfavorably to Ford's late brother Rob. The exchange underscores deepening friction over tariffs and trade policy (Fox News Politics).

**The conservative concern:** While Republicans generally support Trump's hardline trade stance against China and other adversaries, National Review warns that a prolonged trade war with Canada—America's closest ally and a resource-rich neighbor—risks long-term damage to bilateral relations that took decades to build.

For Colorado specifically, the stakes are real. Canadian energy imports and cross-border electricity grids support Colorado's power supply. Agricultural exports to Canada matter for Colorado ranchers and farmers. A sustained trade war could disrupt both sectors and raise costs for Colorado consumers and businesses.

Trump's tariff strategy aims to pressure allies into better trade terms, but critics argue alienating Canada—which shares the continent, critical minerals, and strategic interests—differs fundamentally from confronting Beijing.
