# Meta Platforms Down Friday; Colorado Users Locked Out of Key Campaign Tool

A major outage knocked Facebook and Instagram offline Friday morning, leaving Colorado businesses, political campaigns, and residents unable to access Meta's platforms during peak hours.

Both 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31 reported widespread service disruptions affecting users across the state and nation. The exact cause remained unclear as Meta worked to restore service, though the company did not immediately issue public statements about the scope or duration of the failure.

For Colorado conservatives and Republican campaigns heading into election season, the outage underscores a critical vulnerability: reliance on a single tech giant to reach voters and supporters. Meta platforms dominate digital political advertising and grassroots organizing, meaning any extended downtime directly impacts campaign operations, fundraising coordination, and message delivery during crucial periods.

The incident also highlights broader concerns about Big Tech's market concentration and the absence of meaningful competition in social media—issues conservatives have long flagged as threats to free speech and political independence. When one company controls the primary digital town square, service failures become political failures.

Colorado taxpayers and business owners should monitor whether Meta faces regulatory scrutiny or whether this prompts serious discussion about platform diversification and resilience in political communications.
