# America 250: Conservatives Celebrate National Strength While Debating the Nation's Future

America's 250th birthday is drawing conservative voices to reflect on national abundance and power—but also to wrestle with fundamental questions about citizenship, sovereignty, and what it means to be American in an increasingly contested world.

National Review celebrates the "little secret of American abundance" that even World Cup tourists are discovering, arguing conservatives must "keep sharing and defending it for a long time to come." The Daily Wire frames the milestone as "America-maxxing," invoking Rome's strength and suggesting that as long as constitutional conservatives remain in charge, the republic can avoid historical decline.

But the Washington Examiner raises sharper questions: Who counts as American? Which America are we celebrating? The outlet warns that "benign neglect is not an option in an increasingly multipolar world" where U.S. power faces mounting threats—both absolute and relative.

For Colorado conservatives, the subtext matters. As the nation marks 250 years, debates over immigration, citizenship, and national identity directly affect state policy on border security, labor, and resource management. The semiquincentennial moment offers an opportunity to reassert constitutional limits on federal overreach and reaffirm the principles of limited government that built American prosperity.
