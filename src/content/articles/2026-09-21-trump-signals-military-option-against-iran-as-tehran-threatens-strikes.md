# Trump Signals Hardline Iran Stance as Tehran Threatens Retaliation

President Trump is taking a muscular approach to Iran, warning Sunday that he's weighing options ranging from economic strangulation to military action—a stark contrast to the Obama-Biden appeasement playbook that left American interests vulnerable across the Middle East.

Speaking to Fox News, Trump said he's in "deciding mode" on Iran, considering "wiping Iran out, letting them rot economically or making a deal." He criticized Iranian officials for "hiding like rats" and signaled that negotiations require strength, not weakness (The Daily Wire).

Iran responded predictably, with its military threatening "sustained, effective and painful" attacks on U.S. bases and interests if Washington strikes again (Breitbart). The threat underscores why deterrence—not dialogue—works with hostile regimes.

For Colorado, this matters. Energy markets react sharply to Middle East tensions. Oil and gas producers here benefit from stable, higher prices when geopolitical risk rises. Conversely, any miscalculation could spike energy costs for Colorado families and businesses already squeezed by inflation.

Trump's willingness to credibly threaten force contrasts with years of Iranian aggression under the previous administration. Conservatives argue that strength prevents war; weakness invites it.

**Watch whether Trump follows through with concrete action or whether Iran's threats force a negotiated settlement.**
