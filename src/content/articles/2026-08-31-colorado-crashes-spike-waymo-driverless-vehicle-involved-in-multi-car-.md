# Colorado Roads See Rash of Serious Crashes; Driverless Waymo Also Involved

Colorado highways are proving dangerous this week, with multiple serious incidents raising questions about road safety—and now, the safety of autonomous vehicles operating on public roads.

An unoccupied Waymo was involved in a three-vehicle crash Thursday afternoon in the Denver area, with a trailing driver cited in the incident (KDVR). Meanwhile, Colorado State Patrol stopped a wrong-way driver on I-76 early Sunday by intentionally colliding with the vehicle; that driver now faces DUI and reckless driving charges (9News). The same morning, Greenwood Village Police are searching for a hit-and-run driver responsible for a fatal crash on I-25 near the town.

The Waymo incident raises a conservative concern: as driverless vehicles proliferate on Colorado roads, who bears liability when an autonomous vehicle is involved in a crash? The trailing driver was cited, but questions linger about whether unoccupied vehicles should operate on congested highways without additional safeguards. Colorado taxpayers and motorists deserve clarity on regulatory oversight before autonomous fleets expand further.

The rash of serious crashes—DUI, reckless driving, hit-and-run—underscores a persistent problem: enforcement and personal accountability on Colorado roads.
