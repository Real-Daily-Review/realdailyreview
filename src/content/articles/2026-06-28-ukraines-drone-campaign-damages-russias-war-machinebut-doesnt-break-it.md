# Ukraine's Drone Campaign Stalls Russia's War Machine—But Falls Short of Knockout Blow

Ukraine's long-range drone strikes on Russian energy infrastructure have inflicted real damage, yet Russia's adaptive capacity and resilience have prevented a decisive breakthrough. According to the Washington Examiner, Western strategists have long viewed Russia's oil industry as the linchpin of Moscow's war economy, making energy infrastructure a logical target. Ukrainian drones and missiles have created fuel shortages, power cuts across occupied territories like Crimea, and massive logistical bottlenecks—including traffic jams near the critical Kerch Bridge.

The problem: Russia keeps adapting. Despite months of sustained strikes, Moscow has managed to maintain enough operational capacity to sustain its invasion. The campaign has delivered "tangible victories," but not the game-changing collapse some hoped for.

For American conservatives, the lesson is clear: degrading an adversary's war machine requires sustained, coordinated pressure—not hope that attrition alone will force surrender. The Washington Examiner's opinion section argues that the Trump administration should capitalize on Russia's current vulnerability by ramping up support for Ukraine's offensive capabilities rather than rushing toward a negotiated settlement that locks in Russian gains.
