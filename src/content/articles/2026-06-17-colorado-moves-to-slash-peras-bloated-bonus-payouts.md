# Colorado Lawmakers Move to Rein In PERA's Lavish Bonus Culture

Colorado lawmakers are finally cracking down on the Public Employees' Retirement Association's habit of handing out millions in performance bonuses to investment staff—a practice that has quietly drained resources meant for retirees' pensions.

The proposal, sparked by a Colorado Sun investigation, targets what conservatives have long criticized: bloated public-sector compensation packages that reward bureaucrats while taxpayers foot the bill. PERA's investment team has collected substantial bonuses in recent years, raising questions about whether those payouts align with the fund's fiduciary duty to pensioners or simply reflect the agency's insular spending culture.

Republicans and fiscal hawks argue that bonus structures in government pension systems should be tightly constrained. Public employees already enjoy defined-benefit pensions far more generous than private-sector alternatives; additional performance incentives amount to double-dipping at Colorado taxpayers' expense. The real measure of PERA's success should be whether retirees receive promised benefits—not whether investment managers hit Wall Street-style comp targets.

Democrats have not mounted a strong defense of the current system, suggesting even the left recognizes the political vulnerability of defending six-figure bonuses in a government agency.

**Watch whether the legislature passes meaningful caps or waters down the proposal with loopholes that preserve PERA's bonus machinery.**
