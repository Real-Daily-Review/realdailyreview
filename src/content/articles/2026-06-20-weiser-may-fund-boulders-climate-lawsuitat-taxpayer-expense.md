# Boulder's Climate Lawsuit Could Cost Colorado Taxpayers—and Weiser May Be Involved

Colorado's Attorney General Phil Weiser may be directing state resources toward Boulder's aggressive climate litigation against energy companies, a move that could expose taxpayers to massive costs and higher energy bills.

Boulder has filed a sweeping climate lawsuit targeting fossil fuel producers, but conservatives warn the case represents regulatory overreach disguised as environmental protection. National Review argues the lawsuit amounts to "a tax on every American," circumventing Congress and federal energy policy through litigation. Complete Colorado reports the Boulder District Attorney has hinted at Weiser's involvement, raising questions about whether Colorado's top law enforcement officer is using public funds to bankroll climate activism.

**Republicans counter** that climate lawsuits like Boulder's are backdoor attempts to impose energy restrictions courts should reject. They argue such litigation drives up energy costs for working families and threatens Colorado's oil and gas industry, which supports thousands of jobs and state revenue.

**Democrats and climate advocates** contend the lawsuits hold polluters accountable for climate damages and represent legitimate legal recourse when federal action stalls.

The case hinges on whether courts will allow states and municipalities to weaponize tort law against energy producers—a precedent that could reshape American energy policy outside the legislative process.

Watch whether Weiser's office formally joins the lawsuit and how much Colorado taxpayer money gets committed to the fight.
