# California's Rail Disaster Should Warn Colorado Against Wasteful Government Megaprojects

California's high-speed rail boondoggle—hemorrhaging billions with nothing to show but broken promises—offers a cautionary tale for Colorado policymakers tempted by similar government-led infrastructure fantasies.

The project has become a poster child for progressive overreach and fiscal recklessness. While California burned through massive taxpayer dollars, China completed enough track to circle America three times, underscoring how market-driven development outpaces government central planning (Daily Caller). National Review notes the state's regulatory overreach extends beyond rail failures to tire regulations, exemplifying how progressive bureaucracies strangle innovation and competitiveness.

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** Government rarely builds infrastructure efficiently. When politicians control project timelines and budgets, cost overruns, delays, and mission creep are features, not bugs. California's rail debacle proves that throwing money at ambitious projects doesn't guarantee results—it guarantees waste.

**Democrats defend such initiatives as necessary long-term investments**, though they struggle to explain where billions vanished without delivering promised service.

Colorado should resist similar temptations. Whether it's transit schemes, renewable energy mandates, or other government-directed projects, Coloradans should demand private-sector solutions and transparent accounting before a single tax dollar gets committed.

Watch for whether Colorado's Democratic legislature pushes rail or transit megaprojects using California's failures as a template rather than a warning.
