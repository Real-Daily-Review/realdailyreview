# DOJ Probes TV Networks Over White House Pool Coverage Suspension

The Justice Department is investigating whether major television broadcasters violated antitrust law when they briefly halted presidential pool coverage after the White House barred certain outlets from full access to the grounds, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The networks—ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC, and Fox News—have been ordered to produce communications and materials related to the coverage suspension. The dispute centers on whether the broadcasters' coordinated decision to pause pool reporting constituted illegal collusion under antitrust statutes.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans argue that media outlets coordinating to punish the administration for access restrictions raises legitimate questions about industry coordination. The DOJ's willingness to investigate suggests concern that networks may have acted in concert rather than independently—a potential antitrust violation that would normally draw scrutiny from the left.

**The media's position:** Networks contend they suspended coverage to protest what they viewed as censorship and unequal access to the president, framing the action as a principled stand for press freedom rather than coordinated economic conduct.

The investigation underscores the tension between executive authority over White House access and media claims to First Amendment protections. For Colorado conservatives skeptical of media gatekeeping, the case raises uncomfortable questions about whether Big Media coordination deserves antitrust scrutiny—or whether it's simply editorial judgment.
