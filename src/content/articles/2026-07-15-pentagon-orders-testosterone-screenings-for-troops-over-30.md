# Pentagon's New Testosterone Screening Program Aims to Boost Military Readiness

War Secretary Pete Hegseth is betting that better hormone health means better warfighters. The Department of Defense announced Wednesday a new "High-T" initiative requiring annual testosterone screenings for active-duty service members aged 30 and older as part of their standard physical exams, with voluntary testing available for younger troops (The Daily Wire, Washington Examiner).

Hegseth framed the program as a military readiness measure, arguing that testosterone deficiency can undermine soldier performance and long-term health. The screenings will identify service members who may benefit from replacement therapy, positioning hormonal optimization alongside traditional fitness metrics.

The move reflects Hegseth's broader philosophy that military strength depends on the physical vitality of individual warfighters. Whether the Pentagon's medical establishment will embrace the initiative—or whether it becomes another unfunded mandate layered onto already-stretched military medical systems—remains unclear.

For Colorado, home to Fort Carson, Schriever Space Force Base, and other major military installations, this policy could affect thousands of service members and their families accessing military healthcare. The real question: Does the Pentagon have the medical capacity and budget to implement this without degrading other critical health services? Watch whether Congress demands cost projections and whether military doctors push back on screening scope.
