# Surveillance Creep: Colorado Should Reject Flock Cameras Before They Spread

License plate reader cameras marketed as crime-fighting tools are the latest Trojan horse for mass surveillance—and Colorado communities need to pump the brakes before they become ubiquitous.

Flock Safety's automated cameras capture and store license plate data on a massive scale, ostensibly to help police solve crimes. National Review argues law enforcement views them as "a valuable asset," but that framing glosses over the real problem: these systems create permanent digital records of where citizens drive, when, and how often—without warrants or individualized suspicion.

Republicans and civil libertarians across the spectrum worry Flock represents exactly the kind of government overreach conservatives should oppose. Once installed, these cameras become infrastructure for tracking law-abiding Coloradans. Police departments gain a surveillance tool that operates 24/7 with minimal oversight. The data persists indefinitely, ripe for mission creep and abuse.

Colorado's gun owners, ranchers, and rural residents should be especially wary. License plate readers don't distinguish between a trip to the range, a hunting expedition, or a visit to a political rally. In the hands of a hostile administration, that data becomes a weapon against constitutional rights.

The real question isn't whether Flock helps cops solve crimes—it's whether Coloradans want their movements catalogued by algorithm and stored in corporate servers.

Watch whether Denver, Boulder, or other Colorado cities move to deploy Flock before the legislature acts.
