# Missouri Judge Blocks Democratic Redistricting Referendum, Hands GOP Midterm Edge

A Missouri judge dealt Democrats a stinging defeat Wednesday by blocking a referendum that would have overturned the Republican-controlled legislature's congressional map—a ruling that preserves GOP gains ahead of the midterms and underscores the stakes of redistricting battles nationwide.

Cole County Circuit Judge Daniel Green ruled that voters cannot override the state legislature's authority to draw congressional districts, siding with Republican Secretary of State Denny Hoskins (Washington Examiner). The decision clears the way for Republicans to use their map in November's general election, expected to secure GOP control of a Kansas City-area seat that Democrats had targeted (Fox News).

**The conservative takeaway:** This is how the system should work. Legislatures—accountable to voters—draw maps, not unelected judges or ballot initiatives that circumvent representative government. Democrats have spent years weaponizing redistricting courts; this ruling reasserts legislative authority where it belongs.

**Colorado angle:** While this Missouri victory won't directly affect Colorado's maps, it signals judicial pushback against the left's strategy of using referendums to override legislative redistricting. Colorado Republicans should note how courts are beginning to enforce the principle that mapmaking is a legislative function, not a judicial or populist one.

Watch for Democratic appeals and whether this precedent influences how Colorado courts handle future redistricting challenges.
