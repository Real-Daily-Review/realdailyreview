# Federal Price-Posting Mandate Failed to Lower Hospital Costs—Another Government Fix That Backfired

Washington's five-year experiment in forcing hospitals to post prices has collapsed, proving once again that government mandates don't create markets—they create bureaucratic theater.

The federal government required hospitals to disclose pricing data, betting that transparency would spark competition and drive costs down. Instead, according to the *Washington Examiner*, the files are posted, the market never formed, and prices never fell. Researchers examining the disclosed data found it so voluminous and poorly structured that it failed to enable meaningful price comparison shopping.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Regulatory mandates cannot substitute for genuine market forces. When government tries to engineer outcomes through disclosure requirements rather than removing barriers to real competition—certificate-of-need laws, insurance regulations, scope-of-practice restrictions—it produces compliance theater, not lower prices.

Colorado taxpayers and employers self-funding health plans should take note. State-level CON laws and insurance mandates similarly restrict supply and inflate costs. Until policymakers embrace actual deregulation and allow genuine price competition, mandates to "post" or "disclose" will remain expensive window dressing.

**Watch whether Colorado legislators learn this lesson or double down on more regulatory "solutions" to problems regulation created.**
