# Senate Punts Budget Crisis Past Election—Again

Congress has once again chosen political convenience over fiscal responsibility, passing a short-term continuing resolution to fund federal agencies through early December rather than tackle a real budget (9News Denver). The measure avoids a shutdown during campaign season but leaves the fundamental problem untouched: runaway federal spending and a ballooning national debt.

Republicans argue this pattern—kicking the can down the road election cycle after election cycle—enables Washington to avoid hard choices on spending restraint. The CR does nothing to address the debt crisis or rein in federal overreach, they contend. Democrats counter that a short-term measure prevents disruption to federal services and allows time for post-election negotiations.

For Colorado, the implications are clear: as long as Washington refuses to control spending, pressure mounts for higher taxes and regulations that trickle down to states and businesses. Federal agencies that oversee public lands, water rights, and energy development will continue operating under uncertainty, making long-term planning impossible for Colorado's ranchers, energy workers, and outdoor economy.

This is governance by crisis management—and taxpayers foot the bill.

**Watch next:** whether Republicans use post-election leverage to demand genuine spending cuts, or whether the pattern repeats in December.
