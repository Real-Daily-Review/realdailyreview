# Colorado HOA President Accused of Raiding Nearly $150K in Neighborhood Funds

A former Severance HOA president has been charged with stealing nearly $150,000 in association funds through unauthorized expenses spanning from 2017 to 2025, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31.

The case underscores a persistent vulnerability in homeowners associations: weak financial oversight and limited accountability mechanisms that leave residents' mandatory dues vulnerable to misappropriation. HOAs across Colorado collect billions annually in fees, yet many operate with minimal transparency or independent auditing—a recipe for abuse that disproportionately harms middle-class homeowners who have little recourse once their money disappears.

The accused former president allegedly diverted association funds for personal use over an eight-year period before the theft was discovered. Residents typically have limited ability to recover stolen funds, and criminal prosecution alone rarely compensates victims fully.

**What conservatives should watch:** Colorado lawmakers should consider legislation requiring mandatory financial audits, transparent budget reporting, and stronger bonding requirements for HOA officers—without creating new government bureaucracies. Market-driven accountability, not regulatory expansion, is the answer.
