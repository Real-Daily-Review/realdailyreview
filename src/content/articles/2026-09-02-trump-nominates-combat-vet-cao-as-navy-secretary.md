# Trump Nominates Hung Cao as Navy Secretary—Military Leadership Gets Combat Veteran

President Trump has officially nominated retired Navy Captain Hung Cao as permanent Navy secretary, cementing a military-first approach to Pentagon leadership after Cao's four-month stint in an acting capacity since April.

Cao, a special operations veteran and career Navy officer, brings genuine warfighting experience to the role—a sharp contrast to the revolving-door civilian bureaucrats who've historically treated the Pentagon as a policy laboratory. Trump praised Cao as a "true warrior" essential to rebuilding Navy and Marine Corps readiness (Fox News).

The nomination signals Trump's intent to prioritize military capability and readiness over progressive social experiments that have plagued the armed forces in recent years. Conservatives have long argued that the military's focus should remain on winning wars, not advancing ideological agendas—and Cao's combat background suggests a return to that priority.

For Colorado, this matters: the state hosts significant military installations and defense contractors whose workforce depends on a Navy and Marine Corps focused on operational excellence rather than distraction. A secretary with Cao's operational pedigree could mean clearer procurement priorities and stronger support for defense industrial base jobs across the state.

Watch for Senate confirmation hearings, where Republicans will likely highlight Cao's readiness credentials while Democrats probe for culture-war vulnerabilities.
