# Colorado Judge Tightens Screws on Morphew: Bond Hiked to $10M

A Colorado judge has raised Barry Morphew's bond to $10 million and made it cash-only, signaling judicial skepticism about the accused murderer's ability to follow court orders while awaiting trial in his wife Suzanne's 2020 death.

The bond increase came after Morphew was involved in a hit-and-run crash in Denver, prompting the judge to declare that Morphew "has shown time and again he has no respect for any order of this court," according to the Colorado Sun. The decision shifts the burden substantially: Morphew must now post cash rather than secure a bond through a bail company, making release far more difficult.

Morphew has been jailed awaiting trial on murder charges stemming from Suzanne's disappearance and death in the Salida area. The escalating bond reflects judicial frustration with his conduct—a rare move that underscores how seriously the court views his pattern of disregarding court authority.

The case has drawn statewide attention and raises questions about pretrial detention standards and judicial discretion in high-profile criminal matters. Conservatives generally support judges' authority to set meaningful bail conditions that protect public safety and ensure defendants appear for trial.
