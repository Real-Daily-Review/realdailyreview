# Colorado Grocery Shelves Hit by Major Egg Recall Over Salmonella Risk

A massive egg recall spanning millions of dozens is hitting supermarket shelves nationwide, including Colorado Kroger locations, over salmonella contamination concerns. The recall affects approximately 1.6 million dozen eggs sold under multiple brands at major chains including Kroger and Brookshire's (9News Denver), with some reports citing figures as high as 19 million eggs (New York Post).

The timing compounds food-safety frustrations for Colorado families already reeling from recent foodborne illness outbreaks. Consumers are rightfully asking: where are the guardrails?

**The conservative concern:** While food safety inspections exist, critics argue the FDA's regulatory apparatus has grown bloated and expensive without preventing recurring contamination crises. Republicans contend that market accountability—brand reputation and liability—often proves more effective than bureaucratic oversight. Yet the current system delivers neither: consumers face both heavy regulatory costs *and* dangerous products reaching shelves.

Colorado shoppers should verify egg purchases against recall lists immediately and check for symptoms including diarrhea, fever, and abdominal cramps within 6-72 hours of consumption.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado health officials demand new regulatory mandates or whether industry self-policing and transparency prove sufficient to restore consumer confidence.
