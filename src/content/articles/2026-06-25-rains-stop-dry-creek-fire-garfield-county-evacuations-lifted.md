# Overnight Rains Halt Dry Creek Fire Growth in Garfield County

Colorado dodged a bullet this week when overnight rains stopped the Dry Creek fire from expanding across Garfield County rangeland. The blaze, which ignited Tuesday south of Rifle, held steady at approximately 315 acres as of Thursday morning—a rare win for fire crews battling the state's relentless wildfire season (Colorado Sun).

Evacuations for Grass Mesa were lifted early Thursday after conditions stabilized, according to Colorado River Fire Rescue (9News). While the reprieve is welcome news for ranchers and residents in the region, the incident underscores a persistent conservative concern: Colorado's forests and rangelands remain tinderboxes partly due to decades of federal mismanagement and environmental restrictions on timber harvesting and controlled burns.

Republicans and energy advocates have long argued that overzealous environmental regulations prevent the forest management practices—thinning, selective logging, and prescribed burns—that reduce catastrophic wildfire risk. The Biden administration's push to restrict oil and gas development on federal lands, meanwhile, has reduced revenue streams that historically funded land management in rural Colorado counties like Garfield.

The temporary containment offers breathing room, but fire season runs through fall. Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether state and federal agencies finally embrace aggressive forest management or continue relying on luck and rain.
