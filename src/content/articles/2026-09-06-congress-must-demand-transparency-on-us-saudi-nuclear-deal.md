# U.S.-Saudi Nuclear Deal Demands Congressional Scrutiny and Transparency

A landmark nuclear arrangement between the United States and Saudi Arabia is moving toward finalization, but conservatives warn the Biden administration must not hand adversaries a backdoor to American security interests.

The White House announced the deal on July 22, positioning it as a strategic partnership with a longtime ally. However, the Washington Examiner's editorial board argues that Congress cannot rubber-stamp a classified agreement without demanding full transparency and ironclad safeguards.

**Republicans' core concern:** The deal's classified nature raises red flags. Conservatives argue that any nuclear arrangement involving U.S. interests must be subject to congressional oversight and public scrutiny—not negotiated behind closed doors where potential vulnerabilities to hostile actors could go unexamined. The editorial board specifically calls for the administration to declassify essential provisions and implement binding security measures.

**The stakes for Colorado:** While the agreement itself is national security policy, Colorado's energy sector and uranium producers have long-standing interests in U.S. nuclear policy. How America structures nuclear partnerships abroad can affect domestic uranium markets and energy independence strategies.

Taxpayers and national security hawks should watch whether Congress demands full briefings and declassification before ratification—or whether this administration proceeds with minimal legislative input.
