# Colorado Restaurant Earns Michelin Star as Guide Expands Statewide

The Michelin Guide's expansion into Colorado represents a vote of confidence in the state's free-market culinary economy—and a reminder that excellence thrives without government mandates or subsidies.

Denver's Milpero has earned a coveted Michelin Star as the prestigious restaurant rating service broadens coverage across Colorado (9News Denver). The guide, which began surveying four major Colorado culinary scenes in 2023, now covers the entire state with anonymous inspectors evaluating establishments statewide (Colorado Sun).

The recognition underscores what happens when entrepreneurs compete on merit: Colorado's restaurant sector has matured enough to attract international scrutiny and accolades without heavy-handed government "culinary development" programs or taxpayer-funded marketing schemes. Market forces—customer demand, word-of-mouth, and genuine quality—drive success far more effectively than bureaucratic intervention.

Unlike some states that throw public money at restaurant districts or subsidize hospitality ventures, Colorado's dining renaissance reflects organic business growth and consumer choice. The Michelin Guide's decision to expand here signals that the state's restaurant community has reached critical mass through private investment and entrepreneurial risk-taking.

Conservatives should watch whether local or state officials attempt to capitalize on this success by proposing new hospitality taxes, regulations, or "culinary district" schemes that would burden the very businesses earning this recognition.
