# Former Sen. Jon Kyl, Water Policy Architect, Dies at 84

Jon Kyl, the Arizona Republican who shaped Western water compacts that affect Colorado's Colorado River allocation, has died at 84 (Fox News, Just The News).

Kyl served as GOP whip and represented Arizona in the Senate from 1995 to 2013, then returned in 2018 following John McCain's death. Beyond his legislative record, Kyl became a powerful Washington lobbyist and guided confirmation of Supreme Court Justice Brett Kavanaugh as a private citizen (9News Denver).

His most consequential work for Colorado and the West involved water policy. As a senator and later advocate, Kyl shaped landmark agreements governing the Colorado River—the lifeline for agriculture, municipalities, and energy production across the Upper Basin states including Colorado. Those compacts determine how much water flows to Colorado versus downstream states, making Kyl's influence directly relevant to Colorado ranchers, farmers, and water districts.

Kyl represented the old-school Republican establishment: pro-business, hawkish on defense, and deeply versed in the arcane but vital machinery of Western resource management. His death removes a seasoned voice on water negotiations at a time when Colorado faces mounting pressure from environmental groups and lower-basin states over river allocation and dam operations.

Watch for how Arizona and Colorado coordinate on Colorado River renegotiations without Kyl's institutional knowledge and relationships.
