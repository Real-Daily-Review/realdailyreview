# House GOP Fractures Over SAVE America Act, Stalls Defense Bill

House Republicans blocked their own defense authorization bill this week, refusing to advance the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) unless leadership agreed to pair it with the SAVE America Act—a measure designed to tighten election security and voter ID requirements. The standoff forced Speaker Mike Johnson to send members home early for the July 4 recess, marking the second consecutive week the House floor ground to a halt over procedural votes (Washington Examiner).

Fourteen House Republicans voted against the rule allowing the NDAA to advance, effectively killing floor action on the defense measure (Daily Caller). The blockade reflects deep frustration among conservatives over what they view as insufficient election integrity protections in federal law. Republicans argue that linking voter ID and election security reforms to must-pass defense legislation is a legitimate legislative tactic to force Democrats to negotiate on election rules.

Democrats and defense hawks counter that holding the NDAA hostage risks weakening military readiness and leaves critical defense priorities in limbo. The impasse underscores the GOP's internal divisions on strategy heading into an election year.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's defense contractors and military installations depend on timely NDAA passage for funding certainty and procurement schedules.

**Watch next:** Whether Speaker Johnson can broker a compromise before the August recess, or if the SAVE Act fight continues to paralyze defense legislation.
