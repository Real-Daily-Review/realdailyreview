# Colorado GOP Nominates First-Time Candidate Marx for Governor—Party Fractures Heading Into Fall

Victor Marx, a nonprofit leader and political newcomer with a controversial personal history, narrowly won Colorado's Republican gubernatorial primary over veteran state Sen. Barb Kirkmeyer, edging her by roughly 2,000 votes (Denver Post). The razor-thin margin—so close the race remained uncalled for days—signals deep fractures within Colorado's already-weakened GOP heading into a general election Democrats are heavily favored to win.

Conservative outlets are sounding alarms. Complete Colorado bluntly called the primary outcome "the Colorado GOP's death wish," warning that liberty advocates face a bleak general election landscape. Only two of 18 Republican gubernatorial candidates advanced from the GOP assembly, reflecting the party's organizational struggles in a state trending blue.

Marx's victory over an experienced legislator like Kirkmeyer underscores the GOP's internal divisions at a moment when Republicans need unity to compete statewide. Democrats, meanwhile, are already pivoting to kitchen-table issues: strategist David Boian told the Denver Post the fall race will hinge on "people's pocketbooks"—whether voters can afford groceries and basic necessities.
