# Michigan Democrat's Radical Agenda Fractures Minority Coalition

Abdul el-Sayed's Senate bid is collapsing among the very voters Democrats depend on—and his embrace of fringe policies shows why progressive overreach alienates working families.

The Michigan Democrat's campaign has drawn fire from Black and Jewish voters alike, according to *National Review*, over concerns about his judgment and loyalties. Those fractures widened this week when former President Barack Obama lent his name to el-Sayed despite the candidate's support for abolishing ICE, imposing Medicare for All, and what *Washington Examiner* critics call "disarming Israel"—positions once confined to the Democratic fringe now mainstream under progressive pressure.

President Trump seized on the divide Tuesday, arguing Black voters reject el-Sayed because the candidate "mocks and disrespects them," per *Breitbart*.

**The Colorado angle:** While this race unfolds in Michigan, it mirrors tensions within Colorado's own Democratic establishment. As the state leans purple, radical positions on immigration enforcement, healthcare mandates, and foreign policy risk alienating the moderate and minority voters Democrats need statewide. Colorado Republicans should watch whether similar fractures emerge in 2026 Senate and gubernatorial races as the left's activist base pushes candidates further left.
