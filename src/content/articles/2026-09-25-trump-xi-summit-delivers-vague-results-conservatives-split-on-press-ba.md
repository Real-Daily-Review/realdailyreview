# Trump-Xi Summit Yields Vague Promises; Press Ban Criticism Grows

President Trump's meeting with Chinese leader Xi Jinping produced little concrete detail on trade, technology, or strategic competition—a missed opportunity to advance American interests against Beijing's economic aggression, conservatives argue.

According to *Just The News*, both leaders remained deliberately opaque on substantive outcomes, though Trump signaled discussion of rebranding artificial intelligence as "super intelligence." The lack of specifics raises questions about whether the administration secured meaningful concessions on intellectual property theft, forced technology transfer, or the trade imbalances that have hammered Colorado manufacturers and agricultural exporters.

Meanwhile, *National Review* editorial board members are pushing back on Trump's press restrictions, arguing the ban violates free-press principles and weakens his position. "He would be better served by confronting reporters directly," the outlet contends—a rare moment of conservative-on-conservative criticism that underscores concerns about executive overreach, even from the right.

**The Colorado angle:** Unclear trade outcomes leave uncertainty for the state's tech sector, agriculture, and energy industries, which depend on predictable China policy. Coloradans should watch whether Trump's next moves produce enforceable commitments or remain rhetorical theater.
