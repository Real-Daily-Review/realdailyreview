# Former Colorado Elections Official Tina Peters Eyes California Voting Job—With No Legal Barriers

Colorado's disgraced ex-Mesa County Clerk Tina Peters—convicted of crimes tied to her role in spreading 2020 election conspiracy theories—faces no parole restrictions preventing her from working in elections again, even as she pursues an assistant registrar position in California's Shasta County, according to the *Colorado Sun*.

Peters' sentence was commuted, and she has since become a fixture on the conservative speaking circuit, appearing at conspiracy conferences and meeting with President Trump at the White House. Shasta County Clerk Clint Curtis confirmed the job offer in a recent radio appearance, the *Denver Post* reports.

Republicans and election security advocates have raised alarms. Peters' original conviction stemmed from her involvement in promoting baseless claims about Colorado's election systems and her role in a data breach. Conservatives who support election integrity—not conspiracy—argue that placing someone with her record in any elections office undermines public confidence in voting systems and validates the very falsehoods she promoted.

Governor Jeb Polis has remained silent on whether he's had contact with Trump regarding Peters' situation, the *Denver Post* notes.
