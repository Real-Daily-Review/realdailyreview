# Colorado Celebrates Pollinator Month—But Regulatory Overreach Threatens Local Gardens

Colorado's pollinator season is drawing attention to the insects and birds that sustain the state's agriculture and ecosystems, with the Denver Botanic Gardens highlighting the importance of native species management this month. Sonya Anderson, a horticulture manager at the gardens, is educating Coloradans about the role of bees, butterflies, and other pollinators in maintaining healthy plant communities (9News Denver).

The celebration comes as Colorado's farming and ranching communities depend heavily on pollinator health—a reality that makes regulatory clarity essential. While environmental stewardship is important, conservatives warn that heavy-handed EPA regulations and state mandates on pesticide use can burden farmers and small businesses without delivering measurable results. Republicans argue that landowners and agricultural operators, not distant bureaucrats, are best positioned to manage their own property and protect pollinator populations through market-driven innovation and voluntary conservation.

The gardens' educational efforts represent the kind of community-led awareness that works. But taxpayers should remain vigilant: watch whether state or local officials use pollinator concerns as a pretext for new regulations that could restrict farming practices or increase compliance costs for Colorado's agricultural sector.
