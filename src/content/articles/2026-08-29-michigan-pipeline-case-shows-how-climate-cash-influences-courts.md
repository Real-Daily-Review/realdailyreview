# Colorado Should Watch How Courts Weaponize Campaign Cash Against Energy Development

Colorado's energy sector faces a cautionary tale from Michigan: judicial decisions on critical infrastructure may be compromised by activist donations. The Daily Caller reports that Michigan Supreme Court justices received campaign contributions from a Jane Fonda-backed climate organization weeks before voting to freeze a pipeline project—a pattern conservatives warn could spread to Colorado's courts and regulatory bodies.

The timing raises hard questions about judicial independence. As the Daily Caller notes, industry observers argue this represents "one more tool the anti-energy left is using to go after anybody that wants to provide low-cost energy to Americans." For Colorado, where energy development supports thousands of jobs and state revenue, this matters enormously.

National Review's analysis of Supreme Court election cases emphasizes that justices must maintain fidelity to their judicial role and resist the appearance of political bias. Yet state courts operate under looser campaign finance rules, leaving them vulnerable to exactly this kind of influence.

Colorado conservatives should scrutinize whether similar donation patterns are occurring in state judicial races—particularly those involving judges who rule on energy permits, water rights, and land-use disputes. The stakes are too high for courts to be captured by ideological donors.

**Watch for:** disclosure of campaign contributions to Colorado Supreme Court and appeals court candidates from environmental groups in 2026-2027.
