# RNC Challenges Colorado's Overseas Voter Residency Rules Under Griswold

Colorado's Secretary of State Jena Griswold faces a federal lawsuit from the Republican National Committee over voting residency standards that allow overseas voters to establish Colorado domicile through a parent or spouse—a practice the RNC argues violates the Constitution (Complete Colorado).

The complaint centers on whether proxy residency claims meet constitutional muster for federal elections. Republicans contend that allowing someone who has never lived in Colorado to vote here through family connections creates vulnerability to fraud and dilutes the voting power of actual residents.

Griswold's office has not yet responded publicly to the suit. Democrats typically argue that overseas voters—including military personnel, expatriates, and their families—deserve streamlined access to voting rights without unnecessary bureaucratic barriers.

The lawsuit arrives amid broader scrutiny of Griswold's tenure. The Denver Post's editorial board recently noted that her "latest scandal should get voters to look seriously at her opponent," citing litigation expertise and a cleaner public record as advantages for Republican challenger Michael Allen.

For Colorado taxpayers and election integrity advocates, this case tests whether residency rules can withstand constitutional challenge—and whether Griswold's handling of election administration will factor into her political future.
