# Colorado Voters Face Ballot Showdown Over TABOR, Budget Spending

Colorado taxpayers are being asked this November to gut TABOR—the Taxpayer's Bill of Rights—and unlock billions in new state spending, a direct assault on the fiscal guardrails that have protected Colorado wallets for three decades.

Amendment 87 and Proposition NN target TABOR's spending caps and refund requirements, the mechanisms that force Colorado government to return excess revenue to taxpayers rather than hoard it. Proponents argue the state faces a budget crisis and needs flexibility to fund schools, transportation, and services. Republicans and fiscal conservatives counter that TABOR isn't the problem—wasteful spending and bloated government are. They argue that loosening TABOR opens the door to permanent tax-and-spend politics, turning Colorado into another high-tax blue state.

The Colorado Sun reports both measures are central to the 2026 election, with charts breaking down the budget mechanics behind the push (Colorado Sun).

Meanwhile, Colorado voters are also weighing transgender youth ballot measures. CPR News examined Utah's similar legislative bans, which produced unintended consequences—a cautionary tale for Colorado policymakers and voters considering how far to go with restrictions on youth medical care and sports participation (CPR News).
