# Colorado DSA Activists Push Radical Agenda as National Movement Stumbles

Colorado's Democratic Socialist Alliance is channeling hard-left ideology that echoes Bolshevik revolution tactics, even as the national DSA movement faces electoral backlash.

According to Complete Colorado, local DSA activists are embracing what observers describe as "vengeful dogma" reminiscent of early Soviet revolutionary narratives. The timing is notable: nationally, the Democratic Socialist movement is losing momentum. In Wisconsin, radical DSA-backed candidate Francesca Hong suffered a stunning primary defeat to moderate Democrat David Crowley in the Milwaukee County Executive race (National Review), signaling voter rejection of far-left economics at the ballot box.

**The Colorado angle:** As DSA activists gain visibility in state politics, conservatives warn that their anti-market, anti-property ideology threatens Colorado's energy sector, agricultural heritage, and business climate. Republicans argue that DSA-style wealth redistribution and government control contradict the limited-government principles that have driven Colorado's economic growth.

Democrats counter that DSA represents grassroots activism on inequality and climate, though the Wisconsin primary suggests mainstream Democrats are distancing themselves from the movement's most radical elements.

Conservatives should monitor whether Colorado Democrats distance themselves from DSA rhetoric ahead of 2026 state races—or double down on it.
