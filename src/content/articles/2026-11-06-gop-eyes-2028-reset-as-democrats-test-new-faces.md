# Colorado Republicans Eye 2028 as National Party Resets After Midterm Losses

Republicans are already looking past this cycle's defeats to position themselves for 2028, with the party's midterm losses triggering a wide-open presidential field among GOP contenders (Washington Examiner). Meanwhile, Democrats are testing whether figures like Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez can build national viability, though she faces significant structural headwinds in a general election (National Review).

For Colorado conservatives, the 2028 race matters immediately. The state's purple status and growing suburban swing-voter population make it a perennial battleground. Republicans will need a candidate who can appeal to both the party's base and Colorado's independent-minded voters—no small task given the state's recent Democratic gains in statewide races.

The early jockeying also signals that both parties are abandoning any pretense of focusing on governing. Instead of tackling inflation, energy policy, or water scarcity—issues that directly hit Colorado families and businesses—Washington is already in campaign mode. That's bad news for taxpayers hoping Congress might actually address the regulatory overreach and spending that's hammering Colorado's energy sector and small businesses.

Watch whether any 2028 Republican frontrunner commits to defending Colorado's oil and gas workers and ranchers against federal land grabs and ESG-driven energy restrictions.
