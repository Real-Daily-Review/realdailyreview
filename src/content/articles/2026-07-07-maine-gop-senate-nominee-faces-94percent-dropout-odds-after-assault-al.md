# Maine GOP Candidate Faces Collapse as Rape Allegation Tanks Campaign—Colorado Republicans Watch Closely

A Maine Republican Senate nominee is imploding after a sexual assault allegation, signaling potential trouble for GOP momentum heading into 2026.

Prediction market Kalshi now pegs Graham Platner's odds of dropping out at 94% following a woman's allegation that he sexually assaulted her nearly five years ago—an accusation Platner denies (Fox News). The candidate's campaign has triggered a party revolt, with Republican insiders abandoning ship as the scandal deepens (Daily Caller).

Democrats smell blood. Troy Jackson, a progressive who finished third in Maine's gubernatorial primary, has already filed exploratory paperwork to replace Platner as the Democratic nominee (Washington Examiner). Jackson is positioning himself as the "progressive candidate most acceptable to Platner's base," betting Democrats can flip a seat Republicans hoped to defend.

**The Colorado angle:** Senate Republicans were counting on gains in Maine and other purple states to offset Democratic strength in the West. A collapse here weakens the national GOP narrative heading into 2026—a cycle where Colorado's own Senate seat will be hotly contested.

Republicans argue the party must vet candidates rigorously to avoid self-inflicted wounds that hand Democrats winnable seats. Conservatives should watch whether Maine Republicans can salvage this race or whether Jackson's entry signals a Democratic pickup in a state Republicans needed to hold.
