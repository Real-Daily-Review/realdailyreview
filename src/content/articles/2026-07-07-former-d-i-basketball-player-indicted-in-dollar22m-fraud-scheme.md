# Former College Hoops Star Indicted in $2.2M Colorado Fraud Scheme

A former Division I basketball player has been indicted on federal charges for allegedly defrauding two victims out of $2.2 million through an elaborate impersonation scheme, according to prosecutors.

Kerr Kriisa, who played college basketball at Kentucky, Cincinnati, West Virginia, and Arizona, allegedly posed as family members and fake acquaintances to convince victims to wire money, 9News Denver reports. The scheme represents a textbook case of identity fraud that exploited personal trust—exactly the kind of white-collar crime that undermines confidence in financial systems and victimizes ordinary Coloradans.

Federal prosecutors are pursuing the case aggressively, signaling that law enforcement remains committed to prosecuting fraud regardless of a defendant's athletic pedigree. The indictment underscores a broader concern: sophisticated scammers continue to target Americans through social engineering and impersonation, often with devastating financial consequences for victims who may lack recourse.

For Colorado residents, the case is a reminder to verify the identity of anyone requesting money transfers, especially those claiming family emergencies or urgent financial needs. Victims of fraud should report incidents to local law enforcement and the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center.

Watch for trial developments and whether Kriisa's legal team attempts to negotiate a plea deal or mount a defense challenging the prosecution's evidence.
