# Colorado Families Facing Thanksgiving Sticker Shock as Inflation Squeezes Holiday Budgets

Inflation is hitting Colorado dinner tables hard this Thanksgiving, forcing families to choose between tradition and their shrinking wallets.

According to a recent survey cited by 9News Denver, fewer Americans are planning to host Thanksgiving dinner this year due to financial strain—a direct consequence of the Biden-Harris administration's inflationary policies. Colorado families, already burdened by rising energy costs, property taxes, and grocery prices, are feeling the squeeze acutely as they plan holiday meals.

Republicans and conservatives argue that unchecked government spending, Federal Reserve money-printing, and regulatory overreach have destroyed the purchasing power of working families. While Democrats blame supply-chain disruptions and corporate greed, the reality for Colorado households is simple: their dollars buy less than they did two years ago.

The good news: savvy shoppers can still find deals on turkey and traditional sides by comparing prices across retailers and shopping early. But the underlying problem remains: middle-class Coloradans shouldn't have to hunt for bargains just to afford a family holiday meal.

Watch whether Colorado legislators address cost-of-living concerns in the 2025 session—or whether they'll double down on policies that keep inflation high and wages stagnant.
