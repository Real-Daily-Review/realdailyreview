# Tucker Carlson's GOP Exit Exposes Fracture Over Foreign Policy and Party Direction

Tucker Carlson announced he is leaving the Republican Party, declaring "No chance I would support the Republican Party" and citing disagreement over foreign policy direction (Daily Caller). Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene followed suit, criticizing the GOP as "America LAST" amid divisions over an Iran conflict that polling shows most Americans want to end immediately (Just The News).

The departures highlight a genuine split within conservative ranks. Carlson and Greene object to what they view as hawkish interventionism that prioritizes foreign entanglements over domestic concerns—a critique that resonates with many grassroots Republicans and Trump supporters skeptical of endless wars.

However, establishment conservatives push back sharply. The Washington Examiner's editorial board welcomed Carlson's exit as "good riddance," arguing that President Trump has successfully purged extremists from the GOP while Democrats have embraced their own fringe elements. This framing suggests party leadership views Carlson-style nationalism as incompatible with Republican orthodoxy.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado Republicans and independent conservatives should watch whether this fracture affects 2026 midterm turnout and party unity in a state trending purple. Grassroots energy matters.

**What to watch:** Whether other high-profile conservatives follow Carlson out, or whether the GOP consolidates around Trump's foreign-policy skepticism before the next election cycle.
