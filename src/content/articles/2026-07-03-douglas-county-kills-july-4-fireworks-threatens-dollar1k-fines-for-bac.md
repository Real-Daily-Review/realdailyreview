# Douglas County Cancels July 4 Fireworks Under Fire Restrictions—and Eyes $1,000 Fines

Colorado's fire season is shutting down Independence Day traditions. Douglas County has cancelled its July 4 fireworks display at Highland Heritage Regional Park due to Stage 2 Fire Restrictions, leaving residents without the county's official celebration (9News Denver).

The move reflects legitimate wildfire concerns in a state where dry conditions and fuel loads pose real risks. But the county isn't stopping there. Douglas County officials are now threatening fines up to $1,000 for illegal fireworks, effectively criminalizing what many Coloradans have done for generations (KDVR Fox31).

A Douglas County commissioner pitched drone shows as the "safer" alternative during high fire danger—a solution that sidesteps the problem: government is now dictating how Coloradans celebrate, not just managing public land.

**The conservative concern:** Stage 2 restrictions are appropriate when fire danger peaks. But once restrictions ease, county officials should restore traditional fireworks displays rather than use the crisis to permanently shift toward expensive, government-approved drone spectacles. Coloradans shouldn't face four-figure fines for backyard celebrations on the nation's birthday.

Taxpayers should watch whether Douglas County makes drone shows a permanent fixture—and whether other Colorado counties follow suit, using environmental concerns to expand regulatory control over Independence Day.
