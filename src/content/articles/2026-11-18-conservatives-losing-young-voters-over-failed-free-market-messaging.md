# Conservatives Hemorrhaging Young Voters—and They Know Why

Conservatives are losing the youth vote because they've failed to articulate a compelling free-market alternative to big government, according to analysis in the *Washington Examiner*. Young voters correctly sense the economy is rigged—but they're blaming capitalism instead of the real culprit: government overreach and crony corporatism.

The problem, conservatives argue, is messaging. While Democrats offer simple (if destructive) solutions—more regulation, wealth redistribution, state control—Republicans have largely ceded the narrative to the left. Young people see corporate consolidation, inflation, housing unaffordability, and student debt crushing their prospects. Instead of explaining how *government* created these problems through Fed policy, zoning restrictions, and education cartel protections, conservatives have offered platitudes.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. The state's youth population leans progressive, and Denver's tech and service sectors attract young workers skeptical of free-market solutions. If Republicans can't convince Colorado's under-35 voters that limited government and genuine competition—not more bureaucracy—solve real economic pain, the state will continue drifting blue.

The conservative case is straightforward: break up regulatory barriers, unleash competition, and get government out of the way. But it requires actually *making* that case to voters who've never heard it.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans develop a coherent economic message for young voters ahead of 2026.
