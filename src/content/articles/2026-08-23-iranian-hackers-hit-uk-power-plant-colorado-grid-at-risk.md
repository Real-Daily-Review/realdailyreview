# Iranian Hackers Prove U.S. Energy Grid Vulnerability—Colorado Must Prepare

Iranian-linked hackers successfully disabled a British power plant for four days in what experts are calling the first successful cyberattack on a Western energy facility—a stark warning that America's power infrastructure, including Colorado's grid, remains dangerously exposed to hostile state actors.

The Telegraph first reported the breach, which the Washington Examiner and New York Post confirmed. While UK officials downplayed the immediate threat to Britain's wider electrical supply, the successful shutdown demonstrates that Iran has both the capability and willingness to target critical infrastructure. For Colorado, which depends on reliable power for its growing tech sector, agriculture, and mining operations, the implications are sobering.

Republicans and energy security hawks argue the Biden-Harris administration has failed to adequately harden U.S. energy infrastructure against cyberattacks while simultaneously pushing unreliable renewables that fragment grid resilience. The administration's focus on climate mandates over grid hardening leaves America vulnerable to exactly this kind of attack.

Democrats counter that infrastructure investment and grid modernization require federal coordination and funding—though they've offered no credible plan to defend against state-sponsored Iranian hackers.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses should demand their representatives in Congress prioritize cybersecurity funding for NORAD, the Front Range power grid, and critical water infrastructure before the next attack hits closer to home.
