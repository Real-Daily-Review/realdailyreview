# Americans Still Believe in the Dream—But Expect Government to Guarantee It

Colorado voters and taxpayers should take note: most Americans still believe in the American dream, but a dangerous share now expect Washington to hand it to them.

According to Gallup polling cited by the *Washington Examiner*, 78% of Americans say the dream is worth pursuing, and 69% believe they'll achieve it personally. Yet an AP-NORC survey found only 34% embrace the *traditional* understanding of the dream—the idea that hard work and opportunity, not government checks, build success.

The gap reveals a troubling shift in American thinking. Conservatives argue this reflects decades of progressive messaging that frames opportunity as a government entitlement rather than something earned through individual effort and risk-taking. When citizens expect the state to guarantee outcomes instead of protecting their right to compete freely, they're primed to accept the tax hikes, regulations, and dependency programs that kill the very dynamism that made the dream real.

For Colorado—a state built by miners, ranchers, and entrepreneurs who bet everything on themselves—this ideological drift matters. It fuels support for wealth-redistribution schemes and regulatory overreach that punish the risk-takers and job creators our economy depends on.

Watch whether Colorado's 2026 ballot initiatives and legislative races reflect this national confusion about opportunity versus entitlement.
