# Colorado Rightly Rejects State Favoritism Toward Political Parties

Colorado is taking a principled stand against government playing favorites with political parties—a move conservatives say restores proper separation between state power and partisan machinery.

According to Complete Colorado, state officials have moved to eliminate special recognition and privileges previously granted to political parties through government channels. The reasoning is straightforward: government resources and official endorsements belong to taxpayers and citizens, not to Democratic or Republican party machines.

This matters because it cuts to a core conservative principle—limiting government's ability to pick winners and losers, even in the political arena. When state agencies grant preferential treatment to any party, they're using taxpayer dollars and official authority to advantage one faction over another. That's exactly the kind of government overreach conservatives oppose, regardless of which party benefits.

The move also signals Colorado's willingness to enforce institutional boundaries that progressives often blur. While the left frequently uses government platforms to amplify preferred causes and candidates, this decision reasserts that state neutrality isn't partisan—it's constitutional.

The practical impact: Colorado taxpayers stop funding political party infrastructure through the back door of government recognition and support.

Watch whether other states follow Colorado's lead, and whether Democratic-controlled legislatures elsewhere resist similar reforms.
