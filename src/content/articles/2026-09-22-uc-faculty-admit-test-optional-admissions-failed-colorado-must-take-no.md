# UC Faculty Admit Test-Optional Admissions Failed—Time to Restore Standards

University of California faculty are now calling on their system to reverse course on test-optional admissions, acknowledging what conservatives have argued for years: lowering standards doesn't help students—it hurts them. The Washington Examiner reports that UC-Berkeley and hundreds of other schools across the nation have spent years experimenting with dropping SAT requirements, and the results have been "startling" in their failure.

The collapse of test-optional policies matters for Colorado. State universities—including CU Boulder and Colorado State—have adopted similar approaches, potentially weakening academic rigor and graduate readiness at institutions taxpayers fund. When admissions standards slip, completion rates and job-market competitiveness suffer, leaving students and families worse off.

Conservatives have long argued that standardized testing, despite its flaws, provides objective measurement of college readiness. Abandoning those metrics in the name of equity doesn't level the playing field—it obscures which students are prepared and which aren't, leading to higher dropout rates and wasted tuition dollars.

The UC faculty reversal is a quiet vindication of merit-based admissions. Colorado policymakers and university trustees should take note: lowering standards to boost enrollment numbers is a false economy that ultimately harms students and wastes public resources.

Watch whether Colorado's higher-education leadership will follow UC's lead and restore testing requirements—or double down on ideology over outcomes.
