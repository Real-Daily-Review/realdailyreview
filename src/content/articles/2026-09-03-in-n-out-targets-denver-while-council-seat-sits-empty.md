# In-N-Out Targets Denver Vacant Lot While City Council Seat Remains Unfilled

Private enterprise is stepping in to revitalize Denver's blighted commercial real estate while city government struggles to fill a vacant council seat, underscoring the difference between market-driven recovery and bureaucratic gridlock.

California-based In-N-Out Burger has submitted a concept plan to develop a central Denver location on a long-vacant restaurant property, according to 9News Denver. The move signals investor confidence in Denver's core despite years of progressive policies that have left downtown struggling with crime, homelessness, and commercial vacancy.

Meanwhile, Denver voters will choose among 10 candidates in a special November election to fill the at-large City Council seat vacated by Sarah Parady's August resignation, the Denver Post reports. The race comes as the council continues pushing tax increases and regulatory expansions that conservatives argue have made Denver less business-friendly.

**The conservative takeaway:** In-N-Out's interest proves that Denver can still attract quality employers—but only when private operators, not city planners, lead the way. The company's willingness to invest in vacant space shows what happens when profit motive, not government mandates, drives development decisions.

**Watch next:** Whether Denver's special election produces a council member who will actually reduce regulations and welcome business investment, or another progressive rubber-stamp for downtown's failed experiment in government-managed recovery.
