# Illegal Immigrant Lawsuits Expose Breakdown in Justice System Accountability

A convicted murderer who entered the country illegally is now suing for $75 million and U.S. citizenship—a stunning illustration of how broken immigration enforcement has become under progressive policies that prioritize criminal defendants over victims.

Brandon Ortiz-Vite, convicted of murdering Michigan resident Ruby Garcia, filed a handwritten lawsuit against Trump demanding cash, a public apology, and citizenship (Fox News). Meanwhile, in Missouri, a county prosecutor was removed from office after allegedly conducting an affair with an illegal immigrant her office was investigating for sexual assault—then failing to prosecute him because of the relationship (The Daily Wire).

These cases underscore what conservatives have long warned: sanctuary policies, prosecutorial discretion run amok, and immigration enforcement collapse create a two-tiered system where criminal aliens receive legal protection while American victims are left behind. When prosecutors prioritize relationships over justice, and convicted murderers believe they have grounds to sue for citizenship, the rule of law itself is in jeopardy.

Colorado's own sanctuary policies—particularly in Denver and Boulder—have invited similar vulnerabilities. Local law enforcement's refusal to cooperate with federal immigration authorities means dangerous criminals can slip through the cracks while taxpayers foot the bill for their incarceration and litigation.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to strengthen cooperation with ICE or doubles down on sanctuary protections ahead of 2026.
