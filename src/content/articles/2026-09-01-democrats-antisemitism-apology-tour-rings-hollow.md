# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Abdul El-Sayed's carefully staged apology tour with Michigan's Democratic Jewish Caucus won't solve the left's deepening antisemitism crisis. The Michigan Democrat met with Jewish leaders to walk back his association with Hasan Piker, a streamer known for anti-American rhetoric and antisemitic commentary, but refused to fully denounce him—drawing sharp criticism from Republican Mike Rogers, who called the mea culpa a "sham apology" (Fox News).

The real problem: Democrats aren't actually distancing themselves from Piker. The Washington Examiner reports that only six Democrats have explicitly condemned Piker's latest rant, which blamed Jews for failing to reject their own culture before violent retaliation. That's a pathetically small number for a party claiming moral leadership on tolerance.

This matters beyond Michigan. Colorado Democrats have increasingly embraced progressive activists and influencers without vetting their records on antisemitism, Israel, and basic decency. When national figures like El-Sayed treat apologies as public-relations exercises rather than genuine reckoning, it signals to the activist base that these views carry no real cost.

Republicans have seized on the hypocrisy: Democrats lecture conservatives endlessly about extremism while harboring their own fringe elements. Colorado voters should watch whether state Democratic leaders—from Gov. Jared Polis on down—will actually break with this crowd or simply offer more hollow apologies.
