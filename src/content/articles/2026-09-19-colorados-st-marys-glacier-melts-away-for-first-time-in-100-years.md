# St. Mary's Glacier Vanishes: Colorado's Climate Reality Check

Colorado's iconic St. Mary's Glacier—a perennial snowfield that survived over a century of summers—has completely melted for the first time on record, according to reporting from the *Colorado Sun* and KDVR Fox31 Denver.

The disappearance follows a record-hot summer in the state, with snow and ice melting faster than seasonal snowfall can replenish it. Visitors to the glacier near Idaho Springs in Clear Creek County are arriving to find nothing but bare rock where the frozen landmark once stood.

The development raises hard questions about Colorado's water future. While the *Colorado Sun* notes that future snowfalls could theoretically restore the glacier, conservatives and water managers should focus on what this means for the state's already-strained water supplies—particularly as Colorado faces competing demands from agriculture, municipalities, and downstream states under the Colorado River Compact.

This is not primarily a case for more federal climate regulation or green-energy mandates, as the left typically frames glacier loss. Rather, it's a wake-up call for Colorado to invest in proven water infrastructure: reservoirs, efficient irrigation, and responsible snowpack management—not ideological restrictions on energy development that weaken the state's economy while doing nothing to restore mountain ice.
