# Trump Signals Iran Peace Deal Possible, With Threats Attached

President Trump said Monday that a deal with Iran could be "within reach" following productive talks over the last 48 hours, according to CBS News. Yet he simultaneously threatened Iran with intensified military strikes if it refuses to negotiate, posting on social media that the country would be bombed at a "much higher level" if it doesn't agree to terms, per CNBC.

The apparent shift marks what The Hill describes as a "sudden pivot toward peace deal," with Washington and Tehran reportedly closing in on a framework. Trump himself has tempered expectations, injecting "a note of caution" into the momentum, according to BBC World.

The mixed messaging—combining diplomatic overtures with military threats—reflects the administration's dual-track approach. Financial markets responded positively to deal prospects: stocks rose and oil prices fell on the news, per CNBC.

The potential agreement comes as regional tensions remain high, with Israel conducting bombing campaigns in Beirut, according to Al Jazeera.

Experts are divided on prospects. PBS NewsHour convened analysts including Alan Eyre, a former Obama administration negotiator on Iran's nuclear deal, and Miad Maleki of the Foundation for the Defense of Democracies to assess the framework's viability.

Watch for whether Iran formally responds to Trump's ultimatum and whether concrete terms emerge from the reported negotiations.
