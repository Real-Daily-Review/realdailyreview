# Permitting Reform Gains Traction as Rare Bipartisan Priority

Washington is finally getting serious about cutting the red tape strangling American infrastructure—and Colorado's energy and construction sectors should pay close attention.

Permitting reform, long discussed but rarely enacted, is emerging as genuine common ground in a fractured Congress. According to the *Washington Examiner*, stakeholders across industries, regions, and political perspectives are coalescing around one urgent idea: America needs to build.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. Energy development, mining, water infrastructure, and commercial construction all languish under Byzantine federal permitting processes that can stretch projects years beyond necessity. Conservatives have long argued that excessive environmental reviews, duplicative agency approvals, and litigation-friendly timelines kill jobs and investment while enriching lawyers and delaying critical infrastructure.

The shift is significant because permitting reform typically divides Republicans (pro-development) from Democrats (cautious on environmental grounds). That both sides now see the urgency suggests real momentum—though the devil will be in the details. Any reform must actually streamline timelines and reduce bureaucratic veto points, not merely shuffle paperwork between agencies.

Colorado taxpayers and energy workers should monitor whether Congress translates this bipartisan sentiment into actual legislation that cuts permitting timelines and limits frivolous litigation delays.
