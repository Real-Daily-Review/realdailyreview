# Social Media Is Undermining Immigrant Assimilation, Conservative Analysis Warns

Social media has fundamentally altered how immigrants integrate into American society—and not for the better, according to conservative analysis in the *Washington Examiner*.

The Left routinely invokes the immigrant-built-America narrative to deflect criticism of ICE enforcement and deportation measures. But conservatives argue the comparison ignores a critical difference: modern immigrants face technological barriers to assimilation that their predecessors did not.

Historical immigrants—whether colonial-era arrivals or 20th-century newcomers—lacked the ability to maintain separate cultural enclaves through digital means. They had to learn English, adopt local customs, and integrate into their communities out of necessity. Today's immigrants can remain linguistically and culturally isolated indefinitely through social media platforms, ethnic online networks, and streaming content in their native languages.

This dynamic matters for Colorado, where immigration policy directly affects labor markets, housing demand, and community cohesion in growing metros like Denver and Boulder. If assimilation stalls, the economic and social benefits of immigration—which conservatives support when coupled with rule of law—diminish.

The conservative case isn't anti-immigrant; it's pro-assimilation. Successful integration strengthens communities and labor markets alike.
