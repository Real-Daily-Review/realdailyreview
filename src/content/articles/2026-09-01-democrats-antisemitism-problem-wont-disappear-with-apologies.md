# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed's carefully staged apology to Jewish Democrats won't solve the party's deepening credibility crisis on antisemitism. El-Sayed met with Michigan's Democratic Jewish Caucus to walk back his association with Hasan Piker, a streamer known for anti-American rhetoric and antisemitic commentary, but refused to fully distance himself from the controversial figure (Fox News).

The problem runs deeper than one candidate's damage control. According to the Washington Examiner, only six Democrats have explicitly condemned Piker's latest antisemitic remarks—a stunning silence from a party that claims to champion civil rights. Republicans argue this selective outrage exposes the left's hollow commitment to fighting bigotry when it emerges from their own coalition.

El-Sayed's "sham apology," as GOP operative Mike Rogers characterized it, illustrates a broader Democratic vulnerability heading into 2024. When party leaders tolerate or minimize antisemitism from influential voices within their base, they forfeit moral authority on the issue entirely.
