# Boulder Firebomber Pleads Guilty to Federal Hate Crimes; Life Sentence Secured

Mohamed Sabry Soliman, who launched a deadly Molotov cocktail attack on Boulder's Pearl Street Mall in 2025, pleaded guilty to federal hate crimes this week—closing the door on a case that tested Colorado's justice system and shook one of the state's most liberal communities.

Prosecutors secured a plea deal requiring a life sentence plus 50 years on federal charges, sparing Soliman the death penalty (Colorado Sun). He already faces life imprisonment on state charges (CPR News), meaning he will spend the remainder of his life incarcerated. The federal indictment included nine hate crime counts tied to the attack (9News Denver).

A victim told KDVR Fox31 Denver that Soliman's guilty plea and admission represented "an important step to moving forward," offering closure after months of legal proceedings.

The case underscores how Colorado courts handle violent extremism and hate-motivated attacks. While the plea avoids a protracted trial, conservatives and crime victims alike have emphasized the importance of swift, certain justice—and the finality this guilty plea delivers.

Watch for any appeals or federal sentencing hearings that could delay final resolution or become a platform for further legal maneuvering.
