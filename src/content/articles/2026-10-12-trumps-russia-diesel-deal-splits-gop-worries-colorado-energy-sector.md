# Trump's Diesel Deal with Russia Draws Fire from Right—and Colorado Energy Interests Should Pay Attention

President Trump's announced diesel agreement with Russia has triggered rare pushback from Republican lawmakers and conservative outlets, raising questions about energy policy and geopolitical strategy that could ripple through Colorado's oil and gas sector.

According to *Fox News*, some GOP lawmakers have voiced "staunch opposition" to the deal. *National Review* went further, calling it "a gross abuse of power" that delivers "an absolute coup for Putin" while offering "no economic benefits for the U.S." The criticism cuts across conservative lines—not over Trump's pro-business instincts, but over whether importing Russian diesel actually serves American energy independence or undercuts domestic producers.

Ukrainian President Zelensky added skepticism from abroad, arguing (*Just The News*) that the import volume is too small to meaningfully lower global prices—raising the question: what's the point?

For Colorado, this matters. The state's oil and gas industry depends on stable domestic markets and predictable policy. Importing Russian diesel could depress prices and undercut Colorado producers already navigating tight margins. Republicans who champion American energy dominance worry the deal signals weakness on energy independence—a core conservative principle.

Conservatives should watch whether this deal actually moves forward and what volume of Russian diesel enters U.S. markets; if it undercuts domestic producers without delivering cheaper fuel to consumers, it becomes a cautionary tale about dealmaking divorced from principle.
