# Parasitic Outbreak Spreads Across 18 States as Health Officials Scramble for Answers

Colorado residents should brace for potential exposure to cyclosporiasis, a parasitic infection causing severe intestinal illness that's now been reported across 18 states this year.

Cases of the infection—marked by what health officials bluntly call "explosive diarrhea"—have surged dramatically. According to the New York Post, cases in the hardest-hit state jumped over 400% in a single week, with the CDC likely undercounting the true number of infections nationwide (New York Post).

Health agencies remain in the dark about the source. 9News Denver reports that officials still haven't pinpointed what's driving the outbreaks, leaving public health departments scrambling to contain spread and identify contaminated food or water sources.

For Colorado, this matters. State health officials need to determine whether the parasite has reached local food supplies, water systems, or agricultural products. Cyclosporiasis typically spreads through contaminated produce or water—a particular concern in a state with significant agricultural exports and tourism.

Conservatives should demand transparency from state and federal health agencies: What's the actual infection count? Which food sources are implicated? And why has it taken this long to identify the cause? Government's primary duty is protecting public health, not burying data.

Watch for Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment guidance on food safety and whether any local outbreaks emerge.
