# Turkey Arms Deal Raises Red Flags for U.S. Security

The Trump administration's consideration of F-35 fighter jet sales to Turkey is drawing sharp pushback from defense hawks who argue Ankara cannot be trusted with America's most advanced military technology.

National Review warns that selling F-35s to Turkey would "needlessly jeopardize American security and service personnel," citing Turkey's unreliable NATO partnership and past flirtations with adversarial powers. The Washington Examiner's opinion section goes further, challenging the president's characterization of Turkey as a "great ally," noting that Turkish leadership has openly contemplated joining conflicts against both Israel and the United States.

Republicans and defense conservatives argue Turkey's NATO membership masks a pattern of strategic unreliability: the country has deepened ties with Russia despite sanctions, purchased Russian air-defense systems incompatible with NATO standards, and maintained ambiguous positions during Middle Eastern conflicts. Selling advanced F-35 technology risks exposing classified systems to Russian intelligence networks through Turkish channels.

The White House has not formally announced a sale, but the diplomatic signals suggest serious consideration. Supporters of the deal argue it strengthens NATO's eastern flank and keeps Turkey within the Western alliance.

**Watch for:** Congressional Republican opposition and whether the Defense Department's security assessment blocks the sale outright.
