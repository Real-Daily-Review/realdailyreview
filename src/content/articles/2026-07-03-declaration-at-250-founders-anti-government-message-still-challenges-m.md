# Declaration at 250: America's Founding Principles Still Challenge Modern Government Overreach

As the Declaration of Independence turns 250, conservatives are reminded that the Founders' core grievances—unchecked taxation, regulatory control, and government expansion—remain strikingly relevant to today's political battles.

The Declaration's economic DNA centered on what Breitbart calls "America First Economics": control over taxes, immigration policy, trade, and limiting government growth. Those weren't abstract ideals—they were direct responses to British tyranny. Jefferson's Monticello, now a vibrant museum and scholarly center, stands as a living reminder of the principles that animated the Revolution.

For Colorado, the parallel is uncomfortable. State lawmakers have repeatedly pursued tax hikes on energy companies, implemented strict regulations on water usage and land development, and expanded government agencies at a pace the Founders would have recognized as creeping tyranny. Democrats argue such measures protect the environment and fund essential services. Republicans counter that Colorado's regulatory burden stifles business growth, drives out energy investment, and erodes the individual liberty the Declaration promised.

The 250th anniversary offers a useful reset: Are we governing closer to 1776 principles—limited government, low taxes, free markets—or drifting toward the centralized control the Founders rejected?

Watch whether Colorado Republicans use this moment to challenge the state's expanding regulatory state and demand tax relief for working families and businesses.
