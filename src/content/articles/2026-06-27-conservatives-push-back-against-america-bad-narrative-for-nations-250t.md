# America's 250th Should Celebrate Liberty, Not Self-Loathing

As the nation marks its 250th anniversary this summer, conservatives are pushing back against what they see as a corrosive "America Bad" narrative dominating the celebration.

National Review argues the commemorations should highlight America's genuine achievements—including often-overlooked international support for the Revolution, such as Spain's crucial aid to the Patriot cause—rather than dwelling on national guilt and oppression themes. The Washington Examiner emphasizes that anniversary events from the National Mall to Philadelphia should center on founding principles, individual liberty, and shared heritage.

For Colorado conservatives, the stakes matter. How America's founding story gets told shapes education curricula, civic understanding, and the philosophical foundation for debates over constitutional rights—from Second Amendment protections to property rights and limited government. If the 250th becomes a vehicle for progressive revisionism, it undercuts the constitutional arguments conservatives rely on to challenge government overreach.

Republicans argue that authentic commemoration means honest reckoning with both achievements and failures, not self-flagellation. They contend that celebrating American exceptionalism and the revolutionary idea of individual liberty strengthens the nation's civic fabric.

Watch whether Colorado's own 250th events—from local commemorations to school programming—embrace this liberty-centered framing or adopt the progressive guilt-and-grievance model.
