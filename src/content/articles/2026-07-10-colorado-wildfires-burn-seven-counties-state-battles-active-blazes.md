# Colorado Wildfires Spread Across Seven Counties as State Battles Active Blazes

Active wildfires are burning across Pueblo, Custer, Dolores, Fremont, Mesa, Lake, and Ouray counties, with crews working to contain the threat to homes, property, and livelihoods across the state (9News Denver). The fires come as Colorado faces the dual challenge of suppression efforts and the cascading impacts on residents—smoke exposure, property damage, and psychological strain from evacuation orders and uncertainty.

State officials are reminding Coloradans of available mental health resources as the stress of wildfire season compounds. BHA Commissioner Beasley noted that "the stress and worry of wildfires, along with the physical impacts of smoke and potential damage to lives, homes and other property, can be overwhelming" (KDVR Fox31).

Conservatives have long argued that aggressive forest management—thinning overgrown timber, removing deadwood, and allowing controlled burns—remains critical to reducing catastrophic fire risk. Yet environmental regulations and litigation have often blocked or delayed such preventive measures. The state's ability to act swiftly on fuel reduction and land stewardship directly affects whether future fire seasons prove less destructive.

Taxpayers should watch whether state and federal land managers prioritize practical forest management over regulatory delays in the coming months.
