# CDC Expands Cyclosporiasis Outbreak Alert to Six More States

A parasitic infection linked to contaminated lettuce has spread to six additional states, the CDC reported, bringing the outbreak to more than 20,000 cases nationwide since May. The microscopic parasite—found in human feces—has caused hundreds of hospitalizations and at least two deaths, according to *Just The News*.

Health officials say the outbreak may be peaking, though cases continue to emerge across the country. The contamination points to serious gaps in food safety oversight and supply-chain traceability—issues that should concern Colorado consumers and agricultural producers alike. Colorado's produce industry depends on consumer confidence; any outbreak tied to local or regional farms could devastate sales and livelihoods.

The outbreak underscores a conservative concern: federal food-safety bureaucracies often move slowly to identify sources and warn the public. While the CDC has expanded its alert, questions remain about why contamination wasn't caught earlier and how farms can better protect their crops from human-waste contamination in irrigation water and soil.

Colorado agriculture stakeholders should demand faster outbreak response protocols and clearer guidance on water-source testing—without imposing costly new regulations that burden small and mid-sized farms already struggling with labor and input costs.
