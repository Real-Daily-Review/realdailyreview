# America's Founding Principles Still Matter—And Colorado Should Remember Them

As America marks 250 years since 1776, conservatives are calling for a return to the Founders' radical idea: that government exists to protect individual rights, not expand its own power.

The Washington Examiner argues that the American founding was historically unique—the first nation built explicitly on the principle of inalienable human rights rather than conquest or dynastic rule. That philosophical foundation, conservatives say, has been eroded by decades of regulatory growth, government overreach, and progressive policies that treat rights as privileges granted by the state.

For Colorado, this matters concretely. When the state imposes new environmental regulations on energy development, restricts water rights, or expands gun control measures, conservatives argue these violate the Founders' vision of limited government protecting property and liberty. The same applies to tax hikes and spending expansions that treat earnings as government resources to redistribute.

The conservative case is straightforward: the Founders understood that concentrated government power threatens freedom. Modern progressives, by contrast, see government as the primary tool for solving social problems—a fundamentally different worldview.

Colorado voters and taxpayers should watch whether state lawmakers invoke constitutional limits on government power or continue expanding it in the name of solving problems.
