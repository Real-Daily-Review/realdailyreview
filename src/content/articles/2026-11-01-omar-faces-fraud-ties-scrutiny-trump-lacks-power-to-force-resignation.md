# Omar Scandal Deepens, But Constitutional Reality Limits Trump's Options

Minnesota Democrat Ilhan Omar faces mounting scrutiny over alleged ties to individuals involved in fraud schemes, but President Trump's calls for her resignation bump against a constitutional wall: only the House can remove its own members.

Fox News reports that Trump has demanded Omar's ouster, yet the Constitution vests expulsion power exclusively in Congress. Republicans, meanwhile, are pressing harder. Minnesota State Rep. Kristin Robbins told The Daily Wire that Omar was "thick as thieves" with people caught up in a "massive fraud scandal," describing her as "one fed-up Republican" demanding answers. The New York Times recently exposed another layer to the controversy, though details remain contested.

Republicans argue the allegations warrant serious investigation and potential House disciplinary action. Democrats have largely remained silent or dismissed the claims as politically motivated attacks.

**Colorado angle:** While Omar represents Minnesota, her legal and ethical troubles underscore broader conservative concerns about progressive politicians' accountability—a theme resonating with Colorado Republicans skeptical of Denver's Democratic supermajority.

Conservatives should watch whether House Republicans formally demand an ethics investigation or expulsion proceedings, and whether Democrats will defend Omar or distance themselves from the controversy.
