# Federal Review Uncovers Hundreds of Thousands of Noncitizens on Voter Rolls in Key States

The Department of Homeland Security has identified at least 278,000 noncitizens registered to vote across four critical swing states—California, New Jersey, Nevada, and Pennsylvania—raising fresh alarm among conservatives about election integrity and the consequences of lax voter verification standards.

According to reporting from Fox News and the New York Post, the DHS discovery emerged after those states declined to use a new federal citizenship database designed to cross-check voter registrations. The finding underscores a core conservative concern: without robust verification mechanisms, election rolls become vulnerable to administrative error or worse.

Republicans argue this represents a systemic failure of basic election security. They contend that states have a constitutional duty to maintain accurate voter rolls and that noncitizen registration—whether intentional or bureaucratic negligence—undermines the legitimacy of federal elections and dilutes the voting power of citizens.

Democrats have not directly addressed the DHS findings in available reporting, though they have historically resisted stricter voter ID and citizenship verification measures, citing concerns about voter suppression.

**Colorado angle:** While Colorado was not flagged in this review, the state's automatic voter registration system and its acceptance of federal ID alternatives for voting raise questions about whether similar vulnerabilities exist on Colorado's rolls.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado election officials will voluntarily audit voter registration data against federal citizenship databases—or resist such scrutiny.
