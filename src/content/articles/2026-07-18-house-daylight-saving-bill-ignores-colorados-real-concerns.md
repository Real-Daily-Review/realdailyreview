# House Pushes Permanent Daylight Saving Time—But Colorado Should Pump the Brakes

Congress is once again trying to impose permanent Daylight Saving Time nationwide, a one-size-fits-all federal mandate that ignores Colorado's unique geography and citizen preferences.

The House has passed legislation to make DST permanent, but the proposal faces real-world headwinds. Airlines warn the change will disrupt airport scheduling and operations across the country (New York Post), a concern that hits Colorado's aviation hubs directly. KDVR reports that not everyone supports the shift—and for good reason.

Conservatives argue this is classic federal overreach: Washington deciding what's best for farmers, ranchers, and families across vastly different time zones and climates. Colorado's high-altitude, early-sunrise geography makes permanent DST particularly problematic for morning routines and school schedules. The state already has the authority under federal law to opt out of DST entirely and remain on Mountain Standard Time year-round—a simpler, more sensible approach than forcing permanent clock changes on everyone.

Republicans counter that this is yet another example of Congress solving problems that don't exist while ignoring legitimate concerns from businesses and citizens. The airline industry's warning signals real economic costs that lawmakers are brushing aside.
