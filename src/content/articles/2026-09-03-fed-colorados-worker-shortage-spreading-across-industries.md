# Colorado's Worker Shortage Deepens as State Grapples With Labor-Market Reality

Colorado's economy is hitting a wall: the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City reports that worker shortages are now spreading across industries and job types, constraining growth across Colorado and neighboring states (CPR News). The shortage, no longer confined to hospitality or construction, is broadening into sectors that typically attract stable workforces—a sign that Colorado's labor market faces structural challenges beyond pandemic-era disruptions.

Conservatives point to regulatory burden and high cost-of-living as culprits. Colorado's aggressive tax policies, housing regulations, and business compliance costs have made the state less competitive for workers and employers alike. When combined with out-of-state migration patterns and workforce participation challenges, the result is predictable: businesses can't find workers, wages rise without productivity gains, and economic growth stalls.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs faces a separate legal battle over municipal election timing. A lawsuit challenges the city's April election schedule under Colorado's 2025 voting law (Complete Colorado)—marking the first enforcement action since the statute took effect. The case signals ongoing disputes over election administration in the state.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado lawmakers will address labor-market competitiveness through tax relief or deregulation, or double down on policies that discourage workforce participation and business investment.
