# Wisconsin Judge's Slap-on-Wrist Sentence Exposes Judicial Activism on Immigration

A former Milwaukee County judge convicted of obstructing an illegal immigrant's arrest walked free Wednesday with nothing but a $5,000 fine—a lenient sentence that underscores how progressive judges are undermining immigration enforcement across the country.

Hannah Dugan, a Circuit Court judge, was found guilty of felony obstruction after she attempted to help an undocumented immigrant evade ICE during a court hearing. U.S. District Judge Lynn Adelman, a Clinton appointee, declined to impose prison time or probation, effectively treating a serious obstruction conviction as a minor infraction (Washington Examiner).

**Republicans argue** this case exemplifies judicial overreach and the consequences of activist judges prioritizing ideology over the rule of law. State Sen. Doug Mastriano pointed to similar failures in Pennsylvania, where an illegal immigrant truck driver killed a state trooper—exposing how lax enforcement and judicial leniency compound border and highway safety failures (Fox News).

For Colorado, this matters. Progressive judges in Denver and Boulder have signaled sympathy toward sanctuary policies and resistance to immigration enforcement cooperation. If federal judges nationwide treat obstruction of ICE as a minor offense, Colorado law enforcement and prosecutors face pressure to do the same—leaving taxpayers and citizens vulnerable.

**Conservatives should watch** whether Colorado's state courts follow this permissive sentencing model or push back against judicial activism that prioritizes illegal immigrants over public safety.
