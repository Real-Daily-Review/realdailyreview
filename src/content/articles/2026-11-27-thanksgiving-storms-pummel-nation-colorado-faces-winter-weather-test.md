# Thanksgiving Storms Hammer Nation; Colorado Braces for Winter Weather Ahead

Torrential rain and dangerous winds battered much of the United States over Thanksgiving, leaving the East Coast underwater and knocking out power to nearly a million customers—even forcing the first-ever postponement of the Macy's parade (KDVR Fox31).

While Colorado largely escaped the worst of the holiday deluge, the state faces its own weather challenges as winter approaches. Heavy precipitation and flooding on both coasts underscore the unpredictability of severe weather and the importance of robust infrastructure resilience—a concern for Colorado communities vulnerable to flash flooding in mountain canyons and along Front Range corridors.

The storms highlight why Colorado needs reliable, diverse energy sources to maintain power during extreme weather events. As the state continues pushing aggressive renewable mandates, conservatives argue that baseload power from natural gas and coal remains essential to grid stability when wind and solar falter during storms. Blackouts across the Eastern Seaboard demonstrate the real-world cost of over-reliance on intermittent renewables without adequate backup capacity.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses should watch whether state regulators use this national weather event to justify accelerated coal plant retirements or further grid restrictions—moves that could leave the state vulnerable to similar outages.
