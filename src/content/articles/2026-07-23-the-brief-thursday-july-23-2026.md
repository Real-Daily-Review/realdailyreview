# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, July 23, 2026

Colorado conservatives have plenty to watch today — from a federal audit hitting the state's Medicaid books to a high-profile ICE enforcement action at Denver International that's already drawing the usual outrage from the left.

## Jordan Moves to Hold Jack Smith Accountable

House Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan issued a criminal referral against former special counsel Jack Smith, accusing him of making false statements to Congress about accessing lawmakers' text messages. Republicans argue this is exactly the kind of accountability the weaponized-prosecution crowd never expected to face. For Colorado conservatives who watched the left cheer on Smith's politically motivated prosecutions, this referral is a long-overdue reminder that no one — not even a Democratic Party hit man with a law degree — is above Congress.

## Colorado Medicaid Owes Feds at Least $8 Million

A federal audit found Colorado's Medicaid program owes the federal government at least $8 million for in-home disability care payments, with an additional $45 million flagged for further review. State officials insist there was no fraud — but Colorado taxpayers should be asking why the books were sloppy enough to trigger a federal inspector general review in the first place. This is what happens when government programs expand faster than anyone can manage them. Conservatives have warned for years that Medicaid bloat invites exactly this kind of fiscal mess.

## ICE Enforces the Law at DIA — Left Erupts

ICE agents detained a 27-year-old woman at Denver International Airport who is accused of overstaying her visa, and predictably, emotions erupted among bystanders and activists. Federal immigration law is not optional, and overstaying a visa is a violation of the terms under which someone was permitted to enter this country. Democrats and their allies in the media will frame this as cruelty. Conservatives call it the rule of law — the same rule of law Colorado's sanctuary-friendly politicians have spent years trying to obstruct.

## Trump Honors Fallen Service Members

President Trump attended a dignified transfer ceremony for four U.S. service members killed in action in Jordan and Iraq, calling them "great heroes." Whatever the political noise of the week, moments like this are a reminder of the cost of American service — and of a commander-in-chief who shows up. Colorado is home to a large and proud military and veteran community, and they deserve a president who treats their sacrifice with the respect it demands.

## House Passes Stock Trading Ban — With Voter ID Attached

The House passed the Stop Insider Trading Act in a bipartisan vote, banning members of Congress from buying individual stocks while in office. Republican leaders added a voter ID requirement to the bill. Democrats who claim to love "clean" legislation will now have to decide whether they hate voter ID more than they hate congressional insider trading. For Colorado conservatives, the voter ID provision is common sense — and it's smart politics to attach it to a bill with genuine popular support.
