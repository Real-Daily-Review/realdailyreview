# Colorado DNA Analyst Misconduct Forces Denver Police to Re-Test 13 Cases

Denver Police are being forced to re-test 13 criminal cases after discovering misconduct by former Colorado Bureau of Investigation forensic DNA analyst Yvonne "Missy" Woods, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver.

The re-testing effort underscores a critical problem: when government forensic labs fail basic quality controls, innocent people can be wrongly convicted and guilty criminals walk free. Colorado taxpayers now foot the bill for correcting these failures.

Woods' misconduct raises hard questions about CBI oversight and the reliability of evidence that may have already sent defendants to prison. Defense attorneys and prosecutors alike will need to review whether her work compromised case integrity—a costly and time-consuming process that erodes public confidence in the criminal justice system.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that government agencies, even those handling life-altering evidence, lack sufficient accountability mechanisms. This case validates those concerns: bureaucratic negligence in a state lab can unravel years of litigation and cost taxpayers hundreds of thousands in re-investigation and potential settlements.

The state must implement stricter forensic lab audits, independent oversight, and consequences for analysts who cut corners—or Colorado's criminal justice system will continue paying the price for government incompetence.

Watch whether the CBI conducts a full audit of Woods' entire case history and what systemic reforms emerge from this scandal.
