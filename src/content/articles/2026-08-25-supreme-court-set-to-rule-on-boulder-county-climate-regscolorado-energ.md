# Boulder County Energy Case Heads to Supreme Court as Colorado Costs Climb

Colorado families and businesses are watching closely as the U.S. Supreme Court prepares to hear *Suncor Energy v. County Commissioners of Boulder County* in October—a case that could determine whether local governments can impose sweeping climate regulations that drive up energy costs.

The Daily Wire reports that national energy costs have already surged nearly 9% since early 2026. If Boulder County's aggressive climate ordinances survive Supreme Court scrutiny, Colorado ratepayers could face even steeper bills. The case centers on whether counties can impose regulations that effectively restrict energy production and development.

Conservatives argue the case tests a fundamental question: Can unelected local boards override state energy policy and impose de facto bans on fossil fuel operations? Republicans contend that such overreach kills jobs, raises costs for working families, and surrenders Colorado's energy independence to ideological activists.

The left frames the case as protecting local environmental authority and fighting climate change, but conservatives counter that Boulder County's approach is a backdoor ban that bypasses the legislature and ignores Colorado's balanced energy economy.

National Review emphasizes that the Court's legitimacy depends on principled jurisprudence, not political outcomes—a reminder that this case should turn on constitutional law, not climate ideology.
