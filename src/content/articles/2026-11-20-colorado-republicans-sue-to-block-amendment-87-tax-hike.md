# Colorado Republicans Challenge Amendment 87 Tax Hike in Court

Colorado Republicans are fighting back against voters' approval of Amendment 87, arguing the graduated income tax violates the state constitution's Taxpayer's Bill of Rights.

The conservative nonprofit Advance Colorado filed suit to block implementation of the measure, which passed in November (CPR News). Republicans contend that the amendment—designed to raise income taxes on higher earners—circumvents TABOR protections that require voter approval for tax increases and spending hikes. The lawsuit also alleges voters were misled by official Blue Book materials (Colorado Sun).

**The conservative argument:** Supporters of the lawsuit say Amendment 87 represents exactly the kind of tax-and-spend overreach TABOR was designed to prevent. They argue that even voter-approved measures cannot override constitutional guardrails protecting taxpayers from unlimited government growth. For Colorado businesses and high earners already burdened by state regulations, this represents another assault on economic competitiveness.

**Democrats counter** that Amendment 87 received clear voter mandate and that the graduated tax structure is constitutional and necessary to fund schools and infrastructure.

The measure would generate hundreds of millions annually by raising income tax rates on top earners—a direct hit to Colorado's business climate and job creators during an already uncertain economic period.

Watch whether Colorado courts side with Republicans' constitutional argument or defer to voter will.
