# Measles Surges Across Northeast as Blue-State Governance Fails

Democratic-led states are grappling with measles outbreaks that haven't been seen in decades, raising hard questions about public health priorities and border security. New York Governor Kathy Hochul declared a state of emergency after roughly 100 measles cases emerged, while Pennsylvania reported over 1,000 cases—the largest outbreak in three decades (Just The News). The CDC confirms nearly 3,900 U.S. cases this year, the highest since 1991.

The timing is politically awkward for Democrats. Even liberal actress Debra Messing abandoned Governor Hochul's re-election bid, citing affordability and quality-of-life concerns that plague blue states (Just The News). Hochul's emergency order signals alarm, but critics argue Democratic policies—including lax immigration enforcement and sanctuary-state rules—have created conditions where vaccine-preventable diseases spread unchecked.

Colorado conservatives should note: while measles remains rare here, the outbreak underscores how open borders and weak vetting of incoming populations pose genuine public health risks. The left's reflexive resistance to immigration enforcement and medical screening has real consequences.
