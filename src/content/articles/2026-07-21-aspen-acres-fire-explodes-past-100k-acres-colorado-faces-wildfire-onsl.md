# Colorado Wildfire Season Intensifies as Aspen Acres Fire Explodes to Over 100,000 Acres

Hot, dry conditions are pushing Colorado's wildfire season into overdrive, with the Aspen Acres Fire in Southern Colorado now consuming over 101,000 acres and forcing mandatory evacuations across Custer County. According to KDVR, the blaze is 61% contained despite 1,256 personnel fighting it—a sign that even massive resource deployment struggles against weather-driven fire growth.

Officials have reinstated evacuation orders as crews patrol the western edge of the fire zone, with multiple other blazes—Hartman Hill, Gold Mountain, Ferris, Willow, Fishhook, and Elk—adding to the strain on Colorado's firefighting capacity (Colorado Sun). The rapid expansion underscores a hard reality: no amount of government spending on firefighting can overcome the physics of drought and heat.

Conservatives have long warned that Colorado's forest management policies—shaped by environmental regulations that restrict timber harvesting and controlled burns—leave our state's wildlands as tinderboxes. While Democrats tout "climate action," Coloradans are watching their homes threatened by fuel-choked forests that could have been thinned under less restrictive land-use rules.

Watch whether state lawmakers will finally loosen environmental restrictions on forest management, or double down on climate rhetoric while Coloradans lose everything to preventable megafires.
