# USPS Finalizes Mail-Voting Rule, But Courts Block Implementation

The U.S. Postal Service has published a 95-page rule requiring states to share voter lists before processing mail-in ballots—a move conservatives argue is necessary to prevent fraud, though federal injunctions currently block its enforcement.

The rule, finalized Friday following President Trump's March executive order, would restrict mail voting in states refusing to provide voter registration data to USPS (Fox News Politics). Republicans argue voter list verification is a basic election-security measure; Democrats and voting-rights groups contend it creates barriers to ballot access and could disenfranchise eligible voters.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado, a mail-voting state since 2014, maintains its own voter rolls and election procedures. The rule's fate could reshape how the state processes ballots—a significant issue for Colorado Republicans concerned about election integrity and Democrats protective of the state's mail-voting infrastructure.

The rule cannot take effect unless courts lift existing injunctions blocking Trump's executive order (9News Denver). Legal challenges are ongoing, meaning implementation remains uncertain heading into 2024 and beyond.

**Watch:** Whether federal courts ultimately allow the rule to proceed—and how Colorado's Democratic-led election officials respond if forced to comply.
