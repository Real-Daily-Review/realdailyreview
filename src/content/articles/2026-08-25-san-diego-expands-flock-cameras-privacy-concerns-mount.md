# San Diego's Flock Camera Push Exposes Surveillance Divide

San Diego city officials are doubling down on Flock license plate reader cameras—automated surveillance tools that scan and log vehicle movements—despite growing privacy concerns from both left and right. A man dressed as Darth Vader even testified in favor of the cameras at a recent city council hearing, joking that "the Emperor is a fan of Flock" (9News Denver).

The pitch from law enforcement is straightforward: Flock cameras help solve crimes and locate stolen vehicles. National Review argues that rolling back the technology would "deny law enforcement a valuable asset," though the outlet acknowledges the "tectonic change" in surveillance infrastructure that's been building for decades.

But conservatives and privacy advocates raise legitimate questions. These systems create permanent records of where citizens drive—a tool ripe for mission creep, political targeting, and abuse. Without strict limits on data retention, access, and use, Flock cameras represent exactly the kind of government overreach that should alarm anyone who values the Fourth Amendment and individual liberty.

**Colorado angle:** Denver and other Front Range cities have quietly deployed similar plate-reader networks. Coloradans should demand transparency about how local police use this data and push for legislation requiring warrants before accessing records.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers introduce bills to regulate—or ban—warrantless license plate tracking in 2026.
