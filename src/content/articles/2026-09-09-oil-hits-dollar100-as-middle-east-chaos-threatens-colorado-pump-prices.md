# Oil Spikes Past $100 as Middle East Chaos Threatens Colorado Drivers and Energy Workers

Middle East attacks on oil infrastructure have pushed crude above $100 per barrel for the first time in six weeks, and Colorado families will feel it at the pump.

The price surge follows coordinated strikes on regional oil facilities and shipping lanes, according to 9News Denver and Just The News. The national average for gasoline hit $4.22 per gallon Wednesday, with California drivers paying the steepest premiums and Indiana the lowest.

For Colorado—a state with significant oil and gas production and a workforce dependent on energy development—the implications cut both ways. Higher crude prices support domestic producers and energy jobs, but they also mean higher costs for consumers, farmers, and businesses across the state.

The Biden-Harris administration's hostility toward domestic energy development compounds the problem. While the White House has restricted federal leasing and pipeline projects, geopolitical instability abroad now forces Americans to rely on volatile global markets. Republicans argue this underscores why energy independence through robust domestic production matters; instead of drilling more in Colorado and the Rockies, the administration has strangled U.S. output while gas prices climb.

Watch for whether Colorado's congressional delegation demands expedited permitting for domestic energy projects—or whether they'll continue prioritizing environmental restrictions over consumer relief.
