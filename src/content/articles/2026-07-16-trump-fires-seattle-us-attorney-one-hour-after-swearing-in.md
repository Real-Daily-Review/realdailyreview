# Trump Fires Seattle U.S. Attorney Within an Hour of Swearing In

President Trump fired newly appointed U.S. attorney Roger Rogoff less than an hour after he was sworn in Wednesday, reasserting executive authority over federal prosecutors against what conservatives view as judicial overreach.

Rogoff, a former Washington state judge, was sworn in by federal judges in Seattle but was terminated by Trump administration officials before the day ended, with Todd Blanche announcing the firing on X. The move sets up a potential legal battle over presidential removal powers.

**The conservative argument:** The Daily Wire frames this as Trump "standing his ground against judicial overreach," arguing the President—not local judges—controls the Executive Branch. Conservatives contend that courts overstepped by effectively installing a prosecutor without proper executive vetting, and that Trump's swift action reestablished proper constitutional separation of powers.

**The broader question:** Legal scholars will likely debate whether Trump has authority to fire a court-appointed U.S. attorney, a relatively rare scenario. The Washington Examiner notes this could trigger litigation over presidential removal authority.

**Colorado angle:** While this case involves Washington state, it signals how the Trump administration will handle federal law enforcement nationwide—relevant to Colorado's federal prosecutors and how they'll approach cases involving energy development, immigration enforcement, and Second Amendment issues.

Watch whether this firing triggers congressional or judicial challenges that could reshape how U.S. attorneys are appointed and removed.
