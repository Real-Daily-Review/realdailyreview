# A Generation Removed From 9/11 Faces a Nation Still at War With Itself

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students born after the towers fell are learning about terrorism as history—while America's ideological defenses against the threats that spawned it continue to erode.

CPR News reports that younger Coloradans are grappling with 9/11 as a distant national trauma rather than lived experience. That generational distance matters. When terrorism becomes textbook material instead of memory, the vigilance that once unified the country fades fast.

National Review argues the real problem runs deeper: while Americans have largely moved past the immediate terror, the ideological forces behind the attacks—what the outlet calls "Sharia supremacism"—continue advancing globally and within Western institutions. Conservatives worry that two decades of nation-building fatigue, combined with progressive reluctance to name religious extremism plainly, have left America intellectually unprepared for persistent threats.

For Colorado specifically, this matters to military families, defense contractors, and communities hosting federal counterterrorism operations. A generation that doesn't viscerally understand why these institutions exist is a generation vulnerable to defunding them or dismantling them through neglect.

Watch whether Colorado schools and universities teach 9/11 as a cautionary tale about the cost of unpreparedness—or sanitize it into a generic "diversity and tolerance" lesson that obscures the actual enemy.
