# DU Sends 40 Students to Sundance Film Festival

Denver University is bankrolling a new film festival class that will send about 24 enrolled students plus 16 additional applicants to Utah for the Sundance Film Festival, according to 9News Denver. The university is positioning the program as an educational opportunity for students interested in cinema and festival culture.

The move reflects DU's broader investment in arts programming and experiential learning. Meanwhile, the Sundance Institute has announced its Legacy program, which will feature classic films alongside new selections at the festival (KDVR Fox31).

**The conservative angle:** While arts education has merit, Colorado taxpayers should ask hard questions about university spending priorities. DU's tuition already ranks among the highest in the region—and students are increasingly burdened by debt. Before universities fund expensive out-of-state trips for select students, they should justify why this spending serves the broader student body better than tuition relief, core curriculum improvements, or job-ready technical programs.

This is emblematic of how higher education institutions prioritize prestige projects over fiscal discipline. When universities treat student fees as blank checks for experiential extras, they're pricing out working-class Coloradans and inflating the cost of a degree.

**Watch next:** Whether DU discloses the full cost of this program and whether other Colorado universities follow suit with similar festival trips.
