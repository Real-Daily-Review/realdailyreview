# DU Sends 40 Students to Sundance on Taxpayer Dime While Colorado Families Struggle with Tuition Costs

Denver University is bankrolling a Sundance Film Festival junket for 40 students—24 enrolled in a dedicated class and 16 additional applicants—in what amounts to another example of how higher education prioritizes luxury experiences over affordability (9News Denver). The university is footing the bill to transport these students to Utah for the prestigious festival, part of a broader "Legacy program" showcasing selected films (KDVR Fox31).

**The conservative concern:** While Colorado families face skyrocketing tuition and student debt, DU is spending institutional resources on festival trips that benefit a tiny fraction of its student body. This reflects the misaligned priorities of academia—experiential perks for the few rather than cost containment for the many. Taxpayers and parents already shoulder the burden of inflated higher-education costs; subsidized travel to industry events adds insult to injury.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that universities should demonstrate restraint on discretionary spending and redirect resources toward core academics and tuition relief. Instead, DU's approach mirrors the broader higher-ed problem: bloated administrative budgets and lifestyle amenities that drive up the cost of a degree.

**Watch:** Whether DU discloses the total cost of this program and whether Colorado legislators question how public and private university funds are allocated for non-academic purposes.
