# Trump Energy Secretary Plans SPR Refill as Oil Markets Tighten—Colorado Energy Workers Watch Closely

The Trump administration is moving to rebuild America's Strategic Petroleum Reserve after the Biden presidency drained it to 40-year lows, Energy Secretary Chris Wright announced this week. Wright said refilling will begin "in the next few months" as global oil prices climb amid Middle East tensions and terrorist seizures of strategic ground (The Daily Wire).

The Biden administration released over 130 million barrels from the SPR in a bid to suppress gas prices ahead of the 2020 election—a move conservatives argue was politically motivated and left the nation's emergency reserves dangerously depleted. Republicans contend that maintaining robust energy reserves is essential to national security and economic stability.

For Colorado, the SPR refill signals potential demand for domestic crude production. Colorado's oil and gas sector, which supports thousands of jobs and generates substantial state revenue, could benefit from sustained market conditions that incentivize continued development. However, the state's energy industry remains under pressure from progressive regulations and federal land-use restrictions that limit drilling permits on public lands.

Wright's plan also reflects the Trump administration's broader commitment to energy independence—a stark contrast to Biden-era policies that discouraged fossil fuel development. Conservatives argue this approach strengthens both the economy and national security.

Watch whether the administration can execute the refill while navigating continued pressure from environmental groups and progressive lawmakers opposed to domestic oil production.
