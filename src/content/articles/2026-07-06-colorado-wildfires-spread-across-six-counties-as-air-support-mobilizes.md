# Colorado Wildfires Spread Across Six Counties as Heat, Drought Persist

Active wildfires are burning across Pueblo, Custer, Dolores, Mesa, Lake, and Ouray counties as Colorado faces persistent heat and dry conditions, according to 9News Denver. Crews are mobilizing air support from across the nation to combat the blazes, with multiple agencies coordinating response efforts (KDVR).

The fires underscore a critical vulnerability: Colorado's forest management practices have left vast tracts of state and federal land overgrown and tinder-dry. Conservatives have long argued that aggressive timber harvesting and controlled burns—blocked or delayed by environmental regulations and litigation—would reduce catastrophic wildfire risk. Instead, federal land-management agencies have prioritized preservation over active stewardship, leaving communities and watersheds exposed.

The economic and environmental costs are staggering. Wildfires destroy private property, threaten water supplies for Front Range communities, degrade air quality across the state, and strain emergency budgets. Yet federal agencies continue to move slowly on forest thinning projects that could mitigate future disasters.

Colorado taxpayers and property owners should watch whether state and federal officials finally embrace science-based forest management—or continue deferring to environmental groups that oppose the very interventions proven to reduce wildfire severity.
