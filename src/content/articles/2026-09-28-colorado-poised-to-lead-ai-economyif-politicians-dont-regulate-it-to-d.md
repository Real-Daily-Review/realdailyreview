# Colorado Should Embrace AI Growth, Not Strangle It With Panic

Colorado has a genuine opportunity to lead the artificial intelligence economy—if state leaders resist the urge to regulate first and think later.

A new Magellan Strategies survey, released with timely relevance, shows Colorado voters are open to betting on AI and data center development (Complete Colorado). The state's tech corridor, energy infrastructure, and business climate position it to compete for high-wage jobs and innovation hubs that other states are actively courting.

Yet the national conversation remains clouded by doomsday rhetoric. While Pope Leo's recent encyclical on AI safety has drawn headlines (9News Denver), conservatives argue that blanket precaution and regulatory overreach will simply cede competitive advantage to other states and nations less burdened by bureaucratic hesitation.

The conservative case is straightforward: Colorado's economy thrives when government gets out of the way. Heavy-handed AI regulation—whether on data privacy, algorithmic transparency, or energy use—will drive companies and jobs elsewhere. Safety concerns deserve attention, but through industry standards and market competition, not government mandates that stifle innovation.

Colorado taxpayers and workers should watch whether the state legislature resists the temptation to pass restrictive AI legislation in 2027, or whether it doubles down on attracting data centers and tech talent with a business-friendly regulatory environment.
