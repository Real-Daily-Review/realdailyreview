# Colorado Voters to Get Stronger Mail Ballot Security Under Initiative 362

Colorado's all-mail voting system is about to get tougher—and not before time. Initiative 362, headed for the November ballot, would require voter ID authentication for mail ballots, tightening security on a system that has processed millions of votes with minimal fraud but room for improvement.

The measure comes as Colorado grapples with real vulnerabilities. The *Denver Post* reported that three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024 after passing signature verification—a reminder that even low fraud rates expose gaps. Two Mesa County residents who submitted fraudulent ballots to "prove" election fraud was possible were eventually caught, but only after their votes were already counted.

Republicans and election-security advocates argue Initiative 362 closes a genuine loophole. Conservatives say Colorado's signature-matching system, while generally effective, relies too heavily on subjective verification and leaves room for determined bad actors. Stronger ID requirements would add an objective layer of authentication.

Democrats and voting-access groups counter that Colorado's mail system already includes multiple safeguards and that new ID requirements could create barriers for eligible voters. They argue the three fraudulent ballots prove the system works—fraud was detected and prosecuted.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether Initiative 362 passes and how Secretary of State implementation handles the practical mechanics of ID verification without slowing ballot processing.
