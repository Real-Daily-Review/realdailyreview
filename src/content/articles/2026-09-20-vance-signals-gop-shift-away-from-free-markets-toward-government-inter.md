# GOP's Hamiltonian Pivot: What It Means for Colorado Conservatives

Vice President JD Vance is openly embracing a fundamental shift in Republican economic doctrine—away from free-market principles and toward active government intervention—and calling it progress.

Vance told allies that right-of-center policy is now "much more Alexander Hamilton than it is Milton Friedman," and he's framed this departure as "obviously a good thing" (Washington Examiner). The distinction matters: Friedman's tradition treated markets as the default and government action as the exception requiring justification. Hamilton's model treats a strong central government as the engine of economic policy.

**What conservatives should worry about:** This rhetorical shift signals the Trump-Vance administration may embrace industrial policy, tariffs, and strategic government spending—moves that sound nationalist but historically expand bureaucratic power and create winners and losers through political favor rather than competition.

For Colorado, this could mean anything from federal energy mandates to subsidized manufacturing initiatives that bypass market discipline. It's a departure from the limited-government conservatism that has animated the right for decades.

The real question: Does "Hamiltonian" mean protecting American workers and energy independence, or does it mean bigger government dressed in patriotic language? Colorado taxpayers and business owners need to watch whether this philosophy translates into targeted tax breaks for connected industries or genuine pro-growth policy.
