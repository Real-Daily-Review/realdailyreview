# America's AI Edge Over Europe Widens—Colorado Should Capitalize

The U.S. economy is lapping Europe in the artificial intelligence race, and Colorado tech companies should be positioning themselves to win.

The American economy has grown nearly twice as fast as the European Union's over the past decade, with U.S. GDP now standing at $32.5 trillion versus the EU's $23.1 trillion (Washington Examiner). Per capita GDP in the U.S. runs roughly 85% higher than the European average—a stark gap that reflects fundamental differences in regulatory philosophy and economic freedom.

The disparity matters for Colorado. While the state has built a growing tech corridor in Denver and Boulder, regulatory overreach—whether from state lawmakers or federal agencies—threatens to undermine that advantage. Europe's heavy-handed approach to AI regulation and business taxation has made the continent less attractive to innovation-hungry companies. Colorado can't afford to follow that playbook.

Republicans and conservatives argue that lighter regulation, lower taxes, and respect for property rights create the conditions where AI companies thrive and invest. The EU's cautious, bureaucratic stance has cost it competitiveness and jobs.

Colorado's business community should watch whether state lawmakers resist the temptation to over-regulate emerging tech sectors in the name of "safety" or "fairness"—and whether they'll keep taxes competitive with other innovation hubs.
