# Colorado GOP Narrows Field: Only 2 of 18 Gubernatorial Candidates Clear Assembly Hurdle

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary field down to two finalists after the party assembly, a significant culling that reflects internal disagreement over direction and electability.

According to Complete Colorado, only two candidates advanced from the initial field of 18 Republican hopefuls seeking the party's nomination for governor. The assembly process—where party delegates vote rather than primary voters—allowed the GOP to consolidate behind fewer contenders before the general primary ballot.

The dramatic reduction underscores the challenge facing Colorado Republicans as they prepare to challenge Democratic leadership in a state that has trended blue in recent cycles. With fewer candidates splitting the conservative vote, the two assembly-approved nominees will have clearer paths to build name recognition and fundraising momentum heading into the primary election.

Republicans argue the assembly process helps the party nominate candidates with the strongest appeal to both the base and general election voters—avoiding the fragmentation that can weaken a nominee. The two finalists now face the task of uniting the broader GOP coalition while articulating a compelling alternative to Democratic governance on taxes, regulation, and education policy.

Conservatives should monitor whether the assembly's two picks can energize grassroots donors and volunteers needed to compete statewide in what's shaping up as a competitive 2026 cycle.
