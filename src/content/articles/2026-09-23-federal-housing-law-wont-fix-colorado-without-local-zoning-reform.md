# Federal Housing Law Won't Fix Colorado's Affordability Crisis Without Local Reform

The federal government just passed the ROAD Act—but don't expect it to build a single house in Colorado without real zoning reform at home.

This summer's landmark 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act became law with bipartisan fanfare, promising to ease the housing crunch that has priced out working Coloradans and strained communities across the state. But as the Washington Examiner notes, passing legislation is the easy part. The hard part—actually removing the regulatory barriers that prevent developers from building—falls to local governments, many of which remain "trapped by systems that make solutions difficult to implement."

Conservatives argue the real problem isn't federal incentives; it's local zoning restrictions, permitting delays, and regulatory red tape that strangle housing supply. Colorado cities and counties have spent decades layering on restrictions that make new construction prohibitively expensive. No federal law fixes that without local action.

The ROAD Act may provide tools and nudges, but Colorado municipalities must choose to use them. That means streamlining zoning codes, cutting permitting timelines, and reducing fees—moves that face resistance from NIMBYs and local planning boards.

Watch whether Colorado's cities and counties actually implement the reforms the ROAD Act encourages, or whether they pocket federal money while keeping the same restrictive rules in place.
