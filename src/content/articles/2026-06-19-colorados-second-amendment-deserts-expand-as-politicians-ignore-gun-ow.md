# Colorado's Second Amendment Access Crisis Deepens as Politicians Stay Silent

Colorado gun owners face a widening access problem: entire regions of the state lack firearms dealers, yet state and local officials refuse to address the "Second Amendment deserts" taking root across the state, according to *Complete Colorado*.

While progressive activists obsess over "food deserts," they've been conspicuously quiet about the erosion of gun-owner access to lawful purchases and services. The silence from Denver's Democratic leadership is deafening—no policy proposals, no public concern, nothing.

The timing matters. A recent Supreme Court ruling (cited in the Hunter Biden case) struck down a federal law barring marijuana users from Second Amendment protections, with all nine justices agreeing such categorical bans violate constitutional rights. That decision underscores a fundamental principle: government cannot arbitrarily restrict access to constitutional rights.

**The Colorado angle:** As dealer networks shrink and anti-gun sentiment hardens in urban centers, rural and suburban Coloradans face longer drives and fewer options to exercise their Second Amendment rights. This isn't accidental—it's the predictable result of progressive hostility toward gun ownership.

Conservatives argue that if politicians truly believed in equal access to constitutional rights, they'd treat Second Amendment deserts with the same urgency they claim to apply to food access. They don't.
