# A Generation Born After 9/11 Now Faces a Threat America Forgot How to Name

A quarter-century after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students and young adults have no living memory of that morning—and educators are struggling to convey why it mattered. CPR News reports that Denver schools are grappling with how to teach a generation for whom 9/11 is ancient history, not national trauma.

Meanwhile, National Review argues the real problem runs deeper: America has lost its strategic clarity about the ideological threat that drove the attacks. The outlet contends that "Sharia supremacism" continues to advance globally, yet American institutions—from schools to foreign policy—have largely abandoned the vocabulary and resolve to name or counter it.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts military installations, tech workers, and communities with significant Muslim and immigrant populations. How schools teach 9/11's lessons shapes how young Coloradans understand national security, religious tolerance, and the balance between vigilance and openness.

Conservatives argue that sanitizing 9/11's ideological roots—treating it as a generic "tragedy" rather than a specific civilizational conflict—leaves Americans unprepared for persistent threats. Schools should teach history clearly, not erase uncomfortable truths.
