# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push hungry animals into residential areas at unprecedented rates, forcing a grim choice between public safety and wildlife management.

The spike reflects a cascade of environmental pressures. Wildfires destroy natural food sources; drought withers the berries and vegetation bears depend on. Desperate for calories, bears raid garbage, bird feeders, and livestock across the state, triggering dangerous human-wildlife conflicts that end in lethal removal.

Colorado Parks and Wildlife faces a no-win scenario: allow bears to habituate to human areas—risking attacks and property damage—or cull them. Conservatives and rural Coloradans argue the agency must prioritize human safety and property rights over predator preservation, especially as climate and land-management policies (including fire suppression failures and water scarcity) compound the problem.

Environmental groups typically oppose lethal removal, advocating instead for stricter human behavior—securing trash, removing attractants—but that places the burden on residents rather than addressing root causes like forest health and water management.

The real question: Will state and federal agencies tackle the underlying drought and wildfire crisis, or will Colorado families keep paying the price in dead bears and destroyed livelihoods?
