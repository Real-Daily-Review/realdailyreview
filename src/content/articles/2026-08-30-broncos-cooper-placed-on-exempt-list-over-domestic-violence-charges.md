# Broncos' Cooper Placed on Exempt List Over Domestic Violence Charges

Denver Broncos linebacker Jonathon Cooper will start the season on the NFL's commissioner's exempt list following two domestic incidents in June, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31. Cooper faces a felony strangulation charge and is expected to miss at least the first two games while on paid leave.

The exempt list placement is a standard NFL procedure when a player faces serious legal allegations. Cooper's absence removes a starting outside linebacker from Denver's defensive rotation at the season's outset, forcing the team to adjust its depth chart and game-day roster decisions.

The move reflects the NFL's handling of off-field conduct cases, which has drawn scrutiny from fans and observers over consistency and due process. Cooper remains entitled to the presumption of innocence in the criminal justice system, and the exempt list status does not constitute a finding of guilt.

For the Broncos organization, the timing complicates early-season preparation and defensive scheme execution. The team will need to evaluate backup linebacker options and adjust defensive calls accordingly.

Watch whether Colorado's criminal justice system moves swiftly on the felony charge and whether the NFL's disciplinary process—separate from criminal proceedings—extends Cooper's absence beyond the initial games.
