# Colorado Faces Marijuana Impairment Testing Gap While Road Deaths Mount

Colorado's legalized marijuana market has created a dangerous enforcement blind spot: police can test for alcohol impairment in seconds, but have no reliable roadside test for cannabis impairment—leaving impaired drivers on the road while law enforcement guesses.

The problem is acute. A 2023 incident described in the *Washington Examiner* illustrates the stakes: a head-on collision caused by an under-the-influence driver could have been fatal. Yet Colorado law enforcement lacks the scientific tools to quickly identify and prosecute marijuana-impaired drivers with the same certainty they apply to alcohol DUIs.

This gap reflects a broader tension in Colorado's marijuana policy. Since legalization in 2014, the state has prioritized tax revenue and market expansion over robust impairment detection. Blood tests exist but require lab processing—too slow for roadside enforcement. Field sobriety tests remain subjective and vulnerable to legal challenge.

**Conservatives argue** that Colorado's rush to legalize cannabis prioritized profits over public safety. Without reliable impairment testing, the state cannot fairly enforce DUI laws or protect taxpayers from the costs of marijuana-related accidents and injuries.

Democrats have largely avoided the issue, treating marijuana legalization as settled policy.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers push for funding to develop or deploy rapid marijuana impairment tests—or whether road safety remains secondary to cannabis industry interests.
