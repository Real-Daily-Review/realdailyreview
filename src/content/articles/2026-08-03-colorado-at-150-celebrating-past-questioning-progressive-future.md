# Colorado at 150: A Milestone to Reflect On—and Reckon With

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives amid a state transformed almost beyond recognition from its frontier roots—and conservatives should ask hard questions about what comes next.

The state marked its 150th birthday with celebrations at the Capitol, where leaders reflected on Colorado's past while projecting confidence about its future (CPR News). The Colorado Sun highlighted 150 things that "lean into Colorado's awesomeness," from outdoor recreation to natural resources that built the state's early prosperity.

But here's the rub: modern Colorado has drifted far from the limited-government, self-reliant ethos that defined statehood. Today's Denver establishment has embraced expansive regulation, aggressive taxation, and progressive social engineering that would baffle the pioneers and miners who settled this state. Water rights—once the lifeblood of Colorado agriculture and energy—face constant assault from environmental mandates. Energy development, which powered Colorado's growth, now battles green-energy zealots in the legislature. And Second Amendment rights, central to frontier Colorado, face steady erosion.

The sesquicentennial is a moment to celebrate genuine Colorado achievements: rugged individualism, economic dynamism, and natural abundance. But it's also a warning: without a return to constitutional limits on government and respect for property rights, Colorado's next 150 years will belong to bureaucrats, not Coloradans.

Watch whether state leadership uses this milestone to recommit to limited government—or doubles down on the progressive experiment.
