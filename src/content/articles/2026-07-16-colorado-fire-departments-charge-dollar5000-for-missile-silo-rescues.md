# Colorado Fire Departments Now Charging $5,000 for Missile Silo Rescues

Colorado fire officials are fed up with trespassers turning abandoned Cold War missile silos into death traps—and they're billing for the privilege of saving reckless lives.

After two separate rescue operations in Arapahoe County within a week, fire chiefs announced a $5,000 fee for silo rescues, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver. Officials stress this is not a fine but cost recovery for specialized equipment, training, and manpower required for these extraordinarily dangerous missions.

The move makes fiscal sense. Taxpayers shouldn't bankroll rescue operations for people knowingly trespassing on hazardous federal property. As 9News Denver reported, fire officials have issued formal warnings after the incidents, signaling they're serious about deterrence.

Three trespassers from the first incident face criminal charges, KDVR reported, which is the appropriate response. Colorado's fire departments have finite resources and budgets already stretched thin—diverting them to rescue adventure-seekers from abandoned silos wastes money that should go to legitimate emergency response.

Conservatives argue that personal responsibility and consequences—both legal and financial—are essential. Those entering restricted, dangerous federal property should expect to pay for their own rescue, not shift the burden to hardworking Colorado taxpayers and overtaxed emergency services.
