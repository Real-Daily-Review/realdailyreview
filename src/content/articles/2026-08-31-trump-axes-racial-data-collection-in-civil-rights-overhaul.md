# Trump Administration Tightens Civil-Rights Enforcement, Axes Racial Data Collection

The Trump administration is overhauling federal civil-rights enforcement with a muscular approach that abandons what conservatives call "racial bean-counting"—the forced collection of demographic data across companies (National Review). The shift marks a sharp departure from previous Republican administrations and targets what the administration views as counterproductive diversity mandates.

The civil-rights agency has struck what National Review calls "a blow against racial bean-counting," arguing that mandatory demographic data collection has done more harm than good. Conservatives contend these requirements fuel divisive identity politics and distract from merit-based hiring and advancement.

Democrats are pushing back hard. Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) told CBS's "Face the Nation" that Trump's immigration enforcement is "repugnant and inhumane" (Breitbart), signaling the left will frame the administration's broader enforcement posture as cruel overreach.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado businesses and nonprofits receiving federal contracts or grants should prepare for revised compliance requirements. The shift away from demographic reporting could ease administrative burden on employers, though federal contractors will need clarity on new standards.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado's Democratic-led legislature moves to impose state-level demographic reporting mandates to offset federal rollbacks—and what that costs employers.
