# Americans Still Believe in the Dream—If Government Gets Out of the Way

Two-thirds of Americans say they've achieved or are pursuing the American Dream, according to a new survey cited by the *New York Post*, but conservatives warn that government bloat and lack of accountability threaten to kill opportunity for the next generation.

The *Washington Examiner* argues the real problem isn't the dream itself—it's that public institutions from schools to agencies operate without the fiscal discipline demanded of families and businesses. "Families cannot budget with good intentions," the outlet notes. "Schools, charities, public agencies, and civic organizations should not be exempt from the same standard."

For Colorado, this hits home. State and local governments have expanded spending and regulations while productivity stagnates. Conservatives argue that bloated bureaucracies, excessive licensing requirements, and tax burdens on small businesses and workers make it harder for ordinary Coloradans to build wealth and pass it to their kids.

Republicans counter that the path forward requires real accountability: cutting wasteful spending, eliminating red tape, and letting markets—not government planners—drive growth. The Left typically responds that more public investment and regulation are needed to ensure "equitable" outcomes.

**Watch:** whether Colorado lawmakers embrace spending restraint in the next budget cycle or continue the progressive expansion that's made the state less affordable for working families.
