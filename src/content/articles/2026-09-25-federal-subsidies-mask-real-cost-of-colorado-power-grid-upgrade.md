# Federal Cash Fuels $1.2B Colorado Power Line Project—But Ratepayers Foot the Bill

Federal subsidies are bankrolling a $1.2 billion electric transmission project across eastern Colorado, but don't expect your power bill to stay flat. Xcel Energy will pass most construction costs to customers under the 28-mile buildout, which the utility says will improve grid reliability (Colorado Sun). The project exemplifies how Washington's green-energy spending ultimately shifts burdens to Colorado ratepayers rather than truly subsidizing infrastructure.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs residents face steeper solar bills after the city council approved new net-metering rules that align solar customers with the utility's standard rate structure (CPR News). The change hits early solar adopters hardest—a cautionary tale about how government-mandated rate restructuring punishes those who invested in distributed energy first.

Adding to the grid's woes, another lawsuit has been filed against San Isabel Energy Association over the Aspen Acres fire in Pueblo County, with residents alleging a downed power line sparked the blaze (Colorado Sun). The mounting liability claims underscore how aging infrastructure and regulatory complexity create cascading costs for cooperatives and consumers alike.
