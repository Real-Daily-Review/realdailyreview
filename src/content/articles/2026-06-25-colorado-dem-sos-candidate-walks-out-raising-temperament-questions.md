# Colorado Democrat's Thin Skin Raises Questions About Election Administration Role

A Colorado Democratic candidate for Secretary of State walked out of a media interview rather than face tough questioning, raising concerns about temperament for a position overseeing the state's elections and voting systems.

According to the Daily Caller, the candidate abruptly ended the interview, citing unwillingness to be "yelled at"—despite no evidence of aggressive questioning. The exchange underscores a broader conservative concern: whoever wins Colorado's Secretary of State race will wield enormous power over ballot access, voter rolls, and election procedures.

Republicans argue that the Secretary of State's office demands thick skin and impartiality, especially when handling disputes over election integrity, ballot challenges, and voter eligibility. A candidate who storms out when pressed suggests potential problems managing contentious election decisions fairly and transparently.

The Democratic primary race between Jessie Danielson and Amanda Gonzalez (per 9News Denver) will determine which Democrat faces the Republican nominee in November. Colorado conservatives worry that either candidate may push progressive voting policies—mail-in ballot expansion, same-day registration, or relaxed voter ID requirements—that Republicans believe invite fraud and undermine election security.
