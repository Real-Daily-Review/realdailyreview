# Polis Grants 29 More Pardons as Final Year Clemency Spree Continues

Gov. Jared Polis has issued his second round of pardons this year, freeing 29 individuals—a move that raises questions about judicial restraint and public safety priorities in Colorado's final stretch under Democratic leadership.

The pardons follow Polis' controversial commutation of former Mesa County Clerk Tina Peters' sentence just three months earlier, signaling an aggressive clemency agenda as the governor's term winds down (Colorado Sun). One beneficiary, Sabrina Flanagan, received a pardon after 20 years, marking what officials framed as a "second chance" (KDVR Fox31).

**The conservative concern:** Polis' clemency pace—two major rounds in a single year—suggests the governor is prioritizing rehabilitation narratives over victims' rights and public safety. Republicans argue that mass pardons and commutations undermine the judicial system and send a troubling message about accountability. The Peters case, in particular, drew fire from conservatives who viewed her prosecution as politically motivated but questioned whether clemency was the appropriate remedy.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether Polis accelerates clemency decisions in his final months—and whether the legislature responds with reforms to the pardon process itself.
