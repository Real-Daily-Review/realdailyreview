# Deep-Pocket Liability Targets Colorado Businesses and Taxpayers

Colorado companies and taxpayers face growing exposure to lawsuits targeting the deepest pockets rather than actual wrongdoers, a trend that threatens economic growth and job creation across the state.

The Washington Examiner warns that American civil litigation has drifted from holding truly responsible parties accountable to pursuing whoever has money to pay. When a defendant lacks resources, plaintiffs' lawyers increasingly sue tangentially connected businesses, contractors, and even government entities—shifting liability costs to those with assets rather than culpability.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. Energy companies, construction firms, manufacturers, and agricultural operations face ballooning legal exposure. A single incident can trigger cascading lawsuits against suppliers, insurers, and neighboring businesses with no direct connection to the harm. These costs don't disappear—they're passed to Colorado consumers through higher prices, reduced investment, and fewer jobs.

Conservatives argue tort reform is essential to restore the principle that liability should follow actual fault, not just financial capacity. Without reform, Colorado risks becoming a magnet for lawsuit abuse, deterring business investment and innovation.

Republicans and business groups have long pushed for reasonable caps on damages and limits on joint-and-several liability. Democrats typically oppose such measures, citing victim compensation concerns.

**Watch whether Colorado's legislature takes up meaningful tort reform to protect businesses and keep the state competitive.**
