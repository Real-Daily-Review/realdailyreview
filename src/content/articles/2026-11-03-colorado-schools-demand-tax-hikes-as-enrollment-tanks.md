# Colorado Schools Push Tax Hikes While Enrollment Craters

Colorado school districts are demanding higher taxes to fund education even as student enrollment continues its downward spiral—a disconnect that should alarm taxpayers footing the bill.

According to Complete Colorado, districts are marketing tax increases as necessary "for kids," yet enrollment projections show sustained declines ahead. The math doesn't add up: fewer students means lower per-pupil costs, yet districts keep seeking more revenue. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this reveals the real priority—protecting bloated bureaucracies and union contracts, not classroom quality.

The squeeze on Colorado families extends beyond schools. As 9News Denver reports, Coloradans are now paying roughly $19 million more per month in health insurance premiums in 2026 compared to 2025—a direct hit to household budgets already strained by inflation and housing costs. The ACA marketplace's open enrollment period highlights how federal healthcare mandates continue driving up out-of-pocket expenses for working families.

Together, these stories paint a troubling picture: Colorado taxpayers face relentless pressure from both school districts and Washington's healthcare bureaucracy, while their actual purchasing power shrinks. Districts won't acknowledge that fewer students should mean lower spending; Democrats won't admit that Obamacare's regulatory structure inflates premiums.

Watch whether Colorado legislators propose *real* education reform—merit pay, charter expansion, administrative cuts—or simply rubber-stamp another tax hike.
