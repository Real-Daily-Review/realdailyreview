# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Wage War on Growth

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are quietly becoming the backbone of American digital infrastructure, yet progressive activists and local governments continue to throw regulatory roadblocks in the way of this economic engine.

According to *Complete Colorado*, these facilities don't just hum in the background—they power everything from cloud computing to AI to financial markets. Data centers represent high-value jobs, substantial tax revenue, and Colorado's competitive advantage in the tech economy. The state's geography, power infrastructure, and business climate have made it a natural hub.

But here's the rub: environmental activists and some local jurisdictions treat data centers as villains, citing water use and energy consumption. They've pushed for restrictive zoning, higher permitting fees, and environmental reviews designed to slow or block expansion. Democrats and green groups frame this as "sustainability," but conservatives argue it's really about blocking economic development and killing good-paying jobs.

Republicans counter that Colorado can—and does—operate data centers responsibly while meeting energy and environmental standards. Strangling this industry with regulation simply hands the competitive advantage to Texas, Arizona, or other states with friendlier business climates.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether the state legislature moves to streamline data center permitting or whether activist pressure wins out and chokes off growth.
