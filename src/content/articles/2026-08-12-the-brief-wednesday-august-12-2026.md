# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Wednesday, August 12, 2026

Colorado conservatives, the midterm stretch run is here — and Washington is doing its level best to make a mess of it. From a federal judge rewriting election rules to a Douglas County tax deal that raises real questions, there's plenty to watch this morning.

---

## Federal Judge Blocks Trump's Mail-In Ballot Order Ahead of Midterms

A federal judge in Boston has blocked the Trump administration from enforcing its executive order tightening mail-in voting rules ahead of the 2026 midterms. Republicans argue mail-in ballot integrity is a legitimate and urgent concern — and that a single unelected judge in Massachusetts has no business overriding a presidential directive weeks before a national election. Colorado already runs an all-mail election system, which means this ruling lands close to home. Conservatives here should be watching whether this emboldens state Democrats to resist any future federal election integrity measures.

---

## Douglas County Approves 35-Year Tax Waiver for Data Center — 16 Jobs

Douglas County commissioners have approved a business personal property tax rebate for a data center in Parker — waiving those taxes for 35 years. The project is expected to create just 16 new jobs. Conservatives generally support pro-growth incentives, but a 35-year tax holiday for 16 positions deserves hard scrutiny from Colorado taxpayers. County commissioners owe constituents a clear accounting of what this deal actually costs local government over its lifetime and whether the math pencils out for Parker residents.

---

## South Carolina Senate Runoff Set: Nordone vs. Norman to Replace Graham

The race to fill the late Sen. Lindsey Graham's South Carolina seat heads to a runoff in two weeks, with Darline Graham Nordone and Rep. Ralph Norman advancing. The seat is critical to Republicans maintaining and expanding their Senate majority. Colorado conservatives have a stake in this: every Senate seat shapes the balance of power on energy policy, judicial confirmations, and federal spending — all of which hit Colorado directly. Republicans need to hold South Carolina and run up the score in November.

---

## White House Pushes DOJ to Revisit Reflecting Pool Case After Felony Drop

The White House is asking the Justice Department to consider a misdemeanor prosecution in the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool case after prosecutors moved to drop the felony charge against Olympic canoeist David Hearn. Conservatives say the episode illustrates the inconsistency of federal prosecutorial discretion — and raises fair questions about whether the original felony charge was proportionate. The broader principle matters: equal application of the law shouldn't depend on who's watching or who's asking.

---

## Michele Tafoya Wins Minnesota GOP Senate Primary

Former NFL sideline reporter and four-time Emmy winner Michele Tafoya has won the Republican Senate primary in Minnesota, setting up a general election contest for the seat being vacated by Democratic Sen. Tina Smith. Tafoya made a sharp turn from sports media to conservative politics and has been outspoken on issues the left would rather not debate. A competitive Minnesota Senate race stretches the Democratic map and forces the party to spend resources defending territory it expected to hold easily.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Boston court ruling blocking Trump's mail-in ballot order — an appeal is the logical next step, and the timeline before November is tight. In Douglas County, taxpayers should press commissioners for a full cost-benefit breakdown on that 35-year data center deal before it becomes a template for future giveaways. And watch the South Carolina runoff: the Norman-Nordone contest will signal whether GOP primary voters are in an establishment or insurgent mood heading into the fall.
