# NYC Official's Immigrant Map Snub Shows Progressive Identity Politics Gone Wrong

A New York City official's "cultural map" excluding Italian, Irish, and Jewish neighborhoods—three of the city's most historically significant immigrant communities—exemplifies how progressive identity politics devours its own. The map, promoted by NYC Tourism + Conventions under the Eric Adams administration, sparked immediate backlash from Italian American groups who called it "cultural erasure" (Fox News).

Zohran Mamdani, the city official defending the map, blamed the Adams administration for the omission and promised updates (Washington Examiner). The damage, however, illustrates a broader conservative concern: when government bureaucrats decide which communities merit recognition based on fashionable progressive categories, they inevitably slight groups that don't fit the current narrative.

This isn't a Colorado story—yet. But it's a cautionary tale for Coloradans watching their own cities and state agencies expand "diversity and inclusion" initiatives. When government agencies prioritize ideological framing over historical accuracy and equal treatment, taxpayers foot the bill for the corrections and the political fallout.

**Watch for similar erasures in Colorado tourism, education, and municipal planning as progressive identity frameworks spread westward.**
