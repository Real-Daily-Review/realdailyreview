# Colorado Voters Weigh In on Data Center Taxes, Energy Policy

Colorado voters faced crucial decisions on ballot measures affecting energy development and tax policy, with data center proposals drawing particular scrutiny from both sides of the aisle.

According to the Washington Examiner, data center ballot measures were among the key contests voters decided, though the outlet's reporting on specific Colorado outcomes remains incomplete. The Daily Wire noted that across the nation, voters "rewrote the rules on taxes, abortion, marriage, and even how Americans cast their votes," signaling a broader appetite for direct democracy on contentious issues.

For Colorado specifically, data center taxation matters. These facilities consume significant electricity and water—resources Colorado guards jealously—while generating tax revenue and jobs. Conservatives argue that excessive taxation on data centers risks driving investment to neighboring states with friendlier business climates. The left counters that data centers should pay their fair share for infrastructure and environmental costs.

Republicans and business groups have warned that punitive ballot measures targeting data centers could undermine Colorado's competitive position in the tech economy and cost workers high-paying jobs. Democrats and progressive activists argue stronger regulations and higher taxes ensure corporations don't externalize costs onto taxpayers and ratepayers.

The full results and implications for Colorado's energy and tax landscape remain to be seen as final tallies come in.
