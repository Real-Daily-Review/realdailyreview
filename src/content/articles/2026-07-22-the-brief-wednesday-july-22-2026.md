# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Wednesday, July 22, 2026

Congress is playing budget games while Colorado's border communities stay under pressure, and Washington's foreign policy moves are finally catching up to threats conservatives have been warning about for years. Here's what you need to know this morning.

---

## House GOP Punts Shutdown Fight Past Midterms

The House passed a continuing resolution by a mostly party-line vote of 220-205, kicking the government funding battle past November's midterms. Republicans argue the move buys time to lock in spending cuts without handing Democrats a shutdown narrative heading into election season. Colorado conservatives should watch this closely — a post-election lame-duck session could open the door to a spending blowout that sticks Colorado taxpayers with the bill. Fiscal discipline delayed is fiscal discipline denied.

---

## Gold Star Family Puts a Face on the Cost of Chaos in the Middle East

The U.S. service member killed in Iraq during a controlled detonation of an Iranian drone has been identified, and his widow's heartbreaking tribute is a reminder that America's enemies are still active and still lethal. Iran's reach — including its documented ties to proxy forces in Iraq — is not an abstraction. Colorado has a proud military community, from Fort Carson to Buckley Space Force Base, and every family there deserves a foreign policy that takes these threats seriously and holds Iran accountable.

---

## Aurora ICE Facility Shooter's Own Words Condemn Him

A new affidavit reveals the suspect in the Aurora ICE facility shooting offered this defense: "Because somebody was hurt by the words that I was saying to him, that doesn't give you the right to shoot somebody." That's the shooter's own account. The attack on a federal immigration enforcement facility in Aurora was a direct consequence of years of anti-enforcement rhetoric that painted ICE officers as villains. Colorado Republicans have been right to call out the political climate that makes federal law enforcement a target.

---

## Denver's Housing Answer: More Government, More Listening Tours

Mayor Mike Johnston used his 2026 State of the City address to announce a citywide listening tour and promises of thousands of affordable housing units. Denver renters are now being pointed toward a new city program to help find "healthy housing." Conservatives say the real barrier to affordable housing in Denver is the regulatory maze, zoning restrictions, and permitting costs that city hall itself erected. More government programs and more listening tours won't fix a problem that government largely created — but they will grow the bureaucracy.

---

## House Republicans Move to Make Trump's Border Wins Permanent

House Republicans are pushing the Permanent Trump Secure Border Act, which would criminalize visa overstays, end catch and release, and tighten asylum eligibility. Meanwhile, a federal appeals court has paused a lower court order that would have required the release of illegal migrants into the U.S. without a hearing — a ruling centered on three men in Texas arrested during routine traffic stops. For Colorado, where border-driven crime and fentanyl trafficking have hit communities from Pueblo to the Eastern Plains, making these enforcement tools permanent isn't optional. It's overdue.

---

## Cuba: The Threat Washington Finally Had to Name Out Loud

The State Department released a report charging Communist Cuba with actively helping America's enemies — including Iran and Hamas — spread influence across the Western Hemisphere. Conservatives have been saying for decades that Havana is not a relic of Cold War nostalgia but a live threat. The report confirms it. Colorado's national security community and defense contractors tied to Buckley and Peterson should take note: the Western Hemisphere is not a safe backyard, and pretending otherwise has real consequences.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the House floor as the Permanent Trump Secure Border Act moves toward a vote — any Republican defections will be telling, and Colorado's congressional delegation will face pressure from both directions. Watch whether Denver's housing promises survive contact with the city's own regulatory reality, or whether Johnston's listening tour becomes a substitute for actual reform. And as the Cuba report circulates through foreign policy circles, watch for whether Congress uses it to tighten the screws on Havana or lets it gather dust on a shelf.
