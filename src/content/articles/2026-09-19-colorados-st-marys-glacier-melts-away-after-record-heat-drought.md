# St. Mary's Glacier Vanishes: Colorado Icon Succumbs to Heat, Drought

Colorado's St. Mary's Glacier—a landmark that has drawn hikers and tourists for generations—has melted away, leaving behind what one observer called "just a big rock pile" (CPR News).

The disappearance follows an abnormally hot summer compounded by record snow drought, according to reporting from CPR News and KDVR Fox31 Denver. Visitors arriving at the Clear Creek County site expecting to see the iconic glacier are finding only bare rock and disappointment.

The melt raises uncomfortable questions about Colorado's water future. While climate activists will inevitably weaponize this image to push for more government climate mandates and energy restrictions, conservatives note that Colorado's water challenges stem from complex natural cycles, population growth, and aging infrastructure—not simple political solutions. Heavy-handed regulations on energy development and agriculture won't restore a glacier; smart water management and investment in reservoirs will.

For Colorado taxpayers and outdoor enthusiasts, this is a stark reminder that our state's natural treasures are fragile. The real question: Will state and federal policymakers respond with sound science and market-driven conservation, or with the usual progressive playbook of expanded regulations and energy restrictions that harm Colorado's economy?
