# Omar Saga Exposes Constitutional Limits on Trump's Removal Push

Trump's demand that Rep. Ilhan Omar resign over alleged immigration fraud highlights a hard constitutional reality: the president can't force a House member out, no matter how damaging the allegations (Fox News Politics). Republicans, however, are pressing the case aggressively. Minnesota GOP State Rep. Kristin Robbins told The Daily Wire that Omar was "thick as thieves" with individuals tied to a massive fraud scandal—claims Omar has not publicly addressed.

The controversy centers on Omar's alleged connections to fraudsters and unanswered questions about her own immigration history. Republicans argue the Minnesota Democrat's ties to scandal-plagued associates raise serious questions about her fitness for office and demand transparency. Democrats have largely remained silent on the specifics, instead framing criticism as politically motivated.

Here's the constitutional wrinkle: only the House can remove its own members through expulsion, requiring a two-thirds majority. Trump's calls for resignation carry no legal weight, though they keep pressure on Omar and embarrass Democratic leadership.

For Colorado conservatives, this underscores a broader frustration: progressive members of Congress face minimal accountability from their own party, even when credible questions arise. Watch whether House Democrats demand Omar answer the fraud allegations directly or continue stonewalling.
