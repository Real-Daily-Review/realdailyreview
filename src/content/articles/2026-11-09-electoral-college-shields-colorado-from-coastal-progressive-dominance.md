# Electoral College Remains Bulwark Against Coastal Progressive Dominance

The Electoral College continues to function as the Founders intended: preventing a handful of high-population urban centers from dictating national policy to the rest of America.

According to Complete Colorado, Democrats and their allies have long understood that their political power concentrates in coastal metropolitan areas. Without the Electoral College, those densely populated regions—dominated by progressive voters and aligned with big-government, high-tax agendas—would effectively control every presidential election. The system forces candidates to build geographically diverse coalitions and ensures that rural America, the heartland, and less-populated states retain meaningful influence.

For Colorado specifically, the Electoral College matters. While the state leans purple, its nine electoral votes command attention that a pure popular-vote system would never guarantee. Rural Colorado counties—home to ranchers, energy workers, and Second Amendment advocates—would be drowned out by Denver's urban progressives in a national popular-vote scenario.

Conservatives argue the Electoral College protects federalism and prevents tyranny of the majority. It ensures that winning the presidency requires appealing beyond the coasts to Middle America's values: limited government, individual liberty, and economic freedom.

The left continues pushing for the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact, which would effectively abolish the Electoral College. Watch whether Colorado's legislature attempts to join that compact—a move that would dilute Colorado voters' power and hand control to California and New York.
