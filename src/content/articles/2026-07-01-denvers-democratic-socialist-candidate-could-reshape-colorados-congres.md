# Denver Could Send Democratic Socialist to Congress—and Colorado Should Pay Attention

A 29-year-old democratic socialist, Melat Kiros, is poised to represent Denver in Congress, according to CPR News. The development underscores how far-left ideology has taken root in Colorado's urban core—and what it could mean for state energy policy, gun rights, and fiscal restraint.

Kiros's Democratic Socialist Alliance affiliation signals support for positions that typically oppose fossil fuel development, favor aggressive gun control, and back expansive government spending. For Colorado—a state with significant oil and gas interests, a robust hunting and shooting culture, and taxpayers already burdened by state spending—a DSA voice in Congress could translate to pressure for federal policies that harm the state's energy economy and individual liberties.

Republicans and conservatives argue that democratic socialism represents a dangerous expansion of government power and a threat to free-market energy development that sustains Colorado jobs and tax revenue. Democrats counter that such candidates represent grassroots frustration with establishment politics and offer bold solutions to climate and inequality.

The race reflects Denver's leftward shift, but it also reveals the political divide between Colorado's urban centers and its rural and suburban regions—a tension that will shape the state's congressional delegation for years.
