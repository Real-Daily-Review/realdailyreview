# Colorado Faces Five-Day Heat Wave With Temps Near 100

A dangerous heat wave is sweeping into Colorado starting Saturday, with Denver-area highs potentially reaching near 100 degrees and lasting up to five days, according to 9News Denver forecasts.

The system represents part of a broader heat dome settling across the Southwest and Great Plains, with triple-digit temperatures expected in some regions before spreading eastward, the Washington Examiner reports. Meteorologists warn the high-pressure system could trap oppressive heat for a week or longer.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses, the timing matters. Peak summer heat strains the state's power grid—a concern as Colorado's energy mix shifts away from reliable coal generation toward renewables. Conservatives have long warned that aggressive decarbonization policies leave the state vulnerable to supply shortages during extreme weather events. Colorado's grid operator, XCEL Energy, will face real-time pressure to meet demand without the baseload generation progressives have systematically phased out.

Forecasters are advising residents to stay hydrated and seek air-conditioned spaces. The heat also threatens livestock and agriculture across the state's ranching regions, where water availability is already strained by competing demands and regulatory restrictions on water use.

Watch whether Colorado's grid holds steady through the heat event—and whether state energy officials acknowledge the reliability risks posed by their renewable-heavy generation strategy.
