# Federal Cash Fuels $1.2B Colorado Transmission Project—But Ratepayers Foot the Bill

Federal subsidies are bankrolling a $1.2 billion electric transmission expansion across eastern Colorado, but don't expect your power bill to stay flat. Most of the cost will be passed directly to customers through rate increases, the Colorado Sun reports.

The 28-mile project, backed by federal funding, aims to improve electricity reliability in the region. While Washington foots part of the bill upfront, Xcel Energy customers will absorb the long-term infrastructure costs—a familiar pattern where taxpayer-funded "investments" become ratepayer obligations.

The timing is notable: Colorado Springs is already grappling with rate shock. The city council recently approved changes to how solar customers are billed, hiking their rates significantly while slightly lowering rates for non-solar customers (CPR News). Meanwhile, southern Colorado faces mounting liability questions after another lawsuit blamed San Isabel Energy Association's power line for the Aspen Acres fire, raising questions about whether aging infrastructure—not just new projects—demands immediate attention.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that federal infrastructure spending, while politically popular, simply shifts costs downstream to working families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation. They contend Colorado should prioritize maintaining existing systems safely before expanding new ones.

**Watch whether Colorado regulators demand Xcel and other utilities justify rate increases against actual reliability gains—or rubber-stamp them as "green energy" necessities.**
