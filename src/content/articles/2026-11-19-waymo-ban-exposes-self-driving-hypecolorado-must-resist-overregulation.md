# California's Waymo Ban Exposes Self-Driving Hype—and Colorado's Regulatory Mess

California regulators have yanked Waymo's driverless cars off freeways after the vehicles repeatedly drove into active construction zones, forcing the company to suspend freeway operations nationwide. The move vindicates skeptics who warned that autonomous vehicle technology isn't ready for prime time—and it's exposing cracks in Colorado's own regulatory framework.

Here's the problem: Denver city leaders are now demanding state lawmakers let them regulate self-driving cars more heavily, claiming current state law handcuffs local enforcement after incidents involving automated vehicles (KDVR). But this is exactly backward. Colorado doesn't need *more* regulation stifling innovation; it needs *clarity* about who's responsible when things go wrong—and it needs to let the market, not bureaucrats, determine whether this technology works.

Conservatives argue that piling on regulations before the technology matures will simply push development to friendlier states. California's heavy-handed approach—banning Waymo from freeways—shows what happens when government micromanages emerging industries. Colorado should resist the urge to follow suit.

The real lesson: companies like Waymo have every incentive to fix safety problems fast. Liability and reputation matter more than any city council ordinance. Denver's frustration is understandable, but the answer isn't more red tape—it's letting the courts and market competition sort out winners and losers.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers cave to Denver's regulatory demands or hold the line on light-touch oversight.
