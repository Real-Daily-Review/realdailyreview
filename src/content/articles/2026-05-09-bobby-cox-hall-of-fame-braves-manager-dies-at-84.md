# Bobby Cox, Hall of Fame Braves Manager, Dies at 84

Bobby Cox, the Hall of Fame manager who led the Atlanta Braves to prominence during the 1990s, has died at age 84, the team announced Saturday.

Cox managed the Braves for 25 seasons, guiding them to the World Series championship in 1995—the franchise's only title during his tenure. According to PBS NewsHour, his teams "ruled the National League during the 1990s" and reached the World Series multiple times, though most fell short of winning. Cox retired after the 2010 season.

The longtime skipper transformed the Braves into a consistent contender during a dominant stretch that defined the franchise's modern era. His tenure spanned from the late 1970s through 2010, establishing Atlanta as a baseball powerhouse and delivering the city its first major championship.

Cox was inducted into the Hall of Fame, cementing his legacy as one of baseball's most successful and influential managers.

Tributes from the baseball community are expected to follow in coming days.
