# Biden NLRB's Secret Ballot Grab Faces Reversal Push

The Biden administration's National Labor Relations Board stripped workers of secret-ballot protections in union elections—and now momentum is building to undo the damage.

On September 1, NLRB General Counsel Crystal Carey formally called for the agency to reverse its 2023 Cemex decision, a ruling that effectively eliminated secret ballots in union organizing votes. The move represents a significant win for worker freedom and a rebuke of one of the most aggressive pro-union power grabs of the Biden era.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Secret ballots protect workers from intimidation and coercion. Without them, union organizers can pressure employees into signing cards publicly, eliminating genuine worker choice. The Cemex ruling handed unions a tool to organize workplaces without authentic worker consent—exactly the kind of government-enabled overreach conservatives have fought for years.

Colorado's construction, manufacturing, and energy sectors—industries where union organizing battles are common—stand to benefit from restored ballot protections. Workers in these fields deserve the right to vote on union representation without fear of peer pressure or organizer intimidation.

Democrats and union advocates argue card-check organizing accelerates unionization and empowers workers. Republicans counter that it strips workers of privacy and democratic safeguards.

**Watch whether the incoming administration follows through on reversing Cemex and protecting secret ballots in all union elections.**
