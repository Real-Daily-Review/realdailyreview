# LIV Golf's Collapse Shows Free Market Competition Works—Even When It Fails

Saudi-backed LIV Golf is imploding under mounting losses, lawsuits, and unpaid bills, but the failed venture accomplished what market competition should: it forced the PGA Tour to wake up and stop taking fans for granted.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, LIV Golf's collapse represents a vindication of competitive pressure. The upstart league, despite its financial troubles, shook a complacent PGA establishment that had grown stale and disconnected from what golf fans actually wanted. Rather than mourning LIV's demise, conservatives should recognize that the market worked—bad business models fail, and survivors improve or perish.

The broader lesson: competition drives innovation and accountability. LIV Golf's Saudi funding and controversial ownership made it an easy target for critics, but its willingness to challenge the PGA's monopoly forced real change in professional golf's structure, player compensation, and fan engagement. That's capitalism functioning as intended.

For Colorado golf enthusiasts and business-minded voters, this case study illustrates why protecting free markets—even messy, imperfect ones—matters more than picking winners and losers through regulation or subsidy.

Watch whether the PGA Tour's reforms stick or whether it slides back into complacency now that its rival is gone.
