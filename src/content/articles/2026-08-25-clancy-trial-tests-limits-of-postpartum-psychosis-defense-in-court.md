# Colorado Brief: Lindsay Clancy Trial Exposes Postpartum Psychosis Defense Skepticism

A Massachusetts murder trial is raising hard questions about mental-illness defenses that Colorado courts and juries should watch closely. Lindsay Clancy, a mother accused of strangling her three young children, claims postpartum psychosis drove her to kill—yet prosecution experts testify she was criminally responsible, and legal analysts note a critical gap: Clancy never reported hearing voices commanding her to murder until *after* she killed her first child (9News Denver).

The case has also exposed Big Tech's role in shaping legal narratives. TikTok removed a video challenging the postpartum-psychosis narrative, citing "Community Guidelines violations" (The Daily Wire)—a move that raises concerns about how social-media platforms curate evidence and public debate around serious crimes.

For Colorado, this matters. Our courts regularly handle insanity and diminished-capacity defenses. If juries begin accepting uncorroborated mental-illness claims—especially when the defendant's own timeline contradicts the narrative—Colorado's criminal-justice system could face pressure to lower the evidentiary bar for acquittals based on psychiatric testimony alone.

Conservatives and crime victims' advocates argue courts must demand rigorous proof, not sympathetic narratives, before excusing violent crime. The Clancy trial shows why that standard matters.
