# Maine Democrats Scramble With Last-Minute Replacement as GOP Readies $42M Assault

Democrats' emergency Senate swap in Maine exposes the chaos lurking beneath their vaunted "unity" messaging—and Republicans smell blood in the water.

After original Democratic nominee Graham Platner imploded over sexual assault allegations, Maine Democrats installed Troy Jackson, a fifth-generation logger and former state Senate president, as his replacement. The move was meant to reset the race against Republican Susan Collins in what's now a toss-up.

But the damage control is already cracking. A GOP-aligned group has pledged $42 million against Jackson, citing what they describe as a pattern of volatile behavior and anger issues. Meanwhile, Sen. Bernie Sanders—who endorsed the now-disgraced Platner—doubled down on Sunday, telling CBS News he doesn't regret the endorsement and rates Jackson's chances as "excellent." Sanders' refusal to acknowledge the Platner debacle underscores how the Democratic left circles wagons regardless of the facts.

Jackson's logging background may play better in rural Maine than typical progressive credentials, but Republicans are betting voters won't forget Democrats' rushed, last-minute scramble to replace a nominee credibly accused of rape. The contrast with GOP discipline on vetting is stark.

**Watch whether Jackson's blue-collar appeal can overcome the stench of Democratic mismanagement—and whether Colorado Democrats take notes on how to handle their own candidate implosions.**
