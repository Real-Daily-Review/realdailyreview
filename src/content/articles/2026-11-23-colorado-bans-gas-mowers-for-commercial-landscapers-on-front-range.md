# Colorado Regulators Ban Gas Mowers on Front Range—Another Burden on Small Business

Colorado regulators are imposing new restrictions on gas-powered lawn equipment for commercial operators on the Front Range, forcing landscaping companies into costly conversions to electric tools during summer months.

The Colorado Air Quality Control Commission's mandate targets the commercial lawn care sector, which CPR News reports accounts for 13% of regional ozone pollution. Under the rules, landscapers must phase out gas-powered mowers, trimmers, and blowers during peak summer season—a move the Colorado Sun describes as a "gradual mandatory switch to clean electric."

**The conservative case against this:** Small landscaping businesses already operate on thin margins. Forcing expensive equipment replacement during an arbitrary seasonal window creates compliance costs that large corporations can absorb but mom-and-pop operators cannot. The regulation assumes electric alternatives are cost-effective and readily available—assumptions worth questioning. Conservatives argue Colorado should prioritize economic growth and job creation in the lawn care sector, not regulatory mandates that drive up costs for homeowners and commercial property managers.

**What's at stake:** This is classic regulatory overreach masquerading as environmental necessity. Colorado's Front Range is already one of America's most regulated air basins. Each new restriction chips away at business competitiveness and consumer choice.

Watch whether the landscaping industry challenges these rules or whether other commercial sectors face similar phase-out mandates next.
