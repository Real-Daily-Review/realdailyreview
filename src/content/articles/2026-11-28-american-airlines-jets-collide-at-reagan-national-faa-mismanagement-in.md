# Reagan National Wing-Clip Highlights FAA's Struggle to Manage Crowded Airspace

Two American Airlines jets collided on the tarmac at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport Friday evening, with one aircraft clipping the wing of another during taxiing operations near Runway 19 at approximately 5:10 p.m. ET, according to the Federal Aviation Administration (The Daily Wire, Washington Examiner). Dallas-bound Flight 638 struck Chicago-bound Flight 2152, breaking off a piece of wing and forcing an arriving aircraft to abort its landing.

No injuries were reported, but the incident underscores growing concerns about airspace congestion and ground operations at one of the nation's busiest airports. The FAA has faced mounting criticism over staffing shortages, aging infrastructure, and controller workload—issues that have contributed to near-misses and operational delays across U.S. aviation.

Conservatives argue the incident reflects broader government mismanagement: the FAA, a federal agency, has struggled to modernize air traffic control systems and maintain adequate staffing levels despite substantial taxpayer funding. Republicans have long pushed for privatization or public-private partnerships in air traffic control to improve efficiency and safety—a model used successfully in Canada and Europe.

Colorado travelers and businesses relying on connecting flights through major hubs should monitor whether the FAA implements meaningful operational reforms or continues Band-Aid fixes that leave the system vulnerable to further incidents.
