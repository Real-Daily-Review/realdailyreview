# Washington's AI Panic Could Trigger New Corporate Overreach—and Colorado Tech Firms Should Worry

Washington's escalating AI anxiety is morphing into a regulatory power grab that threatens American innovation and Colorado's growing tech sector. Following Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's warning about foreign AI models built on "stolen American intellectual property," federal officials are laying groundwork for sweeping corporate governance controls under the guise of national security (Washington Examiner).

The real problem: Washington and Silicon Valley are locked in a turf war over export controls, cloud infrastructure, and equity stakes—and everyday Americans will foot the bill through compliance costs, slower innovation, and reduced competition. Conservatives warn that AI "panic" is being weaponized to justify the kind of heavy-handed regulation that stifles startups and entrenches incumbent tech giants who can afford armies of compliance lawyers.

Colorado's emerging AI and cloud computing ecosystem—from Denver's tech corridor to Front Range software firms—faces mounting pressure from federal mandates that haven't been fully debated or justified. Republicans and free-market advocates argue Washington should focus on protecting intellectual property through existing law, not inventing new regulatory levers that hand government veto power over corporate governance decisions.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republicans—will push back against federal overreach or quietly accept another layer of Washington control over American business.
