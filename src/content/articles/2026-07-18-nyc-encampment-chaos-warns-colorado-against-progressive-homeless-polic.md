# NYC's Homeless Crisis Shows Dangers of Progressive Encampment Policy

New York City's sprawling 12-block homeless encampment near the Intrepid Museum is a cautionary tale for Colorado cities tempted by similar permissive policies. Mayor Zohran Mamdani's ban on NYPD encampment sweeps has sparked backlash from residents and tourists alike, as *Fox News* reports, while *National Review* documents how the administration's anti-landlord ideology—treating evictions as "violence"—has paralyzed property management and worsened the crisis.

The core conservative argument is straightforward: policies that criminalize enforcement and prioritize encampment tolerance over public safety create humanitarian disasters, not solutions. When cities abandon basic order-maintenance, they don't reduce homelessness—they concentrate it, degrade public spaces, and drive out the tax base needed to fund actual services.

Colorado's progressive cities—Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins—have flirted with similar approaches: hostile zoning for shelters, restrictions on sweeps, and rhetoric that frames enforcement as cruelty. The result: visible encampments, rising crime, and business flight to suburbs and red counties.

The question isn't whether to help homeless Coloradans—it's whether permissive encampment policies actually do. Evidence from NYC suggests they don't.

Watch whether Colorado's Democrat-controlled legislature pushes statewide restrictions on local encampment enforcement this session.
