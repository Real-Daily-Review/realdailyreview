# Democratic Socialist Eyes Denver House Seat, Raising Questions About Colorado's Political Direction

A 29-year-old democratic socialist could soon represent Denver in Congress, signaling a sharp leftward shift in Colorado's urban politics. According to CPR News, Melat Kiros—a Democratic Socialists of America member—is positioning herself as a viable candidate for the House seat, reflecting the growing influence of far-left ideology in the state's most populous city.

Republicans and conservatives worry that a DSA-backed representative would push for massive government expansion, higher taxes on Colorado businesses and workers, and radical environmental regulations that could cripple the state's energy sector. The DSA platform historically opposes market-driven solutions, favors wealth redistribution, and backs aggressive climate mandates—policies that would hit Colorado's oil, gas, and coal industries hard while raising costs for working families already squeezed by inflation.

Democrats counter that younger voters, particularly in Denver, demand bold action on climate change, housing affordability, and social programs—issues they argue the mainstream left hasn't addressed aggressively enough.

The race underscores a broader battle for Colorado's soul: Will the state remain business-friendly and fiscally responsible, or drift toward the progressive-socialist model already entrenched in Boulder and parts of the Front Range?
