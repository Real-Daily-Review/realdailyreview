# Democratic Socialist Eyes Denver House Seat, Raising Questions About Colorado's Political Direction

A 29-year-old democratic socialist could soon represent Denver in Congress, signaling a sharp leftward shift in Colorado's political center of gravity. Melat Kiros, a Democratic Socialist Alliance member, is positioning herself as a viable candidate for the seat—a development that should alarm Colorado conservatives concerned about the erosion of moderate Democrat influence in the state (CPR News).

Democrats argue Kiros represents grassroots energy and younger voters demanding bold action on housing, healthcare, and climate policy. Republicans counter that democratic socialism—with its hostility to free markets, private enterprise, and limited government—is fundamentally at odds with Colorado's entrepreneurial heritage and the prosperity that comes from economic freedom, not government mandates.

The race matters beyond Denver. Colorado has trended purple-to-blue over the past decade, and the ascendancy of DSA-backed candidates signals that the state's Democratic Party is no longer the party of fiscal restraint or business-friendly pragmatism. If Kiros wins, expect pressure for higher taxes, aggressive regulation of energy development, and expanded government control over housing and healthcare—policies that would ripple across Colorado's economy.
