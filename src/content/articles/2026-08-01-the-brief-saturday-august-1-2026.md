# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, August 1, 2026

America is at war, Iran is in the crosshairs, and back home, Colorado conservatives have plenty to watch — from a White House pressing hard on the Islamic Republic to a welcome gesture for the men and women who served. Here's what matters this morning.

---

## Trump Presses Iran as Strikes Loom This Weekend

The United States and Israel are reportedly preparing strikes against Iranian energy infrastructure — possibly as soon as today. President Trump is vowing the U.S. will "win," even as he acknowledges the five-month conflict could drag into November's midterm elections. Conservatives argue a strong, decisive posture is the only language Tehran understands. Colorado's energy sector should watch closely: sustained Middle East instability has a direct line to global oil prices, and what happens in the Persian Gulf doesn't stay there.

---

## Free State Park Entry for Colorado Military and Vets This August

Colorado Parks and Wildlife is offering free state park access to active-duty military members and veterans throughout August — a straightforward, well-deserved gesture of gratitude. This is government doing something right: honoring service without bureaucratic strings attached. Colorado is home to a significant military and veteran community, and this is the kind of practical recognition that costs little and means a great deal. If you know a vet, pass the word.

---

## Jack Smith's Senate Phone Records Probe Gets Uglier

Sen. Chuck Grassley and 15 fellow senators are demanding Verizon explain why it failed to disclose that special counsel Jack Smith subpoenaed congressional phone records under what's being called the "Arctic Frost" operation. Republicans argue this is exactly the kind of weaponized prosecutorial overreach that conservatives have warned about for years — a federal prosecutor quietly vacuuming up lawmakers' phone data. The implications for congressional oversight and separation of powers are serious, and Coloradans should demand their senators are asking hard questions.

---

## Google Yanks AI Satellite Image Tool After Misinformation Fears

Google pulled a new Google Earth AI feature that allowed users to generate realistic-looking satellite images of real locations — after critics pointed out how easily it could be used to fabricate geographic "evidence." This is a useful reminder that Big Tech's rush to deploy AI tools often outpaces any serious consideration of consequences. Conservatives who have long warned about the left's cozy relationship with Silicon Valley gatekeepers should note: when these tools go wrong, the correction comes from public pressure, not government mandate.

---

## FIFA Won't Sell World Cup Stakes to Private Equity

FIFA President Gianni Infantino announced Friday the organization will not sell stakes in the World Cup to private equity investors, saying the divisions the proposal created are "no longer in the interest of the objective." With the 2026 World Cup set to be hosted across North America — including matches that will draw enormous economic activity — the governance of the tournament matters. Free-market conservatives can appreciate that not every asset needs a private equity buyer, but the real story is how quickly institutional pressure can reverse a bad idea when leadership decides to listen.

And as August begins, Colorado conservatives should be tracking what the Democrat-controlled state legislature has queued up for fall, because the spending bills don't stop just because the summer does.
