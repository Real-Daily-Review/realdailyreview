# Trump Tightens Nvidia Chip Export Rules After Controversy Over Huang Donations

The Trump administration is cracking down on loopholes that allowed advanced Nvidia chips to reach China—reversing earlier signals that corporate donations might have loosened export controls.

The Commerce Department issued new regulations Monday to block workarounds that circumvent restrictions on Nvidia's most advanced products bound for Beijing, according to the Washington Examiner. The move came after National Review raised questions about whether payments from Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang to Trump-aligned causes correlated with the administration's earlier loosening of export restrictions.

**The Conservative Take:** Republicans have long argued that protecting advanced semiconductor technology from China is essential to national security and U.S. technological dominance. The concern here cuts both ways: first, that corporate influence might have compromised that principle; second, that the administration is now correcting course. National Review noted "an undeniable correlation" between Huang's donations and the earlier policy shift, raising red flags about pay-to-play dynamics in Washington—a charge conservatives regularly level at the Biden administration but less often at Republicans.

**Colorado Angle:** While Colorado has no major Nvidia operations, the state's tech corridor and defense contractors depend on secure supply chains and robust national security policy.
