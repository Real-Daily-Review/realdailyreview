# Bernie Sanders Doubles Down on Progressive Kingmaking in Michigan Senate Race

Bernie Sanders is betting his political credibility on Abdul El-Sayed's Michigan Senate primary, framing the race as a battle against the "billionaire class" rather than against his Democratic rival, Rep. Haley Stevens. The Vermont socialist is "pulling out all the stops" (Washington Examiner) for El-Sayed, lending his name and full endorsement to yet another progressive primary candidate this cycle.

This matters for Colorado conservatives watching the Democratic Party's ideological tug-of-war. Sanders' aggressive intervention signals the left's determination to push Senate races further left—exactly the kind of progressive overreach that has energized Republican voters nationwide. His dismissal of Stevens, a more moderate Democrat, reveals the socialist wing's contempt for centrists within their own party.

The race also underscores a broader pattern: Sanders has positioned himself as a "kingmaker" in multiple Democratic primaries, testing whether his brand of democratic socialism can reshape the party's Senate caucus. If El-Sayed wins, it validates Sanders' influence and likely means more radical economic and energy policies at the federal level—policies that would hit Colorado's energy sector and working families hard.

Republicans should watch whether Sanders' endorsement strategy succeeds in Michigan and whether similar progressive primary challenges emerge in other competitive Senate states.
