# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Colorado Mountain Town

The Aspen Acres fire has left Beulah—a small community of roughly 800 residents—facing a dual crisis: homes lost and water systems crippled, a reminder that Colorado's wildfire season doesn't just threaten lives and property, it threatens the basic utilities that keep towns functioning.

According to the Colorado Sun, water managers are now scrambling to restore clean water service after the fire damaged critical infrastructure. Unlike most other active fires across the state, this one directly hit essential utilities—a worst-case scenario for a rural community already stretched thin by evacuation and loss.

CPR News reports that Beulah residents have fought back despite the devastation, but the road ahead is long. Water system repairs in fire-damaged areas are expensive, time-consuming, and often fall on already-strained local budgets. State and federal disaster assistance will be critical, but Colorado taxpayers should brace for the costs of rebuilding infrastructure that private insurance often won't fully cover.

The broader lesson: Colorado's fire season increasingly threatens not just homes but the water systems that sustain entire communities—a problem that demands serious investment in forest management and defensible infrastructure, not just post-fire cleanup.
