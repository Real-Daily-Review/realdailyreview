# U.S.-Iran Deal Hands Tehran Leverage Over Global Oil Routes—and Colorado Energy Markets

The Trump administration has digitally signed a memorandum of understanding with Iran to end hostilities and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but the agreement hands Tehran dangerous leverage over one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints.

According to the Washington Examiner, the U.S. and Iran signed the deal remotely Wednesday, with Trump confirming the signing to reporters in France. The accord calls for uranium dilution, sanctions waivers, and restoration of pre-war conditions, per 9News Denver.

Here's the catch: while the deal guarantees 60 days of toll-free passage through the Strait of Hormuz, the Daily Wire reports that long-term control of the waterway falls to Iran, Oman, and other Gulf states—meaning Tehran could eventually impose tolls on global shipping and oil transit.

**The conservative concern:** Ceding strategic leverage to a hostile regime threatens American energy independence and global markets. For Colorado—home to oil and gas producers and refineries—instability in Middle Eastern energy supplies translates to price volatility and economic uncertainty for workers and consumers.

Republicans argue the deal repeats past mistakes of appeasement, surrendering negotiating power without securing permanent safeguards. The left frames it as pragmatic conflict resolution.

**Watch next:** Whether Iran honors the 60-day toll-free window or moves unilaterally to monetize the Strait, testing American resolve and Colorado's energy sector.
