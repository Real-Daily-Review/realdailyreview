# Federal Prosecutors Drop Vandalism Charges in Reflecting Pool Case; Trump Blasts Decision

Federal prosecutors have moved to drop criminal charges against former Olympian David Hearn, who faced accusations of intentionally damaging the Lincoln Memorial's Reflecting Pool—a move that has drawn sharp criticism from President Trump.

Hearn's office claimed new information indicated the pool damage resulted from a "rushed job" rather than deliberate vandalism, prompting the dismissal. Trump responded forcefully, telling reporters he was "really disappointed" with the handling of the case and insisting vandals *had* damaged the pool (9News Denver, Just The News).

The reversal raises questions about prosecutorial judgment and case vetting. Republicans argue the dropped charges reflect either sloppy initial investigation or overzealous prosecution—neither inspiring confidence in federal law enforcement. Trump's public criticism underscores frustration with what conservatives see as inconsistent application of justice.

For Colorado taxpayers and voters, this case exemplifies a broader concern: federal agencies making high-profile arrests without solid evidence, then quietly backing away when scrutiny arrives. The pattern fuels skepticism about DOJ credibility and selective prosecution.

Watch whether Congress demands answers on how federal prosecutors brought charges without confirming basic facts about the alleged damage.
