# Rubio's Western Defense Draws Conservative Pushback on Strategy

Secretary of State Marco Rubio is right to champion Western values against authoritarian rivals—but his framing misses the mark, according to conservative critics across the right.

Speaking from Greece with the Acropolis as backdrop, Rubio outlined an ambitious case for rekindling confidence in Western traditions and institutions as counterweight to China, Russia, and Middle Eastern theocracies. The goal is sound: defending the civilization that built free markets, individual liberty, and constitutional governance.

Yet both *National Review* and the *Washington Examiner* argue Rubio's approach glosses over uncomfortable truths. *National Review* contends that "the persistence of Western values depends on our remembering the hubris of those who came before us"—a warning that sanitized nostalgia won't persuade skeptics or shore up eroding confidence at home. *Washington Examiner* critics suggest his framing doesn't adequately reckon with Western failures and contradictions that undermine the rhetorical case.

For Colorado conservatives, the debate matters: defending Western civilization means defending the property rights, energy independence, and constitutional protections that built Colorado prosperity. A defensive crouch based on selective history won't convince younger voters or counter Beijing's narrative.

Watch whether the State Department refines this messaging or doubles down on Rubio's current approach as 2026 unfolds.
