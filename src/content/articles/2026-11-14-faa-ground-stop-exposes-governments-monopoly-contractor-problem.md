# FAA Ground Stop Exposes Government's Contractor Trap

A nationwide FAA ground stop Friday night revealed what conservatives have long warned: government agencies are locked into expensive, underperforming contracts with monopoly providers—and taxpayers foot the bill while flights get grounded.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed telecommunications provider L3Harris for the outage, which he said resulted from routers becoming overloaded with data (Breitbart). At a Saturday press conference, Duffy acknowledged the real problem: the Department of Transportation is "stuck" with L3Harris until Congress approves funding for competing companies to upgrade the system (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case is straightforward.** When government relies on sole-source contractors without competitive pressure, accountability vanishes and innovation stalls. L3Harris has little incentive to modernize infrastructure if no rival can replace them. Meanwhile, the FAA's aging, fragile telecommunications backbone—dependent on a single vendor—becomes a national security and economic vulnerability.

Duffy's plea for funding to bring in competitors is tacit admission that the current monopoly arrangement is broken. Colorado's aviation industry, from Denver International Airport to regional carriers and aerospace manufacturers, depends on reliable FAA systems. A ground stop costs the economy millions in minutes.

**Watch whether Congress actually approves competitive funding or lets the FAA remain shackled to L3Harris.**
