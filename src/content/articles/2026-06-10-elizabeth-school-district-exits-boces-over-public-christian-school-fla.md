# Elizabeth School District Abandons Education ReEnvisioned BOCES Over "Public Christian School" Controversy

Colorado's Elizabeth School District is cutting ties with Education ReEnvisioned BOCES after the regional education agency authorized what it billed as the state's "first public Christian school"—a move that triggered state scrutiny and now district defections.

The decision reflects growing tension over how far public education can go in accommodating religious instruction. Education ReEnvisioned BOCES faced regulatory pressure from state officials following the school's authorization, prompting at least one other district to consider leaving as well, according to the *Colorado Sun*.

Conservatives have long argued that public schools should allow greater flexibility for faith-based educational options within the public system, viewing such programs as expanding parental choice. Critics counter that public funding for explicitly Christian curriculum crosses constitutional and secular lines, even when enrollment is voluntary.

The controversy highlights a broader Colorado debate: whether traditional public education monopolies should yield to more diverse schooling models—including religious ones—or whether the public-private distinction demands strict secular neutrality in state-funded institutions.

Taxpayers should watch whether other districts follow Elizabeth's lead and whether state education officials move to formally restrict BOCES' authority to authorize faith-based public schools.
