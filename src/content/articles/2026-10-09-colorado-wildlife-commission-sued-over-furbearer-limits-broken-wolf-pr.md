# Colorado Wildlife Commission Faces Legal Challenge Over Furbearer Limits

Colorado Parks and Wildlife is under fire for what ranchers and hunters say is a broken promise and regulatory overreach. A lawsuit challenges the state wildlife commission's furbearer bag limit decision, seeking to have it declared unlawful and vacated, according to *Complete Colorado*.

The dispute centers on CPW's credibility with rural Coloradans. In January 2025, CPW's director promised ranchers the state would kill problem wolves if they complied with state directives—a commitment that appears to have fallen short, *9News Denver* reports. Ranchers relied on that assurance to manage predation threats to livestock and livelihoods.

Conservatives argue this exemplifies how unelected bureaucrats make promises they don't keep, then impose regulations that harm working Coloradans without accountability. The lawsuit represents pushback against what critics see as agency mission creep: wildlife management decisions made by commissioners who answer to no voters and increasingly ignore the needs of rural communities, hunters, and agricultural interests.

The case highlights a broader tension in Colorado between environmental regulators and the ranching, hunting, and energy communities that have stewarded the state's land and wildlife for generations. Both the furbearer limits and the wolf-management dispute suggest CPW's priorities may have shifted away from traditional wildlife stewardship toward preservation models that don't account for rural economic realities.

Watch whether the court sides with ranchers and hunters challenging CPW's authority, or whether the agency's decisions stand unchecked.
