# Trump Taps DNI Clayton to Lead 'Super Intelligence Force'—But Colorado Should Demand Clarity on AI Regulation

President Trump has announced a new "Super Intelligence Force" under Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to coordinate federal AI policy across tech companies, infrastructure providers, and religious groups. The move signals Trump's intent to position America as the global leader in superintelligence development rather than cede the field to China or Europe.

The initiative marks a significant pivot from the Biden administration's heavy-handed regulatory approach. Where progressives pushed restrictive AI guardrails and compliance burdens, Trump's framework emphasizes coordination and American competitive dominance. Republicans argue this market-friendly stance will accelerate innovation and job creation in the tech sector—critical for Colorado's growing tech hubs in Denver and Boulder.

However, conservatives should scrutinize what "coordination" actually means. Will the SIF become another federal bureaucracy imposing hidden mandates on private companies? Or will it genuinely facilitate innovation while protecting American interests? The devil is in the implementation.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses need to know: Does this force expand federal hiring and spending, or streamline existing agencies? Will it protect Colorado's energy and agriculture sectors from AI-driven regulatory overreach? Expect detailed guidance on the SIF's budget, staffing, and actual authority over private enterprise in coming weeks.
