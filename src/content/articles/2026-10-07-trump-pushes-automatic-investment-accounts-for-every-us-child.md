# Trump Pushes Automatic Child Investment Accounts—But Questions Loom on Government Role

President Trump announced automatic enrollment of all Americans under 18 in "Trump Accounts," government-maintained investment vehicles funded by Treasury money and private contributions. The move aims to build wealth for young Americans, but conservatives are raising hard questions about federal overreach and the proper role of government in family finances.

Republicans backing the plan argue it democratizes investment access and builds generational wealth. However, fiscal conservatives worry about the mechanics: Who controls these accounts? What are the fees? How much Treasury money flows into them? These details matter enormously to Colorado taxpayers already burdened by federal debt and inflation.

The accounts would be automatic unless parents opt out—a default that reverses traditional parental authority and raises libertarian concerns about government paternalism. Critics also question whether this is genuine wealth-building or another federal program dressed up in market language.

Colorado families should scrutinize the fine print before assuming this benefits their kids. The devil, as always, lives in implementation details—and in how much of your tax dollars fund accounts the government controls.

Watch closely for Treasury funding levels and whether Congress demands oversight hearings before rollout.
