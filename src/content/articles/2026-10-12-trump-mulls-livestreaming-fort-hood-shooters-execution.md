# Trump Weighs Public Execution Livestream as Pentagon Considers Transparency on Hasan Case

President Trump is deliberating whether the Pentagon should livestream the firing squad execution of Nidal Malik Hasan, the Army psychiatrist convicted of murdering 13 soldiers at Fort Hood in 2009—a decision that pits transparency against operational security concerns.

The Pentagon has proposed making Hasan's execution public, a move some conservatives argue serves a deterrent purpose and ensures accountability in military justice. Trump told reporters he hasn't yet decided whether to approve the livestream, signaling the White House is actively weighing the proposal (Fox News).

Supporters of public execution proceedings argue transparency reinforces the rule of law and demonstrates that even high-profile military cases face swift, certain justice. The Daily Wire notes that public witness to capital punishment has become increasingly controversial, with recent botched executions raising questions about how the modern state conducts such proceedings.

The case carries symbolic weight for military justice advocates who view Hasan's 2009 attack as terrorism and believe public accountability matters. However, concerns about operational security, victim family privacy, and the precedent of livestreaming executions remain unresolved.
