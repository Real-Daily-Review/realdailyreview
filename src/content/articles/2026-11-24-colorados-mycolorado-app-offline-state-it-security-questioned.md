# Colorado's myColorado App Down Ahead of Holiday Travel Crunch

Colorado's state-run myColorado app has gone dark just as thousands of Coloradans prepare to travel for Thanksgiving—a reminder that government digital infrastructure remains vulnerable to cyberattacks and poor contingency planning.

State officials disclosed a cybersecurity incident affecting between 50,000 and 150,000 users, with no timeline for restoration (9News Denver). The app outage compounds travel headaches as major storms threaten the region, with forecasters warning of high winds and potential bomb cyclone conditions hitting the Northeast by Thanksgiving (The Daily Wire).

**The Conservative Angle:** Colorado taxpayers funded this digital platform. When government IT systems fail during critical periods—especially without clear restoration timelines—it exposes the risks of centralized state control over essential services. Republicans have long argued that government agencies often lack the agility, accountability, and technical expertise of private-sector alternatives. This incident underscores why conservatives favor market-driven solutions and robust cybersecurity standards with real consequences for negligence.

State officials have offered no public explanation of how the breach occurred, what safeguards failed, or why backup systems weren't operational. Coloradans deserve answers about how their personal data was exposed and what the state will do differently.

Watch for legislative demands next session: Will Republicans push for independent audits of state IT security, or demand the state contract critical services to private vendors with proven track records?
