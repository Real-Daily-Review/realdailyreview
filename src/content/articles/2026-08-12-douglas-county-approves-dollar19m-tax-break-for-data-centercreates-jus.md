# Douglas County Hands $19M Tax Break to Data Center—for Just 16 Jobs

Douglas County Commissioners just approved a $19 million tax rebate for a Flexential data center in Parker, waiving business personal property taxes for 35 years. The catch: the project is expected to create only 16 new jobs.

That's roughly $1.2 million per job—a sobering return on foregone tax revenue that Colorado taxpayers won't see. While data centers can generate some economic activity, conservatives question whether selective tax breaks for individual corporations represent sound fiscal policy or corporate welfare dressed up as economic development.

Republicans and fiscal hawks argue that broad, predictable tax policy—not targeted rebates—attracts sustainable business investment. They worry that once counties start handing out 35-year tax holidays, other companies demand the same deal, eroding the tax base permanently while creating few lasting jobs.

Proponents counter that data centers bring infrastructure and ancillary benefits. But the math here is hard to defend: $19 million in foregone revenue to add 16 positions is the kind of deal that should trigger serious scrutiny from county commissioners concerned about long-term fiscal health.

Watch whether other Colorado counties follow Douglas's lead—or whether taxpayer groups challenge the precedent.
