# Colorado Springs Fire Captain Dies From 9/11 Ground Zero Cancer; City Faces Election Law Lawsuit

Patrick Rogacki, a Colorado Springs fire captain who responded to Ground Zero recovery efforts after 9/11, has died from cancer linked to his service—a sobering reminder of the long-term toll on first responders who answered the call two decades ago (9News Denver). Rogacki will be added to the Fallen Firefighter Memorial in Colorado Springs this year.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs faces legal pressure over its municipal election timing. The city is being sued for holding April elections rather than aligning with state voting law changes that took effect in 2025 (Complete Colorado). This marks the first enforcement action since the law's implementation—raising questions about how Colorado municipalities interpret and comply with state election mandates.

The lawsuit underscores a broader Colorado elections trend: unaffiliated voter registration is rising, and primary participation increasingly determines general election outcomes (Complete Colorado). Party-registered voters who skip caucuses effectively cede influence to primary voters, fragmenting the electorate.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether Colorado Springs prevails in the election timing dispute and how aggressively state officials enforce voting law compliance across municipalities.
