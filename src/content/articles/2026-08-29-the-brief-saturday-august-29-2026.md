# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, August 29, 2026

Colorado's Saturday opens with a homegrown hockey hero making history and Front Range taxpayers being handed the bill for a government rail project they never asked for. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Makar Signs Richest Contract in NHL History — And He's Staying in Colorado

Cale Makar and the Colorado Avalanche have agreed to an eight-year, $163.2 million contract extension, making it the richest deal in NHL history. For a state that has watched too many sports franchises bleed talent to bigger markets, this is unambiguously good news. Makar earned every dollar on the open market — no government subsidies, no sweetheart deals, just a franchise betting big on the best defenseman in the game. Colorado wins.

---

## Front Range Voters Face Sales Tax Hike for Government Rail Boondoggle

The Front Range Passenger Rail District's board voted to put a 33-cent sales tax increase on every $100 spent before voters, aiming to raise $295 million a year for the so-called Colorado Connector — a passenger rail line stretching from Fort Collins to Pueblo. Conservatives should call this what it is: a tax hike to fund a government-run transportation project that the market has never been able to sustain on its own. Every Colorado family and small business along the Front Range would pay more every time they swipe a card, with no guarantee the trains ever run on time — or at all.

## Greeley Veteran Honored for Vietnam Service — A Family That Answered the Call

Bill Brewster of Greeley is being honored for his Vietnam service, part of a family with a tradition of military sacrifice stretching across multiple American wars. In an era when too many politicians treat veterans as props for campaign ads while gutting military budgets or burdening service members with social engineering mandates, stories like Brewster's are a reminder of what real duty looks like. Colorado has a proud military community — and it deserves leaders who match that commitment.

## Big Bend Border Wall Pause Extended — Tribal Sovereignty or Open-Border Delay Tactic?

The pause on border wall construction in the Big Bend region has been extended into September after the Tohono O'odham Nation claimed masked CBP agents blocked tribal police as contractors drilled on land the tribe considers sacred. The border security debate is real and the tribal sovereignty concerns deserve a fair hearing — but conservatives should watch closely whether legitimate land-rights arguments are being weaponized by open-border advocates to stall construction indefinitely. Colorado ranchers and law enforcement know better than most what an unsecured southern border costs in fentanyl, trafficking, and crime.
