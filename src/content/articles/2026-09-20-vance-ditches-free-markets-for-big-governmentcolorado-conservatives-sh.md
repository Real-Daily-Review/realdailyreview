# Vance Signals GOP Shift Away From Free-Market Conservatism—And Colorado Should Pay Attention

Vice President JD Vance is openly abandoning Milton Friedman's free-market conservatism in favor of Alexander Hamilton's big-government approach, calling the pivot "obviously a good thing." That's a red flag for Colorado conservatives who've long championed limited government and market-driven solutions.

Vance's Hamiltonian turn—which treats strong central government as the default rather than the exception—represents a fundamental break from the Reagan-era limited-government consensus that built the modern conservative movement. Where Friedman's tradition required government to justify intervention, Hamilton's model presumes government should lead.

**Conservatives argue** this represents dangerous mission creep: if Republicans embrace expansive federal power now, what stops Democrats from weaponizing that same apparatus later? The track record isn't encouraging. Colorado taxpayers and energy producers have already felt the sting of federal overreach on water rights, land use, and energy policy. Handing Washington more economic authority—even under Republican leadership—invites the same regulatory capture and crony capitalism that plague progressive administrations.

The GOP's Hamiltonian drift also threatens the coalition that elected Trump: working-class voters and small-business owners who voted for deregulation and tax cuts, not industrial policy and government-picked winners.

**Watch whether Colorado Republicans in Congress push back on this ideological shift or fall in line.**
