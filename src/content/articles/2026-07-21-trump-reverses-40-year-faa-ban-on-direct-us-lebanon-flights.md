# Trump Lifts 40-Year FAA Ban on Direct US-Lebanon Flights

President Trump is reversing a Reagan-era flight prohibition that has kept direct U.S.-Lebanon air service grounded since 1985, when the FAA suspended routes following the hijacking of TWA Flight 847. Trump announced the decision Tuesday during a bilateral meeting with Lebanese President Joseph Aoun at the White House (Washington Examiner, Just The News).

The move signals Trump's willingness to normalize diplomatic and commercial ties with Lebanon, a country long shadowed by security concerns and regional instability. The original ban reflected legitimate safety protocols—the 1985 hijacking killed one American and exposed vulnerabilities in Middle East travel security.

Conservatives will likely view this through a security lens: does Lebanon's current stability justify reopening air corridors? The country remains influenced by Hezbollah and faces ongoing political fragility. Trump's calculation appears to be that improved U.S.-Lebanon relations serve broader Middle East strategy, though critics may question whether commercial aviation should precede demonstrated, sustained security improvements.

The practical impact remains limited—airlines must still assess demand and safety before launching routes—but the symbolic shift is clear: Trump is betting on engagement over isolation.
