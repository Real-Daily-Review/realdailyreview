# Colorado Supreme Court Rejects Tina Peters' Conviction Appeal; Toll-Lane Cases Head to High Court

Colorado's Supreme Court has declined to hear an appeal from former Mesa County Clerk Tina Peters, leaving her felony conviction intact (CPR News). Peters, who served prison time for her role in a 2020 election-security breach, sought to overturn her conviction but the court refused to revisit the case.

Meanwhile, the same court will weigh two cases challenging Colorado's toll-lane weaving enforcement. Drivers argue the state cannot fine them for crossing solid lines into express lanes when their transponders automatically paid the toll—raising questions about whether the fines constitute double-dipping by the state (9News Denver).

**The conservative angle:** Peters' case underscores the importance of election integrity safeguards, though her conviction stands. On tolls, Colorado taxpayers should scrutinize whether CDOT is using weaving fines as a revenue grab rather than genuine safety enforcement. If drivers' transponders paid the toll, the state's legal argument for additional fines weakens considerably.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that toll-lane systems can become cash cows for government agencies. This case tests whether Colorado will use technical violations to extract extra revenue from commuters already paying tolls.

**Watch whether the Supreme Court sides with drivers or expands CDOT's enforcement authority.**
