# Denver Crime Wave: Mail Carrier Robbed, Bike Theft Ring Busted

Colorado's streets are becoming increasingly unsafe as criminals target both essential workers and residents' property with brazen theft and violence.

Denver police arrested a suspect after a letter carrier was robbed in an armed attack, with stolen mail later recovered from the suspect's dishwasher (9News Denver). A separate robbery of a postal worker reported October 1 remains under investigation but appears unrelated to this incident.

Meanwhile, Boulder authorities arrested three suspects and recovered numerous high-end bikes and scooters following an investigation into a coordinated theft operation (KDVR Fox31 Denver). The recovery suggests an organized ring targeting affluent neighborhoods—a pattern consistent with Colorado's broader property crime surge.

These incidents underscore a troubling reality: Denver and Boulder residents face rising crime despite progressive leadership's promises of public safety through social spending and criminal justice reform. Armed robbery of essential workers and organized theft rings indicate that catch-and-release policies and reduced law enforcement resources are failing to deter criminals or protect communities.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Colorado needs stronger prosecution, meaningful consequences for repeat offenders, and adequate police funding—not more social programs that excuse criminal behavior. Democrats typically counter that root causes like poverty and addiction require investment in treatment and housing.

Taxpayers should watch whether these arrests lead to meaningful prosecution and jail time, or whether Colorado's revolving-door justice system releases these suspects back onto Denver streets.
