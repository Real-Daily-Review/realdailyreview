# Michigan Democrats' Last-Minute Scramble Exposes Deep Primary Rift

Elizabeth Warren's Monday appearance for Abdul el Sayed in Detroit underscores a Democratic Party fracturing over its nominee just hours before Michigan voters decide—a cautionary tale for Colorado progressives banking on party unity in tight races.

El Sayed, the Democratic Senate nominee, barely survived his primary with roughly a 1-point victory over Rep. Haley Stevens, according to the *Washington Examiner*. That razor-thin margin—far below the expected 10-point win—revealed a coalition of moderate Democrats and Jewish voters resisting el Sayed's more progressive platform. The intraparty rupture intensified when Stevens initially refused to endorse el Sayed, forcing Warren and national Democrats into damage-control mode.

Republicans argue the spectacle demonstrates that progressive candidates struggle to unite their own party, let alone win general elections. When Democrats must deploy U.S. senators in final hours to shore up a nominee, it signals weakness and internal ideological warfare—not strength.

For Colorado, this matters: Democratic candidates here often rely on similar progressive coalitions. If Michigan's model holds, Colorado Democrats could face similar fractures in competitive statewide races, particularly if nominees lean too far left for moderate suburban and Jewish voters.

Watch whether el Sayed's general-election performance validates Republican warnings about progressive overreach, or whether Democratic turnout machines overcome the primary damage.
