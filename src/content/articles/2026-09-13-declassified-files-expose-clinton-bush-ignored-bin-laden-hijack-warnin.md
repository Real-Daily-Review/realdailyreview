# Declassified Files Show Clinton, Bush Ignored Repeated Bin Laden Hijack Warnings

Newly declassified CIA documents reveal that both the Clinton and Bush administrations received multiple warnings that Usama bin Laden's network planned to hijack commercial aircraft and strike U.S. cities—intelligence that went unheeded before the September 11 attacks, according to Fox News and Just The News.

The CIA's latest document release represents the agency's largest tranche of 9/11-related materials to date, exposing a critical intelligence-to-action gap that conservatives argue exemplifies government bureaucratic failure at the highest levels.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans and national security hawks contend the declassified warnings underscore how intelligence agencies failed to connect dots and how political leadership failed to act decisively on credible threats. The failures raise hard questions about government competence, information-sharing between agencies, and whether adequate resources were devoted to counterterrorism before 9/11.

**What's at Stake:** While this is a national security matter, Coloradans should note that government intelligence failures and interagency dysfunction remain relevant to how federal agencies handle threats today—from border security to domestic terrorism. Taxpayers fund these agencies; accountability matters.

Watch for congressional Republicans demanding fuller explanations from intelligence officials about why actionable warnings weren't escalated or acted upon.
