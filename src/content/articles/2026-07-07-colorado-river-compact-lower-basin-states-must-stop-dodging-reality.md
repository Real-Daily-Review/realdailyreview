# Colorado River Compact Faces Reckoning as Lower Basin States Dodge Hard Choices

Colorado and Upper Basin states have spent decades adapting to climate realities on the Colorado River—while Arizona, California, and Nevada continue demanding the same water allocations as if nothing has changed. According to the Colorado Sun, the Lower Basin states must finally acknowledge what Colorado already knows: the river is shrinking, and pretending otherwise only delays the inevitable pain.

Republicans and water-rights advocates argue that California and Arizona have refused to make proportional cuts, instead pressuring Upper Basin states like Colorado to shoulder disproportionate sacrifice. Colorado's agriculture, energy production, and municipal water supplies depend on predictable allocations under the 1922 Compact. If Lower Basin states won't accept reality-based reductions, Colorado taxpayers and farmers face the bill.

The Left frames this as a climate-adaptation problem requiring federal intervention and "equitable" redistribution. Conservatives counter that the Compact is a binding legal agreement—not a negotiating document to be rewritten every decade based on political pressure.

Colorado's position is clear: we've already cut our consumption. It's time for the desert states to do the same instead of gaming the system.

**Watch for:** Whether the Biden administration pressures Colorado into additional cuts while letting California off the hook.
