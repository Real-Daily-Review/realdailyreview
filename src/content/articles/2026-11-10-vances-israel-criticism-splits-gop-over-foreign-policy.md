# Vice President Vance's Israel Criticism Splits GOP Over Foreign Policy Direction

Vice President JD Vance is drawing fire from fellow conservatives for blaming U.S.–Israel alliance commitments and Iran tensions for recent Republican electoral losses, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Vance has framed the criticism under an "America first" doctrine, arguing that alleged Israeli influence operations have become a political liability for Republicans.

The critique marks a significant fault line within the GOP. Critics argue Vance is scapegoating a key ally rather than addressing substantive policy failures or messaging problems that cost Republicans votes. They contend that weakening U.S.–Israel ties—a cornerstone of Republican foreign policy for decades—abandons a democratic partner in a volatile region and signals unreliability to American allies globally.

Vance's position, first aired on Joe Rogan's podcast, reflects a growing America First faction within the party that questions decades of Middle East commitments. Supporters of this view argue the U.S. should prioritize domestic priorities over foreign entanglements.

For Colorado conservatives, this debate matters: the state has significant defense and aerospace industries tied to Middle East policy, and many voters hold strong pro-Israel convictions rooted in religious and strategic values.
