# Trump's Iran Deal Draws Fire From Conservative Hawks—and Support From Skeptics

Trump's new Iran memorandum is splitting Republicans, with hardliners calling it a diplomatic surrender while others defend the 60-day negotiating window as a pragmatic reset.

Conservative critics (Fox News) argue the deal represents one of the most humiliating U.S. agreements in history, citing what they call billions in concessions for minimal Iranian commitments. They're urging Trump to walk away entirely.

But Trump himself is downplaying urgency. According to Just The News, he told allies the 60-day timeline in the digitally signed memorandum—expected to be formally inked Friday—is not a "hard" deadline. Both sides committed to "negotiating and achieving the final deal in a maximum 60 days, extended with mutual consent," leaving room for flexibility.

Notably, some Republicans are rallying behind Trump's approach. Senators Lindsey Graham and Rand Paul (Breitbart) have backed the Memorandum of Understanding, suggesting the party isn't monolithic on Iran strategy.

The split reflects a deeper GOP divide: hawks who view any Iran deal as appeasement versus pragmatists who see negotiation as preferable to escalation. Trump's language—soft deadlines, mutual consent extensions—suggests he's threading that needle.
