# Trump Tightens Visa Rules for Foreign Students, Journalists—Here's What Colorado Should Know

The Trump administration is scrapping decades of open-ended visa policy, replacing indefinite "duration of status" permits with fixed time limits for international students, journalists, and temporary visitors effective Sept. 15 (9News Denver, Just The News).

Under the old system, foreign students and visa holders could remain in the U.S. as long as they maintained their status—no hard deadline. The new rules impose concrete expiration dates, forcing reapplication and background checks at regular intervals.

**The conservative case:** Tighter visa controls strengthen border security and reduce administrative ambiguity. Fixed timelines give immigration authorities clearer oversight and prevent visa holders from exploiting indefinite stays. Republicans argue this closes a loophole that has allowed foreign nationals to operate without meaningful re-vetting.

**The left's objection:** Universities and media outlets warn the policy will deter top international talent and damage America's competitive edge in research and journalism.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado universities—particularly CU Boulder and Colorado State—enroll thousands of international students. The policy could affect enrollment, research funding, and campus diversity. Colorado's tech and energy sectors also rely on skilled foreign workers.

Watch whether Colorado's higher-education lobby pushes back on implementation or seeks exemptions—and whether the state's Republican legislature backs the Trump administration's stricter line.
