# Trump Calls Off Iran Strike After Deal Framework Emerges

President Trump has canceled a planned military strike on Iran after securing what he describes as a breakthrough agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and neutralize Tehran's nuclear threat, according to reports from Just The News and Breitbart.

Trump announced Saturday night that he halted the operation after Iran and other Middle Eastern nations requested a pause in hostilities. According to the president, the "perimeters" of a deal have been established that would fully reopen critical shipping lanes and eliminate Iran's nuclear weapons program—a longtime conservative priority that the Obama-era nuclear deal failed to achieve.

"Get to work, everybody, and get it DONE," Trump said, noting that Israel has joined him in the commitment.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy markets and gas prices matter to Colorado families and businesses. A reopened Strait of Hormuz reduces global oil supply disruptions and could ease pressure on fuel costs—a direct benefit to Colorado's economy and households already burdened by inflation.

Conservatives have long argued that Trump's maximum-pressure strategy on Iran—withdrawing from the flawed JCPOA and imposing sanctions—created leverage for genuine negotiations. This deal framework, if finalized, would represent vindication of that approach over the failed appeasement of the previous administration.
