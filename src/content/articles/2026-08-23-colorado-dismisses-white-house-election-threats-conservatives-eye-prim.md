# Colorado Election Officials Brush Off White House Pressure on Mail Ballots

Colorado's election establishment is dismissing White House threats over mail-in voting ahead of 2026, even as conservatives argue the state's primary system needs an overhaul.

The Denver Post reports that Colorado election officials predict "little change" to the general election despite executive orders targeting mail ballots, voter registration lawsuits, and federal funding threats. State officials say they're "confident" in their processes.

But conservatives see a different problem. Complete Colorado argues the real issue isn't federal pressure—it's Colorado's primary system itself. In a crowded field, candidates can win nominations with small vote shares even when voters broadly prefer someone else. That dynamic, conservatives say, enabled what they call the "Griswold fiasco"—a reference to recent state Democratic Party turmoil.

Republicans have long criticized Colorado's all-mail voting model as vulnerable to fraud and ballot harvesting, though election officials maintain security measures work. The White House pressure on mail ballots reflects national GOP skepticism of the practice, but Colorado Democrats control the election apparatus and show no appetite for change.

**Watch:** whether Colorado Republicans push primary reform legislation or whether the 2026 cycle forces a reckoning on how the state nominates candidates.
