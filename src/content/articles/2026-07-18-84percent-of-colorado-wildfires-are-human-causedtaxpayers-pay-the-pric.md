# Human Negligence Driving Colorado's Wildfire Crisis—And Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Colorado faces a catastrophic wildfire season, with two active fires ranking among the state's 10 largest on record, according to 9News Denver. University of Colorado researchers have confirmed what land managers have long known: humans start roughly 84% of wildfires nationwide, per the Colorado Sun.

That's the real story conservatives should focus on. While environmental groups reflexively blame "climate change" for fire intensity, the data points to preventable human error—carelessness, unattended campfires, equipment use, and arson. Colorado taxpayers are now bankrolling massive firefighting operations, emergency response, and post-fire recovery because of individual negligence and, in some cases, criminal behavior.

Republicans and land-use advocates argue that aggressive forest management—thinning overgrown timber, clearing deadfall, and controlled burns—remains the most cost-effective long-term solution. Yet regulatory barriers and environmental litigation often block these efforts. Meanwhile, progressive policies prioritizing "wilderness preservation" over active stewardship have left Colorado's forests dangerously dense and fuel-laden.

The state should prioritize accountability for human-caused fires and remove regulatory obstacles to preventive forest management before the next fire season arrives.
