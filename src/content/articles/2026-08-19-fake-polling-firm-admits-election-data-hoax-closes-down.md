# Fake Polling Firm's Shutdown Exposes Election Integrity Gaps

A polling outfit called Median Strategies admitted to fabricating election surveys—including data on a closely watched Wisconsin primary and Los Angeles mayor race—before abruptly closing shop, claiming the whole thing was a "social experiment" (Fox News Politics, Daily Caller).

The firm's admission raises serious questions about election integrity safeguards. If a pollster can manufacture data on high-profile races without immediate detection, what's stopping bad actors from poisoning the information well during Colorado's own elections? Voters and campaigns rely on accurate polling to make informed decisions; fake data corrupts that process.

Republicans and election-integrity advocates argue this incident underscores the need for stronger vetting of polling firms and tighter standards for data transparency. The left has largely ignored the story, preferring to focus on claims of "misinformation" from conservative outlets rather than address institutional failures in polling oversight.

Colorado election officials and the state GOP should demand clarity on whether any Median Strategies data influenced Colorado races or polling in 2024 or 2026—and whether state election law provides adequate penalties for data fraud.

Watch whether Colorado's Secretary of State's office investigates whether fake polling affected state contests or whether this becomes another election-integrity scandal the mainstream media buries.
