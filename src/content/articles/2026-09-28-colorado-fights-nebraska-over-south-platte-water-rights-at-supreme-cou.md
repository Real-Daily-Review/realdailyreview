# Colorado Stands Firm Against Nebraska Water Grab

Colorado is fighting back hard in a Supreme Court battle over the South Platte River—and state officials say Nebraska is the real water thief here. According to the Colorado Sun, the state's attorney general is arguing that Nebraskans, not Coloradans, are overdrawn from the shared river system ahead of what could be a landmark justices' decision.

This matters enormously for Colorado's agricultural economy and Front Range water security. The South Platte supplies irrigation to thousands of farms and ranches across northeastern Colorado, and any adverse ruling could force the state to cut water allocations—devastating to rural communities already squeezed by drought and competing urban demands.

Republicans and water-rights advocates argue Colorado must aggressively defend its legal entitlements under interstate compacts. Ceding ground to Nebraska would set a dangerous precedent, they warn, potentially opening the door to similar claims from other states and federal overreach on Colorado's water management.

The case also intersects with broader conservative concerns about judicial activism on environmental issues. Justice Samuel Alito recently recused himself from a separate landmark climate case involving Suncor Energy in Boulder (Daily Caller), signaling the Court's sensitivity to these disputes.
