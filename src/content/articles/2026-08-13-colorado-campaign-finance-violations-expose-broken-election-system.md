# Colorado Election Chaos Exposes Need for Real Reform

Colorado's election system is broken—and the latest campaign finance violations prove it. Secretary of State Jena Griswold's office has alleged that gubernatorial candidate Victor Marx accepted over $78,000 in improper donations, with at least 197 violations from 111 donors exceeding the $1,450 individual contribution limit (Denver Post).

The mess underscores a deeper problem conservatives have long flagged: Colorado's electoral rules create perverse incentives. As Complete Colorado notes, a crowded field allows candidates to win with small vote pluralities even when voters prefer someone else—a recipe for weak mandates and fractured governance.

Republicans argue the state needs genuine reform: ranked-choice voting alternatives, stricter enforcement mechanisms, and clearer contribution rules that don't invite violations. Democrats, meanwhile, have resisted meaningful changes, preferring to tinker at the margins while protecting incumbent advantages.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the stakes are real. Weak electoral legitimacy breeds weak leadership, and weak leadership means regulatory chaos, tax hikes, and policy whiplash. When governors and legislators lack clear mandates, they compensate with executive overreach.

Watch whether the Griswold office pursues enforcement aggressively or lets this fade—a telling sign of whether election integrity matters more than partisan protection.
