# Colorado Voters to Decide Whether Child Sex Traffickers Get Life Sentences

Colorado voters will decide this November whether to impose mandatory life sentences on those convicted of sexually trafficking minors, a measure that reflects growing public demand for tougher penalties on predators.

Proposition 133 would elevate sexual trafficking of a minor to a Class 1 felony—Colorado's most severe classification—ensuring convicted traffickers face life imprisonment (Complete Colorado). Ballots will begin arriving in Colorado mailboxes within two weeks, giving voters a direct say on criminal justice policy.

The measure represents a conservative approach to protecting children: mandatory, serious consequences for serious crimes. Supporters argue that trafficking minors warrants the harshest penalties available, removing ambiguity from sentencing and ensuring predators cannot return to prey on vulnerable victims.

Democrats and criminal-justice reformers typically oppose mandatory minimums, arguing they limit judicial discretion and can perpetuate incarceration disparities. The left generally favors rehabilitation-focused approaches over fixed sentences, though few openly defend leniency for child trafficking.

Colorado has seen high-profile trafficking cases in recent years, making this a visceral issue for families and law-and-order voters statewide. The ballot measure gives Coloradans a chance to signal whether they prioritize victim protection over prosecutorial flexibility.

Watch whether Proposition 133 passes with bipartisan support—a likely outcome that could signal voter appetite for similar tough-on-crime measures in 2026.
