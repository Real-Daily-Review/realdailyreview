# SEC Sues LSU Over Illegal Player Recruitment Scheme

The Southeastern Conference is taking LSU to court over what amounts to a brazen end-run around college sports rules—and it signals how badly the NCAA's authority has collapsed.

The SEC alleges LSU launched a "deliberate and coordinated campaign" to recruit former NFL players who had signed contracts with teams including the Cleveland Browns, New Orleans Saints, and Tampa Bay Buccaneers (Just The News). Head coach Lane Kiffin is named in the lawsuit, which centers on eligibility violations that have even drawn state political figures into the fray (9News Denver).

**The conservative take:** This mess exposes what happens when governing bodies lose enforcement credibility. The NCAA spent decades micromanaging minor infractions while ignoring systemic corruption—now schools openly flout rules because penalties are toothless. LSU's gambit isn't novel; it's the logical endpoint of a system that lost moral authority years ago.

The real question isn't whether LSU broke rules. It's whether the SEC can actually enforce them, or whether college sports has devolved into a free-for-all where the biggest checkbooks and loosest ethics win.

**Watch for:** Whether the SEC's lawsuit sticks or gets buried in appeals—a sign of whether any governing body in college athletics still has teeth.
