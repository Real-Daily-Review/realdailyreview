# Trump's Georgia Endorsement Fails as Voters Pick Jackson Over Jones

President Trump's endorsement machine hit a speed bump in Georgia's Republican gubernatorial primary—and it's a reminder that even MAGA momentum can't override voter skepticism about establishment picks.

Businessman Rick Jackson, a healthcare executive, won the GOP nomination for Georgia governor Tuesday night, decisively defeating Trump-endorsed Lt. Gov. Burt Jones in one of 2026's most expensive and closely watched primary battles. Jackson's victory came despite—or perhaps because of—Trump Jr.'s aggressive backing of Jones and Trump's own endorsement.

The race underscores a broader conservative tension: while Trump remains a powerful force in Republican politics, voters are increasingly willing to chart their own course. Jackson's self-funded campaign and outsider positioning resonated with Georgia Republicans tired of establishment politics, even when that establishment wore a MAGA hat.

For Colorado conservatives watching national GOP dynamics, the Georgia result matters. It signals that Trump endorsements, while still potent, aren't automatic primary winners—a lesson worth remembering as 2026 midterms approach and Colorado Republicans prepare their own slate of candidates. Expect more primary challenges to Trump-backed candidates as Republican voters assert independence.

Watch whether Jackson's nomination strategy—heavy spending, outsider branding, skepticism of Trump's picks—gets replicated in other 2026 GOP primaries.
