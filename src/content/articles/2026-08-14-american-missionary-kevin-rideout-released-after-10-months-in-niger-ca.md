# American Missionary Kevin Rideout Released After Nearly Year-Long Captivity in Niger

Kevin Rideout, a Christian missionary pilot, has been freed after roughly 10 months in captivity in Niger's capital, Niamey, according to SIM International, his mission organization. The Washington Examiner reports Rideout "is in good health in the care of U.S. officials" following Friday's announcement of his release.

Rideout was kidnapped outside his home last year. The Daily Wire notes that details remain sparse—how he escaped, which group held him, and the circumstances of his release have not been disclosed. U.S. officials are now overseeing his care as arrangements for family reunification proceed.

The case underscores ongoing security risks facing American citizens abroad, particularly Christian workers in unstable regions. Niger, a key U.S. counterterrorism partner in West Africa, has faced mounting jihadist violence and instability in recent years, complicating diplomatic and military operations.

Rideout's release comes as a relief to his family and the broader Christian missionary community, though the lack of transparency about the negotiation or rescue raises questions about what concessions, if any, were made to secure his freedom—a concern conservatives typically flag when Americans are held hostage overseas.

Watch for official statements from the State Department clarifying the circumstances of Rideout's release and whether any ransom or prisoner exchanges were involved.
