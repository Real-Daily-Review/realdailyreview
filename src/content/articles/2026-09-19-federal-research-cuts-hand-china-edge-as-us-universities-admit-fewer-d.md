# Federal Research Funding Cuts Threaten U.S. Competitiveness Against China

Washington's failure to adequately fund scientific research is handing China a decisive edge in the innovation race—and American universities are already feeling the squeeze.

Universities admitted 15% fewer doctoral students this fall, marking the second consecutive year of decline, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The culprit: federal research funding constraints that are forcing institutions to scale back graduate programs and research capacity.

The conservative argument is straightforward: America cannot afford to cede scientific leadership to Beijing. Federal research investment, particularly in STEM fields, is a legitimate government function that yields returns far exceeding the cost. When Washington freezes or cuts research budgets, it's not fiscal restraint—it's strategic surrender.

Colorado universities, including CU Boulder and Colorado State, depend heavily on federal research grants. Reduced doctoral admissions mean fewer trained scientists and engineers entering the workforce, directly weakening Colorado's tech sector and energy innovation capacity at a moment when American energy independence demands advanced research in nuclear, solar, and other technologies.

The broader concern: if the U.S. loses its scientific edge, China fills the vacuum. That's not a trade-off conservatives should accept.

Watch whether Congress prioritizes sustained, competitive federal research funding in the next appropriations cycle—or continues the slow-motion surrender of American innovation leadership.
