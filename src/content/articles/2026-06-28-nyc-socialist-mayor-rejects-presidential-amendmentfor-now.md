# NYC Socialist Mayor Rejects Constitutional Amendment—For Now

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, a self-described socialist who rose to prominence after winning a Democratic primary, said Sunday he does not support amending the Constitution's natural-born citizen requirement for the presidency—despite being ineligible himself. Born in Uganda and naturalized in 2018, the 34-year-old dismissed speculation about his own eligibility as his national profile grows (Washington Examiner).

Mamdani's statement on ABC News's *This Week* appears designed to preempt questions about constitutional workarounds as progressive Democrats increasingly float radical institutional changes. The move reflects a broader left-wing pattern: propose sweeping constitutional rewrites, then retreat when public scrutiny arrives.

Conservatives argue that the natural-born citizen clause exists precisely to prevent foreign influence in the presidency—a safeguard the Founders understood as essential. Mamdani's quick disavowal suggests even Democratic operatives recognize the political toxicity of openly campaigning to weaken constitutional guardrails, especially one protecting executive power.

The incident underscores how far the Democratic Party's socialist wing has drifted. A decade ago, such a figure would be fringe; today, he's a major-city executive with national ambitions. Colorado voters should watch whether similar constitutional-amendment talk gains traction among state Democrats—particularly around gun rights, energy regulation, or election law.
