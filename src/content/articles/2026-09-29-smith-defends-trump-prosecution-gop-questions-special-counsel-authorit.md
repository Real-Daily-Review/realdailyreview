# Ex-Special Counsel Smith Doubles Down on Trump Prosecution, Faces GOP Skepticism

Jack Smith told the Senate Judiciary Committee he stands by his decision to charge President Trump, insisting Trump committed "serious crimes" related to classified documents and efforts to overturn the 2020 election—even as Republicans question whether the special counsel's appointment itself was lawful.

Smith testified he believes "the rule of law is being threatened like never before" and said he won't be "silenced" by Trump's prosecution threats (9News Denver). But GOP senators, including John Cornyn, pressed Smith on the constitutional basis for his appointment as special counsel, raising concerns about prosecutorial overreach and political weaponization of the Justice Department (Just The News).

**The conservative argument:** Republicans contend Smith's prosecution represents exactly the kind of selective, politically motivated justice that erodes public confidence in institutions. They question whether an unelected special counsel should wield such power against a former president without clear congressional authorization. Conservatives worry this sets a dangerous precedent for future administrations to weaponize the DOJ against political opponents.

Smith's defiant posture—insisting Trump engaged in serious crimes—suggests the legal battles over Trump's conduct will remain central to national politics heading into 2024 and beyond.
