# America's Energy Paralysis Is Strangling Growth—and Colorado Pays the Price

America needs energy abundance, not regulatory gridlock—and Colorado's economy depends on breaking through Washington's self-imposed infrastructure paralysis.

The nation faces a critical moment. As *Washington Examiner* columnists note, America is entering its biggest energy expansion since the post-war boom, driven largely by artificial intelligence and massive data center construction. Yet across the country, more than 2,000 gigawatts of needed capacity face delays from permitting mazes, environmental lawsuits, and regulatory red tape.

The problem is clear: "the power to block has systematically triumphed over the freedom to build," according to infrastructure reform advocates. Natural gas pipelines, transmission lines, and critical mineral mines languish in bureaucratic limbo while politicians from both parties claim to support domestic energy independence.

For Colorado—home to oil, gas, uranium, and critical minerals essential to the energy transition—this paralysis directly threatens jobs, tax revenue, and economic growth. Energy workers and rural communities bear the cost of Washington's regulatory overreach.

Conservatives argue that streamlining permitting timelines, limiting frivolous litigation, and trusting market forces—not government mandates—will unlock American energy abundance. The left counters that environmental protections justify delays, but even some Democrats now acknowledge that energy reform cannot wait.
