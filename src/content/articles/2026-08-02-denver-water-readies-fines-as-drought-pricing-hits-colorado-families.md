# Denver Water Readies Drought Penalties as Bureaucrats Ignore Real Problem

Denver Water is preparing to hit Colorado residents and businesses with fines and "drought pricing" schemes to punish water use during a historic drought—the latest example of government reaching for the regulatory hammer instead of addressing supply (Colorado Sun). The agency's approach reflects progressive water management ideology: penalize consumption rather than invest in new sources or improve infrastructure efficiency.

Republicans and water-use advocates argue that rationing through fines simply transfers wealth to government while doing nothing to increase Colorado's water supply. The state faces genuine scarcity driven by climate and competing demands; the answer isn't making water more expensive for families and farms, but developing new reservoirs, improving conveyance systems, and protecting senior water rights that Colorado agriculture depends on.

Meanwhile, Denver's city government is expanding cooling centers for homeless encampments while simultaneously sweeping street camps—a contradiction that wastes taxpayer dollars on feel-good programs that don't solve homelessness (9News). Advocates demand "pauses" on enforcement, but conservatives note that permanent shelters require sustainable funding and accountability, not endless emergency measures.
