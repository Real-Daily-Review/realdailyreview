# Colorado Democrats Race to Out-Litigate Each Other Against Energy Industry

Colorado's Democratic primary for attorney general has devolved into a bidding war over who will most aggressively weaponize the office against oil and gas producers—a troubling sign for the state's energy sector and the workers who depend on it.

According to Complete Colorado, the AG primary has become "a contest over who will be most aggressive in using the office to target energy producers." This reflects a broader Democratic strategy of using litigation and regulatory enforcement as a political cudgel rather than pursuing legitimate legal claims.

**The conservative concern is clear:** Colorado's energy industry—which generates jobs, tax revenue, and affordable power for millions—faces an attorney general's office potentially weaponized by partisan climate activists. Democrats are essentially promising to weaponize state law against a legal industry that powers Colorado's economy.

Meanwhile, Complete Colorado also reports that unaffiliated voters are rising in Colorado, and party-registered voters who skip caucuses are "simply limiting your options under the current rules." This underscores a structural problem: closed primaries increasingly push both parties toward their activist bases rather than the broader electorate.

**Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether the winning Democratic AG candidate follows through on anti-energy litigation promises—and whether Colorado Republicans can capitalize on voter frustration with a captured primary system.**
