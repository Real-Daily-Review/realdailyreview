# Seven House Republicans Break Ranks on Iran War Powers, Reject Trump's Military Authority

Seven House Republicans voted with Democrats this week to pass a war powers resolution that would restrict President Trump's military engagement in Iran beyond a 60-day window without explicit congressional approval—a rare bipartisan rebuke of executive war-making authority.

The resolution, backed by Democrats and the GOP defectors, asserts Congress's constitutional power over military decisions. Republicans who voted against the measure argue the resolution hamstrings the commander-in-chief's ability to respond swiftly to Iranian threats, particularly given Tehran's history of hostility toward U.S. interests and allies in the Middle East.

The split reflects a broader tension within the GOP between Trump-aligned hawks and Republicans skeptical of open-ended military commitments. Conservative critics of the resolution contend that constraining presidential flexibility invites adversaries to exploit the window before congressional action can be mobilized.

Meanwhile, National Review and other right-leaning outlets are urging Republicans to sharpen their 2026 messaging against Democratic proposals for Medicare for All, warning that such a system would require "crippling tax hikes" and disrupt coverage for hundreds of millions of Americans—a preview of the healthcare debate likely to dominate midterm campaigns.
