# AI Industry Leaders Call for Caution—But Who's Watching the Watchers?

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is sounding the alarm: artificial intelligence could pose catastrophic risks within six to 12 months, potentially capable of orchestrating internet-wide attacks (9News Denver). His call for the industry to pump the brakes has drawn swift backing from OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk (Breitbart), signaling rare consensus among tech titans on AI safety.

But here's the rub: Amodei and his peers control the very technology they're warning about. The New York Post's take cuts to the heart of the matter—if these companies genuinely believe their work is dangerous, they can slow down *now* without waiting for federal regulators to mandate it. Self-regulation from Silicon Valley has a spotty track record.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts growing tech corridors in Denver and Boulder where AI development and deployment are accelerating. If federal regulators respond to these warnings with heavy-handed AI oversight—licensing requirements, compliance mandates, innovation taxes—Colorado's tech workforce and startups could face costly new burdens. Conservatives rightly worry that government "safety" rules often become competitive moats protecting entrenched players while crushing smaller competitors.

The real question: Will these CEOs voluntarily slow their own profits, or is this performative hand-wringing designed to head off regulation?

Watch whether Congress moves to regulate AI development—and whether Colorado's tech community gets a seat at the table before Washington decides.
