# Trump's Tariff Gamble Threatens Colorado Energy, Agriculture

President Trump's escalating trade war—including a newly announced 50% tariff on Canadian imports—risks hitting Colorado's economy where it counts: energy, timber, and agricultural exports that depend on cross-border commerce.

The tariffs target lumber, agricultural products, and consumer electronics flowing from Canada, according to the Washington Examiner. While Trump's populist trade doctrine aims to protect American manufacturing, conservatives are split on the strategy's wisdom.

**Republicans argue** the tariffs leverage U.S. economic power to force better trade terms and rebuild domestic production. Trump's team contends that decades of unfair trade deals have hollowed out American industry.

**Conservatives counter** that broad tariffs function as hidden taxes on consumers and businesses. Colorado ranchers, loggers, and energy companies that export to Canada or rely on Canadian inputs face higher costs and potential retaliation. The National Review calls the approach "absurd," warning that trade wars rarely deliver promised gains—they typically trigger price hikes and supply-chain chaos.

Colorado's outdoor economy, agriculture sector, and timber industry have historically benefited from integrated North American trade. Retaliatory Canadian tariffs could squeeze Colorado exporters already navigating thin margins.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican congressional delegation—particularly those representing rural and energy-dependent districts—breaks with Trump on tariff scope or pushes for sector-specific exemptions.
