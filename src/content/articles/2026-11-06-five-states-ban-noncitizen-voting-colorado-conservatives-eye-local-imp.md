# Democrats Ignore Voter Mandate on Border, Citizenship as Red States Lock Down Voting Rights

Five states just delivered a blunt message: Americans want elections decided by citizens only. Kansas, Alaska, Arkansas, South Dakota, and West Virginia approved noncitizen voting bans by wide margins—a decisive rebuke to progressive open-borders ideology that Republicans say Democrats have pushed despite clear public opposition (Fox News).

The wins underscore a broader pattern. Republicans argue Democrats are "divorced from voters" on immigration, citizenship, and government overreach, even as some Democratic voters themselves are shifting rightward on these kitchen-table issues. A Supreme Court case looms that could reshape the legal landscape around noncitizen voting nationwide.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. The state has grappled with sanctuary policies and immigration enforcement debates that pit Denver's progressive leadership against rural counties and law-enforcement officials. Conservative Coloradans have long warned that lax citizenship verification invites fraud and dilutes the franchise—concerns now validated by voters across the heartland.

Democrats counter that noncitizen voting bans are solutions in search of a problem, but exit polling and ballot initiatives suggest ordinary Americans—including young and working-class voters—prioritize border security and electoral integrity over progressive immigration experiments.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats double down on sanctuary policies or recalibrate ahead of 2026, when voter sentiment on citizenship and immigration will likely dominate state races.
