# GOP Senator Tillis Blocks Anti-Lobbying Reform, Exposing Swamp Priorities

Outgoing North Carolina Republican Thom Tillis just blocked Sen. Rick Scott's bill to ban former lawmakers from lobbying Congress—a move that crystallizes why Washington reform efforts routinely fail.

Tillis objected to Scott's proposal, arguing he should retain the right to "make a call" to Capitol Hill once he leaves office (Breitbart). The bill would have closed a revolving-door loophole that lets ex-members cash in on their connections and insider access almost immediately after departing Congress.

**The conservative case for reform:** Republicans like Scott argue that former lawmakers-turned-lobbyists create perverse incentives. They vote with an eye toward lucrative post-Congress gigs, and their insider relationships give special interests unfair leverage over current members. It's the definition of swamp behavior—using public service as a stepping stone to private enrichment.

**Tillis's counter:** The outgoing senator claims the ban infringes on his personal liberty to earn a living and maintain relationships. He's not wrong on principle, but his objection rings hollow when the "relationship" in question is access purchased by corporate clients.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners competing fairly without K Street connections, this is a reminder that self-dealing in Congress remains bipartisan and entrenched. Watch whether Republicans who campaigned on draining the swamp will actually support Scott's next attempt—or whether Tillis's objection becomes the template for killing reform.
