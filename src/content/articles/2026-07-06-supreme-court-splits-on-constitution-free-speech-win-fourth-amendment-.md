# Supreme Court Delivers Mixed Rulings on Constitutional Rights, Disappoints Conservatives on Citizenship

The Supreme Court's recent decisions reveal a fractured bench struggling to interpret the Constitution's original meaning—with major implications for Colorado gun owners, free speech advocates, and immigration enforcement.

In a win for First Amendment protections, the Court sided with free speech advocates against progressive justices who sought broader government censorship powers (Washington Examiner). Conservatives hailed the decision as a rebuke to the left's expanding view of permissible speech restrictions.

But the Court stumbled on Fourth Amendment privacy protections, with National Review warning the justices adopted an unreasonably vague standard for what constitutes a "reasonable expectation of privacy"—potentially opening the door to government overreach that Colorado's gun owners and property owners should watch closely.

Most troubling to conservatives: the Court upheld the 14th Amendment's guarantee of citizenship to children born in the U.S. to illegal immigrants, rejecting originalist arguments that the amendment's authors never intended such broad birthright citizenship. The Washington Examiner's legal team argues the ruling contradicts the framers' intent and will perpetuate what critics call the "anchor baby" loophole—a particular concern for Colorado border communities and taxpayers funding services for illegal immigrants' U.S.-born children.
