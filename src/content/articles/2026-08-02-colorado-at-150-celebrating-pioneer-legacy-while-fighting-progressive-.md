# Colorado Marks 150 Years: A State Built on Pioneer Grit, Now at a Crossroads

Colorado's 150th birthday arrives amid celebrations at the State Capitol, but the Centennial State's founding principles of self-reliance and limited government face mounting pressure from progressive overreach.

The Colorado Sun and CPR News both covered Friday's sesquicentennial festivities, highlighting the state's unique character and storied past. From the gold rush to ranching, from water rights battles to energy development, Colorado was built by individuals willing to take risks and solve problems without waiting for government permission.

Yet today's Colorado increasingly diverges from that legacy. Democrats control the statehouse and have spent the past decade expanding regulations on energy production, water use, and land management—policies that burden the ranchers, oil and gas workers, and small-business owners who embody Colorado's pioneer spirit. Meanwhile, state spending has ballooned, and progressive social experiments have reshaped Denver and Boulder.

Conservatives argue Colorado's future depends on reclaiming the limited-government, pro-growth principles that made the state prosperous in the first place: protecting property rights, streamlining regulations, and trusting Coloradans—not bureaucrats—to chart their own course.

Watch for whether Colorado's Republican minority can articulate a compelling vision for the next 150 years rooted in those founding ideals.
