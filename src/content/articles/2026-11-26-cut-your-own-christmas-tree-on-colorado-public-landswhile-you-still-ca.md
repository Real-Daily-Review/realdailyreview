# Colorado Christmas Tree Permits: Cut Your Own on Public Lands

Colorado taxpayers can still exercise their right to harvest their own Christmas trees from national forests and Bureau of Land Management land across the state—a straightforward use of public resources that beats paying retail prices. The Colorado Sun reports permits are available for those willing to do the work themselves, offering a practical alternative to commercial tree lots.

This is a rare win for property-rights advocates: public lands actually serving the public rather than sitting locked away by federal bureaucrats. Coloradans can access these permits without jumping through excessive regulatory hoops, making it one of the few remaining ways ordinary families can use federal land for legitimate personal use.

Conservatives should note this model works because it's simple and decentralized—no complex permitting schemes, just straightforward access. It's the kind of common-sense land management that should apply to hunting, fishing, and resource development across Colorado's public lands.

The real question: why can't the same permitting ease apply to timber harvesting, mineral development, or grazing on these same forests? Watch whether environmental groups push to restrict tree-cutting permits in the name of "forest health"—a familiar pretext for locking up Colorado's public lands.
