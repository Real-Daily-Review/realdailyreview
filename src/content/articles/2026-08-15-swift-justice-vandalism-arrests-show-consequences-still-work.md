# Law and Order: Two Vandalism Cases Show Need for Swift Justice

Colorado and the nation are seeing a welcome uptick in arrests for property destruction—a reminder that consequences still matter when criminals target public spaces and private businesses.

In Washington, D.C., Melissa L. Farris was arrested and charged with two felonies for vandalizing the World War II Memorial, according to Fox News. Meanwhile, Denver police made headway on the East Colfax corridor, where a suspect was arrested for a string of vandalism incidents that caused thousands in damage to local businesses (KDVR Fox31 Denver). Tipsters played a crucial role in identifying the perpetrator.

These cases underscore a conservative principle: swift arrest and prosecution deter crime. When suspects face real consequences—felony charges, not catch-and-release—businesses and public monuments are better protected. Colorado's East Colfax corridor, a commercial hub struggling with crime and disorder, needs exactly this kind of enforcement.

The contrast is stark. Cities that embrace soft-on-crime policies—bail reform, reduced prosecutions, lenient sentencing—see vandalism and property crime spiral. Denver's success here came through old-fashioned detective work and community tips, not progressive social programs.

**Watch for:** whether Denver prosecutors pursue maximum penalties and whether similar enforcement continues across the city's crime hotspots.
