# California's Billionaire Tax Heads to Ballot—A Warning for Colorado Wealth Confiscation

California Democrats are pushing a one-time wealth tax targeting billionaires onto November's ballot, and Colorado conservatives should take note of what happens when government treats asset seizure as fiscal policy.

The measure would impose a five percent tax on billionaire assets, according to Breitbart. The Washington Examiner frames it as "retroactive wealth confiscation," warning that Democrats are using ballot propositions to drive the state toward "further decay."

Republicans argue wealth taxes are economically destructive—they punish success, incentivize capital flight, and create dangerous precedent for government asset seizure. Conservatives counter that California's real problem isn't billionaire wealth; it's runaway spending, regulatory overreach, and a hostile business climate that's already pushing companies and earners out of state.

Colorado hasn't proposed a billionaire tax, but progressive activists have repeatedly floated wealth and income tax hikes to fund government expansion. If California voters approve this measure, expect Colorado Democrats to cite it as justification for similar schemes targeting high earners and business owners.

The outcome in California will signal whether voters accept the premise that government has a claim on private assets—a principle that could reshape tax policy across the West.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature introduces copycat wealth-tax proposals in the 2025 session.
