# BTS Snubs Grammys Over 'Asian' Category—A Win for Merit Over Identity Politics

Korean supergroup BTS just delivered a sharp rebuke to the Recording Academy's latest identity-politics gambit: they're withdrawing from the 2027 Grammys entirely rather than submit to a newly created "Best Asian Pop Music Performance" category.

BTS's position is clear and principled. The group stated they want music judged without being divided by region or language—a direct rejection of the Academy's categorical segregation (9News Denver, New York Post). Critics on the right, including Breitbart's John Nolte, argue the new category amounts to a "ghetto for Asian artists," a well-intentioned but ultimately patronizing move that fragments the playing field rather than elevates talent.

The Recording Academy introduced the category weeks before BTS's announcement, apparently aiming to recognize Asian pop's global dominance. Instead, the industry's leading act in that space saw through the optics: separate categories based on ethnicity or geography undermine the very meritocracy award shows claim to champion.

This isn't a Colorado-specific story, but it mirrors a broader conservative argument playing out nationwide—that identity-based categorization, even when framed as "inclusion," often backfires by reinforcing the divisions it claims to remedy. BTS's refusal to participate is a market correction against bureaucratic overreach in the entertainment world.

Watch whether other major Asian artists follow BTS's lead or accept the category, signaling whether merit-based judging still matters in Hollywood.
