# Trump's Press Ban Draws Fire From Right and Left—But for Different Reasons

Donald Trump's restrictions on press access have triggered rare bipartisan criticism, though conservatives and liberals disagree sharply on the remedy.

*Colorado Sun* columnist Mike Littwin argues Trump's "war on the press" mirrors his foreign policy failures, framing it as a strategic disaster. Meanwhile, *National Review*—a leading conservative publication—breaks with Trump, arguing he should rescind the ban outright. The *National Review* piece contends that restricting press access violates "the spirit of a free press" and that Trump would be "better served by confronting reporters directly" rather than limiting their access.

Republicans and conservatives are split. Some Trump allies defend limiting hostile media coverage, citing what they call unfair treatment. But establishment conservatives worry the ban weakens the First Amendment principle that underpins limited government—a core conservative value. They argue Trump's case is stronger when made openly, not through censorship.

The broader conservative concern: if a Republican president can ban press access, what precedent does that set for Democratic successors? Limited government means limited *executive* power, too.

Colorado taxpayers and voters should watch whether this becomes a defining Trump-era conflict or a temporary dust-up—and whether Republican leaders in Congress push back on executive overreach.
