# Cruise Ship With Hantavirus Outbreak Heads to Canary Islands After Evacuations

Three people with confirmed or suspected hantavirus have been evacuated from the MV Hondius, a luxury cruise ship at the center of the first recorded hantavirus outbreak at sea, according to the U.N. health agency. Two patients have confirmed cases; one is suspected of infection. Among those evacuated was Martin Anstee, 56, a British expedition guide.

The ship departed Cape Verde on Wednesday with nearly 150 people still aboard, isolated in their cabins, and is now headed to Spain's Canary Islands, where authorities have approved docking. The vessel originally departed southern Argentina in March carrying 149 people from 23 countries.

According to The Guardian, the World Health Organization has identified seven confirmed or suspected cases aboard the ship, with three passengers having died. CBS News reports more than 140 people remain on board, including 17 Americans, as investigators race to control the outbreak.

Hantavirus is a rare but potentially deadly disease. The specific strain involved in this outbreak has not been detailed in available reports, though The Guardian notes it originated in South America.
