# Thanksgiving Storms Expose Infrastructure Fragility as East Coast Reels

Severe weather hammering the East Coast this Thanksgiving has laid bare the vulnerability of aging infrastructure—a cautionary tale for Colorado as the state grapples with its own water and power grid challenges.

Nearly 2 million residents in Maryland's Prince George's and Montgomery counties faced mandatory water conservation after power disruptions crippled two filtration plants, forcing authorities to issue an "Essential Water Use Only" directive (Washington Examiner). Meanwhile, Central Park recorded its wettest Thanksgiving since 1870, and storms across the nation knocked out power and disrupted holiday travel (New York Post, Breitbart).

The Maryland crisis underscores a conservative concern: government-run utilities often lack redundancy and resilience. When centralized systems fail—whether due to weather, poor maintenance, or deferred investment—millions suffer immediately. Colorado's water infrastructure, already strained by competing demands between agriculture, municipalities, and environmental mandates, faces similar risks.

Republicans argue that infrastructure modernization requires private-sector efficiency and market discipline, not endless government spending without accountability. The left typically responds with calls for massive federal "investment"—code for taxpayer-funded bailouts of mismanaged systems.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses should watch whether state and local officials use this East Coast wake-up call to audit our own grid resilience and water redundancy, or whether they'll simply demand more federal money with no performance guarantees.
