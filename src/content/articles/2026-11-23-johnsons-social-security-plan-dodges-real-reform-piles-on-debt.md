# Senate Republicans' Social Security Plan Kicks the Can Down the Road—Again

Sen. Ron Johnson's new "Stop Scaring Seniors Act" would cover Social Security shortfalls by raiding the Treasury's general fund and letting Washington borrow past the debt limit—a move that conservatives say abandons fiscal responsibility for political expediency (Washington Examiner).

The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget immediately flagged the danger: transferring general revenue to Social Security and suspending debt-limit rules amounts to mortgaging future generations' prosperity to avoid hard choices today. Republicans are divided on how to fix Social Security's 2032 insolvency crisis. Johnson's approach sidesteps the real problem—unsustainable benefit promises and payroll-tax shortfalls—by simply printing money and borrowing more.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado taxpayers and workers already shoulder federal debt burdens through inflation and reduced economic growth. Kicking Social Security's structural crisis down the road means younger Coloradans face either massive tax hikes or benefit cuts later.

Conservatives argue the only honest fix requires either raising the payroll-tax cap, gradually adjusting benefits for higher earners, or some combination—not Treasury raids and unlimited borrowing. Democrats, meanwhile, typically oppose any benefit reform and favor tax increases on high earners.

Watch for whether Republicans coalesce around a genuine reform plan or continue offering band-aids that preserve the status quo at taxpayers' expense.
