# Trump's Iran Deal Splits Conservative Base as Voters Show Cautious Support

President Trump's preliminary agreement with Iran is drawing mixed reactions from the right, even as polls show Americans narrowly favor negotiated settlement over regime change.

A Reagan Institute Summer Survey found 39% of Americans support a negotiated settlement with Iran, while 36% back regime change—a statistical dead heat (Fox News Politics). Meanwhile, a separate Quantus poll cited by Breitbart found "strong majority approval" for Trump's Iran deal among voters, though specific numbers weren't disclosed.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei endorsed the memorandum of understanding Thursday, saying he authorized it despite reservations after receiving assurances the deal protects Tehran's interests (Breitbart).

**The conservative divide is real.** Some Trump allies view this as pragmatic dealmaking; others worry the agreement doesn't go far enough to constrain Iranian nuclear ambitions or regional aggression. The Daily Wire's analysis suggests negotiations are still in early stages, not concluded.

For Colorado, this matters: energy policy, sanctions regimes, and Middle East stability affect oil markets and state energy development. Any agreement that stabilizes global energy markets could benefit Colorado's oil and gas sector—but only if terms genuinely limit Iranian nuclear capability.

Watch whether Congress demands full transparency on deal terms and whether Iran actually complies with verification measures.
