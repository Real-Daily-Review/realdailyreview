# Colorado Ski Season Looms, but Winter Tourism Takes Hit in Mountain Towns

Colorado's ski resorts are gearing up for the 2026-2027 season with most hoping to open as early as possible, but the state's winter tourism economy is taking unexpected blows. Ice Castles, the popular seasonal attraction that drew visitors to Cripple Creek, will not return for the 2026-2027 winter, according to a city announcement (KDVR). The departure marks another loss for small mountain communities already struggling with seasonal revenue volatility and rising operational costs.

While major ski resorts prepare to capitalize on winter demand, the loss of complementary attractions like Ice Castles highlights a broader challenge: Colorado's tourism-dependent towns face mounting pressure from labor shortages, regulatory compliance, and the rising cost of seasonal operations. Cripple Creek officials have not detailed the reasons behind Ice Castles' departure, but similar attractions nationwide have cited staffing difficulties and thin profit margins.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the question is whether state and local governments will respond with tax relief and regulatory streamlining to help tourism operators survive, or whether they'll pile on more mandates that drive attractions elsewhere. Watch whether Cripple Creek and other mountain towns pursue business-friendly policies to attract replacement attractions—or default to the progressive playbook of subsidies and red tape.
