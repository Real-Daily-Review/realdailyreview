# Trump Administration Extends China Trade Deal—But Colorado Ag Still Waiting for Real Wins

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed Wednesday that the U.S. extended its trade agreement with China through January 10, rolling back restrictions on critical minerals and high-tech exports that threatened global supply chains (Just The News). While Bessent told Fox News the administration achieved an "economic détente" with Beijing, he acknowledged a significant gap: China has followed through on soybean purchases but remains reluctant on broader agricultural deals (Breitbart).

For Colorado farmers and ranchers—who depend heavily on export markets—the partial victory rings hollow. Soybeans matter, but Colorado's wheat, beef, and specialty crops need the same access Beijing promised. The extended agreement buys time for negotiations, but Bessent's own words suggest China is cherry-picking compliance rather than honoring a comprehensive trade reset.

Republicans argue the extension prevents supply-chain chaos while keeping pressure on Beijing. Democrats, predictably, will claim any deal that doesn't immediately restore pre-tariff conditions amounts to capitulation.

The real test comes in January: either the Trump administration secures genuine reciprocal access for all American agricultural exports, or Colorado producers face another year of uncertainty and reduced margins.
