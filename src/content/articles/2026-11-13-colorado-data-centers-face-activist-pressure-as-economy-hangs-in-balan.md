# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Plot Restrictions

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are quietly powering the modern internet, yet progressive activists and local officials are increasingly hostile to the infrastructure that makes digital life possible. According to *Complete Colorado*, these facilities are "not the enemy of modern life" but rather "what makes it run"—yet they face mounting regulatory pressure and environmental opposition from the left.

Data centers require significant electricity and water, making them targets for climate activists who want to restrict their expansion. Democrats and green groups argue the facilities consume too many resources; conservatives counter that blocking data center development means ceding critical infrastructure to other states and nations, undermining Colorado's competitive advantage and job creation.

The stakes are real for Colorado's economy. Data centers generate tax revenue, create high-skilled jobs, and attract tech investment. Restricting them through burdensome regulations—whether on energy use, water consumption, or emissions—would push this essential industry elsewhere, costing the state billions in economic opportunity.

Meanwhile, youth documentaries and cultural programming continue to receive media coverage, but Colorado's policymakers should prioritize protecting the unglamorous infrastructure that actually powers the economy.
