# Colorado Springs Pursues Space Command Reconciliation While State Grapples With Election Law Enforcement

Colorado Springs leaders are heading to Huntsville, Alabama for a four-day summit aimed at repairing ties with the city that won the bitter U.S. Space Command competition (CPR News). The chamber-sponsored trip signals an attempt to move past years of acrimonious fighting over the lucrative federal installation—a major economic prize that ultimately went to Alabama.

Meanwhile, Colorado's 2025 voting law is facing its first enforcement challenge. Colorado Springs is being sued over holding municipal elections in April rather than conforming to new state voting requirements (Complete Colorado). The lawsuit marks the initial test of the statute's teeth—a sign that election law disputes will likely intensify as local jurisdictions navigate state mandates.

On a broader front, unaffiliated voter registration continues climbing in Colorado, reshaping primary dynamics. Voters registered with a party who skip caucuses are effectively ceding influence to party activists, concentrating power among the most ideologically committed (Complete Colorado). This structural advantage for primary voters raises questions about whether Colorado's nomination process adequately represents the broader electorate.
