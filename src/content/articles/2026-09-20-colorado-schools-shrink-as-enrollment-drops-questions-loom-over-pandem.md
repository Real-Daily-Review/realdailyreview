# Colorado School Districts Face Enrollment Collapse—and Tough Questions About How They Spent Federal Cash

Colorado's Front Range school districts are confronting a hard reality: too many buildings, too few students, and mounting pressure to make cuts that will anger parents no matter what.

Poudre School District exemplifies the problem. Shrinking enrollment has left the district with excess capacity, forcing administrators to weigh school closures and consolidations (CPR News). The situation reflects broader demographic and migration patterns hitting Colorado's urban corridors—a direct consequence of the state's rising cost of living and families relocating elsewhere.

But there's a secondary scandal brewing: how districts spent federal pandemic relief funds. While schools nationwide received billions in COVID recovery money ostensibly earmarked to address learning loss, some blue-state districts took detours. Albuquerque Public Schools, for example, spent hundreds of thousands in pandemic relief funds on anti-racism training rather than tutoring, remediation, or classroom instruction (Fox News Politics).

Conservatives argue this misallocation exemplifies government waste and bureaucratic priorities divorced from classroom results. When federal dollars flow to districts, they should go directly to student achievement—not administrative pet projects.
