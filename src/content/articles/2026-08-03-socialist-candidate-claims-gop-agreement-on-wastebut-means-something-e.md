# Socialist Candidate Claims Common Ground With GOP—But the Devil's in the Details

A socialist House candidate is claiming agreement with Republicans on government waste, but his actual target tells the real story. While he says he opposes "waste, fraud and abuse"—language that sounds conservative—he's really focused on eliminating corporate subsidies and taxing the wealthy, according to Fox News Politics.

This is a classic bait-and-switch. Republicans have long argued that government waste means bloated bureaucracies, failed programs, and spending that doesn't reach intended beneficiaries. A socialist's definition typically means corporate tax breaks and defense spending—not the regulatory bloat and entitlement inefficiency conservatives target.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this matters. The state's energy sector, agriculture, and small manufacturers already face pressure from progressive taxation and regulation. A national shift toward socialist economics would accelerate that squeeze, regardless of rhetorical agreement on "waste."

The Washington Examiner warns that Republicans face real midterm headwinds, with their narrow House majority vulnerable to even modest seat losses. If Democrats gain ground, expect aggressive pushback against Colorado's oil and gas industry, water rights development, and Second Amendment protections—areas where the left sees corporate interests as the real "waste."

Watch whether Colorado Democrats embrace this socialist framing on corporate subsidies as a 2024 campaign talking point against the state's energy economy.
