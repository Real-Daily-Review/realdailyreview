# Colorado I-70 Winter Pileups Expose Infrastructure Vulnerability as Safety Concerns Mount

Colorado's I-70 corridor proved once again this weekend why it remains one of the state's most dangerous stretches of highway—a reality that demands honest answers about road management and driver responsibility.

Multiple multi-car pileups struck I-70 on Friday and Saturday, including a 12-car collision amid snowy conditions that forced westbound closures near Floyd Hill (9News Denver). Videos captured the intense, chaotic conditions that prompted renewed safety warnings from authorities.

The recurring nature of these disasters raises a conservative concern: Colorado's transportation infrastructure investments haven't solved the core problem. Rather than reflexively calling for more government spending on road projects, policymakers should focus on what actually works—enforcing existing speed and safety regulations, maintaining clear communication about road conditions, and holding drivers accountable for reckless behavior in adverse weather.

Colorado taxpayers already fund CDOT extensively. The question isn't whether we're spending enough; it's whether we're spending wisely and whether personal responsibility is being emphasized alongside infrastructure.

Separately, a retired FBI agent warned that a breach of the National Crime Information Center (NCIC) database could endanger police officers during traffic stops if criminal records were altered (Fox News)—a cybersecurity vulnerability that compounds safety risks on Colorado's highways.

**Watch for:** Whether CDOT releases data on whether speed enforcement and driver behavior changes—not just road conditions—correlate with accident reduction on I-70.
