# Colorado Springs, Aurora Grapple With Election Timing as State Voting Law Creates Friction

Colorado Springs faces a lawsuit over defying state law requiring municipal elections in April, while Aurora voters will decide whether to shift city elections to even-numbered years—a sign that Colorado's 2025 voting law is forcing uncomfortable changes on local governments resistant to state mandates.

The Colorado Springs case (Complete Colorado) marks the first enforcement action since the voting law took effect, signaling that state officials won't tolerate municipal foot-dragging. Meanwhile, Aurora's Ballot Question 3D (Denver Post) asks voters to realign city elections with federal cycles, a move that could reshape local political dynamics and voter participation patterns.

**The conservative case:** Standardizing election timing reduces administrative costs and voter confusion. Consolidating municipal and federal elections on even-numbered years boosts turnout efficiency and cuts redundant election expenses—a win for taxpayers tired of funding multiple election cycles annually.

**Democrats and progressive cities counter** that odd-year municipal elections insulate local races from national partisan waves, allowing voters to focus on local issues. They argue state mandates override local control.

Colorado Springs' resistance to the April requirement and Aurora's ballot measure reveal deeper tensions: cities want autonomy over election calendars; the state wants uniformity. Conservatives should watch whether Colorado Springs capitulates or doubles down—and whether Aurora voters embrace efficiency or local independence.
