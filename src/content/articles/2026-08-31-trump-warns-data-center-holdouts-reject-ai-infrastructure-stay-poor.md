# Trump: Communities Blocking Data Centers Risk Economic Stagnation

President Trump is calling out local resistance to AI data centers as economic self-sabotage—and he has a point worth Colorado's attention.

Trump warned Monday that communities rejecting data center construction risk becoming "backwards and poor" while ceding technological and economic advantage to China (Fox News, Daily Wire). The blunt message reflects a real tension: data centers are essential infrastructure for AI development and economic competitiveness, yet they demand massive electricity and water resources that strain local grids and aquifers.

Colorado faces this exact calculus. The state's abundant hydropower and cooling-friendly climate make it attractive for data center development, but water scarcity in the West and grid reliability concerns fuel legitimate pushback from municipalities and rural counties. Republicans argue that blocking such facilities amounts to regulatory self-harm—sacrificing jobs, tax revenue, and technological leadership to appease environmental activists.

Democrats and local officials counter that unchecked data center sprawl threatens water supplies and energy costs for residents and farmers already squeezed by drought and inflation.

The real issue: Colorado needs a clear-eyed cost-benefit analysis, not ideological opposition masquerading as caution. Communities that thoughtfully welcome data centers with reasonable environmental safeguards will prosper; those that reflexively block them will watch opportunity migrate to friendlier states.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature and local governments pursue balanced permitting frameworks or continue blocking critical infrastructure.
