# Colorado Marijuana Regulators Warn of Contaminated Vape Devices; Health Insurance Premiums Set to Spike Again

Colorado's marijuana regulatory apparatus is scrambling to contain a contamination crisis after state health officials issued warnings over tainted vape devices—a stark reminder that legalization's promised consumer protections remain inconsistent.

The Colorado Department of Revenue and Department of Public Health and Environment flagged multiple marijuana vape products, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver. The advisory underscores a persistent problem: despite years of licensing and testing requirements, contaminated products still reach shelves, exposing consumers to unknown risks.

Meanwhile, Colorado health insurance premiums are heading sharply higher in 2027, CPR News reports. While state and federal subsidies will cushion the blow for lower-income enrollees, middle-class Coloradans and small businesses face another round of sticker shock—a direct consequence of Colorado's regulatory and tax environment driving up healthcare costs.

Republicans and conservatives have long warned that heavy-handed regulation and government intervention inflate costs without delivering promised safety or affordability. The marijuana contamination episode and the insurance premium spike illustrate the gap between progressive promises and market reality.

Taxpayers should watch whether Colorado's Department of Revenue tightens testing standards—or whether it continues rubber-stamping products while collecting licensing fees.
