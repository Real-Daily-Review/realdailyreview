# NSA's Classified Network Down; Latest in String of Critical U.S. Tech Failures

The National Security Agency's classified communications network went dark this week, leaving tens of thousands of personnel unable to access NSA Net—the system used to transmit and receive classified intelligence—forcing the agency to stagger employee arrivals to prevent system overload (The Daily Wire, Daily Caller).

The outage is the latest in a cascade of critical infrastructure failures hitting American institutions. Charles Schwab, which manages roughly $13 trillion in client assets, suffered a widespread outage Tuesday after customers encountered internal server errors, with the disruption traced to network-services provider Akamai (The Daily Wire). Akamai itself reported similar errors Monday.

The string of technological meltdowns raises urgent questions about the resilience and redundancy of systems protecting both national security and the financial system—two pillars of American strength that cannot afford to fail. The NSA's inability to maintain continuous classified communications is particularly alarming given the agency's role in counterintelligence and counterterrorism operations.

While the sources do not detail root causes, conservatives and taxpayers should demand answers: Why are government and private-sector infrastructure so fragile? Were adequate resources allocated to cybersecurity and system redundancy? Colorado's defense and tech sectors depend on stable, secure networks—this matters locally.

Watch for congressional oversight hearings and whether the Biden-Harris administration takes responsibility for critical infrastructure vulnerabilities.
