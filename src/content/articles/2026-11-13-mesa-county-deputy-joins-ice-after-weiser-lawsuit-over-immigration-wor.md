# Colorado Deputy's ICE Move Highlights Immigration Enforcement Divide

A former Mesa County sheriff's deputy who faced disciplinary action for working with federal immigration authorities has landed a job at ICE—a move that underscores the sharp divide between Colorado's progressive attorney general and those who support immigration enforcement.

Alexander Zwinck resigned last year after facing discipline and a lawsuit from Attorney General Phil Weiser over his collaboration with Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Rather than back down, Zwinck has now joined ICE directly, according to the Colorado Sun.

Republicans and immigration hawks argue that Weiser's aggressive legal posture against local law enforcement cooperation with federal immigration agents hampers legitimate border security work and undermines public safety. They contend that sheriffs should have the discretion to assist federal authorities in enforcing immigration law without fear of state-level retaliation.

Democrats and progressive advocates counter that such cooperation can entangle local police in federal immigration enforcement, eroding community trust and diverting resources from local public safety priorities.

The case reflects Colorado's ongoing tension between a Democratic state government pushing back against federal immigration enforcement and conservative law enforcement officials who view cooperation with ICE as a lawful duty.

Watch whether Weiser's office pursues further legal action against other Colorado law enforcement agencies assisting federal immigration authorities.
