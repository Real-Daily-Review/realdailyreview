# U.S. Job Market Stumbles as Geopolitical Strain Hits Payrolls

The American economy unexpectedly shed 23,000 jobs in July—a sharp reversal that should alarm Colorado workers and business owners already navigating higher energy costs and regulatory headwinds.

The Labor Department's Friday report marked a stunning miss. Forecasters had predicted gains between 83,000 and 88,000 jobs, adjusted for seasonal variation (Washington Examiner, Just The News). Instead, payrolls contracted, with the unemployment rate holding steady around 4.1 percent (9News Denver).

The culprit: an ongoing energy supply shock stemming from the Iran conflict, which has strained businesses nationwide and disrupted supply chains. For Colorado—a state dependent on energy production, agriculture, and small business—geopolitical disruption to fuel markets translates directly to higher operating costs, delayed hiring, and reduced consumer spending.

Republicans and conservatives argue this underscores the folly of energy policies that weaken domestic production capacity. When America relies on volatile global markets instead of maximizing Colorado's oil, gas, and coal resources, workers and families pay the price. Democrats, by contrast, have pushed renewable mandates and restrictions on fossil fuel development, which critics say leave the economy vulnerable to exactly these kinds of shocks.

The unemployment rate's stability masks underlying weakness. Watch whether Colorado's energy sector—already pressured by state regulations—begins laying off workers as businesses tighten belts in response to sustained geopolitical uncertainty and higher input costs.
