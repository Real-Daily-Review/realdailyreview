# Trump, Cotton Clash Over Daylight Saving Time While Real Issues Loom

President Trump is publicly pressuring Senator Tom Cotton (R-AR) over the "Sunshine Protection Act," which would make daylight saving time permanent—a move that has exposed a rare rift between the administration and a key Republican ally. According to Fox News, Trump shared Cotton's phone number to lean on the senator, but Cotton warns that permanent daylight saving time would mean dark winter mornings for constituents (Fox News Politics).

The dispute underscores a broader debate conservatives should care about: government mandates on how Americans live their lives. While the Sunshine Protection Act sounds minor, it represents federal overreach into a decision better left to states and individuals. Colorado, like other states, has grappled with daylight saving time policy—and Coloradans have shown they'd rather keep the status quo than endure perpetual clock-switching.

Cotton's resistance reflects legitimate concerns about winter darkness affecting school commutes and public safety. Republicans should ask: Why is Washington dictating time zones when states can decide for themselves?

Democratic Senate nominee Hallie Shoffner (Arkansas) predictably seized on the infighting, claiming Trump and Cotton should focus on "real issues" facing voters (Washington Examiner)—a tired talking point that ignores how government overreach on small matters compounds into larger liberty erosions.
