# Colorado Primary Delivers Leftward Lurch, Griswold Advances Despite Reform Calls

Colorado's primary elections have handed Democrats another victory and exposed structural flaws in the state's nomination process that conservatives say demand urgent reform.

Secretary of State Jena Griswold, a Democrat, won her party's attorney general nomination and will face Republican El Paso County District Attorney Michael J. Allen in November (Denver Post). But Griswold's advance came amid criticism of Colorado's primary system: in a crowded field, candidates can win with only a small plurality of votes, even when another candidate is more broadly preferred—a dynamic Complete Colorado argues makes the case for election reform.

On the Republican side, first-time candidate Victor Marx holds a narrow lead over state Sen. Barb Kirkmeyer in the gubernatorial primary, with roughly 2,000 votes separating them and the race still too close to call as voters fix deficient ballots (Denver Post). Only two Republican candidates advanced from the GOP assembly out of 18 total gubernatorial hopefuls.

Complete Colorado's analysis warns that Colorado's primary results reflect a broader leftward shift in the state, with money proving less decisive than expected—underfunded candidates often outperforming well-funded rivals.
