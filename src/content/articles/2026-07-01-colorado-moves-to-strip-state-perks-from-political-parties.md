# Colorado Rightly Rejects Government Favoritism for Political Parties

Colorado officials are moving to strip special state recognition and privileges from political parties—a rare moment of fiscal and constitutional common sense in a state increasingly hostile to limited government.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state has long granted various official designations and perks to Democratic and Republican party organizations, effectively using taxpayer resources to subsidize partisan machinery. The proposal to eliminate these arrangements represents what supporters call a "courageous" separation of state from party.

Conservatives argue this is overdue. Government should remain neutral on partisan matters; taxpayer dollars shouldn't fund the organizational infrastructure of either major party. When the state grants special status or resources to political organizations, it blurs the line between public administration and partisan politics—exactly the kind of entanglement limited-government advocates oppose.

The move also reflects a broader principle: Colorado's government exists to serve all citizens equally, not to pick winners among competing political factions. Removing party privileges levels the playing field and prevents the state from becoming a tool of whichever party controls the statehouse.

Democrats haven't formally objected, though the party apparatus has historically benefited from these arrangements.
