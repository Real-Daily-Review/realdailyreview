# McConnell's Absence Stalls Farm Bill; Republicans Lose Key Vote

Senate Republicans are learning a hard lesson about thin majorities: one absent leader can derail crucial legislation. Sen. Mitch McConnell, discharged from rehab following a June fall in Kentucky, missed a critical Senate Agriculture Committee vote on the farm bill, and the measure failed on a party-line 10-11 split (Fox News Politics). McConnell and Sen. Tommy Tuberville (R-Ala.) attempted proxy votes, but those didn't count toward the final tally (Just The News).

The farm bill matters to Colorado—agriculture underpins rural economies across the state, and federal farm policy shapes water rights, crop insurance, and conservation funding that ranchers depend on. Republicans needed McConnell's vote to advance the legislation, but his recovery took priority.

**The conservative concern:** With Republicans holding a razor-thin Senate majority, even temporary absences expose vulnerability. Democrats are unified; Republicans cannot afford defections or gaps in attendance. McConnell's team should have coordinated better with leadership to ensure proxy votes counted, or delayed the committee action until he returned.

The farm bill remains stalled. Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether Republicans can muster the discipline and coordination to pass this legislation before the window closes—or whether Democratic obstruction will force another extension of outdated farm policy.
