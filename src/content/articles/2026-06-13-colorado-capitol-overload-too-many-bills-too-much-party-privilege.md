# Colorado Capitol Drowning in Bill Overload While Taxpayers Foot the Party Line

Colorado legislators are introducing so many bills that meaningful debate is becoming impossible—and taxpayers are still forced to bankroll primary elections for parties they don't even support.

Complete Colorado reports that legislative session overload has become a serious problem at the Capitol, with lawmakers proposing far too many measures for proper scrutiny. The outlet argues there's a strong case for restricting the number of bills legislators can introduce annually, a common-sense reform that would force prioritization and reduce government bloat.

Meanwhile, Colorado taxpayers face another indignity: state government compels them to finance primary elections for political parties to which many don't belong. Complete Colorado questions why this "political party privilege" persists, forcing citizens to subsidize partisan machinery against their will—a classic example of government overreach disguised as routine procedure.

Election integrity concerns loom as well. Complete Colorado argues that independent audits of Colorado elections would strengthen public confidence rather than dismiss voter skepticism. The outlet contends that transparency and trustworthy systems—not dismissing concerns—protect democracy.

These three issues converge on a central conservative principle: Colorado government is doing too much, spending too freely, and insulating itself from accountability. Legislators should cap bill introductions, end taxpayer funding of party primaries, and embrace independent election audits.

Watch whether the 2027 legislative session actually implements meaningful reforms or simply talks about them while the Capitol machine grinds on.
