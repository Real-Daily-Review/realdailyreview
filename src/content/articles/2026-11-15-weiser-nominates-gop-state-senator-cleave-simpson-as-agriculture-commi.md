# Colorado's New Governor Taps GOP State Senator for Agriculture Post—a Rare Bipartisan Nod

Phil Weiser, Colorado's incoming Democratic governor, has nominated Cleave Simpson, the top Republican in the state Senate, as the state's next agriculture commissioner, signaling an early willingness to work across the aisle on rural issues (Denver Post). The move comes as Senate Republicans nationally grapple with internal divisions following the recent election cycle, with GOP leadership clashing over party direction (Fox News).

Simpson's nomination suggests Weiser recognizes that agriculture—critical to Colorado's rural economy and water management—requires experienced hands regardless of party affiliation. Republicans have long argued that agricultural policy should prioritize property rights, water security for farmers and ranchers, and minimal regulatory burden on producers. Democrats typically emphasize environmental stewardship and climate considerations in land and water management.

The nomination reflects Colorado's practical political culture, where rural interests often transcend partisan lines. However, conservatives should scrutinize Simpson's actual record on water rights, grazing regulations, and energy development on agricultural lands to ensure he defends producer interests against expanding state environmental mandates.

Watch whether Weiser's cabinet selections continue this bipartisan pattern or revert to partisan orthodoxy on energy, taxes, and land-use policy.
