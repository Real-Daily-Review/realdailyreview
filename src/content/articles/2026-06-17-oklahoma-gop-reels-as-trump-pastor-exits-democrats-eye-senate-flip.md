# Oklahoma GOP Faces Messy Primary Runoffs as Trump-Backed Pastor Exits House Race

Oklahoma Republicans are staring down an embarrassing primary season: Democrats are mounting a long-shot bid to flip a deep-red Senate seat held by the GOP for over 35 years, while a Trump-endorsed pastor just imploded his House campaign after winning fewer votes than expected.

Jackson Lahmeyer, the pastor backed by President Trump for Oklahoma's 1st Congressional District, dropped out of his runoff Wednesday following reports he exchanged flirtatious messages with a campaign aide, according to the Washington Examiner. Trump has already flipped his endorsement to another candidate in the race (Just The News).

Meanwhile, Democrats are pressing their advantage in the Senate primary, where two candidates are heading to a runoff in what Republicans dismiss as a long-shot attempt to reclaim a seat the GOP has held since the late 1980s.

The twin stumbles underscore a broader challenge for Trump's endorsement strategy: vetting matters. While Trump's picks often energize the base, candidates who advance on his backing alone—without strong ground operations or clean records—can crater fast.

**Conservatives should watch whether Trump's next Oklahoma endorsement comes with actual due diligence, or whether the pattern repeats.**
