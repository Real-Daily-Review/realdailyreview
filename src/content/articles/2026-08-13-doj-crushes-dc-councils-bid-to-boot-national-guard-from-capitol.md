# DOJ Crushes D.C. Council's Attempt to Eject National Guard from Capitol

The Justice Department has flatly rejected D.C. Council demands that governors recall National Guard troops from the nation's capital, calling the effort "impotent theatrics" in a scathing letter.

Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald's response to the council's letters was unsparing: the DOJ made clear that state-deployed National Guard units operating under federal authority cannot be removed by local D.C. officials—a constitutional reality the council apparently overlooked (Fox News Politics, The Daily Wire).

The D.C. Council's push represents the latest Democratic attempt to undermine security measures at the Capitol, framed as concerns about "militarization." Republicans counter that the National Guard presence is a lawful, necessary response to genuine threats and that local politicians have no authority to override federal security decisions.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado Gov. Jared Polis, a Democrat, has not deployed Guard troops to D.C., but the precedent matters. If progressive jurisdictions can pressure governors to recall Guard units from federal missions, it sets a dangerous template for politicizing national security—and could affect Colorado's role in future federal operations.

Conservatives should watch whether Democrats escalate these pressure campaigns or attempt legislative workarounds to strip governors of Guard deployment authority.
