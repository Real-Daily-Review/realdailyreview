# Trump Administration Eyes Federal Funds for Stay-at-Home Parents

The Trump administration is reportedly drafting a rule to redirect federal child care dollars toward families with stay-at-home parents—a move that challenges decades of progressive assumptions about how Washington should subsidize child rearing.

According to *The Daily Wire* and *Breitbart*, officials are working to open the Child Care and Development Fund—a roughly $12 billion program currently reserved for working families—to married couples who lose income when one parent stays home. The proposal would let families tap existing federal resources rather than create new spending.

Conservatives view this as a welcome recognition that not all families want institutional child care. It respects parental choice and family autonomy over government-mandated workforce participation. The move also aligns with Trump's broader push to prioritize traditional family structures and reduce reliance on federal child care bureaucracies.

The proposal inverts progressive policy logic: instead of pushing parents into the workforce and subsidizing external care, it acknowledges that some families value one parent at home—and shouldn't be financially penalized for that choice.

For Colorado families, this could mean federal flexibility in how child care assistance flows, though details remain sparse. Conservatives should watch whether Democrats block the rule change or demand it be narrowed to low-income households only.
