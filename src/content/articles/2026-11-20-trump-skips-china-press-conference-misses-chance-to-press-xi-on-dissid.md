# Trump Skips Press Conference After China Talks—Missed Opportunity on Hong Kong Dissident, Critics Say

President Trump concluded his China trip without holding the customary press conference to detail discussions with Xi Jinping and Putin, a departure from standard presidential protocol (CPR News). The move drew conservative criticism for sidestepping accountability on key geopolitical matters.

**Republicans argue** Trump should have used the platform to press Xi on democracy and human rights—specifically the detention of Hong Kong activist Joshua Wong, sentenced for the offense of meeting with American lawmakers (National Review). Conservatives view such advocacy as essential to U.S. credibility on freedom and a non-negotiable element of great-power diplomacy.

The absence of formal questioning also left unanswered questions about the substance of Trump's conversations with both leaders on trade, Taiwan, and strategic competition—issues directly affecting American interests and Colorado's energy and agricultural sectors, which depend on stable trade relationships.

**Democrats and media critics** typically use such moments to demand transparency, though the sources here don't detail their specific response to this incident.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether Trump addresses these diplomatic discussions in subsequent statements, and whether the administration clarifies its position on human rights leverage in future China negotiations.
