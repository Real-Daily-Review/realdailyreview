# U.S. Indicts Former Cuban Leader Raúl Castro; Island Rallies in His Defense

The U.S. Department of Justice has indicted former Cuban leader Raúl Castro on murder and other charges related to his alleged role in the 1996 downing of two civilian aircraft, according to BBC World.

The indictment has triggered sharply divided reactions. Thousands of people gathered outside the U.S. embassy in Havana to protest the charges and express support for Castro, according to Al Jazeera English. The rallies underscore deep divisions between Cuba and the United States over the incident and Castro's legacy.

Cuban-Americans, meanwhile, hold varied perspectives on the indictment, BBC World reports, reflecting the complex political dynamics surrounding Castro's rule and U.S.-Cuba relations.

The 1996 aircraft downing killed four people and remains a contentious episode in U.S.-Cuba relations. The indictment represents a rare criminal prosecution of a former foreign leader by U.S. authorities and signals a harder line toward Castro's government.
