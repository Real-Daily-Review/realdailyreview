# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push starving wildlife into residential areas at unprecedented rates, forcing state officials into a grim cycle of human-wildlife conflict management.

The surge reflects a hard reality: when government land-management policies prioritize environmental restrictions over forest health and water availability, Colorado's ecosystems suffer, and wildlife pays the price. Drought conditions and fuel-laden forests—partly the result of decades of fire-suppression policies and water-diversion regulations—have created a tinderbox that forces bears to abandon their natural habitat and raid homes, garbage, and livestock.

Conservatives argue that Colorado's regulatory approach to water and land use has worsened these conditions. Rather than aggressive forest management, thinning, and controlled burns—which require cutting through environmental red tape—state and federal agencies have allowed forests to become overgrown powder kegs. The result: more wildfires, less water, hungrier bears, and dead animals.

Parks and Wildlife officials say they have no choice but to euthanize problem bears. They're right—but the root cause lies upstream in failed environmental management, not in the bears themselves.
