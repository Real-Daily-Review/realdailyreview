# Colorado Student Among Ole Miss Deaths Linked to Kratom Use

A Colorado resident is among multiple University of Mississippi students found dead in recent weeks, raising fresh concerns about the largely unregulated kratom market and its dangers to young people.

One of the deceased, identified as a 20-year-old junior at Ole Miss, was found at an off-campus residence (9News Denver). The deaths have drawn national attention to kratom—a plant-derived substance legal in most states, including Colorado—that is marketed as a dietary supplement despite psychoactive properties and addiction potential.

Kratom occupies a regulatory gray zone. The FDA has not approved it as a dietary supplement, yet it remains widely available online and in retail shops across Colorado without age restrictions or potency labeling. Conservatives and public health advocates argue this gap exposes young consumers to unknown risks: the substance can cause dependence, respiratory issues, and potentially fatal interactions with other drugs or alcohol.

The kratom industry counters that deaths are rare and often involve multiple substances, not kratom alone. Nonetheless, Colorado parents and policymakers face a choice: whether to tighten state-level oversight or rely on federal action.

Watch for whether Colorado legislators propose age-restriction or labeling requirements for kratom sales—and whether the state's libertarian streak on supplements will collide with parental safety concerns.
