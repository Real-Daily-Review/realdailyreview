# Trump's Census Move Could Shift Colorado's Congressional Clout

President Trump's administration is attempting to exclude non-citizens from the census count used to apportion House seats—a move that could reshape Colorado's congressional representation and voting power.

The Trump administration argues the current system dilutes the votes of American citizens by allocating seats partly based on non-citizen populations. Republicans contend this corrects a decades-old problem that has inflated representation in high-immigration states while reducing seats in states with lower non-citizen populations. (Washington Examiner)

Democrats are expected to challenge the move as unconstitutional and argue the census has historically counted all residents regardless of citizenship status. The left claims the policy targets immigrant communities and misrepresents the Founders' intent.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's congressional delegation size depends directly on population counts. If non-citizens are excluded, Colorado could see its House representation shift—potentially gaining or losing seats relative to states with higher non-citizen populations like California and Texas. This affects not just political power but federal funding formulas tied to population.

The policy also reflects Trump's broader skepticism of unchecked immigration and his push to prioritize citizen interests in resource allocation—a position resonating with Colorado conservatives concerned about rapid demographic change and strain on state services.

Watch for legal challenges and whether Colorado's Democratic-led legislature attempts to intervene in the census process.
