# Denver Airport Leads on Airfare Deals—But Competition, Not Government, Drives the Bargains

Denver International Airport tops the nation for domestic flight deals, according to a new report, but don't expect politicians to take credit—the real story is free-market competition at work.

The report, covered by 9News Denver, found that airports with the cheapest fares share a common trait: robust airline competition and shared hub operations. Denver's position reflects what happens when multiple carriers compete aggressively for Colorado travelers' business. No government subsidy, no regulatory mandate—just carriers fighting for market share.

This matters for Colorado taxpayers and business travelers. Lower fares mean more disposable income stays in your pocket and companies can travel more efficiently. It's a textbook example of how competitive markets, not government intervention, deliver real consumer benefits.

Meanwhile, the airline industry faces its own credibility crisis. The New York Post reported that American Airlines is facing a lawsuit after allegedly mocking an assault victim and then accusing *him* of misconduct—a stunning indictment of corporate culture and customer service standards. When airlines prioritize liability management over passenger safety and dignity, market competition becomes the only real accountability mechanism.

Watch whether Colorado legislators resist the urge to regulate airfares or impose new airline taxes—and whether Denver's competitive advantage survives if they don't.
