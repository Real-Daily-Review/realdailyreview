# VP Vance Steps Up to Press Podium as White House Messaging Shifts

Vice President JD Vance is taking the briefing room podium for his first White House press briefing since Karoline Leavitt's departure last month, signaling a notable shift in how the administration communicates with the press corps.

Vance has previously filled in for Leavitt during her maternity leave, giving him experience at the lectern. His appearance underscores the Trump administration's reliance on senior officials to manage media relations directly—a departure from the traditional press secretary model that some see as a way to bypass filter-heavy messaging and speak directly to Americans.

The briefing comes as the White House navigates several pressing issues, including reports of Iranian troop deployments and broader foreign policy challenges. For Colorado conservatives, Vance's direct engagement with the press offers an opportunity to hear unfiltered administration positions on energy policy, border security, and economic priorities that affect the state.

Vance's willingness to step into the briefing role reflects confidence in the vice president's communication skills and the administration's strategy of having top officials own the message rather than delegate it entirely to a press secretary. Whether this becomes a regular practice or a temporary measure remains to be seen.
