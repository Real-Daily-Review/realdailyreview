# Federal Investigators Blame Steering Failure in New Orleans Tanker-Riverboat Collision

A catastrophic steering failure caused a tanker to plow into a New Orleans riverboat cruise ship, federal investigators determined—raising fresh questions about maritime safety oversight and whether lax regulatory enforcement contributed to the disaster.

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) identified steering malfunction as the cause, though investigators said they're still determining the precise mechanical failure (9News Denver). The collision forced crew members to abandon ship in desperate circumstances; one riverboat worker, Leelah Hager, made an anguished FaceTime call to her father while treading water in the Mississippi River after jumping from the struck vessel (New York Post).

**The conservative concern:** This incident underscores a broader problem—federal maritime agencies often prioritize compliance paperwork over actual safety audits. Rather than expanding bureaucratic oversight or imposing new regulations that burden operators, investigators should focus on whether existing safety standards were properly enforced and whether vessel maintenance protocols failed.
