# Denver Braces for Dangerous Triple-Digit Heat This Weekend

Colorado's Front Range is about to get a hard lesson in why reliable, affordable energy matters—as temperatures are forecast to shatter records this weekend.

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for the entire Front Range starting at noon Sunday, with Denver expected to hit 100 degrees both Sunday and Monday (9News Denver). The National Weather Service warns limited moisture means few clouds or showers until Tuesday, leaving residents exposed to dangerous conditions.

This isn't just uncomfortable—it's a stress test on Colorado's power grid. As demand for air conditioning spikes, Coloradans will depend on stable electricity supply. Yet state regulators continue pushing aggressive renewable mandates and coal plant retirements that threaten grid reliability during exactly these peak-demand moments. Democrats in Denver and Boulder have championed aggressive climate policies that prioritize ideology over practical energy security.

Conservatives argue Colorado needs a diverse, resilient energy portfolio—including natural gas and responsible coal use—to handle extreme weather events. Shuttering reliable baseload power plants before proven alternatives can handle the load puts families and elderly residents at risk.

The heat advisory extends through Monday; residents should limit outdoor activity, stay hydrated, and check on vulnerable neighbors.
