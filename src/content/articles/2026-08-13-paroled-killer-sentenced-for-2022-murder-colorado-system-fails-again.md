# Colorado Parole System Fails Again: Killer Sentenced for 2022 Murder While on Release

A Denver-area man sentenced to 30 years in prison for the 2022 murder of Margaret Eberhart committed the crime while on parole—the latest indictment of Colorado's revolving-door criminal justice approach.

Ricky Lee Roybal-Smith pleaded guilty to second-degree murder in Eberhart's death, which occurred over four years ago. According to 9News Denver, Roybal-Smith is already serving prison sentences for murders of three additional men in 2025, suggesting a pattern of violent recidivism that parole supervision failed to prevent.

The case underscores a persistent conservative concern: Colorado's parole and early-release policies prioritize offender rehabilitation rhetoric over public safety. When violent criminals remain on the streets pending trial or sentencing—and then kill again—the system has failed its primary duty: protecting law-abiding Coloradans.

Republicans and crime-focused conservatives argue that Colorado's lenient parole standards and inadequate supervision create preventable tragedies. Victims' families and taxpayers bear the cost of both the initial crimes and the lengthy re-prosecution that follows.

The state's criminal justice establishment rarely faces accountability when paroled murderers kill. Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand parole reform or continue defending a system that has demonstrably endangered residents.
