# Colorado Heat Wave Looms as Storm System Moves Through Denver This Week

Denver faces a volatile weather pattern that will test the state's infrastructure and energy grid heading into summer. Afternoon storms Thursday will bring temporary relief and improve air quality across eastern Colorado, but forecasters warn of a dangerous heat wave building for early next week that could push temperatures to 100°F by Monday (9News Denver).

The storm system offers a brief respite with seasonal highs near 90 degrees and cloud cover Thursday (KDVR Fox31), but the reprieve is short-lived. A potential Weather Impact Alert looms as the heat dome settles over the Front Range, raising concerns about power demand, water availability, and strain on Colorado's grid during peak summer conditions.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses, extreme heat cycles underscore why reliable, affordable energy matters—and why the state's push to shutter coal plants and rely on intermittent renewables deserves scrutiny. Conservatives argue that baseload power from natural gas and coal remains essential to meet demand spikes when Coloradans need air conditioning most.

Watch for whether Colorado's grid operators issue conservation alerts or rolling blackout warnings if the heat wave intensifies demand beyond available supply.
