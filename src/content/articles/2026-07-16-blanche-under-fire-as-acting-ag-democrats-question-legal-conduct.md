# Acting AG Blanche Faces Democratic Fire Over Legal Conduct

Todd Blanche, now serving as acting attorney general after Pam Bondi's departure, is drawing sharp criticism from Senate Democrats who question his legal judgment. Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI) accused Blanche on CNN of "affirmatively committing wrongful acts" in his current role, escalating partisan tensions over the Justice Department's direction.

Republicans counter that Blanche brings needed reform to an agency they argue has been weaponized against conservatives. The Washington Examiner's editorial board argues Blanche can be "the attorney general American children need," framing him as a champion for restoring institutional integrity after years of progressive overreach.

Blanche previously served as deputy attorney general from March 2025 to April 2026 before assuming the acting role. His nomination now faces Senate Judiciary Committee scrutiny, with Democrats signaling they'll challenge his record aggressively.

For Colorado conservatives, this fight matters: the Justice Department's posture on Second Amendment enforcement, energy regulation, and election integrity directly affects state policy and individual liberties. Watch whether Senate Republicans can hold the line on Blanche's confirmation or whether Democratic obstruction forces a prolonged vacancy at Justice.
