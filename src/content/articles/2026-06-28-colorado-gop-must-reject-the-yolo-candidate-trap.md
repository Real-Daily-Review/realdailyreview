# Colorado Voters Should Demand Better Than Character-Compromised Candidates

Both parties are fielding Senate candidates with troubling personal records, and voters—especially in competitive states like Colorado—shouldn't settle for the lesser of two evils.

The Washington Examiner flags a troubling trend: Democrats nominated a problematic candidate in Maine, while Republicans did the same in Texas. This "YOLO candidate" phenomenon—the idea that personal conduct no longer disqualifies anyone from high office—corrodes public trust and weakens conservative messaging on accountability and constitutional governance.

Colorado Republicans should take note. Voters here care about character and competence. They've rejected candidates with baggage before, and they're right to do so. When Republicans nominate flawed figures, it undercuts the party's credibility on fiscal responsibility, rule of law, and limited government—the core principles that should define conservative candidates.

The real problem isn't just the candidates themselves; it's that party establishments have grown too comfortable with moral compromise. Conservatives argue that winning elections means nothing if it means abandoning the principles you claim to defend. Colorado's Republican primary voters have the power to demand better.

**Watch whether Colorado GOP primary voters hold the line on character, or whether national party pressure to "win at all costs" overrides local standards.**
