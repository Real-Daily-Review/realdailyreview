# ICE Arrests Illegal Immigrant Who Won $1M Massachusetts Lottery Prize

Federal immigration enforcement just caught a wanted man red-handed—literally trying to cash in on the American dream while dodging a deportation order. Immigration and Customs Enforcement arrested Ariel Antonio Velasquez-Morales, an El Salvador national with a 2014 deportation order, days after he claimed a $1 million Massachusetts State Lottery prize (Fox News, New York Post). Officers say Velasquez-Morales ran from agents in East Boston before his arrest.

The case underscores a conservative concern: lax border enforcement and inadequate interior immigration controls allow deportation orders to go unenforced for over a decade. Republicans argue that sanctuary policies and limited ICE resources enable illegal immigrants to remain in the country, work, and access public benefits—including state lottery winnings—despite prior removal orders.

Democrats typically counter that immigration enforcement should focus on serious criminals, not lottery winners, and that deportations strain resources better spent elsewhere. The left also argues that lottery eligibility shouldn't hinge on immigration status.

For Colorado: state lottery systems should verify citizenship or legal status before distributing prizes, and taxpayers deserve assurance that public funds aren't flowing to people in the country illegally.

**Watch whether Massachusetts or other states tighten lottery eligibility verification—and whether Colorado follows suit.**
