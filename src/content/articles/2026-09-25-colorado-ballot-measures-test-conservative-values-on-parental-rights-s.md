# Colorado Ballot Measures Pit Conservative Values Against Progressive Spending and Government Overreach

Colorado voters face a November choice between protecting parental rights and bodily autonomy versus bankrolling wealthy environmental nonprofits with taxpayer dollars.

**Proposition 135** centers on adolescent medical decisions. Conservatives argue the measure protects parental involvement and recognizes that teenage judgment is still developing—some choices cannot be undone. Supporters emphasize bodily integrity as a fundamental principle that should apply equally to all medical procedures (Complete Colorado).

**Proposition 137**, meanwhile, would direct state funding to land conservation efforts. Critics argue it amounts to a backdoor subsidy for wealthy NGOs, particularly The Nature Conservancy—the measure's primary sponsor. Taxpayers would foot the bill while private organizations reap the benefits, conservatives contend (Complete Colorado).

Both measures reflect a broader pattern: Colorado's progressive legislature and ballot initiatives increasingly bypass voter scrutiny and expand government's reach into family decisions and spending priorities.

The ballot also highlights election integrity concerns. The Jena Griswold controversy demonstrated how Colorado's current system allows candidates to win with minority support in crowded fields, raising questions about whether election rules adequately reflect voter preferences (Complete Colorado).
