# Trump's FIFA Call Gets US Star Folarin Balogun's Ban Overturned—and International Soccer's Credibility Takes a Hit

President Trump's direct intervention with FIFA leadership has reversed a one-game suspension for American forward Folarin Balogun, allowing the U.S. team's leading scorer to play against Belgium in Monday's World Cup round of 16 match (Washington Examiner). Balogun received a red card for stepping on an opponent's foot in the previous game—a controversial call that drew immediate scrutiny.

According to reports, Trump personally called FIFA's president requesting a review, and the organization suspended the ban within days (Daily Caller). U.S. Soccer coach Mauricio Pochettino praised the reversal, saying the team "was punished enough" (New York Post).

The decision has sparked international outrage. Belgium's soccer federation expressed being "astonished" that FIFA allowed Balogun to play, particularly given Belgium's direct competitive interest (Breitbart). The reversal also raises questions about FIFA's consistency: England's Jarrell Quansah faced a one-game suspension for a similar red card issued around the same time, highlighting the appearance of unequal treatment (New York Post).

While Trump's willingness to fight for American interests resonates with his base, the episode underscores how political pressure can undermine the integrity of international sports governance. Conservatives should watch whether FIFA faces pressure to explain its decision-making standards or whether this sets a precedent for executive intervention in athletic competition.
