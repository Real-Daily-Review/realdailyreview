# Democrat Senate Hopeful Flips on Data Centers as Power Costs Spike

North Carolina Democratic Senate candidate Roy Cooper is discovering that campaign rhetoric and governing reality don't always align—a lesson Colorado energy workers and ratepayers should heed.

As governor, Cooper championed data center expansion as economic growth. Now, running for Senate, he's pivoting sharply, blaming data centers for driving up electricity costs and targeting their energy consumption (Fox News). The reversal exposes a core problem with progressive energy policy: Democrats celebrate green-energy mandates and grid expansion until voters' power bills climb, then hunt for convenient villains.

Colorado faces similar pressures. The state's push toward aggressive renewable targets and data center recruitment—particularly around Denver and northern Colorado—mirrors North Carolina's trajectory. If Cooper's flip-flopping becomes a campaign liability there, it signals Democratic vulnerability on the cost-of-living crisis their own policies helped create.

Republicans argue that unreliable wind and solar, combined with grid constraints and heavy industrial demand, inevitably spike rates. They contend that embracing natural gas and nuclear—not scapegoating data centers—keeps power affordable while meeting demand.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats distance themselves from aggressive rate-hike policies ahead of 2026, or double down on blaming industry for problems their regulations created.
