# House Passes Stock Trading Ban—But GOP Adds Voter ID Rider

Congress finally moved to ban members from trading stocks on insider information, a rare bipartisan win that conservatives should view with cautious optimism—but not before Republicans attached a voter ID requirement that Democrats immediately attacked as a poison pill.

The House passed the "Stop Insider Trading Act" in a bipartisan vote, according to *Just The News*. The measure prohibits members of Congress and their families from buying new individual stocks while in office. Republicans, however, added a voter ID measure to the bill, a move *CPR News* reports has sparked debate over whether GOP leaders are acting in good faith or using the popular anti-corruption measure as cover for election-security priorities.

Democrats who opposed the legislation argued the bill doesn't go far enough—it allows members to retain existing stock holdings, creating a loophole for accumulated wealth. But their real objection, conservatives note, centers on the voter ID rider, which the left views as voter suppression despite evidence that ID requirements enjoy broad public support.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado voters and taxpayers should watch whether this bill survives the Senate and whether the voter ID provision survives Democratic obstruction—a key test of whether Congress can actually police itself or if partisan gridlock will kill even bipartisan anti-corruption measures.
