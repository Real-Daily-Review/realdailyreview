# Colorado's Primary Problem: Unaffiliated Voters Are Gaming the System

Colorado's primary election system is quietly handing power to unaffiliated voters who skip party caucuses, then show up to decide Republican and Democratic nominees—without ever committing to either party.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state's rules allow unaffiliateds to vote in either primary, effectively letting non-members pick party candidates. This matters in Colorado because primaries increasingly *are* the general election in heavily Democratic districts and counties. Republicans, meanwhile, face a narrower field: only two of 18 GOP gubernatorial candidates advanced out of the assembly process, meaning the party's base had limited say in the final ballot.

Conservatives argue this system weakens party accountability and allows ideological outsiders to shape nominee selection without skin in the game. Party members who actually attend caucuses—the traditional vetting ground—get overruled by drive-by voters with no party loyalty.

The unaffiliated surge reflects broader Colorado voter trends: registration without party affiliation is climbing, and many registered Republicans and Democrats don't participate in caucuses at all, further diluting insider influence.
