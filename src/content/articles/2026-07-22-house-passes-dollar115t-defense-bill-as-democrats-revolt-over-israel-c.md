# House Passes $1.15T Defense Bill as Democrats Split Over Israel Provision

The House narrowly passed a $1.15 trillion defense authorization bill Wednesday—a rare moment when national security trumped progressive ideology, though barely. The National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) for fiscal 2027 cleared on a 216-212 vote, with 205 Democrats and seven Republicans voting against it, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The contentious sticking point: Section 219, which strengthens U.S.-Israel defense cooperation. Progressive Democrats opposed the Israel language, arguing it inflames tensions in the Middle East. Republicans countered that weakening U.S.-Israel military ties amid Iranian hostilities abandons a critical ally and emboldens antisemitic rhetoric in Congress.

The razor-thin margin signals Democratic fracture over foreign policy. Historically, defense bills sailed through with bipartisan support. No longer.

**Colorado angle:** Defense spending flows to Colorado contractors and military installations. Weakening defense-industrial partnerships—or allowing partisan gridlock to paralyze military readiness—directly threatens Colorado jobs and national security posture.

**What to watch:** Whether Senate Democrats attempt to strip the Israel provision or whether the bill holds firm through reconciliation. Either outcome signals how far the party has drifted from Cold War-era defense consensus.
