# Michigan Democrat's Apology Tour Rings Hollow on Antisemitism

Abdul El-Sayed, Michigan's Democratic U.S. Senate nominee, met with the state's Democratic Jewish Caucus to apologize for his association with Hasan Piker, a streamer with a documented history of antisemitic rhetoric and anti-American commentary. Yet conservatives say the damage control doesn't add up.

El-Sayed attempted to distance himself by dismissing Piker as merely "a streamer in California"—a characterization that drew sharp pushback. MSNBC's Katy Tur countered that Piker actively campaigns alongside numerous Democrats, making him far more than a fringe online personality (Breitbart). Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner's analysis found that despite media claims of Democratic distancing, only six Democrats had explicitly condemned Piker's latest antisemitic remarks blaming Jews for potential violence against them.

Republicans argue this episode exposes a deeper problem: the Democratic Party's reluctance to genuinely police extremism within its ranks. Mike Rogers, El-Sayed's GOP opponent, called the apology a "sham" (Fox News), suggesting voters should question whether Democrats truly reject the antisemitic and anti-American views circulating in their coalition.

While this race unfolds in Michigan, Colorado conservatives should note how national Democratic figures navigate similar pressure—and whether Colorado's own progressive politicians maintain similar standards or remain silent.
