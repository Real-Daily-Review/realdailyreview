# Oil Prices and Iran Tensions Could Upend GOP's Midterm Hopes

Oil-market chaos tied to Middle East conflict could tank Republican momentum before November's elections, warns the *Washington Examiner*. With Iran and Gulf states meeting this week over Strait of Hormuz passage and Houthi threats at Bab al-Mandeb, crude prices could spike toward $100 per barrel—a political disaster for Republicans heading into midterms.

The timing is brutal for Colorado's energy sector and GOP candidates. Higher gas prices hit working families and small businesses hardest, undercutting the administration's economic message. Colorado oil and gas workers, already facing regulatory headwinds from state Democrats, would see their industry's gains threatened by geopolitical shocks beyond Washington's control.

President Trump has signaled he won't pursue an Iran deal until *after* the election, but the *Examiner* argues that waiting risks letting oil markets spiral out of control before voters head to the polls. Republicans need stable energy prices to defend their economic record; Democrats will hammer any spike as proof of GOP mismanagement.

Vice President JD Vance, meanwhile, is juggling multiple roles—RNC finance chair, campaign trail regular, and anti-fraud point man—but his real job is keeping the party unified and on message as external shocks threaten the midterm narrative.

**Watch whether the Trump administration moves to stabilize oil markets before November, or gambles that geopolitical restraint will hold.**
