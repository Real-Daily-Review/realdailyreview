# Colorado Republicans Split on Data Center Boom as Water, Power Costs Mount

Colorado's conservative establishment faces a growing fault line: embrace AI infrastructure investment or protect water and electricity for ranchers and families. Republican leaders nationwide are increasingly skeptical of data center expansion, even as the Trump administration pushes hard for development (The Daily Wire). The tension centers on real costs—electricity prices and water consumption in arid states where both resources are already strained.

National Review reports that while data centers use less water than irrigation agriculture, the question remains: where does that water actually go in water-scarce Colorado? That's no academic debate for a state where agriculture depends on every drop and rural communities already face rising power bills.

Republicans who once welcomed the economic investment promised by AI infrastructure are now "pumping the brakes" as constituents raise concerns (The Daily Wire). Colorado's ranchers and rural counties—traditional GOP strongholds—are watching their water tables and electricity costs closely.

The conservative case for caution is straightforward: no amount of tech-sector jobs justifies straining Colorado's water supply or pricing out working families from reliable power. Yet some Republicans still see data centers as essential economic development and national security infrastructure.
