# Bear Attacks Estes Park Woman in Home—Wildlife Management Failures Mount

An Estes Park woman in her 80s was attacked and scratched by a bear that entered her home, underscoring Colorado Parks and Wildlife's inability to manage the state's exploding bear population.

The woman was knocked to the floor and sustained abdominal scratches after waking to find the bear in her kitchen during early Thursday morning hours, according to Colorado Parks and Wildlife (KDVR). The incident marks another in what the Colorado Sun describes as an "extraordinary bear year"—a pattern conservatives attribute to mismanagement of wildlife populations and overly restrictive hunting regulations that have allowed bear numbers to surge.

Republicans and hunters argue that Colorado's progressive wildlife policies prioritize animal welfare over public safety and property rights. Stricter hunting seasons and reduced harvest limits, they contend, have created conditions where bears lose their natural fear of humans and increasingly raid homes and trash. The state's bear management strategy, critics say, reflects ideology over science—protecting bears at the expense of residents' security.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that habitat loss and climate change drive bears toward populated areas, and that lethal removal isn't a sustainable solution.

Coloradans should watch whether CPW expands bear hazing programs or finally increases hunting pressure to restore population balance before more residents are harmed.
