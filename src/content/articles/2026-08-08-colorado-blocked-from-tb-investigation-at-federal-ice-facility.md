# Federal Overreach Blocks Colorado Health Officials From TB Investigation at Aurora ICE Facility

Colorado public health authorities remain stonewalled in their effort to investigate an active tuberculosis case at the GEO Group's Aurora ICE detention facility—a stark reminder that federal immigration enforcement trumps state and local authority, even when communicable disease is at stake.

An active TB case was confirmed in June (CPR News), but ICE has since halted all compliance with local public health investigations. Adams County officials say they're unlikely to pursue further legal action against the private contractor running the facility, citing an ongoing lawsuit.

Colorado lawmakers passed a state law allowing the attorney general to issue $50,000 fines starting this week for denying public health access (9News Denver). Yet that remedy may prove toothless if federal authorities continue asserting immunity.

**The conservative concern:** This case exposes the dysfunction when federal immigration enforcement operates beyond state oversight. Coloradans deserve answers about disease control in their communities—but federal agencies routinely block local transparency. The GEO Group, a private prison operator, shouldn't hide behind federal authority to dodge accountability.

Democrats and public health officials argue the state fine mechanism is necessary leverage. Republicans counter that real solutions require Congress to clarify federal-state coordination on detention facility health standards, not another layer of state bureaucracy.
