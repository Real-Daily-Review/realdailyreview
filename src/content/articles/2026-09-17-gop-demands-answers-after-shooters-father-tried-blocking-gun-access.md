# GOP Blindsided by New Details in Trump Butler Assassination Attempt

Republican lawmakers are demanding answers after learning that the shooter's own father tried blocking his gun access months before the July assassination attempt on Donald Trump in Butler, Pennsylvania.

Thomas Matthew Crooks' father, Matthew Crooks, reportedly sought ways to prevent his son from accessing firearms in May 2024—roughly two months before the attempt—according to Fox News and Just The News. The revelation has sparked GOP outrage, particularly because a federal task force investigating the incident was apparently unaware that federal authorities had already interviewed Crooks' family members.

"Awfully strange," Republican lawmakers told Fox News, signaling serious questions about intelligence failures and inter-agency communication breakdowns.

The details raise uncomfortable questions for gun-control advocates: if a father's private efforts to restrict his own son's firearm access couldn't stop the attempt, what makes Democrats think new gun regulations will? Conservatives argue the case underscores that the real problem isn't access to firearms—it's identifying and stopping individuals with clear warning signs before they act.

For Colorado gun owners and Second Amendment advocates, this case is a cautionary tale about how government agencies fail at their core job: connecting dots and preventing violence through existing tools, not expanding restrictions on law-abiding citizens.

Watch whether congressional Republicans use these revelations to demand accountability from federal law enforcement and intelligence agencies.
