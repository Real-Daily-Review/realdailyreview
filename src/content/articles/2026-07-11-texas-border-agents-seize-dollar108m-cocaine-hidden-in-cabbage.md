# Border Agents Seize $10.8M Cocaine Stash Hidden in Cabbage at Texas Port

Federal border officers at the Pharr port of entry in Texas intercepted a massive drug smuggling operation this week, discovering 477 packages of cocaine weighing 1,173 pounds concealed in a commercial cabbage shipment, according to Fox News and the New York Post. The seizure, valued at approximately $10.8 million on the street, underscores the ongoing challenge of transnational drug trafficking through America's southern border.

The cocaine was uncovered during a canine inspection of a tractor-trailer, a routine screening that proved critical in stopping the contraband from reaching U.S. markets. The bust represents another data point in the broader border security debate—one where Republicans argue that stronger enforcement, adequate staffing, and physical barriers remain essential to combating the flow of narcotics that devastate American communities.

For Colorado, which has struggled with fentanyl and cocaine distribution networks fueling overdose deaths and crime, the seizure illustrates why border security matters to state taxpayers and families. Drug trafficking organizations use sophisticated smuggling methods precisely because they profit enormously when shipments slip through.

Conservatives should watch whether the Biden-Harris administration uses these enforcement wins to justify current border policies or whether Republicans will demand accelerated deportations and stricter port-of-entry protocols.
