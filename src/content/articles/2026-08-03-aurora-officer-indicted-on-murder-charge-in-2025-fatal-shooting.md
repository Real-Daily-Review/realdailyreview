# Aurora Officer Indicted in Fatal 2025 Shooting

An Aurora police officer faces serious charges nearly a year after a deadly encounter that raises hard questions about police accountability and use-of-force standards in Colorado.

Matthew Neely, 54, has been indicted on second-degree murder and reckless manslaughter charges in the August 30, 2025 shooting death of Rajon Belt-Stubblefield, 37, near East 6th Avenue and Billings Street, according to 9News Denver and the Colorado Sun. The case was referred to an Arapahoe County grand jury earlier this year, which voted to indict.

The indictment marks a significant moment in a case that will test Colorado's approach to officer-involved shootings. Law enforcement accountability remains contentious: conservatives generally support due process for officers facing charges while opposing reflexive anti-police sentiment, while progressive activists often demand swift prosecution and systemic reform.

The delay between the shooting and indictment—nearly a year—underscores how lengthy these investigations can be, frustrating both families seeking justice and officers awaiting resolution. Colorado taxpayers will likely foot the bill for both prosecution and the officer's defense.

Neely's case will proceed through Arapahoe County courts. Watch whether the trial becomes a flashpoint for broader debates over police training, de-escalation protocols, and qualified immunity in Colorado.
