# Mexico Shortens School Year for World Cup, Drawing Parent Backlash

Mexico's federal government announced it would allow schools to end the academic year on June 5—roughly a month early—citing both the FIFA World Cup and extreme summer heat, according to BBC World. The decision has sparked widespread criticism from parents who oppose disrupting the school calendar for a sporting event.

The government framed the early closure as a response to dual pressures: hosting World Cup matches and protecting students from dangerous temperatures during Mexico's hottest months, according to Deutsche Welle. Officials suggested the shortened year would address both logistical demands and student welfare.

Parents argue the move prioritizes a sporting tournament over education. The backlash reflects broader concerns about how major events can reshape public institutions and whether such accommodations are justified.

The government has not yet responded to the criticism in detail, and it remains unclear whether the June 5 end date will be implemented as proposed or modified following the public outcry.
