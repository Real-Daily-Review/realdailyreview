# Trump's Drug Price Reforms Deliver Real Savings—Here's What Colorado Families Should Know

Trump administration action on pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) reform is driving prescription costs down sharply, with some medications dropping from $1,000 to $149—the largest price reduction in 60 years, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The reforms target middlemen PBMs that have long inflated drug costs while blocking transparency between pharmacies, manufacturers, and patients. By loosening PBM control over pricing and formularies, the administration is letting market competition work—exactly what conservatives have argued would happen if government got out of the way.

For Colorado families already squeezed by inflation and rising healthcare premiums, this matters. Seniors on fixed incomes and working Coloradans without generous coverage can now afford medications they've been rationing or skipping. This is market-driven reform, not government price-fixing or rationing.

Democrats have historically favored heavy-handed price controls and government negotiation schemes that stifle innovation and limit drug availability. Republicans counter that removing regulatory barriers and increasing transparency achieves lower prices without killing the incentive for pharmaceutical companies to develop new treatments.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature attempts to layer state-level PBM regulations that could undermine these federal reforms—or whether they'll let this success speak for itself.
