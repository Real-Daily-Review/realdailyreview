# Trump Hosts Xi at White House State Dinner—But Colorado's Energy Sector Watches Trade Talks Closely

President Trump rolled out the red carpet for Chinese President Xi Jinping at a White House state dinner Thursday, featuring a pescatarian menu and an ornate bald eagle sculpture gift—a symbolic nod to American strength as the two leaders seek to settle trade differences (Washington Examiner, New York Post).

The guest list included House Speaker Mike Johnson, Elon Musk, Trump family members, and Fox News personalities (Just The News). Trump invited attendees to tour a newly renovated White House ballroom, signaling an effort to project confidence and openness during high-stakes diplomatic engagement.

**The Colorado angle:** While the pageantry unfolds, Colorado's energy sector—oil, gas, and coal producers—remains acutely aware that U.S.-China trade tensions directly impact their bottom lines. Tariffs, supply chains, and market access to Chinese buyers affect Colorado energy workers and rural economies dependent on resource development. Republicans argue that Trump's willingness to negotiate directly with Xi, rather than through multilateral bodies, protects American interests and domestic energy independence.

Conservatives should monitor whether these talks yield concrete commitments on trade fairness and intellectual property protection—or devolve into symbolic gestures that leave Colorado's energy producers exposed to Chinese market manipulation.
