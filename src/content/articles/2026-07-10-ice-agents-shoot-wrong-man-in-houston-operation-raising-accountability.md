# ICE Operation Ends in Tragedy as Agents Shoot Wrong Man in Houston

A Department of Homeland Security operation in Houston has sparked fresh questions about federal law enforcement protocols after agents fatally shot a man who was not their intended target. According to 9News Denver, DHS officials said someone in a van "resembled" the target of the ICE operation before the shooting that killed Lorenzo Salgado. Texas U.S. Rep. Sylvia Garcia reported that the acting ICE director confirmed Salgado was not the intended target.

The incident underscores conservative concerns about federal law enforcement overreach and the risks of aggressive immigration enforcement operations that lack proper safeguards. Republicans and civil liberties advocates argue such operations demand ironclad protocols to prevent tragic mistakes—especially when lives hang in the balance.

Meanwhile, Fox News reported a brighter moment from ICE operations: agents in Milwaukee paused an arrest operation to rescue a 31-year-old woman trapped in a rollover crash, demonstrating the dual role federal agents play in public safety.

For Colorado taxpayers and gun owners, the Houston case raises uncomfortable questions about federal agency accountability and use-of-force standards that extend far beyond immigration enforcement.

**Watch for:** Whether Congress demands a formal review of ICE operational protocols and whether DHS leadership faces pressure to tighten identification procedures before agents deploy force.
