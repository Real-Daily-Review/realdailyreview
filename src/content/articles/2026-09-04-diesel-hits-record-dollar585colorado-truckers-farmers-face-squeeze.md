# Diesel Hits Record High—Colorado Truckers and Farmers Face Crushing Fuel Costs

Diesel prices have surged to an all-time high of $5.85 per gallon nationally, with two states now averaging over $7 per gallon, as geopolitical turmoil in Iran and Ukraine disrupts global fuel supplies (Washington Examiner). This marks a new peak beyond the previous record of $5.81 set in May 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine (9News Denver).

For Colorado—home to thousands of trucking operations, agricultural businesses, and rural ranchers—the spike threatens already-thin profit margins. Diesel fuel is essential to moving goods across the state and powering farm equipment during harvest season. Higher fuel costs ripple through supply chains, raising prices on groceries, construction materials, and consumer goods that Colorado families depend on.

Republicans argue that Biden-era energy policies—including restrictions on domestic oil and gas leasing, pipeline cancellations, and regulatory hostility toward fossil fuels—have weakened U.S. energy independence and left America vulnerable to global shocks. Conservatives contend that expanding domestic energy production would insulate Colorado and the nation from Middle East instability and reduce reliance on foreign oil.

Democrats counter that long-term energy transition and renewable investment are necessary, though they offer little immediate relief at the pump.
