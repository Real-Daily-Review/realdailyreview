# Trump Administration Moves to Open 45 Million Forest Acres to Development

The Trump administration is moving to rescind the 2001 Roadless Area Conservation Rule, potentially opening nearly 45 million acres of national forest to logging and road construction—a major win for Colorado's timber industry and rural economies that have chafed under decades of environmental restrictions.

Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins announced the Forest Service has filed a proposed rule to end the protections (Washington Examiner). Environmentalists warn the move threatens old-growth forests, wildlife habitat, and water supplies (9News Denver). But conservatives and Republicans argue the rule has locked up productive land and strangled rural communities dependent on timber harvests and resource development.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's mountain counties, already squeezed by federal land restrictions, stand to benefit from renewed timber sales and economic activity. The state's outdoor recreation and water interests, however, may face pushback from environmental groups claiming the rule protects watershed health.

This is a direct clash between free-market resource development and progressive environmental lockup. Conservatives say opening these acres restores balance and supports working forests; the left claims it invites ecological damage.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's congressional delegation and state officials back the rollback, and how quickly environmental lawsuits attempt to block the rule change.
