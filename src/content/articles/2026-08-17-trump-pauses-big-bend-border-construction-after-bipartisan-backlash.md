# Trump Administration Pauses Big Bend Border Work After Bipartisan Pushback

The Trump administration has hit pause on border security construction inside Big Bend National Park in Texas, bowing to rare bipartisan criticism over environmental and conservation concerns. Customs and Border Protection Commissioner Rodney Scott announced Monday that all construction activity within the park is temporarily halted pending further review (Washington Examiner).

The original plan called for a 30-foot-high wall along the park's Mexican border, but CBP had already scaled back to "limited" vehicle barriers equipped with detection technology (Just The News). Even that reduced footprint drew fire from both Republican and Democratic lawmakers worried about damage to the pristine desert landscape and wildlife habitat.

**The conservative angle:** While border security remains a Republican priority, this pause reflects legitimate concerns about federal overreach into protected lands. Conservatives have long championed property rights and limited government intrusion—principles that extend to preserving public lands from unnecessary development. The question now is whether CBP can find a security solution that doesn't sacrifice conservation values that many Colorado outdoor enthusiasts and Western Republicans hold dear.

Colorado taxpayers and land-use advocates should watch whether this becomes a template for balancing border security with environmental stewardship, or whether it signals weakness on the southern border.
