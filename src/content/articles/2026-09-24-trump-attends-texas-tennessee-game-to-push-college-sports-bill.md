# Trump Attends Texas-Tennessee Game to Push College Sports Bill

President Trump is heading to Neyland Stadium this Saturday for the Texas-Tennessee matchup—a high-profile appearance aimed at drumming up support for the Protect College Sports Act, according to the Washington Examiner and reporting from Clay Travis at OutKick.

The White House confirmed Trump's attendance as he continues pressing Congress to pass legislation addressing college athletics policy. The timing is strategic: a marquee game on the biggest college football Saturday of the season gives the president a national platform to advance his sports agenda.

Republicans have framed college sports reform as essential to protecting athlete rights and competitive integrity. Democrats and progressive activists, however, have raised concerns about regulatory overreach and the federal government's role in what has traditionally been NCAA and conference territory.

For Colorado, the implications matter. CU Boulder and Colorado State compete in the Big 12 and Mountain West respectively—conferences that would be affected by any federal college sports legislation. Changes to athlete compensation rules, transfer portal regulations, or Title IX enforcement could reshape how Colorado schools recruit, budget, and operate their athletic programs.

Conservatives should watch whether Congress actually moves on Trump's bill or whether it stalls amid competing priorities and lobbying pressure from universities and the NCAA.
