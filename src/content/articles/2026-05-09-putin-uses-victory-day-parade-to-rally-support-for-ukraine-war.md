# Putin Uses Scaled-Back Victory Day Parade to Rally Support for Ukraine War

Russian President Vladimir Putin used his annual Victory Day address to justify the ongoing war in Ukraine, invoking Soviet triumph in World War II as he presided over a downsized military parade on Red Square, according to the BBC and Al Jazeera.

The parade, smaller than in previous years, featured Putin pledging victory in what Moscow calls its "special military operation" in Ukraine. According to Al Jazeera, Putin told troops that "our heroes move forward," drawing a parallel between the Soviet victory over Nazi Germany and Russia's current military campaign.

The scaled-back nature of the parade—held annually on May 9 to commemorate the Soviet Union's 1945 victory—reflects the strain of Russia's nearly three-year war in Ukraine, which has drawn international condemnation and Western military support for Kyiv.

Putin's speech reframed the Ukraine conflict within Russia's historical narrative of defending against external threats, a rhetorical strategy designed to maintain domestic support for the costly military operation.

Watch for any announcements regarding Russian military mobilization or strategy shifts in the coming weeks, as Victory Day speeches often signal policy directions.
