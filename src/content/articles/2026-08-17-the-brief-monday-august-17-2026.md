# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, August 17, 2026

Colorado starts the week with big questions about national security posture, economic headwinds hitting working families, and the left's relentless war on welfare reform. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Trump Orders Pentagon to Scale Back South Korea Drills

President Trump directed Defense Secretary Hegseth to substantially reduce joint military exercises with South Korea — specifically the Ulchi Freedom Shield drills — citing costs and concerns about provoking North Korea. Republicans who back the move argue it's a pragmatic step in ongoing diplomacy with Pyongyang. Critics on the right warn that scaling back exercises signals weakness to Kim Jong Un at a dangerous moment. For Colorado, which is home to major military installations including NORAD and Schriever Space Force Base, shifts in Pacific strategy carry real weight.

---

## Colorado's Minimum Wage Hike: The Data Tells a Complicated Story

As Colorado's minimum wage heads higher again in 2027, early data shows the lowest-paid food workers are seeing the biggest pay bumps — but the gains aren't spreading broadly. Meanwhile, Denver inflation is running at 3.9%, and the University of Colorado has cut its job growth forecasts for the state. Conservatives have long warned that mandated wage floors push up costs, slow hiring, and squeeze small businesses — and the numbers are starting to bear that out. Colorado employers and taxpayers should brace for more of the same as the next increase approaches.

---

## Hawaii Hurricane Devastation: A Federal Response Test

A deadly hurricane ravaged Hawaii's largest islands over the weekend, knocking out power to more than 130,000 households and killing at least one person. Houses were swept away as the storm tore through. The disaster will test federal emergency response capacity and reignite debates over disaster preparedness spending and FEMA's effectiveness. Conservatives will be watching closely to see whether the federal government delivers results efficiently — or whether this becomes another case study in bureaucratic failure when Americans need help most.

---

## The Left's Endless War on Welfare Reform

Despite decades of evidence that work requirements and welfare reform produce better outcomes for families and taxpayers alike, progressives continue targeting the gains made since the landmark 1996 reforms. The left claims the safety net needs to be expanded and work requirements eliminated — conservatives say that's a recipe for dependency, not dignity. For Colorado taxpayers already carrying a heavy load of state spending, any federal rollback of work-oriented welfare policy would mean more costs pushed down the line. The battle isn't over, and conservatives can't afford to stop fighting it.

## Denver Weather: Calmer Start, But Stay Alert

The Front Range gets a warmer, drier start to the workweek after a stormy stretch. Isolated flash flooding, large hail, and strong winds remain possible, particularly in the mountains and foothills. Colorado's outdoor workers — ranchers, energy crews, construction trades — should keep an eye on afternoon conditions as the week develops.

Keep your eyes on Colorado's economic trajectory: with Denver inflation at 3.9%, job growth forecasts being cut, and another minimum wage hike on the horizon, the state's business climate is flashing yellow. Watch whether Republican lawmakers push back hard on the wage mandate before 2027 hits.
