# Colorado Gun Buyers Rush to Beat New Restrictions; Background Checks Triple

Colorado firearm purchases surged to record levels in July as gun owners raced to beat new state restrictions taking effect Aug. 1. The Colorado Bureau of Investigation processed 59,452 background checks last month—nearly triple the 20,172 processed in July 2025 (Colorado Sun, 9News Denver).

The spike reflects widespread concern among Second Amendment advocates over Senate Bill 25-003, which tightens Colorado's already-strict gun regulations. Republicans and gun-rights groups have argued the law infringes on constitutional rights while doing little to address actual criminal behavior. Supporters claim the measure enhances public safety, but conservatives counter that law-abiding Coloradans bear the regulatory burden while criminals ignore new rules.

The rush to purchase before the law's effective date demonstrates what happens when government signals intent to restrict rights: citizens exercise those rights while they still can. Colorado's gun-owning community—from Front Range urbanites to rural hunters and ranchers—clearly views the new restrictions as a threat worth acting on immediately.

Meanwhile, Colorado Parks and Wildlife announced routine fishing closures on multiple rivers due to record heat, citing stress on trout populations (Colorado Sun). While unrelated to the gun law, the closure illustrates how state agencies increasingly restrict outdoor recreation in the name of environmental management.
