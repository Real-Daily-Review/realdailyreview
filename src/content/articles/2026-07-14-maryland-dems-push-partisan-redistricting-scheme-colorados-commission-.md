# Maryland Democrats' Redistricting Power Grab Shows Why Colorado Must Guard Its Own Maps

Maryland Democrats are calling a special legislative session this August to ram through another redistricting amendment—a naked attempt to redraw congressional lines and dilute Republican voting power under the guise of "democracy reform," according to the Washington Examiner.

If three-fifths of both chambers approve, Maryland voters could face a constitutional amendment that effectively disenfranchises Republican voters by packing them into fewer districts. Democrats control the state legislature, making passage likely.

Republicans argue this is textbook partisan gerrymandering dressed up in reform language. The maneuver exposes how far the left will go to entrench power when they hold state government.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's independent redistricting commission, created in 2018, was designed to prevent exactly this kind of partisan map-rigging. While imperfect, the commission model has shielded Colorado from the worst abuses seen in Maryland and other blue states. Conservatives should remain vigilant that future legislatures don't weaken or eliminate the commission to pursue their own gerrymanders—a temptation both parties face when power shifts.

Watch whether Maryland's gambit succeeds and whether other Democratic-controlled states follow suit, signaling a broader assault on fair electoral maps.
