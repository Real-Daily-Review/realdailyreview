# Trump's AG Pick Distances Himself From Controversial IRS Settlement

Todd Blanche, Trump's nominee for Attorney General, is clearing a major confirmation hurdle by backing away from a $1.8 billion IRS settlement deal that critics say gave preferential treatment to the Trump family.

According to Fox News and the New York Post, Blanche met demands to rescind or severely limit the scope of an audit immunity agreement the president negotiated with the IRS earlier this year. The move signals Blanche is willing to distance himself from the controversial arrangement to secure Senate confirmation.

Republicans have defended the settlement as routine, while Democrats and government watchdogs argue it represents special treatment that undermines IRS independence and public trust in tax enforcement. The settlement's existence became a flashpoint during Blanche's confirmation process, with senators questioning whether it violated norms around presidential conflicts of interest.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the outcome matters: how the IRS operates under a Trump-appointed AG will shape audit practices and enforcement priorities across the state. Conservatives generally support reining in IRS overreach, but the optics of a presidential family settlement—even if technically legal—handed ammunition to critics of executive power.
