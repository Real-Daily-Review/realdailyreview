# WHO Weighs Experimental Vaccines as Ebola Outbreak Spreads in DRC

The World Health Organization is considering deploying experimental vaccines and medicines to combat a rapidly spreading Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, as case counts climb sharply.

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said he was "deeply concerned" by the outbreak's speed and scale, according to The Guardian. Case counts vary by source: The Guardian reported at least 500 suspected cases and 130 deaths, while Al Jazeera cited WHO figures of 600 suspected cases and 139 suspected deaths. The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola.

The WHO warns that numbers are expected to rise further. Global health leaders are now evaluating whether investigational vaccines or treatments still in development could be deployed to contain the spread, though the sources do not detail which specific candidates are under consideration or timelines for potential use.

European officials have emphasized that the risk of an Ebola outbreak reaching Europe remains low, according to Al Jazeera, though the sources do not elaborate on their reasoning or what preventive measures may be in place.

Watch for announcements on whether the WHO will authorize emergency use of experimental vaccines and updates on case counts as the outbreak evolves.
