# Supreme Court Faces Intimidation Campaign as New Term Begins

The Supreme Court opens its 2024 term with major cases on gun rights, voter citizenship, and religious liberty—but justices are operating under siege from a coordinated left-wing pressure campaign that threatens judicial independence itself.

Justice Samuel Alito warned Sunday of "concerted" efforts to intimidate the Court, citing threats against justices and relentless public pressure tied to recent rulings (Washington Examiner). The Court's approval ratings have hit record lows, a metric that should alarm anyone who values an independent judiciary insulated from mob politics.

This term matters enormously for Colorado. The Court will hear cases affecting Second Amendment protections—critical for gun owners across the state—and voter citizenship rules that could reshape electoral integrity nationwide. Religious liberty cases also loom, with implications for faith-based organizations and schools.

Democrats and progressive activists have made no secret of their campaign: public protests, threats, and demands that justices reverse decisions they dislike. This is precisely the intimidation Alito flagged. Conservatives argue that a Supreme Court bowing to street pressure ceases to be a court at all—it becomes just another political body.

Republicans and constitutional conservatives say the answer is clear: defend judicial independence, reject intimidation, and let justices rule on law, not politics.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democrats—distances itself from intimidation tactics or doubles down on pressure campaigns against the Court.
