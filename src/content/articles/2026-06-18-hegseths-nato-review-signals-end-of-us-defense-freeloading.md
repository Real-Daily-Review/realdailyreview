# Pentagon Launches Europe Review: Hegseth Demands NATO Allies Stop Freeloading on U.S. Defense

War Secretary Pete Hegseth is forcing a reckoning with America's free-riding NATO allies, announcing a six-month Pentagon review of U.S. force deployment across Europe that could significantly reduce American military presence on the continent.

Hegseth, speaking at a NATO defense ministers meeting in Brussels, made clear the review—dubbed "NATO 3.0"—will examine whether the U.S. continues bankrolling European security while allies underfund their own defenses. The Pentagon intends to reduce forward-deployed forces, including fighter jets and strategic bombers, unless NATO members step up (Fox News, Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case is straightforward:** For decades, European nations have relied on American troops and taxpayer dollars to guarantee their security while spending far less on defense than they should. Hegseth's review reflects the Trump administration's position that this arrangement is unsustainable—especially as the U.S. faces rising threats in the Pacific and Middle East.

Democrats and European allies will likely argue that reducing U.S. presence destabilizes the continent and weakens the NATO alliance. But Republicans counter that burden-sharing must be real, not rhetorical.

**Colorado conservatives should watch whether this review leads to actual troop reductions and whether European nations finally meet their 2% GDP defense spending commitments—or if they continue gaming the system.**
