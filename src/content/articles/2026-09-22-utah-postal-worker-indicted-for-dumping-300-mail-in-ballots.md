# Utah Postal Worker Indicted for Dumping 300+ Mail-In Ballots—Colorado Should Pay Attention

A former U.S. Postal Service worker in Utah has been indicted for allegedly separating and discarding over 300 mail-in ballots intended for voters in Eagle Mountain ahead of the state's June primary (Fox News, Washington Examiner). Damon Matai Seei, 34, of Payson, Utah, allegedly removed the ballots from the mail stream within 90 minutes of his shift start and tossed them in a dumpster, according to federal prosecutors. He faces charges of unlawful secretion, destruction, and delay of mail.

The case underscores a persistent conservative concern: the vulnerability of mail-in voting systems to human error—or worse, deliberate misconduct. Republicans have long warned that expanded mail-in voting creates security gaps that bad actors can exploit, even if prosecutions like this one remain rare.

Colorado, which conducts elections almost entirely by mail, should take note. While state election officials tout security measures, this Utah indictment demonstrates that USPS workers handle ballots at scale, and accountability depends on federal law enforcement catching problems after the fact. The incident raises uncomfortable questions about whether mail-in ballot chains of custody are as airtight as election officials claim.

Taxpayers and election integrity advocates should watch whether the DOJ aggressively investigates similar incidents nationwide—or whether this case becomes a one-off prosecution that obscures a broader systemic risk.
