# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field at Assembly

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary field down to just two candidates through the state assembly process, a sharp culling that reflects the party's effort to consolidate behind viable nominees early (Complete Colorado).

Out of 18 total Republican hopefuls, only two advanced past the assembly threshold—a dramatic filtering mechanism that gives grassroots delegates significant power over who reaches the primary ballot. This approach stands in contrast to wide-open primaries that can fragment conservative votes and drain resources across multiple candidates.

The assembly system allows Colorado Republicans to signal party preference before general voters weigh in, potentially strengthening the eventual nominee's position against Democratic incumbents who have dominated statewide races in recent cycles. With Democrats controlling the governor's office and both U.S. Senate seats, Republicans face an uphill climb in 2026—making early consolidation strategically important.

The two advancing candidates will now compete in the primary, where they can appeal directly to registered Republicans statewide. This process gives the party a clearer message and unified resources heading into what will likely be a costly general election battle.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether the assembly's choice can unite the broader GOP base and articulate a compelling alternative to Democratic spending and regulatory expansion.
