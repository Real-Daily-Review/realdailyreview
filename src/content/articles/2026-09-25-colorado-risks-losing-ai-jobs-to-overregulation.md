# Colorado Should Embrace AI Jobs, Not Regulate Them Into Oblivion

Colorado has a chance to lead on artificial intelligence—if lawmakers resist the urge to strangle the industry with red tape.

The state's AI and data center sector is generating real job opportunities, but progressives are pushing apprenticeship mandates and regulatory frameworks that could slow growth before it starts. According to Complete Colorado, a recent Magellan Strategies survey shows Coloradans recognize genuine economic potential in AI development, yet the left keeps pumping the brakes with cautionary tales about job displacement.

The Colorado Sun's opinion piece advocates for AI-enabled apprenticeships—a reasonable idea on its surface. But conservatives worry such programs, if government-mandated, become another layer of bureaucratic overhead that discourages companies from hiring and training workers in the first place. Free-market apprenticeships already exist; forced compliance kills flexibility.

Here's what matters for Colorado taxpayers: the state's energy infrastructure, water resources, and business climate make it attractive to data centers and AI firms. Those jobs pay well and don't require shipping raw materials across the country. But if Denver and the legislature start treating AI like a threat requiring heavy regulation—rather than an opportunity requiring light-touch policy—companies will simply relocate to Texas or Arizona.

**Watch whether Colorado lawmakers push for AI-specific regulations or let market competition drive worker training and safety standards naturally.**
