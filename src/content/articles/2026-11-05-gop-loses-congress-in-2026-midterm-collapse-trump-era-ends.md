# GOP Faces Reckoning After 2026 Midterm Collapse

Republicans suffered a decisive defeat in the 2026 midterms, losing control of both chambers of Congress and handing Democrats significant new power to obstruct conservative governance. The Washington Examiner reports that President Trump's "golden age is over," with Democrats now commanding majorities in the House and Senate—including newly elected Democratic senators from traditionally red states like Texas, Kansas, and South Carolina. National Review and The Daily Wire describe the result as a "total collapse" of the Republican and Trump coalition, not merely a typical midterm correction.

**What conservatives argue:** Republicans contend that Trump's polarizing approach and divisive rhetoric cost the party winnable seats and energized Democratic turnout. The Washington Examiner notes that Trump "ended it himself," suggesting his performance and social-media conduct alienated swing voters. Conservatives warn that two years of divided government will mean Democratic investigations, impeachment threats, and legislative gridlock—precisely the brinkmanship that stalls pro-growth, pro-liberty reforms.

**What's next:** Watch whether Colorado Republicans—facing their own state-level challenges—distance themselves from Trump's brand or double down on populist messaging ahead of 2028.
