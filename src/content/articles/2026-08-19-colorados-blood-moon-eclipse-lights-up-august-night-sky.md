# Colorado Skywatchers Get Rare Treat: Partial Lunar Eclipse Turns Sturgeon Moon Blood Red

A partial lunar eclipse will paint Colorado's night sky this month, turning the full Sturgeon Moon a rusty red as Earth's shadow crosses the lunar face.

The eclipse occurs the night of August 27 and will be visible across most of the U.S., including Colorado, according to 9News Denver. The phenomenon occurs when the moon passes through Earth's shadow, creating what observers call a "blood moon"—a spooky, reddish hue visible to the naked eye (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

Coloradans won't need special equipment to catch the show, though clear skies will be essential. Cloud cover and light pollution could obscure the view, so rural locations away from Denver's urban glow offer the best vantage points.

This marks a rare celestial event for Front Range residents. Unlike solar eclipses, lunar eclipses are safe to observe directly without protective eyewear, making it an accessible outdoor activity for families across the state.

Stargazers should mark their calendars and check the forecast closer to late August—Colorado's summer monsoon season could cloud out the view.
