# Trump Secures Trade Deals in Asia, Shifts Focus Away from China-Dependent Supply Chains

President Trump is moving fast on trade deals in Southeast Asia, signing agreements with Thailand and the Philippines while meeting with Quad leaders at the ASEAN Summit in the Philippines this week. According to the New York Post, Trump called the Philippines deal "very big" and "magnificent for both" sides, noting the agreement came together quickly.

This matters for Colorado. American manufacturers and agricultural exporters—including Colorado's ranching and food-processing sectors—benefit when the administration opens new markets and reduces reliance on China-dependent supply chains. Republicans argue that bilateral trade deals, negotiated directly rather than through multilateral frameworks, give the U.S. stronger leverage and protect American workers and businesses from unfair competition.

The broader strategy reflects Trump's skepticism of the post-Cold War trade consensus that left American workers vulnerable to offshoring and currency manipulation. Conservatives say direct negotiations with individual nations allow the U.S. to demand reciprocal market access and enforce intellectual property protections more effectively than one-size-fits-all agreements.

Democrats have historically criticized bilateral deals as unpredictable and fragmented, though they've offered limited alternative trade strategies beyond the status quo.
