# Vance Signals GOP Shift Away From Free Markets—Conservatives Should Worry

Vice President JD Vance is openly embracing a more interventionist economic model, declaring that Republican policy is now "much more Alexander Hamilton than it is Milton Friedman"—and calling that departure "obviously a good thing" (Washington Examiner).

The distinction matters. Friedman's free-market tradition treated government intervention as the exception requiring justification. Hamilton's approach elevated a strong central government as the economic driver. Vance's endorsement signals the GOP is moving away from the limited-government, pro-market principles that have anchored conservative economics for decades.

For Colorado, this pivot carries real stakes. If Republicans embrace Hamiltonian industrial policy—government picking winners, subsidizing favored sectors, and managing markets—Colorado's energy sector, agriculture, and small businesses face unpredictable regulatory and fiscal terrain. What looks like "strategic" government support today becomes tomorrow's cronyism and market distortion.

Conservatives who built their case on free markets, deregulation, and individual economic liberty now face a Republican Party leadership openly rejecting that framework. The question isn't whether government *can* manage the economy—it's whether it *should*. History suggests it shouldn't.

Watch whether this Hamiltonian turn translates into actual policy: tariffs, industrial subsidies, and targeted government spending that benefit connected industries over open competition.
