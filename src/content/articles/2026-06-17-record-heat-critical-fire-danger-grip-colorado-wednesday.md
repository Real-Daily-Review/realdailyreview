# Colorado Faces Dangerous Fire Season as Record Heat Grips State

Denver and much of Colorado are bracing for near-record temperatures Wednesday, with the National Weather Service warning of critical fire danger across the western half of the state—a stark reminder that Colorado's dry climate demands smart land management, not more federal land grabs.

Highs are expected to reach the low 90s across the Front Range, marking some of the hottest temperatures recorded so far in 2026 (9News Denver). Breezy conditions will compound the threat, pushing fire danger to critical levels in western Colorado counties (KDVR Fox31).

**The conservative concern:** While weather happens, Colorado's vulnerability to catastrophic wildfires is exacerbated by decades of federal mismanagement of public lands. Overgrown forests choked with dead timber and undergrowth—the result of well-intentioned but counterproductive fire-suppression policies—turn natural heat into tinderboxes. Republicans and land-use conservatives argue that Colorado needs more active forest management, timber harvesting, and controlled burns, not more restrictions on private property or energy development that could fund forest restoration.

The state's ranchers, energy workers, and rural communities depend on sensible land stewardship. Instead, they often face regulatory obstacles to the very practices that reduce fire risk.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado's Democrat-led legislature will finally embrace forest thinning and timber sales as legitimate wildfire mitigation—or double down on restrictions that leave forests more dangerous.
