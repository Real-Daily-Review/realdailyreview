# DOJ Halts Canada Antitrust Cooperation as Trade War Escalates

The Justice Department's antitrust division has been ordered to freeze all cooperative work with Canada, signaling a sharp escalation in the U.S.-Canada trade conflict, according to Fox News and the New York Post.

Section chiefs in the antitrust division received instructions to halt ongoing collaboration and submit lists of all Canadian cooperation areas by end of business Friday. The freeze represents a significant breakdown in cross-border regulatory coordination at a moment when trade tensions between Washington and Ottawa are intensifying.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that protecting American economic interests—including domestic energy production, agricultural exports, and manufacturing—requires tough negotiating posture. Freezing bureaucratic entanglement with Canada can reduce regulatory friction that often favors foreign competitors over U.S. workers and businesses.

**Democrats counter** that severing antitrust cooperation undermines rule-of-law frameworks and invites retaliatory measures that could harm bilateral trade and investment.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado energy companies, agricultural exporters, and manufacturers depend on stable cross-border trade relationships. Any prolonged U.S.-Canada trade war could disrupt supply chains, raise input costs, and complicate market access for Colorado oil, gas, and agricultural producers who rely on Canadian partnerships.

Watch whether the freeze extends to other federal agencies or whether Congress moves to impose tariffs that would directly hit Colorado exporters.
