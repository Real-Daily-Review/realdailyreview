# Child Trafficking Prevention Bill Gains Traction as Bipartisan Priority

Colorado lawmakers should back federal legislation designed to protect vulnerable children in foster care from predators—a straightforward public-safety measure that transcends partisan divides.

The push centers on the Foster Care Child Trafficking Right to Representation Act, which would ensure that children in the system have legal representation to combat exploitation. The bill draws urgency from stark realities: according to advocates cited by the *Washington Examiner*, a child goes missing every 12 minutes, with foster youth disproportionately targeted by traffickers who exploit gaps in oversight and legal protection.

The case of Alicia Kozakiewicz—abducted at 13 after meeting an online predator, held captive, and ultimately rescued—illustrates both the horror and the possibility of intervention. Her family's determination to find her, combined with law enforcement action, saved her life. The proposed legislation aims to replicate that protective framework systematically for all vulnerable children.

For Colorado, this matters directly: the state's foster care system serves thousands of children annually, and trafficking networks actively exploit gaps in legal representation and case management. Conservatives and Republicans generally support targeted, efficient measures that protect children and hold predators accountable without expanding bureaucratic overhead.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republican members—co-sponsors this bill and pushes for swift passage.
