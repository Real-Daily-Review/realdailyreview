# Trump Rebrands AI as 'Super Intelligence'—But Colorado Should Focus on Real Tech Policy

President Trump has signed an executive order officially renaming "artificial intelligence" to "Super Intelligence," claiming the term "artificial" undersells the technology's actual power and capability. Trump teased the change during a state visit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and formalized it Tuesday at a White House event, saying the new branding reflects that the technology is "very powerful, it's very brilliant" and decidedly not artificial.

The move comes after Trump met with top AI leaders at the White House to discuss balancing innovation with regulation—a conversation conservatives generally welcome, given the left's appetite for heavy-handed AI oversight that could stifle American competitiveness.

**The conservative case:** Rebranding removes stigma from transformative technology and signals the administration's pro-innovation stance. Republicans argue regulatory restraint, not semantic games, drives American tech leadership against China.

**What matters for Colorado:** While the White House plays with nomenclature, Colorado's tech corridor, energy sector, and workforce development depend on actual AI policy—not renamed talking points. Colorado businesses and workers need clarity on federal AI regulation, data privacy rules, and how the administration will handle AI's impact on energy development and grid modernization.

Watch whether Trump's "Super Intelligence" branding translates into concrete deregulation that lets Colorado innovators compete globally—or remains a rhetorical flourish while Washington bureaucrats keep the rulebook thick.
