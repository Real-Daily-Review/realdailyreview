# Colorado Tourism Economy Hits Record Revenue—But Growth Is Stalling

Colorado's tourism sector posted record revenues this year, but the underlying numbers reveal troubling weakness that should concern state policymakers relying on hospitality taxes to fund government operations.

The state's $29.2 billion tourism economy grew again in 2026, according to the Colorado Sun, but the gains mask a concerning trend: visitation and spending growth have slowed to a crawl over the past two years. That's a red flag for a state that has increasingly leaned on tourism-dependent tax revenue to cover budget shortfalls without raising income or sales taxes directly.

The slowdown reflects broader economic headwinds—higher travel costs, consumer caution, and competition from other destinations. Colorado's tourism industry, while still robust, is no longer the growth engine it once was. For a state government that has grown accustomed to rising hospitality tax collections, flat or declining visitation poses a real problem.

Conservatives argue Colorado should resist the temptation to squeeze more revenue from an already-pressured tourism sector through new taxes or fees. Instead, the state should focus on controlling spending and removing regulatory barriers that make Colorado less competitive for visitors and tourism businesses.

**Watch whether state Democrats propose new tourism taxes or fees to offset slowing growth—a classic government response that would further burden the industry.**
