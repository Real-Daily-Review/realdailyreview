# U.S.-Mexico Colorado River Deal Locks in Western Water Cuts—Colorado Farmers Face Tighter Squeeze

The Biden administration has finalized a water-sharing agreement with Mexico that imposes additional cuts on the Colorado River, following steep reductions already imposed on Arizona, Nevada, and California. The deal prioritizes environmental compliance and international obligations over the water security of Colorado's agricultural economy.

Colorado farmers and water districts are bracing for impact. The state depends on Colorado River allocations for irrigation across the Western Slope and beyond—cuts here mean reduced flows for ranches, orchards, and rural communities that have already absorbed years of drought-driven scarcity. Republicans and water-rights advocates argue the administration should have prioritized domestic agricultural needs and state sovereignty over international agreements that further constrain Colorado's water supply.

The negotiations themselves drew criticism from CPR News, which reported that states sharing the river "failed to forge a deal" and questioned the transparency of closed-door talks. Conservatives worry that backroom negotiations between federal bureaucrats and foreign governments are determining Colorado's water future without adequate input from local stakeholders and elected representatives.

The deal reflects the Biden administration's emphasis on environmental mandates and international commitments—priorities that often conflict with the practical needs of Colorado's farming and ranching communities.
