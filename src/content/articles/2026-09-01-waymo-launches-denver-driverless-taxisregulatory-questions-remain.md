# Waymo's Driverless Taxis Hit Denver Streets—But Questions Loom on Safety, Liability, and Regulation

Waymo launched its driverless taxi service in Denver this week, operating across 60 square miles of the metro area (Colorado Sun, 9News Denver). The rollout marks another step toward autonomous vehicle deployment in Colorado's largest city—and raises urgent questions conservatives should be asking about liability, insurance frameworks, and whether state and local regulators are ready.

While tech enthusiasts tout efficiency gains, Republicans and taxpayer advocates worry about the regulatory vacuum. Who bears liability when a driverless car causes an accident? What insurance requirements apply? How are Denver and Colorado ensuring public safety without strangling innovation through overregulation? The state's current autonomous vehicle framework remains murky, and neither Denver city government nor state lawmakers have clearly spelled out enforcement mechanisms or consumer protections.

Waymo's presence also highlights Colorado's tech-sector ambitions—a potential economic bright spot if regulations stay light and predictable. But conservatives rightly question whether local officials will resist the urge to impose new taxes, fees, or restrictions on autonomous fleets under the guise of "public safety."

Watch whether Denver or Colorado legislature moves to impose special licensing fees, congestion charges, or data-collection mandates on driverless operators.
