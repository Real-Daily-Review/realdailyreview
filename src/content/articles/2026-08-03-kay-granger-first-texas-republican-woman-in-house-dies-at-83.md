# Republican Pioneer Kay Granger, Texas House Legend, Dies at 83

Kay Granger, the first Republican woman elected to the U.S. House from Texas, has died at 83, marking the loss of a trailblazer who served nearly three decades in Congress (Fox News Politics). Granger represented Fort Worth and became the city's first female mayor before her long House career, breaking significant barriers for conservative women in elected office.

House Speaker Mike Johnson honored her legacy, noting she "broke barriers" throughout her career (Washington Examiner). Her nearly 30-year tenure in the House made her a fixture of Texas Republican politics and a mentor to younger GOP members.

Granger's death represents the passing of a generation of Republican women who fought their way into leadership when few doors were open. She proved that conservative principles and effective governance weren't the exclusive domain of men—a lesson the GOP continues to build on today as it recruits and elevates women candidates nationwide.

Republicans should watch whether her Fort Worth seat remains firmly in GOP hands and how party leadership uses her legacy to energize female voters heading into 2024.
