# Colorado Voters Keep Democrats on a Short Leash

Colorado voters are sending a clear message: elect Democrats to statewide office, then block their agenda at the ballot box. According to Complete Colorado, Coloradans have developed a pattern of ticket-splitting—voting for Democratic candidates while simultaneously using citizen initiatives to protect themselves from Democratic policy priorities.

This reflects a fundamental disconnect between Colorado's electoral preferences and its policy preferences. Voters are willing to elect Democrats to executive and legislative positions, yet they consistently use the ballot initiative process—a direct democracy tool—to constrain what those elected officials can actually do.

The strategy reveals voter skepticism of one-party Democratic control. Rather than trust the legislature and governor to govern responsibly, Coloradans have taken matters into their own hands, using initiatives to lock in protections against tax hikes, regulatory overreach, and other progressive priorities that might otherwise pass through the statehouse.

This is a warning sign for Democrats: winning elections doesn't guarantee governing latitude when voters fundamentally distrust your policy direction. It's also a reminder that Colorado's initiative process remains a powerful check on government overreach—and that taxpayers will use it.

Watch whether Democrats attempt to restrict Colorado's citizen initiative process in response to repeated ballot defeats.
