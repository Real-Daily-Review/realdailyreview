# Vandals Deface WWII Memorial as D.C. Monument Desecration Continues

Vandals struck the World War II Memorial on the National Mall, spray-painting "clean hands, dirty money" in pink and sabotaging the fountain with dish soap to create overflowing bubbles, according to Fox News and the Washington Examiner. U.S. Park Police launched an investigation into the incident.

The Interior Department confirmed to the Washington Examiner that the bubbles resulted from deliberate vandalism, not any authorized cleaning operation. This marks another attack on a major D.C. monument, continuing a pattern of desecration that has plagued the nation's capital.

The timing is notable: Just The News reports the incident comes amid President Trump's stated efforts to beautify Washington and restore existing structures. Conservatives argue that such vandalism reflects broader cultural contempt for American history and institutions—and that adequate security and swift prosecution are essential to protecting monuments honoring those who sacrificed for the nation.

Colorado taxpayers and veterans should note that federal monument maintenance and security ultimately fall on the National Park Service budget, raising questions about resource allocation and enforcement priorities in protecting America's most sacred public spaces.

Watch for whether D.C. authorities pursue charges aggressively or whether this joins the growing list of unsolved monument vandalism cases.
