# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, June 19, 2026

Colorado woke up this week to grief, geopolitical uncertainty, and a Congress still sorting out who actually holds the constitutional reins. Here's what every Colorado conservative needs to know heading into the weekend.

---

## Colorado Mourns: Larimer County Sheriff, Deputy Among 3 Killed in Idaho Crash

Three Colorado motorcyclists are dead following a crash in Idaho, and a Colorado driver has been arrested in connection with the incident. Among the victims: a Larimer County sheriff and a deputy — public servants who dedicated their lives to protecting their communities. This is a gut-punch for Northern Colorado. Coloradans should hold the families of these officers and the third victim in their thoughts this weekend, and demand accountability in the legal proceedings ahead.

---

## Trump's Iran Deal: A Win on Paper, or a Negotiator's Trap?

A new Reagan Institute Summer Survey shows Americans are split on Iran — 39% favor a negotiated settlement, 36% back regime change. Critics on the right are already raising alarms, pointing out that Trump himself has said Iran never wins a war but never loses a negotiation — and warning this deal may prove his own adage correct at America's expense. For Colorado's defense and energy communities, a nuclear-capable or sanctions-relieved Iran is a direct threat to regional stability and global oil markets that hit close to home at the pump.

---

## Congress Handed Away Its War Powers — Again

Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), with nothing left to lose after Trump backed his primary opponent, cast a vote this week that puts a spotlight on one of Washington's most chronic failures: Congress surrendering its constitutional war-making authority without a fight. Conservatives have long argued that Article I powers belong to the legislature, not the executive — regardless of which party sits in the White House. Colorado's congressional delegation should be on record about where they stand on restoring that balance before the next conflict forces the question.

---

## Rural Colorado's Moment: AI Infrastructure and the Energy Opportunity

For decades, rural Americans were told their industries were relics — "learn to code" was the smug dismissal from coastal elites and regulators who systematically strangled coal, oil, ranching, and manufacturing. Now, the AI revolution demands massive, reliable power — and rural America sits on exactly the energy resources needed to deliver it. Colorado's rural communities, energy workers, and ranchers have a real opportunity here, but only if state and federal regulators get out of the way and let responsible energy development proceed at the pace the market demands.

---

## Denver Runway Scare: WestJet Evacuated After Blown Tires

Passengers aboard a WestJet flight were evacuated on a Denver International Airport runway Thursday after two landing gear tires shredded just before takeoff. No serious injuries were reported. DIA remains one of Colorado's most critical economic arteries — any safety incident deserves a full, transparent investigation, and travelers deserve straight answers from the airline and airport authority without bureaucratic delay.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Iran negotiations — the details of any agreement will matter enormously for U.S. credibility, energy prices, and national security, and Colorado conservatives should pressure their delegation to demand congressional review before any deal is finalized. Back home, watch how the Larimer County crash investigation develops and whether Colorado's courts deliver a sentence in the Highway 93 DUI case that actually reflects the gravity of taking a life behind the wheel. And if you're in Denver this weekend, Juneteenth celebrations are underway across the city — a good reminder that freedom is worth celebrating, in every form it takes.
