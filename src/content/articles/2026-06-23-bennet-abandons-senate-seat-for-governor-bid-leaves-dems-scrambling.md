# Bennet's Senate Seat Up for Grabs as He Chases Governor's Office

Colorado Democrats face an open-seat scramble in 2027 after U.S. Sen. Michael Bennet pivoted to the governor's race, leaving his Senate replacement to a primary vote that effectively amounts to a Democratic coronation.

Bennet, who has built his political brand on government experience and elected office, is now betting his record as senator translates to the statehouse. But his departure creates a vacuum: Colorado Sun reports that Bennet has "narrowed" his successor search to roughly 500,000 people—a euphemism for letting primary voters decide. The Denver Post notes Bennet's campaign emphasizes his long tenure in government roles, a pitch that may resonate with the Democratic base but rings hollow to conservatives skeptical of career politicians.

Republicans see opportunity. A competitive Senate primary on the Democratic side could drain resources and expose fractures in the party's bench. Conservatives argue that Bennet's exit underscores the problem with entrenched incumbents: they treat elected office as a stepping stone rather than a public trust.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans field a credible Senate challenger—and whether Bennet's gubernatorial ambitions survive scrutiny of his voting record on inflation, border security, and energy policy.
