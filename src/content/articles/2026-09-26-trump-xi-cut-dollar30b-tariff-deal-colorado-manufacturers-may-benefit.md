# Trump, Xi Cut $30B Tariff Deal—Colorado Manufacturers Could See Lower Input Costs

President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping finalized a landmark tariff reduction on $30 billion in goods during this week's Washington summit, marking a significant shift in the administration's trade posture toward Beijing.

The agreement slashes duties on small appliances, holiday decorations, toys, and children's car seats—categories that Colorado manufacturers and retailers have long complained drive up production and inventory costs (Fox News, Just The News). The deal also includes a two-year coal agreement and advances AI cooperation talks, signaling Trump's willingness to negotiate rather than escalate the trade war that defined his first term (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the tariff reduction reflects Trump's pragmatic dealmaking—lowering costs for American consumers and businesses without surrendering leverage on strategic sectors. Lower input tariffs could ease pressure on Colorado's small manufacturers and retailers struggling with supply-chain inflation.

**What to watch:** Whether this tariff détente extends to energy goods and raw materials critical to Colorado's mining and manufacturing base, or if Trump reserves tariff leverage for sectors he deems strategically vital to U.S. interests.
