# Federal Overreach on Endangered Species Threatens Colorado Property Rights and Housing

The Biden administration's expansive interpretation of the Endangered Species Act has quietly strangled private land development across America—and Colorado landowners are paying the price.

The Fish and Wildlife Service and National Marine Fisheries Service have wielded the ESA far beyond its original intent, using a now-rescinded "harm definition" to regulate private property with minimal oversight. That sweeping authority has made it nearly impossible for developers to build affordable housing in sensitive areas, effectively treating housing as the real endangered species (New York Post).

**The conservative case is straightforward:** The ESA was never designed to give unelected bureaucrats veto power over private land use. Republicans argue the rescinded harm definition represented regulatory overreach that chilled development, killed jobs, and priced ordinary Americans out of homeownership. National Review notes the FWS and NMFS were never meant to have this level of authority.

Colorado ranchers, developers, and property owners have long complained that ESA enforcement strangles productive use of their land. When a species listing can halt a housing project or ranch expansion indefinitely, the burden falls on working Coloradans—not Washington.

**Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for ESA reform that restores property rights and streamlines species protections without sacrificing economic growth.**
