# Trump's Russian Diesel Deal Sparks Rare Bipartisan Backlash—Colorado Energy Sector Watches Closely

President Trump's announced deal to import 800,000 tons of Russian diesel over two months has triggered an unusual alliance of Republican and Democratic senators demanding he "immediately reverse course," raising questions about energy policy and sanctions enforcement that could ripple through Colorado's oil and gas economy.

Six senators—crossing party lines—have formally opposed the arrangement. Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) told NBC's *Meet the Press* that lifting sanctions on Russian diesel is "likely illegal," citing the Senate's unanimous 2022 vote to ban Russian energy imports (Breitbart). Republicans argue the deal undermines U.S. energy independence and contradicts Congress's explicit ban on Russian fuel.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's energy sector—already navigating federal regulatory headwinds—faces uncertainty if Trump's Russia policy signals a shift toward cheaper foreign energy over domestic production. Lower diesel prices might ease transportation costs for ranchers and businesses, but conservatives worry the move signals weakness on sanctions and could undercut American energy workers' competitiveness.

Democrats, predictably, are seizing on the move as proof of Trump's Russia ties, though their energy-independence rhetoric rings hollow given their own hostility toward domestic oil and gas development.

**Watch next:** Whether Congress moves to block the deal legislatively or if Trump reverses course under pressure—and what this means for Colorado's energy workforce heading into 2026.
