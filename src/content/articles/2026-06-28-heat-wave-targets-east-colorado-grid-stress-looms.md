# Heat Wave Headed East; Colorado Likely Spared Worst of It

A dangerous heat wave will pummel the central and eastern United States this week ahead of July Fourth, with the National Weather Service warning that high humidity will make temperatures feel even more oppressive than the thermometer reads (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The brunt of the heat is expected to spare Colorado, which typically enjoys lower humidity and cooler mountain elevations compared to eastern states. However, Front Range communities and lower elevations could still see elevated temperatures and increased demand on power grids—a reminder that Colorado's energy infrastructure, increasingly strained by progressive policies favoring unreliable renewables over dispatchable natural gas and coal plants, faces real-world stress during peak demand periods.

Republicans and energy advocates have long warned that Colorado's aggressive renewable mandates and coal plant retirements leave the state vulnerable to summer blackouts when air conditioning demand spikes. The state's grid operator, Public Service Company of Colorado, has acknowledged capacity concerns, yet Democrats continue pushing accelerated coal phase-outs without adequate backup generation.

Coloradans should monitor whether rolling blackouts or grid stress occur during this heat event—and whether state lawmakers acknowledge the connection between ideological energy policy and grid reliability.
