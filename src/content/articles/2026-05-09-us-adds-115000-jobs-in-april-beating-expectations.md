# U.S. Adds 115,000 Jobs in April as Unemployment Holds Steady

The Labor Department reported Friday that the U.S. added 115,000 jobs in April while unemployment remained flat, exceeding analyst expectations, according to PBS NewsHour. The figure surpassed the Dow Jones consensus forecast of 55,000 new jobs, according to CNBC.

However, the stronger headline number masked underlying concerns. CNBC reported that the jobs report contained "several red flags for the economy," suggesting weakness beneath the surface gains.

Mohamed El-Erian, a Wharton School professor and chief economic advisor at Allianz, discussed the implications of the data with PBS NewsHour, though specific details of his analysis were not provided in available excerpts.

The mixed signals—robust job creation paired with cautionary indicators—reflect ongoing uncertainty about the economy's trajectory as inflation, interest rates, and consumer spending remain focal points for policymakers and investors.

Watch for the Federal Reserve's response to the April employment data and whether officials adjust their interest-rate outlook at upcoming meetings.
