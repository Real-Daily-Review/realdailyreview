# Colorado Election System Exposes Ranked-Choice Voting Flaws

Colorado's recent election chaos under Secretary of State Jena Griswold's watch has handed conservatives a powerful argument against the state's ranked-choice voting experiment: the system allows candidates to win with minority support when voters are fractured across a crowded field.

According to Complete Colorado, the Griswold fiasco demonstrates how ranked-choice voting can produce winners that don't reflect broad voter preference—a vulnerability that even national Democrats are now acknowledging. National Review reports that the DNC itself is reconsidering the ranked-choice model, recognizing the system's structural perils.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Colorado's progressive election "reforms" have created chaos instead of clarity. Ranked-choice voting was sold as more democratic, but it's delivered the opposite—allowing candidates with narrow bases to game the system while genuine consensus candidates lose. Republicans argue the state should return to simple plurality voting that produces clear winners and transparent results.

For Colorado taxpayers and voters, this matters enormously. Election integrity and voter confidence are foundational. If the system is rigged—even unintentionally—toward minority-preferred outcomes, it delegitimizes results and erodes trust in democracy itself.

**Watch whether Colorado Republicans push for legislation to scrap ranked-choice voting before the next statewide election cycle.**
