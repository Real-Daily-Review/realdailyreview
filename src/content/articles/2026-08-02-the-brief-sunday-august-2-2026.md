# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Sunday, August 2, 2026

Colorado turns 150 years old today — and the Centennial State has never had more to fight for. From record heat baking the Front Range to a presidency being tested by war in the Middle East, here's what every Colorado conservative needs to know this morning.

---

## 🎂 Happy 150th, Colorado

The Centennial State hits its sesquicentennial today, and it's worth pausing to remember what made Colorado great: rugged individualism, wide-open land, a work ethic built on ranching, mining, and enterprise — not government programs. One hundred fifty years of statehood is a reminder that Colorado's best chapters were written by free people making free choices. The left's vision of a regulated, taxed, and managed Colorado is a betrayal of that legacy. Today, we celebrate the real Colorado.

---

## 🌡️ Triple-Digit Heat Hammers the Front Range

Denver is under a Weather Impact Alert Sunday and Monday, with temperatures expected to hit 100 degrees both days. Limited cloud cover and scarce moisture mean little relief until Tuesday. For Colorado ranchers, outdoor workers, and families already stretched by high utility costs, this kind of sustained heat is no small matter. It's also a reminder that reliable, affordable energy — not the intermittent wind and solar the left insists on — is what keeps Coloradans safe when temperatures spike.

---

## 🇺🇸 The Iran War Is Consuming Washington

President Trump has threatened additional strikes on Iran as the U.S. works to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and the conflict is increasingly dominating his presidency. Conservatives broadly support a strong posture against the Iranian regime — but the scope and duration of military engagement is a question every taxpayer and policymaker must take seriously. For Colorado's energy sector, the Strait of Hormuz matters directly: prolonged closure means oil price volatility that ripples from global markets straight to the pump prices Coloradans pay every week.

---

## 🔒 Terrorist Deportation Sets Legal Precedent

The Department of Justice is using the U.S. Alien Terrorist Removal Court — a rarely invoked legal mechanism — to deport an Afghan national from Texas who, according to FBI Director Kash Patel, "supported a plot by ISIS-sympathizing family members to commit an act of terrorism in America." It's the first time DOJ has used this court for such a case. Republicans argue this is exactly the kind of enforcement tool the federal government should be using, and Colorado conservatives should be watching whether the state's congressional delegation supports expanding its use.

---

## 💸 Socialist Legislator Begs for Rent Money Before $142K Salary Kicks In

Brian Romero, a newly elected democratic socialist headed to a state legislature seat that pays $142,000 a year, posted a GoFundMe asking for $2,000 to cover rent and groceries — then deleted it after public backlash. The episode is a perfect encapsulation of the modern progressive politician: demanding higher taxes and bigger government for everyone else while personally struggling to manage basic finances. Coloradans watching their own Democratic-controlled legislature spend freely should file this one away.

---

## What to Watch

Keep an eye on how the Iran conflict affects oil prices and what that means for Colorado's energy producers and consumers heading into the week. Back home, the triple-digit heat will test the reliability of Colorado's increasingly green-tilted grid — watch for any strain on power supplies Monday. And as Colorado marks 150 years of statehood, conservatives should be asking a pointed question: is the direction Denver's political class is taking this state worthy of its founding spirit?
