# Senate Filibuster Blocks SAVE America Act as GOP Pushes Immigration Reform

The Republican-backed SAVE America Act remains stalled in the Senate, with Gov. Ron DeSantis saying there's "basically zero" chance it passes before the chamber's recess, according to Just The News. The 60-vote filibuster threshold—a Democratic tool for blocking legislation—has effectively killed momentum on the bill, which aims to tighten immigration enforcement and border security.

The holdup underscores a core conservative frustration: Senate Democrats can block Republican priorities without offering alternatives. Republicans argue the SAVE America Act addresses real border security gaps and illegal immigration enforcement, yet Democrats have shown little appetite for supporting it.

The stalled legislation also highlights divisions within the Democratic Party itself. In Minnesota's Senate primary, Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan is attacking Rep. Angie Craig—a more centrist Democrat—for voting *for* the Laken Riley Act, a similar immigration enforcement measure (Washington Examiner). Flanagan, a progressive, is using Craig's vote as evidence she's too moderate for the party's base.

For Colorado, where illegal immigration and border security remain top voter concerns, the Senate's inaction signals that immigration reform will remain a campaign issue heading into 2026. Republicans should watch whether Senate Democrats offer any counter-proposal on border enforcement—or whether they simply continue blocking GOP bills while hoping voters forget.
