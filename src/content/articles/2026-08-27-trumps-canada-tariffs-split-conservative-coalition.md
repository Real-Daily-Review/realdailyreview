# Trump's Canada Tariffs Draw Fire From Right—and Unexpected Support From Maine Dem

President Trump's tariffs on Canadian goods are fracturing his own coalition, with conservative critics questioning whether protectionist measures against America's largest trading partner serve the stated mission of confronting China.

National Review argues the tariffs miss the mark, noting Trump was elected partly to reorient U.S. trade policy toward Beijing—not Ottawa. The move has created an unusual political inversion: a Maine Democrat is backing Trump's tariff approach while Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine) urges negotiations instead of escalation (Just The News).

For Colorado, tariffs on Canadian energy imports and agricultural goods could ripple through the state's economy. Colorado ranchers and farmers rely on cross-border trade, while energy producers face uncertainty over Canadian oil and gas dynamics. Higher input costs and retaliatory measures could squeeze margins across the state.

**The conservative case against:** Blanket tariffs on allies distract from the real threat—Chinese trade practices and intellectual property theft. Protectionism raises costs for American consumers and businesses, undermining free-market principles conservatives champion.

**Democrats' angle:** Some see political opportunity in tariff pain, though the Maine Democrat's support suggests the issue cuts across party lines in border states.
