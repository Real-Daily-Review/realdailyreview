# Trump's Caucasus Win Shows Europe Still Stuck in Old Playbook

The Trump administration's brokering of a peace framework between Armenia and Azerbaijan exposes Europe's irrelevance in regional diplomacy—a lesson Colorado voters should note as they weigh America's global standing.

According to the Washington Examiner, when European leaders convened in Yerevan, Armenia, in May, Azerbaijani President Ilham Aliyev delivered the summit's sharpest rebuke via video link, criticizing the European Parliament's approach to South Caucasus stability. The message was clear: Europe's outdated diplomatic posture has ceded ground to American leadership.

Conservatives argue that Trump's success here demonstrates the value of pragmatic, results-oriented foreign policy over the moralizing rhetoric that has defined European and Biden-era approaches. Rather than lecturing regional players about process, the Trump framework prioritized actual peace outcomes—a model that resonates with Colorado's preference for no-nonsense governance.

The contrast matters for Colorado taxpayers. While Europe dithers over procedural niceties, American credibility and influence expand, strengthening U.S. leverage in critical regions. That translates to better terms for American interests, from energy markets to security partnerships.

Watch whether the incoming administration applies this same pragmatic approach to other frozen conflicts and whether European allies finally recognize that American leadership—not consensus-building committees—moves the needle.
