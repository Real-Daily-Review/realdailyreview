# Trump's Lake America Rename Draws Backlash From Native American Leaders, But Google Complies

President Trump's executive order renaming Lake Ontario to "Lake America" has sparked immediate pushback from the Seneca Nation, which argues the move violates the 1794 Canandaigua Treaty—a foundational agreement protecting tribal sovereignty and land rights (Fox News). The Seneca Nation's leadership called the renaming "unpresidential" and demanded reversal.

Despite the controversy, Google Maps began displaying "Lake America" for U.S. users on Saturday, complying with Trump's Thursday executive order directing the Interior Department to update the federal Geographic Names Information System (GNIS), the database governing official U.S. place names (The Daily Wire). The rename appears tied to Trump's escalating trade tensions with Canada.

**The conservative case:** Trump supporters argue the president has broad executive authority over federal naming conventions and that reasserting American sovereignty over shared waters reflects nationalist priorities. Critics—including tribal leaders—counter that unilateral renaming ignores treaty obligations and tribal consultation, setting a troubling precedent for federal overreach on indigenous affairs.

**Colorado angle:** While this primarily affects the Northeast, it signals how Trump's second term may handle federal land and resource management—an issue directly affecting Colorado's water compacts, public lands, and energy development.

Watch whether Congress or courts challenge the GNIS update, or if other federal agencies follow suit with similar rebranding initiatives.
