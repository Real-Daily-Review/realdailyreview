# Colorado's Amendment 87 Contains a Hidden Tax-Bracket Trap for Taxpayers

Colorado voters face a deceptively worded tax proposal that could quietly drain wallets for decades. Amendment 87, which modifies the state's income tax structure, omits a critical safeguard: its tax brackets are not indexed to inflation, according to *Complete Colorado*. This means as wages rise with inflation, more Coloradans will be pushed into higher tax brackets without any legislative action—a stealth tax increase that compounds year after year.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this design flaw transforms Amendment 87 into a perpetual revenue grab. Without inflation indexing, middle-class earners will face bracket creep, paying higher effective tax rates on the same real income. The proposal's supporters have not adequately explained why this protection was excluded.

Meanwhile, *Denver Post* reporting highlights that Colorado's election security remains robust: only three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024 out of millions cast, and both were caught. Amendment 84, which would require Social Security numbers for mail-in voting, could tighten verification further—though critics note the current system's "many checks and balances" already function effectively.

For Colorado taxpayers and small-business owners, the Amendment 87 bracket trap deserves scrutiny before November: watch whether proponents add inflation indexing language or defend the omission.
