# Trump Taps Stephen Miller as Chief of Staff After Midterm Losses

Trump is cleaning house at the White House, replacing Susie Wiles with hardline immigration and policy hawk Stephen Miller after Republicans lost control of Congress in the midterms.

Wiles, who served 21 months as Trump's longest-serving chief of staff across both administrations, resigned Thursday evening (Fox News). Trump announced Friday that Miller, currently deputy chief of staff for policy, will assume the role. According to the Washington Examiner, Trump called Miller "one of my most trusted advisors."

The shake-up signals a potential rightward shift in Trump's second-term agenda. Miller, a fierce immigration restrictionist and architect of Trump's first-term border policies, has long pushed for aggressive executive action on border security and regulatory reform—priorities that align with conservative demands for stricter immigration enforcement and reduced federal overreach.

For Colorado, Miller's elevation matters: his hardline stance on immigration could affect labor markets in agriculture, construction, and energy sectors where undocumented workers are prevalent. Conservatives will watch whether Miller pushes Trump toward bolder deregulation of energy development and water policy—areas where Colorado's economy intersects federal control.

The real test: whether Miller can translate Trump's midterm mandate into concrete policy wins or whether internal West Wing friction will slow the agenda.
