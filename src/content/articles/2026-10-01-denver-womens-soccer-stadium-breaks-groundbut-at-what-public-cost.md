# Denver Women's Soccer Team Finally Breaks Ground on Long-Delayed Stadium

After years of delays, Denver Summit FC is moving forward with its permanent home—a 12,500-seat stadium at Santa Fe Yards near Interstate 25 and Broadway, with an expected opening by the 2029 season (Colorado Sun, 9News Denver).

The groundbreaking marks a milestone for the National Women's Soccer League franchise, which has operated without a permanent venue since its 2023 launch. The Santa Fe Yards project represents private development on Denver real estate, though conservatives should scrutinize whether any public subsidies, tax abatements, or city land deals sweetened the arrangement—details not disclosed in initial reporting.

Women's professional soccer remains a niche market in Colorado. While the team's arrival signals Denver's growth as a sports hub, taxpayers deserve transparency on whether municipal resources supported the project. Denver's track record of stadium deals (see: Broncos, Rockies) shows how easily public money gets funneled to private sports ventures under the guise of "economic development."

The stadium's completion timeline—three years out—gives Colorado conservatives time to demand full disclosure of any public financing or regulatory favors granted to Summit FC before the project breaks ground on taxpayer goodwill.

**Watch whether Denver City Council releases a full accounting of public subsidies, tax breaks, or land contributions to the Santa Fe Yards development.**
