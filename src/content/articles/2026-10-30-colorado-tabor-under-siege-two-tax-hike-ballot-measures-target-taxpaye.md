# Colorado's TABOR Under Fire as Tax-Hike Ballot Measures Loom

Colorado's Taxpayer's Bill of Rights—the constitutional provision that has protected taxpayers from unchecked government spending for decades—faces a direct assault in November through two ballot measures that would weaken the state's fiscal guardrails.

Amendment 87 would raise taxes on higher earners, ostensibly to fund schools, childcare, and healthcare. Proposition NN would redirect taxpayer refunds—money that belongs to Coloradans under TABOR—straight into public education coffers. Democrats argue the state faces a budget crisis requiring new revenue; Republicans counter that Colorado's spending problem isn't a revenue problem, and that both measures represent a fundamental breach of the taxpayer protections enshrined in TABOR since 1992.

According to the Colorado Sun, these measures directly challenge TABOR's core mechanism: the automatic refund of surplus revenue to taxpayers. Supporters of the amendments claim the state cannot adequately fund schools and services without breaking TABOR's constraints. Conservatives argue this is precisely the point—government should live within its means, not raid taxpayer wallets whenever bureaucrats claim a crisis.

For Colorado families and businesses already burdened by inflation and rising costs, these measures represent a choice: trust government to spend responsibly, or protect the constitutional limits that have kept Colorado competitive.

Watch whether voters reject these tax-hike proposals or signal willingness to abandon TABOR protections.
