# Ohio Dem Dodges Debate, Handlers Shut Down Press

When a candidate's campaign staff physically blocks cameras and refuses to answer basic questions about debate avoidance, voters deserve to know why.

Ohio Democratic gubernatorial candidate Amy Acton's handlers confronted a cameraman this week, demanding "no pictures" when pressed on her refusal to debate Republican Vivek Ramaswamy (Fox News, New York Post). The exchange reveals a troubling pattern: progressive candidates avoiding direct confrontation with opponents on the issues.

Republicans argue that debate avoidance signals weakness on policy. Ramaswamy's team has called out Acton for dodging the stage, suggesting she fears scrutiny of her record. Democrats counter that selective debate participation is standard campaign strategy, though they rarely frame it that way publicly.

The incident underscores a broader conservative concern: when media-friendly Democrats face tough questions, their first instinct is often to shut down the camera rather than defend their positions. Acton's handlers' aggressive response—rather than a simple "we'll consider it"—suggests the campaign knows debate exposure could hurt.

For Colorado conservatives, this Ohio race matters as a bellwether. If progressive candidates can dodge debates and suppress press access without electoral consequence, expect the same playbook in 2026 gubernatorial and Senate races here.
