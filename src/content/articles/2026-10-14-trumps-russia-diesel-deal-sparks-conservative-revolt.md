# Trump's Russia Diesel Deal Draws Fire From His Own Party

President Trump's agreement to import Russian diesel fuel has sparked rare bipartisan backlash, with conservative outlets and Republican senators calling it a strategic blunder that strengthens Putin while weakening American leverage.

A bipartisan group of senators sent a letter demanding Trump scrap the deal, calling it a "betrayal" of U.S. allies (Fox News). The agreement would bring over 300,000 tons of immediate Russian diesel supply to American and global markets, with additional millions of tons to follow—ostensibly to lower fuel prices.

But conservatives aren't buying it. National Review argues the deal signals "domestic political weakness" to Moscow and Beijing, offering them a roadmap for future pressure campaigns. Another National Review piece calls it a "gross abuse of power" that delivers "an absolute coup for Putin" while yielding no economic benefit to America. The Washington Examiner editorial board dismissed it as a "dud," questioning whether the fuel-price rationale justifies the geopolitical cost.

The core conservative complaint: Trump has handed Putin a propaganda win and legitimacy at a moment when energy independence and allied unity matter most. Relying on Russian energy contradicts years of Republican rhetoric about energy security and countering Moscow's influence.
