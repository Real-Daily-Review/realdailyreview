# Douglas County Voters Face Power-Grab Ballot Measure on Commissioner Expansion

Douglas County taxpayers will decide Nov. 3 whether to expand the county commission from three to five members—a move that could bloat government and increase costs without clear justification.

The ballot initiative, now facing legal challenge set for Thursday, would also alter how commissioners are elected. Conservatives argue that adding two more commissioners expands the bureaucracy and the tax burden on residents already squeezed by Colorado's rising cost of living. Proponents haven't made a compelling case that Douglas County's current three-member structure is inadequate to serve the county's needs.

The timing matters: Douglas County has seen explosive growth, and commissioners will control land-use decisions affecting ranches, development, and property rights. More commissioners could mean more regulation, longer approval processes, and higher government overhead—classic government bloat dressed up as "representation."

The legal challenge suggests serious questions about the initiative's validity. Taxpayers should scrutinize whether this expansion serves the public or simply entrenches more government positions and spending.

**Watch whether the court blocks this measure or whether Douglas County voters reject it outright—a referendum on whether Coloradans still believe in lean, efficient government.**
