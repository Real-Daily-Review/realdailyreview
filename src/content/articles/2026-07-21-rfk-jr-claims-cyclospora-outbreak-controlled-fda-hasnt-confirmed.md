# RFK Jr. Claims Cyclospora Outbreak "Under Control"—But FDA Hasn't Confirmed His Story

Robert F. Kennedy Jr., now leading the Department of Health and Human Services, declared the cyclospora outbreak "under control" and claimed the source has been identified—yet the FDA has not confirmed a positive lab result backing up his assertion (Fox News Politics).

Meanwhile, 9News Denver reports the FDA continues linking lettuce to the growing cyclosporiasis outbreak, suggesting the situation remains unresolved despite Kennedy's optimistic pronouncement.

The disconnect raises questions about transparency and coordination between Kennedy's HHS and the FDA. Conservatives have backed Kennedy's appointment as a skeptic of federal health bureaucracy, but this mixed messaging undermines confidence in government food-safety communications. Colorado consumers and produce distributors need clarity: Is the outbreak contained or not? What's the actual source?

Republicans argue Kennedy brings needed scrutiny to bloated health agencies. Democrats counter that his anti-establishment posture may be creating confusion when Americans need straight answers about food safety.

Taxpayers should watch whether Kennedy's HHS clarifies the outbreak status publicly and coordinates credibly with the FDA—or whether ideological friction between them leaves consumers guessing.
