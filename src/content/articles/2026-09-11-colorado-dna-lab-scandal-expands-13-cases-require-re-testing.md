# Colorado Forensic Lab Faces Credibility Crisis as DNA Evidence Gets Re-tested

Denver Police are re-testing evidence from 13 cases potentially compromised by former Colorado Bureau of Investigation forensic analyst Yvonne "Missy" Woods, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver. The move underscores a serious problem: when government crime labs fail basic quality controls, innocent people can be convicted and guilty ones walk free.

Woods' misconduct raises hard questions about how many other cases across Colorado may be tainted. Conservatives and law-and-order advocates argue that public sector labs—like their counterparts nationwide—often lack the accountability and competitive pressure that private labs face. When a single analyst's negligence can invalidate years of investigative work, taxpayers foot the bill for retrials, appeals, and potential settlements.

The retesting effort is necessary but expensive. Denver and state officials have not disclosed how many additional cases may be affected or what the total cost of remediation will be. Republicans have long warned that bloated government bureaucracies—even those tasked with public safety—can become complacent without rigorous oversight and performance metrics.

This scandal also highlights why Colorado's criminal justice system needs transparency: defendants deserve to know the full scope of contaminated evidence, and taxpayers deserve to know how much this failure will cost.

Watch whether the state legislature demands independent audits of other CBI analysts' work and whether officials hold anyone accountable for the lab's lax oversight.
