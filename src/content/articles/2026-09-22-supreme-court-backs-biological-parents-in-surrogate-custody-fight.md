# Supreme Court Sides With Biological Parents Over Surrogate in Custody Fight

The U.S. Supreme Court has declined to intervene in a custody dispute between a California couple and their surrogate, effectively allowing the biological parents to retain custody of a baby boy born in Dallas. Justice Elena Kagan denied a request from surrogate McKenna West, a nurse, seeking conservatorship over the child.

The case hinged on a deeply contested issue: the couple had asked West to terminate her pregnancy in April after prenatal testing revealed the baby had hypoplastic left heart syndrome at roughly 20 weeks. West refused and carried the pregnancy to term. The biological parents then sought custody, which lower courts granted.

**The conservative angle:** This ruling affirms parental rights and the sanctity of contractual agreements in surrogacy arrangements—principles conservatives have long championed. It also reflects judicial restraint; the Supreme Court's refusal to second-guess lower courts' custody decisions respects the family law framework as written, rather than imposing new judicial activism around reproductive disputes.

The case underscores the legal and ethical minefield surrounding surrogacy contracts when abortion becomes central to the dispute. Conservatives argue that biological parenthood and contractual clarity should prevail; the left has framed surrogate rights as paramount.

**Watch:** Whether this decision influences pending state-level surrogacy legislation, particularly in Colorado, where family law and reproductive autonomy remain politically charged.
