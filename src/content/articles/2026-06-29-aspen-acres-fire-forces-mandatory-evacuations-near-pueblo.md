# Wildfire Forces Evacuations Near Pueblo; Beulah Under Mandatory Orders

Colorado's wildfire season is hitting hard again, with the Aspen Acres Fire forcing mandatory evacuations in Pueblo and Custer counties. The entire town of Beulah is now under evacuation orders as of Monday morning, according to the Pueblo County Sheriff's Office (KDVR Fox31). The fire has already consumed more than 30 acres, with Bishop Castle also evacuated as a precaution (9News).

Residents can access the evacuation shelter at the Pueblo County Recreation Center. Fire conditions in the region remain active, and authorities are urging compliance with evacuation orders.

This latest fire underscores a persistent challenge for Colorado: the state's vulnerability to catastrophic wildfires, often exacerbated by decades of federal forest mismanagement and environmental policies that have restricted timber harvesting and forest thinning. Conservatives argue that overly restrictive environmental regulations and litigation from green groups have prevented the active forest management necessary to reduce fuel loads and prevent megafires. Meanwhile, progressive policies prioritizing "wilderness preservation" over practical land stewardship have left Colorado's forests dense, dry, and dangerous.

Taxpayers should watch whether state and federal officials finally embrace aggressive forest management and timber harvesting as wildfire prevention—or continue blocking the solutions that could protect Colorado communities.
