# Amendment 87 Masks a Bracket-Creep Trap for Colorado Taxpayers

Colorado voters face a deceptive tax scheme disguised as "soak the rich" populism. Amendment 87 promises to raise taxes only on the top 3% of earners and 5% of businesses—generating $2.7 billion annually, according to proponents cited by the *Colorado Sun*—while cutting taxes for everyone else. But conservatives warn the math doesn't hold.

The real danger, according to *Complete Colorado*, lies in bracket creep. Once inflation pushes middle-class Coloradans into higher tax brackets, the amendment's rate structure will ensnare ordinary workers and small-business owners who were supposedly protected. What starts as a tax on the wealthy becomes a stealth tax hike on all Coloradans over time.

This matters for Colorado's economy. Higher earners and successful businesses drive job creation and investment. Pushing them toward the exits—or forcing them to relocate—shrinks the tax base and ultimately hurts working families dependent on those jobs. The state's energy sector, already under regulatory pressure, faces additional headwinds.

Republicans argue Amendment 87 is a classic progressive bait-and-switch: promise targeted relief, deliver broad-based pain. Democrats counter that the wealthy should pay their "fair share" to fund education and services.

Watch whether Colorado voters see through the bracket-creep trap or embrace the amendment's initial promise—a test of whether tax-hike messaging still works in a state increasingly skeptical of government spending.
