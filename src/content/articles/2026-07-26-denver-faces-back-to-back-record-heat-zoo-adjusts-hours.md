# Denver Braces for Back-to-Back Record Heat as Zoo Adjusts Operations

Denver is about to sweat through another dangerous heat wave, with Sunday's forecast tracking to match or exceed Saturday's record-breaking 101 degrees (KDVR, 9News). Heat Advisories remain in effect across the Front Range as meteorologists warn of isolated storms that could complicate conditions further.

The extreme temperatures are forcing local institutions to adapt. The Denver Zoo announced Sunday operating hour changes, opening early to allow visitors to enjoy cooler morning conditions before peak heat arrives (9News). The strategy reflects growing recognition that Colorado's summer heat is becoming increasingly unpredictable and severe.

For Colorado taxpayers and families, the message is straightforward: prepare for dangerous conditions. Stay hydrated, limit outdoor activity during peak afternoon hours, and check on elderly neighbors and relatives. Power grids across the state will face strain as air conditioning demand spikes—a reminder that reliable energy infrastructure matters when the heat turns deadly.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's energy policies remain flexible enough to meet demand without rolling blackouts. Ideological commitments to renewable-only generation could leave residents vulnerable when the grid needs dispatchable power most.
