# Young Americans Losing Patriotism—and Schools Aren't Teaching It Back

Only 56% of Americans ages 18 to 34 consider themselves patriotic, a troubling sign that educators and civic leaders are failing to instill love of country in the next generation.

The decline reflects a broader retreat from teaching foundational American principles in schools. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the erosion of patriotic sentiment among young people raises urgent questions about whether institutions are actively discouraging national pride or simply neglecting it altogether.

National Review argues that truly neutral education is a myth—schools inevitably teach *something* about values and citizenship. The real question is whether educators will teach students to appreciate America's founding ideals or remain silent while progressive curricula emphasize grievance narratives over gratitude.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado schools, like those nationwide, face pressure to either reinforce civic virtue or abandon it. Parents and taxpayers funding public education deserve clarity on whether their schools are building patriotic citizens or ideological skeptics.

**Conservative concern:** If schools won't teach patriotism, parents must. The alternative—a generation indifferent to the nation that sustains their freedom—threatens both social cohesion and the republic itself.

Watch whether Colorado school boards explicitly commit to teaching American exceptionalism and civic pride, or continue dodging the question.
