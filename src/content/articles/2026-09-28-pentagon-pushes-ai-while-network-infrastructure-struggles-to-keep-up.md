# Pentagon Races to Integrate AI Into Military Operations—But Network Infrastructure Lags Behind

The U.S. military is moving fast on artificial intelligence, but it may be moving faster than its own infrastructure can handle. The Navy recently announced a new Robotic and Autonomous Systems Warfighting Development Center, while the Defense Department has added ChatGPT and Grok to its GenAI.mil platform alongside Gemini (Washington Examiner). The Defense Information Systems Agency's fiscal 2026 watchlist now prioritizes agentic AI and AI-augmented software engineering.

The catch: military readiness depends on network architecture that may not be ready for the speed and scale of AI deployment. Without robust, secure, and resilient networks, even cutting-edge AI tools become liabilities rather than force multipliers.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts significant military installations and defense contractors who will compete for AI modernization contracts. Colorado taxpayers and defense workers should watch whether the Pentagon invests adequately in network infrastructure—or whether it repeats the pattern of buying shiny new tech while neglecting the unglamorous backbone that makes it work.

Conservatives should demand accountability: Are we spending wisely on AI that actually strengthens deterrence, or are we funding another bloated Pentagon initiative that enriches contractors without delivering real warfighting advantage?
