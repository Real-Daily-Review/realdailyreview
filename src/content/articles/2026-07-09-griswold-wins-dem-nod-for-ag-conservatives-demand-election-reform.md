# Colorado AG Race Sets Up Griswold Rematch; Election Reform Debate Intensifies

Democrat Jena Griswold's narrow primary victory as Colorado's secretary of state has reignited conservative calls for election reform, even as she advances to face Republican El Paso County District Attorney Michael J. Allen in November's attorney general race.

Griswold won the Democratic nomination in a crowded field—a result that has conservatives questioning Colorado's primary system. According to Complete Colorado, the outcome illustrates how "a candidate can win with a small minority of voters even if another candidate is more broadly preferred," highlighting what Republicans argue is a structural flaw favoring fragmented candidacies over consensus choices.

Republicans contend Colorado's primary rules allow nominees to emerge without demonstrating broad party support, potentially weakening general-election viability. The Denver Post confirmed Griswold and Allen will compete for the attorney general post this fall.

Allen, the GOP nominee, enters the general election as Griswold's Republican challenger—a significant rematch dynamic given her tenure as secretary of state overseeing Colorado elections. Conservatives will scrutinize how the AG race unfolds as a referendum on Democratic election administration and regulatory approach.

Watch whether Republicans push formal primary-reform legislation in the 2027 General Assembly, and whether Allen's campaign capitalizes on voter frustration with Griswold's record.
