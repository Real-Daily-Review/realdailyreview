# U.S. Must Back Words on Nigeria Christian Persecution With Real Pressure, Republicans Say

The Trump administration's designation of Nigeria as a Country of Particular Concern for Christian persecution one year ago has yet to produce measurable results, according to House Republicans pressing for concrete action.

Rep. Riley Moore (R-WV) told the House Foreign Affairs subcommittee on Africa this week that success requires "a definitive" shift in U.S. policy—not merely symbolic labeling. The designation, while rhetorically important, has failed to translate into the kind of diplomatic and economic pressure needed to halt systematic violence against Nigerian Christians, conservatives argue.

Republicans contend that naming human rights abuses means little without enforcement mechanisms: targeted sanctions, aid conditions, and public diplomatic pressure on the Nigerian government to prosecute perpetrators and protect religious minorities. The Washington Examiner notes that a genocide "the world won't name is a genocide it won't stop"—underscoring conservative frustration with performative foreign policy that lacks teeth.

For Colorado's faith communities and those concerned with religious liberty globally, the debate highlights how Republican administrations must distinguish between rhetorical commitments and actionable strategy. Coloradans who value religious freedom should watch whether Congress backs this rhetoric with legislation conditioning aid or imposing sanctions on Nigerian officials complicit in persecution.
