# Alaska's Fat Bear Week Winner Has Nothing to Do with Colorado—But Here's Why You Should Care About Federal Land Management

A brown bear named Backpack won Alaska's annual Fat Bear Week competition at Katmai National Park and Preserve, defeating last year's champion, Chunk, in the early rounds (9News Denver, New York Post). The contest celebrates bears that successfully pack on weight before winter hibernation.

While the story is lighthearted, it highlights a broader conservative concern: how federal agencies manage public lands and wildlife. Katmai is a federally controlled preserve where the National Park Service sets all the rules—from bear viewing to resource extraction restrictions.

Colorado conservatives should note the parallel. Federal land agencies in Colorado—the Bureau of Land Management, Forest Service, and National Park Service—control roughly 37% of the state's territory. These agencies often prioritize environmental restrictions over energy development, grazing rights, and water access that benefit Colorado ranchers, miners, and communities.

Fat Bear Week itself is harmless fun. But it reflects the broader federal wildlife-management philosophy that sometimes prioritizes spectacle and preservation over the practical needs of rural economies that depend on public-land access.

Watch how the Biden-Harris administration's successor handles Colorado's federal lands—especially energy leasing, water rights, and grazing permits that directly affect working families.
