# Rural Colorado Could Power AI Boom—If Regulators Get Out of the Way

Colorado's rural communities face a stark choice: embrace the energy infrastructure needed for artificial intelligence data centers, or watch opportunity pass to states with fewer regulatory obstacles.

The Washington Examiner argues that rural America has endured decades of progressive hostility toward traditional industries. Regulators and cultural elites dismissed coal, oil, and gas workers with a dismissive "learn to code"—then regulated those industries into decline. Now, as AI demand explodes and data centers require massive, reliable power supplies, rural regions possess exactly what the tech economy needs: land, existing transmission infrastructure, and energy capacity.

For Colorado specifically, this matters enormously. The state's rural counties—already hammered by coal-to-renewables transition policies—could leverage natural gas and hydroelectric resources to attract high-paying data center jobs. But only if state and federal regulators don't impose new permitting delays, environmental reviews, or grid-connection barriers that make development uneconomical.

Conservatives argue rural communities deserve a genuine economic renaissance, not another round of regulatory punishment dressed up as climate virtue. The irony is sharp: progressives spent years shutting down the industries that powered rural life, then expect those same communities to celebrate "green energy transitions" that benefit coastal tech companies.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature and Public Utilities Commission fast-track permitting for data center power infrastructure—or repeat the regulatory mistakes that hollowed out rural economies.
