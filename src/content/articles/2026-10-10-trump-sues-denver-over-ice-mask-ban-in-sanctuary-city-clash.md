# Federal Government Sues Denver Over ICE Officer Mask Ban

The Trump administration is taking Denver to court over a city ordinance that bars federal law enforcement—including ICE agents—from wearing masks while operating within city limits, a direct clash between federal authority and local sanctuary-city politics.

The Justice Department argues the Supremacy Clause gives Washington control over federal law enforcement operations, not Denver (9News). The city counters that the ordinance is constitutional. Notably, the mask ban hasn't generated a single citation or arrest since its passage, raising questions about why Denver is defending a largely symbolic measure at taxpayer expense.

This lawsuit exposes the absurdity of sanctuary-city overreach. Denver's progressive leadership has repeatedly hamstrung federal immigration enforcement—a core federal responsibility—through local obstruction. Republicans argue cities have no authority to regulate how federal agents do their jobs. Democrats and Denver officials claim local control over law enforcement practices is their right.

The case will likely turn on whether cities can impose operational restrictions on federal officers. Conservatives say the answer is no; the left disagrees. Either way, Colorado taxpayers are footing Denver's legal bills to defend a regulation that hasn't been enforced once.

Watch whether this ruling sets precedent for other sanctuary jurisdictions nationwide—and whether Colorado cities follow Denver's lead in defying federal authority.
