# Colorado Faces Tax-Hike Ballot Blitz as Conservatives Bypass Legislature

Colorado voters will face a November ballot packed with tax increases and spending measures—a sign that both the left and right have lost faith in the legislature to advance their agendas.

School districts across the state are pushing hundreds of millions in property tax hikes (Complete Colorado), while a new Yampa Valley transit authority is seeking a sales tax increase to fund operations. Most significantly, Democrats have qualified a measure to impose a graduated income tax on Colorado's highest earners (Denver Post), marking a major shift in the state's tax structure.

Republicans, meanwhile, have weaponized the ballot process through Advance Colorado, a conservative organization that has become the primary vehicle for passing GOP-backed measures without relying on elected officials. "Conservatives have long said that our ideas win, even if our candidates don't win," Republican consultant Tyler Sandberg told the Denver Post. The group has racked up ballot wins as GOP legislative power has eroded statewide.

The Colorado Sun notes that Advance Colorado has supplanted elected officials as the engine of Republican policymaking—a tacit admission that Republicans lack the votes in the legislature to advance their agenda directly.

**What conservatives and taxpayers should watch:** Whether November's tax measures pass, and whether the GOP can leverage ballot initiatives to offset Democratic control of state government.
