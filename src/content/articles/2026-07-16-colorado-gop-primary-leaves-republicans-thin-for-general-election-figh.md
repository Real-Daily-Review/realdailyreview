# Colorado GOP Primary Delivers Weak Slate for General Election Fight

Colorado Republicans just handed Democrats a gift: a thin bench of statewide candidates that conservatives worry won't compete in November. Out of 18 gubernatorial hopefuls, only two cleared the GOP assembly (Complete Colorado), signaling a fractured primary that left liberty advocates questioning whether the party can mount a credible challenge to Democratic incumbents.

The weakness extends beyond the governor's race. In the competitive 8th Congressional District, Rep. Gabe Evans and Manny Rutinel advanced to face off in a general election where Democrats are already framing the battle around "pocketbooks" and cost-of-living—terrain where Colorado's inflation-weary voters are vulnerable to either party's message (Denver Post).

**The conservative concern:** A depleted primary field suggests the Colorado GOP failed to recruit and energize candidates capable of capitalizing on Democratic vulnerabilities on taxes, spending, and regulatory overreach. When only two gubernatorial candidates survive an assembly process, it signals organizational weakness and lack of grassroots momentum heading into a general election where Republicans need to flip purple districts.

Democrats will exploit this opening. Expect aggressive messaging on inflation, housing costs, and kitchen-table economics—issues where a stronger Republican field could have forced the left to defend their spending record and failed energy policies.

**Watch next:** Whether Evans and other GOP nominees can overcome the primary's organizational deficit and mount a credible fiscal-conservative message before November.
