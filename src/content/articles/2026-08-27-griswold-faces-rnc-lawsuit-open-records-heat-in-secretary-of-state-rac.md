# Colorado's Secretary of State Faces Legal, Ethical Pressure as Election Integrity Questions Mount

Jena Griswold's tenure as Colorado Secretary of State is drawing fire from Republicans and conservative critics over election administration practices and a brewing open-records controversy.

The Republican National Committee has sued Griswold, arguing her office improperly allows overseas voters to establish Colorado residency through proxies—parents or spouses—rather than direct personal connection to the state (Complete Colorado). The RNC contends this practice is unconstitutional and creates vulnerability to voter fraud.

Meanwhile, a separate scandal involving open-records handling has prompted conservative voices to question her fitness for office. Krista Kafer, writing in the Denver Post, argues that Republican challenger Michael Allen brings superior litigation expertise and a scandal-free record in public service—a contrast she frames as material to voters weighing the race.

Griswold, a Democrat, has not publicly detailed her response to the residency lawsuit or the records controversy in these reports.

**The Colorado angle:** Election administration directly affects voter confidence and ballot integrity. If courts side with the RNC, Colorado's overseas voting rules could face significant revision—potentially affecting military families and expatriates.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado courts rule on the RNC's residency challenge before the 2026 election, and whether the open-records issue gains traction in the campaign.
