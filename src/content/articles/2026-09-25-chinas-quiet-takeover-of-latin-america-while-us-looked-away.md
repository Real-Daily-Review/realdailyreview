# China's Latin America Grip Tightens While Washington Slept

Washington's assumption that geography alone secures American influence in Latin America has proven dangerously naive. According to the Washington Examiner, while U.S. foreign-policy attention scattered across distant conflicts, China methodically constructed economic and strategic dominance across the Western Hemisphere—controlling trade routes, infrastructure, ports, energy assets, telecommunications networks, mining operations, and access to critical resources.

For Colorado, this matters. American energy independence and domestic mineral production directly compete with Chinese-controlled Latin American supplies. If Beijing controls the hemisphere's critical minerals and energy infrastructure, Colorado's mining, oil, and gas industries face intensified global competition and potential supply-chain vulnerabilities. Conservatives argue that rebuilding American economic presence in Latin America requires aggressive trade policy, energy development at home, and strategic investment in domestic resource extraction—not the passive approach that allowed China to fill the void.

The Trump administration's belated recognition of this strategic failure signals a potential policy shift toward reasserting Western Hemisphere influence through economic competition rather than diplomatic neglect. Republicans contend that countering Chinese expansion requires prioritizing American energy independence and removing regulatory barriers to domestic resource development.

Watch whether the incoming administration pairs rhetorical toughness on China with concrete policies that actually boost Colorado's competitive advantage in critical minerals and energy production.
