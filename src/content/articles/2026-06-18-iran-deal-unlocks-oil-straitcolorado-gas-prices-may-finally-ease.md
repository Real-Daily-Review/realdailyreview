# Oil Flows Again After Iran Deal—Relief for Colorado Gas Pumps

An interim peace agreement between the U.S. and Iran has unlocked the Strait of Hormuz, with more than 12.5 million barrels of oil crossing the critical chokepoint overnight, according to Vice President JD Vance. The breakthrough follows months of supply disruptions that have kept energy prices elevated across American markets—including at Colorado gas pumps.

Saudi vessels alone carried roughly six million barrels through the strait, while supertankers from the UAE, Qatar, and China also resumed transit (Washington Examiner, Just The News). The flow signals meaningful relief for global oil markets after geopolitical tensions artificially constrained supply.

For Colorado, this matters. Higher energy costs ripple through the state's economy—affecting transportation, heating, and the cost of goods. Colorado's own oil and gas sector, already burdened by Biden-Harris regulatory overreach, benefits from stable global pricing that doesn't punish domestic producers competing against foreign crude.

Republicans have long argued that energy security demands both domestic production and stable international markets. The deal suggests the incoming Trump-Vance administration is prioritizing pragmatic energy diplomacy over the previous administration's Iran appeasement strategy.

Conservatives should watch whether this supply relief holds and whether the administration uses it to roll back anti-energy regulations that have hamstrung Colorado's oil and gas workers.
