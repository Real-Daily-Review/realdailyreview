# Surveillance Tech Giant Flock Safety Rebrands After Privacy Backlash

Flock Safety, the vendor behind automatic license plate readers (ALPRs) deployed by police departments nationwide, is scrambling to rebrand itself as a privacy champion after months of fierce criticism—a move that exposes the company's credibility problem, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The company previously dismissed privacy advocates as "terrorists," yet now claims to be reforming its policies and messaging. ALPRs capture and store vehicle plate data indiscriminately, raising serious Fourth Amendment concerns among conservatives and civil libertarians alike who argue the technology enables mass surveillance without warrant or probable cause.

**The conservative case against ALPRs is straightforward:** unlimited plate-scanning creates a de facto tracking system that threatens individual liberty and invites government abuse. While law enforcement touts the devices as crime-fighting tools, critics rightfully note that blanket surveillance of law-abiding citizens violates constitutional principles of limited government intrusion.

Colorado communities considering ALPR deployment should demand strict data governance: limited retention periods, warrant requirements for access, and explicit prohibitions on sharing data with federal agencies or third parties. Flock Safety's sudden pivot to "privacy-first" messaging rings hollow—the company's track record suggests it prioritizes profit over principle.

Watch whether Colorado cities and counties impose genuine privacy safeguards or simply accept Flock's rebranded pitch at face value.
