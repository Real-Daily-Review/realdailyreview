# Midwest Tornadoes Leave Trail of Destruction; Colorado Should Brace for Severe Season

Severe weather doesn't wait for government permission—and neither should Colorado's disaster preparedness.

At least three tornadoes tore through communities outside Chicago on Thursday, leveling homes, snapping trees, and toppling power poles across the Midwest and Northeast (9News Denver, New York Post). Hundreds of thousands lost power as the storms swept through multiple states, with flight disruptions adding to the chaos.

For Colorado, the message is clear: spring and early summer severe weather season demands readiness. While the Left often uses weather events to push climate-change narratives and justify expansive environmental regulations, conservatives emphasize that tornadoes are a natural phenomenon requiring practical preparedness—not new government mandates or spending programs.

Colorado communities should ensure emergency response systems are current, backup power infrastructure is robust, and families have basic disaster plans in place. The state's energy grid, already strained by progressive policies favoring unreliable renewables over reliable baseload power, must be hardened against severe weather impacts.

Watch for whether Colorado Democrats use this Midwest disaster to justify additional environmental regulations or energy mandates that would further burden Colorado businesses and ratepayers.
