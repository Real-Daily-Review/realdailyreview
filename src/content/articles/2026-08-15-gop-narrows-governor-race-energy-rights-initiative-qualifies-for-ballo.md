# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their crowded 2026 gubernatorial primary down to just two candidates through the state party assembly—a sharp contrast to the 18 hopefuls who entered the race. Meanwhile, voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution through Initiative 177, brought forward by Advance Colorado and approved by the Secretary of State's office.

The dual developments signal competing priorities within Colorado's right-leaning coalition. The assembly process, which requires delegate support rather than open primary voting, reflects the party establishment's effort to coalesce around viable nominees early. Republicans argue this streamlines resources and messaging ahead of a general election where Democrats control the statehouse.

Initiative 177 targets Colorado's energy sector directly. Conservatives and energy advocates contend the measure protects property rights and economic opportunity against what they view as creeping regulatory restrictions on natural gas development and use. The initiative reflects growing concern among Republicans and industry groups that progressive environmental policies—particularly at the state and local level—threaten Colorado's energy workforce and affordable heating for families.

Democrats and environmental groups are expected to oppose the measure as a barrier to climate goals, though their formal campaign messaging remains unclear.

Watch whether Initiative 177 gains traction with suburban and rural Colorado voters, and whether the two GOP nominees can unite the fractured primary field behind a single challenger to the Democratic incumbent.
