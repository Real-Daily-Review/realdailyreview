# Trump Signals Iran Nuclear Deal Imminent—But Tehran Pumps the Brakes

President Trump says a new Iran nuclear agreement could be signed as soon as Sunday, with Pakistan mediating what officials describe as the closest negotiating parties have come to a deal. Trump has promised the accord will keep Iran from developing nuclear weapons and reopen the Hormuz Strait to international shipping—critical for global energy markets and U.S. interests.

But Tehran is signaling caution. Iranian officials say more time is needed, tempering expectations despite Pakistan's optimism and Trump's public confidence (Washington Examiner, Daily Wire).

**The Colorado angle:** Any Iran deal affects energy prices and geopolitical stability. A reopened Hormuz Strait could ease oil markets, potentially benefiting Colorado's economy. Conversely, a weak agreement that fails to constrain Iran's nuclear program would undermine regional security and energy reliability—concerns conservatives have raised about previous Iran negotiations.

Republicans backing Trump's approach argue a credible nuclear agreement with real enforcement mechanisms protects American interests and allies. Critics worry about verification and whether Iran will honor commitments, given past violations of the 2015 JCPOA.

**Watch:** Whether the deal actually materializes Sunday or faces further delays—and what enforcement mechanisms are included.
