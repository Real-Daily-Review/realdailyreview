# Supreme Court Strikes Down Federal Gun Ban for Marijuana Users—Colorado's Second Amendment Win

The Supreme Court has ruled that federal law cannot categorically bar marijuana users from owning firearms, a major Second Amendment victory that will ripple across Colorado and the nation.

Justice Neil Gorsuch's majority opinion found the Justice Department's prosecution of a marijuana user under federal law unconstitutional, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The ruling notably mirrors the statute used to convict Hunter Biden on gun charges related to drug use, per *9News Denver*.

Conservatives view this as a vindication of Second Amendment protections. The *Daily Caller* quoted supporters arguing that "marijuana use today is like alcohol use at the founding"—a direct challenge to the government's power to strip rights based on substance use alone.

Gorsuch's opinion carefully preserved federal authority in narrow cases: "We do not address efforts to ban addicts, or those presently intoxicated, from possessing a firearm," *Just The News* reported. This distinction matters for Colorado, where recreational marijuana is legal but federal prohibition remains on the books, creating legal tension for gun owners.

For Colorado conservatives and Second Amendment advocates, this ruling affirms that the government cannot use drug policy as a backdoor to disarm law-abiding citizens—even in states where cannabis is legal.

Watch for how Colorado courts and federal prosecutors interpret this ruling's boundaries in future cases.
