# GOP Reckoning: Florida Leader Urges Party to Fight Back After Midterm Losses

Florida Lt. Gov. Jay Collins is telling Republicans to stop licking their wounds and start swinging—but the party's soul-searching reveals deep fractures over strategy and messaging.

Dubbed the "Chuck Norris of Florida politics" by Ron DeSantis, Collins is pushing the GOP to reclaim its fighting spirit after what (Fox News) calls a "brutal midterms" showing. His rallying cry reflects conservative frustration: the party lost ground when it should have dominated, and finger-pointing has begun in earnest.

The infighting cuts both ways. While Collins demands aggressive repositioning, even some international conservative voices are breaking ranks. UK Conservative Party Leader Kemi Badenoch told (Breitbart) that Trump "deserved to lose" the midterms, criticizing his "dirty" political style—a rebuke that underscores how Trump-skeptic Republicans are fracturing the coalition.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado Republicans face similar reckoning. The state has trended blue in recent cycles, and GOP candidates here need clarity on whether they're running as Trump-aligned fighters or as a reformed, softer alternative. Voters in swing districts won't tolerate mixed messages.

Conservatives should watch whether the GOP settles on a unified message or splinters further heading into 2026—because a divided house cannot hold purple states like Colorado.
