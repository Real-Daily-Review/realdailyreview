# Rural Colorado Faces Maternity Care Crisis as Labor Units Shutter Nationwide

Nearly 100 labor and delivery units have closed across 35 states in just two years, leaving more than one-third of U.S. counties without adequate maternity services—a trend that threatens rural Colorado families facing longer travel times and fewer options during pregnancy emergencies.

According to a March of Dimes report cited by 9News Denver, the closures have widened gaps in maternity care, particularly in rural and underserved regions. The Daily Caller notes the report identifies "unnecessary barriers to maternity care" as a contributing factor.

**The conservative angle:** Rural hospital closures often stem from regulatory burden, Medicaid reimbursement rates that don't cover costs, and compliance expenses that squeeze already-thin margins. Colorado's rural counties—where ranching families and small-town residents depend on local healthcare infrastructure—are especially vulnerable. When Denver-based regulators and progressive health mandates make it uneconomical to operate obstetric units, rural Coloradans pay the price in travel time, risk, and lost local economic activity.

Republicans and conservatives argue that reducing regulatory overhead and improving reimbursement rates would help rural hospitals stay open. The left typically blames "healthcare inequity" while proposing more government programs—rarely addressing the regulatory and financial barriers driving closures.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado policymakers will address regulatory barriers to rural maternity care or simply expand government subsidies without fixing the underlying cost structure.
