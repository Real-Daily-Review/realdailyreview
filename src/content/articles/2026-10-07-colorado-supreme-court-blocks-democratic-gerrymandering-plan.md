# Colorado High Court Blocks Democrat Gerrymandering Scheme

Colorado's Supreme Court has shut down a Democratic gerrymandering plan, keeping the state out of the national redistricting wars—at least for now. The ruling is a win for voters tired of partisan map-rigging, regardless of which party wields the pen.

Democrats had pushed a redistricting scheme that conservatives argue would have carved up congressional districts to maximize Democratic seats. Republicans contend that gerrymandering—whether by the left or right—corrodes representative democracy and voter trust. The Colorado high court apparently agreed the plan crossed the line.

This matters for Colorado taxpayers and voters because redistricting directly determines which representatives control state and federal spending, regulation, and policy. Rigged maps mean rigged outcomes: politicians choose their voters instead of voters choosing politicians. The Denver Post's editorial board, typically center-left, condemned gerrymandering broadly—a rare moment of bipartisan clarity that even partisan Democrats should heed.

The ruling keeps Colorado's districts drawn fairly, at least through the next election cycle. Conservatives will watch closely whether Democrats attempt an end-run around the court's decision or whether the state's redistricting commission respects the ruling and draws maps based on communities of interest rather than partisan advantage.
