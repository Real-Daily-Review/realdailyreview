# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their crowded 2026 gubernatorial primary to just two candidates through the party assembly process, tightening the race ahead of the general election. Meanwhile, voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution—a direct conservative pushback against progressive efforts to phase out fossil fuels.

Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado, qualified for the ballot and reflects growing concern among Colorado energy workers, manufacturers, and rural communities that Democratic-led climate mandates threaten jobs and affordable energy. Conservatives argue the measure protects property rights and energy independence from government overreach; progressives counter that it locks in fossil fuel dependence and undermines climate goals.

The Republican primary narrowing—from 18 candidates to two—signals party unity around a core message: limited government and economic freedom. The GOP will need both a strong nominee and turnout to compete in a state trending blue.

Taxpayers and energy advocates should watch whether Initiative 177 gains traction with independent voters and whether the Republican nominee can capitalize on voter frustration over energy costs and regulatory burden.
