# Federal Court Backs Trump-Era Changes to Washington's Philadelphia Home Exhibit

A federal appeals court has cleared the way for the Interior Department to replace a slavery-focused exhibit at George Washington's Philadelphia home with revised interpretive panels—a win for those who say historical sites shouldn't reduce founding figures to a single narrative.

The Philadelphia court's Friday ruling allows the Trump administration's approach to stand, letting the National Park Service install new educational signage that provides broader context alongside discussion of slavery at the site. Conservatives argue that comprehensive historical interpretation—rather than exhibits centered on America's moral failures—better serves visitors seeking to understand the full complexity of Washington's life and legacy.

The decision reflects a broader conservative pushback against what critics call "woke" reframing of American history at federal sites. Republicans contend that founding figures deserve nuanced treatment that acknowledges both their achievements and their flaws, rather than exhibits designed primarily to condemn them by modern standards.

The left has countered that slavery exhibits are essential to honest historical reckoning and that downplaying this reality whitewashes America's past.

While this fight plays out in Philadelphia, Colorado conservatives should watch whether similar battles emerge at federal lands and monuments across the state—particularly as the Biden administration's National Park Service policies face potential reversal.
