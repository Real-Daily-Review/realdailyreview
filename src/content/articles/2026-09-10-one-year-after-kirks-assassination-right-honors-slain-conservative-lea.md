# One Year After Charlie Kirk's Death, Conservative Movement Honors Slain Activist

A year after conservative firebrand Charlie Kirk was assassinated on September 10, 2025, during Turning Point USA's "American Comeback Tour" stop in Colorado, the right has marked the anniversary with memorials, tributes, and calls to continue his work (9News Denver).

Turning Point USA unveiled an 8-foot statue honoring its founder at the organization's Phoenix headquarters, while the White House released a tribute video emphasizing Kirk's importance to the conservative movement (Just The News). Utah's governor declared September 10 "Charlie Kirk Day," and conservative groups nationwide held remembrances throughout the day.

National Review urged young conservatives to adopt Kirk's "mode of public persuasion as a model for how to conduct themselves in the public square," while the Daily Caller framed the moment as a call to continue Kirk's campus activism and dialogue efforts—"one campus and one conversation at a time."

Kirk's suspect remains in trial, with proceedings ongoing nearly a year after the Denver event that cut short the activist's cross-country tour.

The conservative movement's challenge now: translating memorial sentiment into sustained organizational momentum for Turning Point USA and campus organizing efforts Kirk championed.
