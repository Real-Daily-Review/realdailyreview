# German Right-Wing Party Wins Big—But Establishment May Block Power

Germany's anti-immigration Alternative for Germany (AfD) secured a historic first-place finish in Sunday's Saxony-Anhalt regional election, but the country's political establishment appears poised to block the party from forming a government—a cautionary tale for conservatives watching how entrenched elites protect power.

The AfD's strong showing reflects voter backlash against the federal government's mass migration policies, according to analysts cited by the *New York Post*. The party's 35-year-old charismatic candidate ran on an anti-mass migration platform that resonated with voters frustrated by establishment consensus.

Yet despite winning decisively, the AfD faces a wall: other German parties have signaled they will refuse to govern with the right-wing party, potentially denying it executive power despite its electoral mandate. The *Washington Examiner* notes it remains "uncertain" whether the AfD can form the country's first right-wing state government since World War II.

The outcome underscores a troubling pattern: when voters reject progressive orthodoxy—whether on immigration, energy policy, or government overreach—institutional gatekeepers sometimes work to nullify the result. American conservatives should note how quickly Germany's establishment moved to quarantine a democratically victorious party.

Watch whether German courts or federal authorities move to further constrain the AfD's influence.
