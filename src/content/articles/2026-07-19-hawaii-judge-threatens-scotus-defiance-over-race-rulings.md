# Hawaii Judge Threatens Supreme Court Defiance Over Race-Based Rulings

A Hawaii circuit judge is openly threatening state defiance of the U.S. Supreme Court, accusing the conservative majority of dismantling constitutional rights and resurrecting discredited 19th-century precedents. Justice Todd Eddins claimed the Roberts Court's "colorblind" approach to equal protection ignores the 14th Amendment's original purpose of protecting formerly enslaved Black Americans—a framing that signals the left's rejection of race-neutral constitutional interpretation.

This isn't a Colorado story yet, but it matters here. If Hawaii follows through on judicial defiance, it sets a dangerous precedent for progressive states to ignore Supreme Court rulings they dislike. Colorado Democrats, already hostile to recent SCOTUS decisions on affirmative action and other race-conscious policies, could cite Hawaii's example to push their own state-level resistance—potentially through legislation or executive action that contradicts federal constitutional law.

Republicans argue that equal protection means equal protection: the Constitution should apply the same standard to all citizens regardless of race. Conservatives counter that the left's "original purpose" argument is a backdoor to justify race-based preferences the Constitution explicitly forbids.

Colorado taxpayers and business owners should watch whether Democratic lawmakers attempt to mirror Hawaii's defiance through state policy, effectively creating a two-tiered legal system where progressive states ignore the Supreme Court.
