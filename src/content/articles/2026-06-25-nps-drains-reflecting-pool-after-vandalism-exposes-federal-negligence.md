# Reflecting Pool Vandalism Exposes National Park Service Maintenance Failures

Federal bureaucrats are draining the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool after July Fourth to repair damage from deliberate vandalism—yet another example of government negligence in maintaining America's most iconic monuments. According to the National Park Service, someone used a sharp knife or razor to cut the pool's liner and destroy sealant material, forcing costly repairs that should never have been necessary with adequate oversight.

The NPS deputy director for operations announced the drainage plan in a court filing (Washington Examiner), confirming what conservatives have long argued: federal agencies tasked with protecting national treasures lack the resources, accountability, or competence to do the job effectively. The pool's damage—caulking cut through foam sealant—represents both security and maintenance failures on a site visited by millions annually.

Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for this federal mismanagement. While Washington spends trillions on bloated agencies, iconic monuments deteriorate under NPS watch. Republicans have repeatedly called for streamlining federal park management and holding bureaucrats accountable for stewardship failures, yet the agency's response remains reactive rather than preventive.

Watch whether Congress demands answers on NPS security protocols and whether this incident sparks serious debate over privatizing or devolving monument maintenance to states and private stewards.
