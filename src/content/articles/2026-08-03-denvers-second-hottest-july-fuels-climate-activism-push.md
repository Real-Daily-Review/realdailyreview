# Denver Swelters Through Second-Hottest July on Record—But Don't Expect Climate Activists to Acknowledge Natural Cycles

Denver is experiencing genuinely hot weather, with July 2026 ranking as the second-hottest on record for the month, according to KDVR Fox31. The city hit 100 degrees again Sunday during the Rockies' game at Coors Field—the second-hottest first-pitch temperature in stadium history—with downtown and Denver International Airport both reaching triple digits for the fourth time this summer (9News Denver).

**Here's what matters for Colorado:** While progressive activists will inevitably weaponize heat records to demand more government climate mandates and energy restrictions, conservatives note that Denver's temperature fluctuations have occurred throughout recorded history. Natural climate variability is real; using isolated hot spells to justify economically destructive policies that cripple Colorado's energy sector is not.

Republicans and energy advocates argue that Colorado's oil, gas, and coal industries—which employ thousands and generate substantial state revenue—should not be sacrificed on the altar of climate alarmism based on seasonal weather patterns. The state's economy depends on reliable, affordable energy, not virtue-signaling regulations that drive jobs and investment elsewhere.

Coloradans should watch whether Democratic lawmakers use this summer's heat to justify new carbon taxes, renewable mandates, or restrictions on energy development that would hit working families and businesses hardest.
