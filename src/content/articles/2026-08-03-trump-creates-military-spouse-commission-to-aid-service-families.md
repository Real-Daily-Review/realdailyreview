# Trump Creates Military Spouse Commission to Support Service Families

President Trump signed an executive order Monday establishing the President's Military Spouse Commission, signaling renewed federal focus on the families of active-duty service members—a constituency that includes thousands of Colorado military families stationed at Fort Carson, Schriever Space Force Base, and other installations across the state.

The commission will be chaired by Jennifer Hegseth, wife of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and includes Christie Mullin, wife of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, according to the Washington Examiner. The order was signed in the Oval Office with Sens. Tommy Tuberville and Katie Britt of Alabama present.

The initiative targets support for military spouses, who often face employment gaps, credential recognition barriers, and relocation challenges as service members move between postings. Colorado's military community—particularly around El Paso County's Fort Carson, home to roughly 12,000 active-duty personnel—stands to benefit from streamlined federal coordination on spouse employment and family services.

Republicans frame the commission as a practical recognition of military families' sacrifices and economic contributions. The move avoids new spending by leveraging existing federal resources and inter-agency coordination rather than creating a costly bureaucracy.

Watch whether the commission produces measurable outcomes on spouse employment rates and credential reciprocity across states—metrics that will determine if this is genuine reform or symbolic window-dressing.
