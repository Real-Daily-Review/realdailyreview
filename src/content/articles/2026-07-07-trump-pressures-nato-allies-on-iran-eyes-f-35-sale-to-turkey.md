# Trump Presses NATO Allies on Iran While Weighing F-35 Sales to Turkey

President Trump arrived at the NATO summit in Ankara frustrated with allies who refused to provide military bases and overflight rights for Operation Epic Fury against Iran, signaling that America's traditional alliance structure may not align with U.S. strategic interests in the Middle East.

During bilateral talks with Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, Trump indicated he was considering selling advanced F-35 fighter jets to Turkey—a move that underscores his transactional approach to alliance management. "It's a decision we are going to make," Trump said, noting that Turkey "has been, in many ways, much more loyal than other" NATO members (Washington Examiner).

The Iran rift exposes a fundamental divide: while the Trump administration views aggressive action against Tehran as essential to regional stability and U.S. security, several NATO allies have balked at providing logistical support, reflecting their reluctance to escalate Middle East tensions or complicate their own diplomatic interests.

Conservatives argue this moment vindicates Trump's skepticism of traditional alliances that don't serve American interests directly. The F-35 sale to Turkey, however, raises questions about technology transfer and strategic alignment—concerns worth monitoring as the administration weighs defense industry priorities against geopolitical leverage.
