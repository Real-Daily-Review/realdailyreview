# Colorado's Sports Betting Boom: A Cautionary Tale About Government-Enabled Vice

Colorado's embrace of legalized sports betting has put gambling at every Coloradan's fingertips—and conservatives should ask whether the state is profiting from addiction rather than protecting citizens' wallets.

What was once confined to Nevada casinos and back-room bookies is now mainstream. The Washington Examiner warns that widespread sports betting is "ruining sports, and more importantly, people's livelihoods." The ease of mobile wagering means Coloradans can bet anytime, anywhere—a recipe for financial ruin disguised as entertainment.

**The conservative case:** While states tout gambling tax revenue, they're essentially monetizing human weakness. Colorado collects licensing fees and tax revenue from sportsbooks, but at what cost? Problem gambling destroys families, increases demand for social services, and preys on lower-income Coloradans who can least afford losses. The state becomes complicit in vice for a budget fix—a short-term revenue grab with long-term social costs.

**Democrats counter** that legalization brings regulation, consumer protections, and tax revenue that funds education and infrastructure. They argue prohibition simply drives betting underground.

The real question: Is Colorado's government in the business of protecting citizens or exploiting their worst impulses for tax dollars?

Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to restrict mobile betting access or fund gambling-addiction treatment—or doubles down on expansion.
