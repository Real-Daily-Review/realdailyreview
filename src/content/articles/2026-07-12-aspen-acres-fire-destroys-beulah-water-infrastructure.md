# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has left the small community of Beulah without reliable water service—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis now threatens the basic utilities that keep towns alive. According to the Colorado Sun, water managers are scrambling to restore clean water supplies after the fire damaged critical infrastructure, a recovery effort that could take months or longer.

The blaze tore through Beulah's roughly 800 residents, destroying numerous homes and leaving the community facing both immediate humanitarian needs and long-term infrastructure repair costs. CPR News reports that residents fought back against the flames, but the damage to water systems underscores a hard truth: Colorado's fire season doesn't just burn forests—it cripples the utilities and services that depend on functioning landscapes.

Conservatives should note that while most active Colorado fires have spared water systems so far, Beulah's experience exposes vulnerabilities in how the state manages forest health and fire prevention. Poor forest management—driven partly by environmental regulations that restrict logging and fuel reduction—has created tinderbox conditions across the state. Rebuilding water infrastructure will cost taxpayers millions, money that could have been spent on prevention.
