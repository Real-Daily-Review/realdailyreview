# Federal Overreach on Species Protection Threatens Colorado Housing, Property Rights

Washington's expansive interpretation of the Endangered Species Act is pricing Coloradans out of their own state—and it's time to rein in the bureaucrats.

The Fish and Wildlife Service and National Marine Fisheries Service have weaponized vague "harm" definitions to regulate private land across the West, blocking development and driving up housing costs. *National Review* reports the agencies were never granted sweeping authority over private property, yet they've used it anyway. *New York Post* analysis shows the ever-expanding endangered species list has become a de facto affordable housing killer, making it nearly impossible for working families to buy homes in Colorado's competitive markets.

Republicans argue the ESA has metastasized into a tool for federal land grabs and environmental activism, strangling property rights and economic development. The rescinded "harm" definition was a step toward sanity—but Democrats and environmental groups continue pushing for expansive protections that treat animal habitat as more important than human livelihood.

For Colorado, this means ranchers face regulatory nightmares, developers abandon projects over species concerns, and young families get priced out of homeownership. The state's energy sector also faces constant ESA-based litigation over water rights and land use.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican delegation pushes for legislative limits on ESA enforcement and whether state officials challenge federal overreach in court.
