# 310 Fire Forces Evacuations Near Parachute as Blaze Spreads to 600 Acres

Colorado's Western Slope is battling another major wildfire as the 310 Fire near Parachute has forced immediate evacuations and grown to 600 acres, threatening communities in Garfield County.

Residents on County Road 338 in Morrisania Mesa received evacuation orders from Garfield County 911 as the fire spread rapidly across the region (9News Denver). The blaze underscores the persistent wildfire threat facing Colorado's rural communities and the infrastructure—including oil and gas operations, ranches, and transmission lines—that depends on Western Slope stability.

Conservatives and energy advocates have long argued that aggressive forest management, including thinning overgrown timber and controlled burns, could reduce catastrophic fire risk. Instead, federal land management agencies have faced litigation from environmental groups opposing timber harvests, leaving forests dense and dangerous. Meanwhile, Colorado taxpayers foot the bill for emergency response and recovery.

The 310 Fire also highlights the tension between environmental restrictions on land use and practical fire prevention. Garfield County residents and businesses—many dependent on energy development and agriculture—face real economic and safety consequences when prevention takes a backseat to regulatory caution.

Watch for how state and federal agencies respond to post-fire recovery and whether they'll prioritize forest management reforms or continue blocking the thinning projects that could prevent the next catastrophe.
