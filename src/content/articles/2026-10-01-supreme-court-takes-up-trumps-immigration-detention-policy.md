# Supreme Court to Rule on Trump's Immigration Detention Policy

The Supreme Court has agreed to hear a constitutional challenge to the Trump administration's mandatory detention policy for criminal noncitizens awaiting deportation—a major test of the president's mass deportation agenda.

The case, *Rhoney v. Barbosa*, centers on whether illegal immigrants held in ICE custody are entitled to bond hearings after extended detention periods. Lower courts have already dealt the policy several defeats, but the Supreme Court's decision to hear the case signals the justices will weigh in on a core Trump immigration priority.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that mandatory detention of criminal aliens awaiting deportation is a reasonable exercise of executive authority to protect public safety and ensure deportation compliance. The Trump administration contends that bond hearings for this population create unnecessary delays and undermine enforcement efforts.

**The opposition:** Civil liberties groups and lower courts have argued that prolonged detention without individualized bond hearings raises due process concerns under the Fifth Amendment.

For Colorado, this case matters: the state has become a flashpoint in the immigration debate, with sanctuary policies in Denver and Boulder clashing with federal enforcement priorities. A Supreme Court ruling upholding Trump's detention policy could reshape how Colorado's sanctuary jurisdictions interact with ICE operations.

**Watch next:** The Court's decision on oral argument timing and the eventual ruling will signal how far the justices are willing to go in backing Trump's immigration enforcement machinery.
