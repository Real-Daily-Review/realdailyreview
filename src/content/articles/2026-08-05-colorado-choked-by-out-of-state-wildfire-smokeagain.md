# Colorado Choked by Out-of-State Wildfire Smoke—Again

Colorado's air quality is being held hostage by wildfires burning hundreds of miles away, a recurring problem that highlights the failure of neighboring states to manage their forests responsibly.

The smoky haze blanketing Colorado skies this week originates from fires outside the state, according to CPR News, leaving Front Range residents and their pets breathing hazardous air through no fault of their own. Local animal experts warn that the smoke impacts pets as severely as people, with veterinarians recommending pet owners limit outdoor activity during poor air quality events (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

This isn't a new problem. Colorado taxpayers and businesses suffer economic losses—reduced tourism, outdoor recreation shutdowns, and healthcare costs—while upstream states drag their feet on forest management and controlled burns. The Biden administration's environmental policies have made federal forest management even more restrictive, leaving millions of acres of overgrown timber as tinderboxes.

Republicans have long argued that aggressive forest thinning and prescribed burns prevent catastrophic wildfires. Democrats and environmental groups often block or delay these projects, citing spotted owl habitat and other regulatory concerns. The result: Colorado residents pay the price in smoke and lost productivity.

Conservatives should demand that Colorado's congressional delegation pressure the federal government to accelerate forest management permits and hold neighboring states accountable for wildfire prevention.
