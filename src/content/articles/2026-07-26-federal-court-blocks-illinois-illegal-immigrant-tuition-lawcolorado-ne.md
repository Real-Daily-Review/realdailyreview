# Federal Court Strikes Down Illinois Illegal-Immigrant Tuition Benefits—Colorado Should Take Note

A federal judge has ruled that Illinois' law granting in-state tuition and financial aid to illegal immigrants violates the Constitution, handing the Trump administration another immigration-enforcement victory (Fox News). The decision undercuts Governor J.B. Pritzker's sanctuary-state agenda and raises urgent questions for Colorado taxpayers and policymakers.

Colorado has not adopted Illinois-style tuition subsidies for illegal immigrants, but progressive lawmakers have repeatedly pushed in-state tuition access and expanded benefits. Republicans argue that rewarding illegal immigration with taxpayer-funded education incentives is both fiscally reckless and legally indefensible—it diverts resources from citizens and legal residents while incentivizing further illegal entry.

Conservatives say the ruling vindicates the principle that states cannot use public funds to circumvent federal immigration law. Democrats counter that education access promotes integration and economic mobility regardless of immigration status, framing it as a civil-rights issue.

The Illinois decision signals that courts are increasingly willing to block state-level sanctuary policies that conflict with federal authority. Colorado conservatives should watch whether Democratic legislators attempt to expand illegal-immigrant benefits in the next session—and whether they'll face similar legal challenges.
