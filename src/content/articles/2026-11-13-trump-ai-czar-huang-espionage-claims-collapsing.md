# Trump AI Czar Dismisses Huang Espionage Claims as 'Collapsing'

The alleged spy scandal surrounding Nvidia founder Jensen Huang is falling apart under scrutiny, according to David Sacks, Trump's former AI Czar and co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (Breitbart).

Sacks says the espionage narrative—which centers on Huang's alleged connections to a Chinese businesswoman identified as Alice Huang—"does not hold up to scrutiny" and is "collapsing already." His pushback undercuts earlier conservative concerns about whether Nvidia should be selling advanced AI chips to China in the first place, a question National Review raised pointedly.

The distinction matters for Colorado's tech and defense sectors, which depend on robust AI development and export controls that actually protect American interests rather than chase phantom conspiracies. If the allegations crumble, conservatives should focus on the real policy question: What *should* U.S. export policy toward Chinese AI acquisition look like?

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that national security requires strict limits on advanced chip sales to Beijing—a position grounded in legitimate geopolitical concern, not tabloid innuendo. The Huang story's collapse shouldn't let policymakers off the hook for crafting coherent, enforceable AI export rules.

Watch whether the Trump administration clarifies its actual stance on Nvidia-to-China sales, or lets the scandal's implosion bury the underlying policy debate.
