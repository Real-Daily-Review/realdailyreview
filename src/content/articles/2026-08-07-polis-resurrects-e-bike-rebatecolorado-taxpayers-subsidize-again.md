# Colorado Revives E-Bike Rebate Program—Taxpayers Foot the Bill Again

Governor Jared Polis and the Colorado Energy Office have announced the return of the state's e-bike rebate program, offering up to $1,600 in savings for eligible residents (9News, KDVR).

The program, which had lapsed, will now resume under Polis's direction. While the administration frames this as a popular initiative, conservatives argue the rebate represents another example of government picking winners and losers in the transportation market—subsidizing a product for affluent early adopters while Colorado taxpayers absorb the cost.

Republicans and fiscal watchdogs contend that e-bike subsidies distort the free market, artificially inflate demand for a niche product, and divert state resources that could fund core services or tax relief. The rebate effectively transfers wealth from working Coloradans to those wealthy enough to purchase premium bicycles, they argue.

The program's return signals Polis's continued commitment to progressive climate and transportation policy, even as Colorado faces budget pressures and inflation concerns. Supporters claim the rebates encourage sustainable transportation and reduce emissions, but skeptics question whether government-funded incentives are the most efficient way to achieve those goals.

**Watch for:** How much state funding Polis allocates to the program and whether Republicans push back on the spending when the legislature reconvenes.
