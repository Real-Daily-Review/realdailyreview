# Federal Child Nutrition Fraud Exposes Massive Oversight Failure

A Minnesota nonprofit scam that diverted $250 million in federal child nutrition funds should alarm every Colorado taxpayer watching Washington squander hard-earned dollars with virtually no accountability.

Two fraudsters, Mekfira and Abduljabar Hussein, were recently sentenced to a combined 97 months after pleading guilty to wire fraud conspiracy—they personally pocketed $8.8 million in federal child nutrition money. But they're just the tip of the iceberg: at least 78 defendants have been charged in the sprawling investigation into Feeding Our Future, the nonprofit that orchestrated the scheme.

This isn't a victimless accounting error. Federal child nutrition programs are supposed to feed vulnerable kids, not enrich criminals operating through shell organizations with minimal oversight. The fact that a quarter-billion dollars could vanish before anyone noticed speaks volumes about how Washington manages taxpayer money.

Conservatives argue this disaster proves the case against massive federal spending programs: when government distributes money through layers of nonprofits and contractors, fraud flourishes and accountability evaporates. Republicans have long warned that bloated federal agencies lack the capacity to police their own grants effectively.

Colorado families and businesses fund these programs through federal taxes. When bureaucrats fail to vet recipients or monitor spending, Coloradans foot the bill twice—once through taxes, again through wasted resources.

Watch for whether Congress tightens nonprofit oversight or simply throws more money at the problem.
