# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Wednesday, July 8, 2026

Colorado conservatives woke up this morning to a primary hangover, a voter fraud case that cuts to the heart of election integrity, and a reminder that the Left's grip on this state isn't loosening on its own. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Colorado's Primary Moved the Needle Left — Again

Tuesday's primary results are in, and the takeaway for Colorado conservatives is sobering: the state's leftward drift continues. Notably, money didn't always win — underfunded candidates beat well-funded opponents in several races, which means the progressive grassroots is organized and motivated. Republicans need to understand that checkbook politics alone won't reverse Colorado's slide. Showing up — at caucuses, at the polls, and in between — is the only answer.

---

## Unaffiliateds Are Deciding Your Elections

Colorado's unaffiliated voter rolls keep growing, and they're increasingly shaping primary outcomes that once belonged to party faithful. If you're a registered Republican who skips caucuses and party organizing, you're handing influence to voters with no party loyalty and no conservative commitment. Republicans argue the solution is engagement, not retreat — and that means getting involved before Election Day, not just on it. The rules reward participation. Start participating.

---

## Noncitizen Voted in Federal Elections — Twice

A lawful permanent resident allegedly lied about her U.S. citizenship to register to vote and cast ballots in two federal elections. This is exactly the kind of case conservatives have been warning about for years while the left dismissed election integrity concerns as paranoia. Every fraudulent ballot cancels out a legitimate one — and Colorado, with its all-mail voting system and automatic registration infrastructure, is not immune to this vulnerability. Lawmakers who blocked election security reforms owe voters an explanation.

---

## ICE Shoots Illegal Immigrant After Alleged Vehicle Ramming in Houston

Federal agents fatally shot an illegal immigrant in Houston after an alleged vehicle ramming incident. Separately, DHS called out Virginia — now under Democratic Gov. Abigail Spanberger — as a hotbed of illegal immigrant crime, detailing 17 serious cases in 2026 alone, nine of them in Fairfax County. Democrats counter that enforcement is cruel and counterproductive. Conservatives say the body count tells a different story. Colorado's own sanctuary-friendly policies and Democratic leadership in Denver deserve the same scrutiny Spanberger is now getting.

---

## Jefferson County Case: Work Release for Shooting Teen on Private Property

A former Mountain View councilman received work release and probation — not prison — after a 2024 confrontation in which he shot a teen who had come onto his Jeffco property before a homecoming dance. The case is a complicated one: the teens were trespassing, but the outcome left both sides of the property rights and self-defense debate unsatisfied. Colorado's self-defense laws and how local prosecutors apply them remain a live issue for gun owners and property rights advocates across the Front Range.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado GOP's post-primary positioning — the window to consolidate around strong general election candidates is short, and Democrats will be organized and funded. Watch for any legislative response to the noncitizen voter fraud case; if Colorado Democrats resist tightening registration verification, that's a campaign issue that writes itself. And as Washington continues pressing immigration enforcement, pay attention to whether Colorado's Democrat-controlled state government moves to obstruct federal operations — it wouldn't be the first time.
