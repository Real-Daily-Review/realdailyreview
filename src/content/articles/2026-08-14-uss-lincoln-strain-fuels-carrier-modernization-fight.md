# USS Lincoln Deployment Strains Raise Questions About Navy Readiness and Carrier Modernization

The USS Lincoln's record-breaking 240-plus-day deployment is exposing serious wear-and-tear issues aboard the carrier, just as the Trump administration reportedly moves to overhaul aircraft carrier launch systems Navy-wide—a costly modernization that Democrats are already attacking.

According to 9News Denver, the Lincoln has logged unprecedented continuous time at sea, but emerging reports detail mounting problems with the vessel. Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner reports that President Trump has signed a memorandum directing the Navy to switch fighter jet launch systems on carriers, a move expected to carry substantial costs.

Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) blasted the reported overhaul, telling the Examiner that Trump should "stick to bulls***"—Democratic shorthand for opposing what they view as wasteful defense spending. Republicans counter that modernizing carrier capabilities is essential to maintaining U.S. naval dominance against China and other peer competitors, and that the long deployments underscore why the Navy needs upgraded, more reliable systems.

The tension highlights a broader conservative concern: while the military faces real readiness challenges, Democrats reflexively oppose defense investments as wasteful, even when operational necessity demands them.

Watch whether Congress demands transparency on the total cost of carrier system upgrades and whether Democrats block or delay funding.
