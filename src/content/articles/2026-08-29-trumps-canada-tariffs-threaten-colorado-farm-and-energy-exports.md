# Trump's Tariff Gamble Threatens Colorado's Energy and Agricultural Exports

President Trump's escalating trade war—now targeting Canada with 50% tariffs on lumber, agricultural products, and electronics—risks serious blowback for Colorado's economy, even as conservatives debate whether protectionism serves Main Street or Wall Street.

The tariffs hit Colorado where it counts. Canadian trade partners absorb significant Colorado agricultural exports and timber products. Energy companies operating across the border face supply-chain disruption. Meanwhile, consumers face higher prices on goods flowing through North American supply chains—a hidden tax on Colorado families already squeezed by inflation.

**Republicans are split.** Trump's base argues tariffs protect American workers and manufacturing from unfair foreign competition. But free-market conservatives warn that trade wars invite retaliation, destroy jobs in export-dependent sectors, and hand Washington bureaucrats power to pick winners and losers—the opposite of limited government.

The Washington Examiner notes the tariffs are already straining negotiations with Michigan's auto industry, a bellwether for broader economic pain. National Review questions whether Trump's "absurd" approach will actually deliver the promised reshoring or simply raise costs for American businesses and consumers.

Colorado taxpayers and ranchers should watch whether the administration negotiates a quick resolution or doubles down—because every month of tariff escalation means lower commodity prices for farmers and higher input costs for manufacturers.
