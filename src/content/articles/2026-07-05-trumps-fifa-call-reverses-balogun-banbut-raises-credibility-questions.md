# Trump's FIFA Call Gets Balogun Back on Field—But at What Cost to American Credibility?

President Trump's direct intervention with FIFA president Gianni Infantino to overturn Folarin Balogun's red card suspension raises uncomfortable questions about executive overreach, even for conservatives who back the president's America-first agenda.

According to the New York Post and Washington Examiner, Trump called Infantino directly to request a review of the USMNT star's one-game ban following a controversial red card against Bosnia and Herzegovina. FIFA reversed the suspension Sunday, clearing Balogun to play Monday's Round of 16 match against Belgium—a decision that left the Belgian Football Association "astonished" and investigating "all potential options" (New York Post).

The move worked tactically: Balogun, the U.S. men's leading scorer, is back in the lineup, and celebrities from Tom Brady to Patrick Mahomes celebrated the reversal on social media.

But here's the conservative concern: **When the president personally lobbies international sports bodies to overturn disciplinary decisions, it blurs the line between national interest and executive favoritism.** Conservatives rightly criticize government overreach at home; applying different standards abroad undermines that principle. Belgium's objection isn't baseless—it's the complaint any nation would lodge if roles reversed.

The USMNT needed Balogun for competitive reasons. The question is whether winning one soccer match justifies the precedent of presidential interference in sports governance.
