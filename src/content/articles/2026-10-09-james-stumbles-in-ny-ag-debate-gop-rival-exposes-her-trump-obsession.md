# New York AG Debate Exposes James's Out-of-Touch Priorities

New York Democratic Attorney General Letitia James stumbled badly in Thursday's debate when asked basic questions about her reading habits and professional heroes—revealing how disconnected she is from ordinary New Yorkers' concerns.

When pressed to name a former attorney general she admired, James went silent for several seconds, unable to answer. Her Republican challenger, by contrast, delivered what observers called a "zinger for the ages," contrasting James's focus on January 6 grievances with his own engagement in everyday parenting (Fox News).

The gap widened when both candidates were asked what they'd recently read. James cited "Unthinkable," a memoir by Rep. Jamie Raskin about the January 6 riot and Donald Trump. Her GOP opponent named a children's book he reads to his 2-year-old—a stark illustration of competing worldviews (The Daily Wire). While James obsesses over Trump and partisan narratives, Republicans argue she's ignoring the kitchen-table issues New Yorkers actually care about: crime, inflation, and government overreach.

The exchange underscores a broader conservative critique: Democratic attorneys general have weaponized their offices for political theater rather than protecting citizens and enforcing the rule of law fairly. Colorado voters should note how progressive AGs nationwide prioritize partisan crusades over competent governance—a cautionary tale as state elections approach.
