# Vance Dodges War Question on Iran as GOP Hawks Cry Foul

Vice President JD Vance is refusing to set a timeline on U.S. military operations against Iran, citing ongoing threats to the Strait of Hormuz and a hardened stance against Tehran diplomacy. But his assertion that America isn't technically "at war" with Iran has drawn sharp criticism from Republican hawks.

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) blasted Vance's framing as an "insult to the men and women fighting" (Washington Examiner), arguing that six months of sustained military operations constitute war by any honest definition. The dispute reflects a broader GOP divide: Trump's military-first Iran policy has support among defense hawks, but constitutional conservatives like Massie worry about executive overreach and mission creep without formal congressional authorization.

For Colorado, the stakes include energy prices—Iran tensions directly affect oil markets and gas pump costs for families and businesses—and defense contractor employment in the state's aerospace and defense sectors.

Conservatives should watch whether Congress demands a formal war powers debate or allows the administration to continue indefinite operations under existing authorizations.
