# Castle Rock Family Battles $300K Medical Debt After Police K-9 Attack on Toddler

A Castle Rock family is still fighting for accountability two years after a police K-9 mauled their toddler during what should have been a backyard birthday party. The attack left the child with severe injuries and the family buried under $300,000 in medical debt (KDVR Fox31).

The incident raises hard questions about police liability and government accountability that conservatives have long pressed: when government agencies cause harm, who pays, and how transparent is the process? The family says they "trusted the process"—a trust that appears to have been broken (9News Denver).

This case underscores a tension in law enforcement oversight. While conservatives strongly support police and public safety, they also demand that government agencies be held to account when negligence harms citizens. A toddler's life-altering injury during a routine neighborhood event demands clear answers about training protocols, deployment decisions, and whether taxpayers are footing the bill for inadequate oversight.

Colorado taxpayers deserve to know: Was the K-9 handler properly trained? Did Castle Rock police follow established safety procedures? And if the city is liable, will they cover the family's medical costs, or will this burden fall on citizens already squeezed by inflation and rising property taxes?

Watch for whether Castle Rock settles this case transparently and what policy changes, if any, the city implements to prevent future incidents.
