# Trump Administration Seeks Supreme Court Backing for Citizenship Voter Verification System

The Trump administration is asking the Supreme Court to overturn a lower court's block on a voter verification database that would cross-reference Social Security and citizenship records to identify non-citizen voters before elections.

U.S. Solicitor General John Sauer argued in the filing that the lower court's ruling is "indefensible" and "threatens the integrity of upcoming elections" (Just The News). The administration modified the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) database last year to combine citizenship and Social Security records, aiming to improve the speed and accuracy of voter rolls (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that verifying voter citizenship is a basic election-integrity safeguard. Removing non-citizens from voter rolls protects the legitimacy of elections and prevents fraud—a concern that resonates across Colorado, where election integrity remains a flashpoint after 2020.

**The left's position:** Democrats and voting-rights groups have opposed the system, claiming it risks purging eligible voters and disproportionately affects certain populations. They argue the database is unreliable and could suppress legitimate votes.

Colorado election officials and Republican lawmakers will be watching closely. If the Supreme Court sides with the Trump administration, states could face pressure to implement similar citizenship verification systems—a move conservatives see as overdue election security, while progressives warn of voter suppression risks.
