# Colorado Election Officials Face Federal Pressure on Record-Keeping as Misinformation Spreads

Colorado's county clerks are caught between mounting federal mandates and a tide of election misinformation that's testing their resources and patience this cycle.

The Department of Justice has warned state election officials that failing to preserve election records constitutes a federal crime, according to the Daily Caller. The warning underscores Washington's expanding oversight of local election administration—a development conservatives argue represents federal overreach into traditionally state and county functions.

Meanwhile, CPR News reports that Colorado's election clerks have battled persistent misinformation and disinformation about how elections actually work, with new challenges emerging this year beyond the usual suspects. Clerks say they're spending time and taxpayer dollars debunking false claims rather than focusing on efficient administration.

**The conservative concern:** Federal mandates on record-keeping, while ostensibly reasonable, add compliance costs and bureaucratic burden to county operations already stretched thin. Republicans argue that election integrity is best served by local accountability and transparency—not by Washington imposing one-size-fits-all rules that may not fit Colorado's specific needs. The real problem, conservatives say, isn't federal record-keeping requirements; it's the misinformation ecosystem that erodes public confidence in elections.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado counties push back on DOJ compliance costs or whether state legislators move to clarify local election authority and reduce federal entanglement.
