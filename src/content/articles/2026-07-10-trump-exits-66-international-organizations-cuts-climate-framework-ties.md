# Trump Exits 66 International Organizations, Including UN Climate Framework

The Trump administration's January withdrawal from 66 international organizations—31 of them UN bodies, including the three-decade-old climate diplomacy framework—signals a fundamental shift away from multilateral entanglement, not isolationism.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, critics abroad have predictably branded the move "reckless" and "isolationist," but conservatives argue the administration is simply refusing to bankroll and legitimize organizations that don't serve American interests. The climate framework withdrawal is particularly significant for Colorado: it frees the U.S. from international climate agreements that have driven costly regulatory burdens on Colorado's energy sector—oil, gas, and coal operations that employ thousands of state residents and generate substantial tax revenue.

Republicans contend that decades of UN climate commitments have imposed asymmetrical costs on American workers and businesses while allowing competitors like China to dodge equivalent obligations. By exiting these bodies, the administration reclaims sovereignty and redirects resources toward domestic priorities rather than funding bloated international bureaucracies.

The Left predictably warns of climate catastrophe and American isolation, but conservatives counter that unilateral climate virtue-signaling hasn't reduced global emissions—it's only weakened American competitiveness.
