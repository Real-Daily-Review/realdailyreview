# Colorado Lawmakers Move to Rein In PERA's Lavish Bonus Culture

Colorado lawmakers are finally asking hard questions about why a struggling public pension fund keeps handing out millions in performance bonuses to its investment staff.

Following a Colorado Sun investigation, legislators are drafting proposals to limit bonuses at the Public Employees' Retirement Association (PERA)—a fund already burdened by unfunded liabilities that ultimately fall on Colorado taxpayers. The investigation revealed PERA has paid its investment team substantial performance bonuses in recent years, even as the fund faces long-term solvency challenges.

**The Conservative Take:** Republicans and fiscal hawks argue that PERA's bonus structure is backwards. When a pension fund is underwater, rewarding staff with millions in extra compensation—regardless of performance—signals misaligned incentives and wasteful management. Taxpayers and public employees who depend on PERA shouldn't subsidize Wall Street-style compensation packages at a struggling state agency.

Democrats haven't publicly opposed the bonus limits, suggesting rare bipartisan agreement that the practice warrants scrutiny.

The real question: will lawmakers go far enough? Conservatives will watch whether the final legislation actually caps bonuses meaningfully or merely creates the appearance of reform while PERA's structural problems—unfunded liabilities and unsustainable benefit promises—remain unaddressed.
