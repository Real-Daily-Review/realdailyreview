# Federal Red Tape Is Strangling America's Power Supply—and Colorado Pays the Price

Washington's Byzantine permitting rules are choking off the electricity America desperately needs. The nation faces a projected 25% surge in power demand over the next decade, driven by manufacturing growth, artificial intelligence, and electrification of heating and transportation. Yet federal bureaucracy is blocking the grid upgrades and new generation capacity required to meet that demand.

**The Conservative Case:** Republicans and energy advocates argue that outdated permitting timelines and interconnection standards are the real bottleneck—not a shortage of power or willingness to build. Washington Examiner editors note the grid infrastructure that powered the 20th century cannot sustain the 21st. The solution isn't more regulation; it's streamlined permitting and interconnection reform that lets private developers and utilities deploy generation and transmission faster.

**Colorado's Stake:** As a major energy producer and transmission hub, Colorado has direct interest in grid modernization. Delays in permitting new generation—whether natural gas, nuclear, or renewables—ripple through the state's economy and workforce. Energy workers and manufacturers depend on reliable, affordable power; federal red tape drives up costs and delays projects.

**What to Watch:** Expect pressure on Congress to pass genuine permitting reform that cuts timelines and reduces litigation risk—not another round of "studies" that delay action another decade.
