# Vandals Steal Water Valve, Flood Bennett Park—$10K in Damage

Eastern Plains communities are learning a hard lesson about the cost of crime: thieves stole a backflow preventer valve at Bennett's Civic Center Park, triggering major flooding and over $10,000 in damage to public infrastructure (9News Denver). The Arapahoe County Sheriff's Office is investigating the theft, which has left a beloved community gathering space damaged and unusable.

This isn't just vandalism—it's a direct hit to taxpayers' wallets. Bennett residents and county officials now face repair bills for damage that could have been prevented with basic security measures. The incident underscores a broader problem: inadequate protection of public assets in smaller Colorado communities, where budgets are already stretched thin.

Conservatives have long argued that government must prioritize core functions—maintaining public safety and protecting taxpayer-funded infrastructure—before expanding into new programs or spending. When basic civic maintenance fails due to crime, it reveals misplaced priorities and poor stewardship of public resources.

The Sheriff's Office investigation is ongoing, but the real question for Bennett taxpayers is whether county leadership will invest in preventive security or simply absorb the cost of repeated incidents.

Watch whether Arapahoe County commissioners demand accountability from park management and implement cost-effective security upgrades—or quietly pass the bill to taxpayers again.
