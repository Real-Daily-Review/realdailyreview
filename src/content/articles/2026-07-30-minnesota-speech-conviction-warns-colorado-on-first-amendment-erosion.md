# Minnesota Case Raises Free Speech Red Flags for Colorado Gun Owners and First Amendment Advocates

A Minnesota jury's conviction of a woman for screaming a racial slur at a stranger signals how far prosecutors will stretch speech laws—a warning for Coloradans who value First Amendment protections.

Shiloh Hendrix was convicted in Rochester, Minnesota, after a nine-hour jury deliberation for directing offensive language at a man filming her on his phone. She was acquitted on a count tied to a boy she'd cursed at but convicted on the count involving the adult who recorded her (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative concern:** This case exemplifies how vague "harassment" and "disorderly conduct" statutes become weapons against unpopular speech. Once prosecutors and juries decide that offensive words—however crude—constitute criminal conduct, the First Amendment's protections evaporate. Colorado conservatives should pay attention: similar statutes exist on Colorado's books, and activist prosecutors could weaponize them against political opponents, gun rights advocates, or anyone whose speech offends the right judge or jury.

The distinction between Hendrix's acquittal and conviction hinges on who was targeted, not the words themselves—a dangerous precedent that makes criminal liability depend on a jury's subjective judgment about intent and harm.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado courts adopt Minnesota's expansive interpretation of speech crimes, and whether gun rights groups and free speech advocates challenge similar statutes statewide.
