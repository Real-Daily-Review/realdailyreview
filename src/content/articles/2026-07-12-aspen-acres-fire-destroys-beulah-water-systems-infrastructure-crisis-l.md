# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has left the small community of Beulah without reliable water service—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis now threatens the utilities and infrastructure that sustain rural life. According to the Colorado Sun, water managers are scrambling to restore clean water supplies after the blaze damaged critical systems serving the town of roughly 800 residents. Many homes were lost in the fire, compounding the disaster (CPR News).

This is precisely the kind of cascading infrastructure failure conservatives have warned about: when government fails to maintain adequate forest management and fuel reduction, taxpayers and private citizens pay the price. The state's decades-long resistance to aggressive logging and controlled burns—driven by environmental regulations and green ideology—has left Colorado's forests tinderboxes. Now Beulah residents face not just property loss but months or longer without basic utilities.

The good news: most other active fires in Colorado have spared critical water systems so far. But the bad news is grimmer. Rebuilding water infrastructure will demand substantial public spending, likely drawing on state and federal disaster funds that could have been prevented through smarter forest policy.

Watch whether state lawmakers will finally loosen environmental restrictions on forest management—or double down on the regulatory approach that helped create these conditions.
