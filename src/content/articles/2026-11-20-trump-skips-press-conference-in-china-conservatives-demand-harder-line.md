# Trump Skips Press Conference in China; Conservatives Want Harder Line on Beijing

President Trump concluded his China trip without holding a formal press conference—a break from typical presidential protocol on foreign visits, *CPR News* reports. The absence of on-the-record questioning fuels conservative concerns about how aggressively the administration is pressing Beijing on human rights and American interests.

*National Review* argues Trump should use such diplomatic moments to demand the release of democracy activist Joshua Wong, imprisoned for meeting with U.S. lawmakers. Conservatives view Wong's detention as a direct affront to American values and a test of whether the administration will prioritize human rights alongside trade negotiations with China.

The optics matter: Colorado voters and business leaders watching U.S.-China relations want clarity on whether Trump is securing favorable terms for American workers and energy exports, or whether diplomatic niceties are softening the administration's edge. Without a press conference, the public gets no direct accounting of what was negotiated—or conceded.

Watch whether Trump's team releases a detailed readout of Xi and Putin discussions, and whether future high-level meetings include formal press availability.
