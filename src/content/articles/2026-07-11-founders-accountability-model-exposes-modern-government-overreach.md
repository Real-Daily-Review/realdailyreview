# Founders' Vision of Accountability Still Beats Modern Government Overreach

America's founders understood something today's bloated federal bureaucracy has forgotten: government exists to serve the people, not the reverse.

That's the core argument emerging from conservative voices reflecting on the founders' legacy of auditing power. The Washington Examiner notes that 250 years ago, the founders "gathered facts, named abuses, and concluded that government is legitimate only when it answers to the people." That principle—accountability as the bedrock of liberty—stands in sharp contrast to modern regulatory agencies that operate with minimal oversight and answer primarily to themselves.

Kelley Paul's new children's book, "Good Night, Young American," frames America's founders as "the first civil rights heroes," emphasizing their commitment to individual liberty and limited government (Fox News). The book takes young readers through early American history, reinforcing foundational principles that progressives have systematically eroded through decades of regulatory expansion and centralized power.

For Colorado conservatives, the lesson is clear: the founders' insistence on checking government power remains the best defense against the regulatory overreach that strangles energy development, constrains water rights, and threatens property rights across the state. When unelected bureaucrats in Denver and Washington make decisions affecting Colorado ranchers, energy workers, and small businesses without meaningful accountability, they've abandoned the founders' core principle.

Watch whether Colorado legislators will embrace genuine accountability measures that actually constrain executive power—or merely pay lip service to limited government while expanding it.
