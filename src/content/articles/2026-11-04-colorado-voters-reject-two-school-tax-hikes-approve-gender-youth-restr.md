# Colorado Voters Reject Two of Three Major School Tax Hikes, Pass Gender-Policy Measures

Colorado taxpayers sent a clear message Tuesday: they're tired of blank checks for schools. Two of the state's three largest school districts saw their tax-increase ballot measures fail, even as voters narrowly approved two contentious transgender-youth policies (Complete Colorado).

Denver, Boulder, and Cherry Creek all sought additional funding. The mixed results suggest Coloradans want accountability before they open their wallets—a rebuke to districts that haven't made the case for fiscal discipline.

Meanwhile, Proposition 134 and Proposition 135 narrowly passed (CPR News). Prop 134 restricts transgender youth participation in sports; Prop 135 bans gender-affirming surgeries for minors. Democrats and progressive groups opposed both measures, arguing they discriminate against vulnerable youth and infringe on parental rights. Republicans countered that protecting fairness in women's sports and preventing irreversible medical procedures on minors reflects common-sense policy that majorities support.

The school-funding rejections underscore a broader Colorado trend: voters will fund education, but only when districts prove they're spending wisely. The gender-policy margins—narrow as they were—show the state remains divided on progressive social experiments, even as Denver's political class pushes them.

Watch whether failing districts return with leaner, more transparent budget proposals next cycle.
