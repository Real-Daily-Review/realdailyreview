# Denver's Soft-Touch Response to Illegal Street Takeovers Raises Questions About Public Safety

Denver police responded to an illegal street takeover near Broadway early Saturday but made zero arrests, instead relying on a civil nuisance ordinance to pursue drivers after the fact (9News Denver). The chaotic event—where neighbors reported disruption—highlights a troubling pattern: law enforcement using administrative tools rather than criminal enforcement to address lawlessness.

Republicans and public safety advocates argue that civil ordinances are no substitute for immediate arrest and prosecution. Without consequences at the scene, street takeovers signal to would-be offenders that Denver's response is toothless. The nuisance approach may generate paperwork, but it doesn't deter the next illegal gathering or protect residents from dangerous driving and property damage.

Democrats and progressive city officials counter that civil enforcement allows flexibility and avoids the criminal justice system's burden—a position consistent with their broader push to reduce arrests and incarceration.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners in Denver, the question is stark: Will administrative citations actually stop these events, or will they proliferate? Coloradans should watch whether Denver's police department receives political cover to make arrests at future takeovers, or whether the city doubles down on the civil ordinance gamble.
