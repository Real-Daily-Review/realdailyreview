# ICE Detentions at Seattle Airport Highlight Border Enforcement Amid Open-Border Pushback

Federal immigration officers arrested two visa overstay violators on an Alaska Airlines jet bridge at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport Saturday, sparking predictable outrage from progressive state officials who called the enforcement action "distressing" (Washington Examiner).

The masked ICE agents detained the two illegal immigrants during boarding, drawing complaints from state officials and the airline over the manner and location of the arrests. Democrats and open-border advocates argue such enforcement is heavy-handed and creates a chilling effect on air travel. They frame immigration enforcement as inherently aggressive when conducted visibly.

Republicans and immigration hawks counter that visa overstays represent a straightforward violation of federal law—these individuals had no legal right to remain in the country. Conservatives argue that enforcing immigration law is precisely ICE's mandate, and that states opposing such enforcement are undermining federal authority and rule of law.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado has seen its own immigration enforcement debates, particularly around sanctuary policies in Denver and Boulder. How state and local officials respond to federal immigration operations signals whether Colorado will cooperate with or obstruct ICE operations targeting criminal aliens and visa violators.
