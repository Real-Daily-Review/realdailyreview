# Trump Administration Eyes Venezuelan Oil Deal—But at What Cost to American Energy Independence?

The Trump administration is reportedly negotiating access to Venezuelan oil reserves, a move that raises hard questions about U.S. energy strategy and the rule of law. While proponents argue diversified foreign reserves could backstop American dominance if domestic shale production declines (Just The News), conservatives are split on the wisdom of the approach.

National Review warns the deal risks undermining the stable international order built on property rights and the rule of law—the very foundation that made America prosperous. The outlet notes the irony: Trump has repeatedly criticized Venezuela's Chávez regime for seizing American company assets, yet now appears willing to negotiate directly with that same government, potentially legitimizing past thefts.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil and gas industry depends on predictable, rules-based markets and respect for property rights. A U.S. deal that normalizes Venezuelan asset seizures could weaken the legal protections Colorado energy companies rely on abroad and at home.

Republicans and conservatives must ask whether short-term energy gains justify abandoning the principle that governments honor contracts and respect private property. Coloradans should watch whether this deal includes ironclad protections for American companies' historical claims—or whether it amounts to capitulation dressed up as pragmatism.
