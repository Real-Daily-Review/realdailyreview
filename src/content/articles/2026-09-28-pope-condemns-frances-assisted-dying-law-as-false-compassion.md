# Pope Rejects 'False Compassion' of Assisted Suicide as France Expands Death Law

Pope Leo XIV has directly challenged France's newly enacted assisted-dying law, declaring that "what is legal is not necessarily moral" during a hospital visit this week. Speaking at the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes, the pontiff condemned assisted suicide and euthanasia as expressions of "false compassion," emphasizing that "the human person is someone who is conceived and born" (Daily Caller).

The papal rebuke comes weeks after France legalized medical assistance in dying—joining a growing list of Western nations normalizing state-facilitated death. The pope's message cuts against the secular progressive consensus that frames such laws as compassionate policy.

**The Colorado angle:** While assisted dying remains illegal in Colorado, progressive activists have pushed similar legislation in blue states. The pope's intervention underscores the moral objection conservatives and religious Americans raise: that expanding death-on-demand policies, however framed, represent a fundamental shift away from protecting vulnerable life. Colorado's Catholic community and pro-life advocates will likely cite the pontiff's words in future debates over end-of-life legislation.

Conservatives should monitor whether Colorado Democrats attempt to resurrect assisted-dying bills in the coming legislative session—and whether the Vatican's moral authority influences the conversation.
