# Missouri GOP Fights for Favorable Map as Election Ballots Near

Missouri Republicans are back in federal court trying to salvage a congressional map that would give the GOP a 7-1 House advantage—a stark contrast to the 6-2 map currently set to govern November's elections after the U.S. Supreme Court blocked the GOP-friendly lines last week.

The Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals is now weighing whether to revive Missouri's attempt to reinstate the more favorable map, potentially sending the dispute to the Supreme Court for a third time (Washington Examiner). State officials are proceeding with the 6-2 map as ballots prepare to go out, but Republicans argue the newer 7-1 configuration better reflects voter preferences and GOP strength in the state.

The legal ping-pong underscores a broader conservative frustration: courts increasingly second-guess redistricting decisions made by elected state officials. Republicans contend that states should have broad authority to draw maps reflecting their political composition, while Democrats and progressive groups argue GOP maps constitute partisan gerrymandering that dilutes Democratic voting power.

**Colorado angle:** While Colorado's own redistricting battles have cooled, this Missouri case signals how redistricting disputes will remain a flashpoint heading into 2024. Colorado conservatives should watch whether the Supreme Court ultimately sides with state authority or continues blocking maps deemed too partisan.
