# Iran's Hormuz Demands Threaten U.S. Energy Security—and Colorado's Wallet

Negotiations over the Strait of Hormuz are unraveling as Iran's National Security Council Secretary Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr released a fresh list of demands, throwing this week's peace talks into chaos, according to the Washington Examiner. The uncertainty matters directly to Colorado: roughly 21% of global oil passes through the Hormuz Strait, and any disruption spikes energy prices at the pump and threatens Colorado's oil and gas industry.

Just The News reports that while Iran claims a deal is "near," the waterway may not reopen immediately even if negotiations succeed—leaving shipping companies and energy markets in limbo. That delay translates to sustained price pressure on Colorado consumers and businesses already battered by years of Biden-Harris energy policy that has strangled domestic production.

**The conservative argument:** This is what happens when the U.S. negotiates from weakness. Iran keeps moving the goalposts because it knows Washington is desperate for a deal. Republicans argue the Biden-Harris administration should have maintained Trump-era "maximum pressure" sanctions instead of chasing diplomatic fantasies that embolden Tehran.

Colorado taxpayers and energy workers should watch whether the Biden-Harris team capitulates to Iran's expanding demands—or finally takes a hard line that protects American energy independence and Colorado jobs.
