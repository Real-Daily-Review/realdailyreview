# World Leaders Honor Lindsey Graham as Senate Weighs Final Foreign Aid Push

Sen. Lindsey Graham's death removes a reliably hawkish voice from the Senate at a pivotal moment for U.S. foreign policy. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky are both traveling to Washington for Graham's funeral at the National Cathedral, underscoring his outsized influence on military aid to Israel and Ukraine (Fox News Politics, Washington Examiner).

The timing is significant: Graham's Russia sanctions bill faces a key Senate vote Tuesday night, hours after his funeral concludes. The legislation reflects Graham's long-standing advocacy for aggressive military support to Ukraine and Israel—positions that defined his final years in office.

**The conservative angle:** While Graham commanded respect across the aisle for his foreign policy engagement, his departure leaves questions about the Senate's appetite for continued massive aid packages to overseas conflicts. Republicans remain divided on Ukraine funding levels, with fiscal conservatives increasingly skeptical of open-ended commitments. Graham's bill will test whether his legacy can drive votes in a chamber now more cautious about defense spending priorities.

Colorado voters should watch whether the Senate passes Graham's sanctions measure and what it signals about future aid votes—a bellwether for how aggressively Washington will continue writing checks for foreign conflicts.
