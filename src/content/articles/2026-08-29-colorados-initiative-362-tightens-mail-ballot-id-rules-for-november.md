# Colorado Voters to Get New ID Requirements for Mail Ballots—Finally

Colorado's all-mail voting system is getting tighter security. Initiative 362, heading to November voters, would add new voter authentication requirements for mail ballots—a long-overdue safeguard that conservatives have championed as essential to election integrity (Complete Colorado).

Republicans and election-security advocates argue that Colorado's current mail-voting model, while convenient, lacks sufficient identity verification. Initiative 362 addresses that gap directly. The measure comes as Colorado election officials have faced mounting pressure over ballot security, with the Trump administration and Republican leaders nationally pushing states to tighten mail-voting protocols (Denver Post).

Democrats and voting-rights groups counter that Colorado's existing mail system is secure and that new ID requirements could suppress turnout among eligible voters. Attorney Andrew Garber argued that "the president still has no authority to determine who gets to vote by mail," signaling left-wing resistance to federal pressure on state election procedures (Denver Post).

Colorado's all-mail system has long drawn criticism from conservatives who worry about ballot chain-of-custody and signature-verification standards. Initiative 362 represents a chance for voters to strengthen those protections at the ballot box—without federal mandates.
