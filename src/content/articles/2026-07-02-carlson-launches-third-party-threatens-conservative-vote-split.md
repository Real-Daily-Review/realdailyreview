# Tucker Carlson's Third-Party Gambit Could Fracture Conservative Vote in Colorado and Beyond

Tucker Carlson announced Wednesday he's abandoning the Republican Party to build a third political party, declaring to the Columbia Journalism Review that "there should be a good-faith effort to figure out what benefits the country." The move marks a dramatic break from the GOP after months of tension with former President Trump and party leadership.

Carlson, a prominent right-wing commentator, has blasted what he calls a "lock-step" political establishment dominated by war and finance interests that ignore ordinary Americans. According to the Daily Caller, he vowed "to do everything I can" to construct an alternative vehicle for voters dissatisfied with both major parties.

**The Colorado angle:** Third-party movements historically splinter conservative and Republican votes in swing states and competitive races. Colorado, increasingly purple and home to libertarian-leaning voters, could see Carlson's effort siphon support from GOP candidates in 2026 and 2028 races—potentially handing seats to Democrats in state legislative contests and congressional races where margins are tight.

Republicans argue that third-party ventures weaken conservative electoral power precisely when unified opposition to progressive taxation, gun control, and regulatory overreach is essential. Conservatives worry Carlson's venture could repeat the 2016 Libertarian spoiler effect.
