# Vice President Vance Downplays Iran Escalation as "Fight," Not War

Vice President JD Vance is drawing a semantic line between U.S. military strikes on Iran and an actual declaration of war, even as Iranian forces continue retaliatory attacks on American allies in the Gulf region (9News Denver). The distinction matters: calling it a "fight" rather than a "war" sidesteps congressional war powers requirements and keeps the administration's hands freer operationally.

Meanwhile, a separate controversy underscores the stakes of vetting foreign nationals for sensitive government work. Canada awarded over $2 million in law enforcement and defense contracts to Mohammad Reza Ghafouri Fard, an Iranian immigrant with alleged ties to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and son of a former Iranian vice president (Breitbart). The arrangement raises hard questions about due diligence in allied governments—and by extension, U.S. vetting standards.

For Colorado, the broader concern is clear: unclear rules of engagement in the Middle East create uncertainty for energy markets and military families stationed at bases like Fort Carson and Buckley Space Force Base. Vague legal frameworks around military action also invite executive overreach—exactly the kind of unchecked power conservatives typically oppose.

Watch whether Congress demands clarity on Iran escalation rules or whether the administration continues operating in the gray zone between "fight" and "war."
