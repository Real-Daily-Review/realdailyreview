# Denver's Aging Water Infrastructure Crumbles—Again

Denver's crumbling water infrastructure is failing residents once more, with a 69-year-old water main bursting on South Holly Street and leaving eight homes without water while the city scrambles to make repairs (KDVR). The break forced a multi-day closure of South Holly Street between East Evans and East Iliff avenues, with the road not expected to reopen until Sunday night (9News Denver).

This isn't an isolated incident. Denver Water's aging pipe network—much of it dating back decades—continues to rupture under pressure, disrupting traffic, cutting off water service, and forcing expensive emergency repairs that ultimately fall on taxpayers. While city officials tout infrastructure investment, the reality is that deferred maintenance and poor long-term planning have left Colorado's largest city vulnerable to preventable service failures.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that Denver's government has prioritized spending on progressive pet projects over basic infrastructure maintenance—the unglamorous but essential work that keeps a city functioning. Better budgeting and accountability could have prevented this disruption and the costs associated with emergency repairs.

The broader lesson: government must focus on core services and fiscal discipline, not mission creep.

**Watch whether Denver Water's next budget proposal includes real accountability measures or simply requests more taxpayer funding to patch problems that should have been addressed years ago.**
