# Colorado Voters Face Perfect Storm: TABOR Refunds May Vanish Forever

Colorado taxpayers are staring down a fiscal cliff that could eliminate TABOR refunds permanently, even as voters weigh competing ballot measures on redistricting and tax policy (Complete Colorado). Proposition NN threatens to redirect what would otherwise be refunds back to state coffers—a move conservatives warn amounts to a backdoor tax increase that erodes the Taxpayer Bill of Rights that has protected Colorado wallets for decades.

Meanwhile, Initiative #256 would impose new guardrails on congressional redistricting, banning maps drawn to favor either political party. While anti-gerrymandering language sounds appealing, Republicans worry the measure could constrain legitimate map-drawing and hand power to unelected commissions.

The timing is no accident. Democrats are using Prop NN to reframe tax refunds as "lost revenue" that could fund schools and infrastructure—classic progressive framing that treats taxpayer money as government property first. Conservatives counter that TABOR refunds belong to Coloradans, not the state, and that spending discipline, not higher taxes, should drive budget decisions.

For Colorado's business community and working families already squeezed by inflation, these measures represent a fundamental question: Who owns your money—you or Denver?

Watch whether voters reject Prop NN and affirm TABOR protections, or whether the perfect storm of ballot confusion and progressive messaging finally cracks Colorado's tax-limitation armor.
