# Trump Administration Eyes Yosemite Land Deal; Colorado Should Demand Clarity on Public Lands

The Trump administration is reportedly pursuing a land exchange to transfer a portion of Yosemite National Park to a private Nevada-based company for development, according to the Washington Examiner. The deal would involve swapping federal acreage for private holdings—a mechanism that raises immediate questions for Colorado conservatives wary of precedent.

Land exchanges are legally permissible under federal law, but they require transparency and public input. Republicans generally favor private stewardship over federal bureaucracy, yet public lands remain a flashpoint: Colorado's outdoor economy and hunting heritage depend on predictable access to federal acreage. Any Yosemite precedent could signal the administration's willingness to privatize portions of Colorado's own federal holdings—including lands managed by the Bureau of Land Management and National Forests that hunters, ranchers, and energy developers rely on.

The administration has not detailed the Yosemite exchange's terms, the acreage involved, or whether similar deals are planned elsewhere. Conservatives should support market-driven solutions to land management inefficiency, but only with full disclosure. Federal land transfers affecting Colorado require legislative approval and public scrutiny—not backroom Nevada deals.

Watch whether the administration seeks Congressional authority for broader land-exchange authority or pursues similar swaps affecting Colorado's BLM and Forest Service holdings.
