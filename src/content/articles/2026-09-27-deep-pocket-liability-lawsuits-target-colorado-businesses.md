# Deep-Pocket Liability Lawsuits Are Shaking Down Colorado Businesses

Colorado companies face a growing threat from lawsuit tactics that target deep pockets rather than actual wrongdoers—a trend that inflates costs for taxpayers and businesses statewide.

The problem is straightforward: when accidents happen, trial lawyers increasingly sue solvent companies tangentially connected to harm, even when those companies bear no direct responsibility. The *Washington Examiner* warns this "deep-pocket liability" strategy has become a feature of American civil litigation, shifting accountability from the guilty party to whoever has the deepest wallet.

For Colorado, this matters. Energy companies, manufacturers, construction firms, and agricultural businesses operating here face exposure to lawsuits designed to extract settlements rather than achieve justice. When companies get hit with inflated liability claims, those costs get passed to Colorado consumers and workers through higher prices and fewer jobs.

Republicans and tort-reform advocates argue the solution is clear: courts should enforce actual causation. Companies should only pay for harm they genuinely caused. Democrats and trial lawyers counter that broad liability exposure encourages safety compliance across supply chains—though critics say that's just legal-speak for "shake down the rich guy in the room."

Colorado lawmakers should watch whether trial lawyers push for legislation that expands liability standards, making it easier to sue businesses far removed from actual negligence.
