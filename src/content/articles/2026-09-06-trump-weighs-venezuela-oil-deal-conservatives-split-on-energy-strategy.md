# Trump Administration Weighs Venezuela Oil Deal—But at What Cost to American Energy Independence?

The Trump administration is reportedly exploring an oil deal with Venezuela that has conservatives divided: some see it as pragmatic energy strategy, others as a dangerous compromise of American principles and domestic energy dominance.

Just The News reports the arrangement could shore up U.S. energy leadership if domestic shale production declines over time, positioning America to maintain global energy dominance through diversified resources. The logic is straightforward—secure foreign supply chains before domestic wells age out.

But National Review raises sharp objections. The outlet argues the deal reeks of cynicism, particularly given Trump's past criticism of Venezuela's seizure of American company assets. More fundamentally, National Review contends that abandoning commitment to stable international rule of law—the foundation of American prosperity—undermines the very world order that made the U.S. prosperous in the first place.

For Colorado, the stakes are direct: the state's oil and gas industry depends on robust domestic shale development and market confidence in American energy policy. Any pivot toward Venezuelan crude could signal weakening commitment to Colorado's energy workers and producers.

Conservatives should watch whether this deal includes enforceable protections for American companies and whether it genuinely supplements—or quietly replaces—investment in Colorado's proven shale reserves.
