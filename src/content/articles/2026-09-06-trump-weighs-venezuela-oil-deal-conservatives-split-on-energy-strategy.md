# Trump Administration Weighs Venezuela Oil Deal—But at What Cost to American Energy Independence?

The Trump administration is reportedly exploring an oil deal with Venezuela, a move that has split conservatives over whether it secures U.S. energy dominance or abandons American companies and the rule of law.

*National Review* argues the proposal is cynical: Trump has repeatedly attacked Venezuelan strongman Nicolás Maduro for seizing American property, yet now appears willing to negotiate with the regime—potentially nullifying legitimate claims by U.S. firms. The outlet warns that abandoning a stable international order based on property rights and rule of law undermines the very foundation of American prosperity.

Supporters counter that diversifying energy sources makes strategic sense. *Just The News* notes that while American shale production remains world-class, those wells naturally decline over time. Securing Venezuelan reserves could help the U.S. maintain global energy leadership if domestic output falls.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's oil and gas industry depends on stable markets and predictable policy. Any deal that props up a hostile regime while potentially flooding markets with cheap foreign oil could undercut domestic producers and energy workers here at home—exactly when Colorado needs robust energy development to fund schools and infrastructure.

Conservatives should watch whether this deal includes protections for American companies' existing claims and whether it genuinely strengthens U.S. energy independence or simply enriches a dictator.
