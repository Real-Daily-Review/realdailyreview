# Aspen Acres Fire Exposes Colorado's Insurance Crisis in Wildfire Country

Colorado's wildfire insurance problem is no longer theoretical—it's burning down Pueblo County homes right now. The Aspen Acres Fire has displaced nearly 240 animals into emergency shelters while exposing a critical gap: many homeowners in fire-prone zones can't find or afford coverage, leaving them financially naked when flames arrive.

A Colorado State University researcher told 9News the insurance crunch threatens not just individual families but the long-term recovery of entire wildfire-prone communities. When insurers flee high-risk areas or jack up premiums beyond reason, property values crater, tax bases shrivel, and rebuilding becomes impossible for ordinary Coloradans.

This is what happens when government regulation and market uncertainty collide. Insurers face unpredictable liability exposure, wildfire frequency keeps climbing, and state regulators limit how much companies can charge to cover actual risk. The result: carriers abandon Colorado's most vulnerable communities, leaving residents to either self-insure or go without.

Drone footage from the Pueblo County Sheriff's Office shows the scale of destruction in Beulah's North Creek Road area—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire season isn't slowing down.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether state lawmakers will loosen insurance regulations to let the market function, or double down on government mandates that chase carriers out of state entirely.
