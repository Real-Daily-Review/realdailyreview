# Aurora's Nine Mile Bridge: Another Government Infrastructure Project Amid Safety Concerns

Aurora is pouring taxpayer dollars into a new pedestrian bridge at Nine Mile Station, expected to wrap by next fall (9News Denver). The project will connect RTD's Nine Mile Station to the north side of Parker Road—a infrastructure play city officials say will improve transit access.

But here's the rub: Aurora continues grappling with pedestrian safety failures. Just this week, a 35-year-old man was killed after being struck by two vehicles in Aurora (KDVR Fox31), underscoring the gap between transit infrastructure spending and actual street safety enforcement.

Conservatives question whether Aurora's priorities are aligned. While the city greenlights expensive bridge construction, basic traffic enforcement and driver accountability remain inconsistent. The real issue isn't always more infrastructure—it's whether existing roads are managed safely and whether transit projects deliver real ridership gains or simply expand government's footprint.

RTD's track record of cost overruns and ridership shortfalls across the metro area raises legitimate questions about whether this Nine Mile investment will justify its price tag or become another example of government spending that benefits contractors more than commuters.

Watch whether Aurora's city council demands accountability metrics—actual ridership numbers, safety improvements, and cost controls—before breaking ground on the next transit project.
