# Insurance Tracking Apps Punish Safe Driving—and Your Privacy

State Farm's usage-based insurance app promises lower premiums for safe drivers, but the reality for Colorado motorists is far grimmer: the algorithm penalizes defensive maneuvers like hard braking to avoid accidents, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The pitch sounds consumer-friendly in an era of runaway inflation. Download the app, let your insurer monitor your driving habits, and watch your rates drop. Major carriers market this as a fair system that rewards genuinely safe drivers while ditching one-size-fits-all risk pools.

But here's the catch: State Farm's Drive Safe app doesn't distinguish between reckless braking and emergency braking. A driver who slams on the brakes to avoid a collision—the definition of defensive driving—gets dinged the same as someone who drives recklessly. That's not fairness; it's algorithmic punishment for responsible behavior.

Conservatives and privacy advocates raise two core objections: First, insurers are collecting granular personal data through constant vehicle monitoring, raising serious privacy concerns for Colorado drivers. Second, opaque algorithms make it impossible for consumers to understand why their premiums rise or fall, creating a system where Big Insurance holds all the cards.

Colorado regulators should scrutinize whether these tracking programs actually incentivize safety or simply extract more data and money from drivers who thought they were playing by the rules.
