# Denver's Driverless Future: Job Losses Loom as Waymo Launches

Denver is about to learn whether Silicon Valley's automation dreams trump working Coloradans' paychecks. Waymo's driverless taxi service officially launched in Denver this week, and rideshare drivers are understandably nervous—this technology could eliminate thousands of jobs across the state's transportation sector.

The company's autonomous vehicles are now picking up passengers in the Mile High City, marking another step toward a future where tech giants replace human workers with algorithms. While tech enthusiasts celebrate the innovation, the economic reality for Denver's gig-economy workers is stark: fewer rides for human drivers means fewer dollars in their pockets.

**Conservatives argue** that while free markets should reward innovation, policymakers must acknowledge the genuine disruption this causes working families. Uncontrolled automation without workforce transition planning leaves workers stranded. Colorado's economy depends on jobs that pay mortgages and support families—not just venture capital returns.

Democrats and progressive advocates typically frame automation as inevitable progress, but they've offered little concrete support for displaced workers beyond vague retraining promises that rarely materialize.

The question for Colorado taxpayers: Will the state rush to subsidize Waymo's expansion while leaving rideshare drivers to fend for themselves, or will policymakers demand accountability from tech companies reshaping our labor market?
