# Supreme Court Splits on Presidential Power to Fire Regulators

The Supreme Court has handed President Trump a mixed verdict on his authority to remove federal regulators, upholding his firing of FTC Commissioner Rebecca Slaughter while blocking his removal of Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook without due process.

The Slaughter decision (9News Denver) represents a significant win for presidential power, testing decades-old limits on executive authority over independent agencies. Conservatives argue this restores proper checks on the regulatory state—a key Trump priority to dismantle what they view as unaccountable bureaucratic overreach.

However, the Cook ruling (Breitbart) imposes procedural guardrails: the president must provide notice and a hearing before removing Fed governors, limiting Trump's ability to reshape monetary policy leadership quickly.

**The Colorado angle:** These decisions affect how aggressively the administration can reshape financial regulation and consumer protection rules that impact Colorado businesses, energy companies, and taxpayers. Conservatives have long argued federal agencies like the FTC and Federal Reserve operate with too little presidential accountability; this ruling is a partial victory but leaves significant constraints in place.

Republicans view the split decision as incomplete—they wanted clearer presidential removal authority to streamline the regulatory apparatus. Democrats counter that procedural protections preserve agency independence from political pressure.

**Watch next:** Whether Trump pursues legislative action to clarify removal authority or focuses energy on agencies where his power remains unchecked.
