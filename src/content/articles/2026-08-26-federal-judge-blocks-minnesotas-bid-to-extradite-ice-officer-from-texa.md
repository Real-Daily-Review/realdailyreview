# Federal Judge Blocks Minnesota's Bid to Extradite ICE Officer from Texas

A federal judge has dealt a significant blow to Minnesota's attempt to force Texas Gov. Greg Abbott to extradite an ICE officer facing assault charges, potentially allowing the agent to walk free—a decision that underscores the limits of one state's power to compel another into extradition proceedings.

U.S. District Judge Fernando Rodriguez Jr. rejected Minnesota's lawsuit as premature, ruling that Abbott had not actually denied the extradition request, according to the Washington Examiner. The ICE agent, Christian Castro, is currently in Texas custody but is wanted on state charges in Minnesota stemming from a nonfatal shooting of an immigrant.

Minnesota argued that Abbott's role in the extradition process was merely ministerial—that he had no discretion to refuse—but the judge disagreed (Just The News). The ruling raises concerns that Castro could be released without facing Minnesota charges if the state cannot compel Texas cooperation.

The decision highlights a federalism question: whether governors can be forced by federal courts to comply with extradition demands from other states. Texas conservatives and Abbott allies likely view the ruling as protecting state sovereignty against federal judicial overreach, while Minnesota officials see it as a roadblock to justice.

Coloradans should watch whether this precedent affects how states handle interstate extradition disputes involving federal agents.
