# Thanksgiving Storm Hammers Nation; Colorado Braces for Winter Weather Ahead

Severe weather swept across much of the United States over Thanksgiving, with torrential rain, high winds, and flooding affecting both coasts and prompting the first-ever postponement of Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver and 9News Denver.

The storm knocked out power to nearly a million customers and flooded East Coast communities. While Colorado escaped the worst of this particular system, the state faces its own winter weather challenges as the season progresses—a reminder that reliable energy infrastructure and emergency preparedness matter far more than climate-panic theater.

Conservatives note that extreme weather events are neither new nor proof of climate catastrophe. What matters is whether Colorado maintains affordable, dependable power generation (including natural gas and coal) to keep families warm and hospitals running when storms hit. Progressive demands to shutter reliable baseload power plants in favor of intermittent renewables risk leaving Coloradans vulnerable during exactly these kinds of emergencies.

The state's grid operators and emergency management agencies should prioritize resilience and redundancy—not ideological energy mandates that sacrifice reliability for green credentials.

Watch for how Colorado's Public Utilities Commission responds to winter demand and whether it continues pushing utilities toward aggressive renewable timelines that could compromise grid stability.
