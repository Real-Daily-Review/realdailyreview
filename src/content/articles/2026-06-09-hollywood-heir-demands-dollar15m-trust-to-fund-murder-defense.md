# Hollywood Heir Seeks $1.5M Trust to Fund Murder Defense

A California court is weighing whether to release trust funds to Rob Reiner's son Nick—the very person accused of killing the parents who established that trust.

Nick Reiner is demanding access to approximately $1.5 million from a 1993 trust his parents created, claiming he needs the money to hire lawyers for his defense against murder charges (Breitbart). According to the petition, distributions were mandatory at ages 30 and 35, and Reiner says he never received them.

The case presents a stark legal question: should a defendant accused of murdering his parents be allowed to raid their estate to pay for his defense? The trust language specifies distributions are "mandatory and unconditional" (Just The News), which could force the trustee's hand—though courts may intervene on public policy grounds.

Reiner's legal team argues "time is of the essence" (Daily Caller), suggesting he cannot mount an adequate defense without immediate access. Prosecutors and the trust's administrators will likely argue that allowing a murder defendant to liquidate his alleged victims' assets for legal fees sets a dangerous precedent and potentially taints the estate.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether this case influences how state courts handle similar trust disputes involving criminal defendants—a principle that could affect property rights and estate law statewide.
