# Colorado conservatives should watch Democratic infighting—but don't expect it to change Colorado policy anytime soon

Minnesota Democrats face a Tuesday primary showdown between Rep. Angie Craig and Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan, signaling an internal battle over the party's direction after 2024 losses (Washington Examiner). The race pits the progressive wing against pragmatists—a split that mirrors national Democratic fractures.

For Colorado, the takeaway is limited. While Minnesota Democrats wrestle with ideology, Colorado's Democratic supermajorities in the legislature continue advancing their own agenda without meaningful internal resistance. Whether Minnesota picks a progressive or centrist nominee won't slow Colorado's tax-and-regulate machine or protect Colorado energy workers from the state's aggressive climate mandates.

Republicans argue that Democratic civil wars, while entertaining, distract from the real issue: unified Democratic control of Colorado government. Conservatives say the focus should remain on 2026 state elections, where Republicans can actually challenge Democratic dominance on taxes, education freedom, and energy policy—not on Democratic primary theater in other states.

The lesson for Colorado taxpayers: internal Democratic disagreements rarely translate to policy relief. Watch instead for Colorado Republican primary races and whether the GOP can field competitive candidates for governor and legislature.
