# U.S. Strikes Iran Again as Ceasefire Collapses Over Shipping Attacks

A fragile interim ceasefire between the U.S. and Iran is unraveling fast. After Tehran launched a drone attack on a commercial vessel in the Strait of Hormuz early Saturday, the U.S. military responded with strikes against multiple Iranian targets (Washington Examiner). Iran then claimed it had attacked American military assets, further escalating tensions (Daily Caller).

The cycle of tit-for-tat strikes threatens the deal both sides had reached to wind down hostilities. According to 9News Denver, the ongoing strikes in the Persian Gulf come despite the interim agreement meant to end the war. CENTCOM officials stated the U.S. strikes were "in direct response to continued Iranian aggression against commercial shipping" (Washington Examiner).

**The Colorado angle:** Instability in the Strait of Hormuz—a critical global oil chokepoint—directly affects energy prices at the pump and heating costs for Colorado families and businesses. Any sustained conflict risks spiking fuel costs and disrupting the energy markets Colorado's economy depends on.

Conservatives argue the Biden-Harris administration's weak negotiating posture and inconsistent deterrence messaging invited Iranian aggression in the first place. A credible threat of overwhelming force, not endless diplomacy, prevents wars.

**Watch:** Whether the administration can stabilize the ceasefire or whether this becomes a larger regional conflict that drives energy prices higher heading into winter.
