# Trump Inks Iran Deal at Versailles—But Colorado Energy Sector Watches Closely

President Trump signed a physical copy of a U.S.-Iran agreement Wednesday while dining with French President Emmanuel Macron at the Palace of Versailles, according to 9News Denver. The deal marks a significant diplomatic shift after years of tension between Washington and Tehran.

Just The News reports that Pope Leo has commended the agreement ahead of the formal signing, noting the pontiff and Trump have previously clashed over the president's Iran policy.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy markets will be watching. Any normalization with Iran—a major oil and gas producer—could affect global energy prices and domestic production decisions. Colorado's oil and gas industry, already navigating regulatory headwinds from state Democrats, faces potential market volatility if Iranian crude floods global markets. Lower energy prices might ease consumer costs but could pressure Colorado producers' margins and employment.

**What conservatives say:** Republicans have traditionally opposed Iran deals they view as appeasement, arguing Tehran cannot be trusted and that sanctions leverage is more effective. Trump's previous withdrawal from the 2015 nuclear agreement reflected that skepticism. Whether this new deal includes sufficient verification mechanisms and sanctions relief remains a key conservative concern.

Watch whether this agreement includes provisions that protect American energy independence and Colorado's oil and gas workers—or whether it opens the door to cheaper Iranian oil that undercuts domestic production.
