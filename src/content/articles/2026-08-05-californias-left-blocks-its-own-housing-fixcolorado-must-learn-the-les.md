# California's Housing Chaos Shows Why Colorado Must Guard Against Progressive Overreach

California's left-wing establishment is eating itself alive over housing policy—and Colorado should take notes on what not to do.

Jane Fonda and Wendy Schmidt, wife of Google billionaire Eric Schmidt, are opposing Prop. 45, a proposal meant to ease California's housing crisis by loosening zoning restrictions (New York Post). The irony is sharp: ultra-wealthy progressives blocking market-friendly reforms from their palatial estates while claiming to champion affordability.

Meanwhile, California Democrats doubled down on economic dysfunction by endorsing a "billionaire tax"—a 5% wealth levy that the Washington Examiner warns will accelerate the state's taxpayer exodus and fund endless government spending.

Here's the conservative takeaway: California's housing shortage isn't a market failure—it's a government failure. Decades of restrictive zoning, environmental red tape, and anti-development regulations created scarcity. The left's answer? More taxes and wealth confiscation, not deregulation. Even progressive billionaires recognize that Prop. 45's zoning reforms would actually work.

Colorado faces similar pressures. Progressive lawmakers and activist groups routinely block housing development and push tax hikes under the guise of "affordability" and "climate action." Yet they resist the one thing that actually works: removing government barriers to building.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats embrace California's failed wealth-tax model or learn that free markets, not confiscation, solve housing crises.**
