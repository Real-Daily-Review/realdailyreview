# Colorado GOP's Messy Primary Leaves Liberty Advocates Bracing for General Election Disaster

Colorado Republicans have nominated Victor Marx, a first-time candidate and nonprofit leader with a controversial personal history, as their gubernatorial pick—a choice that has conservative observers openly worried about November's prospects.

Marx narrowly defeated state Sen. Barb Kirkmeyer in a race that remained too close to call for days, with roughly a 2,000-vote margin separating the two (Denver Post). The primary process itself drew sharp criticism from the right: Complete Colorado's analysis suggests primary voters delivered "on the Colorado GOP's death wish," with only two of 18 Republican gubernatorial candidates advancing out of the assembly process—a winnowing that left the field dangerously thin.

The general election matchup now shifts focus to Colorado's 8th Congressional District, where Rep. Gabe Evans faces Democrat Manny Rutinel. Democratic strategist David Boian framed the race squarely around kitchen-table economics: "Where the race is going is strictly to people's pocketbooks. Can they get to the store and get food for their kids?" (Denver Post)—a messaging strategy that underscores how inflation and cost-of-living remain central to Colorado voters' concerns heading into fall.

Conservatives should watch whether Marx can consolidate GOP support and whether Evans' economic messaging can overcome Democratic turnout advantages in the 8th.
