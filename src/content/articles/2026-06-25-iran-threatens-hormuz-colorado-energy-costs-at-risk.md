# Hormuz Tensions Threaten Colorado Energy Prices as Iran Escalates

Escalating Iranian threats to the Strait of Hormuz—a critical global oil chokepoint—risk spiking energy costs for Colorado families and businesses already burdened by years of regulatory overreach on domestic energy production.

The flareup follows intensified fighting between Israel and Iranian-backed Hezbollah militants, which has destabilized the region and prompted Iran's Revolutionary Guard to issue fresh warnings against tanker traffic (9News Denver). Oil shipments through the strait remain contested, creating supply uncertainty that typically translates to pump prices and heating costs Coloradans can't afford.

Republicans and conservatives argue the Biden-Harris administration's war on domestic oil and gas—through permitting delays, lease restrictions, and environmental regulations—has left America dangerously dependent on volatile Middle Eastern supplies. National Review notes the administration has no constitutional authority to impose tolls or controls on international shipping, underscoring the limits of executive overreach even in foreign policy.

Colorado's energy sector, which supports thousands of jobs and state revenues, has already suffered from federal restrictions on drilling and development. Tighter global supplies only worsen the case for unleashing American energy independence through expanded domestic production.

Watch whether the administration pivots toward supporting Colorado oil and gas development as a hedge against Middle Eastern instability—or doubles down on green mandates that keep Americans reliant on foreign energy.
