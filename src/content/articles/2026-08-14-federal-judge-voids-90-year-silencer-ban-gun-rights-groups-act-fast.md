# Federal Judge Strikes Down 90-Year-Old Silencer Restrictions, Vindicating Gun Rights

A federal court has ruled that decades-old National Firearms Act restrictions on silencer transfers are unconstitutional, handing gun rights advocates a major victory that Colorado Second Amendment supporters have long sought.

Gun Owners of America members have already begun completing silencer transfers without federal registration—the first time in 90 years such transactions have occurred outside the NFA framework (Fox News, New York Post). The ruling represents a significant rollback of federal firearms regulations that conservatives argue have infringed on constitutional rights without meaningful public safety justification.

**The conservative case:** Gun rights groups contend the NFA's silencer restrictions, enacted in 1934, were never grounded in constitutional authority and have served primarily to burden law-abiding citizens while criminals ignore the rules. Advocates argue silencers reduce hearing damage and are standard equipment in other countries with no correlation to violent crime.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** The state's gun owners—already operating under some of the nation's strictest magazine and "red flag" laws—now have potential federal relief from one major regulatory burden. However, Colorado's Democratic legislature may attempt to impose state-level restrictions to circumvent the federal ruling.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats move to codify silencer restrictions at the state level, or whether the ruling prompts legal challenges to other federal firearms regulations.
