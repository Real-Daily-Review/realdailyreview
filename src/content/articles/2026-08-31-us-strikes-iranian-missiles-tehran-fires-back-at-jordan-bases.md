# U.S. Strikes Iranian Missile Sites; Tehran Retaliates Against Jordan Bases

The U.S. military struck Iranian missile launchers on Larak Island Sunday, prompting an immediate Iranian ballistic missile attack on American bases in Jordan—a dangerous escalation that underscores the fragility of Middle East stability and the stakes of confronting Tehran's regional aggression.

U.S. Central Command targeted the Iranian positions after intelligence indicated Tehran was preparing to deploy sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global shipping lane (Just The News). Iran responded by firing ballistic missiles at U.S. military installations in Jordan; all incoming missiles were intercepted with no reported impacts (Washington Examiner).

The exchange reflects the Biden administration's stated commitment to defending American forces and regional partners against Iranian threats. However, conservatives argue the administration's inconsistent messaging on Iran—coupled with earlier concessions on sanctions and the nuclear deal—has emboldened Tehran's provocations. Republicans contend that strength and clarity deter adversaries; mixed signals invite escalation.

For Colorado, this matters: defense contractors and energy markets tied to Middle East stability employ thousands of state residents. Disruptions to global oil supplies could ripple through Colorado's economy and energy sector.

Watch whether the White House escalates further or signals restraint—and whether Congress demands clarity on the administration's Iran strategy before the situation spirals.
