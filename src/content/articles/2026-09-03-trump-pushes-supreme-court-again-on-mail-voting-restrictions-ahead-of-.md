# Trump Escalates Supreme Court Fight Over Mail-Voting Rules as Midterms Loom

The Trump administration is back before the Supreme Court pushing restrictions on mail-in voting—a move that could reshape how Colorado and other states conduct elections. According to *Just The News*, Trump's team is asking the high court to allow an executive order that would block the U.S. Postal Service from delivering absentee ballots to voters not on state-approved rolls.

Trump has long opposed mail voting, blaming it (without evidence, per *9News Denver*) for his 2020 loss, though he himself uses absentee ballots. A federal judge already blocked the order; the Supreme Court's 6-3 conservative majority previously struck down an earlier injunction, signaling potential sympathy for the administration's position.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue mail-voting systems invite fraud and administrative chaos, and that tightening voter rolls protects election integrity. Stricter ballot-delivery rules could reduce errors and ensure only eligible voters receive ballots.

**Democrats counter** that mail voting is secure and that restricting it disenfranchises legitimate voters, particularly in rural and underserved areas.

For Colorado—a state with robust mail-voting infrastructure and a Democratic lean—this fight matters. Any Supreme Court ruling could force the state to overhaul its election procedures, potentially sparking legislative battles in Denver.

Watch whether the Supreme Court takes the case and how quickly it rules before the midterms.
