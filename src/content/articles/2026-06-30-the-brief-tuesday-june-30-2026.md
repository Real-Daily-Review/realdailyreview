# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Tuesday, June 30, 2026

Colorado's Fourth of July week opens with a fight over political independence at the state level, a major legislative maneuver in Washington that could reshape federal spending, and a reminder that the left's contempt for American symbols isn't slowing down. Here's what you need to know.

## Colorado Takes a Stand: Separating State from Government Party Privilege

A push is underway in Colorado to strip special government recognition and privileges from political parties — and conservatives are calling it the right move. The argument is straightforward: the state has no proper business elevating one political organization over another, or entangling government machinery with party structures. Republicans and limited-government advocates say this kind of separation is long overdue in a state where Democrats have used institutional power to entrench their political advantage. Watch this one closely — it's a structural reform with real teeth.

---

## Johnson Ties SAVE America Act to Defense Bill — High-Stakes Gamble

Speaker Mike Johnson announced a plan to attach the SAVE America Act to the $1.15 trillion annual defense spending bill, a bold move aimed at breaking the House gridlock. The strategy is risky: bundling the two could complicate passage of the defense bill itself. Conservatives say the maneuver reflects the hard reality of governing with a razor-thin majority, but Colorado's defense contractors, military families at Fort Carson and Buckley, and the broader national security community will be watching whether Johnson can thread the needle without tanking the Pentagon's budget.

---

## Trump Nominates Sonderling to Lead Labor Department

President Trump nominated acting Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling to permanently lead the Department of Labor, announcing the pick on Truth Social. Conservatives have broadly welcomed Sonderling's tenure, viewing him as a counterweight to the regulatory overreach and union-first agenda that defined the Biden Labor Department. For Colorado's small businesses, ranchers, and energy employers — who spent years absorbing burdensome federal labor rules — a permanent, confirmed secretary aligned with free-market principles matters more than Washington insiders might admit.

## The Left's War on the American Flag

With Independence Day days away, a pointed reminder: the left's hostility toward the American flag is not a fringe position — it's increasingly mainstream on the progressive side of the aisle. Conservatives argue the flag represents freedom, prosperity, and national identity earned through sacrifice. For Coloradans preparing to celebrate the Fourth, the contrast couldn't be sharper: one side sees the flag as a symbol worth honoring; the other treats it as an object of grievance. That divide is not a media narrative — it's a values test.

## Chatfield Boat Capsizing: 9 Rescued on Colorado Waters

Nine people were rescued by fellow boaters after a boat capsized at Chatfield State Park, with Mesa County Search and Rescue teams working through the night to evacuate rafters in a separate incident. No government program pulled these people to safety — other Coloradans on the water did. It's a reminder that community, personal responsibility, and neighbor-helping-neighbor remain the first line of response in this state, long before any bureaucracy shows up.
