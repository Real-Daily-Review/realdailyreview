# Colorado Amendment 87 Faces Skepticism Over Incomplete Arguments, While Election Security Debate Heats Up

Colorado conservatives are raising red flags over Amendment 87, with critics arguing the measure is being sold to voters on incomplete and misleading premises. According to *Complete Colorado*, state media outlets and the legislature have buttressed the amendment with selective framing that obscures its full impact on Colorado taxpayers and businesses.

The criticism comes as Colorado grapples with election integrity questions. The *Denver Post* reported that three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024 before Mesa County residents attempting to expose vulnerabilities were caught—a finding that has reignited debate over Amendment 84, which would require Social Security numbers for voter registration. While the Post notes Colorado's voting system has "many checks and balances," conservatives argue the cases demonstrate why stronger verification measures remain necessary.

Republicans contend that incomplete disclosure of amendment details prevents voters from making fully informed decisions. Democrats and progressive outlets counter that Colorado's low fraud rate proves the system works and that additional restrictions could suppress legitimate voting.

The stakes are significant: both amendments will reshape Colorado election law and government authority over voter verification and ballot access. Taxpayers and election integrity advocates should closely monitor how these measures perform on the November ballot and what implementation costs they impose on county election offices.
