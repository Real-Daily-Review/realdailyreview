# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial primary to just two candidates after the state assembly, a sharp culling that signals party establishment preference in a crowded field. Meanwhile, voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution—a direct conservative pushback against progressive energy mandates.

Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado, qualified for the ballot and reflects growing alarm among Colorado energy workers and families over aggressive climate regulations that threaten affordable heating and electricity. Supporters argue the measure protects consumer choice and Colorado's energy independence; opponents on the left will likely frame it as obstruction of climate goals.

The dual developments underscore 2026 as a pivotal year for Colorado conservatives. The gubernatorial race will shape energy policy, regulatory direction, and tax burden for years. Initiative 177 tests whether Colorado voters reject the progressive green agenda that has already reshaped the state's power grid and driven up utility costs.

Republicans should watch whether Initiative 177 gains traction with suburban and rural voters—a bellwether for whether energy affordability can override climate messaging in purple Colorado.
