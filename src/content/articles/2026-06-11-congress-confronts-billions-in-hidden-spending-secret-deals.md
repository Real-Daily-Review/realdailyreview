# Secret Spending, Hidden Deals: Congress Confronts Billions in Unaccountable Spending

Congress is finally asking hard questions about where taxpayer money actually goes—and the answers are infuriating.

A UAP whistleblower has accused intelligence agencies of stashing billions in secret spending through alleged slush funds kept hidden from congressional oversight, according to Fox News. Meanwhile, the House is grilling the NFL over a federal antitrust exemption that lets the league rake in billions from broadcast deals while fans foot the bill with skyrocketing TV costs, per Just The News. NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell notably declined to testify.

These cases expose a bipartisan failure: Congress has lost control of the purse strings. Whether it's intelligence agencies operating black budgets or special interests exploiting federal exemptions, the pattern is identical—unaccountable spending that benefits the powerful while ordinary Americans pay more.

Republicans have long warned that unchecked government agencies and corporate-government collusion drain resources and distort markets. The left's answer is typically more regulation and oversight—which rarely works when the agencies themselves are hiding the money.

Colorado taxpayers should care: federal spending waste inflates the national debt that future generations inherit, and corporate exemptions like the NFL's distort free markets that Colorado businesses compete in fairly.

Watch whether Congress actually claws back these hidden billions or merely holds performative hearings and moves on.
