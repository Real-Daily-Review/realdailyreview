# Avian Flu Returns to Colorado Dairy—State Quarantines Weld County Operation

Another Colorado dairy has tested positive for avian flu, marking the first confirmed case since the 2024 outbreak that roiled the state's dairy industry. State health officials have quarantined a Weld County operation to contain the virus (9News Denver).

Colorado's dairy sector—a $2.7 billion industry and major employer in the northeastern plains—faces renewed pressure as the highly pathogenic bird flu resurfaces. The Colorado Sun reports that state officials are emphasizing that pasteurized milk remains safe for consumers, a critical message for a state where dairy farming underpins rural economies and food security.

Republicans and industry advocates have criticized federal and state regulatory responses to previous outbreaks as heavy-handed, arguing that quarantine orders and production restrictions impose crushing costs on family operations without proportional public health benefit. Conservatives warn that overregulation of agriculture—whether through disease protocols or environmental mandates—threatens Colorado's competitive advantage in food production.

State health officials say the quarantine is precautionary and that human transmission risk remains low with proper milk handling. The dairy industry is watching closely to see whether state regulators impose the same stringent restrictions that marked the 2024 crisis.

Taxpayers and farmers should monitor whether state and federal agencies use this case to justify expanded regulatory authority over Colorado's dairy operations.
