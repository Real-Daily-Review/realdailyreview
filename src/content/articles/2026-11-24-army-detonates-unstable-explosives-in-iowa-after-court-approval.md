# Army Moves Forward With Hazardous Chemical Detonation in Iowa

The U.S. Army will detonate 68 barrels of crystallized lead azide—an unstable explosive compound—at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown on Wednesday after securing court approval and bipartisan state support.

Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle announced the decision Monday following a ruling by the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals. Both Iowa Gov. Kim Reynolds (R) and Gov.-elect Rob Sand (D) endorsed the controlled detonation as the safest option for disposing of the degraded materials, which have become increasingly unstable over time.

The Army characterized the decision as "a consensus decision," signaling alignment across military leadership and state officials on the necessity of the operation. The detonation represents the culmination of a lengthy review process to determine the safest disposal method for the hazardous chemicals stored at the facility.

While the operation carries environmental and safety considerations, the court's approval and bipartisan gubernatorial support suggest confidence in the Army's containment and safety protocols. The detonation is expected to proceed as scheduled Wednesday.
