# America's Founding Principle at 250: Are the Unborn Included?

As America marks 250 years since the Declaration of Independence, conservatives are asking a pointed question: does "all men are created equal" apply to the unborn?

The Washington Examiner's opinion section reflects the conservative case that the nation's founding principle—that all humans are "endowed by their creator" with inalienable rights—logically extends to life in the womb. The phrase, penned in 1776, sparked "an unequaled explosion of freedom and prosperity," yet conservatives argue America has strayed from that foundational commitment to universal human dignity.

For Colorado, this debate carries real weight. The state has become a destination for abortion tourism since neighboring states tightened restrictions, and Colorado Democrats have doubled down on abortion access as a centerpiece of their political identity. Republicans counter that true equality cannot ignore the most vulnerable—the unborn—and that Colorado's permissive abortion laws contradict the nation's founding promise.

The tension is stark: progressives frame abortion access as essential freedom; conservatives see it as incompatible with the Declaration's core claim that all humans possess inherent rights from creation.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans make fetal personhood and abortion limits central to their 2026 messaging, or whether they continue to treat the issue as secondary to economic and energy concerns.
