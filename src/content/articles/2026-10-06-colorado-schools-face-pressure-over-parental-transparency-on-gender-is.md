# Colorado Parents, Schools, and the Right-to-Know Battle

Colorado parents are increasingly at odds with schools over transparency regarding their children's gender identity and medical decisions—a clash that mirrors a broader national fight over parental rights.

A new podcast featuring Colorado parents discussing transgender children aims to "address misinformation," according to 9News Denver. But conservatives argue the real issue isn't misinformation—it's whether schools should inform parents when children request gender transitions or social changes at school.

The Trump administration has weighed in forcefully. In a letter to state attorneys general, the administration reminded them that under federal law, parents have "the right to know and be informed of the most sensitive and personal information pertaining to their children" (Just The News). Republicans argue Colorado schools have systematically hidden these conversations from parents, allowing minors to make life-altering decisions without parental knowledge or consent.

Democrats and progressive educators counter that such disclosure policies could endanger LGBTQ youth by forcing unwanted "outings" in unsupportive homes. They frame parental notification as a threat to student safety.

For Colorado taxpayers, the stakes are clear: who controls decisions about children's welfare—parents or government institutions? The state's school boards will face mounting pressure to clarify their policies on parental notification and medical transitions.
