# Colorado Wildfires Prompt Tax Relief—But Questions Loom on Prevention

Two of Colorado's largest wildfires in state history are now burning simultaneously, forcing Governor Jared Polis and the Department of Revenue to announce extended tax deadlines and relief options for affected residents and businesses (KDVR Fox31).

The tax relief measures offer some immediate help to fire-impacted Coloradans—a reasonable response to genuine hardship. But conservatives should ask harder questions: Why are Colorado's forests in such catastrophic condition? For years, environmental regulations have restricted timber harvesting and forest management on state and federal lands, leaving millions of acres choked with dead trees and dense undergrowth that fuel megafires. The result: preventable disasters that destroy homes, livelihoods, and ecosystems far worse than responsible logging would.

Democrats and environmental groups have long blocked aggressive forest thinning and fuel reduction projects, citing habitat concerns. Republicans argue this ideological approach has made Colorado's wildfire crisis worse, not better. Polis has made some moves toward forest management, but progress remains slow against entrenched green opposition.

Tax relief is necessary relief. But Coloradans deserve a state government willing to prioritize forest health over environmental activism—before the next catastrophe strikes.

Watch whether the legislature funds meaningful forest management and removes regulatory barriers to timber harvest on state lands this session.
