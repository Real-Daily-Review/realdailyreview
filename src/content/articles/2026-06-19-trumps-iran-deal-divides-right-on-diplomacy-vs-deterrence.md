# Trump's Iran Deal Splits Conservative Opinion on Diplomacy vs. Deterrence

President Trump's framework agreement with Iran has divided the right over whether pragmatic diplomacy or hardline deterrence better serves American interests. A Reagan Institute poll shows Americans themselves are split: 39% favor negotiated settlement while 36% back regime change, with the remainder undecided—reflecting genuine uncertainty about Tehran's intentions.

Supporters of the deal, including Trump, argue it represents a historic diplomatic breakthrough that reduces war risk and opens communication channels between Washington and Tehran. They frame it as pragmatic statecraft that avoids costly military entanglement in the Middle East.

Conservative skeptics counter that Iran's track record of deception and regional aggression demands caution. The Daily Wire notes negotiations "have not really begun" in meaningful substance, while the Washington Examiner warns of "unthinkable next steps" if the agreement fails to constrain Iranian ambitions. Critics worry the framework may legitimize Tehran without securing verifiable concessions on nuclear development or proxy warfare.

For Colorado, this matters: energy markets respond to Middle East stability, and any agreement affecting oil supplies ripples through state economies dependent on affordable fuel and stable energy prices.

Watch whether Trump secures congressional buy-in and whether Iran actually honors verification protocols—or whether this becomes another diplomatic mirage.
