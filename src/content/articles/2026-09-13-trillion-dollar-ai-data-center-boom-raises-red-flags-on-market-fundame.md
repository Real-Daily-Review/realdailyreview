# AI Data Center Boom Raises Questions About Infrastructure Strategy and Colorado's Role

The nation's trillion-dollar sprint to build AI data centers risks repeating past mistakes in technology infrastructure, according to critics who warn that massive capital commitments may be chasing the wrong technological path.

Capital commitments for AI data center construction have already exceeded hundreds of billions of dollars and are forecast to top $1 trillion by 2029—larger than the GDP of most countries, *Washington Examiner* reports. Massive campuses spanning thousands of acres are planned across the nation.

Conservatives raise a critical question: Are these investments driven by genuine market demand or government incentives and subsidies that distort competition? The scale of this buildout demands scrutiny. If federal or state tax breaks, accelerated permitting, or other government sweeteners are fueling this expansion, Colorado taxpayers and businesses deserve transparency about whether their state is offering corporate welfare to attract these facilities.

The broader concern: massive infrastructure projects often require regulatory shortcuts, environmental waivers, and long-term commitments that lock in today's technology choices. If the industry is betting wrong on AI architecture—as some technologists suggest—Colorado could be left holding the bag on stranded assets and depleted water resources.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers propose tax incentives or regulatory relief to lure AI data centers to the state, and demand full accounting of the true cost to taxpayers and water availability.
