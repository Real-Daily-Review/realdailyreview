# White House Faces Backlash Over Unauthorized Use of Katy Perry Song in Military Video

The Trump administration drew criticism from pop star Katy Perry after the White House TikTok account used her song "Firework" as a soundtrack for military strike footage without her permission, according to *Just The News* and the *Washington Examiner*.

Perry said she was "deeply appalled and angry" at the use, stating the song's message of "self-worth and upliftment" was being "weaponized to soundtrack destruction and violence" — a complete violation of the track's intent. She emphasized she neither approved the use nor was asked for permission beforehand.

The incident highlights a broader tension between the White House's social media strategy and intellectual property rights. While the administration's goal may have been to project strength and military capability to younger audiences via TikTok, the unauthorized use of a copyrighted work without artist consent raises legal and ethical questions about government overreach in the digital space.

For Colorado conservatives, this serves as a reminder that even Republican administrations must respect property rights and contractual obligations — core conservative principles. The misstep also underscores how government agencies sometimes operate without proper vetting or legal review before posting to social platforms.
