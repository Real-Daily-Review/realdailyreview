# A Rare Declaration Copy Surfaces—Reminding Us Why America's Founding Principles Still Matter

A previously unknown copy of the Declaration of Independence, captured by the British Royal Navy from an American privateer on Christmas Eve 1776, has been discovered in UK archives (Breitbart), underscoring the enduring power of our founding creed even as it was being fought over.

The discovery arrives as conservatives reflect on what the Declaration actually means for American law and governance. National Review notes the document "has some important consequences for American law"—consequences worth defending against progressive reinterpretation. The Declaration articulates principles of individual liberty, limited government, and natural rights that stand in stark contrast to modern regulatory overreach and government expansion.

For Colorado specifically, this moment invites scrutiny: Are our state leaders defending the constitutional limits on government power that the Founders enshrined? Or are they expanding state authority over energy development, water rights, land use, and individual choice in ways the Declaration's authors would have resisted?

The Washington Examiner rightly calls the Declaration "the greatest public affairs campaign in American history"—a reminder that words matter when they bind a nation to principles of freedom and self-governance.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's legislature uses this 250th anniversary to recommit to constitutional limits—or quietly ignores the Founders' warnings about unchecked state power.
