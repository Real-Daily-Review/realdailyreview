# Trump's Jones Act Waiver Proves Protectionist Law Needs Real Reform, Not Band-Aids

President Trump's Jones Act waiver during the recent emergency failed to meaningfully address supply shortages, exposing what conservatives have long argued: the 1920 maritime protectionist law itself is the problem, not presidential flexibility around it.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, while waivers give presidents emergency authority—a tool every administration should retain—history shows they rarely solve the crises they're meant to address. The recent waiver, issued during the Strait emergency, delivered minimal relief, underscoring a fundamental truth: temporary workarounds don't fix broken policy.

The Jones Act artificially restricts domestic maritime shipping to U.S.-flagged vessels, driving up costs for American consumers and businesses while protecting a shrinking domestic shipping industry. For Colorado—a landlocked state dependent on efficient supply chains and affordable goods—the Jones Act's inflationary effects ripple through the economy.

Republicans and conservatives argue the real solution isn't more waivers; it's repealing or substantially reforming the Jones Act to allow genuine free-market competition in maritime shipping. Temporary relief measures only delay the inevitable reckoning with protectionist policy that harms working families and businesses.

Watch for whether Congress moves to address the Jones Act itself, or whether future administrations continue relying on waivers that don't work.
