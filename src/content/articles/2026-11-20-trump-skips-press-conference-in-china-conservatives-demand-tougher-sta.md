# Trump Skips Press Conference in China; Conservatives Want Harder Line on Beijing

President Trump concluded his China trip without holding a formal press conference—a break from typical presidential protocol on foreign visits, *CPR News* reports. The absence of public questioning raised eyebrows as Trump met with Xi Jinping and other leaders.

Conservative voices are pressing Trump to use his diplomatic leverage more aggressively. *National Review* argues Trump should demand the release of democracy activist Joshua Wong, imprisoned for meeting with American lawmakers, calling his detention "an affront to the U.S." The outlet contends that appeasing Beijing on human rights undermines American values and credibility abroad.

Republicans have long criticized what they see as insufficient pushback against Chinese authoritarianism, intellectual property theft, and trade abuses. Trump's 2024 platform emphasized confronting Beijing's expansionist policies and protecting American interests—a stance that resonates with Colorado's energy and manufacturing sectors, which face competition from state-subsidized Chinese competitors.

The lack of transparency on what was discussed—or conceded—during these high-stakes meetings fuels conservative concern that backroom deals may not serve American interests.

Watch for details on any trade agreements or commitments Trump made to Xi, and whether human rights concerns were meaningfully raised.
