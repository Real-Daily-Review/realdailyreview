# Social Media's Grip on America Demands Real Solutions, Not Just Parental Band-Aids

Colorado families are watching their kids disappear into addictive platforms designed to maximize engagement at any cost—and parental controls alone won't fix it. According to the Daily Caller, the social media crisis has grown beyond what individual parents can manage, with platforms deliberately engineered to hook young users. National Review adds that the cultural pressure to "optimize" every aspect of life through social media has created a guilt-driven treadmill that traps users of all ages.

Republicans and conservatives argue that tech companies bear primary responsibility for designing addictive products targeting minors, and that federal regulation—or at minimum, state-level accountability measures—may be necessary. Some GOP lawmakers have pushed for transparency requirements and age-verification standards. Democrats, meanwhile, tend to frame the issue through a mental-health lens while resisting industry regulation that might limit data collection.

For Colorado, this hits home: state legislators have considered social media bills addressing youth mental health and platform accountability. The question isn't whether parents should supervise their kids—they should—but whether trillion-dollar tech monopolies should face consequences for deliberately engineering addiction.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves on social media regulation this session, and whether any bills prioritize parental rights and transparency over corporate interests.
