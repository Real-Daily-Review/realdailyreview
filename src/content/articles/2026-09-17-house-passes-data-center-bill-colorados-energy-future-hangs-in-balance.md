# Data Center Bill Clears House, But Colorado Faces Real Cost Questions

Congress just passed a bipartisan bill to stop data centers from jacking up your electric bill—but Colorado conservatives should ask whether this actually solves the problem or just shifts costs around.

The House voted 417-3 to pass the Ratepayer Protection Act, requiring data centers to cover grid and energy infrastructure upgrades rather than passing those costs to households (Fox News, Washington Examiner). The overwhelming support signals both parties recognize voter anxiety about skyrocketing energy prices tied to AI and crypto operations.

Here's the catch: Colorado is ground zero for data center expansion. The state's cheap power and tech-friendly climate make it a magnet for these energy-hungry facilities. If data centers must absorb upgrade costs, they'll either relocate to friendlier jurisdictions, shrink their Colorado footprint, or pass expenses to consumers anyway through higher service fees.

Republicans backing the bill argue it protects working families from hidden rate hikes. But conservatives should watch whether this becomes another regulatory trap—one that sounds good but ultimately drives investment and jobs elsewhere while doing little to address the real issue: Colorado's aging grid and restrictive energy policy that limits supply.

The real question: Will Colorado policymakers use this as cover to block data centers outright, or will they finally greenlight the natural gas and nuclear capacity these facilities actually need?
