# NYC Mayor's Anti-Netanyahu Stance Highlights Left's Inconsistent Foreign Policy

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's hostile reception plans for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ahead of his UN address reveal the progressive left's selective outrage on Middle East policy. Israel's U.N. Ambassador Danny Danon accused Mamdani of "sowing chaos" rather than fulfilling a mayor's basic duty to welcome world leaders (Fox News Politics).

The controversy underscores a deeper conservative concern: Democratic-led cities increasingly weaponize their platforms against U.S. allies while remaining silent on genuine threats. Republicans argue that undermining Netanyahu—whose government faces real security challenges—signals weakness to adversaries and fractures the Western alliance.

Meanwhile, the Biden-Harris administration's own messaging has muddied the waters. U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee characterized West Bank settler violence as "an act of terror" (Daily Caller), a statement that conservatives say conflates isolated criminal acts with organized terrorism and ignores Palestinian attacks on Israeli civilians.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's progressive lawmakers and activists often echo New York's anti-Israel rhetoric, particularly on college campuses and in Denver's activist circles. This foreign policy divide increasingly shapes domestic politics and donor bases on the left.
