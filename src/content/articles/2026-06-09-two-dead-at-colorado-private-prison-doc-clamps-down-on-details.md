# Two Inmates Dead at Private Prison as DOC Keeps Details Locked Down

Colorado's private prison system faces fresh scrutiny after two inmates died in an "isolated incident" at Bent County Correctional Facility east of Pueblo over the weekend, prompting state Department of Corrections officials to suspend visitation statewide while refusing to disclose specifics.

Bent County Sheriff Jake Six reported receiving a call around 11:22 p.m. Saturday about a "miscount" at the facility (Colorado Sun), though DOC has remained tight-lipped about what actually occurred. The coroner has identified the two deceased, but authorities have released minimal information about the deaths or circumstances (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

The incident raises hard questions about oversight and accountability at Colorado's private corrections facilities. Conservatives and taxpayers deserve transparency about what happened and whether the private contractor is meeting its contractual obligations. The statewide visitation lockdown suggests officials are concerned about broader systemic problems—or at minimum, are managing optics rather than leading with facts.

This comes as Gov. Jared Polis continues his aggressive commutation spree, recently releasing election-denier Tina Peters after reducing her sentence (Denver Post). The contrast is stark: two inmates dead under state watch, while the governor fast-tracks releases for political prisoners.

**Watch whether the Polis administration releases a full incident report or continues stonewalling—and whether Republican legislators demand accountability from DOC leadership.**
