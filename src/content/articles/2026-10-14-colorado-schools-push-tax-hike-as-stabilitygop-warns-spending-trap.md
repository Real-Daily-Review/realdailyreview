# Colorado Schools Pitch Tax Hike as "Stability"—GOP Warns of Spending Trap

Colorado's education establishment is pushing Proposition NN, a new tax on businesses, claiming schools need "stability" and "long-term planning." But Republicans argue the measure is another raid on job creators that won't fix Colorado's real education problems—bloated administration and misaligned spending priorities.

The Colorado Sun's education advocates say NN funding would expand "real-world programs" and retain teachers. Conservatives counter that Colorado already spends billions on K-12 education; the issue isn't revenue, it's how districts allocate it. Adding another tax burden on employers during economic uncertainty won't address classroom quality or student outcomes—it simply enables more government spending without accountability.

The pitch matters for Colorado taxpayers and small businesses already facing rising costs. School districts have repeatedly sought new revenue streams while enrollment stagnates and administrative overhead grows. Republicans argue voters should demand performance metrics and spending transparency before approving any tax increase.

Meanwhile, the Denver Post's recent critique of Colorado GOP statewide candidates signals a broader problem: Republicans need stronger messengers to challenge Democratic dominance and make the conservative case on taxes, education reform, and fiscal responsibility.

Watch whether Proposition NN gains traction with suburban voters—or whether taxpayer skepticism of "stability" spending holds.
