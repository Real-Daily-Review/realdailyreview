# Fort Collins Dodges Wildfire Evacuation, But Heat Concerns Persist

A grass fire that sparked mandatory evacuations in Fort Collins Wednesday afternoon was quickly contained, allowing residents in the 2900 block of Arbee Lane to remain in their homes (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The evacuation order, issued around 2:39 p.m., was canceled after firefighters brought the blaze under control.

The incident underscores Colorado's intensifying wildfire season. Fort Collins just recorded its hottest summer on record (9News Denver), a trend mirroring heat spikes across Colorado cities. While cooler heads and rapid response prevented disaster this time, the conditions fueling these fires—dry vegetation, record temperatures—remain a serious threat to Northern Colorado communities and the property values and livelihoods they depend on.

Conservatives and taxpayers should note the real-world cost of these emergencies: emergency response resources, potential property damage, and the strain on local budgets. Rather than reflexively blaming climate alone, Colorado policymakers should focus on proven wildfire mitigation—forest management, controlled burns, and removing dead timber—instead of pursuing costly regulatory schemes that burden energy producers and Colorado's economy without meaningfully reducing fire risk.

Watch for how state and local officials respond to Fort Collins' heat records and whether they'll prioritize practical forest management or lean toward expensive, ineffective climate mandates.
