# Colorado Seeks Federal Wildfire Aid After Trump Denied Prior Requests

Colorado is asking the Trump administration for federal disaster relief after the Aspen Acres and Gold Mountain fires ravaged the state, but the request faces an uphill climb—Trump has already rejected two previous disaster declarations from Colorado (CPR News).

The state is tallying at least $18 million in damage to public infrastructure alone from the two fires (Colorado Sun). That's real money Colorado taxpayers will have to shoulder if federal aid doesn't materialize.

Republicans have long argued that federal disaster spending should be reserved for genuine catastrophes and that states bear primary responsibility for emergency preparedness and recovery. The Trump administration's pattern of denying Colorado's prior requests suggests skepticism about the scope or necessity of federal intervention—a position consistent with conservative principles of limited federal spending and state-level accountability.

Colorado's fire season continues to expose the state's vulnerability to catastrophic wildfires, a challenge compounded by decades of forest mismanagement and environmental regulations that have restricted timber harvesting and controlled burns. Conservatives argue that better land stewardship—not federal handouts—is the real solution.

Watch whether Trump approves this request and on what conditions, and whether Colorado's Democratic leadership will acknowledge the need for aggressive forest management reform.
