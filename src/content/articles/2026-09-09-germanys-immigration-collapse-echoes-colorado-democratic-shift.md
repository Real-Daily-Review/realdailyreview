# Germany's Immigration Crisis Mirrors Warnings U.S. Conservatives Have Been Sounding

Germany's far-right Alternative für Deutschland party is surging in regional elections—a direct result of the governing left's refusal to address legitimate voter concerns about immigration and border control, according to *National Review*.

The parallel to American politics is stark. Just weeks before Germany's vote, far-left candidates won Democratic primary victories across multiple states, including Colorado, *Washington Examiner* reports. That ideological shift leftward on immigration and border security is precisely what's fueling backlash both here and abroad.

Conservatives argue the lesson is clear: when establishment politicians ignore voter concerns about uncontrolled immigration and cultural integration, they don't eliminate those concerns—they hand electoral ammunition to more extreme alternatives. Republicans contend that legitimate border security and immigration limits aren't xenophobic; they're foundational to national sovereignty and social cohesion.

The German governing party's failure to address these issues head-on created a vacuum the AfD filled. Democrats pushing open-border rhetoric in Colorado and nationally risk repeating that mistake. Voters in Pennsylvania, Michigan, Florida, and Colorado are signaling they want immigration addressed seriously—not dismissed as illegitimate.
