# U.S. Escalates Iran Strikes as Ceasefire Collapses; Colorado Should Brace for Defense Spending Surge

President Trump's administration is intensifying military operations in the Middle East, with U.S. Central Command conducting seven consecutive nights of strikes against Iran as a months-long ceasefire unravels (Washington Examiner). The conflict resumed this week after negotiations failed; a June memorandum of understanding that had extended the ceasefire by 60 days has now expired (Just The News).

In a primetime address, Trump insisted the war was proceeding as planned (9News Denver). CENTCOM confirmed Friday night that strikes concluded on July 17 at 9:30 p.m. ET, with targets including bridges and infrastructure (9News Denver).

**The Colorado angle:** Sustained Middle East conflict typically triggers increased defense contracting and military spending—benefiting Colorado's aerospace and defense sector, including major employers around Colorado Springs and the Front Range. However, prolonged foreign military engagement also strains the federal budget, potentially pressuring lawmakers to justify spending priorities to taxpayers already burdened by inflation and rising costs.

Conservatives generally support robust military deterrence against hostile regimes but remain watchful that extended conflicts don't become open-ended commitments without clear strategic objectives or exit strategies.

Watch for whether Congress demands a formal authorization for use of military force and how the administration defines victory conditions in Iran.
