# Aurora DA Declines Charges Against Officer in May Shooting; Self-Defense Cases Highlight Colorado's Law-and-Order Shift

Colorado's judicial system is reaffirming what many conservatives have long argued: police officers and citizens have the right to use force when facing genuine threats. The 17th Judicial District Attorney's Office declined to charge an Aurora police officer in the May 2025 shooting of Rashaud Johnson, determining the use of force was justified (9News Denver). The decision aligns with a broader pattern emerging across Colorado: courts recognizing legitimate self-defense claims.

That principle extends beyond badge-holders. In Denver's Montbello neighborhood, investigators determined that a homeowner who shot suspects breaking into his vehicle acted lawfully—the suspects had initiated the confrontation (KDVR Fox31). These cases underscore a fundamental conservative principle: law-abiding Coloradans and officers retain the right to protect themselves and their property without facing prosecution for justified force.

The contrast is stark. Even progressive jurisdictions are walking back "defund the police" rhetoric. New York's former police-reform advocate Mayor Zohran Mamdani has quietly expanded NYPD headcount, drawing backlash from activists who feel betrayed (New York Post)—a tacit admission that public safety demands adequate law enforcement resources.

Colorado conservatives should watch whether district attorneys statewide maintain this principled approach to self-defense or cave to pressure from progressive activists demanding charges against officers and citizens alike.
