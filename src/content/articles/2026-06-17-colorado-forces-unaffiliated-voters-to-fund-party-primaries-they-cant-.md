# Colorado Taxpayers Forced to Fund Primaries They Don't Support

Colorado forces unaffiliated voters—now more than half the state's electorate—to subsidize political party primaries they didn't choose and can't participate in, a system conservatives say amounts to taxpayer-funded party privilege.

Data from the Colorado Secretary of State's Office shows that over 50 percent of Colorado voters are registered as unaffiliated (KDVR Fox31). Yet state law requires taxpayers to finance Democratic and Republican primary elections regardless of party affiliation. "Why does Colorado government force taxpayers to finance the primaries of parties to which they do not even belong?" asks Complete Colorado, the conservative outlet pushing reform.

Republicans and independents argue the arrangement violates basic fairness: unaffiliated voters foot the bill for contests they cannot vote in. Complete Colorado frames it as government overreach—compelling citizens to subsidize private party operations without consent.

Democrats have resisted primary reform, defending the current system as protecting ballot access and party integrity. The left claims open or taxpayer-funded universal primaries would dilute party identity and increase administrative costs.

The issue cuts deeper than money. Conservatives see it as emblematic of government forcing citizens to support institutions against their will—a principle that extends beyond elections to broader tax-and-spend debates.

Watch whether the Colorado legislature takes up primary reform bills that would either eliminate taxpayer funding or allow unaffiliated voters meaningful participation.
