# Trump Admin Chip-Export Rift Threatens Public Spectacle Over China Policy

A brewing dispute within the Trump administration over Nvidia chip exports to China is heading toward Senate testimony that could air internal disagreements in public. Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC) said Thursday he'd welcome Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to testify alongside Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick next month, potentially exposing competing accounts on how aggressively to restrict semiconductor sales to Beijing (Washington Examiner).

The clash reflects a core conservative tension: balancing national security against free-market principles and corporate interests. Republicans argue strict chip controls protect U.S. technological advantage and national defense; others worry excessive restrictions harm American companies' competitiveness and profitability.

Meanwhile, disclosure filings show President Trump's investment accounts executed five Nvidia trades in September—two months before the chipmaker's CEO departed amid scandal (New York Post). The timing raises questions about information flow and potential conflicts, though no wrongdoing is alleged.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's tech and defense sectors depend on semiconductor policy. Restrictive export controls could benefit local defense contractors but harm Colorado tech firms with global supply chains.

**Watch:** Whether Senate testimony becomes a public relations disaster for the administration or a substantive policy debate—and whether Republicans can articulate a coherent China strategy before 2026.
