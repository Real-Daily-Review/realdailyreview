# Data Center Backlash Shows Perils of Flip-Flopping on Economic Development

Pennsylvania's sudden turn against data centers—once hailed as economic saviors—offers Colorado a cautionary tale about political whiplash and anti-business sentiment. According to the *Washington Examiner*, data centers went from "belle of the ball" to "public enemy No. 1" in mere months, with local officials and activists now treating the facilities as environmental threats rather than job creators.

The shift reflects a broader progressive pattern: embrace development when it serves political narratives, then weaponize environmental and community concerns when opposition mobilizes. Pennsylvania's reversal undermines investor confidence and signals that business-friendly policies depend on which way the political wind blows.

**Colorado conservatives should note the parallel risk.** The state has attracted significant data center and tech investment, particularly around Denver and Northern Colorado. If similar anti-development sentiment takes root here—driven by progressive activists citing water use, energy consumption, or vague "community impact" concerns—Colorado could see the same investor hesitation that's now plaguing Pennsylvania.

Republicans argue that consistent, predictable pro-growth policy attracts capital and creates jobs. Democrats counter with environmental and livability concerns, often reversing course once projects face organized opposition.

**Watch for whether Colorado's legislature attempts to impose new restrictions on data center development or energy use in 2025.**
