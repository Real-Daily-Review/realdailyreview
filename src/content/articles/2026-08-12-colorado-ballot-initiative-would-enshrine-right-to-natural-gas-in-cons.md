# Colorado Voters to Decide 'Right to Natural Gas' Constitutional Amendment

Colorado's energy independence just got a ballot fight. Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado, qualified for November's election and will ask voters whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" directly into the state Constitution (Denver Post).

The measure represents a conservative pushback against what supporters argue is creeping regulatory hostility toward natural gas development—a cornerstone of Colorado's economy and energy sector. Republicans and energy advocates contend that constitutional protection would shield the state's gas industry from future legislative bans or phase-outs, similar to restrictions already imposed in California and proposed elsewhere.

Democrats and environmental groups predictably oppose the amendment, framing it as a lock-in for fossil fuels that conflicts with climate goals. They argue Colorado should transition away from natural gas, not constitutionally guarantee its use.

For Colorado taxpayers and workers, the stakes are real: natural gas generates substantial state revenue, supports thousands of jobs across the Western Slope and beyond, and provides affordable energy to households. A constitutional "right" would make it far harder for future legislatures to strangle the industry through regulation.

Watch whether the amendment gains traction with suburban and rural voters who depend on affordable energy and energy-sector employment.
