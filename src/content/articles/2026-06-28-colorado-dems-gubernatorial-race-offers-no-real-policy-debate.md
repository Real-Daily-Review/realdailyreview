# Colorado Democrats' Gubernatorial Race Offers Voters Little Daylight on Policy

Colorado Democrats are offering voters a choice without a difference in their 2026 gubernatorial primary, with U.S. Senator Michael Bennet and Attorney General Phil Weiser staking out nearly identical positions on major issues. According to the *Colorado Sun*, cartoonist Jim Morrissey observed that the two candidates function as a "two-headed candidate," with striking policy alignment across the board.

This convergence reflects a broader Democratic strategy: avoid internal conflict and present a unified front heading into the general election. But for Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the lack of meaningful debate within the Democratic primary means voters won't see serious scrutiny of either candidate's record on spending, regulation, or energy policy—areas where both Bennet and Weiser have leaned left.

Republicans argue that without intra-party pressure, neither Democrat will face tough questions about Colorado's rising cost of living, regulatory burdens on small business, or the state's energy transition policies that have squeezed oil and gas workers. Conservatives say the homogenized primary also means no vetting of either candidate's judicial appointments or approach to Second Amendment rights.

The real test comes in the general election, where GOP candidates can draw sharp contrasts on fiscal responsibility and limited government.

Watch whether Republicans exploit this Democratic unity by hammering both nominees on the same vulnerabilities simultaneously.
