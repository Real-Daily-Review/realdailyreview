# Michigan Democrat Dodges Questions on Alliance With Streamer Who Defended 9/11

Michigan's Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed is brushing aside legitimate concerns about his repeated campaign appearances alongside far-left Twitch streamer Hasan Piker, who has made inflammatory remarks suggesting America deserved the 9/11 terrorist attacks.

When pressed on NBC's "Meet the Press" about whether he disavowed Piker's comments, El-Sayed deflected—claiming he cared about reaching Piker's audience, not addressing the streamer's controversial rhetoric (Breitbart). That's a dodge, not an answer.

**Republicans argue** this association reveals poor judgment. As the Washington Examiner's Peter Laffin noted, questioning El-Sayed's ties to someone with Piker's record "isn't Islamophobia"—it's basic vetting. When a major-party nominee repeatedly campaigns alongside someone who has defended attacks that killed nearly 3,000 Americans, voters deserve clarity on whether he shares those views or simply doesn't care.

**Democrats counter** that Republicans are unfairly weaponizing the association, though they've offered no substantive defense of El-Sayed's decision to campaign with Piker repeatedly.

El-Sayed's non-answer speaks volumes. He won't disavow the comments; he won't distance himself from the streamer. He just wants access to the audience. Colorado conservatives should watch whether national media holds Democratic nominees to the same standard they'd apply to Republicans in similar circumstances.
