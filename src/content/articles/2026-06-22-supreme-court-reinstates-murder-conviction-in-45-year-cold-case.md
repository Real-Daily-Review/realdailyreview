# Supreme Court Backs Murder Conviction in 45-Year-Old Cold Case

The U.S. Supreme Court has reinstated a murder conviction in the decades-old disappearance of 6-year-old Etan Patz, ruling 6-3 that New York prosecutors met their burden of proof despite a lower court's reversal (Just The News). The boy vanished from a Manhattan street in 1979; a convenience store worker wasn't identified as a suspect until 2012—33 years later.

The high court's decision upholds the conviction against a federal appeals court ruling that had thrown out the case, signaling the justices' willingness to defer to state prosecutors on evidentiary standards in cold cases. The 6-3 split reflects the current conservative-leaning court's approach to criminal procedure, where the majority sided with law enforcement over defense arguments about the strength of evidence.

While this case has no direct Colorado angle, it underscores a broader conservative principle: courts should respect prosecutorial judgment and not second-guess convictions on technical grounds when evidence supports guilt. The ruling also demonstrates how a constitutionally sound Supreme Court—one appointed by Republican presidents—can support law-and-order outcomes without abandoning due process.
