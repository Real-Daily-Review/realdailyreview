# Tech Outages Expose Vulnerability in Critical Infrastructure—And Nobody's Got Answers

Federal officials are scrambling to explain a cascade of system failures hitting airlines and banks, with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin pointing fingers at artificial intelligence while offering little reassurance to Americans relying on these services.

Mullin told Fox News the "homeland is secure," but that's cold comfort to travelers stranded by ground stops and customers locked out of banking systems. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called the situation a "head-scratcher"—hardly the confident assessment Americans expect from leadership during a crisis affecting critical infrastructure.

The real problem: nobody's taking clear responsibility. Mullin cited an unnamed technology expert blaming AI for the outages, but vague finger-pointing doesn't fix broken systems or restore public confidence. For Colorado, this hits hard—our energy sector, financial institutions, and aviation hubs depend on reliable infrastructure. If the federal government can't explain what went wrong or prevent it from happening again, that's a governance failure.

Republicans and conservatives should demand answers: What safeguards exist? Why weren't they sufficient? And why are we relying on unnamed experts instead of transparent, accountable investigations?

Watch whether Congress holds hearings and demands concrete answers on infrastructure resilience—or whether this gets buried as a one-week story.
