# Trump Administration's Colorado Power Play Backfires in Court

A federal judge has rejected the Trump administration's attempt to strong-arm Colorado election officials into surrendering unredacted voter rolls, dealing a sharp rebuke to what conservatives say was executive overreach disguised as election security.

According to the Denver Post, the White House summoned officials from five federal agencies to discuss "immediate actions" against Colorado after the state initially resisted releasing voter data. A Trump assistant's email requesting agencies be "prepared to discuss" Colorado-specific enforcement actions prompted Judge R. Brooke Jackson to express concern the effort "makes no sense in our democracy," the Colorado Sun reports.

Republicans argue the administration was pursuing legitimate election integrity measures. Democrats and voting-rights advocates counter that the episode exemplifies weaponized federal power—using agency resources to punish a state for resisting demands many election officials viewed as overreaching.

**The Colorado angle:** This case directly affects how Colorado protects voter privacy and maintains election independence from federal pressure. The judge's dismissal shields Colorado's voter rolls from federal seizure, a win for state sovereignty that conservatives should applaud even when skeptical of the state's election management.
