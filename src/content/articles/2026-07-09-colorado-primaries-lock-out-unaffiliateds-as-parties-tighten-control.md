# Colorado's Primary Wars: Unaffiliateds Growing as Party Insiders Tighten Control

Colorado's primary system is increasingly deciding general elections—and party establishments on both sides are working hard to control the outcome.

Republicans used their assembly process to winnow 18 gubernatorial candidates down to just two finalists, concentrating power among party insiders (Complete Colorado). Meanwhile, unaffiliated voters—now a substantial bloc—face a structural disadvantage: they cannot participate in party primaries unless they re-register, effectively locking them out of races where the primary winner runs unopposed or faces weak general-election opposition.

Democrats faced their own primary battles in metro Denver, where progressive candidates defeated sitting lawmakers despite millions in opposition spending aimed at blocking them (Denver Post). The victories signal that the party's left wing is consolidating control of candidate selection.

**The conservative concern:** When primaries decide elections—which happens in many Colorado districts—party establishments and activist bases, not general electorates, pick winners. Republicans worry about moderate candidates being squeezed out; unaffiliated voters (nearly 40% of Colorado's electorate) have minimal say in outcomes that matter most.

Taxpayers and liberty-minded voters should watch whether Colorado's primary system continues narrowing choices to party-approved candidates, effectively disenfranchising independents who refuse party registration.
