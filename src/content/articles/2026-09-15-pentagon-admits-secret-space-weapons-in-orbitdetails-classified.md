# Pentagon Admits Secret Space Weapons Already Orbiting—But Won't Explain Them

The U.S. Air Force has weapons in space right now, and the Pentagon just admitted it—without saying what they do or how they work. Air Force Secretary Troy Meink revealed the existence of on-orbit space control weapons at the Air, Space, and Cyber Conference, according to Fox News and The Daily Wire, marking the first official acknowledgment of such systems.

The disclosure comes as a Pentagon watchdog report details the toll of Iranian strikes on U.S. military bases, with one official comparing the damage to "peak Iraq and Afghanistan" (Daily Caller). Chinese satellite imagery reportedly linked those Iranian attacks to the deaths of three service members in Jordan.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and defense hawks argue transparency about military capabilities—especially space-based ones—risks operational security in an increasingly contested domain. Keeping adversaries guessing about orbital weapons serves deterrence. However, taxpayers deserve to know what these systems cost and whether they're delivering results.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** Space Force installations in Colorado Springs and weapons development across the state depend on sustained defense spending and technological advantage. Coloradans should watch whether Congress demands a full accounting of space weapons costs and effectiveness before approving further funding.
