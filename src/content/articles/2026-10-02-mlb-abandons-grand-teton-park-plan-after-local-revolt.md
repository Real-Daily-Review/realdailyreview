# MLB Drops Grand Teton Park Scheme After Local Pushback

Major League Baseball is abandoning its plan to build a temporary baseball facility in Grand Teton National Park, a rare win for locals and conservatives who oppose turning public lands into corporate playgrounds.

MLB Commissioner Robert Manfred announced Thursday that the league "will not continue to evaluate Grand Teton National Park as a potential site" (Colorado Sun). The proposal had drawn swift backlash from Teton County residents and Wyoming officials who saw it as an unnecessary intrusion into one of America's most iconic natural spaces.

The scheme exemplified progressive overreach: federal lands managed for conservation and public access being leveraged for private sports entertainment. Conservatives and local stakeholders rightly questioned why a national park should host temporary commercial infrastructure when vast private alternatives exist.

This outcome reflects a broader principle Colorado conservatives understand well—public lands belong to taxpayers and future generations, not to corporations seeking subsidized venues. The decision also signals that grassroots opposition still carries weight when communities unite against government-enabled corporate deals.

Watch for similar proposals targeting Colorado's public lands and national monuments, particularly as the Biden administration continues reshaping federal land policy. Vigilance is essential.
