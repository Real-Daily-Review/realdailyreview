# Polis Fires Clemency Board Members After Peters Commutation Blowback

Governor Jared Polis has fired two Colorado clemency board members who publicly opposed his controversial commutation of Mesa County Clerk Tina Peters, raising serious questions about due process and political retaliation in Colorado's criminal justice system.

Peters, a Republican election official convicted in 2024 of tampering with voting equipment, became a rallying point for conservatives nationwide, including President Donald Trump. When Polis commuted her nine-year sentence, two board members broke confidentiality to publicly oppose the decision—and Polis promptly removed them from their posts (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative argument:** Republicans contend Polis's firing of dissenting board members constitutes political retaliation and undermines the independence of Colorado's clemency process. The move suggests the governor punished officials for exercising their judgment and speaking out against a politically charged commutation. Conservatives also note Peters's case has drawn national attention as emblematic of perceived weaponization of election officials who question voting procedures.

**What's at stake:** This episode exposes how Colorado's clemency system can become a tool for political score-settling rather than genuine criminal justice reform. Taxpayers and voters should demand transparency about whether Polis's administration is systematically silencing dissent within state boards and commissions.

Watch whether the legislature moves to reform Colorado's clemency process or investigate whether other board members face similar pressure.
