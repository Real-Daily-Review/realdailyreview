# Ken Griffin's $3 Billion Gift Shows What Private Philanthropy—Not Government—Can Achieve

Citadel founder Ken Griffin just handed Carnegie Mellon University a $3 billion commitment to expand its Pittsburgh campus and launch a major Miami operation, proving once again that real institutional growth comes from private capital, not taxpayer-funded government mandates.

Griffin's gift—the largest donation to a U.S. research university in over a decade—echoes Andrew Carnegie's own $1 million investment in 1900 that built Carnegie Tech into a powerhouse for practical, skills-based education. The parallel is instructive: Carnegie's industrial-age vision created lasting value through voluntary private investment, not government grants or regulatory pressure.

For Colorado, the lesson cuts deep. While progressive politicians across the country demand massive public spending on education and infrastructure, Griffin's gift demonstrates that the private sector—when left unfettered by excessive taxation and regulation—generates transformative capital for universities, research, and economic development. Colorado's tech and energy sectors depend on world-class talent pipelines that universities like CU Boulder and Colorado State must compete to build. Yet state lawmakers continue raising taxes and expanding regulations that make Colorado less attractive to high-net-worth entrepreneurs and investors.

The real question: Will Colorado's business climate remain competitive enough to attract the next Ken Griffin, or will progressive tax policy and regulatory overreach drive capital and talent to friendlier states?

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers use this moment to cut taxes and regulations—or double down on government spending as the answer.
