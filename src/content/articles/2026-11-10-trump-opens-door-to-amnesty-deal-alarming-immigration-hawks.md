# Trump Signals Possible Amnesty Deal, Alarming Conservative Base on Immigration

President Trump has opened the door to a compromise with Democrats on amnesty for long-term undocumented workers, according to a post-midterm interview with 60 Minutes, in which he acknowledged his administration's immigration enforcement had been "too tough" and cost Republicans at the polls (Washington Examiner).

The admission marks a significant shift from Trump's hardline immigration stance and signals potential deal-making in his final two years—a prospect that divides his coalition.

**The conservative concern:** Immigration hawks argue that amnesty rewards illegal entry and undermines the rule of law. Colorado Republicans, already concerned about border security and its effects on state resources, worry that any legalization scheme without strict enforcement measures will encourage further illegal immigration and strain public services. They contend that Trump's 2016 base mobilized precisely on immigration enforcement, and backing away from that promise risks alienating core supporters.

**Trump's calculation:** The president appears willing to trade immigration enforcement for electoral gains, suggesting Democrats might support other administration priorities in exchange for a legalization pathway.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the stakes include potential impacts on labor markets, public benefits costs, and school system resources—issues that have animated state-level debates over sanctuary policies and driver's licenses for undocumented immigrants.

Watch whether Trump pursues this deal and how Colorado's Republican delegation responds to any amnesty proposal.
