# White House Seeks $87.6B Blank Check for Iran War, Farm Aid, and Ebola Response

The White House is asking Congress to rubber-stamp nearly $88 billion in emergency spending—with roughly $67 billion earmarked for Pentagon operations tied to Iran tensions—even as lawmakers from both parties have signaled opposition to further military escalation in the region.

The Office of Management and Budget submitted the supplemental spending request Wednesday (Washington Examiner), bundling defense costs with agricultural assistance and Ebola response funding in a classic Washington move: bury controversial spending in a larger package.

**The conservative case against it:** Republicans and fiscal hawks argue the administration is exploiting crisis rhetoric to bypass normal budget scrutiny. Lawmakers have already objected to expanded Iran military action (9News), yet the White House is asking for a blank check to fund it anyway. Colorado taxpayers should question why emergency spending—which bypasses regular appropriations debate—has become routine.

The package also includes farmer assistance, which could benefit Colorado agricultural interests, but conservatives worry the broader precedent: treating massive outlays as "emergency" measures sidesteps fiscal discipline and accountability.

**What to watch:** Whether House Republicans will demand itemized justification for Pentagon spending and refuse to tie Iran war funding to unrelated domestic programs—or cave to White House pressure for a combined bill.
