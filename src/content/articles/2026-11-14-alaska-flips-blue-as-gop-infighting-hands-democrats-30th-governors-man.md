# Democrat Flips Alaska Governor's Seat as GOP Infighting Hands Left Another State

Alaska's governor's mansion flipped blue for the first time since 1998, with Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins defeating the Trump-endorsed Republican candidate in a race fractured by GOP internal warfare and ranked-choice voting.

President Trump backed Republican Bernadette Wilson, but bitter infighting within Alaska's GOP—combined with the state's ranked-choice voting system—split the conservative vote and handed Democrats a critical win (Fox News Politics). The loss means Democrats now control the governor's office in 30 states (New York Post), expanding their foothold in executive power nationwide.

For Colorado conservatives, this serves as a cautionary tale. Ranked-choice voting, championed by progressive activists, has proven effective at fragmenting Republican coalitions and elevating Democratic candidates who might lose under traditional plurality voting. Colorado voters have rejected similar ballot measures in the past, but the left continues pushing the system as a "reform." Meanwhile, GOP unity—or lack thereof—remains a recurring vulnerability in competitive races.

Republicans argue that internal party divisions and voting system mechanics that dilute conservative support create openings for Democrats to win in traditionally Republican territory. The Alaska result underscores how electoral rules matter as much as candidate quality.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats renew efforts to implement ranked-choice voting ahead of 2026, and whether state Republicans learn the unity lesson Alaska's GOP just paid for in blood.
