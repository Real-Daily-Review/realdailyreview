# Republicans Face Hard Reckoning After 2026 Losses—Colorado GOP Must Rebuild Policy Platform

Republicans suffered a stinging defeat in 2026, losing control of both chambers of Congress in districts Trump had won just two years prior—a warning sign for Colorado conservatives heading into 2028.

The losses stem from multiple failures, not a single culprit. According to the Washington Examiner, Republicans bungled the AI safety debate, ceded ground on affordability messaging, and struggled with candidate quality and turnout. National Review argues the GOP faces "deep structural problems that only a new policy vision can fix," warning against leading with resentment rather than governance.

For Colorado specifically, the results underscore a troubling trend: Republicans are losing ground in suburban and exurban areas where energy workers, small-business owners, and families care most about kitchen-table economics. The affordability crisis—driven by Democratic spending and inflation—should have been a Republican strength, yet the party failed to articulate a coherent counter-message.

Conservatives point to three immediate failures: no clear regulatory rollback agenda, weak communication on how GOP policies lower costs, and insufficient focus on Colorado's energy sector as an economic engine Democrats are strangling with green mandates.

The Washington Examiner warns bluntly: Republicans must develop real answers on affordability and technology policy before 2028, or face further erosion in purple states like Colorado.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans build a 2028 platform centered on energy independence, tax relief, and deregulation—or repeat 2026's mistakes.
