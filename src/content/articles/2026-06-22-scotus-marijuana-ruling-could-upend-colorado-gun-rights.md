# Supreme Court Marijuana-Gun Rights Ruling Could Reshape Colorado's Second Amendment Landscape

A recent Supreme Court decision on marijuana users' gun rights is poised to test Colorado's enforcement of federal firearms law—and could vindicate Second Amendment advocates who've long argued the government overreaches in stripping rights from otherwise law-abiding citizens.

The SCOTUS ruling raises a critical question for Colorado: how should state officials handle the collision between federal gun prohibitions and Colorado's legal cannabis market? Conservatives argue that blanket bans on firearm ownership for marijuana users—even those using it legally under state law—represent unconstitutional deprivation of constitutional rights without due process. They contend the ruling signals the Court's willingness to scrutinize such categorical restrictions.

Democrats and gun-control advocates counter that federal law, which classifies marijuana as a Schedule I controlled substance, justifies the prohibition. They warn that loosening these restrictions could enable substance-abusers to access firearms.

The practical stakes are high: Colorado has one of the nation's largest legal cannabis industries alongside a strong gun-owning population. State law enforcement and federal authorities will need to clarify enforcement priorities—a gap that could leave Colorado gun owners in legal limbo.

Watch whether Colorado's attorney general's office seeks guidance from federal prosecutors or whether the state legislature attempts to carve out protections for Colorado's legal cannabis users.
