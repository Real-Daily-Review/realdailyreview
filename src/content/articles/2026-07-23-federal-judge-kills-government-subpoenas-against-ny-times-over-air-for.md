# Federal Judge Slaps Down Government Subpoenas Against NY Times Over Air Force One Leak

A federal judge's criticism of sloppy legal work forced the Trump administration to withdraw subpoenas targeting New York Times reporters investigating classified details about a Qatari-donated Air Force One jet, according to 9News Denver.

The Justice Department had sought phone records and grand jury testimony from the journalists, arguing it was targeting the leakers—not the press—to protect classified information (Washington Examiner). But the judge found the government's legal foundation wanting.

The episode highlights a familiar tension: aggressive leak investigations can collide with press freedom. Conservatives generally support rooting out classified disclosures that compromise national security, but they also recognize that heavy-handed subpoenas to journalists can chill reporting and invite judicial pushback. The Trump administration's decision to withdraw suggests the legal case wasn't airtight—a reminder that even leak investigations require solid legal footing.

The broader question remains: how did details about a foreign-donated presidential aircraft become public in the first place? That's where the real security breach lies.
