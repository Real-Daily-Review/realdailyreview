# Colorado Faces Fire Season Crunch After Storm Chaos

Severe storms that battered Colorado this week left communities scrambling with flood cleanup—and now face a dangerous pivot to extreme heat and elevated wildfire risk, according to 9News Denver.

The weather whipsaw underscores a hard reality for Colorado: the state's natural disaster cycle is relentless, and government preparedness matters. While cleanup crews work through storm damage, fire officials are already bracing for conditions that could ignite fast-moving blazes across drought-prone regions.

The storms also triggered a secondary economic response. Hail restoration companies are partnering with local auto shops to handle the surge in vehicle damage claims (KDVR Fox31), a reminder that Colorado's hail season creates real costs for drivers and businesses—often uninsured or underinsured.

Conservatives should note the broader pattern: Colorado's exposure to natural disasters—floods, hail, wildfires—demands smart land management and forest maintenance, not regulatory overreach that ties the hands of ranchers and timber operators. Overzealous environmental restrictions have left forests choked with deadwood, a tinderbox waiting for ignition.

Watch whether state officials use this disaster cycle to justify new spending or regulations, or whether they'll focus on practical forest management and letting private restoration markets do the work.
