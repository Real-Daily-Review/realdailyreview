# Data Center Backlash Threatens Colorado's Tech Economy and Energy Future

Colorado's booming data center industry faces a populist-progressive squeeze that could derail economic growth and energy development across the state. A recent Gallup poll shows 71% of Americans oppose data centers, citing concerns over power consumption, water use, and environmental impact—claims that conservatives argue ignore the sector's genuine benefits to grid stability, job creation, and tax revenue (Washington Examiner).

The opposition reflects a broader pattern: progressives and anti-development activists are weaponizing environmental concerns to block infrastructure that powers the digital economy. Yet data centers drive significant economic activity in Colorado, particularly in northern regions where they anchor local tax bases and attract high-wage employment.

Republicans and industry advocates counter that modern data centers are increasingly efficient, often investing in renewable energy partnerships and water recycling. They argue that blocking this sector doesn't stop demand—it simply exports jobs and tax revenue to competitor states. Colorado risks losing competitive advantage if regulators cave to activist pressure.

The real threat isn't data centers themselves; it's whether Colorado will maintain a business-friendly regulatory environment or surrender to the same anti-growth ideology that's strangling California and New York.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to restrict data center development through new environmental or water-use regulations in the 2025 session.
