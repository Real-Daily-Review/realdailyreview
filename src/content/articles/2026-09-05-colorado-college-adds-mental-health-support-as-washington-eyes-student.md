# Colorado College Expands Mental Health Services—But Watch Washington's Data Grab

A Colorado college is launching round-the-clock mental health support for students, extending crisis services beyond regular counseling center hours (9News Denver). The program aims to fill gaps when on-campus facilities close.

The move reflects genuine student need. But conservatives should scrutinize what's happening in Washington simultaneously: Congress is pushing the so-called College Transparency Act, which would create an unprecedented federal data-collection scheme tracking students throughout their lives in the name of "transparency" (Washington Examiner). Critics argue the initiative amounts to Big Brother on campus—unnecessary government surveillance dressed up as accountability.

**Republicans argue** the transparency push would breach student privacy without fixing actual education problems. They contend Washington already collects enough data and that expanding federal tracking powers over young Americans sets a dangerous precedent.

**Democrats counter** that better data transparency improves higher education outcomes and helps policymakers make informed decisions.

For Colorado taxpayers and families, the question is clear: Will state colleges embrace mental health expansion while resisting federal overreach into student records? Or will they accept Washington's tracking apparatus as the price of federal funding?

Watch whether Colorado's higher-ed leadership pushes back against the College Transparency Act or quietly complies.
