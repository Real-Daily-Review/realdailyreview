# Hantavirus Outbreak on Atlantic Cruise Ship Spreads Across 12 Countries

A hantavirus outbreak aboard the MV Hondius cruise ship has claimed at least three lives and linked passengers to roughly 12 countries, according to the BBC. The World Health Organization confirms at least five confirmed or suspected cases, though the Guardian reports WHO says seven cases, including three passenger deaths.

The ship, which departed Argentina in March carrying 149 people from 23 countries, is en route to the Canary Islands off Spain's coast, where the remaining 147 passengers will be evacuated this weekend, according to CBS News. The U.S. plans to send a charter flight to retrieve 17 American passengers, with the CDC deploying epidemiologists and medical professionals to the Canary Islands, the Hill reports.

A British crew member requires urgent medical care, and another UK passenger remains in critical but stable condition, the Guardian notes. Some passengers have already returned to their home countries, complicating contact-tracing efforts.

Argentina, where the cruise originated, is investigating whether it is the outbreak's source. The WHO consistently ranks Argentina as having the highest incidence of hantavirus in Latin America, according to the Guardian.
