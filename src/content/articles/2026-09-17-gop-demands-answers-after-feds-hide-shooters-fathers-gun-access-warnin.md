# GOP Blindsided by New Details on Trump Shooter's Family Warnings

Republican lawmakers are demanding answers after learning that Thomas Matthew Crooks' father actively tried to block his son's gun access months before the July Butler, Pennsylvania assassination attempt on Donald Trump—and that federal authorities never shared this critical intelligence with the Secret Service task force investigating the threat.

According to Fox News and Just The News, Matthew Crooks sought ways to prevent his son from accessing firearms as early as May 2024, roughly two months before the shooting. Yet the task force investigating the attempt was unaware that federal authorities had even interviewed Crooks' family members, raising serious questions about inter-agency communication failures.

Republicans argue this represents a catastrophic breakdown in threat assessment and information-sharing between federal law enforcement agencies. If the Secret Service had known a family member was so concerned about the shooter's access to weapons that he took preventive action, the protective detail might have escalated scrutiny of Crooks before he fired on Trump.

The revelations underscore a familiar conservative concern: government agencies tasked with protecting national security often fail at basic coordination, yet Democrats routinely push for *more* regulation and *more* federal authority. Here, existing systems failed—not because there were too few rules, but because agencies didn't talk to each other.

Watch for congressional Republicans to demand a full accounting of which agencies knew what and when, and whether this negligence will finally prompt serious reform of federal intelligence-sharing protocols.
