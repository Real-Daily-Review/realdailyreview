# Colorado's Data Centers: The Infrastructure Nobody Talks About—Until They Need It

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are critical infrastructure powering modern life, yet they remain largely invisible to policymakers and the public, according to *Complete Colorado*. These facilities don't just serve Colorado—they're essential to the digital economy nationwide, handling everything from cloud computing to AI workloads that drive commerce, communication, and innovation.

The conservative case for protecting Colorado's data center sector is straightforward: these are high-value, low-impact employers that generate tax revenue without the environmental footprint of traditional heavy industry. They attract skilled workers and private investment with minimal regulatory burden—exactly the kind of economic development Colorado should encourage.

Yet data centers face growing pressure from progressive activists who blame them for energy consumption and water use. Democrats and environmental groups often frame data center expansion as a threat, pushing for stricter regulations and renewable-energy mandates that could drive operators to friendlier states. Colorado's competitive advantage—abundant power, fiber infrastructure, and business-friendly policy—could evaporate if regulators overreach.

Coloradans should watch whether the state legislature moves to impose new environmental restrictions on data centers or mandates that increase operating costs. Such moves would be economic self-sabotage.
