# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, November 27, 2026

The day after Thanksgiving, and Colorado conservatives have plenty to be grateful for — and plenty to keep watching. From a shady taxing district dodging accountability to a blue wave complicating national security, the news doesn't take a holiday.

---

## Mile High Turkey Trot Sets Records — and That's Worth Celebrating

Mile High United Way broke its own attendance and fundraising records at the 53rd annual Turkey Trot in Denver. This is the kind of community-driven generosity conservatives can wholeheartedly applaud — neighbors helping neighbors, no government mandate required. When Coloradans choose to give freely, it works. That's the model. Remember it the next time Denver progressives tell you only a new tax hike can solve poverty.

---

## 'CoCo' Taxing District Dodges Campaign Finance Complaint

A Front Range Passenger Rail taxing district — FRPR — spent money on a blitz campaign pushing the rail line just three months before November's election, and has now dodged a campaign finance complaint over it. Colorado taxpayers should be furious. Government-created taxing districts using public resources to lobby for their own expansion is exactly the kind of self-serving government overreach that conservatives have warned about for years. If a private group pulled this, they'd be buried in legal fees. Watch this one closely — it's not over.

## November's Blue Tsunami Complicates Iran Policy

Complete Colorado's analysis makes the case that November's Democratic wave has thrown a wrench into any coherent strategy for dealing with Iran — a regime that, conservatives argue, is weaker and more vulnerable now than it has been in years. Republicans have long maintained that strength and resolve are the only language Tehran understands. A Congress lurching left at precisely this moment is a dangerous gift to America's enemies. Colorado's defense contractors, military families, and anyone who values a strong national security posture should be paying close attention to what comes next out of Washington.

## East Coast Storm Chaos: A Reminder About Infrastructure Fragility

Nearly 2 million Maryland residents were asked to conserve water over Thanksgiving after storms threatened power to filtration plants. While this hit the D.C. suburbs, not Colorado, the lesson is universal: aging, centralized infrastructure is a liability. Conservatives have long argued for resilient, decentralized systems rather than top-down bureaucratic management. Colorado's own water infrastructure — already under pressure from drought cycles and population growth along the Front Range — deserves the same scrutiny our elected officials too rarely give it.

That's worth more than any line item in a budget.

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the 'CoCo' rail taxing district story — if a government entity can run a political campaign and walk away clean, that sets a dangerous precedent for every taxing district in Colorado. On the national security front, watch how the new Democratic majority in Congress approaches Iran policy heading into the new year; Colorado's military installations and defense industry have real skin in that game. And as the holiday shopping season kicks into full gear, watch for the next round of federal spending debates — Colorado taxpayers will be footing part of that bill whether they voted for it or not.
