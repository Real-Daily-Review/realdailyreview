# Federal Reserve Hikes Rates Despite Trump's Opposition—Colorado Borrowers Face Higher Costs

The Federal Reserve just made borrowing more expensive for Colorado families and small businesses, raising its benchmark interest rate by a quarter percentage point to 3.75%-4.0% on Wednesday—the first hike in over three years and a direct rebuke of President Trump's calls for rate cuts (The Daily Wire).

The move targets persistent inflation but carries real consequences for Colorado households. Credit-card balances, home-equity lines of credit, and adjustable-rate mortgages will all cost more (Daily Caller). For savers, the silver lining is higher yields on savings accounts and CDs—modest compensation for those watching their purchasing power erode.

Republicans and conservatives argue the Fed's timing is tone-deaf. Trump wanted lower rates to fuel economic growth; instead, the central bank doubled down on restrictive policy. Critics contend the Fed's rate-hiking cycle has already slowed hiring and investment without fully taming inflation—leaving working Coloradans squeezed between higher borrowing costs and persistent price pressures.

The Fed claims it's fighting inflation. Democrats largely support the independence of monetary policy. But for Colorado small-business owners and first-time homebuyers already priced out of the market, this hike adds another headwind.

Watch whether Colorado's real-estate market and small-business lending activity contract further as higher rates ripple through the state economy.
