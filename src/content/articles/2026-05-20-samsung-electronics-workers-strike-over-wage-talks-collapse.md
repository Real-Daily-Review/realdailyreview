# Samsung Electronics Strike Begins as Wage Talks Collapse

More than 47,000 Samsung Electronics workers will begin striking Thursday after wage negotiations with the company's union broke down, according to CNBC.

The strike, set to last 18 days according to The Verge, will be limited to Samsung's domestic chipmaking plants. The union and company failed to reach agreement on bonus payments, prompting the labor action.

The timing presents particular concern for the semiconductor industry. The Verge reports the strike raises worries about "already constrained production of memory chips amid the ongoing shortage," as global demand for semiconductors remains elevated.

Samsung Electronics shares moved lower following the announcement, according to CNBC. The union had previously agreed to mediation, The Verge notes, though those efforts ultimately did not prevent the walkout.

The strike represents a significant labor action at one of the world's largest chipmakers at a moment when memory chip supply remains a critical issue for tech manufacturers worldwide.

Watch for updates on whether the company and union return to negotiations or whether the full 18-day strike proceeds as planned.
