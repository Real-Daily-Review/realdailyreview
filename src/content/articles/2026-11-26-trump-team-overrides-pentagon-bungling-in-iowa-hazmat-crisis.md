# White House Takes Charge of Iowa Hazmat Crisis, Sidestepping Pentagon Bungling

The Trump administration's swift intervention in Iowa's dangerous lead azide detonation shows what happens when the White House actually holds bureaucrats accountable—a stark contrast to years of federal fumbling under Biden.

White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller stepped in to manage the Army's botched handling of 5 tons of destabilized explosive material, according to EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin (Washington Examiner). Zeldin credited Miller directly: "He took the reins." The Daily Wire reports Miller tapped Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell to handle communications after senior Trump officials grew alarmed by the military's crisis response.

The detonation itself—necessary to neutralize the volatile stockpile—succeeded, but the real story is institutional: a bloated federal bureaucracy needed a chief of staff to crack the whip and get results. This is exactly the kind of government dysfunction conservatives have warned about for years—layers of unaccountable officials moving slowly while real dangers mount.

For Colorado, the lesson cuts both ways. Federal hazmat sites exist across the state, from military installations to abandoned mining operations. When the feds drag their feet on cleanup, Colorado taxpayers and landowners bear the risk. Conversely, when Washington actually executes decisively, it's usually because someone like Miller forces accountability.

Watch whether this model of White House oversight extends to other stalled federal projects affecting Colorado—water management, energy permitting, and public lands decisions.
