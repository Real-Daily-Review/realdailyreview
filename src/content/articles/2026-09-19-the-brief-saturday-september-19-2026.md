# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Saturday, September 19, 2026

Colorado conservatives head into the weekend with a midterm clock ticking, a public health scare hitting two of the state's most-visited communities, and a reminder that the federal government still can't get out of its own way — or yours.

---

## Tariffs Are Giving Democrats an Opening — And Republicans Know It

With just over six weeks until Election Day, Trump's tariff policy is becoming a drag on Republican candidates nationwide — and Colorado's competitive congressional districts are no exception. Voters who backed the GOP on promises of economic relief are now watching prices stay stubbornly high. Republicans argue the tariffs are a necessary tool to rebuild American manufacturing and counter China; Democrats are hammering the kitchen-table costs. Conservatives should demand their candidates make the affirmative case — or watch the House slip away.

---

## Measles Exposure Hits Denver and Steamboat Springs

An out-of-state traveler exposed Coloradans to measles in both Denver and Steamboat Springs — the second such exposure in two weeks — as cases surge nationally. This is a straightforward public health story, and state health officials are right to track and disclose exposures quickly. What it is not is a justification for federal vaccine mandates or expanded government health bureaucracy. Colorado parents and travelers should check their vaccination status and stay informed through state health channels, not wait for Washington to tell them what to do.

---

## St. Mary's Glacier Gone — The Climate Narrative Machine Cranks Up

St. Mary's Glacier near Idaho Springs has disappeared for the first time in more than a century, and you can already hear the progressive policy apparatus warming up. Yes, the snowfield is gone — that's a real and notable change for a Colorado landmark. But Coloradans should be clear-eyed: the left will use every weather and geology story to justify more energy regulation, higher costs for oil and gas workers, and restrictions on the responsible resource development that powers this state's economy. Watch what policy prescriptions follow the headlines.

---

## AI Is Reshaping Colorado's Startup Scene — and the Broader Economy

Denver's Startup Week made clear that artificial intelligence isn't a future trend — it's already rewriting how Colorado tech companies hire, build, and compete. Separately, security researchers using Anthropic's Claude AI managed to access an OpenAI employee's account, underscoring that AI's rapid expansion carries real cybersecurity risks. For Colorado's growing tech sector, the message is competitive opportunity and serious caution in equal measure. Free-market innovation should lead here — not a wave of federal AI regulation that protects incumbents and stifles the next Colorado startup.

---

## Arapahoe County Attorney Charged in Jail Drug Conspiracy

An Arapahoe County defense attorney and his incarcerated client now face charges for allegedly conspiring to smuggle drugs into the county detention facility. This is a straightforward law-and-order story — and a reminder that the criminal justice system depends on the integrity of every officer of the court. Coloradans who have watched the left push to hollow out accountability in the legal system should note: when attorneys allegedly become co-conspirators, the damage runs deep. Prosecutors deserve full support in pursuing this case to its conclusion.

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the midterm map — if Republican candidates can't make a coherent, winning argument on tariffs and the economy in the next six weeks, Colorado's swing districts could tip blue and hand Democrats the gavel. Watch also for how Colorado's Democratic-controlled state government responds to the measles exposures: legitimate public health response is warranted, but any push to expand mandates or state health authority beyond what's necessary deserves immediate scrutiny. The conservative coalition that held in 2024 will need to show up, and show up unified, in November.
