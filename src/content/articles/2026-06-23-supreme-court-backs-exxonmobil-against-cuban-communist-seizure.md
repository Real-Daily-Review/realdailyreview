# Supreme Court Backs ExxonMobil in Cuban Property Seizure Case

The Supreme Court has handed a major victory to American property owners by allowing ExxonMobil to pursue a lawsuit over assets seized by Fidel Castro's Communist regime more than 65 years ago. The 6-3 decision (9News Denver) marks the second such ruling in as many months favoring U.S. owners of confiscated Cuban property, signaling the Court's willingness to protect American business interests against foreign government theft.

The ruling matters for Colorado's energy sector and broader free-market principles. ExxonMobil's case underscores that property rights—a cornerstone of American capitalism—deserve protection even when foreign governments claim sovereign immunity. Conservatives argue this decision reinforces the rule of law and holds hostile regimes accountable for decades-old confiscation.

The Court also struck down a Rastafarian prisoner's religious rights lawsuit after prison officials forcibly shaved his head (Just The News), and ruled against a lawful permanent resident in a border admission dispute, signaling a more restrictive stance on certain civil rights claims.

Meanwhile, the Court ruled against a lawful permanent resident in a separate border admission case, with more opinions expected this week (Just The News).

Colorado taxpayers and business owners should watch whether this property-rights momentum extends to regulatory takings and land-use disputes affecting ranchers and energy developers.
