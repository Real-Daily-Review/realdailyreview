# Trump's Tariff Strategy Draws Fire From Right-Leaning Critics

President Trump's aggressive tariff push is fracturing conservative consensus, with prominent right-wing outlets questioning whether protectionist trade policy serves American interests or merely repeats failed economic experiments.

The Washington Examiner's opinion section warns that Trump's "Liberation Day" tariffs—announced in April 2025—have already damaged his political standing, with approval ratings sliding following what critics called a "chaotic" rollout and "absurd" justifications. The concern isn't partisan grandstanding; it reflects a core conservative principle: free markets and open trade drive prosperity better than government-managed trade wars.

National Review, meanwhile, argues Trump should adopt Reagan's Cold War playbook on nuclear policy—building strength first to negotiate from a position of power—suggesting the administration should apply similar strategic discipline to economic policy rather than reactive tariff announcements.

**The Colorado angle:** Tariffs hit Colorado agriculture, energy exports, and manufacturing hard. Farmers and ranchers depend on open markets; energy companies face retaliatory measures against U.S. exports. Higher tariffs mean higher consumer costs for Colorado families already squeezed by inflation.

Republicans and conservatives who favor limited government and free-market economics worry Trump is abandoning core principles. The question isn't whether to challenge China—it's whether tariffs or targeted sanctions work better.

Watch whether Colorado's business community and agricultural leaders pressure the administration to recalibrate trade strategy before 2026 midterms.
