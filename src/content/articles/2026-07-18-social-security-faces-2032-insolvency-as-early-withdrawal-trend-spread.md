# Social Security's 2032 Cliff Looms as Early Withdrawal Trend Gains Steam

Colorado retirees and workers counting on Social Security face a hard deadline: the trust fund runs dry in 2032, yet financial influencers are pushing Americans to raid benefits early anyway.

The August 2026 Supplemental Security Income payment of up to $994 arrives in 13 days (Washington Examiner), but the real story is the system's insolvency clock. The Social Security Administration's own 2026 Trustees Report confirms the Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund reserves "will become depleted" in 2032—just six years away (The Daily Wire).

Despite this crisis, a viral trend encourages early withdrawal. Finance personality George Kamel of "The Ramsey Show" blasted news agencies and influencers for promoting the strategy, calling it reckless advice that ignores long-term consequences.

**The conservative case:** Social Security's structural collapse stems from decades of political cowardice—neither party has reformed the system's unsustainable pay-as-you-go model. Encouraging early withdrawals worsens the math. Republicans have long argued for means-testing, raising the retirement age gradually, and allowing younger workers to own personal retirement accounts. Democrats block reform, preferring tax hikes on workers.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether Congress acts before 2032 or punts the crisis to the next generation—betting that neither party has the spine to fix it now.
