# Houthis Strike Saudi Oil Infrastructure as Middle East Tensions Simmer

Iran-backed Houthi rebels are escalating attacks on critical energy infrastructure in the Red Sea, targeting Saudi Aramco facilities and threatening global oil supplies—a reminder that Biden-era restraint toward Tehran has failed to contain regional chaos.

According to *Just The News*, Houthi military spokesman Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree claimed the group launched missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia in response to airstrikes in Yemen, specifically targeting Aramco facilities in the Red Sea cities of Yanbu and Jizan. Meanwhile, *9News Denver* reported that there were no new U.S. strikes on Iran overnight, marking an apparent break from nearly two weeks of consecutive nightly attacks.

The pattern is clear: the Biden administration's inconsistent messaging and initial reluctance to confront Iranian proxies directly has emboldened Houthi aggression. Republicans argue that weakness invites escalation, and these attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure risk spiking energy prices that Colorado families and businesses already struggle to afford.

For Colorado, higher crude prices translate directly to pump prices and heating costs heading into winter—a tax on working families that progressive energy policies have already made worse.

Watch whether the administration finally commits to sustained pressure on Iran-backed militias, or continues the half-measures that have only invited more attacks.
