# Trump's China Softness Raises Conservative Eyebrows—and Questions

President Trump's surprisingly accommodating approach to China during his second term is drawing fire from the right, with critics arguing he's abandoning the hardline stance that defined his first administration.

The concern crystallized this week when Chinese President Xi Jinping greeted Trump on the tarmac during a visit to Shenzhen, a gesture that *Washington Examiner* critics say signals weakness rather than strength. For years, Trump built his political brand on confronting Beijing over trade, intellectual property theft, and military expansion. Yet his current posture—described as "surprisingly soft" by conservative outlets—suggests a departure from that messaging.

**Conservatives argue** Trump risks squandering leverage at the APEC meeting and beyond. The *Washington Examiner* editorial board contends that if Trump doesn't recalibrate, Congress should deploy its oversight authority to investigate what they frame as policy failures on China.

The stakes matter for Colorado: tariffs on Chinese goods affect state manufacturers and consumers, while energy and agriculture sectors depend on trade stability. Any Trump-Xi rapprochement could reshape the economic landscape Colorado businesses navigate.
