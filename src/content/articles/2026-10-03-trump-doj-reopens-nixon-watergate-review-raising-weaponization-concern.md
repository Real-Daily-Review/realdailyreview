# Trump DOJ Revisits Nixon-Era Prosecutorial Conduct, Raising Questions About Federal Overreach

The Trump Justice Department is reviewing how federal prosecutors handled the Watergate investigation, signaling conservative concerns that the Nixon case exemplifies decades of weaponized government power against political opponents.

According to *The Daily Wire*, the DOJ is examining the special prosecutor's office that investigated Nixon, focusing on whether the federal government abused its authority. Conservatives argue this review is overdue: if prosecutors can target a sitting president, no political figure is safe from selective prosecution.

*National Review* offers a more measured take, acknowledging Nixon's accomplishments while cautioning against making him a conservative martyr. The magazine warns the right shouldn't use Nixon's downfall to excuse present-day misconduct—a distinction between defending fair process and defending the man himself.

The broader conservative argument is straightforward: federal law enforcement has a troubling history of political targeting. Whether Nixon deserved his fate or not, the precedent matters. If the Justice Department can be weaponized once, it can happen again—and likely has, conservatives contend.

For Colorado taxpayers and gun owners, this matters because federal overreach doesn't stop at presidents. It extends to regulatory agencies targeting energy companies, ranchers, and Second Amendment advocates.

**Watch whether this DOJ review produces concrete findings about prosecutorial misconduct—and whether any reforms actually limit federal power or simply shift it.**
