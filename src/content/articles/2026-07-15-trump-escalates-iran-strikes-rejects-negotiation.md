# Trump Escalates Iran Standoff, Rejects Negotiation Path

President Trump has doubled down on military pressure against Iran rather than pursue diplomatic talks, ordering fresh strikes on Iranian targets this week as fighting intensifies in the region. The move signals a hardline approach to Tehran that conservative outlets say avoids the prolonged stalemate that plagued previous administrations.

According to *Just The News*, active fighting resumed with Trump ordering strikes on multiple Iranian targets, marking a sharp departure from negotiation-first strategies. *National Review* argues the president should "rip the Band-Aid off now" rather than endure "another protracted humiliation," suggesting Trump's willingness to act decisively distinguishes his Iran policy from predecessors who dragged out diplomatic failures.

The *Washington Examiner* notes Trump's announcement that the U.S. would assume guardian status over the Strait of Hormuz—a critical global shipping chokepoint—though the piece warns the precedent of American economic leverage over the waterway carries long-term implications beyond the immediate 24-hour news cycle.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy markets tied to Middle Eastern oil and regional stability directly affect Colorado's economy and energy sector. Sustained conflict could drive up fuel costs for state businesses and consumers, while clarity on U.S. resolve may stabilize markets.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump sustains military pressure or pivots to negotiation—and whether Congress demands a formal authorization for expanded Iran operations.
