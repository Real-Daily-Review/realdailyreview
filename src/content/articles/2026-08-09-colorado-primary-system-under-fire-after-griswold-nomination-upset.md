# Colorado Election System Faces Scrutiny After Griswold Primary Upset

Colorado's primary election process is broken—and the Jena Griswold debacle proves it. In a crowded Democratic field, Griswold secured the party's gubernatorial nomination despite evidence that another candidate was more broadly preferred among voters, according to *Complete Colorado*. The problem: Colorado's current system allows candidates to win with a small minority of support when the field is fragmented.

Republicans and election-reform advocates argue the state's primary rules create perverse incentives, enabling candidates with narrow bases to capture nominations that don't reflect genuine party consensus. *National Review* warns that Democrats' own flirtation with ranked-choice voting—touted as a fix—carries its own pitfalls and could backfire on the party that champions it.

For Colorado conservatives, the lesson is clear: election mechanics matter. Whether through ranked-choice voting or other reforms, any changes must be scrutinized carefully. Democrats' enthusiasm for "election reform" often masks attempts to rig systems in their favor; conservatives should demand transparency and guard against rules that could disadvantage Republican candidates or voters.

The real issue isn't just how Democrats nominate candidates—it's whether Colorado's election infrastructure serves voters or political insiders. Taxpayers and voters should demand clarity on what reforms are actually proposed and who benefits.
