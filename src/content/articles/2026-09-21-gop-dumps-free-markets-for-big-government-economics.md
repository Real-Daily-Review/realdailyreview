# GOP Shifts Away From Free Markets, Embraces Government-Led Economics

Vice President JD Vance is openly declaring that Republican economic policy has abandoned Milton Friedman's free-market conservatism in favor of Alexander Hamilton's big-government approach—and he's calling it progress.

According to the Washington Examiner, Vance has stated that right-of-center economic policy is now "much more Alexander Hamilton than it is Milton Friedman," and he's labeled that shift "obviously a good thing." The philosophical difference is stark: Friedman's tradition treated markets as the default and government intervention as the exception requiring justification. Hamilton's model treats a strong central government as the engine of economic policy.

This represents a fundamental break from decades of conservative orthodoxy. For Colorado's business community and taxpayers, the implications are significant. A GOP embracing Hamiltonian economics—with its emphasis on state power directing markets—opens the door to industrial policy, targeted subsidies, and regulatory frameworks that could reshape Colorado's energy sector, agriculture, and small-business landscape.

The shift raises hard questions: If Republicans now favor government-directed economics over market forces, what limits exist on intervention? Will Colorado's energy independence and agricultural freedoms survive a GOP that treats Washington as the solution rather than the problem?

Conservatives should watch whether this Hamiltonian turn translates into actual policy—and whether Colorado Republicans embrace or resist it.
