# Early Voting Opens as GOP Faces Midterm Headwinds Over Trump's Tariff Strategy

Early voting has begun in the 2026 midterm elections, with control of Congress hanging in the balance—Republicans currently hold the chamber but face a competitive landscape (9News Denver). The race comes as President Trump's tariff regime draws criticism from within conservative ranks, with some Republicans arguing the trade taxes are hurting GOP momentum heading into the midterms (Washington Examiner).

Trump has touted his tariffs as economically successful, claiming they produced "the most successful two years financially … in the history of the presidency." However, conservative skeptics counter that broad tariffs function as hidden taxes on American consumers and businesses, potentially dampening economic growth and voter enthusiasm for Republican candidates.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's agriculture, energy, and manufacturing sectors depend heavily on trade and export markets. Tariffs risk raising input costs for farmers, refineries, and manufacturers while inviting retaliatory tariffs that could hurt Colorado exports—a real concern for rural economies already facing headwinds.

Republicans must decide whether to double down on Trump's trade protectionism or pivot toward pro-growth, free-market messaging. Watch whether Colorado GOP candidates embrace or distance themselves from tariff policy as they campaign.
