# Trump's Iran Conflict Threatens to Overshadow Economic Agenda as Election Looms

Trump's escalating military posture toward Iran risks derailing the economic stewardship that built his political brand, even as he insists victory remains within reach.

The president warned Iran the fighting "isn't over" and vowed the U.S. will "win," but acknowledged the five-month conflict could extend through November's midterm elections (Fox News). However, National Review argues the Iran war—combined with Trump's tariff push—has caused him to "forfeit his signature issue of economic stewardship," suggesting the prolonged conflict is consuming political capital he needs elsewhere.

For Colorado, the stakes are clear: prolonged Middle East tensions typically spike energy prices, affecting gas pumps and heating costs for families already squeezed by inflation. Colorado's oil and gas workers could see volatility in crude markets, while consumers face uncertainty heading into winter.

Republicans defending the administration counter that military strength deters further Iranian aggression and protects American interests. Critics, including some conservatives, worry the conflict's duration and cost distract from the domestic economic agenda voters elected Trump to deliver.
