# DHS Chief Says Homeland Secure as Tech Outages Expose Vulnerability in Critical Infrastructure

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin is telling Americans to "take a breath" even as major tech failures cripple airlines and banking systems—a reassurance that rings hollow to conservatives worried about government's ability to protect critical infrastructure.

Mullin told Fox News host Sean Hannity on Monday that the homeland remains secure, though he acknowledged speaking with a technology expert who blamed artificial intelligence for the cascading outages (New York Post). Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called the situation a "head-scratcher," offering little comfort to travelers stranded by the disruptions (Washington Examiner).

The outages, which have hit Microsoft systems and triggered nationwide ground stops, raise hard questions conservatives have long posed: *Can government adequately oversee and regulate emerging technologies like AI before they destabilize essential services?* The Biden-Harris administration has pushed aggressive AI regulation and oversight, yet these failures suggest federal agencies lack real-time visibility into threats to the systems Americans depend on daily.

For Colorado—home to major tech hubs, financial services operations, and aviation infrastructure—the vulnerability is acute. If AI-driven failures can paralyze banking and air travel nationwide, Colorado's economy and workers face real exposure.

Watch whether Congress demands accountability on AI safety protocols and whether the incoming administration prioritizes infrastructure resilience over regulatory expansion.
