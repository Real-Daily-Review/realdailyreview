# Brief: Japan's Debt Crisis Could Spike Borrowing Costs for Colorado Businesses and Homebuyers

Japan's collapsing yen is threatening to drive up U.S. borrowing costs, potentially hammering Colorado's already-strained housing market and small businesses, according to analysis from the Daily Caller.

A weakening yen typically forces Japanese investors to pull money out of U.S. Treasury bonds, reducing demand and pushing interest rates higher. That ripple effect could mean steeper mortgage rates for Colorado homebuyers already priced out by Biden-Harris inflation, and costlier credit for ranchers and energy companies refinancing operations.

The broader risk: global market turmoil. When major economies stumble, capital flees to safety—but if U.S. debt becomes less attractive, American borrowing costs spike across the board. Colorado's state budget, already burdened by progressive spending commitments, would face higher debt-service costs if the state needs to borrow.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives have long warned that unchecked federal spending and inflation export instability worldwide. Democrats typically dismiss such warnings as fearmongering, preferring to focus on domestic stimulus and social programs.

**Watch next:** Whether the Federal Reserve responds with rate cuts (risking more inflation) or holds firm (risking recession)—either way, Colorado taxpayers and businesses will feel the squeeze.
