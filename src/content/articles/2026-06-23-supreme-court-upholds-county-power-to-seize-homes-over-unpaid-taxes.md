# Supreme Court Upholds County's Right to Seize Home Over Unpaid Taxes—But Conservatives See Danger in Government Power

The Supreme Court sided with a Michigan county that sold a family's home to cover unpaid property taxes, even though the sale netted far less than the home's market value (9News Denver). The decision marks the Court's latest ruling against property owners in foreclosure disputes, following a similar loss for homeowners three years prior.

Conservatives argue the ruling exposes a troubling vulnerability in property rights protections. When government can seize and liquidate real estate below market value to collect tax debt—keeping the surplus—it creates perverse incentives for local governments to rush sales rather than negotiate payment plans. Property rights advocates worry this opens the door to abuse, particularly in rural counties where tax assessments can spike unexpectedly.

The case carries direct implications for Colorado homeowners and ranchers. Property tax disputes have long vexed rural Colorado communities, where agricultural land values fluctuate and assessment errors can trigger sudden tax bills. If counties interpret this ruling as license to move aggressively on foreclosures, Colorado families could face similar losses.

Republicans and property-rights groups are likely to push for state-level protections—requiring counties to return surplus proceeds to homeowners or imposing waiting periods before forced sales.

Watch whether Colorado legislators introduce bills tightening foreclosure procedures and protecting homeowner equity.
