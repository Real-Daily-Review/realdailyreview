# Big Ten Bans NFL Veterans From College Football—Colorado Buffs Face New Eligibility Rules

The SEC and Big Ten have cracked down on professional athletes attempting to return to college football, a move that could reshape roster construction for programs like the University of Colorado. According to 9News Denver, both conferences are now prohibiting NFL players from competing in college athletics, citing eligibility disputes that have plagued the sport.

The Daily Caller reports the Big Ten's decision as part of a broader effort to "keep it pure college," reflecting frustration among conference leadership over legal challenges and murky rules surrounding player transfers and professional experience.

**The conservative case:** This move restores a basic distinction between amateur and professional athletics. Allowing NFL veterans to return to college creates unfair competitive advantages, inflates rosters with seasoned professionals, and muddies the amateur status that justifies tax-exempt treatment for university athletic programs. It also protects younger players' opportunities and maintains the integrity of college competition.

**Democrats and progressive sports advocates** argue stricter eligibility rules limit player choice and opportunity, particularly for athletes seeking second chances or additional education.

For Colorado, this ruling directly affects CU Boulder's football program and recruiting strategy. The Buffs will need to adjust their roster development without relying on veteran NFL talent returning to campus.
