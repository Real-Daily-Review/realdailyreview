# Trump, Xi Ink Diplomatic Reset With Back-to-Back Summits Planned

President Trump is banking on direct engagement with Beijing to reset U.S.-China relations, announcing two additional face-to-face meetings with Xi Jinping before year's end—a sharp pivot from the trade tensions and strategic competition that defined the previous administration.

Following Friday's White House state visit, Trump called the meetings "very productive" and said the nations have "made great strides" (Fox News Politics). Xi reciprocated, praising the visit as "perfection." The two leaders have already scheduled November meetings at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China, and a December follow-up (The Daily Wire).

Trump also commended media coverage of the visit, noting appreciation for "fair coverage"—a notable moment given his frequent criticism of press bias (Just The News).

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado businesses and energy firms watch China policy closely. Trade tensions and tariffs directly impact state agriculture, manufacturing, and energy exports. Improved U.S.-China relations could ease supply chains and create market opportunities—or, if negotiations falter, trigger retaliatory tariffs on Colorado goods.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether Trump's diplomatic engagement yields concrete concessions on intellectual property theft, forced technology transfer, and military aggression in the Pacific—or becomes a photo-op reset without structural change.
