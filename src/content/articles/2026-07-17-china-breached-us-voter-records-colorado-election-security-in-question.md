# China's Breach of U.S. Voter Records Exposes Election Security Crisis

Colorado's voter rolls may be among millions of records compromised by Chinese hackers—a stark reminder that election integrity depends on far more than mail-in ballot procedures.

According to declassified intelligence released by the Trump White House, China has compromised voter registration data in at least 18 states (Breitbart). Federal agencies warn the stolen records—totaling over 200 million—could be weaponized to manipulate voter rolls and facilitate absentee ballot theft (Just The News).

The breach underscores what conservatives have long argued: election security requires robust voter roll maintenance, strict ballot chain-of-custody protocols, and verification systems that prevent fraud—whether from foreign adversaries or domestic bad actors. Democrats have resisted voter ID requirements and tighter ballot controls, claiming they suppress turnout; Republicans counter that basic security measures protect the franchise itself.

Colorado election officials must now assess whether state voter databases were targeted and what safeguards exist to prevent roll manipulation. The state's reliance on mail-in voting makes this especially urgent. Coloradans deserve transparency on whether their registration data was compromised and what steps are being taken to harden systems against future intrusion.

Taxpayers should demand a full accounting of Colorado's election security posture and whether the state has implemented the verification protocols necessary to prevent foreign interference.
