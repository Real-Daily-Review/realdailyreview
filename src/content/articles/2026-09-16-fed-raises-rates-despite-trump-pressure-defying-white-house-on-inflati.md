# Federal Reserve Raises Rates, Defying Trump Pressure on Inflation Fight

The Federal Reserve under new Chairman Kevin Warsh voted Wednesday to raise interest rates for the first time in three years—a move that risks conflict with President Donald Trump, who has repeatedly urged the central bank to lower them instead.

After holding rates steady through Warsh's first meetings as chair, the Fed's monetary policy committee announced a quarter-point rate increase following a two-day meeting in Washington. Warsh has emphasized the need to keep inflation under control since taking the helm on May 22, though the Fed had paused rate action in late July amid broader economic uncertainty.

The rate hike puts the Fed on a collision course with Trump, who has made clear his preference for lower rates to support economic growth and reduce borrowing costs. Republicans and Trump allies argue that aggressive rate hikes risk slowing the economy and harming working families through higher mortgage and credit card rates—concerns that resonate across Colorado, where homebuyers and small business owners face mounting debt service costs.

The Fed's independence from political pressure is a longstanding principle, but conservatives question whether the central bank is making sound decisions or simply resisting Trump's legitimate concerns about monetary policy's real-world impact on ordinary Americans and Colorado's economy.

Watch whether Trump responds publicly to Warsh and whether the Fed signals further rate moves ahead.
