# Red Sea Oil Attacks Threaten Colorado Gas Prices as U.S. Strikes Iran Infrastructure

Houthi attacks on Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea are escalating global energy chaos—and Colorado drivers and businesses should brace for pain at the pump.

Iran-backed Houthi rebels claimed two attacks on Saudi oil tankers early Thursday as the U.S. military completed its 12th consecutive night of strikes against Iranian military and civilian infrastructure, according to Breitbart and 9News Denver. The escalating strikes and counter-attacks threaten to disrupt global oil supplies and destabilize energy markets already fragile from geopolitical tension.

For Colorado, the stakes are direct. Disruptions to Middle Eastern oil flow drive up gasoline prices nationwide—hitting working families, small businesses, and trucking operations hard. Colorado's energy sector, which depends on stable global markets, faces uncertainty. Higher fuel costs also ripple through agriculture and tourism, two pillars of the state economy.

Republicans argue the Biden-Harris administration's weak posture toward Iran emboldened the Houthis and created this crisis. Conservatives say strength and deterrence—not endless strikes without clear strategy—protect American interests and energy security.

Democrats counter that measured military responses prevent wider regional conflict.
