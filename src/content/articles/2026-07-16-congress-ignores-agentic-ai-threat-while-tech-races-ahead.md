# Congress Asleep While AI Enters Dangerous New Phase

Congress is fixated on yesterday's AI threats while the technology races ahead into "agentic AI"—autonomous systems that act independently—leaving American innovation vulnerable to regulatory fumbling or, worse, Chinese competition. According to the *Washington Examiner*, policymakers remain stuck debating chatbots and deepfakes while missing the real risk: government inaction that could cripple U.S. tech leadership.

The concern cuts both ways. Conservatives warn that heavy-handed regulation could strangle American innovation and hand dominance to Beijing. But Congress's paralysis is equally dangerous—it invites either a regulatory vacuum that lets bad actors operate unchecked, or a crisis-driven scramble that produces the worst kind of overreach.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts growing AI research and tech clusters in Denver and Boulder. Premature or poorly designed federal rules could chill investment and talent migration here. Conversely, a regulatory free-for-all risks reputational damage that scares away serious companies.

The real problem: Congress moves at a snail's pace while technology sprints. Republicans and conservatives should push for a light-touch framework that protects against genuine harms—fraud, autonomous weapons misuse—without micromanaging innovation.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for AI policy that preserves competitive advantage rather than surrendering it to bureaucratic caution.
