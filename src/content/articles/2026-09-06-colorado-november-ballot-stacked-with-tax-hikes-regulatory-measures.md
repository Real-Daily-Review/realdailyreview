# Colorado Voters Face Tax Hikes and Regulatory Overreach on November Ballot

Colorado voters will decide more than a dozen statewide questions this November—and several amount to a progressive wish list funded by taxpayers' wallets.

The ballot includes a proposal to raise income taxes on Colorado's highest earners, according to the Denver Post and CPR News. Democrats frame this as addressing inequality; Republicans argue it punishes success, drives out earners, and shrinks the tax base—a familiar playbook that's already made Colorado less competitive than neighboring states.

School districts are also asking voters to approve millions in mill levy overrides (CPR News, Denver Post). While education funding matters, conservatives note these requests come during a cost-of-living crisis when families are already squeezed. Districts must make their pitch on crowded ballots where voters face competing demands—a recipe for voter fatigue that favors well-funded establishment campaigns.

The ballot also includes a constitutional right to hunt and fish (CPR News), which aligns with Colorado's outdoor heritage and Second Amendment principles—a rare conservative win in a progressive-leaning state.

Voter ID requirements are also on the table, though details remain sparse. This reflects the national divide: Republicans see voter ID as election security; Democrats oppose it as a barrier to participation.

Watch whether Colorado's tax-weary voters reject the income tax hike or embrace it—a bellwether for whether the state's middle class still has appetite for progressive redistribution.
