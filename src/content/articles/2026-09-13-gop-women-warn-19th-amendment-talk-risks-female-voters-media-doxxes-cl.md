# GOP Women Warn Against 19th Amendment Talk; Media Scrutiny of Clancy Jury Raises Fairness Questions

Online calls to repeal the 19th Amendment following the Lindsay Clancy trial acquittal are creating a political liability for Republicans, according to GOP women sounding the alarm (Fox News). The backlash risks alienating female voters from the party at a critical moment.

But the trial itself has exposed troubling media conduct. An NBC10 Boston affiliate doxxed the lone holdout juror—a Black man who voted to convict—publishing identifying details in what The Daily Wire calls an investigation that "keeps getting deeper" (Daily Wire). Fellow jurors later revealed the holdout believed "she loved her children," suggesting the jury's not-guilty verdict hinged on sympathetic framing rather than evidence.

Republicans argue the media's targeting of a single juror for his dissenting vote represents an assault on jury independence and due process. The selective doxxing of the conviction-minded juror—not those voting acquittal—raises questions about whose narrative legacy media outlets are protecting.

For Colorado conservatives, this case illustrates two dangers: online rhetoric that alienates voters and media institutions willing to compromise judicial fairness to shape public opinion. Both undermine trust in institutions and the rule of law.

Watch whether mainstream outlets face accountability for the doxxing, or whether the incident disappears down the memory hole.
