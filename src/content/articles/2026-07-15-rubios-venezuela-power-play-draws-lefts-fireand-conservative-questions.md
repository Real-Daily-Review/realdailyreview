# Marco Rubio's Venezuela Role Draws Left's Fire—and Raises Questions About Executive Overreach

Secretary of State Marco Rubio's aggressive hand in shaping Venezuela policy—from sanctions to oil revenue management—has made him a target for progressive critics who see executive overreach, *National Review* reports. The left views Rubio's outsized influence as dangerous; conservatives counter that strong leadership on hostile regimes serves American interests.

But here's the tension: while Rubio's hardline stance on Venezuela aligns with Republican foreign policy, his concentration of power in the State Department raises a legitimate question conservatives should ask—whether any cabinet official should effectively govern a foreign nation from Washington without congressional input.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy markets matter here. Venezuela's oil production directly affects global crude prices and, by extension, Colorado's energy sector and gas prices at the pump. Rubio's control over Venezuelan oil revenue and sanctions policy shapes those markets. Colorado oil and gas workers and consumers have a stake in whether this policy is working or simply creating bureaucratic gridlock.

Republicans defend Rubio's role as necessary pushback against a hostile regime. Democrats argue it concentrates too much power in one official's hands. Both have a point worth watching.

**What's next:** Monitor whether Congress demands oversight hearings on Rubio's Venezuela authority—a rare moment where constitutional conservatives and progressive critics might find common ground on executive power.
