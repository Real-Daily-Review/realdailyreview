# Patrick Stewart, Legendary Actor and Cultural Icon, Dies at 86

Legendary British actor Patrick Stewart, whose commanding presence defined Captain Jean-Luc Picard on "Star Trek: The Next Generation" and Professor Charles Xavier in the "X-Men" franchise, has died at age 86 (KDVR, 9News).

Stewart's career spanned decades of stage and screen work, earning him recognition as one of entertainment's most respected performers. His portrayal of Picard became iconic for generations of science-fiction fans, while his role in the Marvel universe introduced him to broader audiences. Beyond major franchises, Stewart demonstrated remarkable range—from Shakespearean theater to comedic turns in films like Mel Brooks' "Robin Hood: Men in Tights" (The Daily Wire).

Tributes have poured in from across the entertainment industry and fan communities worldwide, reflecting the deep cultural impact of his work (9News). Colleagues and admirers have shared memories of his professionalism, gravitas, and distinctive voice that made even minor roles memorable.

Stewart's legacy extends beyond acting; he represented a standard of craftsmanship and dedication to the performing arts that defined an era of television and film. His work on "Star Trek" particularly influenced how science fiction explored philosophical and ethical questions, shaping the genre for decades.
