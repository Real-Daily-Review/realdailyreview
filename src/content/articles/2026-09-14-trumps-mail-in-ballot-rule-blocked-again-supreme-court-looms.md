# Federal Judge Blocks Trump Mail-In Ballot Rule; Legal Battle Heads to Supreme Court

A Trump-nominated federal judge has blocked the administration's new Postal Service rule on mail-in ballots, dealing another legal setback to a policy the White House is pushing ahead of the midterm elections. U.S. District Judge Carl Nichols ruled that Democratic and voting rights groups challenging the rule were likely to succeed in their case, according to *Just The News* and the *Washington Examiner*.

The ruling creates a second judicial obstacle for the policy as the Trump administration simultaneously petitions the Supreme Court to allow the rule to take effect before November's elections.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue mail-in ballot reforms are necessary to strengthen election integrity and prevent fraud. The Trump administration contends the USPS rule modernizes ballot handling procedures.

**The opposition:** Democrats and voting rights groups contend the rule could restrict ballot access and disenfranchise voters, particularly in rural and underserved areas—a concern that resonates in Colorado, where mail-in voting has been the default since 2013.

For Colorado voters and election officials, this legal ping-pong underscores the ongoing tension between ballot access and election security. Watch whether the Supreme Court intervenes before Election Day or allows lower court rulings to stand—a decision that could reshape how Colorado processes mail ballots in future cycles.
