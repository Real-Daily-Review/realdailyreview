# Trump Administration Signals Bipartisan Outreach on Immigration, Food Policy—But Conservatives Demand Guardrails

The Trump administration is opening dialogue with Democrats on immigration reform and food safety, signaling a pragmatic shift toward legislative compromise—but conservatives are watching closely to ensure no ground is surrendered on core principles.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayle Mullin told reporters that Trump directed him to engage Democrats on immigration overhaul, though Mullin emphasized amnesty remains off the table (Fox News). Meanwhile, Make America Healthy Again activist Calley Means urged Trump to partner with Democrats on food reform initiatives, citing Republican losses in recent midterm elections as reason to seek common ground (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative concern:** Republicans worry bipartisan "compromise" on immigration could mean accepting pathways to legal status for illegal aliens already in the country—a non-starter for the base. On food policy, conservatives question whether partnering with the left means surrendering to FDA overreach or accepting regulations that burden Colorado ranchers and small producers.

Trump's team insists these talks are about winning legislative victories, not capitulation. But the messaging matters: any deal must preserve immigration enforcement, border security, and free-market food production without federal micromanagement.
