# UAE Pipeline Project Nears Halfway Point as Oil Markets Stabilize

The United Arab Emirates says a new pipeline designed to bypass the Strait of Hormuz is nearly 50 percent complete, according to CNBC, as the region works to reduce dependence on the chokepoint through which much of the world's oil transits.

The project comes as oil markets showed signs of stabilization. Brent crude fell nearly 6 percent to around $105 a barrel after two China-bound supertankers crossed the Strait of Hormuz, according to the Financial Times, signaling a potential resumption of energy flows to Asia.

The UAE has already begun redirecting some oil exports through an existing pipeline to Fujairah, which has a maximum capacity of 1.8 million barrels per day, CNBC reported. The new pipeline represents a longer-term infrastructure investment to insulate the region's energy exports from disruptions at the Hormuz strait, one of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints.

The developments reflect broader efforts to secure regional energy supplies amid geopolitical tensions. Watch for updates on the pipeline's completion timeline and whether additional Middle Eastern producers pursue similar bypass infrastructure.
