# Alaska Senate Flip Signals Trouble for GOP in Competitive Western States

Democrats flipped Alaska's U.S. Senate seat Tuesday, with former Rep. Mary Peltola defeating two-term Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan and giving the left a commanding 56-44 Senate majority (Washington Examiner). Peltola won 50.1% to Sullivan's 45.8%, according to the AP call reported by 9News Denver and Fox News Politics.

The race drew national attention after a same-name ballot challenger—dubbed "Decoy Dan" by conservatives—muddied the waters for Sullivan in Alaska's ranked-choice voting system. Republicans argue the confusion cost Sullivan crucial votes that should have consolidated behind the incumbent, though Democrats counter that Sullivan's record simply didn't resonate with Alaska voters.

**The Colorado angle:** This loss underscores GOP vulnerability in Western swing states where energy and resource development remain flashpoints. Colorado Republicans should note that Alaska's flip mirrors broader Democratic gains in states where the party has successfully framed oil-and-gas development as incompatible with moderate voters' climate concerns. With Colorado's energy sector under constant regulatory pressure from Democratic state leadership, the Alaska result suggests Republicans must sharpen messaging on jobs and economic growth—not cede the energy narrative to the left.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado Republicans can hold Senate seats in 2026 by running explicitly pro-energy, pro-growth campaigns that directly counter Democratic regulatory overreach.
