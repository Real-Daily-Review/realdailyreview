# FCC Kills Local TV Ownership Caps—Media Consolidation Fears Ignite Debate

The Federal Communications Commission voted to eliminate ownership limits on local television stations, a move the FCC chair argues will strengthen local news operations but that critics warn could accelerate media consolidation and reduce newsroom diversity.

The FCC's deregulatory push reflects the Trump administration's broader skepticism of ownership restrictions. Supporters contend that lifting caps will allow stations to operate more efficiently and invest in local reporting. Opponents—including some conservatives—worry the move invites large corporations to gobble up local outlets, potentially squeezing out independent voices and local coverage that matters to Colorado communities.

Republicans and Democrats split on the issue. Some conservatives embrace the deregulation as pro-market; others, particularly those concerned about corporate consolidation, argue the FCC is trading local accountability for corporate profit. Democrats largely oppose the change, claiming it threatens local journalism and community control (CPR News).

The Daily Caller reports the move has drawn sharp criticism from those who see it as enabling consolidation rather than preventing it.

**What to watch:** Whether Colorado broadcasters use newfound flexibility to expand coverage or whether out-of-state media conglomerates acquire local stations and strip newsrooms to cut costs.
