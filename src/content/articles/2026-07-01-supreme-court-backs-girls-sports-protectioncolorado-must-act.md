# Supreme Court Backs States on Girls' Sports—Colorado Should Follow

The U.S. Supreme Court has handed conservatives a decisive win, affirming that states can protect girls' sports by limiting competition to biological females—a ruling that rejects the progressive ideology now embedded in many state athletic commissions.

National Review notes the Court "deals in reality on sex differences instead of ideology," while The Daily Wire frames the decision as recognizing that "biology is real and biology matters." First Lady Melania Trump celebrated the ruling, citing her memoir's position on fairness in women's athletics (Fox News).

**Colorado's angle:** The state's high school athletic association and universities have increasingly adopted transgender athlete policies aligned with progressive ideology rather than biological reality. This Supreme Court decision creates legal cover for Colorado policymakers to reverse course—protecting opportunities for girls competing in track, swimming, soccer, and other sports where physical differences matter.

Republicans argue the ruling restores common sense to athletics. Democrats and progressive activists counter that such policies discriminate against transgender athletes, though they've offered no answer to the fundamental fairness problem: biological males retain athletic advantages even after hormone therapy.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature and athletic boards respect this ruling or attempt workarounds through regulatory language designed to circumvent the Court's intent.
