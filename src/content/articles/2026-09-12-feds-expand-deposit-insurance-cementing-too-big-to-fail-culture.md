# Federal Regulators Quietly Expand Deposit Insurance, Cementing 'Too Big to Fail' Culture

Washington is about to codify the very moral hazard it claims to prevent. Three years after the Federal Reserve and FDIC bailed out Silicon Valley Bank depositors—covering accounts far beyond the standard $250,000 insurance limit—regulators are now moving to formalize unlimited deposit protection through the so-called Main Street Deposit Protection Act, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

Conservatives argue this represents a $5 million upgrade to the "too big to fail" scam. By guaranteeing deposits regardless of size, the government removes market discipline that normally punishes reckless banking. Large depositors and institutional investors should bear losses when banks fail—that's how capitalism works. Instead, taxpayers absorb the risk while Wall Street banks pocket the gains.

For Colorado, this matters. Regional banks serving Colorado businesses and ranchers compete against mega-banks that now operate with an implicit federal safety net. Smaller institutions face higher compliance costs and capital requirements, tilting the playing field toward the financial giants Washington claims to regulate.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives say this policy invites another crisis by encouraging excessive risk-taking. If every deposit is insured, why would banks maintain prudent lending standards?

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republicans—pushes back on this expansion or quietly accepts it.
