# Colorado Ski Industry Braces for Olympic Boost—and Weather Wildcard

Colorado's winter sports economy just got a shot in the arm: the International Olympic Committee will debut big-mountain freeride skiing and snowboarding at the 2030 Winter Olympics in the French Alps, a move that's already drawing cheers from Colorado skiers and coaches (Colorado Sun). The decision validates the sport's explosive growth and could drive tourism and sponsorship dollars to Colorado resorts and athletes.

But there's a catch. A potentially historic El Niño is forming—potentially the strongest since 1950—and meteorologists warn it could scramble Colorado's winter weather patterns in unpredictable ways (9News Denver). While El Niño can bring moisture to the West, it's also known for warmer-than-normal winters, which would spell trouble for ski season reliability and snowpack-dependent water supplies that Colorado agriculture and municipalities depend on.

For Colorado's ski towns and mountain communities, the Olympic spotlight is welcome news for business. But water managers and ranchers should watch closely: if El Niño delivers a warm, dry winter, Colorado's already-strained water resources could face another lean year, offsetting any tourism gains from Olympic buzz.
