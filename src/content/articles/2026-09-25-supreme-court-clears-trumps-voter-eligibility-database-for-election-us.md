# Supreme Court Backs Trump's Voter Eligibility Database—Election Integrity Wins Round

The Supreme Court handed conservatives a significant victory Friday, allowing the Trump administration to deploy a revamped federal voter eligibility database that cross-references Social Security and citizenship records—a critical tool for rooting out noncitizen voting ahead of elections.

The 6-3 ruling (Washington Examiner) lifts a lower court block on the SAVE database, which combines Social Security information with citizenship verification. The system remains voluntary for states, but its availability represents a major win for election integrity advocates who argue that verifying voter citizenship is fundamental to election security.

Democrats and progressive groups have opposed the database, arguing it risks disenfranchising eligible voters and that noncitizen voting is statistically rare. Republicans counter that any noncitizen votes are unacceptable and that citizenship verification is a basic safeguard of democratic integrity.

For Colorado, this matters: the state's election system could leverage this tool to verify voter rolls, though Colorado officials will decide whether to participate. Conservatives argue robust citizenship checks strengthen election confidence without burdening legitimate voters.

The ruling also comes as the Trump administration faces a separate court order (Just The News) to release suspended homeless assistance funds in Los Angeles—a reminder that judicial oversight cuts both ways.
