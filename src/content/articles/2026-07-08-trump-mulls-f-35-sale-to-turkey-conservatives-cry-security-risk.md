# Trump's Turkey Problem: F-35 Sales Risk U.S. Security, Experts Warn

The Trump administration is reportedly considering selling advanced F-35 fighter jets to Turkey—a move that would hand cutting-edge American military technology to a NATO ally with a troubling record of unreliability and hostile posturing toward Israel and U.S. interests.

National Review warns that such a sale would "needlessly jeopardize American security and service personnel" (National Review). The concern isn't academic: Turkey has a documented history of flirtation with adversaries. During recent Middle East tensions, Turkish leadership contemplated joining forces against Israel—and potentially the United States itself, according to reporting cited by the Washington Examiner.

President Trump, during his state visit to Ankara, characterized Turkey as a "great ally," suggesting the sale may be under active consideration. But conservatives argue this misreads the strategic reality. The Washington Examiner's analysis flatly rejects that framing: "No, Mr. President, Turkey is not a 'great ally.'" A NATO member that hedges its bets with hostile regimes and eyes American military hardware is a liability, not a partner.

For Colorado defense contractors and workers in the aerospace sector, F-35 production remains a critical economic engine. Diluting American technological advantage by proliferating advanced systems undermines long-term U.S. defense dominance—and the jobs that depend on it.

Watch whether Congress moves to block the sale or impose conditions on any transfer.
