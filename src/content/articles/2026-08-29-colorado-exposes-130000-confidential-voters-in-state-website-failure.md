# Colorado Secretary of State Exposes 130,000 Confidential Voters in Website Blunder

A bungled website update by Colorado's Secretary of State's Office left the private voter registration data of more than 130,000 confidential voters exposed online for over a week—a serious breach of election security that raises hard questions about government competence and data protection.

According to the Denver Post, the confidential voter information—requested by individuals facing safety risks, including domestic violence victims and election workers—was inadvertently made public during a routine tool update on the Secretary's website. KDVR reported that the office confirmed the error after the fact.

Confidential voter status is a critical safeguard. Colorado law allows vulnerable citizens to register without publicly listing their addresses, a protection that loses all meaning if government IT failures expose them anyway. This isn't a minor glitch—it's a direct threat to the safety of over 130,000 Coloradans who trusted state officials to keep their information secure.

Republicans and election security advocates will rightfully demand answers: How did this happen? What internal controls failed? How long before the breach was discovered? And most importantly, what's being done to ensure it never happens again?

Taxpayers should watch whether Secretary of State Jena Griswold's office implements real accountability measures or simply moves on.
