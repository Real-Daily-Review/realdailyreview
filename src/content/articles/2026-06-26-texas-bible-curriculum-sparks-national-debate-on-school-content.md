# Texas Bible Curriculum Move Reignites Church-State Debate—Colorado Should Watch Its Own Textbook Wars

Texas just mandated Bible stories in public schools, and the decision exposes a widening divide over what belongs in American classrooms.

The Texas State Board of Education approved required Bible readings for public school students, a move that affects roughly one in 10 U.S. public school students (9News Denver). The Washington Examiner notes that state law and legal precedent permit the requirement, though critics claim it violates the First Amendment.

Conservatives argue the curriculum reflects America's historical and literary heritage. The Bible has shaped Western civilization, law, and culture—omitting it from education leaves students ignorant of foundational texts. Republicans counter that secular progressives have systematically stripped religious and traditional content from schools while promoting ideological curricula of their own.

The left contends public schools must remain secular spaces where no single religious tradition receives preferential treatment. They warn the Texas move opens the door to religious favoritism and coercion.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's education battles have centered on curriculum transparency, parental rights, and what gets taught about American history and values. This Texas precedent will likely fuel similar debates in Colorado school board races and legislative sessions, particularly as parents demand more say in classroom content.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans push comparable curriculum reforms—or whether the state's progressive-leaning education establishment blocks them.
