# Colorado Should Learn From California's High-Speed Rail Disaster

Colorado policymakers should take note: California's high-speed rail project, approved by voters in 2008 and promised for completion by 2020, has become a cautionary tale of government overreach and fiscal mismanagement. The Washington Examiner has documented how the Golden State's ambitious train scheme exemplifies what conservatives call the "Californication" model of governance—where progressive ambitions consistently collide with reality, leaving taxpayers holding an empty bag.

Now Texas Democrats are pushing similar rail projects, following the same playbook that has buried California in cost overruns and delays. Republicans argue these ventures represent classic big-government thinking: massive upfront spending commitments, optimistic timelines that never materialize, and a fundamental misunderstanding of market economics.

For Colorado, the lesson is clear. If the state's Democratic leadership entertains comparable transit schemes—whether rail corridors or other infrastructure megaprojects—taxpayers should demand ironclad cost controls, realistic timelines, and private-sector accountability mechanisms. California's failure wasn't an accident; it was the predictable result of government planners overestimating their competence and underestimating costs.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats propose similar rail or transit projects, and demand they explain how they'll avoid California's fate.**
