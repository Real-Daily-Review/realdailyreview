# Dolly's Sister Warns: AI Deepfakes Are Already Out of Control

Stella Parton is sounding the alarm on AI-generated fakes flooding social media in the wake of her sister's death, calling out what she termed "fake AI garbage" (The Daily Wire). The 77-year-old took to social media Friday to condemn deepfakes and manipulated videos circulating online, while thanking the public for genuine condolences (9News Denver).

This is a preview of coming chaos. As AI tools become cheaper and easier to weaponize, bad actors will exploit grief, confusion, and viral moments to spread misinformation at scale. The Parton case shows how quickly synthetic media can pollute the information space around major news events—and how little recourse families have to stop it.

For Colorado, this matters. Deepfakes could soon target our elections, our business leaders, and our public figures. Bad actors could fabricate videos of candidates, manipulate testimony in legal cases, or impersonate law enforcement. Colorado voters and policymakers should be asking hard questions now: What legal protections exist? Should Colorado pass disclosure laws requiring AI-generated content to be labeled? How do we balance free speech with preventing outright fraud?

Conservatives rightly worry that heavy-handed regulation could become a tool for silencing legitimate speech. But doing nothing invites chaos.

Watch whether Colorado legislators propose AI disclosure or deepfake-specific laws—and whether they get the balance right.
