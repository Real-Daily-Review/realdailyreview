# Rubio's Venezuela Role Draws Left's Fire—And Raises Questions About State Dept. Power

Marco Rubio's aggressive stance on Venezuela is making him a target for the progressive left, who view the Secretary of State as a threat to their foreign policy agenda. According to *The Daily Wire*, Rubio has taken an outsized role in shaping U.S. Venezuela policy from Washington, overseeing sanctions, managing oil revenue flows, and maintaining direct contact with the country's leadership—effectively running key aspects of Venezuelan governance from Foggy Bottom.

*National Review* reports that socialists have identified Rubio as "the foremost threat to their project," triggering coordinated attacks on his tenure. The left's pushback reflects deeper disagreement over how aggressively America should confront authoritarian regimes and their economic networks.

Conservatives argue Rubio's hardline approach—using sanctions and financial leverage to constrain hostile governments—protects American interests and supports democratic movements abroad. Critics counter that his outsized influence concentrates too much power in one official's hands and risks destabilizing already fragile regions.

For Colorado, the broader question is whether State Department overreach on foreign policy translates to regulatory creep at home. Conservatives should watch whether Rubio's Venezuela model becomes a template for expansive executive authority in other policy domains—a cautionary tale about unchecked bureaucratic power, even when wielded by allies.
