# Colorado Traffic Deaths Mount: NTSB Probes Mesa County Teen Crash

Colorado roads claimed multiple lives this week, with the National Transportation Safety Board releasing a preliminary report on a single-vehicle crash in Mesa County that killed five 17-year-old occupants—a stark reminder that highway safety remains a critical issue for families across the state.

Separately, an 85-year-old cyclist, Ralph Derose, was struck and killed by a car while riding on Highway 119 in Boulder County, leaving friends mourning the loss. Meanwhile, a couple died in a crash Sunday evening at the intersection of Highway 34 and Weld County Road 17 in Greeley (9News Denver).

These incidents underscore ongoing concerns about road conditions, driver behavior, and vehicle safety in Colorado. While the NTSB investigation into the Mesa County crash will likely yield recommendations, conservatives and safety advocates alike emphasize that personal responsibility—proper vehicle maintenance, attentive driving, and adherence to traffic laws—remains the foundation of highway safety. Government can enforce laws and maintain roads, but ultimately, driver discipline saves lives.

Taxpayers should watch whether Colorado's transportation department uses these tragedies to justify new spending or regulations, or whether it focuses on enforcing existing safety measures and maintaining current infrastructure.
