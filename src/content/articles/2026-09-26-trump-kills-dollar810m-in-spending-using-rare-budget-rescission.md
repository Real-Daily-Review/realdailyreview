# Trump Uses Rare Budget Tool to Kill $810M in Spending

President Trump invoked a rarely used rescission authority to cancel $810 million in congressionally approved spending on refugee programs, diversity initiatives, and migrant education—a move the Supreme Court allowed to stand, according to Fox News and the Daily Caller.

The maneuver, known as a pocket rescission, represents an aggressive assertion of executive power over the federal budget. Republicans argue this demonstrates fiscal discipline and a willingness to challenge wasteful government spending that Congress approved but the administration deems unnecessary. Conservatives view such action as essential pushback against the bloated federal bureaucracy and progressive priorities embedded in appropriations bills.

Democrats and the left typically oppose rescissions as executive overreach that circumvents congressional authority over the purse. They argue that Congress, not the president, controls spending decisions and that canceling approved funds undermines the legislative process.

For Colorado taxpayers and conservatives, this signals a willingness to challenge federal spending on programs many view as ideologically driven rather than essential. The move targets diversity and migrant education initiatives—priorities that have drawn sustained criticism from the right.
