# Colorado Democrats Face Court Defeat, Internal Fractures

Colorado's Democrat-controlled legislature just took a hit from the state's own high court, which blocked a gerrymandering scheme that would have redrawn congressional districts to entrench Democratic power (Complete Colorado). The ruling keeps Colorado from joining the national gerrymandering arms race—at least for now—and signals judicial skepticism of partisan mapmaking, regardless of which party wields the pen.

The court's decision arrives as Democrats face deepening internal factionalism. According to Complete Colorado, the party is experiencing the kind of public discord and competing interests that Republicans endured for two decades. Left-wing activists, labor unions, and establishment moderates are increasingly at odds over spending priorities, energy policy, and social issues—divisions that threaten Democratic unity heading into the next election cycle.

**The Colorado angle:** Gerrymandering defeats matter. Democrats control the legislature and governor's office, giving them outsized influence over redistricting. The high court's willingness to block partisan maps suggests Colorado voters may get fairer representation than they would under a purely Democratic-drawn map—a win for electoral integrity.

Republicans and conservatives should monitor whether Democrats attempt to resurrect gerrymandering through different legal channels, and whether internal party fractures translate into actual legislative gridlock that constrains spending and regulatory expansion.
