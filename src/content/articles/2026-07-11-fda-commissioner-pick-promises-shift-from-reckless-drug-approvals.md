# FDA Commissioner Pick Signals Shift Away From Reckless Drug Approval

Dr. Heidi Overton, reportedly under consideration for FDA commissioner, represents a sharp departure from an agency that has lost the trust of patients and doctors alike. According to the *Washington Examiner*, Overton—who affirms that "human life is not disposable, but a precious gift from God, with inherent worth and dignity"—could restore accountability to an FDA that has approved drugs with alarming disregard for patient safety.

The FDA's credibility crisis stems from approving experimental treatments with minimal evidence, including cases where patients underwent invasive procedures for placebos. Conservatives argue the agency has become captured by pharmaceutical lobbies and progressive ideology, prioritizing speed-to-market over rigorous safety standards.

Overton's potential appointment signals the Trump administration's intent to rein in regulatory overreach and demand that the FDA return to its core mission: protecting Americans, not rubber-stamping Big Pharma's agenda. Republicans see this as essential reform; Democrats and progressive health advocates worry stricter approval standards could delay access to experimental treatments.

For Colorado patients and families, this matters directly—better FDA oversight means fewer dangerous drugs reaching state hospitals and pharmacies, and restored confidence in the approval process.
