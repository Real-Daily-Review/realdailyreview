# Trump's Endorsement Power Proves Mixed in South Carolina GOP Contests

South Carolina Republicans delivered a split verdict on Donald Trump's endorsement clout Tuesday, as Sen. Lindsey Graham cruised to renomination while Trump-backed Lt. Gov. Pamela Evette's gubernatorial bid faces an uncertain runoff (Fox News, Washington Examiner).

Graham, a Trump ally despite his hawkish foreign policy record, dominated his primary field with 58.4% of the vote against businessman Mark Lynch's 27.6% (Washington Examiner). The senator has served since 2003 and remains a close Trump associate, though conservatives have long viewed him skeptically on spending restraint and interventionist military commitments.

The gubernatorial race tells a different story. While Evette advanced to a runoff, she did not secure outright victory—a softer showing than Trump's endorsement typically delivers. South Carolina Attorney General Alan Wilson also advanced, complicating the Trump-backed candidate's path forward (Fox News).

**The Colorado angle:** These South Carolina results matter for 2026 midterm dynamics. If Trump's endorsement power is weakening in GOP primaries, it could reshape Republican nomination contests nationwide—including potential Colorado congressional and statewide races where Trump's backing has become a kingmaker credential.

**Watch next:** Whether Evette can consolidate Trump-friendly voters and anti-establishment energy to win the gubernatorial runoff, signaling whether the former president's primary influence remains decisive.
