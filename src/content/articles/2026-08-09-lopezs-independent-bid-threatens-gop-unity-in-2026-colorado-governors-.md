# Colorado's 2026 Governor's Race Just Got Messier for Republicans

Greg Lopez, a former Republican congressman and repeat gubernatorial candidate, has qualified for Colorado's 2026 gubernatorial ballot as an independent—a move that could fracture the GOP vote and hand the race to the Democratic nominee.

Lopez's independent bid complicates the path for Victor Marx, the Republican frontrunner, who needs unified conservative support to compete in a state trending blue. By running outside the party, Lopez splits the anti-Democrat vote and raises the specter of a three-way race where the left-leaning candidate wins with a plurality.

Republicans argue that Lopez's independent candidacy is strategically damaging to conservative interests in Colorado. The GOP needs maximum turnout and consolidated support to win statewide office—a divided right fractures that coalition. Conservatives worry that Lopez, who has run for governor before, may be more interested in personal ambitions than party victory.

Democrats, meanwhile, benefit from GOP infighting without lifting a finger. A split conservative base is precisely the scenario that has delivered Colorado to progressive control in recent years.

The 2026 governor's race will determine Colorado's direction on energy policy, water rights, gun regulations, and tax burden—issues where Republicans and Democrats fundamentally disagree. Taxpayers should watch whether Lopez remains on the ballot and whether Marx can consolidate Republican support before the general election.
