# Texas Data Center Moratorium Signals Growing Backlash Against Big Tech's Grid Demands

Texas Gov. Greg Abbott has imposed a moratorium on new data center development, citing the industry's failure to build local support—a warning sign for Colorado as tech companies eye our state's power infrastructure and water resources.

Abbott said data center developers "dug their own grave" by not collaborating with local governments, according to reporting from the Washington Examiner and Breitbart. The Republican governor's move reflects mounting concerns that AI facilities are straining electrical grids and consuming vast water supplies without adequate community input or regulatory oversight.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that tech companies pursuing rapid expansion have ignored legitimate local concerns about infrastructure capacity, environmental impact, and long-term sustainability. Abbott's moratorium enforces accountability—companies must earn community trust, not demand it.

**What this means for Colorado:** Colorado faces identical pressures. Data centers are proliferating across the state, particularly in Northern Colorado, where they compete with agriculture for water and stress the electrical grid. If tech firms don't engage transparently with local stakeholders—ranchers, municipalities, and taxpayers footing infrastructure costs—Colorado policymakers should consider similar guardrails.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's Democrat-led legislature moves to regulate data center expansion or allows market forces and local opposition to set boundaries naturally.
