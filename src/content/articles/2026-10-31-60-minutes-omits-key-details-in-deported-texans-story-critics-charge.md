# 60 Minutes Omits Key Facts in Sympathetic Deportation Profile, Critics Say

CBS's 60 Minutes ran a sympathetic segment Sunday on Moussa Diawara, a deported Texas father of two, but the Washington Examiner argues the network selectively edited the story to shape viewer sympathy.

The segment portrayed Diawara as fearing return to Mali due to his Christian conversion, and noted that the Department of Homeland Security questioned his credibility. However, critics point out that 60 Minutes never disclosed a crucial detail: Diawara's 2012 immigration application claimed he was gay and feared persecution on that basis—a material inconsistency that directly undermines the narrative the network presented.

The omission raises questions about editorial standards at a major news outlet. When networks choose which facts to include and which to suppress, they're not necessarily lying outright—they're curating reality to fit a predetermined storyline. In this case, the full record suggests Diawara's asylum claims shifted significantly over time, a detail viewers deserved to know.

For Colorado conservatives, this is a reminder that mainstream media often frames immigration and deportation cases through an emotional lens while burying inconvenient details that complicate the narrative. Taxpayers should demand transparency about how immigration cases are adjudicated and reported.

Watch for whether 60 Minutes addresses the omission or stands by its editorial choices.
