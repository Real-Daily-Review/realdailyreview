# Colorado's Ruling Class Needs a Reminder: Government Serves Citizens, Not the Other Way Around

Colorado's political establishment has forgotten the first principle of American governance—that power flows from the people, not down from bureaucrats in Denver.

On the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence's public reading, Complete Colorado published a sharp rebuke of the state's ruling class, arguing that government exists to secure individual rights and protect citizens *from* government overreach, not to expand state control over their lives. The piece echoes the founding principle that legitimate authority derives from the consent of the governed—a concept increasingly foreign to Colorado's progressive-dominated legislature and executive branch.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Colorado's trajectory—marked by rising taxes, regulatory expansion, and one-party Democratic dominance—represents precisely the kind of government overreach the Founders warned against. From energy regulations that strangle Colorado's oil and gas workers to gun control measures that infringe Second Amendment rights, the state's ruling class has systematized the very abuses the Declaration condemned.

Democrats, by contrast, frame their policies as protecting public health, the environment, and social equity—framing conservatives reject as paternalistic justification for power grabs.

The real question for Colorado taxpayers: Will this 250th anniversary moment spark genuine reflection on whether state government has drifted too far from its constitutional moorings?
