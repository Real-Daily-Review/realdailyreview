# Constitution Still Stands Guard as America Marks 250 Years

The Supreme Court's final decisions of the term and the nation's Semiquincentennial celebration both underscore a hard truth: constitutional limits on government power remain America's best defense against tyranny, and Coloradans should care deeply about preserving them.

As *National Review* notes, the Court's rulings—particularly on tariffs and executive authority—reaffirm that the Constitution still knows how to say no to unchecked power. That matters here: Colorado's energy sector, water rights, and Second Amendment protections all depend on courts willing to enforce constitutional boundaries against federal overreach.

The 250th birthday festivities, including a UFC event on the White House lawn, have drawn some pearl-clutching from the right. But as the *Washington Examiner* argues, Americans should embrace the messiness of free expression—even if it feels undignified. That's the point: liberty includes the freedom to be tacky.

Conservatives recognize that freedom isn't automatic. As *National Review* warns, "liberty depends on limits," and it remains "just one generation from extinction." Colorado voters and taxpayers should watch whether state and federal courts continue enforcing constitutional guardrails against progressive regulatory expansion—or whether judicial activism begins eroding the protections that keep government in its lane.
