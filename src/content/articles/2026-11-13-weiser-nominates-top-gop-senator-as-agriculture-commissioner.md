# Colorado Governor-Elect Weiser Taps GOP Senate Leader for Agriculture Post

Phil Weiser, Colorado's incoming Democratic governor, has nominated Cleave Simpson—the top Republican in the state Senate—as agriculture commissioner, signaling early bipartisan outreach on a portfolio critical to Colorado ranchers and rural economies (Denver Post). The move suggests Weiser may seek Republican buy-in on agriculture and land-use decisions that directly affect Colorado's farming and ranching communities.

Simpson's nomination matters because agriculture policy intersects with water rights, grazing permits, and regulatory burden—issues where Colorado conservatives have long pushed back against federal overreach and environmental restrictions that squeeze working lands. Republicans will be watching whether Simpson can shield Colorado agriculture from the Biden-Harris administration's aggressive environmental agenda, particularly on water policy and energy development on public lands.

The nomination also reflects political calculation: Weiser needs Republican support in a divided legislature to advance any significant agenda. Whether Simpson accepts the role and how aggressively he defends agricultural interests against progressive environmental mandates will signal whether this is genuine bipartisanship or Democratic window-dressing.

Conservatives should monitor Simpson's confirmation hearings and his first moves on water compacts, federal land leases, and regulatory relief for Colorado's ag sector.
