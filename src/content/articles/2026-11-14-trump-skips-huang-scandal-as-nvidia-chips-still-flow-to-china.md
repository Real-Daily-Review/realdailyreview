# Trump Sidesteps Jensen Huang Scandal as Nvidia Chips Flow to Beijing

President Trump will dodge the Jensen Huang Chinese espionage controversy during next week's meetings with Xi Jinping, according to a senior administration official—a move that leaves unresolved why the U.S. continues allowing Nvidia to sell advanced AI chips to China.

The former Nvidia CEO's downfall involved alleged ties to Chinese intelligence, yet the Trump administration has signaled the matter won't be raised during the trilateral talks with Xi and Putin. National Review questions the core issue: *Why is America exporting cutting-edge AI technology to a strategic competitor?*

Conservatives argue this represents a dangerous blind spot in Trump's otherwise hawkish China policy. If Huang's alleged connections to Beijing warrant his removal from Nvidia, they contend, then the company's continued access to Chinese markets demands scrutiny—not diplomatic silence.

For Colorado, this matters. The state's tech sector and defense contractors depend on robust AI capabilities and secure supply chains. Allowing advanced semiconductor exports to China while a major player faces espionage allegations sends a muddled signal about American security priorities.

Watch whether Congress—particularly Republicans on intelligence and armed services committees—demands answers on Nvidia's China sales and what vetting occurred before Huang's departure.
