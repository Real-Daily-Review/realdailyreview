# Democratic Vetting Disaster: Sanders Dodges Questions on Maine Senate Replacement

Bernie Sanders won't say whether Democrats properly vetted their replacement Senate nominee in Maine after their original pick imploded publicly. During an NBC "Meet the Press" appearance, Sanders praised Troy Jackson's working-class credentials and progressive platform but refused to answer directly whether the Democratic Party conducted thorough vetting of Jackson following former nominee Graham Platner's very public collapse (Daily Wire).

The dodge is telling. Republicans argue the episode exposes Democratic incompetence—first nominating someone so flawed he couldn't survive basic scrutiny, then rushing a replacement without clear answers on due diligence. Sanders' non-answer suggests the party may have prioritized speed over substance, a pattern conservatives say reflects broader Democratic governance failures.

For Colorado, this matters: Senate races shape national policy on energy, taxes, and regulation. When Democrats nominate candidates without proper vetting, it signals a party more focused on narrative than competence—exactly the kind of recklessness that drives inflation, kills energy jobs, and expands government overreach.
