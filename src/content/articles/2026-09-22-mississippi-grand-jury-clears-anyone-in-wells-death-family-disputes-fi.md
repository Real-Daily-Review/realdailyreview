# Grand Jury Finds No Wrongdoing in Nolan Wells Death; Family Disputes Autopsy Findings

A Mississippi grand jury has declined to bring charges in the death of 18-year-old Nolan Wells, who went missing during a July 4 boating trip and was found two days later. According to *Just The News*, the grand jury determined that "the position of, the condition, time of discovery, and location of Mr. Wells' body is consistent with drowning."

However, Wells' family is pushing back hard. As reported by *9News Denver*, the family has emphasized autopsy findings of blunt force trauma and held a news conference to respond to the grand jury's no-charges decision, suggesting the investigation may not have fully accounted for evidence of foul play.

The case highlights a familiar tension: official findings versus family skepticism when a young person dies under unclear circumstances. While the grand jury saw the physical evidence as consistent with an accidental drowning, the family's focus on blunt force trauma raises questions about whether all investigative avenues were thoroughly pursued.

Colorado families watching this case should note how grand jury decisions can diverge sharply from autopsy evidence—a reminder that transparency and thorough investigation matter when lives are at stake.
