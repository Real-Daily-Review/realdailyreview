# Conservative Redistricting Measure Clears Colorado Ballot; Road-Funding Deal Hinges on Democrat Election Wins

Colorado voters will decide a conservative-backed redistricting proposal after courts rejected competing Democratic maps, giving Republicans a rare opportunity to reshape the state's political landscape ahead of 2032.

The Advance Colorado measure is now the only redistricting question on the November ballot, according to the *Colorado Sun*. Supporters argue the proposal would create fairer district lines and reduce partisan gerrymandering—a direct rebuke of Democratic mapmaking efforts that courts found wanting.

Meanwhile, backers of the "Restore Our Roads" initiative have withdrawn their ballot measure after cutting a deal with state officials, the *Denver Post* reports. The agreement includes a legislative package—but with a catch: it depends entirely on Democrats winning key races in November, including the gubernatorial contest featuring candidate Phil Weiser.

**The conservative angle:** Republicans see the redistricting measure as a chance to break Democratic supermajorities in the legislature and Congress. Democrats, by contrast, are betting their road-funding deal survives only if they retain power—a gamble that ties transportation funding to partisan victory.

Conservatives should watch whether the redistricting measure gains traction with independent voters tired of partisan maps, and whether Democrats can deliver on road-funding promises if they lose the governor's race.
