# Colorado Worker Shortage Deepens as Labor Force Stalls Economic Growth

Colorado's economy is hitting a wall—and it's not demand, it's supply. Federal Reserve economists say a persistent worker shortage across multiple industries is now the primary brake on growth in Colorado and neighboring states, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City (CPR News). The shortage has expanded beyond construction and hospitality into new sectors, threatening businesses' ability to expand and hire.

This matters for Colorado taxpayers and employers. When companies can't find workers, they can't grow payroll, can't expand operations, and can't generate the tax revenue that funds schools and infrastructure. The shortage also pressures wages upward—good for workers in the short term, but it can trigger inflation and reduce business competitiveness.

Republicans and conservatives point to Colorado's regulatory environment and high cost of living as culprits. Excessive permitting delays, energy regulations that limit job creation, and skyrocketing housing costs driven partly by zoning restrictions all make Colorado less attractive to workers and employers alike. The left's answer—more government programs and wage mandates—only compounds the problem by raising business costs further.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs faces a separate legal challenge over municipal election timing under a 2025 voting law (Complete Colorado), adding regulatory uncertainty for local governments.

**Watch whether state lawmakers address regulatory barriers to hiring, or double down on mandates that worsen the shortage.**
