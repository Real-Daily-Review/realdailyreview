# Amendment 87's Graduated Tax Threatens Colorado's Economic Edge

Colorado voters just handed Democrats a graduated income tax that threatens to undermine the state's hard-won reputation as a business-friendly destination. Amendment 87, which passed this month, imposes steeper tax rates on higher earners—a progressive staple that conservatives warn will drive talent and investment elsewhere.

A Colorado think tank (CPR News reports) is already flagging competitive concerns, noting that with new leadership in the governor's office and a shifting fiscal landscape, lawmakers must spend any new revenue wisely. That's code for: don't blow it on more government bloat.

But Complete Colorado goes further, warning that Amendment 87 could sink Colorado into the bottom 10 states for tax competitiveness. The concern is straightforward: graduated income taxes punish success and entrepreneurship. When Colorado's top earners face higher rates than neighboring states, they vote with their feet—taking jobs, businesses, and tax base with them.

Republicans argue the amendment is a bait-and-switch: Democrats promised "tax the rich," but graduated systems inevitably creep downward, eventually hitting middle-class earners. Conservatives say Colorado should compete on low, flat taxes and business-friendly policy, not chase revenue through punitive rate hikes.

Watch whether the legislature uses this windfall to cut spending elsewhere or simply expands government programs—a test of whether Democrats meant fiscal responsibility or just wanted more money to spend.
