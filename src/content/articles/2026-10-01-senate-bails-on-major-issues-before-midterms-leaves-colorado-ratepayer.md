# Senate Abandons Major Issues to Hit Campaign Trail

Congress is heading home empty-handed on affordability, AI regulation, and data center policy—leaving Colorado ratepayers and businesses in limbo as midterm politics trump governing.

The Senate wrapped up its pre-election work without addressing critical issues that directly affect Colorado households and the state's energy future. According to CPR News, senators attempted to pass several bills before departing for campaign season, but major legislation stalled. The Daily Caller reports that affordability concerns, artificial intelligence oversight, data center expansion, and ratepayer protections all fell victim to the election calendar.

For Colorado specifically, the failure to address data center policy and ratepayer protections matters enormously. The state is seeing explosive growth in energy-intensive data centers—a boon for economic development but a potential burden on electricity costs for families and small businesses. Without federal clarity on AI regulation and data infrastructure, Colorado utilities and consumers face uncertainty about future rate impacts.

Republicans argue that Congress should prioritize kitchen-table issues—energy costs, inflation, and regulatory clarity—rather than using legislative sessions as campaign props. Democrats counter that gridlock reflects partisan obstruction, though both parties share blame for abandoning substantive work before elections.

**Watch for:** whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes for data center and ratepayer protections in the next session, or whether these issues get buried again.
