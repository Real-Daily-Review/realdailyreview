# Maine Dems' Senate Pick Shows Party's Priorities Are Out of Touch With Working Coloradans

Maine Democrats' primary debate this week offered a stark reminder of how far the national Democratic Party has drifted from kitchen-table issues. One candidate—a transgender songwriter—cited songwriting as a Senate qualification, drawing mockery across social media (Fox News). The moment crystallized what Republicans argue is the left's obsession with identity politics over economic competence.

Eight Democrats competed in the Maine primary debate, hosted by News Center Maine, to replace a previous nominee (The Daily Wire). The field included a logger and other candidates, but it was the songwriter's response that went viral—underscoring how progressive candidates often lead with cultural grievances rather than jobs, inflation, or border security.

**The Colorado angle:** While Maine voters sort their own primary, Colorado Republicans should note how national Democratic messaging increasingly alienates working families. Coloradans who depend on energy jobs, ranching, and small business—not cultural symbolism—are watching whether Democrats can pivot back to economic reality or double down on progressive identity politics.

Republicans argue the Democratic Party has lost sight of what matters to ordinary Americans. Democrats counter that diversity and inclusion strengthen their coalition, but primary debates like Maine's suggest the messaging gap is widening.

**Watch:** Whether Maine's Democratic nominee can appeal to swing voters in a general election, or whether this primary signals deeper trouble for Democrats in purple states.
