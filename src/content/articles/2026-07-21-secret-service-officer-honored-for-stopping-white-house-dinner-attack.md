# Secret Service Officer to Receive Award for Stopping White House Dinner Attack

A Secret Service officer will be honored for stopping an armed suspect from breaching the White House Correspondents' Association dinner in April. Victor Gonzales and hotel staff at the Washington Hilton will receive a special award from the association for their "exceptional service" during the incident, according to 9News Denver and the Washington Examiner.

The officer's quick action prevented what could have been a catastrophic security failure at one of Washington's most high-profile media events. Cole Allen allegedly attempted to rush the dinner; Gonzales intervened and stopped the threat before it reached attendees.

This recognition underscores a critical reality: armed security works. When trained professionals are positioned to respond to threats, lives are protected—a point conservatives have long emphasized in the Second Amendment debate. The incident validates the necessity of armed security at major events and the importance of allowing capable individuals to carry and use firearms defensively.

The award ceremony will be rescheduled to honor those who prevented tragedy through decisive action and professionalism.
