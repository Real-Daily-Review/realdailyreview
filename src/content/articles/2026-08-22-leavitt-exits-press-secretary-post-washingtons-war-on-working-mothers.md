# White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt Steps Down; Work-Life Balance Debate Reignites

Karoline Leavitt, 28, is resigning as White House press secretary after concluding the job's demands are incompatible with motherhood—a decision that exposes how Washington's grueling pace punishes working parents, particularly women.

Leavitt returned from maternity leave before reaching her decision, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Vice President JD Vance responded graciously, telling *Breitbart* the administration is "sad to see her go," signaling no public friction over her departure.

The resignation highlights a real problem conservatives should acknowledge: elite government positions operate on a pre-family schedule designed for a different era. Leavitt's choice isn't a failure of her ambition or capability—it's an indictment of an institution that treats work-life balance as a luxury rather than a necessity.

This matters beyond Washington optics. Colorado families face similar pressures in demanding careers across the private sector. When government and corporate culture penalize parenthood, especially motherhood, talented people exit the workforce entirely. That's not progress; it's waste.

The broader question: Will Washington reform its expectations, or will it continue losing capable leaders to burnout?
