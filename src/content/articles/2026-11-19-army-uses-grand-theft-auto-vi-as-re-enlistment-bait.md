# Grand Theft Auto VI Launches to Rave Reviews—and Unlikely Military Interest

The long-awaited "Grand Theft Auto VI" hit shelves this week after a 13-year gap, drawing massive player interest and critical acclaim (Breitbart). But the real story for taxpayers? The U.S. Army is now using the game as a re-enlistment incentive.

The 9th Brigade Engineer Battalion in Georgia offered soldiers four-day weekends to play "GTA VI" in exchange for signing up for additional service years (The Daily Wire). It's a creative recruitment tactic—and a sign that the military is adapting to compete for younger talent in a tight labor market.

For Colorado conservatives, this raises a straightforward question: Is this the best use of military resources and taxpayer dollars? While unconventional incentives beat across-the-board pay hikes, the optics matter. The Army should be clear about whether this is a one-off morale move or a broader shift in how it retains soldiers.

The game itself has earned widespread praise as a technical and creative achievement, with critics calling it a "masterpiece" (9News Denver). Colorado's gaming and tech communities will likely see interest spike among younger players and developers.

Watch whether other military branches adopt similar gaming-based incentives—and whether Congress questions the precedent.
