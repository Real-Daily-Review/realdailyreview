# Biden's Rural Broadband Bust: $40B Spent, Zero Connected

The Biden administration's rural broadband push collapsed spectacularly—$40 billion spent with not a single American newly connected to the internet, according to the Washington Examiner. Combined with prior Obama-era rural initiatives, taxpayers have flushed roughly $88 billion down a government-spending drain that produced nothing.

This matters for Colorado. Rural counties across the state—from the Western Slope to the Eastern Plains—were promised broadband connectivity that never materialized. Instead of delivering results, Washington bureaucrats designed programs that failed to bridge the digital divide while enriching contractors and consultants.

Republicans argue the fundamental problem is government's inability to execute. Rather than letting private telecom companies compete and innovate in underserved markets, Democrats created bloated federal programs riddled with waste and red tape. The Left's answer to every problem is always more spending; the result is always the same: empty promises and lighter wallets.

The contrast is stark: Trump's rural health transformation program, the Examiner reports, is actually delivering tangible outcomes—not just appropriations that vanish into bureaucratic black holes.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state and federal officials demand accountability for the $40 billion broadband fiasco before approving the next round of rural "investment" schemes.
