# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are bankrolling primary elections for political parties whether they belong to them or not—a government overreach that conservatives say must end.

According to Complete Colorado, the state currently forces taxpayers to finance primaries for parties to which many citizens don't belong. This arrangement amounts to compelled political speech and subsidies, critics argue, violating basic principles of limited government and individual liberty.

Complete Colorado editorial board frames this as a "courageous" move toward separating state from party, noting that Colorado government has no legitimate business granting special recognition or privileges to political organizations. Conservatives contend that if Democrats and Republicans want to run primaries, they should fund them privately—not extract money from every taxpayer's pocket.

The issue cuts to the heart of fiscal responsibility: Why should a libertarian-leaning independent or a conservative who disagrees with GOP leadership be forced to pay for either party's nominating process? Republicans and conservatives argue the practice is indefensible in a free society.

Colorado lawmakers should face pressure to end this taxpayer subsidy and let political parties fund their own elections through voluntary member contributions.
