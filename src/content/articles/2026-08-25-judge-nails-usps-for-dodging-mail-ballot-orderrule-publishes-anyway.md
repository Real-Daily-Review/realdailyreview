# Federal Judge Slams USPS for Dodging Mail-Ballot Order—But Rule Stands Anyway

A federal judge found the U.S. Postal Service in violation of a court injunction over mail-ballot handling rules, yet declined to block the rule's publication—a ruling that underscores the judicial frustration with executive-branch end-runs around court orders.

According to the Washington Examiner, Judge Denise Talwani said Tuesday that USPS violated a nationwide injunction by issuing President Trump's mail-ballot rule for the November election, but refused to impose additional relief preventing its scheduled Wednesday publication. Just The News reported the judge accused USPS of "feigning" compliance by claiming the rule wouldn't apply to the midterms—a distinction Talwani clearly found insufficient.

The ruling exposes a troubling pattern: federal agencies issuing rules while technically skirting court orders through semantic loopholes. For Colorado voters and election officials, this creates uncertainty heading into a major election cycle. Republicans argue the USPS overreach threatens election integrity and state sovereignty over ballot procedures; Democrats counter that mail-ballot access is essential to voter participation.

The broader issue here is institutional accountability. When courts issue injunctions and agencies ignore them with technical workarounds, the rule of law erodes—regardless of which party controls the executive branch.
