# Road Rage, Crime Surge Strain Colorado Law Enforcement Resources

Colorado's streets are getting more dangerous, and state police are overwhelmed tracking the fallout. Two arrests this week—a road rage shooting in Boulder County and a toy-gun bank robbery in Greeley—underscore a troubling trend: Colorado State Patrol fielded more than 33,000 calls to its aggressive driving report line last year (9News), signaling a sharp uptick in road rage incidents statewide.

The Boulder County shooting and Greeley bank robbery, both arrested Thursday, reflect broader public safety challenges that conservatives argue stem partly from soft-on-crime policies and inadequate law enforcement funding. While Democrats often blame "root causes" and demand social spending, Republicans point to rising crime as evidence that Colorado needs stronger enforcement, better officer pay and recruitment, and swift prosecution—not more government programs.

Road rage incidents endanger families and tie up state resources that could address violent crime more broadly. Colorado's aggressive driving problem also raises questions about whether the state's transportation and public safety budgets are properly aligned with actual demand.

Taxpayers should watch whether the legislature funds CSP adequately in the next budget cycle, or whether political correctness toward law enforcement continues starving the agency of resources it clearly needs.
