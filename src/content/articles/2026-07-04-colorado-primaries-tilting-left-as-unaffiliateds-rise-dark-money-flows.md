# Colorado Primary Chaos: Unaffiliateds Surge While Progressive Dark Money Reshapes Statehouse

Colorado's primary system is quietly handing power to unaffiliated voters and well-funded progressive activists—a one-two punch that should alarm conservatives watching their influence diluted.

Unaffiliated voters now represent a growing share of Colorado's electorate, yet party-registered voters who skip caucuses surrender their say entirely under current rules (Complete Colorado). Meanwhile, progressive candidates backed by heavy dark-money spending swept several Democratic statehouse primaries in metro Denver, unseating two incumbent lawmakers despite Republican opposition spending (Denver Post).

Here's the conservative problem: Republicans and party loyalists are effectively locked out of early contests while the left's cash machine operates in the shadows. When primaries decide general elections—as they increasingly do in Colorado's blue-trending urban corridors—dark money and activist energy, not grassroots Republican turnout, determine who represents Coloradans.

The unaffiliated surge compounds this. Voters unmoored from party structures lack institutional backing and clear conservative messaging, making them susceptible to progressive messaging and media narratives.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado Republicans will reform primary rules to restore party relevance, or whether they'll continue ceding early contests to unaffiliated voters and progressive money machines.
