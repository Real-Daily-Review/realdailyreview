# Democrats Eat Their Own Over LGBTQ+ Messaging Calculation

A Washington state Democratic congressional candidate has triggered a civil war within her own party after admitting she deliberately scrubbed LGBTQ+ rights from her campaign messaging to avoid alienating Muslim voters, according to Fox News and the New York Post.

Melissa Chaudhry's strategic omission sparked immediate backlash from Democrat activists. The Washington State Stonewall Democrats called for organizations to rescind their endorsements, signaling that progressive coalition management is fracturing over identity politics priorities (New York Post).

The episode exposes a core tension within the Democratic coalition: how to simultaneously court identity groups with competing values without appearing to abandon either constituency. Chaudhry's calculation—that Muslim voters might reject LGBTQ+ advocacy—suggests Democrats believe these blocs cannot be unified around the same messaging.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that progressive identity politics inevitably creates these contradictions. When you build a coalition on competing grievance categories rather than shared principle, someone gets deprioritized. Here, LGBTQ+ activists discovered they were expendable.

While this fight is playing out in Washington state, Colorado Democrats face similar demographic pressures in competitive districts where Muslim and Hispanic voters are growing constituencies. Watch whether Colorado Democratic candidates adopt similar messaging strategies—and whether state LGBTQ+ groups push back as aggressively as their national counterparts.
