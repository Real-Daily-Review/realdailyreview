# Texas Bible Mandate Reignites Church-State Debate—Colorado Should Watch Its Own Textbook Wars

Texas just became the first state in recent history to require Bible stories in K-12 public school curricula, and the move is already sparking a national fight over what belongs in American classrooms.

The Republican-majority Texas State Board of Education voted Friday to add Bible passages to mandatory reading lists across the state—a significant shift in a system educating roughly one in 10 U.S. public school students (Washington Examiner). Supporters argue the Bible is a foundational text for understanding Western literature, history, and law, and that state law permits the requirement (Washington Examiner opinion).

Critics, however, contend the mandate violates First Amendment protections separating church and state, raising constitutional questions that could ripple across the country.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's own school boards have battled over curriculum content for years—from sex education to civics standards. This Texas precedent will likely embolden both conservative parents demanding religious literacy and progressive activists opposing any religious content in public schools. Expect Colorado's education debates to intensify as both sides cite Texas as either a model or a cautionary tale.

Conservatives should monitor whether Colorado's Republican-led districts move to adopt similar Bible literacy initiatives—and whether the left's inevitable legal challenges succeed or fail.
