# Denver Man Convicted in Hairstylist Death Cover-Up

A Denver jury has convicted Brandon Mumma, 44, of tampering with a body and evidence in the death of hairstylist Jax Gratton—a verdict that underscores the importance of Colorado's criminal justice system holding bad actors accountable, even when prosecutors can't prove the underlying homicide charge.

According to 9News Denver, Mumma faced charges related to the investigation but was not charged with Gratton's death itself. KDVR Fox31 Denver reports he was found guilty of the tampering and evidence-destruction counts, which carry serious penalties under state law.

The conviction reflects Colorado courts functioning as intended: when direct murder charges prove difficult to sustain, obstruction and evidence-tampering statutes provide prosecutors a path to justice. This matters for Colorado families and victims' advocates who depend on the rule of law to work, even in complicated cases.

Conservatives and law-and-order advocates generally support robust prosecution of obstruction charges—they recognize that destroying evidence or tampering with bodies strikes at the heart of the justice system itself. The conviction sends a message that Colorado won't tolerate cover-ups.

Watch whether prosecutors pursue additional charges or whether this conviction stands as the final accountability measure in Gratton's case.
