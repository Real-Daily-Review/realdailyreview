# Bill Gates Admits Epstein Ties Were 'Grave Error' in Closed-Door Congressional Grilling

Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates testified before the House Oversight and Government Reform Committee Wednesday about his relationship with convicted sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, calling the connection a "grave error" (Just The News). Gates told lawmakers that Epstein attempted to exploit his personal life—specifically extramarital affairs—as leverage to maintain influence over him, according to testimony reported by the Washington Examiner.

The billionaire philanthropist claimed he was "never interested" in deepening a personal relationship with Epstein and "never reciprocated" such overtures. However, the closed-door nature of the hearing drew scrutiny from conservative outlets. The Daily Caller questioned why Gates was permitted to testify privately rather than publicly, noting lawmakers could conduct "more in depth, sustained questioning" in open session—raising transparency concerns about how Congress handles high-profile figures with significant wealth and influence.

Gates' acknowledgment of the relationship underscores the broader Epstein scandal's reach into elite circles, a reality that should concern Americans about accountability and the apparent special treatment afforded to the wealthy and connected. Conservatives should watch whether Congress demands public testimony and whether Gates' philanthropic influence on education and health policy receives renewed scrutiny given these newly disclosed details.
