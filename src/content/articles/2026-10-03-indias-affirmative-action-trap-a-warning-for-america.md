# India's Affirmative Action Cautionary Tale Should Warn America Away From Race-Based Hiring

India's decades-long experiment with race-based "Reservations" offers a stark warning: government-mandated preference systems entrench inequality rather than solve it.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, an Indian-born commentator who grew up under the caste system argues that India's affirmative action approach—designed to break centuries of structural barriers—has instead calcified new divisions. The Reservations system, which allocates government jobs and school slots by caste, created its own class of permanent beneficiaries while failing to lift the poorest communities out of poverty.

The parallel to American race-based admissions and hiring is direct. Conservatives argue that preferences based on immutable characteristics—whether caste or race—don't remedy systemic disadvantage; they redistribute opportunity along new lines while breeding resentment among those excluded. Rather than lifting individuals based on merit and need, such systems reward group membership.

Colorado employers and universities should take note: as progressive activists push for expanded DEI mandates and race-conscious hiring, the real-world track record from India shows these policies calcify hierarchies instead of breaking them. Merit-based advancement and need-based support—not racial or ethnic preferences—remain the surest path to genuine opportunity.

Watch for whether Colorado's public universities and state contractors will resist federal pressure to implement India-style preference systems in hiring and admissions.
