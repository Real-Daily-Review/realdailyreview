# Cost-of-Living Crisis Hits Home for Colorado Families

Washington officials keep touting economic growth, but Colorado families know the truth: paychecks aren't keeping up with grocery bills and gas pumps.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, the disconnect between headline economic data and household reality is stark across the nation. Americans from all political stripes report the same squeeze at checkout counters—inflation has outpaced wage growth, making basic necessities feel like luxuries.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. Energy costs directly impact our state's working families and small businesses. While the Biden-Harris administration celebrated economic metrics, Colorado households faced higher heating bills last winter and elevated food costs year-round. Republicans argue that government overspending and regulatory barriers to domestic energy production have fueled inflation, while Democrats blame corporate greed and supply-chain disruptions.

The real issue: when inflation runs hotter than wage growth, working Coloradans lose purchasing power regardless of what the GDP numbers say. Families making $60,000 annually can't afford the same lifestyle their parents did at that income level.

Conservatives point to excessive government spending and anti-energy policies as culprits. The left counters that wage stagnation reflects corporate profit-taking and insufficient worker protections.

**Watch for:** whether Colorado's 2024 legislative session tackles cost-of-living relief through tax cuts or regulatory rollback—or doubles down on spending that fuels inflation further.
