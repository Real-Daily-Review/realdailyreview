# America's Energy Dominance Slipping as China Plays Long Game in Western Hemisphere

The U.S. is ceding strategic energy advantage to China and Russia in Latin America while Washington dithers over domestic production, according to conservative analysts tracking the geopolitical stakes.

Reports that Chevron and other American energy firms are preparing major investments in Venezuela underscore a troubling reality: *China understands the new energy war; America doesn't* (National Review). Beijing and Moscow have spent years cultivating energy relationships across the Western Hemisphere—our backyard—while U.S. policymakers remain fixated on restricting domestic oil, gas, and coal development.

The Washington Examiner warns this competition is "one of America's most consequential geopolitical competitions" unfolding closer to home than most realize. When American companies must look south to Venezuela for investment opportunities rather than developing resources here, it signals policy failure.

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** "Peace through strength" requires energy independence. Colorado's oil, gas, and coal industries aren't just economic engines—they're national security assets. Strangling domestic production through regulation while China locks up Latin American supply chains is strategic malpractice that weakens America's hand against Beijing.

Coloradans should watch whether state and federal policymakers will prioritize energy development as a geopolitical necessity, not an environmental inconvenience.
