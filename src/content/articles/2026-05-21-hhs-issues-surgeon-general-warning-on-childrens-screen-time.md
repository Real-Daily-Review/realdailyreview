# HHS Issues Warning on Children's Screen Time, Citing Mental Health Risks

The Department of Health and Human Services released a surgeon general's advisory Wednesday warning that excessive screen use among children and adolescents poses a public health threat, according to The Hill. The warning specifically cites impacts on sleep and mental health as key concerns, framing "harmful screen use" as a growing problem for young people surrounded by screens.

The advisory comes as global mental health data underscores broader concerns. According to Al Jazeera, nearly one in eight people worldwide lives with a mental disorder, with one suicide occurring every 43 seconds globally—highlighting the scale of mental health challenges that may intersect with screen-related issues.

The HHS warning does not specify recommended screen time limits or propose enforcement mechanisms based on the available excerpts. The advisory appears designed to raise awareness among parents, educators, and policymakers rather than establish binding regulations.

Watch for potential follow-up guidance from HHS on specific screen time recommendations and whether the warning prompts legislative or regulatory action at the federal or state level.
