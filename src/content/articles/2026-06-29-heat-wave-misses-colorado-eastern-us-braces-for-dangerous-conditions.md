# Heat Wave Headed East; Colorado Likely Spared Again

A dangerous heat wave is set to pummel the central and eastern United States this week ahead of July Fourth, with the National Weather Service warning that high humidity will make temperatures feel even more oppressive than the thermometer reads (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The brunt of the heat will spare most of Colorado, though Front Range communities could see elevated temperatures and humidity by midweek. Phoenix and other southwestern cities face the worst of it.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses, the timing matters: while the East Coast grinds through peak cooling costs and potential power grid strain, Colorado's relatively mild summer weather remains an economic advantage. Our lower energy bills and comfortable climate continue to draw residents and businesses fleeing overregulated, high-tax states dealing with infrastructure crises during extreme weather events.

The contrast is worth noting. States with aggressive green-energy mandates and aging grids—California and parts of the Northeast—face rolling blackout risks during heat waves. Colorado's balanced energy portfolio, including natural gas and hydropower alongside renewables, has kept the lights on and rates reasonable.

Watch whether Eastern states' grid failures during this heat event reignite debate over Colorado's own energy policy and whether policymakers will resist calls to abandon reliable baseload power in pursuit of ideological net-zero targets.
