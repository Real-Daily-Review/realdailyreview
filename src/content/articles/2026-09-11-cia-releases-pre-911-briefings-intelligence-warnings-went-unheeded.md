# CIA Declassifies Pre-9/11 Briefings, Raising Questions About Intelligence Failures

The CIA has released 71 presidential daily briefs detailing warnings about Osama bin Laden and al-Qaeda before the September 11 attacks—documents that raise uncomfortable questions about why actionable intelligence didn't prevent the deadliest terror strike on American soil.

According to newly declassified materials, U.S. intelligence warned the Clinton administration as early as 1998 that bin Laden's network could attempt to attack an American city using an "explosive-laden plane" (The Daily Wire). More pointedly, briefings in the weeks immediately preceding 9/11 contained specific warnings of al-Qaeda terrorist plots (Breitbart), yet the hijackings proceeded unchecked.

The release represents the CIA's largest-ever public disclosure of pre-9/11 intelligence (9News Denver), offering a rare window into what officials knew and when they knew it. The documents underscore a critical failure: the government possessed strategic warning but lacked either the operational capability, inter-agency coordination, or political will to act.

For Colorado and the nation, this declassification serves as a stark reminder that government agencies—no matter how well-funded or expansive—often fail at their core mission of protecting Americans. Conservatives have long argued that throwing more money and regulatory authority at federal bureaucracies doesn't guarantee competence or accountability.

**Watch whether Congress demands answers on why these warnings weren't operationalized, and whether any officials face consequences for the intelligence breakdown.**
