# FBI Files Expose How Trump Assassin Evaded Gun Purchase Detection

Federal investigators have uncovered that Thomas Crooks, who attempted to assassinate President Trump in Pennsylvania last year, used a false identity—the name of deceased Kansas Senator Bob Dole—to purchase firearm components online, according to newly released FBI documents cited by *The Daily Wire*. The scheme allowed Crooks to circumvent standard purchase tracking mechanisms that are supposed to flag suspicious acquisitions.

The revelations raise hard questions about e-commerce gun-parts sales oversight. While Second Amendment advocates argue that law-abiding Americans shouldn't face additional barriers to purchase legal components, this case demonstrates how existing regulations can be exploited by bad actors determined to cause harm. The FBI documents also reveal Crooks displayed emotional detachment from childhood, though he maintained high academic performance—a psychological profile that should have triggered closer scrutiny.

For Colorado, this underscores the tension between protecting constitutional rights and preventing criminals from gaming the system. State lawmakers have repeatedly pushed for stricter gun regulations, but this incident shows the real problem isn't legal gun owners—it's enforcement of existing law and identifying dangerous individuals before they act.
