# Nebraska Democrats' Bait-and-Switch Exposes Desperation in Deep Red

Democrats are resorting to electoral gamesmanship in Nebraska—a cautionary tale for Colorado voters watching their own state drift left.

The scheme is straightforward: Democrat Cindy Burbank won the May primary for U.S. Senate, then immediately dropped out, clearing the field for "independent" Dan Osborn to challenge Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts. According to Nebraska Secretary of State Bob Evnen, Burbank had announced this plan in advance (The Daily Wire). It's a naked attempt to hide the Democratic label in a deep-red state where voters reject progressive politics outright.

**Republicans argue** this tactic reveals how unpopular Democratic policy has become. Rather than defend their record on inflation, border security, or energy policy, Democrats are running a shell game—stripping the party label to sneak a left-wing candidate past voters who would otherwise reject him.

The broader pattern is telling. Even Democratic strategists like James Carville acknowledge the party faces a reckoning, suggesting Democrats might benefit from losing elections to reset (Washington Examiner). That's not confidence; that's panic.

**For Colorado**, this matters. As Democrats consolidate power in state government, they're importing the same playbook—regulatory overreach, energy restrictions, and tax hikes wrapped in "independent" or "bipartisan" language. Coloradans should watch whether state Democrats attempt similar label-stripping tactics in 2026 legislative races.
