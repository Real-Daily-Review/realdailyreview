# UT Austin Axes Woke Studies Programs as Enrollment Collapses—Colorado Universities Should Take Note

University of Texas at Austin is shuttering its women's studies, gender studies, and ethnic studies majors due to cratering enrollment and pressure from Texas state leadership—a rare victory for fiscal sanity in higher education that should alarm progressive administrators in Boulder and Denver.

The move signals what conservatives have long argued: ideologically driven academic programs disconnected from market demand and taxpayer value don't survive scrutiny. UT Austin, one of America's largest universities, determined these majors couldn't justify their cost and staffing when enrollment tanked (Fox News Politics reports).

Republicans and conservatives argue that universities bloated by low-enrollment identity-focused programs waste tuition dollars and state funding on activist credentialing rather than education. They contend such programs often prioritize ideological conformity over intellectual rigor—a critique that resonates across Colorado's higher-ed landscape, where CU Boulder and other state schools face mounting pressure to justify spending amid budget constraints.

Democrats and progressive educators counter that eliminating these majors erases important scholarship and marginalizes underrepresented voices, framing the cuts as political retaliation rather than fiscal necessity.

Colorado taxpayers and legislators should demand similar enrollment and cost-benefit audits of low-enrollment programs at CU, Colorado State, and other state-funded institutions—and be willing to make cuts when ideology trumps educational mission.
