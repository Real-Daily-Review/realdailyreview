# Trump Uses College Football Stage to Push Bipartisan Sports Bill—But Leaves CNN Behind

President Trump flew Air Force One over Neyland Stadium Saturday to attend the Texas-Tennessee matchup, using the high-profile venue to promote the Protect College Sports Act and claim rare bipartisan momentum (Washington Examiner). The legislation has secured 78 House votes, Trump said, and he expressed confidence Speaker Mike Johnson will deliver passage—framing it as "bipartisanship for the first time in a while."

The trip underscored Trump's comfort commanding national attention outside traditional media channels. CNN, along with MS NOW and Politico—outlets Trump previously banned from White House grounds—were excluded from Air Force One access (9News Denver), though journalists from those outlets have since regained White House complex credentials.

**The conservative angle:** Trump's push for the Protect College Sports Act reflects Republican concern over NCAA overreach and athlete compensation rules that conservatives argue have grown unwieldy. The bill aims to establish federal guardrails on name, image, and likeness (NIL) deals—a market-based alternative to the current regulatory patchwork.

Colorado conservatives should watch whether this legislation gains traction in the House and whether it includes provisions protecting state-level sports authority, particularly as Colorado universities navigate NIL compliance.
