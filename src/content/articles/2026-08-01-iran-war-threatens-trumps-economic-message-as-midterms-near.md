# Trump's Iran Conflict Threatens Economic Message as Midterms Loom

The escalating Iran conflict is consuming political oxygen that Trump needs for his economic agenda—and Colorado voters watching gas prices and job growth should take notice.

Five months into the fighting, Trump insists the U.S. will "win" and that Iran will "peter out," but admits the conflict could stretch through November's midterm elections (Fox News Politics). That timeline risks overshadowing the economic stewardship message that won Trump Colorado in 2020.

National Review argues Trump has "forfeited his signature issue of economic stewardship" by pursuing both tariffs and the Iran war simultaneously. The dual focus threatens to muddy the clear pro-growth, pro-business case that resonates with Colorado's energy sector, small-business owners, and working families.

Republicans counter that military strength and economic strength aren't mutually exclusive—that deterring Iran protects American interests and energy markets. But the political math is brutal: every month the conflict drags on is a month Trump isn't dominating headlines with jobs data, wage growth, or regulatory rollbacks.

For Colorado conservatives, the stakes are direct. A prolonged conflict could spike energy costs, distract from pro-development policies that benefit the state's oil and gas industry, and hand Democrats an opening to shift debate away from inflation and toward foreign entanglement.

Watch whether Trump can thread the needle: winning in Iran *and* reclaiming economic messaging before midterms.
