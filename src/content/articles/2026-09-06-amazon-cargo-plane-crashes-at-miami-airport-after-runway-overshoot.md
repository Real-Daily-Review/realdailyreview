# Amazon Cargo Plane Overshoots Miami Runway; No Colorado Connection But Safety Questions Loom

A Boeing 767-300 cargo aircraft operating for Amazon's Prime Air network overran the runway at Miami International Airport around 2 p.m. ET on Sunday, Sept. 6, crossing the airport perimeter and striking vehicles on an adjacent road, according to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). The plane, identified as 21 Air Flight 7598, had departed from San Juan, Puerto Rico, before the incident.

Video from the scene showed billowing smoke rising from the aircraft after the crash. The FAA is investigating the circumstances of the overshoot, though initial reports did not specify injuries or fatalities.

While this incident occurred in Florida, it raises broader questions about cargo aviation safety and regulatory oversight—issues that matter to Colorado's aviation industry and taxpayers who depend on reliable air transport for goods and services. The FAA's investigation will be critical in determining whether pilot error, mechanical failure, or other factors contributed to the runway excursion.

Conservatives should monitor whether the FAA's investigation reveals any regulatory gaps or whether the agency recommends new mandates that could burden Colorado's regional carriers and small airports with additional compliance costs.
