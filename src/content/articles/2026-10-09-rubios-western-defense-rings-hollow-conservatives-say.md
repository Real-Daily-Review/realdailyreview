# Rubio's Western Defense Stumbles on First Test

Secretary of State Marco Rubio's recent address on Western civilization and values—delivered against the backdrop of the Acropolis—aimed to rally confidence in American traditions against authoritarian rivals. But conservative commentators are already pointing out serious gaps in his argument.

National Review and the Washington Examiner, both right-leaning outlets, argue that Rubio's framing, while earnest, glosses over uncomfortable truths about Western history and fails to grapple with the hubris of past leaders. The critique centers on whether a defense of the West can credibly ignore its own failures—a weakness that undermines the case against China, Russia, and Middle Eastern theocracies.

For Colorado conservatives, the broader question matters: can American leadership project strength abroad while respecting constitutional limits at home? Rubio's State Department role will shape foreign policy affecting energy markets, trade, and national security—all with direct Colorado implications.

The real test comes next: whether the administration's Western-values messaging translates into coherent policy that doesn't simply replay the interventionist mistakes conservatives have long criticized.
