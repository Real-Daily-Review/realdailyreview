# Colorado Faces Historic Flooding as Three Counties Evacuate

Colorado's southwest corner is bracing for potentially catastrophic flooding as the San Juan River approaches historic levels overnight. According to KDVR Fox31 Denver, Archuleta, Rio Grande, and Conejos Counties have ordered evacuations in low-lying areas around the San Juan, Rio Blanco, Navajo, Conejos, and Piedra Rivers, with the San Juan expected to crest at 19.71 feet Sunday evening—a historic high.

Emergency management officials are mobilizing resources to protect lives and property as water levels surge across multiple river systems. The flooding underscores Colorado's vulnerability to severe weather events and the critical importance of infrastructure resilience in rural mountain and valley communities.

Conservatives and taxpayers should scrutinize how state and federal disaster response funds are deployed and whether Colorado's regulatory environment—including water management rules and land-use restrictions—hampers local preparedness and recovery efforts. The flooding also raises questions about whether Colorado's tax burden leaves counties and municipalities adequately funded for emergency infrastructure maintenance and disaster readiness.
