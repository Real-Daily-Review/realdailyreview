# Colorado's Energy Future Hangs in the Balance as U.S.-China Competition Heats Up

Colorado's oil and gas workers and energy companies face a critical moment as America's strategic competition with China extends into the Western Hemisphere's energy markets.

Recent reports show Chevron and other U.S. energy firms preparing major investments in Venezuela—a move that signals Washington's belated recognition of an energy war already underway. According to the *Washington Examiner*, China and Russia have long understood the strategic value of Latin American energy dominance, yet America has been slow to respond (Washington Examiner). *National Review* argues that true "peace through strength" requires more than military might; it demands energy security and economic leverage (National Review).

For Colorado, the stakes are direct. State oil and gas producers depend on stable global markets and U.S. energy independence. If China locks up Western Hemisphere energy supplies, Colorado's energy sector—and the jobs and tax revenue it generates—faces downstream pressure.

Republicans argue the Biden-Harris administration's regulatory assault on domestic energy development has weakened America's hand in this competition. By strangling U.S. oil and gas production through permitting delays and environmental restrictions, they contend, Washington handed Beijing and Moscow an advantage they didn't have to earn.

Conservatives should watch whether the incoming administration reverses course on energy permitting and whether Colorado's energy industry gets the regulatory relief needed to compete globally.
