# Colorado Rightly Rejects Special Party Privileges

Colorado is moving to strip government-granted special recognition and privileges from political parties—a rare moment of fiscal and constitutional common sense in a state increasingly hostile to limited government.

According to Complete Colorado, state officials are advancing a proposal to separate government from partisan favoritism, eliminating taxpayer-funded perks that benefit Democratic and Republican party apparatus. The move reflects a principle conservatives have long championed: government should serve all citizens equally, not subsidize political machines.

Democrats and progressive advocates typically defend such arrangements as necessary infrastructure for civic participation, though they rarely acknowledge the cost to taxpayers or the inherent unfairness of using state resources to advantage organized parties over independent voters and grassroots movements.

The proposal aligns with Colorado's libertarian streak—the state has a history of skepticism toward government overreach, even when it benefits the majority party. Eliminating special party privileges removes a hidden tax on Colorado citizens and levels the playing field for candidates and movements outside the two-party duopoly.

Conservatives should watch whether the legislature actually passes this measure or quietly buries it under pressure from party leadership in both chambers.
