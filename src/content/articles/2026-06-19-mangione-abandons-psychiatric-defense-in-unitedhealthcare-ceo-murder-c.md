# Luigi Mangione Abandons Psychiatric Defense in UnitedHealthcare CEO Murder Trial

Luigi Mangione has quietly withdrawn his planned psychiatric defense just one day after filing notice of it—a sudden reversal that signals his legal team may be abandoning a strategy that could have reduced murder charges to manslaughter.

Court filings Thursday show Mangione's attorneys formally withdrew their notice to pursue an "extreme emotional disturbance" defense in the killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson, according to The Daily Wire and the Washington Examiner. Had the defense succeeded, the second-degree murder charge would have dropped to first-degree manslaughter—a significantly lighter outcome.

The abrupt pullback raises questions about why Mangione's legal team filed the notice in the first place, only to reverse course within 24 hours. Court documents don't explain the reasoning behind the withdrawal, leaving observers to speculate whether the defense concluded the psychiatric angle was weak, faced unfavorable rulings from the bench, or determined a different strategy offered better prospects.

The case has drawn national attention, with some on the left attempting to lionize Mangione as a folk hero striking back at corporate America. Conservatives have pushed back firmly against that narrative, emphasizing that cold-blooded murder—regardless of the victim's profession—remains a serious felony deserving prosecution.
