# Colorado Voters Face Three Major Ballot Fights Over Taxes, Redistricting, and Rail Spending

Colorado taxpayers will decide this November whether to rewrite the state income tax code, impose new guardrails on congressional redistricting, and fund a costly Front Range passenger rail expansion—three contests that pit competing visions of government power and fiscal responsibility.

Initiative #256 would ban congressional redistricting designed to favor either political party, imposing neutral map-drawing standards (Complete Colorado). Republicans and good-government conservatives argue such guardrails prevent partisan gerrymandering and restore voter choice; Democrats counter that strict neutrality can obscure legitimate demographic representation.

Meanwhile, Front Range voters face a hefty passenger rail tax hike through the so-called CoCo plan. Conservatives point to the failure of Colorado's existing commuter rail systems, particularly RTD's chronic dysfunction, as evidence that throwing more tax dollars at rail transit is wasteful (Complete Colorado). Proponents claim expanded rail reduces congestion; skeptics ask why taxpayers should fund another government transportation experiment when current systems hemorrhage money.

On taxes, two competing income tax initiatives will ask voters to fundamentally restructure Colorado's tax code (KDVR). The specifics matter enormously for Colorado's business climate and household budgets—conservatives will scrutinize whether either proposal locks in lower rates or merely reshuffles the burden.

**Watch whether Colorado voters reject the tax-and-spend agenda or embrace it as a bellwether for 2026 midterms.**
