# Supreme Court Passes on 'Sensitive Places' Case—But Thomas Signals Second Amendment Fight Isn't Over

The Supreme Court declined to hear a challenge to Maryland's sweeping "sensitive places" carry restrictions, but Justice Clarence Thomas used the rejection to fire a warning shot at gun-control advocates nationwide.

In a statement, Thomas argued that bans on public carry in designated "sensitive places"—a category states have been aggressively expanding—lack historical grounding and would likely fail constitutional scrutiny if the Court took up the issue (Daily Caller, Breitbart). He suggested SCOTUS should eventually tackle the question directly to protect Second Amendment rights.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado gun owners should pay attention. Progressive legislatures across the country, including potentially in Denver, are using "sensitive places" language to carve out gun-free zones in parks, transit hubs, and public spaces. If Maryland's restrictions stand unchallenged, expect similar laws to proliferate.

**What conservatives argue:** Second Amendment advocates contend these bans amount to de facto public-carry prohibitions that contradict the Supreme Court's 2022 *Bruen* decision affirming an individual's right to carry firearms outside the home. They say states are exploiting vague "sensitive places" definitions to circumvent constitutional protections.

The Left counters that reasonable restrictions on carry in certain venues serve public safety—a framing conservatives reject as regulatory mission creep.

**Watch for:** Whether Thomas's concurrence signals the Court's appetite for a direct "sensitive places" showdown that could reshape gun-carry law nationwide.
