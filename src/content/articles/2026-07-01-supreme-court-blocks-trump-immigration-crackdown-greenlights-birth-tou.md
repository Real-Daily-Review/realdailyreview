# Supreme Court Blocks Trump Immigration Crackdown, Greenlights Birth Tourism

The Supreme Court has dealt a significant blow to immigration enforcement, rejecting a core Trump administration priority and effectively blessing the birth tourism industry that exploits America's citizenship rules.

In a landmark decision, the Court dismantled a federal law enacted after Watergate, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver, while simultaneously blocking efforts to crack down on so-called maternity hotels—apartment complexes and gated houses where foreign nationals, primarily Chinese, pay handlers $40,000 to $80,000 to enter the country, conceal pregnancies through customs, and deliver babies on American soil to secure citizenship.

The Washington Examiner reports that federal agents spent nearly a decade prosecuting these operations, with the Justice Department bringing indictments against the handlers running the scheme. That enforcement apparatus now faces legal constraints.

**The conservative argument:** Immigration hawks contend the ruling undermines border security and citizenship integrity. Conservatives say birth tourism exploits the 14th Amendment's citizenship clause and strains public resources—a particular concern for Colorado hospitals and schools already stretched thin by illegal immigration costs.

**Democrats counter** that the ruling protects due process and rejects overreach, though they've been largely silent on birth tourism specifically.

Watch for whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republicans—will push for legislative fixes to close the citizenship loophole the Court just widened.
