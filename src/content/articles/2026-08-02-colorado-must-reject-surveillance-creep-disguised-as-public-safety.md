# Colorado Should Reject the Surveillance Trap Disguised as Public Safety

Government agencies are quietly expanding surveillance infrastructure—automatic license plate readers, facial recognition, and data-mining systems—under the guise of fighting crime. Colorado taxpayers and gun owners should be alarmed.

The Washington Examiner warns that a dangerous consensus is taking hold: that every societal problem, from petty crime to traffic violations, can be solved by stripping away Americans' anonymity. Law enforcement's widespread adoption of automatic license plate readers exemplifies this creeping surveillance state. These systems track individuals' movements without meaningful oversight or warrant requirements.

**Conservatives argue** this represents exactly the kind of government overreach the Constitution was designed to prevent. More surveillance doesn't equal more freedom—it equals more control. Colorado's gun owners, in particular, should recognize the danger: today's license plate tracking becomes tomorrow's gun registry.

**The left claims** these tools are necessary for public safety and crime prevention. They downplay privacy concerns as paranoia.

Colorado has a history of resisting federal overreach on land use and energy policy. The same skepticism should apply here. Before your local police department installs another surveillance camera or partners with federal agencies on data-sharing, ask hard questions about warrants, oversight, and what happens to that data.

Watch whether Colorado legislators propose privacy protections or rubber-stamp law enforcement's surveillance wish list.
