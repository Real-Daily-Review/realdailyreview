# Judge Halts Kennedy Center Demolition Push, Demands 30-Day Notice Before Major Changes

Trump's leverage over a $257 million federal repair package has hit a legal speed bump. A judge has ordered the Kennedy Center to provide 30 days' notice before making any major physical changes—a ruling that freezes the administration's ability to fast-track repairs tied to renaming conditions.

The center suffered a partial ceiling collapse earlier this year. Trump has suggested the entire building could face demolition without his administration's cooperation on the repair funding (9News Denver). The dispute centers on the board's composition: Trump replaced the old directors and overhauled operations after returning to office, while plaintiffs in the suit urged the court to appoint a caretaker (Just The News).

The 30-day notice requirement effectively gives opponents time to mount legal challenges before any major work proceeds. It's a reminder that even executive leverage over federal spending faces judicial constraints—and that cultural institutions remain contested terrain between the Trump administration and its critics.

**What conservatives should watch:** Whether this ruling delays repairs indefinitely or whether the administration finds a path around the injunction to unlock the $257 million and move forward with its governance changes.
