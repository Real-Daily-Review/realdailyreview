# Tennessee Death Row Inmate Walking After Botched Execution; Newsom Eyes Mass Commutation

Tennessee's failed execution of murderer Christa Pike is now fueling a push by California Gov. Gavin Newsom to spare all 563 inmates on that state's death row—a move that would gut capital punishment in the nation's most populous state without legislative approval.

Pike survived two lethal injection attempts on September 30, and her attorney reported to *People* that she has since "taken a few steps" and is walking around her hospital room (9News Denver, Breitbart). Meanwhile, Pike's own family expressed being "excited" about the botched execution, according to her aunt (New York Post).

Conservatives argue Newsom's response exemplifies how a single procedural failure is being weaponized to abolish capital punishment entirely—circumventing voters and lawmakers who have repeatedly endorsed the death penalty. Republicans counter that execution protocols can and should be fixed, not abandoned wholesale. The real issue, they say, is that progressive prosecutors and governors use isolated mishaps as cover for ideological opposition to justice for heinous crimes.

For Colorado, this signals how West Coast progressivism—including soft-on-crime policies—continues spreading eastward. Watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature attempts similar death-penalty restrictions or commutation schemes in the 2027 session.
