# Colorado Colleges Chase AI Prestige While Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Colorado's higher education establishment is racing to rebrand artificial intelligence as "super intelligence"—a semantic shell game that obscures the real question: who's paying for these experiments?

Six Colorado colleges and universities have earned the state's new "AI Engaged Campus Designation," according to CPR News, positioning themselves as innovation leaders in the AI space. The honor recognizes "intentional use" of artificial intelligence across campus operations and curricula.

But conservatives should ask hard questions about what this costs. Colorado taxpayers already subsidize higher education through state appropriations. Now universities are dedicating resources—staff, infrastructure, research funding—to AI initiatives that may or may not produce measurable returns for students or the state economy.

The Washington Examiner notes the broader political absurdity: Republicans and Democrats are both scrambling to rebrand AI messaging ahead of elections, with some pushing the term "super intelligence" instead. It's political theater while universities spend real money chasing prestige.

**The Colorado angle:** Are state-funded universities deploying AI to cut costs and improve student outcomes, or simply to burnish their reputations? Taxpayers deserve transparency on spending and ROI.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand accountability reports on AI spending at state universities—or quietly let administrators spend without oversight.
