# House Republicans Demand ActBlue Lawyer Answer for Foreign Donor Screening Contradictions

House Republicans are demanding testimony from ActBlue's attorney over what they say are glaring contradictions in how the Democratic fundraising giant screens foreign donors—a practice that could expose campaigns to illegal foreign cash.

Chairmen Bryan Steil (R-WI), Jim Jordan (R-OH), and James Comer (R-KY) have summoned WilmerHale partner Ronald Machen for a closed-door interview, citing inconsistencies between his prior statements on donor verification and internal ActBlue documents (Washington Examiner). Republicans argue the platform's vetting procedures are inadequate and that Machen's legal review defending those practices conflicts with what the company's own records show.

ActBlue processes hundreds of millions in donations for Democratic candidates and causes nationwide—including Colorado races. If the platform failed to properly screen foreign donors, it could mean illegal foreign contributions flowed into U.S. elections, violating federal law.

Democrats have not publicly responded to the specific allegations. The left generally frames ActBlue as a legitimate fundraising tool, but Republicans contend lax foreign donor screening represents a serious compliance failure that demands accountability.

Conservatives should watch whether Machen's testimony reveals systematic gaps in ActBlue's vetting—and whether Colorado Democratic campaigns that used the platform face scrutiny over their donor sources.
