# Dangerous Heat Dome Set to Bake Colorado Through Week

Colorado faces a dangerous heat wave starting Saturday that could push Denver-area temperatures near 100 degrees and linger for five days, according to forecasters (9News Denver).

The oppressive system is part of a broader weather pattern sweeping the U.S., with triple-digit highs expected across the Southwest and Great Plains before a high-pressure dome traps heat eastward (Washington Examiner). Meteorologists are urging residents to stay hydrated and seek air-conditioned shelter.

For Colorado, the timing matters. Peak summer demand strains the state's power grid—a reality that underscores why reliable, affordable energy infrastructure matters. Colorado's mix of natural gas, coal, and renewables will face real-world stress this week. Blackouts or rolling outages would hit working families and small businesses hardest, yet state regulators continue pushing aggressive coal retirements and grid mandates that critics argue prioritize ideology over reliability.

Coloradans should prepare now: stock water, check on elderly neighbors, and ensure air conditioning is functional. Those without reliable cooling should identify community cooling centers.

Watch whether Colorado's grid holds steady under the load—and whether state energy officials acknowledge the real-world consequences of their transition policies.
