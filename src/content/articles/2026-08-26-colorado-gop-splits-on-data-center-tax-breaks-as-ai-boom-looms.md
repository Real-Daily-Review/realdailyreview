# Data Center Boom Splits Conservatives Over Tax Incentives and Development

Colorado faces a growing divide among Republicans over whether to sweeten the pot for artificial intelligence and data center development—a tension that could reshape the state's economic strategy.

The debate pits growth-minded conservatives against fiscal hawks wary of corporate welfare. National Review argues that rejecting data center development historically backfires, noting "there are no obvious historical examples of Americans rejecting a new development and benefiting from that rejection." The Daily Wire frames the opposition as culture-war theater, suggesting anti-tech sentiment reflects broader progressive anxieties about Big Tech's influence.

But some Republicans push back hard on giveaways. Rep. Rich McCormick (R-GA) stated plainly: data centers are beneficial, but "if you're negotiating any new data centers, I don't think you should give them any tax breaks" (Breitbart).

**The Colorado angle:** The state has courted data center investment as a hedge against energy-dependent economies. Tax incentives are often the bait. Conservatives must decide: do we compete for high-wage jobs and tax base growth, or do we refuse to subsidize corporations that often lean left politically?

The tension mirrors Colorado's broader identity crisis—balancing free-market principles against skepticism of crony capitalism and Big Tech's cultural influence.
