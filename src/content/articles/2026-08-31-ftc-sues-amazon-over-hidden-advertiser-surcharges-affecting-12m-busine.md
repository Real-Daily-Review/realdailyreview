# FTC and 22 States Sue Amazon Over Alleged Hidden Advertiser Surcharges

The Federal Trade Commission and a bipartisan coalition of 22 states filed suit against Amazon Monday, accusing the tech giant of systematically overcharging roughly 1.2 million advertisers through undisclosed fees over seven years—a rare moment of regulatory consensus that underscores growing frustration with Big Tech's market dominance.

According to the Washington Examiner, the complaint alleges Amazon imposed "hidden surcharges" that inflated advertiser costs without transparent disclosure. The 9News Denver report notes more than 500,000 small and medium-sized businesses were affected, raising questions about whether Colorado-based entrepreneurs and retailers using Amazon's advertising platform faced similar deceptive pricing.

The lawsuit reflects bipartisan skepticism of Amazon's market power—a concern conservatives share, though from a different angle. While progressives typically demand aggressive antitrust enforcement and regulation, conservatives argue the real problem is that Amazon's dominance stems partly from government favoritism and regulatory capture. The company's ability to leverage its platform to disadvantage competitors and overcharge small businesses is precisely the kind of corporate cronyism that results when government picks winners and losers.

Colorado small business owners and advertisers should demand transparency about whether they were among those overcharged and consider whether Amazon's market stranglehold justifies serious antitrust scrutiny—or whether the solution is less regulation, not more.
