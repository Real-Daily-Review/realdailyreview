# Taxpayer Cash Fuels Slick Pro-Rail Campaign as Colorado Voters Face Billion-Dollar Choice

Colorado officials are burning millions in public money to sell voters on the Front Range rail project—a textbook case of government using your tax dollars to lobby you into approving more spending.

According to CPR News, the campaign includes paid Instagram influencers and targeted mailers pushing positive messaging about the Colorado Connector. The strategy is straightforward: soften public skepticism with glossy ads before the vote.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue the project itself is the real problem. Complete Colorado laid out the core objection: Colorado voters face a choice between "blowing billions on a train few will ride and keeping that money in their own pockets." Skeptics question ridership projections, long-term operating costs, and whether rail serves Front Range commuters better than existing transit or road improvements.

Supporters counter that the rail project addresses regional congestion and positions Colorado for growth—but they're making that case partly with public funds, which raises an obvious fairness question: Should government use taxpayer money to campaign for tax increases on those same taxpayers?

The Colorado angle cuts deep. This isn't abstract transit policy; it's whether Denver-area workers and businesses will shoulder billions in new obligations.

Watch whether voters reject the self-dealing campaign and demand independent cost-benefit analysis before any rail vote moves forward.
