# Michigan Dems Nominate Radical Left Activist; Republicans Smell Opportunity

Michigan Democrats have handed Republicans a gift by nominating Abdul el-Sayed, a DSA-aligned activist with a radical agenda, over moderate Rep. Haley Stevens in their Senate primary. El-Sayed, who has called for abolishing multiple institutions and claimed "capitalism is the thief of sex," represents the party's lurch toward its socialist fringe—exactly the kind of extremism that energizes conservative voters nationwide, including in Colorado.

President Trump wasted no time attacking el-Sayed's record, while Republicans like Michigan nominee Mike Rogers are already celebrating. The Washington Examiner notes that el-Sayed's victory signals Democrats have fallen to the "Mamdani Left," abandoning pragmatism for ideology (Washington Examiner). National Review warns el-Sayed "does not disclaim the DSA's radical politics" and maintains a lengthy list of institutions he wants dismantled.

Even some Democrats appear nervous. Rep. Pramila Jayapal's claim that Republicans will vote for el-Sayed reads less like confidence and more like damage control.

**Colorado angle:** This race illustrates how progressive activists are capturing Democratic nominations nationwide. Colorado Republicans should watch whether similar candidates emerge in statewide races here—and whether Colorado voters reject the same radical-left overreach Michigan Democrats just embraced.
