# Trump's Venezuela Oil Play Risks U.S. Credibility and Colorado Energy Markets

President Trump's reported push to seize Venezuelan oil reserves signals a dangerous departure from the rule-of-law principles that have anchored American prosperity—and could roil energy markets Colorado depends on.

The move has alarmed even conservative allies. National Review warns that abandoning stable international order in favor of resource confiscation undermines the legal framework that made America prosperous. The Washington Examiner reports that foreign governments, including the Dutch, are already responding by moving gold reserves out of U.S. custody—a vote of no-confidence in American institutional reliability.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. Energy markets thrive on predictability and respect for property rights. If the U.S. government signals it will seize assets when convenient, investors—foreign and domestic—will recalculate their exposure to American markets and Colorado's oil and gas sector. Uncertainty kills capital investment.

Republicans and conservatives argue that projecting strength doesn't require abandoning the legal and institutional guardrails that distinguish America from authoritarian regimes. Confiscation, even of hostile nations' assets, sets a precedent that corrodes the sanctity of contracts and property rights globally.

**Watch whether Colorado energy companies and industry groups publicly distance themselves from the policy—a sign the business community sees real downside risk.**
