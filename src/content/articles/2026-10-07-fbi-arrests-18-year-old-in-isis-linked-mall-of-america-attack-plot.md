# FBI Stops Alleged ISIS-Inspired Mall Attack Plot in Minnesota

Federal agents have arrested an 18-year-old Somali man accused of plotting a mass-casualty attack at Minnesota's Mall of America, underscoring persistent threats from foreign terrorist recruitment on U.S. soil.

According to *The Daily Wire*, Sheikhdoon Abdullahi Mohamud was taken into custody after allegedly attempting to pay an undercover FBI agent $600 to acquire an AK-47 rifle. *Just The News* reports that Mohamud had engaged in conversations with a confidential human source in which he allegedly expressed intent either to travel to Somalia to join Al-Shabaab or to carry out a mass shooting.

The arrest highlights the FBI's ongoing counterterrorism operations and underscores why Second Amendment advocates argue that law-abiding Americans' right to bear arms for self-defense remains constitutionally protected—even as bad actors seek weapons for violent purposes. The distinction between lawful gun ownership and criminal acquisition is critical in the national debate over firearms policy.

While this plot was disrupted before execution, Colorado residents should remain vigilant. Similar radicalization networks operate nationwide, and state and local law enforcement coordination with federal agencies remains essential for public safety.

Conservatives and taxpayers should monitor whether this arrest prompts renewed calls for gun restrictions that would burden lawful owners while doing nothing to stop determined criminals or terrorists from acquiring weapons illegally.
