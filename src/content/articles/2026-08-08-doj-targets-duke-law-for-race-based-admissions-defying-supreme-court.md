# Duke Law Caught Flouting Supreme Court on Race-Based Admissions

Colorado's universities should take note: the Justice Department is cracking down on schools that defy the Supreme Court's landmark ruling against racial discrimination in college admissions.

Duke Law School allegedly gave Black applicants 3.5 times higher admission chances than Asian applicants with identical credentials, according to the DOJ's Civil Rights Division (Fox News). The discrimination reportedly continued through the 2023, 2024, and 2025 admissions cycles—well after the Supreme Court's 2023 decision explicitly prohibiting race-conscious college admissions (Just The News).

**The conservative argument:** The ruling was clear. Schools that ignore it face legal consequences. Duke's conduct exemplifies how progressive institutions have resisted the Court's directive, substituting one form of discrimination for another. Republicans argue this undermines merit-based admissions and equal protection under law.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** The University of Colorado, Colorado State University, and other state schools operate under the same legal constraints. If they're using similar race-based preferences in admissions or hiring, they're vulnerable to DOJ enforcement and costly litigation—ultimately paid by Colorado taxpayers.

The DOJ action signals the administration intends to enforce the Supreme Court's decision. Colorado institutions should audit their admissions policies now to avoid federal action.
