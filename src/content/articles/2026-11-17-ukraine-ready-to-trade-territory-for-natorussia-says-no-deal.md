# Ukraine Signals NATO Deal Possible—But Russia Won't Bite

President Zelensky has signaled Ukraine would accept NATO membership in exchange for ceding Donbas territory, but Moscow has already rejected the trade-off, according to reporting from the *Washington Examiner*. During U.S.-Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi last week, American negotiators posed a hypothetical: would Kyiv abandon its eastern claims for NATO protection? Zelensky indicated openness—but Russia remains the immovable obstacle.

Meanwhile, Ukraine continues striking Russian infrastructure. The *New York Post* reports Ukraine hit the strategically vital bridge linking Russia to Crimea for the second consecutive day, marking what Russian-backed officials claim was the war's largest drone attack.

The dynamic reveals a fundamental problem: any settlement requires Moscow's consent, and the Kremlin shows no willingness to accept Ukraine's NATO membership as compensation for territorial loss. Russia views NATO expansion as an existential threat—the very thing it claims justified the invasion.

For American policymakers, this underscores the limits of negotiation when one party refuses to negotiate seriously. Continued U.S. support for Ukraine's defense remains the only leverage Washington possesses.
