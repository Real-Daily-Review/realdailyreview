# Federal Judge Blocks Trump's Birthright Citizenship Order—Again

A Washington, D.C. federal judge has struck down President Trump's second attempt to restrict birthright citizenship through executive order, handing another legal defeat to an administration trying to enforce immigration limits conservatives say are overdue.

Judge Christopher Cooper, whom Trump previously criticized, ruled that the order violated the Fourteenth Amendment by attempting to exclude children born to certain categories of foreign nationals—including those born via surrogacy—from automatic citizenship (Fox News, Washington Examiner). The decision mirrors an earlier court block of Trump's first birthright citizenship executive order.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans and immigration hawks contend that birthright citizenship has been exploited as a loophole, with foreign nationals deliberately entering the U.S. to secure citizenship for their children. They argue the 14th Amendment's language is ambiguous enough to permit narrow exceptions—particularly for children of diplomats and those born through commercial surrogacy arrangements. Trump's order targeted precisely these edge cases.

**The judicial obstacle:** Federal courts have consistently sided with the broad reading of birthright citizenship, blocking both Trump attempts on constitutional grounds. The repeated defeats suggest any legislative fix would require congressional action—a steep climb in a divided Congress.

Colorado taxpayers and business owners should watch whether Republicans attempt a constitutional amendment or legislative workaround, or whether this issue fades as courts continue to block executive action.
