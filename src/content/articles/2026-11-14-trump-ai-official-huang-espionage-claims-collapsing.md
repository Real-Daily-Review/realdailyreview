# Trump AI Official Dismisses Huang 'Spy' Claims as Espionage Narrative Unravels

Former Trump AI czar David Sacks says allegations that Nvidia founder Jensen Huang engaged in espionage with a Chinese businesswoman don't withstand scrutiny, according to Breitbart. Sacks, now co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, called the "spy narrative" against Huang "collapsing already."

The controversy centers on Huang's alleged connections to a woman identified as Alice Huang and questions over Nvidia's sale of advanced AI chips to China—a practice National Review has questioned as a potential national security vulnerability. National Review's coverage raised the core conservative concern: why is the U.S. allowing a major chipmaker to export cutting-edge AI technology to a strategic competitor?

Sacks's pushback suggests the Trump administration is moving past initial espionage theories, though the underlying policy question—whether Nvidia's China sales serve American interests—remains unresolved. The episode reflects broader tension between Silicon Valley's globalist trade instincts and Trump's America-First technology policy.

For Colorado, this matters: the state hosts significant tech infrastructure and defense contractors who depend on clear, consistent export controls and supply-chain security. Muddled signals on AI chip sales undermine predictability for Colorado's aerospace and defense sectors.
