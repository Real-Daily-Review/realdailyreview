# Pope Votes in US Election—But Don't Expect Him to Weigh In on Colorado Issues

Pope Leo XIV has made history as the first pontiff ever to cast a ballot in a U.S. general election, exercising his rights as an American citizen despite leading Vatican City. The pope, formerly Robert Prevost, requested a mail ballot to vote in Illinois' midterm elections, according to reporting from Fox News and the New York Post. Legal experts confirmed the move was lawful: as a U.S. citizen living abroad, he retained voting rights regardless of his Vatican residency (New York Post).

The development raises an eyebrow among conservatives who've grown weary of institutional leaders—religious and otherwise—injecting themselves into electoral politics. While the Vatican confirmed the vote occurred, there's no indication the pontiff has weighed in on Colorado-specific issues like energy policy, water rights, or Second Amendment protections that matter to state voters and taxpayers.

The broader question for Colorado conservatives: if religious institutions and their leaders are now openly participating in U.S. elections, will they remain neutral on the policy battles that directly affect Colorado families and businesses, or will we see increased pressure from church hierarchies on contentious state issues?
