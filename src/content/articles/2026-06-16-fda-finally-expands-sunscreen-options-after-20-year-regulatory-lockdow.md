# FDA Finally Loosens Sunscreen Rules—But Only After Two Decades of Needless Consumer Harm

The FDA has announced its first expansion of approved sunscreen ingredients in over 20 years, a move that should increase competition and lower prices for consumers (Washington Examiner). While the decision itself is welcome, it underscores a larger conservative complaint: federal agencies routinely delay consumer choice and market competition through excessive regulation.

For Colorado families heading to the mountains and high-altitude recreation areas—where sun exposure is intense—this means more affordable UV protection options hitting shelves this summer. The expansion allows manufacturers to offer formulations previously locked out by bureaucratic gatekeeping, driving down costs through competition rather than government mandate.

Republicans and conservatives argue the FDA's two-decade delay cost American consumers billions in unnecessary spending on limited options. This is regulatory capture in action: the agency protected incumbent manufacturers from competition under the guise of "safety," while consumers footed the bill. The lesson is clear—when government agencies control market entry, innovation stalls and prices rise.

Democrats haven't seriously challenged the FDA's historical sunscreen restrictions, treating them as settled science rather than questioning whether the agency's caution served consumers or entrenched interests.

**Watch whether the FDA applies this same deregulatory logic to other consumer products, or whether this sunscreen decision remains an isolated exception to its usual expansionist posture.**
