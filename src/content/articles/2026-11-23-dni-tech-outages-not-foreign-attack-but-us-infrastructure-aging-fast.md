# Tech Outages Weren't Foreign Attack, But America's Crumbling Infrastructure Needs Serious Fixes

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton confirmed this week that recent technology outages were neither coordinated nor state-sponsored—ruling out the worst-case scenario of a hostile foreign cyberattack. But the real story is more sobering: the outages exposed how dangerously outdated America's digital infrastructure has become.

Clayton told CNN's "State of the Union" that the incidents stemmed from upgrades to aging systems, describing the situation as "a period of technological step change" (Breitbart). While Daily Caller reports Clayton assured Americans the disruptions were not orchestrated by foreign adversaries, conservatives should focus on the underlying problem: decades of underinvestment and regulatory red tape have left critical infrastructure vulnerable and fragile.

For Colorado, this matters. Outages disrupt energy grids, water systems, and agricultural operations that depend on reliable connectivity. Tech-dependent sectors across the state—from Denver's growing startup ecosystem to rural broadband needs—require modern, resilient infrastructure.

The question now is whether Washington will respond with smart deregulation and private-sector innovation, or double down on bloated government "infrastructure" spending that enriches contractors without fixing the core problem. Watch whether Congress pushes for streamlined permitting and competitive upgrades, or tries to nationalize digital infrastructure under federal control.
