# Trump's Iran Deal Splits Right, Leaves Colorado Hawks Skeptical

President Trump has secured a framework agreement with Iran, drawing mixed reactions from conservatives who worry about Tehran's true intentions even as some praise the diplomatic opening.

A Reagan Institute Summer Survey shows Americans divided: 39% favor negotiated settlement while 36% back regime change—reflecting deeper conservative uncertainty about whether talking to Iran's Supreme Leader yields results or merely delays the inevitable confrontation.

The Daily Wire notes Trump is acting as a "pragmatist," acknowledging the negotiations are far from concluded. Supporters argue the deal reduces war risk and opens communication channels. Yet Washington Examiner opinion writers caution that even if the agreement achieves its stated goals, the "unthinkable next step" in Middle East dynamics remains unclear—a concern shared by Colorado defense hawks who've long opposed appeasement of hostile regimes.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei backed the memorandum despite reservations, according to Breitbart, after receiving assurances the deal protects Tehran's interests. That alone raises red flags: if Iran's leadership needed reassurance the agreement favors them, what exactly did America concede?

**Colorado conservatives should watch whether this framework includes enforceable inspections and snapback sanctions—or becomes another toothless Obama-era deal that empowers our adversaries.**
