# Healthcare Workers Ditching Coverage as Affordability Spirals—While Left Eyes HSA Restrictions

Healthcare workers themselves are abandoning health insurance, a stark sign that government-mandated coverage mandates and regulatory bloat have priced ordinary Americans out of the market.

According to the Daily Caller, hospitals are becoming overwhelmed as medical professionals—the very people who understand healthcare costs intimately—drop coverage due to unaffordable premiums. The irony is brutal: the industry most regulated by Washington is collapsing under its own weight.

Meanwhile, progressive policymakers are doubling down on the wrong fix. A Georgetown University study, highlighted by the Washington Examiner, proposes eliminating Health Savings Accounts—one of the few market-driven tools that actually *lower* costs by giving patients skin in the game. Democrats and left-leaning health bureaucrats want to kill HSAs in pursuit of their centralized coverage model, even as evidence mounts that patients flee when premiums spike.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado workers and small businesses already face some of the nation's highest insurance costs. Eliminating HSAs would strip away one of the few tax-advantaged mechanisms that let Coloradans save for healthcare without government intermediaries. Hospitals across the state would face the same staffing crisis.

Conservatives argue the solution is *less* regulation and *more* market transparency—not fewer consumer tools. Watch whether Colorado's Democratic legislature moves to restrict HSAs in the 2027 session.
