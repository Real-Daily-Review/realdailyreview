# Federal Reserve Signals Rate Hikes Ahead Under New Chair Warsh

The Federal Reserve's new chair just signaled the central bank isn't done fighting inflation—and Colorado savers and borrowers should brace for higher rates ahead.

In his first policy meeting, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh dropped dovish language suggesting rate cuts were coming, instead holding the benchmark rate steady while signaling openness to future increases (9News Denver). The move reflects persistent inflation pressures: wholesale business inflation exceeded 6% in May, with consumer inflation still above 4%, according to Just The News.

The shift matters for Colorado. Higher rates mean costlier mortgages for homebuyers already priced out of Denver's heated market, steeper borrowing costs for small businesses and ranchers, and reduced returns on savings accounts. But conservatives argue the Fed's earlier rate cuts—which fueled the inflation spike in the first place—created this mess. Republicans contend the central bank should have tightened sooner instead of keeping rates artificially low for years.

Democrats typically blame external shocks like the Iran conflict for inflation, resisting criticism of the Fed's loose monetary policy. Conservatives counter that the Fed's own money-printing was the primary culprit, and that Warsh's hawkish turn, while necessary, comes too late.

**Watch whether Warsh actually follows through with rate hikes or retreats under political pressure from the Biden administration.**
