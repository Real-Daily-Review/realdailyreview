# Columbus City Hall Raises Somalia Flag During America 250—And Conservatives Aren't Having It

Columbus, Ohio's city government chose a curious way to mark America's 250th birthday: by hoisting the Somali flag at city hall during Somalia Independence Day celebrations, igniting swift backlash from conservatives nationwide.

The move drew immediate criticism from right-leaning outlets and commentators. "No American government building should ever be raising another country's flag," journalist Mark Hemingway posted on X, capturing the sentiment animating conservative objections (New York Post). Fox News and The Daily Wire both highlighted the timing—that Columbus opted to celebrate a foreign nation's independence as Americans prepared for Fourth of July weekend.

The controversy underscores a broader conservative concern: government institutions prioritizing multicultural symbolism over American patriotism, especially during a milestone national anniversary. Critics argue that while celebrating immigrant communities has merit, flying foreign flags at official buildings sends the wrong message about where civic loyalty should lie.

**Colorado angle:** While this occurred in Ohio, similar debates have erupted in Colorado cities over flag displays and municipal recognition of various cultural observances. Coloradans should watch whether Denver or other Front Range municipalities face pressure to adopt similar practices—and whether taxpayers will demand clarity on what symbols represent at government buildings.
