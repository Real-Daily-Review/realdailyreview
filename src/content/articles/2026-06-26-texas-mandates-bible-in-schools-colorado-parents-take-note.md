# Texas Mandates Bible in Schools—A Model for Local Control That Colorado Should Watch

Texas just became the first state in recent years to require Bible stories in K-12 public school curricula, with the Republican-controlled State Board of Education voting Friday to add biblical passages to mandatory reading lists (Washington Examiner).

Critics claim the move violates the First Amendment, but state law and legal precedent support Texas's authority to set its own curriculum standards. Conservatives argue the decision reflects parental rights and local control—principles that should resonate in Colorado, where school boards have increasingly faced pushback over curriculum transparency and parental input.

The Texas move signals a broader conservative push to reclaim education from progressive activists. Republicans contend that Bible literacy is foundational to understanding Western literature, history, and law—not a religious imposition but cultural literacy. The left counters that public schools should remain secular spaces.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado parents frustrated with woke curricula and lack of transparency should note how Texas used its state board authority. Colorado's education establishment leans progressive, but school board elections remain winnable for reform-minded candidates who champion parental rights and academic rigor over ideology.

Watch whether Colorado conservatives push similar curriculum transparency measures and whether the state's education board responds to parent demands for classical literacy standards.
