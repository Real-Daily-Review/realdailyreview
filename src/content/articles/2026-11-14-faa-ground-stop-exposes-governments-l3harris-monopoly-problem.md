# FAA Ground Stop Exposes Government's Dependence on Single Contractor

A nationwide FAA ground stop Friday night revealed what conservatives have long warned: government monopolies on critical infrastructure breed failure. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed telecommunications provider L3Harris for the outage, caused when "routers got overloaded with data," creating inconsistencies that crippled the nation's air traffic control system (Breitbart).

Duffy's frustration cut to the heart of the problem: he said he is "stuck" with L3Harris until Congress approves funding for competing companies to upgrade the system (Washington Examiner). That's government contracting at its worst—a single vendor with no real competition, no accountability, and no incentive to invest in redundancy or modernization.

For Colorado, this matters. Denver International Airport, a major hub, faced disruptions affecting thousands of passengers and businesses dependent on reliable air travel. The outage also underscores how federal regulatory bloat and procurement delays leave critical systems vulnerable.

Republicans argue the FAA's reliance on outdated infrastructure and a single contractor reflects broader government inefficiency. The solution isn't more federal control—it's competition, private-sector innovation, and cutting the red tape that prevents alternatives from stepping in.

Watch whether Congress actually funds those competing systems or whether L3Harris remains the FAA's captive vendor.
