# Iran's Coffin Billboard Shows Regime Desperation as Trump Stands Firm

Iran's theocratic leadership is resorting to crude propaganda because it fears Trump's willingness to challenge its regional ambitions. This week, the Iranian regime unveiled a massive billboard in Tehran depicting President Trump dead in a coffin—a theatrical death wish that reveals Tehran's panic more than its power.

The provocation comes as tensions simmer over the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy markets. The U.S. has reasserted control of the waterway, while Iran has reportedly instructed its Houthi proxies to intervene if American strikes target Iranian infrastructure. According to the Washington Examiner, Trump has effectively "called Iran's bluff," forcing Tehran into a defensive posture.

For Colorado, this matters. Energy markets remain sensitive to Middle East disruptions. Any escalation affecting oil supplies or shipping costs ripples through Colorado's economy and gas pumps. The state's oil and gas industry also watches closely—Trump's assertive posture toward Iran contrasts sharply with the Biden-Harris administration's appeasement-leaning approach that conservatives argue emboldened the regime.

Iran's coffin billboard is less a credible threat than an admission: the regime lacks conventional leverage against a president willing to project American strength. Tehran's resort to propaganda and proxy warfare signals weakness, not confidence.

Watch whether Iran follows through on threats to its Houthi allies or whether the regime blinks under sustained American pressure.
