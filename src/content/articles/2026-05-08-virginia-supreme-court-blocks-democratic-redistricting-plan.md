# Virginia Supreme Court Strikes Down Democratic Redistricting Plan

Virginia's Supreme Court has invalidated a congressional redistricting map approved by voters last month, dealing a significant setback to Democrats ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.

Voters narrowly approved the redistricting amendment on April 21, which would have given Democrats a more favorable map and potentially enabled the party to pick up as many as four U.S. House seats, according to CNBC. Democrats framed the effort as a counter to Republican-led redistricting gains in other states.

The court's ruling renders the voter-approved results meaningless, according to PBS NewsHour. The Washington Examiner reported that the court held Virginia's lengthy voting process had begun before the General Assembly approved the new map, meaning the constitutionally required intervening election between amendment proposals had not occurred.

The decision represents a major win for Republicans in the ongoing redistricting battles between the parties. Al Jazeera characterized it as "handing Republicans a key win before crucial midterm elections."

Democrats had viewed Virginia as a prime opportunity to offset Republican gerrymandering in GOP-controlled states. The court's decision effectively preserves the current congressional map.

Watch for potential Democratic legal challenges or legislative responses to the ruling in the coming weeks.
