# Trump's Religious Liberty Commission Survives Legal Challenge—For Now

A federal judge has rejected a lawsuit challenging President Trump's Religious Liberty Commission, ruling the advisory panel doesn't violate federal balance requirements (Washington Examiner). The commission, established to review threats to religious freedom, faced claims it excluded certain faith perspectives, but U.S. District Judge John Cronan found no violation of the Federal Advisory Committee Act.

The decision matters beyond Washington. Colorado's faith communities—from Catholic institutions to evangelical congregations—have watched religious liberty protections erode under progressive state policies. National Review highlighted how Democratic-led jurisdictions like New York are using assisted-suicide laws to override conscience protections for religious organizations, forcing nuns and other believers to choose between their faith and compliance.

Republicans argue the commission fills a critical gap: documenting how government overreach threatens First Amendment freedoms. Democrats counter that such panels risk becoming vehicles for religious exemptions that undermine civil rights protections.

The ruling clears the commission to deliver its final report this year, potentially shaping federal policy on religious liberty disputes that could ripple into Colorado's own regulatory battles over healthcare mandates, education, and conscience protections.
