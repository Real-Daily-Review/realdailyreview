# Intelligence Community Allegedly Buried China Election Threat in 2020

Declassified records reveal that U.S. intelligence officials may have deliberately suppressed information about Communist China's interference capabilities before the 2020 presidential election, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The reporting documents two central claims: first, that China possessed "an integrated arsenal capable of shaping an American presidential contest"; second, that officials within the intelligence community "worked, in their own written words, to strip that intelligence of its election relevance, fragment it, delay it."

For Colorado voters and conservatives nationwide, this raises urgent questions about institutional accountability. If true, the allegations suggest that unelected bureaucrats—not elected officials or the public—decided what election-related intelligence Americans would see. That's a direct challenge to democratic transparency and the principle that voters deserve complete information before casting ballots.

Republicans have long warned that the intelligence establishment operates with insufficient oversight and that partisan bias can infect national security decisions. This story, if substantiated, would vindicate those concerns and demand congressional investigation.

Conservatives should demand answers: Which officials made these decisions? Under what authority? And what other intelligence was withheld from public view during 2020?

Watch whether Republican-led congressional committees launch formal inquiries into the declassified record and whether mainstream media outlets cover the allegations with the same intensity they applied to other election-interference claims.
