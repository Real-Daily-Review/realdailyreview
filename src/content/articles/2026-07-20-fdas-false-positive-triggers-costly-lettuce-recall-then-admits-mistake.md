# FDA's Lettuce Blunder: Another Government Lab Failure Costs Business Millions

The FDA bungled a food-safety test so badly it triggered a nationwide recall that cost Taylor Farms millions—then quietly admitted the contamination finding was a "false positive." Over the weekend, Taylor Farms voluntarily recalled iceberg lettuce sold across 27 states after the FDA claimed to detect cyclospora contamination. By Sunday, federal regulators reversed course, acknowledging the lab result was wrong (9News Denver, Just The News).

Taylor Farms, which supplies major retailers and restaurants including Taco Bell, acted out of "an abundance of caution"—the corporate equivalent of apologizing for something you didn't do. The company now faces real damage: lost sales, destroyed inventory, and reputational harm from a government agency that couldn't get its lab work right.

This is what happens when bureaucrats move fast without checking twice. The FDA's error illustrates why conservatives distrust regulatory overreach: government agencies wield enormous power to shut down businesses based on faulty science, yet face minimal accountability when they're wrong. Taxpayers foot the bill for FDA incompetence; businesses and consumers bear the cost.

Colorado produce growers and agricultural suppliers should take note: federal regulators can crater your market in 48 hours on a mistake, then issue a quiet correction that never gets the same media attention as the original scare.

Watch whether Taylor Farms or other ag companies pursue damages against the FDA for this costly error.
