# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, November 20, 2026

Colorado conservatives, the week closes with a fight over your wallet, a Senate leadership scramble in Washington, and a reminder that the left's policy experiments have real consequences for real people. Let's get into it.

---

## Advance Colorado Moves to Kill the Graduated Income Tax

Advance Colorado, the conservative advocacy group that put eight measures on the 2026 ballot, has filed new ballot measures aimed at nullifying Colorado's recently passed graduated income tax. Republicans argue the graduated income tax is a direct assault on Colorado's Taxpayer's Bill of Rights tradition and a wealth-redistribution scheme dressed up as "fairness." This fight isn't over — it's just moving to the next round. Colorado taxpayers should pay close attention to whether these measures make it to the ballot and rally accordingly.

---

## Senate Democrats Fracture Over Leadership

Michigan Sen. Elissa Slotkin has broken with Chuck Schumer, endorsing Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen in what is shaping up to be a genuine leadership challenge inside the Senate Democratic caucus. Schumer is fighting to hold his grip on the minority. For Colorado conservatives, the takeaway is simple: the left is in disarray after November's losses, and a fractured Democratic Senate caucus is a weaker obstacle to the conservative agenda moving through Washington. Watch this one — leadership shapes the opposition.

## American Held in China, Wife Pleads After Trump-Xi Meeting

Sylvia Zin is calling for the release of her husband, U.S. citizen Min Zin, who she says is being held in solitary confinement in China without access to legal counsel — even after President Trump's latest meeting with Xi Jinping. Republicans argue that China must face real consequences for detaining American citizens without due process, not just diplomatic pleasantries. Colorado has a significant defense and aerospace sector with deep ties to national security interests; the Chinese Communist Party's treatment of Americans abroad is not a distant problem.

## Denver Man Convicted in Hairstylist Death Case

A Denver man has been found guilty of tampering with a body and evidence in connection with the death of a Denver hairstylist. Conservatives have long argued that soft-on-crime policies from Denver's progressive city leadership create the conditions for violent crime to flourish — and that accountability in the courtroom, when it finally comes, is no substitute for deterrence up front. Colorado's urban crime problem demands honest leadership, not more excuses from city hall.

## Kamala Harris Is Done — And Democrats Know It

Multiple outlets are reporting what most political observers already understood: Kamala Harris will not be the Democratic presidential nominee again. The 2028 field remains unformed, but Harris has fallen from the top of early polling since the midterms. Conservatives should resist the temptation to assume the Democrats' next standard-bearer will be equally weak — the left will regroup, retool, and come back with someone new. Colorado Republicans need to be building their bench and their message now, not coasting on 2026 wins.

## What to Watch
