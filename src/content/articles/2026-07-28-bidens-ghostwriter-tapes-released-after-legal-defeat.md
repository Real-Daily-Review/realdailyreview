# Biden's Hidden Tapes Finally Released—What They Reveal About Classified Documents

Former President Joe Biden's years-long legal fight to keep his ghostwriter tapes under wraps just collapsed, and the nearly 70 hours of audio recordings are now public—exposing what Biden knew about classified materials he kept after leaving office.

The recordings, made during 2017 interviews with biographer Mark Zwonitzer for Biden's memoir "Promise Me, Dad," were released Monday by the Oversight Project, a conservative watchdog group, after Biden abandoned his legal battle (Washington Examiner). A 117-page transcript shows extensive redactions for classified information—some of which Biden himself acknowledged knowing were classified (Just The News).

Republicans have long argued these tapes could prove Biden knowingly retained sensitive documents, undermining his defense that he was unaware of classified material at his Delaware home and Penn Biden Center office. The special counsel's investigation ultimately declined to prosecute Biden, citing his age and memory issues—a decision conservatives say revealed a two-tiered justice system compared to Trump's classified documents case.

For Colorado taxpayers, this matters: it's another reminder that Washington elites face different rules. While ordinary Americans face prosecution for document mishandling, Biden's legal team spent years blocking transparency before capitulating.
