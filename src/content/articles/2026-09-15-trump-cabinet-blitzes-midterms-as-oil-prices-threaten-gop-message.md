# Trump Administration Mobilizes for Midterms as Energy Costs Loom Over Campaign

The Trump administration is pulling out all stops before November's midterm elections, with Cabinet members logging 500 domestic trips to key districts and Vice President JD Vance taking on a central role in Republican fundraising and messaging (The Daily Wire). But energy costs—particularly oil prices tied to Middle East tensions—threaten to undercut Republican gains at the ballot box.

The Washington Examiner reports that rising energy costs tied to data center expansion and geopolitical friction over the Strait of Hormuz could spike gas prices before voters head to the polls. Trump has signaled he'll pursue an Iran deal only *after* November, a timing calculation critics say prioritizes electoral optics over immediate relief for American consumers and Colorado energy workers already navigating volatile markets.

Republicans argue the administration's campaign blitz demonstrates commitment to winning back Congress and consolidating power. Democrats counter that delaying diplomatic solutions to manage election messaging puts partisan politics ahead of economic stability.

For Colorado—a state where energy policy, water rights, and oil-and-gas jobs remain central to voter concerns—the calculus is stark: will voters reward Republicans for aggressive campaigning, or punish them if gas prices spike before Election Day?
