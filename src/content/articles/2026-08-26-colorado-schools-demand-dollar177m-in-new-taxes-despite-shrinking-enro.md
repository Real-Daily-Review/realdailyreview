# Colorado School Districts Seek $177M in New Taxes as Enrollment Declines

Two major Colorado school districts are asking taxpayers to foot massive new bills even as student populations shrink—a fiscally backwards move that should alarm every parent and property owner in the state.

Jefferson County Schools is seeking $133 million more from taxpayers (Complete Colorado), while Denver Public Schools wants a $44 million mill levy override, with $25.8 million earmarked for employee raises (Denver Post). Combined, that's $177 million in new tax burden on Colorado families already squeezed by inflation and rising property values.

The timing is particularly galling: Jeffco is simultaneously locked in federal litigation over its transgender-student policies, yet still demands more money from the same taxpayers it's fighting with in court. Neither district has explained why enrollment declines justify tax *increases*—the math simply doesn't add up.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue these districts are prioritizing bloated payrolls and administrative overhead over classroom results. If student numbers are dropping, spending should contract, not expand. Colorado taxpayers deserve accountability: detailed breakdowns of where every dollar goes, proof that current budgets are being managed efficiently, and honest answers about why schools can't do more with less.

Watch for November ballot language and whether Colorado voters will tolerate unlimited school-tax requests regardless of enrollment trends.
