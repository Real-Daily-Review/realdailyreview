# Pentagon Escalates Leak Hunt With Polygraph Tests on Military Officials

The Pentagon is reportedly ordering polygraph exams for high-ranking military officials and civilian employees to root out leakers disclosing classified information to journalists, according to the Daily Caller and Washington Examiner. The crackdown targets suspected disclosures about U.S. munitions stockpile depletion amid ongoing conflict with Iran.

Pentagon officials argue that "securing classified information is critical to ensuring the security of the United States and of our troops deployed around the world," per the Daily Caller. The leak investigations reportedly stem from multiple news outlets publishing reports on how the Iran conflict has strained American munitions reserves.

**The Colorado angle:** Defense contractors and military installations across Colorado—including NORAD at Cheyenne Mountain, Fort Carson, and aerospace suppliers in the Denver metro—depend on secure classified protocols. Any breach of munitions data or military readiness assessments could expose vulnerabilities affecting Colorado-based defense operations and the thousands of jobs tied to them.

Conservatives generally support robust counterintelligence measures to protect national security and military readiness. However, civil liberties advocates worry that broad polygraph sweeps could chill legitimate whistleblowing on waste, fraud, or abuse—a tension worth monitoring as the Pentagon expands its internal security dragnet.
