# Conservative Group Fights Back Against Colorado's Graduated Income Tax

Colorado conservatives are mounting a two-front assault on the state's newly enacted graduated income tax, refusing to accept what they view as a dangerous expansion of government power over taxpayers' wallets.

Advance Colorado, a conservative advocacy group that already secured eight measures for the 2026 ballot, has filed new ballot initiatives designed to effectively nullify the graduated tax structure that Democrats rammed through (KDVR Fox31). Simultaneously, the group is challenging the tax's constitutionality in court, arguing that Amendment 87 should have been split into two separate measures rather than bundled into a single ballot question (9News Denver).

Republicans and conservatives argue the graduated income tax represents a fundamental threat to Colorado's economic competitiveness and individual liberty. The tax structure punishes success and creates perverse incentives for high earners and businesses to relocate to more tax-friendly states—a real concern for Colorado's economy and workforce.

Democrats counter that the graduated tax ensures wealthy Coloradans pay their "fair share" while funding education and transportation. They dismiss conservative concerns as predictable opposition to any revenue increase.

The legal and ballot challenges signal that this tax fight is far from over. Watch whether Advance Colorado's 2026 ballot measures gain traction with voters and whether courts rule on the constitutional challenge before then.
