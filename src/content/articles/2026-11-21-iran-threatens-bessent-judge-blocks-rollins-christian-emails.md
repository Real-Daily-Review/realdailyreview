# Trump Cabinet Members Face Legal and Security Threats as Administration Settles In

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has relocated to a military base following credible Iranian assassination threats, while Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins faces a federal lawsuit over Christian-themed workplace emails—two early tests of how far the left will push against Trump appointees.

According to Fox News, Iranian officials have escalated rhetoric targeting Bessent, prompting security measures that moved him onto a military installation. The threat level reportedly reached "an advanced stage," underscoring ongoing hostility from Tehran toward the Trump administration.

Meanwhile, a Biden-appointed federal judge in California ruled Thursday that Rollins likely violated the Establishment Clause by sending agency-wide religious messages—including an Easter "He is risen" email and Christmas greetings (The Daily Wire). Judge Trina L. Thompson granted a preliminary injunction against the Agriculture Secretary, effectively barring similar communications.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans contend the lawsuit represents weaponized judicial overreach. Sending seasonal religious greetings—common in American workplaces—hardly constitutes unconstitutional coercion. The ruling signals that progressive judges will aggressively police conservative and Christian expression in federal agencies, even when no employee complained of actual harm.

**Colorado angle:** Federal agriculture policy directly affects Colorado ranchers and rural communities. Rollins' leadership on land use, water rights, and grazing permits matters to the state's agricultural economy.

Watch whether the Rollins ruling survives appellate review or whether other Trump officials face similar litigation designed to distract from policy implementation.
