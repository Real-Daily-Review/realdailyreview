# Supreme Court to Review Colorado's Religious Discrimination in Pre-K Funding

Colorado's hostility toward religious institutions just landed before the nation's highest court. The Supreme Court has agreed to hear a case challenging the state's exclusion of religious organizations from pre-K education funding—a decision that exposes how far Colorado's progressive bureaucracy will go to sideline faith-based providers.

The case centers on Colorado's refusal to allow religious schools and programs to participate in state-funded pre-K initiatives, a policy conservatives argue violates the First Amendment's guarantee of religious freedom. According to *Complete Colorado*, SCOTUS has taken up the matter, signaling the justices see merit in the constitutional challenge.

This isn't Colorado's first religious liberty stumble. Senator John Hickenlooper has called for investigations into what he describes as Trump administration "retaliation" against the state, according to *CPR News*—though conservatives counter that federal scrutiny of Colorado's regulatory overreach is appropriate accountability, not weaponization.

The pre-K case reflects a broader pattern: Colorado's Democratic-controlled government has repeatedly favored secular programs over religious alternatives, effectively discriminating against families who want faith-centered education. Taxpayers foot the bill while religious institutions are locked out.

Watch for SCOTUS's ruling, which could force Colorado to either fund religious pre-K providers equally or lose federal education dollars—a reckoning the state's progressive leadership should have seen coming.
