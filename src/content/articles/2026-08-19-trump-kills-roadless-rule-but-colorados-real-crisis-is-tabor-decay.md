# Trump Administration Targets 'Roadless Rule,' But Colorado's Real Problem Is TABOR Erosion

The Trump administration is moving to scrap the Clinton-era "roadless rule" and ramp up logging on national forests—a move that will face resistance from Colorado's environmental lobby but shouldn't distract from a more urgent threat: the slow-motion gutting of TABOR, the Taxpayer Bill of Rights that once protected Colorado wallets from runaway government spending.

According to CPR News, the administration wants to dramatically increase logging on national forests and eliminate restrictions that have protected millions of acres from road construction. Colorado has its own state-level protections, which may insulate the state from the worst federal changes—but that's precisely the problem. While conservatives debate forest management in Washington, Colorado's political class has spent decades chipping away at TABOR through "creative political maneuvering," as Complete Colorado reports, leaving the state's foundational tax-and-spending restraint looking "more and more like Swiss cheese."

Republicans argue that restoring TABOR's integrity—closing loopholes that let government dodge voter approval for tax hikes and spending increases—matters far more to Colorado's long-term affordability than federal logging policy. Democrats, by contrast, tend to view TABOR as an obstacle to "needed" public investment.
