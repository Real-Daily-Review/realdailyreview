# Trump Signals Support for Credit Card Rate Cap—But Market Concerns Loom

President Trump said Saturday he'd work with Democrats to cap credit card interest rates at 10%, reviving a proposal he floated in January (Washington Examiner). The bipartisan overture marks rare common ground between Trump and incoming Democratic lawmakers on consumer finance.

Conservatives have reason to be skeptical. While lower rates sound appealing to Colorado families struggling with credit card debt, price controls historically backfire. Republicans argue that artificial rate caps discourage lending, reduce credit availability for working people, and hand more power to government bureaucrats rather than letting markets set rates based on risk. The banking industry warns that aggressive caps could shrink credit access for lower-income borrowers who need it most.

Democrats, meanwhile, frame this as consumer protection against predatory lending. They've long pushed rate caps as a remedy for high-interest debt trapping households in poverty cycles.

The real question: Does Trump's proposal include exemptions for Colorado credit unions and community banks, or does it impose a one-size-fits-all federal mandate? Coloradans should watch whether this becomes actual legislation or campaign rhetoric—and whether any final bill protects local financial institutions from Washington overreach.
