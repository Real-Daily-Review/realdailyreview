# GOP Squanders Midterm Advantage While Democrats Fumble Historic Opportunity

Both parties appear determined to snatch defeat from the jaws of victory heading into the midterms, according to *Washington Examiner* analysis. Democrats hold a historic opening to expand congressional control, yet seem intent on self-sabotage. Republicans, meanwhile, possess structural advantages—inflation, border chaos, and voter frustration with progressive overreach—but risk squandering them through infighting and messaging failures.

For Colorado specifically, this dysfunction matters. The state's competitive races for Congress and statewide office hinge on whether Republicans can effectively contrast their limited-government platform against Democratic spending sprees and regulatory expansion. Conservatives argue the party must hammer home kitchen-table issues: soaring energy costs (critical in Colorado's oil and gas communities), property tax burdens, and the cost of living crisis fueled by Biden-era inflation.

Democrats counter that their agenda addresses working families' needs, but voters increasingly reject that framing. Colorado's independent streak means whichever party runs a disciplined, focused campaign on pocketbook issues will likely prevail.

The real question: Can either party get out of its own way long enough to make the case to Colorado voters?

Watch whether Colorado Republicans coalesce around a unified message on energy independence and fiscal restraint—or whether internal divisions hand Democrats an undeserved lifeline.
