# Aurora Cracks Down on GEO Group Over TB Outbreak Stonewalling at ICE Detention Center

Aurora city officials are threatening legal action against the GEO Group, the private contractor operating a crowded immigration detention facility, for refusing to cooperate with public health investigators probing a potential tuberculosis outbreak.

According to CPR News and 9News Denver, Adams County Public Health remains locked out of the facility more than a month after a TB case was confirmed. The city council is demanding GEO provide investigators access and information necessary to contain the spread—a basic public health imperative that shouldn't require threats.

The standoff highlights a broader tension: private detention operators sometimes prioritize liability concerns over transparency, leaving local health officials unable to do their jobs. Colorado taxpayers and residents deserve assurance that facilities housing detainees meet basic safety standards.

Republicans and conservatives have long supported immigration enforcement and private detention operations as cost-effective alternatives to government-run facilities. But that support hinges on operators meeting health, safety, and transparency standards. GEO's resistance to a legitimate TB investigation undermines the case for private management and hands ammunition to open-borders advocates who want to shut these facilities down entirely.

Watch whether GEO capitulates quickly or whether Aurora pursues legal remedies—and whether this incident influences Colorado's stance on private detention contracts statewide.
