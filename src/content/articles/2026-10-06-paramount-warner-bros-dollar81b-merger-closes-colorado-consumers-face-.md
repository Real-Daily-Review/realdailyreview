# Paramount-Warner Bros. Merger Closes: Hollywood Consolidation Raises Consumer Cost Concerns

Paramount's $81 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery officially closed this week, creating a media behemoth that combines two of America's oldest studios under Skydance ownership—and Colorado consumers should brace for higher streaming bills.

The merged entity now controls CNN, CBS, HBO, Paramount+, and legacy film operations, concentrating enormous market power in a single corporation. While details remain murky, industry observers worry the consolidation will pressure Paramount+ and HBO Max users toward premium pricing or forced bundling—classic behavior when competition shrinks.

This deal faced resistance from Democratic state attorneys general and Hollywood unions, who argued the merger would harm competition and workers (Daily Caller reports). But those objections didn't stop the transaction, suggesting antitrust enforcement remains toothless against corporate giants with deep pockets.

For Colorado households already juggling multiple streaming subscriptions, this consolidation is bad news. Fewer competitors mean less pressure to keep prices competitive. The merged company has zero incentive to hold the line on costs—and every incentive to extract maximum revenue from captive audiences.

Conservatives should watch whether this mega-merger triggers actual antitrust scrutiny or becomes another case study in how big business captures regulators while pretending to fear them.
