# Colorado School Districts Face Tough Choices: Tax Hikes or Consolidation

Douglas County voters will decide in November whether to approve a $54 million mill levy override after the school board unanimously backed the measure (KDVR Fox31). Meanwhile, Boulder Valley School District is planning school closures and consolidation, citing declining enrollment—a stark contrast to the tax-hike approach.

The Douglas County move puts the burden squarely on taxpayers already squeezed by Colorado's rising cost of living. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that before asking voters for more money, districts should first demonstrate fiscal discipline through consolidation, administrative streamlining, and waste reduction—exactly what Boulder Valley is now attempting.

The two approaches highlight a fundamental divide: Douglas County is betting voters will fund current operations; Boulder Valley is rightsizing to match its student population. Conservatives contend that the mill levy override sets a precedent for perpetual tax increases rather than forcing districts to make hard budget choices.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether Douglas County's November vote succeeds and whether Boulder Valley's consolidation plan actually delivers savings or simply delays the same funding crisis.
