# Front Range Rail Push Demands November Tax Vote—Colorado Voters Should Brace for Sales Tax Hike

Colorado's rail planning agency is moving toward asking voters in November to approve a one-third penny sales tax to fund an ambitious Front Range Passenger Rail project stretching from Fort Collins to Pueblo, according to reporting from the Colorado Sun and 9News Denver. The district has already outlined station plans, including stops at sports venues like the Broncos stadium and Summit FC facilities.

**The conservative case against it:** Republicans and taxpayers should scrutinize whether this rail corridor will ever operate profitably or simply become a permanent drain on the state budget. Government-backed transit projects routinely exceed cost estimates and fail to attract ridership projections. A new sales tax—even a small one—is still a tax hike that hits working Coloradans and small businesses already squeezed by inflation and existing state levies. Conservatives argue the private sector, not government, should lead transportation innovation.

**Democrats counter** that the rail would reduce traffic congestion, boost economic development along the corridor, and address climate concerns—though they've offered no binding guarantees on final costs or ridership.

The August decision deadline means the rail district will soon determine whether to formally petition for the November ballot. Taxpayers should demand a detailed, independently audited cost-benefit analysis before any vote.
