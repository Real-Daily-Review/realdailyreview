# 310 Fire Forces Evacuations Near Parachute; Colorado Wildfire Season Rages On

Colorado's wildfire season is hitting hard again—the 310 Fire near Parachute has torched 600 acres and forced immediate evacuations on Morrisania Mesa in Garfield County, according to 9News Denver and CPR News.

Residents on County Road 338 received evacuation orders as the blaze spread rapidly across the Western Slope. Garfield County 911 coordinated the emergency response as firefighting crews mobilized to contain the flames.

The 310 Fire is the latest in a string of major wildfires threatening Colorado communities and infrastructure. Western Slope residents—many dependent on energy development, ranching, and outdoor recreation—face mounting risks from uncontrolled burns that destroy property, threaten livelihoods, and strain emergency resources.

Conservatives have long argued that aggressive forest management, thinning of overgrown timber, and responsible land stewardship are critical to reducing catastrophic wildfire risk. Yet environmental regulations and federal land-use restrictions often hamper these preventive measures. The Biden administration's push to restrict oil and gas development on federal lands also limits funding mechanisms that have historically supported forest health projects across Colorado.

Taxpayers should watch whether state and local officials prioritize practical forest management over regulatory gridlock in the coming months.
