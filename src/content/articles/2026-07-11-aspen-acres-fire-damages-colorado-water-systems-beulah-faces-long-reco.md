# Wildfire Scorches Colorado Water Infrastructure; Beulah Faces Long Recovery

The Aspen Acres fire has dealt a direct hit to Colorado's water systems—a reminder that wildfire threats extend far beyond homes and timber to the critical infrastructure that keeps communities functioning.

The blaze tore through Beulah, a town of roughly 800 residents, destroying homes and damaging water utilities that serve the area (Colorado Sun). Water managers are now scrambling to restore clean water delivery, a process officials acknowledge will take considerable time. The good news: most other active fires burning across Colorado have largely spared critical water infrastructure, limiting the scope of the crisis.

For Coloradans dependent on reliable water service—particularly in rural areas where alternatives are scarce—this underscores a hard truth: wildfire prevention and forest management aren't optional luxuries. They're essential to protecting the systems that keep people alive and communities functioning.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that aggressive forest thinning, controlled burns, and responsible timber management are necessary to reduce catastrophic wildfire risk. The Biden administration's environmental restrictions on logging and fuel reduction have drawn criticism from Colorado Republicans who say federal land-use policies prioritize environmental litigation over practical fire prevention.

Watch for how quickly state and local officials can restore water service to Beulah residents—and whether Colorado's legislature will push back against federal barriers to forest management on public lands.
