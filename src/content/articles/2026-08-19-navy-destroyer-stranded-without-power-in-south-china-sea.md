# Navy Destroyer's Four-Day Power Loss Raises Questions About Fleet Readiness

A U.S. Navy destroyer sat dead in the water in the South China Sea for four days this summer—a stark reminder of how vulnerable American naval power has become in contested waters.

The USS Benfold, an Arleigh Burke-class guided missile destroyer carrying roughly 300 sailors, lost all power on July 24 after an engineering failure, according to the Washington Examiner and Just The News. For four days, the ship operated without air conditioning, functioning galleys, working toilets, or showers. The vessel was eventually towed to the Philippines, where power was restored on July 28.

The incident underscores a troubling pattern: America's military readiness is eroding while China expands its naval presence in the Indo-Pacific. A destroyer—one of the Navy's most critical assets—rendered completely helpless in one of the world's most strategically important regions is precisely the kind of vulnerability adversaries exploit.

Republicans and defense hawks have long warned that budget constraints, deferred maintenance, and aging platforms are hollowing out U.S. military capability. This breakdown suggests those warnings aren't hypothetical.

Colorado defense contractors and military families should watch whether Congress will adequately fund Navy modernization and maintenance—or whether budget-cutting priorities will continue leaving American sailors and ships at risk.
