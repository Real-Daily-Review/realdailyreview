# Preventable Tragedies Fuel Demand for Enforcement, Accountability

Colorado families are demanding action after preventable deaths expose failures in law enforcement and immigration enforcement. A Broomfield family is calling for systemic change after police say their daughter was killed by an ex-boyfriend with prior domestic violence allegations—a case that mirrors a broader national pattern of crimes committed by individuals who should have been removed from communities through existing legal mechanisms.

The Broomfield case underscores what conservatives argue is a critical gap: existing laws aren't being enforced with sufficient rigor. Republicans point to similar cases nationwide, including a North Carolina incident where a three-time deported illegal immigrant allegedly killed a 6-year-old child, as evidence that sanctuary policies and lax enforcement endanger American families (Fox News).

Colorado taxpayers and families deserve answers: Why aren't repeat domestic violence offenders being held accountable? Why are deportation orders not being executed? These aren't failures of law—they're failures of will.

The conservative argument is straightforward: enforce the laws already on the books. Hold judges and prosecutors accountable for decisions that release dangerous individuals. Secure the border and remove those with criminal records. Democrats and progressive prosecutors often resist these measures, citing rehabilitation and due process concerns, but families burying their children have little patience for that debate.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers and law enforcement will commit to mandatory enforcement protocols and victim-centered accountability measures.
