# Colorado River Officials Push Emergency Water Release—Again

Colorado River water managers are gambling with another emergency drawdown from Wyoming's Flaming Gorge Reservoir to artificially prop up Lake Powell, betting that next winter's snowpack will bail them out (Colorado Sun). The move is a short-term band-aid on a long-term crisis born from decades of overallocation and federal mismanagement of the Colorado River Compact.

**The conservative case:** This approach treats symptoms, not causes. Rather than pursuing real solutions—like renegotiating the 1922 Compact to reflect actual water availability, encouraging agricultural water transfers to higher-value uses, or investing in desalination—federal bureaucrats keep raiding upstream reserves. Colorado farmers and Front Range municipalities shouldn't bear the cost of Lake Powell's chronic mismanagement or Arizona's refusal to cut consumption.

Democrats and environmental groups argue emergency releases are necessary to prevent catastrophic reservoir collapse and maintain hydropower generation. But Republicans counter that perpetual emergency measures only delay hard choices and weaken Colorado's negotiating position in future water wars.

Colorado taxpayers and agricultural interests depend on stable water policy. Every emergency release weakens Upper Basin states' leverage and signals Washington will always choose short-term fixes over structural reform.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado's congressional delegation demands real Compact reform or accepts another year of crisis management.
