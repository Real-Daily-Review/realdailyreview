# Colorado Republicans Narrow Governor's Race; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their gubernatorial field to two finalists after their state assembly, while voters will simultaneously decide whether to enshrine a "right to natural gas" in the state constitution—a direct rebuke of progressive energy mandates.

Only two of 18 Republican gubernatorial candidates cleared the assembly threshold (Complete Colorado), setting up a competitive primary that will determine who challenges the Democratic incumbent. The streamlined field reflects GOP efforts to consolidate behind a viable general-election candidate.

Meanwhile, Initiative 177—backed by Advance Colorado—qualified for the ballot after gathering sufficient signatures (Denver Post). The measure would constitutionally protect Coloradans' access to natural gas, a direct counterattack against Democratic-led efforts to phase out gas appliances and heating in the name of climate policy.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue that enshrining energy choice prevents unelected bureaucrats from imposing costly, ideologically driven restrictions on how Coloradans heat their homes and power their lives. The initiative protects working families and rural communities from the left's regulatory overreach.

**Democrats counter** that natural gas contributes to climate change and that the measure would lock in fossil-fuel dependence.

For Colorado taxpayers and energy workers, this ballot fight signals a fundamental clash over who controls energy policy: voters or progressive regulators. Watch whether Initiative 177 gains traction among suburban and rural voters who depend on affordable natural gas.
