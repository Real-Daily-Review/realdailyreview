# Colorado at 150: A State Built on Pioneer Spirit, Now at a Crossroads

Colorado's 150th birthday marks a milestone worth celebrating—a state founded on individual enterprise, limited government, and the grit of settlers who built something from nothing. Today's festivities at the State Capitol honor that legacy, even as modern Colorado faces pressure to abandon the principles that made it great.

The Colorado Sun and CPR News both covered the sesquicentennial celebrations, highlighting the state's unique character and achievements. From mining and ranching to outdoor recreation and energy development, Colorado's prosperity has always flowed from free enterprise and respect for property rights—not government mandates.

Yet the Centennial State now grapples with regulatory overreach, water wars driven by environmental ideology, and energy policies that threaten the workers and communities that built Colorado's middle class. Progressive lawmakers have pushed restrictions on oil and gas development, gun rights, and land use that would have been unthinkable to the pioneers who settled here.

Conservatives argue that honoring Colorado's 150-year legacy means returning to the principles of limited government, fiscal responsibility, and individual liberty that created prosperity—not expanding the state bureaucracy that threatens it.

Watch for whether state leaders use this milestone to recommit to Colorado's free-market roots or double down on progressive experiments that contradict them.
