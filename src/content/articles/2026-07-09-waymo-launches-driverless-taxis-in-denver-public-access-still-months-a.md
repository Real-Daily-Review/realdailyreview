# Waymo's Driverless Taxis Coming to Denver—But Don't Expect Them on Your Commute Yet

Waymo is rolling out fully autonomous taxi service in Denver this month, though public access remains months away as the company works through operational details with local regulators.

The service will initially operate rider-only for Waymo employees, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31. The company has not announced a firm public launch date, leaving questions about how Denver's regulatory framework will handle liability, insurance, and labor impacts once driverless cabs hit streets citywide.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, the Waymo rollout raises practical concerns. Will Denver's city government impose burdensome permitting requirements or special taxes on autonomous vehicle operators? How will the city handle displaced taxi and rideshare drivers? And critically: who bears liability when a driverless vehicle causes an accident—the company, the city, or the passenger?

Republicans and free-market advocates generally support innovation in transportation technology, but they're wary of government overreach. Denver should resist the urge to regulate autonomous vehicles into oblivion or create new revenue schemes disguised as "safety fees."

The real test comes when Waymo opens service to the public. Watch whether Denver's progressive city council uses autonomous vehicles as a pretext for congestion pricing, new vehicle taxes, or restrictions on traditional rideshare—a common left-wing tactic to engineer behavior rather than let markets decide.
