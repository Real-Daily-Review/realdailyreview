# Colorado Faces Five-Day Heat Wave With Temps Near 100

A dangerous heat wave is sweeping into Colorado starting Saturday, with Denver-area highs potentially reaching near 100 degrees and lasting through midweek (9News Denver). The broader system will bring triple-digit temperatures across the Southwest and Great Plains before spreading eastward, trapped under a dome of high pressure that meteorologists warn could persist for a week or more (Washington Examiner).

Colorado residents should prepare now: stock up on water, check on elderly neighbors and vulnerable populations, and ensure air conditioning systems are operational. The extended duration means sustained strain on the state's power grid and water resources—concerns that should weigh on policymakers already grappling with Colorado's chronic water shortages and energy reliability challenges.

This is precisely the moment when Colorado's energy independence matters. Reliable baseload power from natural gas and coal plants will be critical to meet peak demand; wind and solar alone cannot shoulder the load during sustained heat events. Yet state regulators continue pushing aggressive renewable mandates that threaten grid stability when Coloradans need it most.

Conservatives should watch whether state energy officials acknowledge the real-world limits of their green-energy agenda when the mercury spikes—or whether they double down on ideology over practicality.
