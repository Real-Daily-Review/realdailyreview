# Wildfire Claims Helicopter Pilot; Colorado Mountain Towns Face Economic Toll

A Canadian helicopter pilot died in a crash while battling the Gold Mountain Fire in southwest Colorado, with a procession honoring Nicholas Dale carrying his body more than 100 miles from Gunnison to Grand Junction, according to CPR News. The tragedy underscores the real human cost of fighting Colorado's relentless wildfire season—a burden that falls on brave individuals willing to risk their lives while state and federal land management policies remain under fire from conservatives.

The economic damage extends beyond the immediate tragedy. Tourism in nearby Ouray has plummeted 40 percent as smoke and proximity to active flames prompt cancellations, KDVR reported. Local businesses, already squeezed by Colorado's rising operating costs and regulatory environment, now face revenue collapse through no fault of their own. While communities rally with "We are open" campaigns, the underlying problem persists: Colorado's forests remain dangerously overgrown due to decades of mismanagement and environmental litigation that has blocked aggressive thinning and timber harvesting.

Republicans and conservatives argue that proper forest management—including controlled burns and selective logging—would reduce catastrophic fire risk, protect lives and livelihoods, and generate revenue for rural economies. Instead, progressive environmental groups have sued to block such measures, leaving forests as tinderboxes.

Watch for whether state and federal agencies will finally prioritize forest health over litigation-prone environmental restrictions.
