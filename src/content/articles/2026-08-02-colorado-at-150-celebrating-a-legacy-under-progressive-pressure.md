# Colorado at 150: A Milestone to Reflect on What Made This State Great—and What's at Risk

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives as the state stands at a crossroads between the free-market principles that built it and the regulatory overreach threatening its future.

The Centennial State marked its 150th birthday with celebrations at the State Capitol, where leaders reflected on Colorado's past while charting its course ahead (CPR News). The Colorado Sun highlighted 150 things that define Colorado's character—from outdoor heritage to entrepreneurial spirit.

But here's what conservatives need to remember: Colorado's prosperity didn't come from government planning. It came from miners, ranchers, farmers, and businesspeople who took risks, developed resources, and built communities with minimal bureaucratic interference. The state's energy sector powered economic growth. Water rights—hard-won and fiercely defended—enabled agriculture and development across the Front Range and Western Slope.

Today, that legacy faces pressure. Progressive policies have expanded regulations on energy development, water use, and land management. Democrats in the legislature have pushed tax increases and spending expansions that burden the very entrepreneurs and workers who built Colorado.

As Colorado looks toward its next 150 years, conservatives should ask: Will the state double down on the limited-government, pro-growth policies that created prosperity? Or will it continue down the path of regulatory expansion and higher taxes?

Watch whether the state's Republican leadership uses this milestone moment to challenge the progressive regulatory agenda—or lets it slide.
