# Federal Cash Fuels $1.2B Colorado Power Line Project—But Ratepayers Foot the Bill

Federal subsidies are bankrolling a $1.2 billion transmission line across eastern Colorado, but don't expect your electric bill to stay flat. The 28-mile project, backed by federal funding through what the *Colorado Sun* reports as a reliability initiative, will pass most costs directly to customers—a familiar pattern of Washington spending that sounds good until Coloradans see their utility bills.

The project aims to strengthen electricity supply in the region, but conservatives question whether federal infrastructure dollars represent genuine need or another example of government picking winners in the energy sector. Meanwhile, Colorado Springs is moving ahead with rate changes that will significantly hike bills for solar customers, *CPR News* reports, bringing them into line with the utility's standard program—a move that could dampen rooftop solar adoption among residential customers who've benefited from favorable net-metering policies.

Adding to the state's power infrastructure headaches, another lawsuit has been filed against San Isabel Energy Association over the Aspen Acres fire in southern Colorado, with residents alleging the co-op's power line sparked the blaze. The *Colorado Sun* reports that a piece of the line was found near the ignition point showing signs of arcing.

**Watch:** whether these rate increases and federal-backed projects actually improve grid reliability or simply shift costs to Colorado families and businesses already squeezed by inflation.
