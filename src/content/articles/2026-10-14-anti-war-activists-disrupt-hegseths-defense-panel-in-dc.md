# Anti-War Activists Disrupt Hegseth's Defense Panel in DC

Pete Hegseth, Trump's Defense Secretary nominee, faced repeated heckling from anti-war protesters at an Army Association panel on autonomous weapons systems and Project Agincourt in Washington, D.C. (Fox News, Just The News).

The protesters—spread throughout the room—interrupted at least five times in the first 30 minutes, shouting accusations of war crimes and Nazi sympathies (Just The News, Daily Caller). Security eventually removed the disruptors.

**The conservative take:** This is par for the course. The activist left routinely uses intimidation tactics to silence debate on defense policy rather than engage substantively. Hegseth's record on military modernization and deterrence is serious policy work; screaming slogans is not argument.

**Why it matters for Colorado:** Defense contractors and military installations across Colorado—from Fort Carson to Buckley Space Force Base—depend on coherent Pentagon leadership and procurement decisions. Hegseth's focus on autonomous systems and strategic readiness directly affects Colorado jobs and national security posture. The disruption underscores how the progressive base opposes the military modernization Colorado's defense sector requires.

**Watch next:** Whether Senate Democrats will use similar tactics during Hegseth's confirmation hearings, or whether they'll attempt substantive questioning on weapons policy and military spending priorities.
