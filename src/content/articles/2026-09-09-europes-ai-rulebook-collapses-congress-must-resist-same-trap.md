# Europe's AI Overreach Hits a Wall—and Congress Should Take Note

Europe's grand experiment in AI regulation is already falling apart, and Washington should learn the lesson before imposing similar red tape on American innovation.

The European Union's vaunted AI Act, passed in 2024 as a "comprehensive rulebook," was supposed to set the global standard. Instead, Brussels had to delay its own law just days before implementation (Washington Examiner). The EU's regulatory ambition crashed into reality so hard that officials scrambled to postpone enforcement—a stunning admission that the rulebook doesn't work.

This matters for Colorado. Tech companies, energy firms relying on AI optimization, and agricultural operations depending on AI-driven efficiency all face pressure from Washington to adopt similar restrictions. Democrats and progressive regulators are already pushing U.S. Congress toward European-style AI mandates that would strangle American competitiveness while lining the pockets of compliance consultants.

Republicans and conservatives argue that heavy-handed regulation kills innovation and hands market dominance to China and other competitors unconstrained by Western bureaucracy. The EU's stumble proves the point: when government tries to micromanage cutting-edge technology, it fails spectacularly.

Watch whether Congress uses Europe's regulatory failure as a cautionary tale—or ignores it and doubles down on the same mistakes.
