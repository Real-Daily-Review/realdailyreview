# Alaska GOP Rejects Trump's Election Lawsuit; 'Decoy Dan' Scheme Under FBI Scrutiny

Alaska's Republican state government has dealt a sharp rebuke to President Trump, urging a federal judge to dismiss his lawsuit demanding the state set aside Senate ballots—a move Alaska officials say is legally impossible and "meritless" (Washington Examiner). The case centers on Alaska's pivotal U.S. Senate race, where Democrats may secure a win.

Meanwhile, the so-called "Decoy Dan" ballot-confusion scheme has drawn federal heat. Conservative commentator Mark Levin called the effort—involving two candidates named Dan Sullivan running simultaneously—a "conspiracy to steal the United States Senate from the American People" (Breitbart). The FBI is now investigating, with agents contacting donors across Alaska and Massachusetts (New York Post).

The dual fiasco exposes a fundamental conservative principle: election integrity demands clean ballots and respect for legal process, not courtroom theatrics or ballot-manipulation schemes. When Republicans themselves reject Trump's legal overreach, it signals that even GOP-led states won't abandon constitutional guardrails for partisan advantage.

**Colorado conservatives should watch whether similar ballot-confusion tactics surface in Colorado's 2026 races—and whether state GOP leadership will similarly stand firm against pressure to abandon legal norms.**
