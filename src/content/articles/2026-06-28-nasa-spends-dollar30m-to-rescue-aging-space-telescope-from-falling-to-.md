# NASA Launches $30M Rescue Mission for Aging Space Telescope

NASA is spending $30 million to save the Swift Observatory from falling back to Earth, with a robotic rescue mission set to launch this week. The telescope, operational since 2004, has been gradually losing altitude and faces destruction without intervention.

The Swift Observatory studies high-energy cosmic explosions and has provided two decades of valuable scientific data. NASA's plan involves deploying a robotic "lifesaver" to nudge the aging satellite into a higher, stable orbit—a technically complex maneuver that underscores both the value of the mission and the costs of space operations.

**The conservative angle:** While space exploration commands broad support, taxpayers should ask hard questions about the $30 million price tag for salvaging a 20-year-old satellite. This raises legitimate concerns about NASA's budget priorities and whether maintaining aging assets represents the best use of federal dollars—especially when the agency faces competing demands and fiscal constraints. Republicans have long argued for tighter NASA oversight and clearer cost-benefit analysis on extended missions.

The mission also highlights how space operations, once launched, often require expensive follow-up spending that wasn't fully anticipated. Coloradans should watch whether this rescue sets a precedent for costlier interventions on other aging space assets.
