# Douglas County Voters Face Governance Expansion as County Weighs Adding Two Commissioners

Douglas County taxpayers will decide whether to expand their board of commissioners from three to five members—a move that would increase government overhead and payroll in one of Colorado's fastest-growing counties. The ballot measure, set for voter approval, mirrors a similar expansion proposal in Boulder County (Complete Colorado).

Proponents of the expansion argue that rapid population growth in Douglas County demands additional representation and administrative capacity. Critics counter that adding two commissioners means more salaries, benefits, and bureaucratic layers—costs that ultimately fall on property taxpayers already facing rising assessments across the metro area.

The timing coincides with recent weather challenges: a severe hailstorm this summer flooded Douglas County intersections and prompted seven rescues by South Metro Fire Rescue (9News Denver), raising questions about whether existing county infrastructure and emergency management can handle growth without bloat.

Conservatives argue that before expanding government, Douglas County should demonstrate that current commissioners cannot manage county affairs efficiently. The real question: does Douglas County need bigger government, or better-managed government?

Watch whether taxpayer groups mobilize against the measure and how commissioners justify the added expense.
