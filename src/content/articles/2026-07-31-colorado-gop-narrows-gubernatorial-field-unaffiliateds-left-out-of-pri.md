# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field at Assembly; Unaffiliateds Reshape State Politics

Colorado Republicans have winnowed a crowded gubernatorial primary field down to two finalists through the party assembly process, even as unaffiliated voters—now a growing bloc—sit on the sidelines of candidate selection (Complete Colorado). The assembly system, which allows only registered party members to participate in early rounds, is increasingly limiting Colorado's political options as unaffiliated registration surges statewide.

Republicans argue the assembly process ensures party activists and committed members shape nominees aligned with conservative principles. However, critics note that as unaffiliated voters grow in number, the primary-driven system effectively excludes them from meaningful input on major candidates—a structural problem that could weaken general-election competitiveness if GOP nominees fail to appeal beyond the party base.

The narrowed Republican field reflects internal party dynamics, but the larger story is Colorado's shifting voter registration landscape. Unaffiliateds now represent a substantial share of the electorate, yet the current primary system keeps them out of the decisive early stages (Complete Colorado). For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers watching 2026, the question is whether Republicans can nominate candidates who appeal beyond the assembly hall—or whether the party's internal process will hand Democrats an opening in a state trending purple.

Watch whether Republicans adjust primary rules or messaging to recapture unaffiliated voters before the general election.
