# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Sunday, September 6, 2026

Colorado's Labor Day weekend closes with a reminder that the policy fights never rest — from a Boulder campus death finally getting answers to a Connecticut city handing police badges to non-citizens, the stakes for law, order, and common sense couldn't be clearer.

---

## Colorado's Bear Crisis Is a Wildlife Management Wake-Up Call

Colorado has euthanized 294 bears this year — more than three times last year's number — as wildfire and drought push hungry animals into neighborhoods and campgrounds at record rates. This isn't a feel-good story about coexistence; it's a consequence of land and water mismanagement compounding over years. Conservatives and sportsmen's groups have long argued that sound wildlife management, responsible grazing policy, and real drought mitigation beat feel-good regulatory posturing every time. Colorado's ranchers and rural communities are living with the results.

---

## Initiative 234: Reading Standards or a Litigation Lottery?

Colorado's Initiative 234 — which would tie certain standards to an 8th-grade reading level — sounds reasonable on its face, but conservatives warn the operative word here is "litigation." As Complete Colorado reports, defining what constitutes an 8th-grade reading level in law is far murkier than it sounds, and vague statutory language is a trial lawyer's best friend. Colorado taxpayers have seen this movie before: well-intentioned ballot language becomes a courthouse revenue stream. Republicans should read this one carefully before November.

---

## CBI Investigation Into CU Boulder Student's Death Confirmed

An independent review has confirmed the Colorado Bureau of Investigation's findings into the death of Megan Trussell, the 18-year-old University of Colorado Boulder student found dead in Boulder Canyon after disappearing in February 2025. Families sending kids to Colorado's flagship university deserve transparency and accountability from both campus administration and law enforcement. The confirmation of findings is a step toward closure, but questions about student safety culture at CU Boulder — and whether the university has done enough — remain fair game.

---

## Connecticut Votes to Put Non-Citizens in Police Uniforms

Danbury, Connecticut's city council voted 18-2 to allow non-citizen immigrants and DACA recipients to serve as police officers — with one Democrat explicitly framing it as a message against ICE enforcement. Let that sink in: a city government is deliberately recruiting a police force it believes will be less cooperative with federal immigration law. Conservatives argue this is sanctuary-city logic taken to its most dangerous extreme. Colorado's own Democrat-controlled legislature has flirted with similar anti-enforcement posturing. Watch for this template to travel west.

---

## Mamdani's 9/11 Record Deserves a Hard Look Before Ground Zero

New York City mayoral candidate Zohran Mamdani is facing sharp backlash over a resurfaced 2023 social media post about 9/11, with families of victims urging him to stay away from this week's Ground Zero commemoration. Republicans and 9/11 families are right to demand accountability. Twenty-five years on, the idea that someone with a troubling record on the worst terrorist attack in American history could hold major elected office should alarm every voter — in New York and nationally. Character and history matter. Full stop.

## What to Watch

Keep your eye on Initiative 234 as the November ballot takes shape — if the litigation risk is as serious as conservatives argue, Colorado taxpayers need to hear that case made loudly and early. Watch also for any Colorado legislative response to the bear crisis; if Denver Democrats treat it as an excuse to restrict hunting or expand bureaucratic wildlife programs rather than pursue real management solutions, that's a fight worth having. And nationally, the Mamdani 9/11 story will either gain traction or get buried by a sympathetic press — conservatives should make sure it doesn't disappear before Election Day.
