# Trump Taps Sonderling for Labor Secretary—Conservative Pick to Rein In Regulatory Overreach

President Trump has nominated Keith Sonderling, acting Labor Secretary, to permanently lead the Department of Labor, signaling a sharp rightward turn at an agency Colorado conservatives have long criticized for strangling small business with red tape.

Sonderling, a former Republican member of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission and attorney with Gunster, Yoakley, &amp; Stewart in West Palm Beach, Florida, would replace Lori Chavez-DeRemer. According to the *Washington Examiner*, conservatives view the nomination as an opportunity to "drain the swamp" at Labor—a department that has aggressively pursued union organizing campaigns, expanded overtime rules, and tightened contractor classifications under the Biden administration.

For Colorado, the stakes are real. The Labor Department's regulatory reach directly affects Colorado's construction, energy, agriculture, and small-business sectors. Stricter wage-and-hour enforcement and pro-union initiatives have driven up compliance costs for family-owned operations across the state.

Conservatives argue Sonderling's EEOC background and private-sector legal experience position him to roll back what they see as ideological overreach and restore balance to workplace regulation. Republicans expect him to prioritize business flexibility and worker choice over union-friendly mandates.

**Watch for Senate confirmation hearings and whether Sonderling signals plans to revisit Biden-era overtime and contractor rules that have hit Colorado employers hard.**
