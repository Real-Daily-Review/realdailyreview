# AI Data Center Backlash Threatens Colorado's Tech Economy

Public opposition to artificial intelligence data centers has reached fever pitch, with Americans now preferring nuclear power plants as neighbors—a stunning reversal that threatens Colorado's bid to capitalize on the AI boom.

According to the Washington Examiner, a recent Gallup survey shows Americans reject AI data centers by a margin exceeding 20 percentage points compared to nuclear facilities. The hostility has intensified dramatically over the past four months, the outlet reports, bordering on what critics call "mass hysteria."

For Colorado, this matters. The state has positioned itself as a competitive hub for data center development, banking on tech investment to diversify its economy beyond energy and agriculture. Local governments have courted these facilities as sources of jobs and tax revenue. But if national sentiment hardens against AI infrastructure, Colorado communities may find themselves fighting an uphill battle against NIMBY opposition—even as they need the economic activity.

Conservatives argue that unfounded fears about data centers reflect the broader pattern of progressive environmentalism blocking productive development. The real issue, they contend, is that Americans lack clear information about data center safety and efficiency. Without a serious public education campaign, Colorado's economic opportunity could evaporate to emotion-driven activism.

Watch whether Colorado municipalities and the state legislature move to preempt local opposition with streamlined permitting or public information efforts.
