# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Nation—While Activists Wage War on Progress

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are quietly becoming the backbone of American digital infrastructure, yet progressive activists and environmental groups continue to oppose the industry's expansion across the state. These facilities—which process everything from cloud computing to AI workloads—represent genuine economic opportunity for Colorado workers and communities, according to *Complete Colorado*.

The data center sector generates substantial tax revenue and high-paying jobs without the environmental footprint of traditional manufacturing. Yet the left frames data centers as energy hogs and water consumers, using regulatory pressure to slow development. Democrats and green groups have pushed for stricter permitting rules and water-use restrictions that threaten Colorado's competitive advantage in attracting this critical infrastructure.

Republicans and industry advocates counter that data centers are essential to modern life—from streaming services to financial systems—and that Colorado's natural advantages (cool climate, reliable power grid, geographic diversity) make the state an ideal hub. Blocking this growth simply cedes opportunity to other states while Colorado loses jobs and tax revenue.

The stakes are real: as artificial intelligence and cloud computing reshape the economy, states that welcome data center investment will capture wealth and employment. Colorado can either lead or fall behind.
