# Trump's AI Czar Dismisses Huang Espionage Claims as 'Collapsing'

The alleged spy scandal ensnaring Nvidia founder Jensen Huang is unraveling fast—and it's raising hard questions about who's pushing the narrative and why.

David Sacks, Trump's former AI Czar and current co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, says the espionage allegations tying Huang to a Chinese businesswoman "do not hold up to scrutiny" (Breitbart). The "spy narrative," Sacks argues, is "collapsing already."

National Review, meanwhile, is pressing a different angle: why has Nvidia been allowed to sell advanced AI chips to China in the first place? The outlet suggests Trump's team needs to answer for the company's export practices, regardless of whether the Huang espionage claims stick.

The split reveals tension within conservative circles. Some Republicans want accountability for Nvidia's China dealings and tighter export controls on critical AI technology. Others—including Trump's own AI advisor—are skeptical the spy allegations have merit and worry the narrative is being weaponized.

For Colorado, this matters: advanced semiconductor and AI development increasingly touches Colorado's tech corridor and defense contractors. Loose export controls undermine national security; baseless spy accusations undermine trust in legitimate oversight.

Watch whether Trump's team moves to tighten AI chip exports to China—or lets Nvidia off the hook.
