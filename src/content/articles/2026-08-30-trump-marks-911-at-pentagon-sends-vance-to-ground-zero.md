# Trump to Mark 9/11 Anniversary at Pentagon, Not Ground Zero

President Trump will observe the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks at the Pentagon rather than New York City's Ground Zero, drawing immediate scrutiny over his absence from the nation's most symbolic 9/11 memorial site.

Trump confirmed the Pentagon visit to reporters, while a White House official stated Vice President JD Vance will travel to New York for ceremonies at Ground Zero (Fox News). The decision marks a departure from recent presidential tradition, with Trump disputing reports characterizing his Ground Zero absence as a snub (9News Denver).

The split itinerary—Trump at the Pentagon, Vance at Ground Zero—raises questions about the administration's messaging on one of America's most solemn commemorations. Critics will likely argue the arrangement diminishes the symbolic weight of Ground Zero, where nearly 3,000 Americans died in the 2001 terrorist attacks.

Trump's team has not provided detailed reasoning for the Pentagon choice, leaving room for interpretation about whether operational, scheduling, or other factors drove the decision. The White House's decision to send Vance to New York appears designed to ensure vice-presidential representation at Ground Zero, though it does not address why the sitting president opted for the Pentagon venue instead.

Coloradans and national observers should watch whether Trump clarifies the rationale and whether the split attendance becomes a flashpoint in broader debates over presidential leadership and 9/11 remembrance.
