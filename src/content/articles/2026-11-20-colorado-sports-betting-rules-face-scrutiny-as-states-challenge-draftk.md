# Colorado Should Watch Sports Betting Regulation Closely as States Clash Over DraftKings

Colorado's already-permissive sports betting landscape could face pressure if state attorneys general crack down on skill-gaming platforms. Florida's AG is challenging DraftKings' classification as a "game of skill" rather than gambling, arguing the $50-entry, $90-payout model operates like an unlicensed casino (Daily Caller). Georgia's AG separately denounced LSU for "destructive behavior" in a letter to the SEC, signaling aggressive state-level pushback against unregulated gaming expansion (Breitbart).

**The conservative concern:** Unregulated gambling platforms exploit regulatory gaps while siphoning tax revenue from states. If DraftKings and similar operators face legal challenges elsewhere, Colorado regulators should clarify whether our licensing framework adequately protects consumers and captures fair tax revenue—or whether we've created a backdoor casino loophole.

Colorado already permits sports betting under strict licensing. But the skill-versus-gambling distinction matters: if other states successfully argue these platforms are casinos operating without proper oversight, Colorado could face pressure to tighten rules or lose tax revenue to stricter jurisdictions.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's Division of Gaming issues guidance clarifying how skill-gaming platforms must comply with state licensing requirements, or whether federal courts intervene in the DraftKings dispute.
