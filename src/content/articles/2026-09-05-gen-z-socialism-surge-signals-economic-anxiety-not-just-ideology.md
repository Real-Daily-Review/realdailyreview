# Gen Z's Socialism Embrace Signals Economic Anxiety, Not Just Ideology

Nearly half of Americans under 30 would vote for a socialist presidential candidate, according to recent polling—a troubling sign that conservatives' traditional anti-socialism arguments aren't landing with younger voters struggling through inflation and housing costs.

The Washington Examiner reports that while conservatives correctly point to socialism's historical failures, they're missing a deeper problem: young people aren't embracing Marx because they've read bad theory. They're doing it because they're economically anxious. Grocery bills are crushing them. Housing is unaffordable. Student debt is crushing. When the free market feels rigged, socialism's promises sound appealing—even if they're false.

Across the country, Democratic Socialist candidates are winning primaries and heading to November ballots in New York, Michigan, Minnesota, and beyond. The Examiner warns that Minnesota could become either a "firewall" against socialism's spread or "the next domino to fall."

For Colorado, this trend matters. As younger voters migrate to the state and reshape its politics, conservatives can't simply recite Cold War talking points. Republicans need to offer real solutions to cost-of-living crises—lower taxes, less regulation, energy independence, and pro-growth policies that actually improve young people's material conditions.

**Watch whether Colorado Republicans develop an economic message that competes for Gen Z voters, or cede the demographic to progressive promises.**
