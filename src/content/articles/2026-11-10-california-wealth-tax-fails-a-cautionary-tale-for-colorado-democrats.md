# California Voters Reject Billionaire Wealth Tax—a Warning for Colorado Democrats

California voters just rejected a radical wealth tax that progressives hoped would become a national template—and that's a lesson Colorado's left-leaning legislature should heed before pursuing similar schemes.

Proposition 40, which would have imposed a one-time 5% tax on billionaires' wealth, failed decisively: 52.8% to 47.2%, according to the Associated Press (Washington Examiner, New York Post). The measure represented what some on the Left saw as a trial balloon for a nationwide wealth tax—the kind of confiscatory policy that sounds good in progressive fever dreams but repels voters when it hits the ballot.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that wealth taxes are economically destructive, difficult to enforce, and fundamentally un-American. They punish success and drive capital and talent out of state. California's rejection proves that even in deep-blue territory, voters understand the danger.

Colorado taxpayers should pay attention. Democratic lawmakers in Denver have shown appetite for aggressive tax hikes and wealth redistribution schemes. If California's progressive voters—in a state that routinely passes left-wing ballot measures—rejected a wealth tax, Colorado voters would likely do the same. Yet that won't stop activists from trying.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats attempt to resurrect wealth-tax proposals or similar confiscatory schemes in the 2026 legislative session.
