# Taylor Farms Recalls Lettuce Across 27 States After Cyclosporiasis Outbreak

A major produce supplier is yanking iceberg lettuce from shelves nationwide after federal investigators linked contaminated product to a multistate illness outbreak. Taylor Farms has voluntarily recalled bagged shredded iceberg lettuce across 27 states following a cyclosporiasis outbreak that has sickened people in 34 states, according to the Washington Examiner. The company sourced the contaminated lettuce from central Mexico and has removed all such product from the U.S. market "because it has the potential to be contaminated with Cyclospora," per the New York Post.

Federal investigators traced the outbreak to lettuce served at Taco Bell restaurants, triggering the broader recall. While the company's swift voluntary action avoids forced government intervention, the incident underscores why food-safety oversight matters—and why Colorado consumers and retailers need reliable supply-chain transparency from produce distributors.

Conservatives generally support market-driven accountability: companies that poison customers face lawsuits, lost revenue, and reputational collapse. That's often more effective than regulatory red tape. Still, this case shows why basic FDA oversight of imported food remains justified—Mexico-sourced produce carries real contamination risks that markets alone may not catch fast enough.

Watch whether the FDA tightens import protocols or whether Taylor Farms' voluntary action satisfies regulators and restores consumer confidence.
