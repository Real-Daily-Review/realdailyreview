# Severe Weather Batters Colorado; Tornado Watch Covers 12 Counties

Severe thunderstorms swept across Colorado on Monday, dumping hail up to 2.75 inches and triggering tornado watches across a dozen counties from the foothills to the Eastern Plains.

The National Weather Service issued a tornado warning for Elbert County and a broader tornado watch covering Denver, Greeley, Castle Rock, and surrounding areas until 9 p.m., with the primary threat zone east of Interstate 25 extending toward the Kansas border (CPR News). A line of storms moved down from the Colorado foothills in the afternoon, then across the Front Range and eastward, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver.

The hail damage—some stones reaching nearly three inches—poses real risks to Colorado agriculture, vehicles, and property across the affected regions. Farmers and ranchers on the Eastern Plains, already managing water scarcity and regulatory pressures, now face potential crop losses from severe weather that no government policy can prevent or adequately compensate.

Coloradans in the watch zone should monitor National Weather Service updates and take shelter as needed. The storm system underscores why Colorado's economy depends on resilient agriculture and energy sectors—not government mandates—to weather both natural and man-made challenges.

Watch for damage assessments and insurance claims in rural counties hit hardest by the hail.
