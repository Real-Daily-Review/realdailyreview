# Colorado Should Stop Playing Favorites With Political Parties

Colorado government is quietly granting special recognition and privileges to political parties—a practice that betrays conservative principles of equal treatment under law.

According to *Complete Colorado*, state officials have been extending preferential status to certain parties, a move the outlet characterizes as anything but "courageous." Republicans and conservatives argue that government has no legitimate role picking winners among political organizations. When taxpayer-funded institutions grant special recognition to parties, they violate the fundamental conservative principle that the state should remain neutral on partisan matters.

The issue cuts to the heart of limited government: Colorado's bureaucracy should serve all citizens equally, regardless of party affiliation. Granting one party advantages—whether in recognition, access, or resources—amounts to government favoritism, precisely the kind of overreach conservatives oppose.

Democrats and progressive officials typically frame such moves as promoting "engagement" or "representation," but conservatives see them as the state using its power to pick political sides. That's not the role of government in a free society.

Coloradans should demand their elected officials clarify which parties are receiving special treatment and on what legal authority—and then end the practice entirely.
