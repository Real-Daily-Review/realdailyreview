# Economic Anxiety, Not Grievance Politics, Should Drive Colorado's Policy Debate

Colorado women voters are worried about their economic futures—but the real question is whether state leaders will address it through growth and opportunity or more government programs.

A new CPR News poll found nearly two-thirds of Colorado women respondents believe women face fewer rights and opportunities than men, with many citing concerns about economic inequality ahead of the midterms. Democrats will likely use these findings to push for expanded social spending and regulatory mandates. Republicans counter that the path to prosperity isn't government redistribution but pro-growth policies: lower taxes, less regulation, and labor-market freedom that create jobs and raise wages for all Coloradans.

The poll underscores a real economic anxiety—but conservatives argue the left's solution of bigger government and more bureaucracy has consistently failed to deliver broad-based prosperity. Colorado's economy thrives when businesses can hire, invest, and compete freely, not when politicians pick winners and losers through mandates and subsidies.

Meanwhile, the FDA recalled Alfredo sauce sold in Colorado over salmonella contamination (Class I designation), a reminder that food safety remains a legitimate government function.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats use this polling data to justify new spending initiatives or whether Republicans can reframe the conversation around economic growth and opportunity.
