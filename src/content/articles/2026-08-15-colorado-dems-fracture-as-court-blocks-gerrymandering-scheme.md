# Colorado Democrats Face Court Defeat, Internal Fractures

Colorado's Democratic Party is imploding from within—and the state Supreme Court just handed them another loss.

The court recently shut down a Democrat gerrymandering scheme that would have redrawn legislative districts to cement party control (Complete Colorado). The ruling keeps Colorado from descending into the partisan map-drawing wars consuming other states, preserving the state's independent redistricting process.

Meanwhile, Democrats are tearing themselves apart over policy direction and leadership priorities. Complete Colorado reports that the party is experiencing "ravenous and public factionalism"—the kind of internal warfare Republicans endured for two decades. Progressive and moderate wings are clashing openly, signaling deeper fractures over spending priorities, energy policy, and regulatory overreach that have defined recent Colorado governance.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this dysfunction matters. Democratic infighting could either paralyze the legislature—potentially blocking new tax hikes and regulations—or produce chaotic, poorly-vetted legislation rushed through without scrutiny. The gerrymandering defeat also suggests Colorado courts remain willing to check partisan power grabs, a small win for electoral integrity.

Republicans should watch whether Democratic divisions translate into legislative gridlock or whether party leadership consolidates control and accelerates the progressive agenda before 2026.
