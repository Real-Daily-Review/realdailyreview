# Colorado Faces Twin Wildfire Evacuations as Fire Season Intensifies

Mandatory evacuations are underway in two Colorado counties as wildfires spread across the state. The Green Ridge Fire has forced evacuations south of Steamboat Springs in Routt County, burning near Stagecoach Reservoir (9News Denver), while the Aspen Acres Fire has triggered mandatory evacuations in Huerfano County along Williams Creek and U.S. Forest Service lands (KDVR Fox31).

These incidents underscore a persistent problem: Colorado's forests remain tinderboxes despite decades of federal land management. Conservatives argue that excessive environmental regulations have prevented necessary forest thinning and controlled burns, leaving dead timber and undergrowth that fuel catastrophic fires. The Biden administration's push to restrict timber harvesting and energy development on federal lands—which dominate Colorado's landscape—has only worsened forest health.

Evacuations disrupt lives and livelihoods across rural Colorado communities already struggling with regulatory overreach. Ranchers, small business owners, and residents bear the costs while federal agencies delay common-sense forest management in the name of environmental protection.

Taxpayers should watch whether state and federal officials finally prioritize aggressive forest thinning and timber removal over environmental litigation that leaves our forests burning.
