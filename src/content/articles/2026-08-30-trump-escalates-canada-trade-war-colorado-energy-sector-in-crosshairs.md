# Trump Escalates Trade War With Canada—Colorado Energy Sector Watches Closely

President Trump is ramping up tariff threats against Canada, calling its leaders the "worst" he's dealt with and declaring he doesn't want "Canadian anything" (Washington Examiner). The escalating trade war raises real stakes for Colorado's energy and manufacturing sectors, which depend heavily on cross-border commerce and integrated North American supply chains.

Trump's hardline stance reflects his long-standing complaint that Canada has "ripped us off for decades" (Just The News). Conservatives backing the president argue that aggressive tariffs are necessary to rebalance trade deficits and protect American workers and industries from unfair competition.

But Colorado businesses—particularly in oil and gas, agriculture, and manufacturing—face genuine exposure. Canadian crude flows through Colorado refineries; Canadian parts feed into U.S. auto and equipment production. A full trade war could disrupt energy markets, raise consumer costs, and squeeze profit margins for Colorado employers already navigating inflation and regulatory headwinds.

National Review has criticized Trump's tariff approach as economically counterproductive, though right-leaning outlets remain divided on trade strategy.
