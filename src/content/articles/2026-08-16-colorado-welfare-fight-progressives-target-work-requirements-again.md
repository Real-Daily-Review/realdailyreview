# Colorado Welfare Debate: Work Requirements Under Fire Again

Colorado taxpayers are watching their state become ground zero in the national fight over welfare reform—and progressives are quietly dismantling the work-oriented safety net that actually lifted families out of poverty.

The 1990s welfare reforms succeeded by tying benefits to employment, reducing dependency and strengthening personal responsibility. But as *National Review* reports, that model remains "a target for progressives" determined to expand the entitlement state. *The Daily Wire* warns that without reinforced work requirements, welfare programs "disincentivize work" and trap families in generational poverty rather than lifting them toward independence.

Republicans argue that Colorado should resist the left's push to weaken work mandates. Conservatives say the data is clear: when you remove the requirement to work, you remove the incentive to work—and you explode the tax burden on Colorado's working families and small businesses already squeezed by state spending.

Democrats counter that safety nets should be more generous and less punitive, but they rarely address the fiscal cost or the moral hazard of dependency.

**Watch for:** Colorado legislative attempts to expand welfare eligibility or soften work requirements in the 2025 session—and track how much it costs taxpayers.
