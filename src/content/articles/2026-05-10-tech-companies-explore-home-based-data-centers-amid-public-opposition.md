# Micro Data Centers May Move Into U.S. Homes as Public Opposition Grows

Facing declining public support for large-scale data center construction, technology companies are developing smaller data centers designed to operate inside individual homes, according to CNBC.

The shift reflects growing resistance to massive data center buildouts across the United States. As communities push back against sprawling facilities, the industry is exploring a distributed model that would place computing infrastructure at a residential scale.

Deutsche Welle has reported on the broader tension between the technology boom driving data center demand and the downsides of such facilities, which typically consume significant energy and water resources and can strain local infrastructure.

The move toward home-based micro data centers represents an attempt to sidestep community opposition while maintaining the computational capacity needed to support artificial intelligence and cloud computing expansion. However, the practical implications—including residential power consumption, cooling requirements, and neighborhood impact—remain unclear.

Watch for regulatory responses from local and state governments as this model develops, and whether residential data centers face their own community resistance.
