# Trump Feuds With Italy's Meloni Over Iran War Support—and Conservative Allies Are Worried

President Trump has publicly rebuked Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, claiming she refused to allow the U.S. to use Italy's landing strips during military operations against Iran and later "begged for a picture" with him (Fox News Politics).

Trump's complaint centers on Italy's unwillingness to provide logistical support for U.S. military action—a concrete measure of allied commitment. The dispute highlights ongoing tension between the Trump administration's transactional approach to foreign policy and traditional European partnerships.

However, even conservative outlets are sounding alarm. The Washington Examiner's editorial board warned that Trump's "outburst" and dismissive tone toward Meloni—a conservative ally—risks damaging the trans-Atlantic relationship at a critical moment. While Trump has long criticized the EU as a bureaucratic institution that undermines national sovereignty, direct personal attacks on allied leaders present a different problem: they erode the diplomatic trust necessary for coordinated defense and intelligence sharing.

Italy's refusal to support U.S. military operations raises legitimate questions about NATO commitment and burden-sharing. Yet conservatives also recognize that alienating right-leaning European partners weakens the West's unified stance against adversaries like Iran and China.
