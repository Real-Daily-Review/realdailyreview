# Senator Hawley Targets Mass Surveillance Network—Colorado Should Pay Attention

Senator Josh Hawley is investigating Flock Safety, an AI-powered camera system that captures 20 billion vehicle snapshots monthly across America, mostly of drivers who've committed no crime. The Missouri Republican argues the technology represents warrantless mass surveillance of ordinary Americans—a concern that should resonate with Colorado gun owners and liberty-minded voters already wary of government overreach.

According to *The Daily Wire*, these cameras photograph commuters, parents picking up kids, and churchgoers, yet most Americans have no idea their movements are being tracked and catalogued. Hawley's investigation targets what conservatives see as a creeping surveillance state enabled by private companies and local law enforcement without adequate constitutional safeguards.

Colorado has seen similar tech deployments in urban areas. Republicans argue that while law enforcement claims these systems aid crime-fighting, the indiscriminate data collection on law-abiding citizens raises Fourth Amendment red flags. Democrats and progressive cities typically embrace such surveillance tools as public safety measures, dismissing privacy concerns as obstacles to crime prevention.

The broader question: who owns your movement data, and what prevents government abuse? Conservatives warn that once normalized, mass surveillance infrastructure becomes a tool for political targeting and control—especially under administrations hostile to conservative speech and Second Amendment rights.

Watch whether Colorado law enforcement agencies expand Flock deployments and whether state legislators demand warrant requirements before deployment.
