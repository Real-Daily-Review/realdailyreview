# Denver Voters Elect Progressive Activist Mejía to City Council

Denver voters have handed another at-large City Council seat to progressive activist Melissa Mejía, continuing the city's leftward drift on taxes, regulation, and spending.

Mejía declared victory Tuesday night in the special election for an at-large seat, according to the *Denver Post*. Her win adds another voice to a council already dominated by Democrats pushing higher taxes, aggressive climate mandates, and expanded city programs—all funded by Colorado taxpayers and Denver businesses already burdened by some of the state's highest tax rates.

Conservatives and fiscal watchdogs worry the election signals Denver voters' appetite for more government expansion. With Mejía's addition, the council will likely accelerate spending on progressive priorities: homeless services, green energy mandates, and regulatory schemes that drive up costs for residents and employers alike.

Republicans argue Denver's trajectory—higher taxes, more regulation, declining quality of life—should alarm Colorado conservatives statewide. As Denver goes, so often follows the state. The city's embrace of progressive governance has coincided with rising homelessness, business flight, and deteriorating public safety.

Watch whether Mejía and allies push for new tax increases or spending measures in the coming budget cycle—and whether Colorado Republicans use Denver's struggles as a cautionary tale in 2026 statewide races.
