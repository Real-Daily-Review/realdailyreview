# Data Center Boom Hits Resistance as Colorado Weighs Growth vs. Skepticism

Colorado's data center expansion is accelerating—but so is public pushback, threatening to derail what could be a major economic driver for the state.

New facilities are moving forward rapidly across Colorado, yet a recent poll shows widespread voter skepticism about the projects, according to the *Colorado Sun*. Colorado Springs is hosting another contentious debate Thursday as communities grapple with the tradeoffs: data centers promise jobs and tax revenue, but residents worry about water consumption, power grid strain, and land use.

Conservatives and business groups argue data centers represent legitimate economic growth and private investment that shouldn't be strangled by regulatory delay. But environmental groups and some local officials counter that Colorado's water scarcity and energy grid can't sustain unchecked expansion—a concern that resonates with rural Coloradans already stretched thin by drought.

The real issue: will Colorado embrace market-driven development, or will progressive environmental regulations and local opposition kill deals that could diversify the state's economy beyond tourism and government spending? Data centers don't require subsidies; they generate property and sales tax revenue directly.

Watch whether the state legislature moves to streamline permitting or whether environmental groups successfully lobby for new restrictions that make Colorado less competitive than Texas or other data center hubs.
