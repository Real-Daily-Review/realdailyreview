# Warren Backs Trump on Defense Contractor Crackdown—But Conservatives Should Stay Skeptical

Elizabeth Warren's rare praise for a Trump executive order targeting defense contractor stock buybacks signals an unusual bipartisan moment—but conservatives shouldn't mistake it for alignment on free-market principles.

The Massachusetts Democrat lauded Trump's crackdown on defense contractors prioritizing shareholder payouts over weapons production, noting that the four largest contractors have cut buybacks by $2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (Fox News Politics). Warren wants Congress to make the order permanent.

Here's the catch: while the outcome—redirecting capital toward actual defense capability—may benefit national security, the mechanism is government mandate, not market discipline. Conservatives should note that Warren's support doesn't reflect a sudden conversion to limited government; she's backing executive overreach because she likes the outcome. That precedent cuts both ways.

For Colorado, defense contracting remains significant to the state economy. If permanent restrictions on corporate capital allocation become law, Colorado defense firms and their workers could face new operational constraints—regardless of whether those constraints boost production efficiency.

The real test: will Republicans insist on sunsetting this order and restoring corporate decision-making authority, or will they accept permanent government control of contractor finances because the current administration wields it?

Watch whether Congress moves to codify Trump's order and what safeguards—if any—protect private enterprise from future weaponization of executive power.
