# Amendment 87 Masks a Stealth Tax Trap for Colorado Middle Class

Colorado voters face a deceptively marketed tax hike disguised as "soak-the-rich" redistribution. Amendment 87 promises $2.7 billion annually by raising taxes on the top 3% of earners and 5% of businesses (Colorado Sun), but conservatives warn the real danger lies in bracket creep—inflation will gradually pull middle-class Coloradans into higher tax brackets, turning a targeted levy into a broad-based tax increase on working families.

**Republicans argue** the amendment's progressive framing obscures its long-term mechanics. Once indexed for inflation, the tax brackets will expand, ensnaring more earners over time and effectively raising taxes on all Coloradans, not just the wealthy (Complete Colorado). This is classic government bait-and-switch: promise relief for the middle class while expanding the tax base.

**Democrats counter** that the revenue is needed for education, transportation, and social programs—and that the wealthy should pay their "fair share."

For Colorado taxpayers and small-business owners, this matters enormously. A $2.7 billion annual grab funds government expansion and sets a precedent for future tax hikes. The question isn't whether the wealthy pay more today—it's whether your family pays more tomorrow.

Watch whether Colorado's business community and taxpayer groups mount an aggressive campaign against bracket creep before November's vote.
