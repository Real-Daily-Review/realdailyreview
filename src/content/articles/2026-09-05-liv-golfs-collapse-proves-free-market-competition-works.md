# LIV Golf's Collapse Shows Free Market Competition Works—Even When It Fails

Saudi-backed LIV Golf is imploding under mounting losses, lawsuits, and unpaid bills, but the failed venture accomplished what conservative free-market principles predict: it forced the entrenched PGA Tour to compete and reform (Washington Examiner).

LIV Golf's aggressive challenge exposed what critics call the PGA's complacency and "woke" corporate posturing. By flooding the market with Saudi capital and luring top players with massive contracts, LIV forced the Tour's hand—ultimately driving merger negotiations that reshaped professional golf's landscape. The tour now faces real pressure to innovate, improve purses, and listen to players and fans rather than rely on institutional monopoly power.

The collapse itself validates free-market dynamics: unsustainable business models fail, but the competitive pressure they create leaves lasting change. LIV's demise doesn't erase the fact that it shook a stale establishment and proved that money and disruption can force even entrenched institutions to adapt.

For Colorado golf fans and course operators, the consolidation of professional golf under a reformed PGA structure could mean clearer tournament scheduling and renewed investment in the sport—though the long-term competitive landscape remains unsettled.
