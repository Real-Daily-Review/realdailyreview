# Trump's Tariff Gamble Divides Right; Colorado Manufacturers Watch Closely

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire from within conservative ranks, even as his administration doubles down on trade protectionism. The "Liberation Day" tariffs announced in April 2025 sparked immediate backlash—approval ratings slid as economists and right-leaning commentators questioned the policy's logic and execution (Washington Examiner).

**The Conservative Split**

National Review argues Trump should adopt Reagan's Cold War "peace through strength" model: build military capacity first, negotiate from dominance later. But the Washington Examiner's right-wing critics contend Trump's tariff approach repeats failed strategies without clear justification, calling the rollout "chaotic" and the rationale "absurd."

**Colorado's Stake**

Colorado manufacturers, agricultural exporters, and energy companies face real exposure. Tariffs on imported materials raise production costs; retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods threaten markets for Colorado beef, aerospace components, and mining equipment. Small manufacturers already operating on thin margins could face layoffs or relocation.

Republicans broadly support fair-trade enforcement and countering China's unfair practices, but many worry blanket tariffs function as a hidden tax on American consumers and businesses—exactly the kind of government-imposed cost conservatives typically oppose.
