# Prescription Drug Prices Fall for First Time Since 1963—Market Competition, Not Government, Deserves Credit

Free-market competition, not government mandates, is driving the first decline in prescription drug prices since 1963, according to new Bureau of Labor Statistics data showing a more-than-3% average drop in 2025 (Washington Examiner).

The breakthrough reflects what conservatives have long argued: when companies compete to win customers, prices fall naturally. The emergence of GLP-1 drugs like Ozempic created genuine market pressure on pharmaceutical pricing, forcing competitors to innovate and reduce costs rather than rely on regulatory schemes or price controls.

This matters for Colorado families and seniors on fixed incomes already squeezed by inflation. Lower drug costs mean more money staying in household budgets—real relief without Washington bureaucrats rationing care or dictating which treatments insurers must cover.

The data also vindicate skeptics of the Biden administration's drug-price negotiation provisions, which conservatives warned would stifle innovation and reduce competition. Market forces, not government price-setting, delivered results.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado lawmakers resist the temptation to layer state-level drug-price controls that could reverse this trend and discourage pharmaceutical companies from investing in new treatments for Colorado patients.
