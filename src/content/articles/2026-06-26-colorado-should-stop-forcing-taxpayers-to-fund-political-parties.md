# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are bankrolling primary elections for political parties whether they belong to them or not—a government overreach conservatives say must end.

According to *Complete Colorado*, the state grants special recognition and privileges to political parties that amount to taxpayer-funded subsidies. The outlet argues Colorado government has "no proper business" extending such benefits, particularly when voters are compelled to finance primaries for parties they've never joined or actively oppose.

The core conservative complaint is straightforward: Why should a libertarian, independent, or Republican voter in Boulder be forced to pay for Democratic primary operations? Or vice versa? It's a textbook case of government picking winners and losers while raiding the public till.

*Complete Colorado* frames this as a "courageous" move toward genuine separation of state and party—treating political organizations like the private entities they are, rather than quasi-governmental arms deserving taxpayer subsidy.

The argument resonates with Colorado's libertarian streak and fiscal conservatives' broader push against wasteful spending. If parties want to hold primaries, they should fund them themselves through membership dues and donations.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado lawmakers take up legislation to end party-privilege subsidies, or if Democratic leadership blocks reform to protect their fundraising advantage.
