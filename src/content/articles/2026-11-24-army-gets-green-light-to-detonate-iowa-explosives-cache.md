# Army to Detonate Unstable Explosives in Iowa After Court Approval

The U.S. Army will proceed Wednesday with detonating a cache of unstable lead azide chemicals in Iowa after securing court approval and backing from both the state's Republican governor and Democratic governor-elect, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle announced the detonation follows a ruling from the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, which sided with the military's plan to safely dispose of the volatile materials. Both Gov. Kim Reynolds (R) and incoming Gov. Rob Sand (D) approved the operation, signaling rare bipartisan agreement on the hazardous-materials disposal.

The lead azide—a compound used in detonators and considered highly unstable—has posed a storage and safety risk. The Army determined controlled detonation was the safest removal method rather than attempting transportation or alternative disposal.

While the operation carries environmental and safety considerations, the court's approval and state-level consensus suggest the military's risk assessment prevailed over environmental concerns that may have initially delayed the process. The detonation reflects the tension between public safety and environmental caution that often stalls federal projects.
