# Cornell's Fraternity Ban Shows How Mob Rule Trumps Due Process at Elite Universities

Cornell University's sweeping ban on all fraternity parties and social events—announced in response to political pressure rather than deliberate policy review—reveals how even prestigious institutions have surrendered to outrage-driven governance, according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The university's panic-driven decision raises a fundamental question: if elite schools can't withstand political backlash without abandoning reasoned deliberation, what separates them from any other institution? Critics argue Cornell is punishing an entire community based on emotion rather than evidence, abandoning the intellectual rigor these universities claim to champion.

For Colorado families paying six-figure tuition bills at similar institutions, this matters. When universities prioritize appeasement over due process, they're teaching students that mob pressure—not facts or fairness—determines outcomes. That's not education; it's capitulation.

The broader concern: if Cornell can unilaterally restrict student association rights without transparent review, what's to stop other universities from similar overreach? Colorado parents and taxpayers should demand accountability from in-state universities about their own governance standards.

**Watch whether Colorado's higher-ed institutions adopt similar blanket restrictions, and whether trustees push back against administrative decisions made under political duress rather than through proper deliberation.**
