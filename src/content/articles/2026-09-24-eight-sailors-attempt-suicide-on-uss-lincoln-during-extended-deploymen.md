# Navy Grapples With Sailor Mental Health Crisis During Extended Deployment

Eight sailors attempted suicide aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln during a grueling 300-plus-day deployment to the Middle East, according to acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao, raising urgent questions about crew readiness and military personnel welfare.

The carrier strike group departed San Diego in November 2025 and faces another two weeks at sea before returning home—stretching an already extended mission that has strained sailors' mental health and morale. The suicide attempts underscore a persistent challenge: maintaining force readiness while protecting the wellbeing of personnel subjected to months-long operational tempo.

Republicans and military readiness advocates argue that extended deployments without adequate shore leave, mental health resources, and crew rotation cycles create dangerous conditions. The Navy must balance global commitments with the human cost of sustained operations. Some conservatives worry that budget constraints and personnel shortages are forcing the service to push sailors beyond sustainable limits.

Democrats typically emphasize the need for expanded mental health funding and support services across the military—though critics counter that throwing more money at the problem without addressing deployment length and crew rotation policies misses the root issue.

Colorado has significant military installations and a substantial veteran population; how the Navy addresses sailor mental health will affect recruitment, retention, and the wellbeing of service members from the state.

Watch whether Congress demands accountability on deployment schedules and whether the Navy commits to concrete changes in crew rotation policy.
