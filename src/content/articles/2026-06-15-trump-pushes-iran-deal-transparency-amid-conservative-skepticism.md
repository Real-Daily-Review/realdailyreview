# Trump's Iran Deal Faces Skepticism Over Transparency and Real Concessions

President Trump is heading into the G7 summit with a new Iran nuclear agreement in hand, but conservatives are demanding full disclosure of what the deal actually contains—and whether Tehran has made meaningful concessions on its weapons program.

Vice President Vance announced the administration aims to release the deal's text this week (Just The News), a move National Review argues is essential to cut through conflicting claims about what Trump and Iran actually agreed to. The transparency push reflects conservative concern that vague diplomacy with Iran has failed before.

Former President Obama has already cast doubt on whether this deal differs materially from the JCPOA that Trump withdrew from during his first term (Just The News). That skepticism mirrors broader Republican concerns: without hard evidence of Iranian nuclear rollbacks, any agreement risks becoming another toothless accord that enriches Tehran while leaving America's regional allies—and American interests—exposed.

Meanwhile, Trump arrives at the G7 facing trade tensions with allies over America First tariff policy, adding complexity to his diplomatic agenda (Fox News).

**Watch for:** whether the released text shows concrete Iranian concessions on uranium enrichment and inspections, or whether this becomes another diplomatic theater piece that conservatives will rightfully oppose.
