# House Ethics Committee Opens Probe Into Rep. Miller Over Domestic Violence Allegations

Colorado Republican Max Miller faces a House Ethics Committee investigation after his ex-wife, Emily Moreno, leveled domestic abuse allegations against him—a probe Miller himself requested hours after the allegations surfaced.

Miller, who represents a competitive Colorado district, has pledged full cooperation with the committee's inquiry. According to *Just The News*, the investigation centers on whether Miller "may have engaged in domestic violence and abuse." The timing is significant: Miller's preemptive request for the probe suggests a strategy to control the narrative and demonstrate transparency rather than face a committee-initiated investigation.

The case underscores the political vulnerability facing Republicans in swing districts. Miller's district has trended competitive in recent cycles, and personal conduct allegations—regardless of their merit—can energize opposition campaigns and demoralize base voters heading into election season.

Republicans have long argued that ethics investigations can become weaponized against political opponents, particularly when timing coincides with electoral cycles. Miller's decision to request the investigation himself may insulate him from that criticism, but the probe's outcome will likely influence both his political standing and donor confidence.

Conservatives and Colorado taxpayers should monitor whether the committee's investigation concludes swiftly or drags into the campaign season—a delay that could prove devastating to Miller's reelection prospects.
