# U.S. and Iran Explore Deal to End Conflict as Oil Markets React

The U.S. and Iran are discussing a potential deal to end their conflict, with Pakistan expressing optimism about near-term progress, according to NPR. Iran is currently assessing a U.S. administration proposal, while President Trump has threatened to resume attacks if negotiations fail, according to Deutsche Welle and NPR.

Trump has expressed optimism about reaching an agreement, though Tehran has not yet formally responded to the latest proposal, according to Deutsche Welle. The potential deal has already moved markets: oil prices fell below $100 per barrel on hopes of a resolution, according to the Financial Times.

The negotiations involve complex geopolitical considerations. PBS NewsHour convened experts including Alan Eyre, a former senior member of the Obama administration's Iran nuclear deal negotiating team, and Miad Maleki, a senior fellow at the Foundation for the Defense of Democracies, to examine the proposal's prospects and implications.

The timeline remains unclear, with Pakistan's optimism about "soon" contrasting with Iran's ongoing review process. Watch for Iran's formal response to the U.S. proposal and any shifts in Trump's negotiating posture.
