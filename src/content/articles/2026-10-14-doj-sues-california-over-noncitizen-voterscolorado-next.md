# DOJ Cracks Down on California's Voter Roll Chaos—Colorado Should Take Note

The Justice Department is threatening to sue California over noncitizens on voter rolls, demanding the state review a list of suspected ineligible voters within 48 hours (9News Denver, Just The News). The move signals federal willingness to enforce election integrity standards that California has resisted.

Republicans argue that noncitizen voting undermines the fundamental principle of one citizen, one vote. The DOJ action reflects growing conservative concern that states with lax voter verification—particularly California—are creating pathways for illegal aliens to cast ballots, whether intentionally or through administrative negligence.

Democrats and California officials have historically downplayed noncitizen voting as rare, resisting voter ID requirements and strict roll maintenance. They frame such enforcement as voter suppression.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's voter rolls have faced similar scrutiny. State Republicans have pushed for stricter citizenship verification and ID requirements at the ballot box, while Democrats have resisted, citing accessibility concerns. If the DOJ prevails against California, it could embolden Colorado Republicans to demand tighter election security measures—and pressure Democratic Secretary of State Jena Griswold to audit Colorado's own voter rolls for noncitizens.

Watch whether Colorado's election officials voluntarily tighten citizenship verification or whether federal pressure becomes necessary.
