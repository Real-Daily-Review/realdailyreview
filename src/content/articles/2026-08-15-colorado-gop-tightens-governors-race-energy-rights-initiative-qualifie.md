# Colorado Republicans Narrow Gubernatorial Field; Energy Rights Initiative Heads to Ballot

Colorado Republicans have winnowed their 2026 gubernatorial race to just two candidates through the party assembly process, tightening the primary field significantly. Meanwhile, voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a constitutional "right to natural gas" after Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado, qualified for the ballot (Denver Post).

The energy measure reflects conservative pushback against what Republicans view as overregulation of Colorado's natural gas sector. The initiative would constitutionally protect access to natural gas—a critical issue for Colorado's energy economy and rural households dependent on gas heating and power generation. Democrats and environmental groups oppose the measure, arguing it would hamstring climate policy and lock in fossil fuel dependence.

The gubernatorial assembly results signal Republicans are consolidating around establishment-favored candidates, potentially sidelining grassroots challengers. This consolidation comes as Colorado Republicans prepare for a competitive general election in a state trending purple.

**What conservatives should watch:** whether Initiative 177 gains traction with suburban and rural Colorado voters, and whether the GOP's narrowed primary field produces a nominee capable of challenging Democratic control of the statehouse.
