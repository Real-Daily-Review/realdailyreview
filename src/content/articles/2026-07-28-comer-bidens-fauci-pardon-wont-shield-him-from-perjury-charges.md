# Biden's Fauci Pardon Won't Stop Congressional Accountability, Comer Warns

Rep. James Comer is putting Dr. Anthony Fauci on notice: President Biden's pardon doesn't give him a free pass to mislead Congress.

Ahead of Fauci's Senate hearing, the House Oversight Committee chairman warned that the former NIH director must still answer for his role in pandemic policy—and face perjury charges if he lies under oath (Fox News). Comer's message is clear: a presidential pardon covers criminal liability, not congressional testimony. Conservatives have long argued that Fauci misled lawmakers about gain-of-function research funding and the origins of COVID-19, making this hearing a critical moment to establish the factual record.

Meanwhile, Sen. Eric Schmitt is pushing Congress to rein in what he calls the Supreme Court's "ridiculous interpretation" of the Constitution, particularly on the 14th Amendment and birthright citizenship (Daily Caller). Schmitt argues the Court has strayed from the Constitution's original meaning and that lawmakers must reassert their authority to correct judicial overreach.

Both moves reflect Republican frustration with executive and judicial power unchecked by accountability. For Colorado taxpayers and voters, the Fauci hearing offers a chance to see whether pandemic decisions that upended schools, businesses, and livelihoods will finally face serious scrutiny.

Watch whether Fauci's testimony produces new evidence of misleading Congress—or whether the pardon's shadow still protects him from real consequences.
