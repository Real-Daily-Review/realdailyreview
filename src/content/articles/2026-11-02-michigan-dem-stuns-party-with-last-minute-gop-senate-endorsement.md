# Dem Rep Haley Stevens' Last-Minute Rogers Endorsement Exposes Left's Fracture on Election Eve

Michigan Democrat Haley Stevens blindsided her own party hours before Election Day by endorsing Republican Mike Rogers over progressive Abdul El-Sayed in the state's open Senate race—a defection that triggered fury from Chuck Schumer, Hakeem Jeffries, and the activist left (Fox News).

Stevens told fellow Democrat Debbie Dingell at midnight that the Rogers endorsement was merely a rumor. Five hours later, she stood beside Rogers at a Detroit church (Fox News). The move signals deep Democratic divisions over El-Sayed's progressive agenda and suggests Stevens—a moderate—concluded Rogers was the safer choice for Michigan voters.

Top Democrats erupted. Schumer and Jeffries unleashed public criticism, while radical left-wing voices including activist Cenk Uygur condemned the switch (Fox News). The Washington Examiner noted that Michigan newspapers buried the story, depriving readers of crucial pre-election information.

The endorsement reflects a broader pattern: union leaders and moderate Democrats are breaking ranks with progressive orthodoxy. The Teamsters president recently endorsed Republican Ashley Moody in Florida's Senate race, another sign organized labor is abandoning its traditional Democratic moorings (Fox News).
