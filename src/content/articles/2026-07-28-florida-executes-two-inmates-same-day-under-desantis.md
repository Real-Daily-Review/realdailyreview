# Florida Set for Rare Double Execution as DeSantis Pushes Death Penalty Cases

Florida will carry out its first double execution in nearly a decade Tuesday, with two inmates scheduled to die by lethal injection hours apart—a rare move that underscores Gov. Ron DeSantis' aggressive approach to capital punishment cases (The Daily Wire).

The executions mark the first time since 2017 that any state has put two people to death on the same day (9News Denver). DeSantis' administration has moved death penalty cases at what observers describe as an "unprecedented rate," signaling a hardline stance on capital punishment that contrasts sharply with progressive states moving toward abolition.

**The conservative case:** Republicans and death penalty advocates argue swift execution of convicted murderers serves justice for victims' families and deters violent crime. DeSantis' willingness to move cases forward reflects voter support in red states for law-and-order governance.

**The left's position:** Death penalty opponents argue executions are irreversible, disproportionately affect minorities, and don't deter crime—a view gaining ground in Democratic-led states that have abolished or paused capital punishment.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado abolished capital punishment in 2020, making it one of 23 states without the death penalty. The state's progressive shift on executions contrasts with neighboring red states' enforcement.

Watch whether other Republican-led states accelerate death penalty cases following Florida's precedent, and whether the issue resurfaces in Colorado's 2026 gubernatorial race.
