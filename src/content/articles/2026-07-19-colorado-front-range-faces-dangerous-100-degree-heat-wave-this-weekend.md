# Colorado Front Range Braces for Dangerous Heat Wave This Weekend

Extreme heat is bearing down on Colorado's Front Range, with temperatures expected to approach 100 degrees Sunday and Monday, prompting heat advisories across the Denver metro and northern plains.

The National Weather Service has issued a Heat Advisory for the Interstate 25 corridor and adjacent plains (9News Denver), with forecasters warning of dangerously hot conditions both days (KDVR Fox31). Denver and surrounding areas face what meteorologists are calling a "Pinpoint Weather Alert Day" as the heat intensifies.

For Colorado families and businesses, the timing matters. Peak summer heat strains the electrical grid precisely when demand spikes—a reminder of why reliable, affordable energy matters. Colorado's power grid relies on a diverse mix of sources, and extreme weather events test that capacity. Conservatives have long argued that premature retirement of coal and natural gas plants in pursuit of aggressive renewable mandates leaves the state vulnerable during peak demand periods.

Residents should take standard precautions: stay hydrated, limit outdoor activity during peak afternoon hours, check on elderly neighbors, and never leave children or pets in vehicles. Heat-related illness can strike fast.

Watch whether Colorado's grid operators need to issue conservation alerts or demand-response calls—a telling indicator of whether the state's energy transition is outpacing reliable baseload capacity.
