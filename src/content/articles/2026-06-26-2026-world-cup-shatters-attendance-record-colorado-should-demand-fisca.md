# 2026 World Cup Smashes Attendance Record—But Colorado Taxpayers Should Demand Accountability

America's 2026 World Cup is drawing massive crowds—over 3.6 million fans have already packed stadiums before the group stage even concludes, shattering the all-time tournament attendance record (9News Denver, Breitbart). The expanded 48-team format is proving a commercial juggernaut, with venues running near capacity across multiple matches still to be played.

The success is undeniable. But conservatives should ask hard questions about what Colorado taxpayers funded. Host cities and states typically absorb infrastructure costs, security upgrades, and public services for major tournaments—expenses that often exceed initial projections. Denver and other Colorado venues may have committed public dollars to accommodate the influx, and voters deserve transparency on the final bill.

Republicans have long warned that mega-events can become taxpayer-funded corporate windfalls. While FIFA and tournament organizers pocket billions in broadcast and sponsorship revenue, local governments often eat the costs of roads, parking, police, and emergency services.

The attendance numbers prove demand exists. The real question: Did Colorado negotiate hard enough to ensure FIFA and participating teams, not taxpayers, covered the public cost of hosting?

Watch for final accounting on Colorado's World Cup expenses—and whether state lawmakers demand similar fiscal discipline for future bids.
