# Denver's Park Hill July 4th Parade Goes Forward While D.C. Cancels Over Heat Concerns

While Washington bureaucrats canceled the nation's capital July 4th parade citing "extreme heat," Denver's Park Hill neighborhood is moving ahead with its 16th annual celebration—a reminder that Colorado communities know how to handle summer weather without surrendering tradition.

The Daily Caller reports that D.C. officials scrapped their parade, claiming temperatures would exceed those in 99% of the world. It's the kind of risk-averse government overreach that has become routine in the progressive Northeast: cancel the event, blame the weather, move on.

Not so in Colorado. Denver's Park Hill parade will stream on 9News, giving Coloradans a chance to celebrate Independence Day the way it's meant to be—outdoors, with neighbors, and without waiting for a government agency's permission slip.

The contrast is instructive. One city lets heat forecasts dictate civic life; another adapts and carries on. Colorado's independent streak—whether it's ranchers managing drought, energy workers building prosperity, or communities refusing to let weather bureaucrats kill tradition—remains a defining strength.

Watch whether other major U.S. cities follow D.C.'s playbook of cancellation or Colorado's example of resilience.
