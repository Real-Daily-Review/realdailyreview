# Democratic Gains in GOP-Drawn Districts Signal Trouble for Republicans Heading Into 2026

Democrats are flipping Republican-drawn congressional seats, a warning sign for GOP mapmakers banking on redistricting to lock in majorities. In Texas's redrawn 35th District, Democrat Johnny Garcia defeated Trump-backed Republican Carlos De La Cruz, defying the GOP's post-2020 redistricting strategy (Fox News). Meanwhile, in California's 6th District, Democrat Richard Pan—a vocal advocate for eliminating religious vaccine exemptions—ousted independent Kevin Kiley with 57.5% of the vote (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative concern:** Republicans invested heavily in redistricting after 2020, expecting durable electoral advantages. These losses suggest demographic shifts and voter realignment are outpacing map-drawing gains. For Colorado, where Democrats control the redistricting commission, Republicans face an even steeper climb in competitive districts like CO-3 and CO-8.

Pan's victory carries particular sting for conservatives: his national profile centers on vaccine mandate expansion and eroding parental choice—policies Colorado progressives have eyed for years. If Democrats weaponize similar mandates in state races, Colorado families could face the same regulatory overreach California pioneered.

**Watch next:** Whether Republicans adjust 2026 strategy away from redistricting-dependent holds and toward candidate quality and message discipline in purple districts.
