# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Colorado Mountain Town

The Aspen Acres fire has crippled water utilities serving Beulah, a small Colorado community of roughly 800 residents, forcing water managers into emergency recovery mode while families rebuild from catastrophic losses (Colorado Sun). Unlike most active fires across the state, this blaze directly damaged essential infrastructure—a stark reminder that Colorado's water systems remain vulnerable to the growing wildfire threat.

Residents of the close-knit mountain town are now facing a dual crisis: displaced families and compromised access to clean water, one of the most basic services government is supposed to protect. Water managers are assessing damage and beginning the long process of restoring service (CPR News), but the timeline remains unclear.

This disaster underscores a conservative concern: Colorado's over-reliance on government-managed utilities and insufficient investment in hardening critical infrastructure against natural disasters. While environmental regulations have constrained forest management and fuel reduction efforts for decades, communities like Beulah pay the price when fires inevitably strike.

Taxpayers should watch whether state and local officials will finally prioritize aggressive forest thinning and fuel reduction—or continue blaming climate while utilities remain exposed to preventable destruction.
