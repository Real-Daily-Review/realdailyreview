# Gun Incidents Highlight Colorado's Enforcement Gaps

A year after the Evergreen High School shooting, Colorado authorities have filed no charges against the teenage shooter's parents—despite renewed scrutiny over gun storage laws. According to CPR News, prosecutors determined there wasn't sufficient evidence to pursue charges, leaving questions about parental responsibility and safe firearm handling unanswered.

Meanwhile, a Colorado middle school's "Teacher of the Year" faces accusations of shooting two men in the street, the New York Post reports. Police say he exited his vehicle and fired multiple shots before fleeing.

**The conservative takeaway:** These cases expose a troubling pattern. Rather than enforce existing laws and hold individuals accountable—whether parents or armed citizens—Colorado's progressive establishment reflexively pushes for new gun restrictions that burden law-abiding owners. The Evergreen case shows prosecutors already struggle to prove negligence under current statutes. The teacher incident demonstrates that background checks and licensing don't prevent criminals or unstable individuals from committing violence.

Republicans argue Colorado needs *better enforcement* of laws on the books, not more regulations that disarm responsible citizens. Conservatives point out that gun storage mandates and expanded liability schemes primarily penalize families who follow the rules while criminals ignore them entirely.
