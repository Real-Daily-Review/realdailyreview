# Colorado Voters Approve New Sales Tax for Passenger Rail Expansion

Colorado taxpayers just approved a permanent new sales tax to fund an expanding passenger rail network they didn't ask for and won't use.

Ballot Issue 7A passed this week, imposing a third of a penny sales tax across the Front Range to fund the Colorado Connector rail district (CPR News). The measure will finance expansion to Pueblo, increased service frequency, and a new station in Trinidad, according to the rail district's post-election statements (KDVR).

Conservatives have long warned that rail subsidies represent government picking winners and losers in transportation—forcing car-driving taxpayers to fund a service most Coloradans won't ride. The permanent nature of this tax means the burden falls indefinitely on Colorado families and businesses already squeezed by inflation and rising costs of living.

The rail district is now mapping implementation, but questions linger: Will ridership justify the ongoing tax? Will the Pueblo and Trinidad expansions prove economically viable, or will they become money-losing operations sustained only by perpetual tax revenue? Colorado's energy sector and rural communities—already bearing regulatory costs—will shoulder this new burden alongside Front Range commuters who may never step aboard.

Watch whether the rail district delivers promised service levels and ridership projections, or whether this becomes another cautionary tale of government overreach funded by permanent taxation.
