# Trump's Self-Inflicted Ruin Hands Congress to Democrats

President Trump's political dominance has collapsed under the weight of his own missteps, handing Democrats control of both chambers of Congress and setting the stage for two years of investigations and gridlock that will cripple any conservative agenda.

Conservative outlets are blunt in their post-mortem. National Review argues Trump has been "the author of his own ruin," while the Washington Examiner's contributors acknowledge Republicans squandered what should have been a "red wave." One Examiner columnist admitted he was wrong to predict GOP gains, noting the party lost the midterms despite favorable conditions—a failure Trump's erratic messaging and off-topic tangents made inevitable.

**The Colorado angle:** With Democrats now controlling Congress, Colorado's energy sector faces renewed pressure from the left on oil and gas development, while gun owners can expect aggressive federal anti-Second Amendment legislation. State Republicans will find little help from Washington on water rights disputes or regulatory overreach from federal agencies.

Republicans argue Trump's refusal to stay disciplined on core economic and border issues—instead dominating news cycles with personal grievances—demoralized voters and handed Democrats a lifeline. Conservatives counter that the party's establishment failed to rally behind Trump's agenda effectively.

Meanwhile, Trump insists the Iran war will end "whenever I want it to" (Breitbart), signaling continued foreign entanglement that will drain resources from domestic priorities.

**Watch:** Whether Colorado Republicans distance themselves from Trump's brand heading into 2028, or double down on his approach.
