# Trump Orders DOJ Probe Into Oil Companies Over Gas Prices

President Trump is taking aim at energy companies for allegedly price gouging consumers, ordering the Department of Justice to investigate why gasoline prices aren't dropping in line with falling crude oil costs (Washington Examiner, New York Post). Trump accused "big oil companies" of failing to pass savings to consumers and demanded prices fall "quickly."

The move puts Trump at odds with his traditional energy-sector allies—a notable shift for a president who has championed domestic oil production and deregulation. Conservatives argue that market forces, not government investigations, should determine pricing; heavy-handed DOJ probes risk chilling investment in American energy production at a time when energy independence remains critical to national security and Colorado's economy.

Colorado's oil and gas industry, which supports thousands of jobs and generates substantial state revenue, could face regulatory headwinds if the investigation expands into broader antitrust or pricing-control measures. Energy companies argue that refining capacity constraints, supply chain costs, and geopolitical factors—not collusion—explain the lag between crude prices and pump prices.

Republicans and industry groups will likely push back against expansive government intervention in energy markets, warning that price controls or punitive investigations discourage the capital investment needed to keep American energy competitive.

Watch whether the DOJ investigation leads to formal antitrust action or new price-regulation proposals that could reshape Colorado's energy sector.
