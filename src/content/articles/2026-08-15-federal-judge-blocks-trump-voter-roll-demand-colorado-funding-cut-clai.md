# Trump Admin Loses Colorado Voter Roll Lawsuit; Funding Cut Claims Emerge

A federal judge has rejected the Trump administration's attempt to obtain Colorado's unredacted voter rolls, dealing a legal setback to the White House effort (Denver Post). The ruling comes as a critical email surfaced in court suggesting the administration may have targeted Colorado with funding cuts after Gov. Jared Polis refused to pardon election clerk Tina Peters (9News Denver).

Republicans argue the voter roll request was necessary for election integrity verification, while Colorado election officials maintained the unredacted data posed security and privacy risks. The judge sided with state officials.

The funding cut allegations, if substantiated, would represent a troubling abuse of federal power—using taxpayer dollars as leverage against states that refuse executive clemency requests. Colorado conservatives should scrutinize whether this reflects broader weaponization of federal agencies against Republican-aligned causes or legitimate policy disagreements.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses dependent on federal funding, the stakes are clear: if the administration is indeed retaliating against the state, it sets a dangerous precedent for politicizing federal dollars. The court-ordered document release may reveal the full scope of that alleged targeting.
