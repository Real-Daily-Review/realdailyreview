# Colorado Election Chaos Exposes Need for Reform

Colorado's election system is broken, and two recent fiascos prove it. Secretary of State Jena Griswold's mishandling of the primary process—where a crowded field allowed candidates to win with tiny vote shares despite broader voter preference—has sparked calls for electoral reform (Complete Colorado). Meanwhile, Republican gubernatorial candidate Victor Marx faces allegations from Griswold's own office that his campaign accepted over $78,000 in improper donations, exceeding individual contribution limits of $1,450 across at least 197 donations from 111 donors (Denver Post).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue Colorado's primary system and lax enforcement create perverse incentives. A candidate can capture the nomination with 20-30% support when another contender might be preferred by a majority—a recipe for general-election losses and unaccountable nominees. On campaign finance, conservatives note the irony: Griswold's office alleges violations while her own mismanagement of the primary process undermined voter intent.

**Democrats counter** that Colorado's system reflects voter choice and that enforcement ensures compliance.

The real issue for Colorado taxpayers: weak primary mechanics and inconsistent enforcement breed distrust in elections and empower fringe candidates. Watch whether the legislature pursues ranked-choice voting or runoff reforms—and whether Griswold's office applies equal scrutiny to Democratic campaigns.
