# Firefighting Plane Crashes in Colorado Reservoir During Gold Mountain Fire Battle

A water-dropping aircraft fighting the Gold Mountain fire near Ouray crashed into Silver Jack Reservoir Sunday evening, prompting a dive team search for the pilot. The Gunnison County Sheriff's Office reported the crash around 5 p.m. as thousands of personnel continued battling multiple blazes across Colorado, including new fires reported near Steamboat Springs.

The incident underscores the dangerous work of aerial firefighting operations—and raises questions about whether Colorado's wildfire response is adequately resourced and coordinated. While crews have made progress slowing some fires, the state continues deploying aircraft and ground personnel across multiple active incidents, straining resources that grow more precious each season.

Conservatives have long argued that Colorado's forest management failures—driven by decades of environmental litigation blocking timber harvests and fuel reduction—leave the state vulnerable to catastrophic fires that no amount of emergency spending can fully contain. Rather than endless firefighting expenditures, Republicans contend the state should prioritize preventive forest thinning and controlled burns on public lands, removing the regulatory and legal barriers that environmental groups have erected.

The crash also highlights the human cost of wildfire season in Colorado. Taxpayers should watch whether state and federal officials move to streamline forest management approval processes or continue deferring to litigation-prone environmental reviews.
