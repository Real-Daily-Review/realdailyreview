# Colorado Ballot Measures Face Scrutiny as Election Nears

Colorado voters are heading into the final stretch before Election Day with two major ballot measures drawing sharp debate—and, conservatives argue, incomplete media coverage.

**Amendment 87**, a tax-increase proposal, is getting short shrift from Colorado's mainstream media outlets, according to *Complete Colorado*. Voters deserve full transparency on the fiscal impact before deciding whether to hike taxes, conservatives contend—a point the state's largest newsrooms have allegedly glossed over in their coverage.

Meanwhile, **Amendment 81** would chip away at Colorado's sanctuary-law restrictions, allowing limited cooperation between local law enforcement and federal immigration authorities. The *Denver Post* reports the measure has drawn mixed reactions even from some law enforcement officials, though critics warn it could enable racial profiling and other civil-liberties risks.

**The conservative take:** Republicans and taxpayer advocates say Colorado media has a duty to spell out exactly what these measures cost and what they change—not bury the details. On Amendment 81, conservatives argue that reasonable cooperation with federal immigration enforcement is common sense; the left frames it as a civil-rights threat.

**What to watch:** Whether Colorado voters get the full fiscal and policy details on both measures before casting ballots—and whether the state's press corps steps up to deliver them.
