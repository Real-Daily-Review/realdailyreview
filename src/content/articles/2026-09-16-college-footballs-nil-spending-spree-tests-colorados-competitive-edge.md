# College Football's Roster Arms Race: Market Forces, Not Government Meddling

College football programs are locked in a spending war over athlete compensation, with Ohio State leading the pack according to a report from The Athletic cited by 9News Denver. Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL) deals have unleashed unprecedented roster costs across top programs.

Conservatives argue the NIL landscape represents free-market competition working as intended. As Daily Caller columnist Ron Hart notes, NIL operates as a legitimate market mechanism—schools and boosters compete for talent through voluntary transactions, not government mandates or NCAA price controls. This is capitalism functioning without bureaucratic interference.

The concern for Colorado taxpayers and CU Boulder boosters: as elite programs spend record sums, mid-tier schools face pressure to keep pace or fall behind competitively. Colorado's flagship university must decide whether to match spending or accept a competitive disadvantage in recruiting.

The real issue isn't that programs spend too much—it's whether state-funded universities should be funneling resources into athlete compensation while tuition rises and academic programs face budget cuts. That's a question for Colorado's Board of Regents and state legislators.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers attempt to regulate or tax NIL deals, or whether they'll let market forces sort out college football's spending hierarchy.
