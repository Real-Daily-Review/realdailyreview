# America's 250th Birthday Raises Questions About National Direction

As America approaches its 250th anniversary in 2026, conservatives are asking whether the nation still embodies the principles of limited government and free enterprise that built it. The milestone arrives amid debate over what "American success" actually means—and whether current policies celebrate or punish it.

National Review questions whether 2026 truly marks America's 250th year, citing constitutional scholar Willmoore Kendall's arguments about the nation's actual founding date. Meanwhile, The Daily Wire highlights how major private achievements—like SpaceX's forthcoming IPO, positioned as the largest in history and open to retail investors—face cultural and regulatory headwinds. The outlet frames this as symptomatic of what it calls "America's war on success," where entrepreneurship and wealth creation face mounting skepticism from progressive policymakers.

The Washington Examiner draws a sharper contrast, recalling the 1876 Centennial Exhibition in Philadelphia, when nearly 10 million Americans celebrated a century of achievement in agriculture, machinery, mining, and invention. That celebration of industrial progress and American ingenuity stands in stark relief to today's regulatory environment, where energy development, manufacturing, and business expansion face constant obstruction.

For Colorado—a state built on mining, ranching, and energy—the question is whether 2026 will mark a return to celebrating productive enterprise or continued hostility toward the industries that built the state's prosperity.
