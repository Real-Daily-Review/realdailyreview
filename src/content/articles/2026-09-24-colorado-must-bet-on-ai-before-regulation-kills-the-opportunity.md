# Colorado Should Seize AI Opportunity Before Regulation Strangles It

Colorado has a genuine chance to lead in artificial intelligence workforce development—but only if state leaders resist the urge to over-regulate the sector before it takes root.

The Colorado Sun recently pitched AI-enabled apprenticeships as a path for young workers to gain real skills under supervision and clear standards. That's sensible. Meanwhile, Complete Colorado reports that a Magellan Strategies survey shows Coloradans are ready to embrace AI opportunity rather than panic about it—a refreshing contrast to the doom-mongering from progressive activists.

But there's a broader threat looming. As The Daily Wire warns, America risks becoming dependent on foreign AI infrastructure the way it did with rare earths and pharmaceuticals. If Colorado doesn't cultivate homegrown AI talent and competitive data-center capacity now, the state will cede economic advantage to coastal tech hubs and—worse—to China.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Colorado's workforce, energy availability, and business climate make it ideal for AI development. Apprenticeships and skills training work. What doesn't work is the regulatory creep that typically follows new industries—unnecessary licensing, data-privacy mandates, and carbon restrictions that drive investment elsewhere.

Democrats will likely push for heavy-handed AI oversight in the name of "safety" and "equity." Coloradans should watch whether the state legislature resists that impulse or surrenders to it.
