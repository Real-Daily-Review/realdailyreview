# Denver's Late Snow Streak: What's Behind the Unusual Pattern

Denver is on track to break its second-latest snowfall record—a reminder that Colorado's weather remains gloriously unpredictable, regardless of what climate models predict.

Saturday's snow blanketed the Foothills and Front Range, leaving Denver itself dry but pushing the Mile High City closer to a historic milestone (KDVR Fox31). The city currently sits in third place for latest seasonal snow; if precipitation falls before the current record date, Denver will claim the second-latest snow on record.

A Winter Weather Advisory remains in effect for the mountains Monday, with light snow expected in higher elevations, according to 9News Denver. Warmer, drier conditions are forecast for midweek before another storm system arrives Friday.

The pattern underscores Colorado's natural climate volatility—something worth remembering when policymakers invoke weather extremes to justify expansive regulations or energy mandates. Late snows and variable conditions have always been part of the high plains and mountain experience. Coloradans should remain skeptical of claims that any single weather event proves the need for costly government intervention in energy markets or land use.
