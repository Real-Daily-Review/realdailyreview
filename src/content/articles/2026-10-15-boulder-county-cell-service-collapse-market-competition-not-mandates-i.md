# Cell Service Collapse in Boulder County Demands Accountability—Not More Government

Colorado's Boulder County is experiencing a cell service crisis that Rep. Joe Neguse and the state's congressional delegation are now demanding federal agencies and carriers fix. Residents report calls won't connect and data won't load—a serious problem in an area where reliable communications can mean the difference between life and death.

Neguse is calling for an end to "finger-pointing" between federal regulators and telecom carriers, urging them to meet and restore service (9News Denver). The congressman's frustration is understandable, but conservatives should ask the hard question: *Why* is federal intervention necessary to compel private carriers to fix their own networks?

The real issue here isn't lack of government pressure—it's whether carriers face sufficient market incentives to maintain reliable infrastructure. If Boulder County customers can switch providers or demand service improvements through competition, that's the solution. If regulatory barriers prevent new competitors from entering the market or building infrastructure, *that's* the problem Washington should address.

The cell service outage also raises safety concerns: in emergencies, dead networks cost lives. This is precisely why Colorado needs robust, competitive telecom markets—not more federal mandates that often entrench incumbent carriers and slow innovation.

Watch whether Neguse pushes for deregulation to enable competition, or simply demands carriers comply with existing rules.
