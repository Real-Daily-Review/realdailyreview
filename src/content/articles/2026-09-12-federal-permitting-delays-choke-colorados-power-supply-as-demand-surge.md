# America Has the Power—Washington Won't Let Colorado Use It

Federal permitting gridlock is strangling America's electricity supply just as demand surges, threatening Colorado's energy independence and economic growth.

The U.S. faces a 25% spike in power demand over the next decade, driven by domestic manufacturing expansion, artificial intelligence, and electrification of heating and transportation (Washington Examiner). Yet the grid infrastructure to meet that demand sits bottlenecked in federal red tape.

Conservatives argue that outdated permitting rules and interconnection delays are the real energy crisis—not scarcity of generation capacity. The problem isn't that America lacks power; it's that Washington's regulatory maze prevents utilities and private developers from connecting new generation to the grid fast enough. Colorado, a major energy producer with vast renewable and natural gas resources, stands to lose competitive advantage if federal permitting delays push investment and manufacturing elsewhere.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that lengthy reviews protect ecosystems and grid stability. But Republicans say those concerns can be addressed through streamlined, not eliminated, permitting—and that delay itself destabilizes the grid by creating artificial scarcity.

The stakes are clear: states that can rapidly deploy new generation will attract manufacturing jobs and lower energy costs. Those trapped in federal bureaucracy will see both flee.

Watch whether Congress acts on permitting reform before the next recession forces the issue.
