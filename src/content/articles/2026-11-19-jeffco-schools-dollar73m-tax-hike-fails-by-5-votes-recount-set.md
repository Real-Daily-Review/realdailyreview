# Jeffco Schools' $73M Tax Hike Fails by Five Votes—Recount Looms

Colorado taxpayers just barely dodged a $73 million tax increase in Jefferson County. Ballot Issue 5A, a mill levy override for Jeffco Public Schools—Colorado's second-largest K-12 district—failed by just five votes out of 301,789 cast, according to the *Denver Post*. A recount is now underway.

The razor-thin margin exposes deep voter skepticism about throwing more money at public education without meaningful reform. Conservatives argue that Colorado schools already consume substantial tax revenue; the problem isn't funding but how districts spend it and whether they're accountable to parents.

The defeat also reflects a broader national pattern. As the *Washington Examiner* notes, Democrats nationwide are moving to restrict school choice—blocking charter schools and trapping low-income families in failing public systems. Colorado has a robust charter sector; any effort to starve it of resources would harm the very students progressives claim to champion.

Jeffco voters sent a message: before asking for more tax dollars, school boards must demonstrate fiscal discipline, academic results, and respect for parental input. The recount will determine if that message sticks—or if a handful of votes flip the outcome and open the door to higher property taxes across the county.

Watch whether Jeffco's board doubles down on tax hikes or finally embraces performance-based accountability.
