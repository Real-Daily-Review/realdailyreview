# Trump Warms to Zelensky as Ukraine War Drags On—But Colorado Conservatives Should Demand Clarity on U.S. Costs

President Trump has shifted his stance on Ukraine, meeting with President Zelensky at the White House and signaling improved relations, according to *National Review* and the *Washington Examiner*. The warming comes as Ukraine faces sustained Russian attacks and desperately needs continued American support.

The timing is significant: Trump received Zelensky shortly before Sen. Lindsey Graham's funeral. Graham, a longtime Ukraine hawk, had spent his final days negotiating a Russia sanctions package with the White House before his sudden death following a trip to Kyiv.

For Colorado taxpayers and conservatives, this development raises a critical question: **What are the actual terms and costs of continued U.S. involvement?** Republicans have long argued that open-ended foreign aid commitments drain resources needed at home—for border security, infrastructure, and deficit reduction. Trump's initial skepticism about Ukraine spending reflected that concern.

While conservatives recognize the strategic importance of containing Russian aggression, they demand accountability. Every dollar sent abroad is a dollar not spent on Colorado schools, roads, or tax relief. The improved Trump-Zelensky relationship could mean either a sustainable, clearly-defined commitment or a return to blank-check spending.

Watch for details on any new aid package or sanctions framework—and whether Congress demands a real exit strategy.
