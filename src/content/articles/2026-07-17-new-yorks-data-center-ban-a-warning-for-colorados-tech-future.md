# New York's Data Center Ban Shows Why Colorado Must Protect Tech Growth

New York Governor Kathy Hochul's one-year moratorium on data center construction is a cautionary tale for Colorado—and a reminder that progressive hostility to infrastructure can cripple economic competitiveness.

Hochul's ban, announced amid environmental concerns, represents what critics call capitulation to "Luddism and hysteria," according to National Review. Washington Examiner columnist Joe Concha argued there is "literally almost no downside" to data centers, noting that New York's restrictions will cause the state to fall further behind economically.

Data centers are critical infrastructure for AI development, cloud computing, and tech jobs. They generate tax revenue, create employment, and attract high-wage industries—precisely what struggling post-industrial regions need. Yet progressive governors increasingly treat them as environmental villains, prioritizing activist pressure over economic reality.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado has positioned itself as a tech hub with reasonable permitting and pro-business energy policy. If Hochul's approach spreads to Democratic-led western states, Colorado could lose competitive advantage. Conversely, if Colorado maintains a rational regulatory environment, it could attract companies fleeing New York's anti-growth stance.

Republicans argue that banning productive infrastructure in the name of environmental precaution is economic self-sabotage. Democrats counter that data centers consume significant power and water—concerns worth monitoring in water-scarce Colorado.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature or local governments attempt similar restrictions on data center development.
