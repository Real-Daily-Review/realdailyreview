# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has left the small community of Beulah without reliable water service—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis now threatens the utilities and infrastructure that keep towns functioning. According to the Colorado Sun, water managers are scrambling to restore clean water supplies after the blaze damaged critical systems, with officials warning residents the recovery will be lengthy. CPR News reports that the fire devastated the town of roughly 800 residents, destroying numerous homes in the close-knit community.

While most active Colorado fires have spared water infrastructure, Beulah's experience exposes a vulnerability: as wildfires grow more intense and unpredictable, the state's aging water systems face mounting risk. Conservatives and taxpayers should demand clarity on whether state and local governments have adequate emergency protocols and funding to harden critical utilities against future fire damage—or whether Colorado will continue reactive, expensive rebuilding cycles.

The real question: Will state lawmakers prioritize preventive forest management and infrastructure resilience, or keep throwing money at post-disaster recovery while ignoring the root causes of catastrophic fire behavior?
