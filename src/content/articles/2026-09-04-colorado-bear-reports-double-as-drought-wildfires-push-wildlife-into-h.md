# Colorado Bear Crisis Explodes as Drought, Wildfires Drive Wildlife Into Neighborhoods

Colorado Parks and Wildlife is drowning in bear calls—more than 8,600 reports so far this year, more than double last year's 3,600, as extreme drought and wildfires push hungry bears into human territory seeking winter calories during hyperphagia season.

The spike underscores a hard truth: when government land-management policies fail to prevent catastrophic wildfires and drought conditions worsen, Coloradans pay the price—not just in firefighting costs and water shortages, but in safety threats to families and property. Bears entering hyperphagia are desperate, dangerous, and increasingly bold around homes and garbage cans across the state.

CPW has responded to the surge, but the underlying problem runs deeper. Conservatives argue that decades of fire-suppression policies and overly restrictive forest-management regulations have left Colorado's forests choked with dead timber and fuel loads—a tinderbox waiting to ignite. Proper thinning, controlled burns, and active management could reduce both wildfire severity and the habitat degradation driving bears downslope into subdivisions.

Coloradans should watch whether state officials acknowledge the connection between failed forest stewardship and wildlife conflict, or whether they simply blame climate and ask taxpayers to fund more bear-response programs without fixing root causes.
