# Denver Voters Face Four New Tax and Spending Questions This Fall

Denver taxpayers will decide four ballot measures this November that could reshape city budgeting and energy costs, but conservatives warn the proposals expand government spending and regulatory control over utilities.

Denver City Council approved referred questions 2H through 2K for the November ballot, covering a new Xcel Energy franchise agreement, budgeting changes, and related matters (Denver Post). The measures represent another round of municipal overreach, Republicans argue—bundling utility regulation with spending authority in ways that limit transparency and increase costs for residents already burdened by inflation and property tax hikes.

The Xcel franchise agreement is particularly concerning to conservatives. Utility franchises often lock in rate structures and regulatory frameworks for years, and Denver's approach could embed progressive energy mandates into law. Taxpayers should scrutinize whether these measures fund bloated city departments or subsidize green-energy experiments at ratepayers' expense.

Meanwhile, Denver's weather pattern is shifting cooler next week after unseasonably warm conditions through Monday (KDVR), offering little relief to residents worried about winter heating bills—which could spike if the new Xcel deal includes costly grid modernization or renewable-energy requirements.
