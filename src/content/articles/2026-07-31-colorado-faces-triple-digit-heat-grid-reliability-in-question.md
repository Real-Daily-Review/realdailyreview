# Colorado Braces for Triple-Digit Heat This Weekend

Colorado faces a dangerous heat spike that will test power grids, water supplies, and household budgets across the state. After isolated storms Friday with highs in the low 90s, temperatures will soar into triple digits Sunday and Monday, according to 9News Denver and KDVR Fox31.

The forecast matters to Colorado taxpayers and businesses. Extreme heat drives up electricity demand—and costs—especially for families already squeezed by inflation. Rural areas dependent on well water face stress during peak summer demand. Energy producers, already operating under Colorado's aggressive renewable mandates, will struggle to meet load without reliable baseload power from natural gas and coal plants the state is phasing out.

Democrats and environmental groups have pushed Colorado toward wind and solar dominance, but critics argue the grid remains vulnerable during peak-demand events like this weekend. Republicans and energy advocates warn that retiring dispatchable power plants without adequate storage or backup capacity risks blackouts and higher rates for working families.

Coloradans should monitor whether the state's grid operator (CISO) issues conservation alerts or demand-response calls—a sign the system is straining under the heat load and ideological energy policy.
