# Jack Smith's Festival Appearance Raises Questions About Prosecutorial Impartiality

Former Special Counsel Jack Smith attended a left-wing "Power to the People" music festival in Maryland over the weekend, where he was photographed wearing a Springsteen-inspired "No Kings" shirt—imagery widely read as anti-Trump messaging (Breitbart). The event featured Bruce Springsteen and other headliners railing against ICE and mobilizing voters ahead of elections.

Trump responded sharply, calling Smith a "Crooked Prosecutor" and questioning his judgment (Just The News). Republicans argue the appearance undercuts Smith's credibility as an impartial law enforcement official. When a prosecutor who brought high-profile cases against a political opponent shows up at a partisan festival wearing anti-Trump apparel, conservatives say it fuels legitimate concerns about weaponized justice and the politicization of federal agencies.

Democrats and Smith's allies would likely counter that his attendance at a music festival is a private citizen's right to free expression, especially after leaving office. But conservatives argue the optics matter: a former special counsel's visible participation in overtly partisan activism raises serious questions about whether his prosecutorial decisions were driven by law rather than ideology.

For Colorado voters and taxpayers, this episode reinforces the broader conservative concern about federal prosecutorial overreach and the need for genuine independence in the Justice Department.

Watch whether congressional Republicans demand answers about Smith's conduct during his tenure and whether similar concerns emerge about other federal prosecutors' political activities.
