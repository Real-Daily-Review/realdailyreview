# FDA Panel Stacked With Industry Ties Votes to Expand Peptide Access—Regulatory Capture in Action

The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee just recommended loosening restrictions on six peptides, and the fix was in from the start. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the panel was loaded with peptide sellers and industry insiders—a textbook case of regulatory capture where the fox guards the henhouse.

Peptides are amino acid chains used in customized medications prepared by compounding pharmacies rather than mass-manufactured drugs. The committee's vote to greenlight previously restricted peptides raises serious questions: Who benefits? Who pays? And why does an agency supposedly protecting consumers stack the deck in favor of the industry it's supposed to regulate?

This is exactly the kind of government overreach conservatives warn about. Unelected bureaucrats, influenced by financial interests, make decisions affecting patient safety and market competition without meaningful public accountability. The FDA should be transparent about panel conflicts of interest, not burying them.

For Colorado patients and taxpayers, this matters: if peptide compounding expands without proper safeguards, insurance costs could spike, and quality control becomes murkier. Watch whether Colorado's medical boards and insurance commissioners push back on the FDA's recommendation or rubber-stamp it.
