# Camp Mystic Files for Bankruptcy After Deadly Texas Floods

A Texas summer camp where 28 people died in catastrophic flooding last year has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, signaling the devastating financial toll of natural disaster liability.

Camp Mystic, an all-girls Christian camp in central Texas, filed for reorganization this week with debts exceeding $10 million, according to the *Washington Examiner*. The camp's owners, members of the Eastland family, listed substantial liabilities tied to the flash-flood disaster that killed 25 girls and two teenage counselors in summer 2025.

The bankruptcy filing underscores a hard reality: even well-established institutions can face existential financial pressure when catastrophic events strike. While the camp's owners will likely face civil litigation from victims' families—a predictable and appropriate legal avenue—the bankruptcy process will determine how remaining assets are distributed among creditors and claimants.

For Colorado families who send children to summer camps, the case is a sobering reminder to scrutinize facility safety records, emergency protocols, and insurance coverage. Colorado's mountain terrain and unpredictable weather patterns create their own flash-flood risks; parents should demand that local camps have robust disaster-preparedness plans and adequate liability insurance.
