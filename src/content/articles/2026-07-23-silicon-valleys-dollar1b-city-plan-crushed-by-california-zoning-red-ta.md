# Silicon Valley's Master-Planned City Hits California's Zoning Maze—A Warning for Colorado

A Silicon Valley billionaire-backed project to build a new city in California's Solano County is running headlong into the state's regulatory stranglehold, proving that even deep-pocketed developers can't escape progressive red tape.

California Forever, founded by Czech-born Jan Sramek and financed by tech wealth, purchased farmland with plans to construct a community under a unified master plan—a straightforward approach that would bypass the region's notorious zoning battles and permitting gridlock (Washington Examiner). The company's vision: a safe, affordable, sustainable city built efficiently without the usual bureaucratic maze.

But California's progressive regulatory apparatus is fighting back. The project faces the same restrictive zoning codes, rezoning fights, and local opposition that plague development across the state—the very obstacles a single master plan was designed to circumvent.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's Front Range communities face similar zoning restrictions and NIMBYism that drive up housing costs and slow development. If billionaires with unlimited resources can't bypass California's regulatory state, what hope do Colorado developers and homebuyers have against our own growth-limiting ordinances and environmental reviews?

Conservatives argue that California's experience proves the fatal flaw of progressive land-use policy: no amount of private capital can overcome government's power to say no. Colorado taxpayers and businesses should watch whether our state doubles down on California-style restrictions or embraces streamlined permitting to unlock affordable housing and economic growth.
