# Washington's Crypto Tax Grab: Another Assault on Innovation and Your Wallet

Washington is doubling down on taxing unrealized cryptocurrency gains—a policy that would punish Colorado's growing tech and digital asset community before they've even sold a coin.

For nearly a decade, Congress has fumbled attempts to fit cutting-edge financial technology into a tax code designed for the 20th century. The result, according to the *Washington Examiner*, is predictable chaos: confusion, ballooning compliance costs, arbitrary tax treatment, and a bloated bureaucracy that ordinary Americans foot the bill for.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Taxing assets people haven't sold violates basic principles of sound tax policy. It creates perverse incentives against wealth creation, forces small investors and entrepreneurs to liquidate holdings just to pay phantom gains, and hands the IRS yet another enforcement nightmare. Colorado's emerging crypto and blockchain sector—from Denver startups to individual miners—would face crushing uncertainty and compliance burdens that larger, out-of-state competitors can absorb.

Democrats and progressive tax advocates argue that crypto wealth should face taxation like any other asset class to fund government priorities. Conservatives counter that the real problem isn't undertaxation—it's Washington's refusal to modernize tax code rather than weaponize it against innovation.

**Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Republicans—will push back against any unrealized-gains schemes in the next tax reform push.**
