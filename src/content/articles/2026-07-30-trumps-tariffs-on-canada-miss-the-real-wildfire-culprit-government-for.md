# Trump's Smoke Tariffs on Canada Miss the Forestry Mark

Trump's unprecedented 50% tariffs on Canadian exports—partly a response to cross-border wildfire smoke—may be economically blunt when the real problem is Canada's government-controlled forest management, according to conservative analysis.

The Trump administration has escalated tariffs to historic levels, citing smoke inundating U.S. air as justification. But the *Washington Examiner* argues the tariff approach sidesteps the actual culprit: Canada's state-dominated forestry system that fails to prevent the catastrophic burns sending smoke into Colorado and across the nation.

**The conservative case:** Rather than punish trade broadly, Republicans contend the focus should be on pressuring Ottawa to privatize forest management and adopt aggressive thinning and controlled-burn practices—the proven method for reducing megafires. Government-run forests, the argument goes, prioritize environmental ideology over active stewardship, leaving tinderboxes that burn hotter and longer.

**Colorado's stake:** Western wildfires, whether Canadian or domestic, choke Colorado's Front Range and mountain communities with hazardous air quality. Conservatives argue the real solution isn't tariffs but demanding competent forest management on both sides of the border—something Colorado's own experience with state and federal mismanagement underscores.

Watch whether Trump's tariff strategy shifts toward direct pressure on Canada's forestry policies, or whether the trade war continues masking the underlying governance failure.
