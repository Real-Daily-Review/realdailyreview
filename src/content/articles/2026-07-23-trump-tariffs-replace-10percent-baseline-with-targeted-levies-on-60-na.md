# Trump Tariffs Hit Colorado Consumers and Manufacturers as 10% Baseline Expires

President Trump is replacing temporary across-the-board tariffs with targeted levies on 60 countries, ranging from 10% to 12.5%, as his initial 10% worldwide tariffs expire Friday (9News Denver). The new duties invoke Section 301 of the Trade Act, citing forced labor violations and trade imbalances (Just The News).

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue targeted tariffs protect American workers and manufacturing from unfair competition while pressuring bad actors on labor standards. Trump's approach shifts from blanket levies to country-specific leverage.

**The counterargument:** Critics—including some business groups—warn tariffs raise prices for consumers and manufacturers reliant on imports, potentially slowing economic growth and triggering retaliation.

**Colorado angle:** The state's agricultural exporters, manufacturers, and energy sector face uncertainty. Colorado farmers selling internationally could face retaliatory tariffs; manufacturers importing components will see higher input costs passed to consumers. Retailers and construction firms dependent on imported materials face margin pressure.

The tariff structure suggests Trump is using trade policy as a negotiating tool rather than permanent protectionism, but the economic impact on Colorado's supply chains and export-dependent businesses remains unclear.

Watch whether Colorado's business community—particularly agriculture and manufacturing—lobbies the administration for sector-specific exemptions or whether retaliation from trading partners targets Colorado exports directly.
