# Trump Pushes Smithsonian to Ditch Abstract Art, Honor Washington with 30-Foot Statue

President Trump is demanding the Smithsonian Institution replace an abstract rotating stainless steel sculpture called "Infinity" with a 30-foot "Washington Colossus" at the National Museum of American History, according to the New York Post and Washington Examiner. The move is part of Trump's broader effort to better honor the nation's first president ahead of Washington's 300th birthday.

Trump criticized the existing sculpture as "strange modernist" art that "inspires no one"—a swipe at the kind of government-funded contemporary art that conservatives often argue wastes taxpayer dollars on elitist aesthetics disconnected from American values. The Smithsonian, a federally funded institution, has long been a flashpoint for debates over how public money should commemorate national heritage.

Republicans generally support Trump's push to center classical, representational art celebrating American founders over abstract modernism. Democrats and progressive curators typically defend artistic diversity and argue that abstract work reflects contemporary perspectives on history.

The proposal underscores a broader conservative argument: federal cultural institutions should prioritize clear, inspiring tributes to American greatness rather than experimental installations that confuse rather than educate visitors.

Watch whether Congress or Smithsonian leadership actually moves forward with the statue—and whether this becomes a template for how Trump reshapes other federal cultural agencies.
