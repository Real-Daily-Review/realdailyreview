# Red Rocks Rescue: Another Reminder That Colorado Outdoors Demands Respect

Two teenagers learned an expensive lesson in poor planning after getting lost hiking back from a Red Rocks concert Wednesday night, requiring West Metro Fire Rescue to mount a search-and-rescue operation (KDVR Fox31, 9News Denver). The hikers decided to trek from the amphitheater to Morrison on foot rather than use standard transportation—a decision that went sideways when darkness fell and they couldn't find their way.

West Metro Fire Rescue responded to the call, ultimately locating and extracting the teens safely. The incident underscores a recurring problem: Colorado's world-class outdoor recreation attracts visitors and locals alike, but not everyone comes prepared for the genuine risks of mountain terrain, weather, and navigation.

Search-and-rescue operations consume taxpayer resources and tie up emergency personnel who could be responding to genuine emergencies. While we're grateful these teens made it out safely, this is a teachable moment about personal responsibility. Colorado's backcountry isn't a theme park—it demands respect, preparation, and honest assessment of your own skills before you venture out.

Taxpayers should watch whether local governments continue absorbing the costs of preventable rescue operations, or whether they'll start holding individuals accountable for reckless decisions.
