# Colorado Springs Sued Over April Election Timing—First Test of 2025 Voting Law

Colorado Springs faces its first legal challenge under the state's 2025 voting law, with a lawsuit challenging the city's decision to hold municipal elections in April rather than the November general election cycle. Complete Colorado reports this marks the first enforcement action since the law took effect, signaling potential friction over election scheduling across the state.

Republicans and voting-law advocates argue that consolidated election cycles boost turnout and reduce voter confusion by aligning local contests with statewide races. The lawsuit suggests opponents believe April elections dilute participation in municipal races—a concern that could reshape how Colorado cities conduct business.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs is deploying nearly half a million dollars from its marijuana tax fund to create a security beat along South Nevada Avenue, according to CPR News. This represents a growing trend of cities using cannabis revenue for public safety initiatives rather than general operations.

The broader context: unaffiliated voters now represent a growing share of Colorado's electorate, and those registered with parties but inactive in caucuses effectively cede influence to primary participants—a structural advantage for organized party activists.
