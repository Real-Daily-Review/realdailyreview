# Colorado Faces Fire Weather Risk as Hot Weekend Looms

Colorado faces elevated wildfire danger this weekend as temperatures climb into the 90s across the Denver metro area, paired with dry conditions and breezy winds—a combination that has state and local officials on alert (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The National Weather Service is monitoring fire weather conditions closely. Hot, dry weekends have historically preceded some of Colorado's most destructive wildfires, including the Marshall Fire near Boulder in 2021. With Colorado's forests already stressed by drought and beetle kill, conditions remain primed for rapid fire spread.

Meanwhile, Denver's business community is bracing for a busy weekend as PrideFest relocates to a new downtown venue (KDVR Fox31). The event is expected to draw significant foot traffic, though organizers will need to coordinate with first responders given the concurrent fire weather alert.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether state and local governments have adequately funded wildfire prevention and response infrastructure—including controlled burns, forest management, and firefighting resources—rather than relying solely on federal disaster declarations after fires occur.
