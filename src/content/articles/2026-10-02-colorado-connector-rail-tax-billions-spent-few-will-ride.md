# Front Range Rail Tax: Colorado Voters Face Billion-Dollar Boondoggle Funded by Taxpayer-Backed PR Blitz

Colorado taxpayers are being asked to bankrupt themselves for a train nobody will ride—and the rail lobby is spending millions in public money to sell them on it.

The Colorado Connector, a proposed Front Range rail project, is heading to voters as a tax measure. But before Coloradans cast ballots, they're being bombarded with a state-funded "education" campaign that looks suspiciously like political advertising. According to CPR News, the push includes paid Instagram influencers and targeted mailers—all bankrolled by taxpayer dollars meant to inform, not persuade.

Complete Colorado's analysis identifies 10 reasons to reject the scheme, starting with the fundamental problem: billions in spending for a train with questionable ridership projections and dubious economic returns. Conservatives argue this is classic government overreach—using taxpayer money to lobby taxpayers into approving more taxes.

Republicans counter that the money would be better left in Coloradans' pockets, where individuals and businesses can spend it productively. The state has no business funding glossy campaigns to manufacture consent for its own pet projects.

This isn't transportation policy; it's a masterclass in how progressive governments use public resources to engineer outcomes they want.

Watch whether Colorado voters reject this tax grab—or whether the influencer campaign succeeds in drowning out fiscal common sense.
