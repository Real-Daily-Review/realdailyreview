# Colorado Lawmakers Quietly Rake In Billions Through Fee Explosion While Voters Say No to Tax Hikes

State lawmakers have engineered a backdoor revenue bonanza: fees now generate more than triple the current income tax rate, even as Colorado voters repeatedly reject direct tax increases at the ballot box.

According to Complete Colorado, the fee structure accumulated over recent years has ballooned into a massive revenue stream—effectively circumventing voter resistance to higher taxes. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this represents government overreach by another name: when lawmakers can't convince taxpayers to approve tax hikes, they simply impose fees on licenses, permits, and services instead. The result is the same—more money flowing to state coffers—but without the transparency or voter consent that comes with a ballot measure.

The tactic highlights a core conservative complaint: government finds ways to expand its take regardless of what voters decide. Colorado taxpayers already shoulder significant regulatory costs through existing fees; this expansion threatens to make government services and business operations even more expensive for ordinary Coloradans.

The Denver Post reports separately that Colorado voters—particularly in competitive districts—express deep skepticism about AI regulation, with majorities believing tech companies alone cannot police themselves. That bipartisan wariness may signal appetite for *actual* legislative restraint, not just fee-based revenue grabs.

Watch whether Republican lawmakers will push back against fee proliferation in the 2027 session or demand voter approval for major new charges.
