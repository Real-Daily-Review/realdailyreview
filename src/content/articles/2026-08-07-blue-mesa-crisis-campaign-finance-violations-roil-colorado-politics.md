# Colorado's Water Crisis Deepens as Blue Mesa Hits Quarter-Full—and Campaign Finance Chaos Erupts

Colorado's largest reservoir is running dangerously dry, signaling a broader crisis on the Colorado River that demands serious water management—not more federal mandates. Blue Mesa's plunge to just 25 percent capacity reflects the strain on Lake Powell and underscores why Colorado needs flexibility in water compacts, not rigid environmental restrictions that hamstring agriculture and energy development (Colorado Sun).

Meanwhile, the state's election apparatus is in overdrive. Victor Marx's gubernatorial campaign accepted more than $78,000 in improper donations—at least 197 contributions from 111 donors exceeding the $1,450 individual limit—according to Colorado's secretary of state (Denver Post). The violations raise questions about campaign finance enforcement and whether candidates across the political spectrum face equal scrutiny.

Republicans argue that Colorado's water crisis demands pragmatic solutions: protecting senior water rights, investing in storage and infrastructure, and resisting federal overreach that prioritizes environmental mandates over Colorado's agricultural and energy economies. Democrats counter that climate adaptation and interstate cooperation are essential.

On campaign finance, conservatives note the importance of transparent enforcement that applies uniformly—not selectively—to all candidates.

**Watch next:** Whether state election officials pursue similar vigor investigating other campaigns, and whether Colorado's water leadership will prioritize Colorado's interests over federal environmental agendas.
