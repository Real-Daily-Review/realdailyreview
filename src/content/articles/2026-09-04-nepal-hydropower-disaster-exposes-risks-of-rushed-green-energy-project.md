# Nepal Hydropower Disaster: A Cautionary Tale for Colorado's Energy Future

A glacier collapse in Nepal has trapped roughly 500 workers in hydropower tunnels, with rescuers managing to pull just two alive nine days after devastating floods swept through the region (New York Post). About 900 workers remain missing across 12 hydropower projects, underscoring the catastrophic risks of aggressive hydropower expansion in mountainous terrain.

The disaster raises hard questions for Colorado policymakers pushing renewable energy mandates without adequate safety oversight. While Democrats and environmental groups champion hydropower as "clean energy," this Nepal tragedy exposes the human and infrastructure costs of rushing development in geologically unstable areas. Rescuers are still searching for over 500 missing workers, many believed trapped in tunnels with dwindling oxygen and food supplies.

Colorado has its own hydropower infrastructure and ongoing debates over water management tied to energy production. As state lawmakers consider expanding renewable projects to meet carbon-reduction targets, conservatives argue regulators must demand rigorous geological surveys, worker safety protocols, and realistic cost-benefit analyses—not ideological commitments to green energy at any price.

The Nepal collapse demonstrates that renewable energy isn't risk-free. Colorado taxpayers and policymakers should demand transparency on safety standards before approving new hydropower projects in the state.

**Watch for:** Colorado's Public Utilities Commission decisions on hydropower expansion permits and whether safety audits will be required.
