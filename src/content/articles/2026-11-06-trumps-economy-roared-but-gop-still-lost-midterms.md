# Trump's Strong Economy Couldn't Prevent Midterm Rout

A roaring jobs market and stock gains proved no match for voter frustration in last week's midterm elections, leaving Republicans scrambling to explain why economic strength didn't translate to ballot-box victories.

In October—just weeks before the midterms—U.S. employers added 165,000 jobs, crushing expectations of 59,000, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data cited by *The Daily Wire*. The unemployment rate dropped sharply. Yet despite these headline wins, Republicans lost ground across the country, including in Colorado's competitive races where the economy ranked high among voter concerns.

The disconnect has sparked finger-pointing within GOP ranks. *National Review* reports the party faces hard questions about what comes next, while *The Daily Caller* notes Trump has taken to mocking his own voters over recent market volatility—a move that's only deepened frustration among his base.

**The conservative problem:** Strong economic data alone doesn't win elections when voters feel squeezed at the grocery store and gas pump. Colorado Republicans, in particular, failed to capitalize on job growth to offset concerns about inflation, energy policy, and water rights that dominated local campaigns.

**What to watch:** Whether GOP leaders will pivot messaging toward cost-of-living relief and regulatory rollback—or continue internal warfare that handed Democrats momentum heading into 2028.
