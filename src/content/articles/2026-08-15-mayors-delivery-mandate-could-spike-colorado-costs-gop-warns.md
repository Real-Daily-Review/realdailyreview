# Mayor's Delivery Mandate Threatens Colorado Affordability, Conservative Critics Warn

A mayor's push for a one-size-fits-all delivery driver mandate threatens to raise costs for Colorado consumers already squeezed by inflation, conservatives argue. The proposal, endorsed by the mayor, would impose uniform employment requirements on delivery platforms—a regulatory overreach that Republicans say will inevitably drive up prices for everyday shoppers (National Review).

Republicans counter that such mandates ignore market realities. With midterm elections looming and voter concerns about affordability running high, the GOP warns that heavy-handed labor regulations on gig economy platforms will backfire—particularly given that sustained price hikes between Trump's nonconsecutive terms already damaged consumer confidence (Washington Examiner). The irony, conservatives note, is that voters returned Trump to office partly *because* of inflation concerns.

For Colorado specifically, this matters. The state's growing gig economy supports thousands of workers and keeps delivery costs competitive in rural and urban markets alike. A mandate that forces platforms into rigid employment models could shrink service availability in less profitable areas and raise prices statewide.

Conservatives argue the real solution to affordability is removing regulatory barriers, not adding them—and that politicians should focus on cost-of-living relief, not employer mandates that punish the very platforms keeping goods accessible and cheap.
