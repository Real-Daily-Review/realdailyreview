# Defense Secretary Hegseth Backs Apache Pilots After Military Brass Suspends Them for July 4th Flyover

Pete Hegseth is cleaning house at the Pentagon, and Colorado defense contractors should take note: the new Defense Secretary is willing to override military bureaucrats who punish initiative and patriotism.

Eight Apache helicopter pilots were suspended after executing a low-altitude flyover during a July 4th celebration at a South Carolina beach—a move that delighted beachgoers but triggered brass concerned about unauthorized airshows. Rather than accept the suspension, Hegseth intervened and had the pilots reinstated, signaling a sharp break from the risk-averse military culture that has dominated recent years (Fox News, New York Post).

The episode reveals a deeper conservative frustration: Pentagon leadership has grown so cautious and rule-bound that pilots executing a patriotic, crowd-pleasing maneuver face career jeopardy. Hegseth's move to reinstate them sends a message that the military should reward initiative and morale-boosting actions, not bury them in bureaucratic punishment.

For Colorado, where defense spending and military installations anchor the state economy—Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, and the aerospace supply chain employ thousands—Hegseth's willingness to challenge Pentagon orthodoxy could mean faster decision-making and fewer regulatory obstacles for defense contractors and military operations.
