# Texas Bible Reading Mandate Reignites School Curriculum Wars—Colorado Should Watch Closely

Texas just became the first state in recent history to require Bible stories in K-12 public school curricula, and the move is already sparking a national battle over what belongs in classrooms.

The Texas State Board of Education, controlled by Republicans, voted Friday to approve the reading list. Supporters argue the Bible is a foundational text for understanding Western literature, history, and law—and that teaching it doesn't violate the First Amendment. State law and legal precedent, they contend, permit the mandate (Washington Examiner).

Critics, however, denounce the curriculum change as unconstitutional government promotion of religion. They warn it crosses the line between teaching *about* religion and teaching *for* religion.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's education establishment leans progressive, and the state's school boards have resisted similar efforts to expand traditional curricula. If Texas's move gains traction nationally, expect Colorado Democrats to dig in against any similar proposals—and conservatives to argue that acknowledging Judeo-Christian heritage in public education isn't establishment of religion, it's honest history.

Republicans counter that secular curricula have systematically excluded religious texts while treating progressive ideology as neutral fact. They see Texas's move as corrective balance.

Watch whether other Republican-led states follow Texas's lead and whether Colorado's legislature or State Board of Education faces pressure to adopt—or explicitly reject—similar measures.
