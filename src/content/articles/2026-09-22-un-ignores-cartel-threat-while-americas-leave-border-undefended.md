# UN Ignores Cartel Threat While Americas Fumble Border Security

The United Nations is failing to address the transnational drug cartels destabilizing the Western Hemisphere—a blind spot that leaves Colorado and other border states vulnerable to trafficking, violence, and fentanyl flows that kill Americans daily.

As world leaders convened for the 81st UN General Assembly this week in New York, the *Washington Examiner* highlighted a critical gap: the international body rarely elevates cartel threats until after crises erupt. Instead of proactive coordination among American nations, the U.N. treats organized crime as a secondary concern, leaving individual countries to manage border security and drug interdiction alone.

For Colorado, this matters enormously. Fentanyl and methamphetamine trafficked through cartels fuel overdose deaths and crime across the state. Without coordinated hemispheric action—what some call the "Shield of the Americas"—Colorado law enforcement and communities bear the cost of federal and international failure.

Conservatives argue the U.N.'s institutional weakness on cartel violence proves why America must prioritize bilateral and regional partnerships over multilateral forums. Stronger border enforcement, intelligence-sharing agreements among Western Hemisphere nations, and support for Mexico's security efforts are far more effective than U.N. resolutions.

Watch whether the Trump administration or Congress pushes for direct hemispheric security pacts that bypass the U.N. entirely.
