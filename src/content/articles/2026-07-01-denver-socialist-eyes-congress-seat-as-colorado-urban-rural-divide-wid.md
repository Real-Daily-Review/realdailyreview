# Denver Socialist Candidate Kiros Eyes Congressional Seat

A 29-year-old Democratic Socialist could soon represent Denver in Congress—a stark reminder that Colorado's capital city has drifted far left of the state's energy-dependent, business-friendly backbone.

Melat Kiros, profiled by CPR News, is positioning herself as Denver's next congressional representative. The Democratic Socialist label carries real weight: it signals alignment with the DSA's agenda of expanded government control, higher taxes, and skepticism toward free markets—positions that clash sharply with Colorado's ranching, oil-and-gas, and small-business communities.

Republicans and conservatives argue that electing self-described socialists to Congress threatens the economic freedom and limited government that built Colorado's prosperity. They point to DSA-backed candidates' typical support for wealth taxes, aggressive climate regulations that would cripple energy production, and expansive entitlements—all costly to Colorado taxpayers and workers in traditional industries.

Democrats counter that younger voters, particularly in urban areas, demand bolder action on inequality and climate change. They frame socialism-adjacent candidates as responsive to constituent demands for economic justice.

The race underscores a widening urban-rural divide in Colorado politics. While Denver lurches left, much of the state remains skeptical of socialist economics.
