# Colorado Marks 150 Years: A Century and a Half of Freedom, Opportunity, and Growth

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives at a moment when the state's founding principles—individual liberty, economic opportunity, and limited government—face mounting pressure from progressive overreach.

The state celebrated its 150th birthday with festivities at the Capitol, as covered by *CPR News* and the *Colorado Sun*. The milestone offers a natural moment to reflect on what made Colorado great: pioneers who settled harsh terrain through grit and self-reliance, not government handouts; miners, ranchers, and entrepreneurs who built wealth and communities; and a culture that valued personal freedom over bureaucratic control.

Yet today's Colorado looks different. Denver and Boulder have embraced the progressive playbook: exploding regulations, aggressive taxation, and government-directed "solutions" to housing, energy, and education. The state that once attracted risk-takers and builders now imposes some of the nation's strictest environmental mandates and highest tax burdens on energy producers and small business owners.

The real question isn't what Colorado was 150 years ago—it's whether the state will reclaim those founding values or continue down the path of regulatory socialism that's already driving businesses and families to Texas, Florida, and Wyoming.

Watch whether Colorado's next legislative session rolls back or doubles down on the regulatory burden choking the state's economy.
