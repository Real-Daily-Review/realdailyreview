# Supreme Court's Climate Case Signals Judicial Retreat—But Senate Control Looms Larger

The Supreme Court's high-stakes climate case *Suncor v. Boulder* is exposing deep fractures on the bench, with liberal justices Jackson and Sotomayor openly questioning whether the Court even has jurisdiction—a potential escape hatch that could spare the left from a major loss on climate regulation.

But Republicans are sounding a louder alarm: control of the Senate in upcoming elections could determine whether conservative justices get confirmed at all. Sen. Steve Daines warns that losing the chamber would block Trump nominees and reshape the Court for decades (Fox News).

**The Colorado angle:** Boulder's climate ordinance—which imposes strict emissions rules on oil and gas operations—hinges on this case. A ruling against Boulder could limit municipal climate mandates statewide, affecting how Colorado cities regulate energy development. Conversely, if liberal justices dodge the case on jurisdictional grounds, Boulder's aggressive climate regime survives unchecked.

Conservatives argue the Court's legitimacy depends on judicial restraint, not political grandstanding (National Review). The left's search for an "off-ramp" suggests they fear losing on the merits—a sign the conservative majority's originalist approach is reshaping constitutional law.

**Watch:** Whether the Court takes the bait on jurisdiction, or forces a ruling that could curtail municipal climate overreach across Colorado and the nation.
