# Trump Envoys Head to Moscow, But Conservative Skepticism Grows on Putin's True Intentions

President Trump's diplomatic envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are heading to Moscow this weekend to meet with Vladimir Putin, followed by a visit to Kyiv—a move aimed at exploring whether a negotiated settlement to end the four-and-a-half-year Ukraine war is possible. This marks their first trip to Ukraine since Trump's second term began, following an earlier January visit to Moscow.

The initiative reflects Trump's stated priority of ending the conflict through diplomacy rather than continued U.S. military aid. However, conservatives are divided on the mission's viability. The Washington Examiner's opinion section warns that meeting with Putin in Moscow risks lending credibility to what it calls "false sincerity," potentially enabling Russian propaganda rather than advancing genuine peace talks.

**Republicans argue** that while diplomatic engagement is preferable to endless military commitments, Putin has shown little genuine interest in meaningful compromise and may simply use such talks to buy time and legitimize his position. **The Trump administration counters** that direct engagement is necessary to determine if any diplomatic off-ramp exists.

For Colorado, the outcome matters: continued Ukraine aid diverts resources from domestic priorities, while a failed negotiation could extend the conflict indefinitely. Watch whether these talks produce concrete movement toward settlement or simply validate conservative concerns about Putin's bad faith.
