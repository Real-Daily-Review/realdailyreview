# Trump's Swamp-Draining Promises Collide With Reality in Second Term

President Trump's pledge to dismantle government cronyism is facing early credibility tests as his administration pursues policies critics say contradict his anti-establishment messaging.

The Daily Caller reports Trump is building what amounts to an "AI empire" through government partnerships, raising questions about whether this represents the kind of public-private entanglement conservatives say they oppose. Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner's opinion section argues Trump's FDA pick undermines his "Make America Healthy Again" platform by potentially refilling the regulatory swamp rather than draining it—particularly concerning given the agency's outsized influence over Colorado's biotech and pharmaceutical sectors.

Republicans and conservatives have long criticized government cronyism: sweetheart deals between politicians and corporations that lock out competition and burden taxpayers. Trump's 2016 and 2020 campaigns centered on reversing this dynamic. His second-term actions on AI infrastructure and regulatory appointments will test whether that rhetoric translates to actual reform.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the stakes are real. If Trump's administration defaults to the same corporate-government partnership model it claims to oppose, it signals that campaign promises about limited government and free-market competition were largely theater.

Watch whether Trump's cabinet nominees and policy decisions actually reduce regulatory capture or simply redirect it toward favored industries.
