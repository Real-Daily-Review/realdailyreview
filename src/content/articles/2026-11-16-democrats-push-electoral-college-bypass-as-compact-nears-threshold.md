# Electoral College Under Siege as Democrats Push Interstate Compact to Bypass Founders' Design

Colorado and other states are inching closer to dismantling the Electoral College through the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact—a backdoor constitutional workaround that would hand presidential elections to coastal population centers and urban machines.

According to Complete Colorado, Democrats understand precisely where their political power concentrates, and they're weaponizing an interstate agreement to neutralize the Founders' safeguard against tyranny of the majority. National Review reports the compact is approaching the 270 electoral votes needed to trigger automatic implementation, meaning participating states would award their electors to the national popular vote winner—regardless of how their own voters cast ballots.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** The Electoral College forces candidates to build geographically diverse coalitions and protects rural America, small states, and flyover country from being ignored. Eliminating it would mean presidential campaigns ignore Colorado's Western Slope ranchers, energy workers, and rural communities entirely, focusing only on Denver metro and Front Range population centers.

**Democrats argue** the current system is undemocratic because it allows a candidate to win the presidency while losing the national popular vote—a feature, not a bug, conservatives say.

Colorado taxpayers and voters should watch whether the state legislature moves to join or withdraw from the compact, and whether Republican-led states mount legal challenges to the scheme's constitutionality.
