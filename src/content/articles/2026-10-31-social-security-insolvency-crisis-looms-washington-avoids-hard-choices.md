# Social Security's Looming Insolvency Crisis Demands Hard Choices Washington Won't Make

Colorado retirees and workers face a reckoning: Social Security is heading toward insolvency, yet neither party is willing to tackle real reform before the trust fund runs dry.

December 2026 Supplemental Security Income payments of up to $994 are being distributed on schedule, but the broader picture is dire. The program's trust fund faces a solvency crisis that demands structural reform—benefit adjustments, payroll tax increases, or means-testing—none of which politicians want to defend to voters (Washington Examiner).

Republicans argue the House Freedom Caucus and fiscal conservatives have long warned that Social Security cannot survive without fundamental changes. Democrats, meanwhile, resist any benefit reductions or eligibility reforms, framing the issue as a "trust fund" problem solvable through modest tax adjustments on high earners.

The reality: kicking the can down the road means steeper cuts or tax hikes later. Colorado workers and retirees will bear the cost of Washington's cowardice.

Conservatives should demand their representatives—state and federal—commit to specific reform proposals before 2026, not after the crisis hits.
