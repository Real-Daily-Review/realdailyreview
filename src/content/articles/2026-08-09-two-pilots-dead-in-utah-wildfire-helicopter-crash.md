# Helicopter Pilots Killed Fighting Utah Wildfire

Two pilots are dead after their Sikorsky Skycrane helicopter crashed while battling a wildfire in Utah's Fishlake National Forest—a stark reminder of the real costs and dangers of America's sprawling wildfire crisis.

The helicopter went down Friday morning, according to the Sevier County Sheriff's Office. The crash itself ignited a new fire, compounding the disaster.

Wildfire suppression across the West has become increasingly costly and dangerous. Colorado faces similar threats annually, with state and federal agencies deploying aircraft, ground crews, and resources at enormous taxpayer expense. The tragedy underscores why conservatives argue for smarter forest management—thinning overgrown federal lands, allowing responsible logging, and reducing the catastrophic fuel loads that turn routine fires into infernos requiring desperate aerial interventions.

The Biden administration's push to lock up more federal land and restrict timber harvesting has only worsened forest health in Colorado and across the West. Meanwhile, wildfire suppression budgets balloon while prevention gets short shrift.

Colorado taxpayers and property owners should demand their state and federal representatives prioritize active forest management and fuel reduction over land-use restrictions that leave our forests tinderboxes.
