# Colorado's Data Center Moratorium Will Chase Jobs and Investment Elsewhere

Aurora Mayor Mike Coffman is proposing a data center moratorium through 2027—a move that will accomplish little except telling capital to relocate to business-friendly states, according to critics of the restriction.

The moratorium reflects growing local concerns about data centers' footprint, but conservatives argue it represents exactly the kind of regulatory overreach that drives employers and tax revenue out of Colorado. Complete Colorado warns that the moratorium "tells capital to go somewhere else, and capital listens"—meaning Colorado risks losing high-value development, jobs, and economic growth to Texas, Arizona, or other states with fewer restrictions.

Data centers represent significant economic opportunity for Colorado communities seeking diversified revenue streams beyond traditional energy and agriculture. The restrictions threaten that potential without addressing underlying concerns through market-based solutions or targeted infrastructure planning.

Republicans and business advocates should push back hard: Colorado cannot afford to price itself out of the AI and computing economy by treating job creators as problems to be managed rather than assets to be welcomed. The state's competitive advantage depends on being open for business, not hostile to it.

**Watch whether other Colorado municipalities follow Aurora's lead—and whether the state legislature steps in to prevent a patchwork of local moratoriums that could cripple Colorado's tech economy.**
