# Colorado IT Office Lays Off 173, Rehires 15, Claims $6M Savings While Cyber Threats Rise

Colorado's Office of Information Technology has axed 173 state workers in what officials call a cost-cutting overhaul, then rehired just 15 of them while creating 98 new positions and filling 88—a shell game that raises hard questions about whether taxpayers are actually saving money.

The Colorado Sun reports the state claims the reorganization will save "at least $6 million," but the math doesn't add up cleanly. If the OIT eliminated 173 jobs, created 98 new ones, and hired 88 people, the net reduction is only 85 positions—hardly a dramatic efficiency gain. Meanwhile, cyber threats against state systems have *increased*, according to the agency itself.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue this looks like typical government restructuring theater: fire workers, rehire consultants or contractors at higher rates, declare victory, and move on. The lack of transparency about actual per-position costs and whether new hires command higher salaries makes it impossible for Colorado taxpayers to verify the claimed savings.

The state should publish a detailed cost-benefit analysis showing salary comparisons, contractor rates, and measurable security improvements before claiming this reorganization succeeded.
