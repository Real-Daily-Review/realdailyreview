# Former Mesa County Deputy Joins ICE After Weiser Lawsuit

Colorado's progressive Attorney General Phil Weiser pursued disciplinary action and a lawsuit against Mesa County Sheriff's Deputy Alexander Zwinck for collaborating with federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement—a move that reflected the state's sanctuary-friendly stance on immigration enforcement. Rather than fight the legal battle, Zwinck resigned last year and has now secured a position with ICE itself, according to the Colorado Sun.

The case illustrates the tension between Colorado's Democratic leadership and federal immigration law. Weiser's office targeted Zwinck for working with ICE, reflecting the left's broader resistance to cooperation between local law enforcement and federal immigration authorities. Republicans and immigration hawks argue that such cooperation is essential to enforce federal law and protect public safety—and that penalizing officers for it undermines both.

Zwinck's move to ICE suggests that at least one Colorado law enforcement professional found the state's anti-cooperation climate untenable. His departure and new federal role raise questions about whether Colorado's aggressive stance against immigration enforcement cooperation is driving experienced officers away from local agencies.

Taxpayers and conservatives should watch whether other Colorado sheriffs face similar pressure from Weiser's office for assisting federal immigration authorities.
