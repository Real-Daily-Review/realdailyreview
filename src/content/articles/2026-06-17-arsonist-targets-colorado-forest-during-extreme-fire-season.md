# Arsonist Targets Colorado Forest During Extreme Fire Season

An arsonist deliberately set fires on multiple trees near Forest Service land in Chaffee County, with investigators discovering a bottle full of accelerant hanging from a branch—a chilling reminder that Colorado's catastrophic wildfire risk isn't just about drought and climate, but criminal negligence (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The Chaffee County Sheriff's Office is hunting the suspect as the state faces one of its driest years on record. The attempted arson underscores a hard reality: Colorado's forests are tinderboxes, and bad actors can ignite disaster in seconds.

Conservatives and property owners have long argued that aggressive forest management—thinning dense stands, removing dead timber, and allowing responsible logging—is essential to reducing fuel loads and wildfire risk. Yet environmental regulations and litigation often block these preventive measures, leaving forests vulnerable to both natural ignition and deliberate arson.

The timing is stark: during peak fire season, when a single match can destroy thousands of acres and threaten lives, someone deliberately tried to start a forest fire. This isn't a policy debate—it's criminal recklessness that exposes the stakes of forest stewardship.

Taxpayers and residents should watch whether prosecutors aggressively pursue this case and whether it reignites the conversation about removing regulatory barriers to forest thinning and management across Colorado's high country.
