# Fort Morgan Cargill Workers End 89-Day Lockout—But at What Cost to Colorado's Meat Industry?

More than 1,700 meatpackers at Cargill's Fort Morgan facility voted to ratify a new contract this week, ending an 89-day lockout that began May 20 when the company shut out workers who rejected an earlier proposal (9News Denver). Negotiations have dragged on since February, with union officials acknowledging gains but emphasizing it was "time to go back to work" (Colorado Sun).

The deal restores paychecks to workers in one of Colorado's critical food-production hubs, but conservatives should ask hard questions about what concessions Cargill made and whether this sets a precedent for future labor demands across the state's agricultural sector. Colorado's meatpacking industry—a backbone of rural economies—faces mounting pressure from union organizing, regulatory compliance costs, and supply-chain disruption.

While union officials claim "some gains," the specifics remain murky. Workers endured nearly three months without income; Cargill absorbed production losses. Both sides declared partial victory, which typically means neither got everything demanded—a costly stalemate for Colorado's economy.

Taxpayers and industry watchers should monitor whether this Fort Morgan settlement emboldens unions to pursue similar aggressive tactics at other Colorado food processors, potentially driving up costs for consumers and squeezing already-thin margins in agriculture.
