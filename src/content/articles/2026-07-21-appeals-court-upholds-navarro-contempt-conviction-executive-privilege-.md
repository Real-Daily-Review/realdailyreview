# Appeals Court Upholds Navarro Contempt Conviction—Raising Questions About Executive Privilege

A federal appeals court in Washington has upheld Peter Navarro's felony contempt of Congress conviction, rejecting his claim that former President Donald Trump's executive privilege shielded him from complying with the House January 6 committee's subpoena. The three-judge panel issued a unanimous 37-page opinion (Washington Examiner), finding no legal basis to shield the White House trade adviser from testifying.

Navarro declined to appear before the House panel, arguing executive privilege protected him. The court disagreed, setting a precedent that could affect how future administrations handle congressional subpoenas. Republicans have long criticized the January 6 committee as a partisan exercise, but this ruling underscores the judiciary's view that executive privilege has limits when Congress seeks testimony on matters of official conduct.

The decision matters beyond Washington: it clarifies that no blanket immunity exists for executive branch officials facing legislative subpoenas, a principle that could shape how Colorado's congressional delegation and state officials navigate similar disputes. Conservatives should watch whether this precedent gets weaponized against Republican administrations in future Democratic-controlled Congresses—or whether courts apply it consistently regardless of party.
