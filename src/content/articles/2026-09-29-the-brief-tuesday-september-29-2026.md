# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Tuesday, September 29, 2026

Colorado's water future hangs in the balance after a brutal year, Denver braces for midweek flooding, and conservatives are pushing back — on AI hysteria, a tone-deaf GOP ad, and a Democratic Senate candidate who thinks your burger is a sin. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Colorado Closes a Rough Water Year — With a Question Mark Ahead

The last 12 months ranked as the hottest on record and sixth driest in Colorado history, and now forecasters are watching whether an incoming wet pattern delivers meaningful relief. Water is the lifeblood of Colorado agriculture, ranching, and energy development — and another dry year would put real pressure on rural communities and water-rights holders across the Western Slope and Eastern Plains. Conservatives should be watching whether state water managers pursue market-based solutions or use drought conditions as cover for more regulatory overreach.

---

## Denver Midweek: Flood Watch, Heavy Rain on the Way

A Flood Watch has been posted for southern Colorado, with significant rainfall expected to push into the Front Range by Wednesday. While lighter impacts are forecast for Denver proper, the storm system carries real flood concerns for communities already dealing with a stressed water year. Colorado taxpayers and local governments will be footing the bill for any infrastructure damage — a reminder that smart, maintained infrastructure beats reactive emergency spending every time.

---

## AI Panic: Coloradans Aren't Buying the Hysteria

With impeccable timing, Magellan Strategies released its Colorado AI and Data Center Opinion Survey, and the results offer good reason to ease off the panic pedal on artificial intelligence. Progressives and regulators in Denver have been eyeing AI as the next frontier for government intervention, but Colorado voters may not be as alarmed as the left needs them to be. Republicans argue that heavy-handed AI regulation would kneecap a growing sector of Colorado's economy before it even gets off the ground.

---

## Conservative Revolt Over Ex-Trump Official's Campaign Ad

Arkansas Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders is facing backlash from conservatives over a reelection campaign ad depicting a white male welfare recipient named "Brad" — a characterization critics on the right are calling insulting and dishonest. The blowback is significant: when a Republican governor loses conservatives over a campaign message, it signals a real disconnect with the base. Colorado Republicans should take note — voters across the country are tired of condescending political theater, even from their own side.

## Kansas Dem Senate Candidate Told His Flock to Repent for Eating Beef

Adam Hamilton, the Democrat running to become the first Kansan sent to the U.S. Senate in nearly a century, once called on his megachurch congregation to repent for driving and eating beef. For Colorado ranchers, feedlot operators, and anyone who's ever ordered a ribeye, this is exactly the kind of radical green moralizing that has no business anywhere near a Senate seat. Democrats counter that Hamilton's faith informs his values — conservatives say those values look a lot like a war on agriculture dressed up in a pulpit.

Keep an eye on how Colorado's water managers respond to the incoming precipitation — whether they treat it as a market opportunity for water-rights holders or a bureaucratic opening for more state control.
