# Republicans Split Message on Economy Ahead of Midterms

President Trump is selling Americans on a "greatest economy in our history," but his own party is simultaneously warning of deindustrialized dystopia—a contradiction that could undercut GOP momentum heading into midterm elections.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, Trump claimed last week in South Carolina that manufacturing is "booming," growth is "exploding," and inflation is "defeated." Yet Vice President Vance and other Republicans are pushing a darker narrative of economic decay and lost industrial capacity—a mixed signal that muddies the party's core argument against Biden-era policies.

For Colorado, this messaging split matters. The state's energy sector, manufacturing base, and small businesses need clarity on whether Republicans believe the economy is recovering or collapsing. A unified message emphasizing genuine gains in energy independence, job creation in oil and gas, and relief from Biden's regulatory overreach would resonate far better with Colorado voters than contradictory talking points.

**Conservatives should watch whether GOP leadership settles on one coherent economic narrative—or whether this internal confusion hands Democrats an opening to define the economy on their terms.**
