# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, July 9, 2026

The Middle East is heating up fast, the Supreme Court handed conservatives a stinging defeat on birthright citizenship, and back home, Denver is about to get its first taste of driverless taxis. Here's what every Colorado conservative needs to know this morning.

---

## Iran Closes the Strait of Hormuz — Energy Markets Brace

President Trump declared the ceasefire with Iran officially over Wednesday, and the situation escalated quickly: Iran has threatened to close the Strait of Hormuz, with at least four oil and LNG tankers already abandoning passage after Iranian attacks. The White House is now drawing redlines that could trigger direct U.S. military action. For Colorado's energy sector — oil, gas, and LNG producers who depend on stable global markets — a prolonged Hormuz closure means price volatility, supply disruption, and economic pressure that hits the Western Slope hard.

---

## Supreme Court Birthright Ruling Hands Trump a Loss — He's Fighting Back

The Supreme Court ruled against the Trump administration on birthright citizenship, and the president isn't taking it quietly — demanding a rehearing and calling the ruling "insane." The Court held that more than a close case and divided dissenters is required to overturn 128 years of precedent. Republicans argue the 14th Amendment was never intended to guarantee citizenship to children of those here illegally, and conservatives say the Court punted on a question that directly affects border enforcement, public resources, and the rule of law.

---

## Waymo Rolls Into Denver — But Don't Hail One Yet

Fully autonomous Waymo vehicles are coming to Denver, though for now rider-only trips are limited to company employees, with a public rollout planned soon. The technology raises legitimate questions conservatives should be watching: regulatory frameworks, liability when no driver is present, and whether Denver's progressive city government will use the rollout as an excuse to restrict private vehicle use or impose new fees on competitors. Free-market innovation is welcome — government using it as a lever for social engineering is not.

---

## Don't Arm Turkey With F-35s

Analysts and defense hawks are sounding the alarm: selling F-35s to Turkey would needlessly jeopardize American security and the lives of U.S. service personnel. Turkey's relationship with NATO has been rocky at best, and handing Ankara one of America's most advanced fighter platforms raises serious questions about technology transfer and operational security. Colorado is home to multiple major military installations — Buckley, Peterson, Schriever, Fort Carson — and the men and women stationed there deserve a foreign policy that doesn't arm potential adversaries.

## The Left Comes for Taylor Swift

This is what the left does: punish success, resent private wealth, and demand that even a private celebration be subject to political approval. Colorado conservatives should recognize the pattern — today it's a celebrity wedding, tomorrow it's your ranch, your business, or your paycheck.

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Strait of Hormuz — if Iran follows through on closure, expect energy prices to spike and Colorado producers to feel it at the contract level before consumers feel it at the pump. On the birthright citizenship fight, watch whether the Trump administration pursues a rehearing or pivots to a legislative strategy; either path runs through a Congress where every vote counts. And in Denver, watch how city officials respond to Waymo's expansion — the first regulatory moves will tell you everything about whether this city welcomes innovation or just wants to tax and control it.
