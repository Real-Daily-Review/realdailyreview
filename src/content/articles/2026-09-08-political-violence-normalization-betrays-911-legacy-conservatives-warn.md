# America's Political Culture Embracing Violence Would Horrify 9/11 Victims

As the nation marks the 25th anniversary of September 11, a troubling reality emerges: segments of American political culture have grown dangerously comfortable rationalizing political violence and the deliberate murder of civilians, according to *Washington Examiner* commentary.

The piece argues that if Osama bin Laden were alive today, he would likely view the normalization of political violence in American discourse as a vindication of his ideology—a stark indictment of how far the nation has drifted from the unity and moral clarity that followed the attacks.

For Coloradans, this cultural deterioration carries particular weight. The state has experienced its share of political extremism and violence. The normalization of such rhetoric threatens not only national cohesion but also the safety of Colorado communities, elected officials, and civic institutions that depend on peaceful disagreement and the rule of law.

Conservatives argue that both major parties bear responsibility for inflammatory rhetoric that dehumanizes opponents and creates space for violence. The left often dismisses such concerns as exaggerated, but the evidence of increasingly hostile political environments is undeniable.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's political leaders—and national figures—will meaningfully condemn political violence across ideological lines, or continue the dangerous drift toward accepting it as normal.
