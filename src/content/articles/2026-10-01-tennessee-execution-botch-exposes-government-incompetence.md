# Tennessee Execution Botch Raises Questions About State Competence

Tennessee's failed execution of death row inmate Christa Pike Wednesday—she survived two lethal injections and remained conscious nearly an hour later—exposes the dangers of government bungling even in its most serious responsibilities.

Pike, convicted of murdering classmate Colleen Slemmer in 1995, was declared dead after the first injection, but witnesses reported her snoring and moving as the second dose was administered (Washington Examiner, Daily Wire). Her attorneys filed emergency motions to halt further execution attempts, arguing the procedure violated her constitutional protections against cruel punishment.

The Supreme Court had cleared the execution to proceed hours earlier. Tennessee officials, however, apparently failed to properly establish IV lines or verify Pike's unconsciousness before administering pentobarbital—basic competency failures in a capital case.

**The conservative takeaway:** This isn't an argument against capital punishment for heinous crimes. Rather, it's a stark reminder that government agencies—whether executing sentences or managing healthcare, elections, or regulations—often lack the competence Coloradans assume. When bureaucrats bungle routine tasks, why should we trust them with complex policy mandates?

Pike was transferred to a hospital; her legal status remained in flux as of reporting.
