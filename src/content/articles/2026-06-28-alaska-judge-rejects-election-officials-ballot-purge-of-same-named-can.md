# Judge Blocks Alaska Election Officials' Attempt to Remove Same-Named GOP Candidate

An Alaskan judge has struck down state election officials' effort to remove a Republican primary candidate simply because he shares the same name as incumbent Senator Dan Sullivan—a win for ballot access and a cautionary tale about government overreach in elections.

The Division of Elections had disqualified Dan J. Sullivan from the GOP primary, citing confusion concerns. But the judge disagreed, ruling that name similarity alone doesn't justify removing a qualified candidate from the ballot (Fox News, Washington Examiner).

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Election officials cannot unilaterally decide which candidates are "too confusing" to appear on ballots. That's a dangerous precedent. Voters are capable of distinguishing between candidates—they have candidate statements, party affiliation, and ballot position to guide them. Allowing bureaucrats to curate ballots based on subjective confusion claims opens the door to weaponizing election administration against disfavored candidates.

While this case involves Alaska, Colorado conservatives should pay attention to how their own election officials handle ballot access disputes. Colorado's election bureaucracy has expanded significantly in recent years. This Alaska ruling reinforces that courts won't rubber-stamp election officials' paternalistic decisions to protect voters from themselves.

**Watch whether Colorado's Secretary of State's office attempts similar ballot-access restrictions in 2024 or 2026—and whether courts here will push back as firmly.**
