# Real Daily Review — The Brief

The Republican National Convention kicked off with a tone Washington rarely hears — one focused on results, not rhetoric. Pennsylvania Sen. Dave McCormick took center stage, introduced as the kind of senator who gets the job done. For Colorado conservatives watching the 2026 midterm landscape, the RNC's opening salvo matters: a unified, confident Republican Party is the best check on the progressive policy experiments that have made Colorado's legislature a cautionary tale for the rest of the country.

## Jim Jordan Floats McCormick for 2028

Rep. Jim Jordan is already thinking past November, telling reporters he sees Sen. Dave McCormick as a serious 2028 presidential contender. It's early — very early — but conservatives know that the next presidential cycle starts the moment the midterms close. Colorado Republicans, still rebuilding statewide infrastructure after years of Democratic dominance in Denver, should be paying close attention to which national figures are building coalitions that could energize the base here at home.

## Rick Scott: AOC and Zohran Mamdani Are Running the Democrats

Sen. Rick Scott isn't mincing words: he says Chuck Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries aren't the ones calling the shots for Democrats — it's Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, New York mayoral candidate Zohran Mamdani, and far-left media personality Hassan Piker. Republicans argue this is exactly right, and Colorado conservatives should take note. The hard left's grip on the Democratic Party has real consequences here — from the spending bills that drive inflation to the regulatory agenda that squeezes Colorado's energy producers and small businesses.

## United Cancels Denver 9/11 Flyover After "Public Concerns"

United Airlines scrapped a planned 9/11 anniversary flyover above Denver — featuring a commercial plane and fighter jets — after Denver's Office of Emergency Management flagged public reaction concerns. Let that sink in: a patriotic tribute to the victims of September 11 was canceled because someone might be upset. Denver officials were apparently more worried about managing feelings than honoring the nearly 3,000 Americans killed 25 years ago. Conservatives say this is exactly the kind of tone-deaf, risk-averse government thinking that has come to define Colorado's capital city under one-party Democratic rule.

## Lyons Center to Honor Christian Glass Opens

A new teen and young adult arts center is opening in Lyons, built by Sally and Simon Glass inside a converted diner to honor their son Christian, who was killed by police in 2022. The Glass family's grief is real and their effort to build something positive from tragedy deserves acknowledgment. Conservatives will note, however, that any policy conversations flowing from this space should be grounded in the full picture — including the dangers law enforcement officers face daily — rather than becoming a platform for anti-police activism that has already made Colorado communities less safe.
