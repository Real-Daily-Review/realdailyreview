# Thanksgiving Storm Hammers Nation; Colorado Braces for Winter Weather Pattern

Torrential rain and dangerous winds battered much of the United States over the Thanksgiving holiday, leaving East Coast communities flooded, knocking power offline for nearly a million customers, and forcing the first-ever postponement of the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade (KDVR).

Colorado faces its own weather challenges as the nation enters a volatile atmospheric pattern. While the holiday storms primarily impacted the coasts, the broader weather system underscores why energy reliability and infrastructure resilience matter—especially as Colorado's power grid increasingly depends on intermittent renewable sources that perform poorly during severe weather events.

The flooding and outages highlight a conservative concern: government should focus on maintaining robust, reliable infrastructure rather than rushing to retire proven energy sources. Colorado's natural gas and coal plants provide stable baseload power during exactly these kinds of emergencies. Conservatives argue that premature retirement of dispatchable generation, pushed by progressive climate mandates, threatens grid stability when Coloradans need electricity most.

The storms also remind taxpayers why emergency preparedness and infrastructure investment in *maintenance*—not just green-energy subsidies—deserve priority funding at state and local levels.

Watch whether Colorado's Public Utilities Commission uses this weather event to reconsider aggressive renewable-only timelines in the state's energy transition.
