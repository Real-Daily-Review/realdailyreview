# Colorado's Mail Ballot Security Tightens as Tax Hike Heads to Voters

Colorado is tightening its all-mail voting system just as voters prepare to decide a major tax increase. Initiative 362 will add new voter authentication requirements for mail ballots this November (Complete Colorado), addressing long-standing conservative concerns about election integrity in the state's all-mail voting model. Meanwhile, Colorado's secretary of state confirmed that a graduated income tax increase on high earners will appear on the November ballot (Denver Post), setting up a direct test of voter appetite for higher taxes on Colorado's top earners.

The Colorado County Clerks Association head expressed confidence the state will run a "safe and secure" election despite federal uncertainty (CPR News), signaling election officials believe the new ID requirements will strengthen ballot security. Republicans have long argued that mail-voting systems require robust authentication safeguards; the new voter ID mandate reflects that pressure.

The tax ballot measure represents a separate battleground. Conservatives oppose the graduated income tax as economically damaging and a step toward permanent tax-code expansion. Democrats frame it as necessary revenue for schools and transportation.

Taxpayers should watch whether Initiative 362's stricter mail-ballot authentication survives legal challenge from voting-rights groups—and whether Colorado voters reject the income tax hike.
