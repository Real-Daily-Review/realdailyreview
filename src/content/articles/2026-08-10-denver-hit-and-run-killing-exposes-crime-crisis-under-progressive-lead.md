# Denver Faces Rising Street Violence as Intentional Hit-and-Run Claims Another Life

Denver's crime problem just claimed another victim—this time, a motorcyclist struck intentionally by a vehicle driver, according to Denver Police investigators (9News Denver). The suspect has been arrested, but the incident underscores a troubling pattern of violent crime in Colorado's capital that shows no signs of slowing.

The same week, a fundraiser was organized for three Eaton High School graduates killed in a separate vehicle crash last month (KDVR Fox31), highlighting how traffic fatalities—whether criminal or accidental—are devastating Colorado families at an alarming rate.

Conservatives and law-and-order advocates argue that Denver's progressive criminal justice policies—including reduced bail requirements, lenient sentencing, and defunded police initiatives—have emboldened criminals and failed to deter violent offenders. Republicans point to rising violent crime statistics as evidence that soft-on-crime approaches don't work. Democrats counter that root causes like poverty and mental health require investment, not enforcement alone.

For Colorado taxpayers and families, the message is clear: Denver's streets are becoming less safe. Whether through intentional violence or reckless driving, Coloradans are dying on city streets while progressive leadership prioritizes rehabilitation rhetoric over victim protection.

Watch for whether Denver's incoming police leadership will reverse course on enforcement priorities—or double down on the failed policies of recent years.
