# Senate GOP Pushes Back on Biden-Era Overreach, Surveillance Expansion

Republicans are ramping up pressure on Democratic-controlled committees to investigate potential double standards in Hunter Biden probes while simultaneously challenging federal surveillance creep—two fronts where Colorado conservatives should pay close attention.

Sen. John Curtis (R-Utah) has demanded the Senate Judiciary Committee investigate both Don Jr. and Hunter Biden, but *Just The News* reports most committee members haven't even opened his letter. Meanwhile, Sen. Rand Paul's bid to grant immunity to a key Fauci aide failed a committee vote, blocking testimony that could have exposed pandemic-era government overreach.

On surveillance, the Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism is examining Flock cameras—AI-powered license plate readers deployed nationwide. The *Washington Examiner* reports Northern Virginia cities have already backed away from the technology after community backlash over privacy concerns.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue selective prosecution and stonewalling undermine accountability, while unchecked surveillance infrastructure threatens Fourth Amendment protections. Colorado gun owners and property rights advocates should note that license-plate tracking systems can map movement patterns and create de facto registries—a concern libertarian-leaning Republicans have long flagged.

Democrats counter that such investigations are politically motivated and that surveillance tools aid law enforcement.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado law enforcement agencies have adopted Flock cameras and what privacy safeguards—if any—are in place.
