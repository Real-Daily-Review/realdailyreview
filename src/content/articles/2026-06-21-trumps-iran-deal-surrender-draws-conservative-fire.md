# Trump's Iran Deal Surrender Draws Fire From Right and Left

President Trump's capitulation on Iran negotiations has triggered rare bipartisan criticism, with conservatives and progressives alike questioning whether the administration abandoned core demands in pursuit of a quick diplomatic win.

National Review argues Trump "abandoned his previous positions in favor of the Iranian position on all the key issues of contention" entering the talks. The conservative outlet's assessment mirrors concerns from the right that the deal fails to secure meaningful constraints on Iran's nuclear program or regional aggression—priorities Trump campaigned on reversing.

Meanwhile, Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin attacks the administration's messaging, citing Vice President JD Vance's inflammatory rhetoric about the agreement as evidence of desperation to spin a weak outcome.

The deal's specifics remain contested. Republicans contend Trump surrendered leverage by accepting Iranian terms without securing reciprocal concessions on sanctions relief, ballistic missiles, or sunset provisions. Democrats, typically skeptical of Trump's foreign policy, appear divided—some viewing any negotiated settlement as preferable to confrontation, others questioning whether the agreement actually constrains Tehran's nuclear ambitions.

For Colorado, the outcome matters: energy markets could shift if sanctions ease, affecting oil and gas development. Defense contractors and workers tied to Middle East operations face potential policy reversals.

Watch whether Congress moves to block the deal or demand renegotiation.
