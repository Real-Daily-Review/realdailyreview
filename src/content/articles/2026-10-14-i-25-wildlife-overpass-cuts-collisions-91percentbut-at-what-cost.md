# Wildlife Overpass on I-25 Delivers Results—But Colorado Taxpayers Footed a Massive Bill

Colorado's $274 million wildlife overpass near Castle Rock is doing what it was supposed to do: keeping elk and deer off the highway. Since opening 10 months ago, the crossing has cut I-25 collisions in the area by 91%, with just two reported incidents, according to the Colorado Sun.

That's the good news. The conservative case for the project rested on a simple calculation: preventing accidents saves lives and reduces insurance costs for drivers. Those benefits appear real.

But here's what taxpayers should remember: this single overpass cost a quarter-billion dollars—funded largely through state and federal spending. While the safety gains are measurable, the price tag raises hard questions about whether similar projects elsewhere represent sound stewardship of public money or environmental spending that could have been addressed through less expensive alternatives like wildlife fencing or adjusted highway design standards.

The project also highlights a broader tension: Colorado's transportation infrastructure competes with other priorities. Every dollar spent on wildlife mitigation is a dollar not spent on pothole repair, bridge maintenance, or tax relief for families struggling with inflation.

**Watch whether state lawmakers cite this success to justify similar mega-projects—and whether they demand rigorous cost-benefit analysis before greenlighting the next one.**
