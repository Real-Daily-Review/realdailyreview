# Supreme Court Faces Dueling Overreach in Trump Cases

The Supreme Court is wading into two high-stakes disputes where both the executive and judiciary have tested constitutional limits—and Colorado voters should care about the precedents being set.

In one case, the Court is examining Trump's mail-in ballot order against a district judge's sweeping countermand, according to *National Review*. Both moves stretch their respective powers, raising questions about judicial restraint and executive authority that will ripple through future administrations.

Separately, Trump is asking the justices to overturn an $83 million judgment to E. Jean Carroll, *Just The News* reports. The president argues his 2024 immunity decision should shield him from liability in the case. The Court must now clarify the boundaries of that immunity ruling—a decision that will define presidential power for decades.

Conservatives argue the lower courts have overreached repeatedly, weaponizing the bench against Trump while ignoring constitutional limits on judicial power. Democrats counter that no president should be above the law, even if immunity protections apply to official acts.

The tension here is real: unchecked executive power threatens liberty; unchecked judicial power does the same. Colorado taxpayers should watch whether the Court actually enforces constitutional guardrails or simply picks sides in a political war.
