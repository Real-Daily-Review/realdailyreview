# American Free Markets Keep Winning—Europe's AI Brain Drain Shows Why

The U.S. economy is lapping Europe, and artificial intelligence companies are voting with their feet.

Over the past decade, the American economy has grown at nearly double the EU rate, according to the *Washington Examiner*. U.S. GDP now stands at $32.5 trillion versus the EU's $23.1 trillion—and per capita GDP in America runs about 85% higher than the European average. The gap reflects a fundamental truth: lighter regulation, lower corporate taxes, and respect for free markets attract capital and talent.

Europe's regulatory stranglehold—particularly its AI rulebook—is driving businesses stateside. The EU's heavy-handed approach to AI governance, combined with higher tax burdens and labor costs, makes the continent uncompetitive. Meanwhile, American entrepreneurs and investors see opportunity in a system that rewards innovation over compliance theater.

For Colorado, this matters. The state's tech corridor and energy sector depend on attracting capital and skilled workers. If federal overregulation or state-level progressive tax hikes take root, Colorado risks becoming another cautionary tale—watching talent and investment flee to freer markets.

Conservatives argue this vindication of free-market economics should inform policy at every level: cut red tape, keep taxes competitive, and let markets work.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers resist the temptation to copy Europe's regulatory mistakes as AI investment accelerates.
