# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push hungry wildlife into residential areas at unprecedented rates, forcing state officials into a grim cycle of human-wildlife conflict management.

The spike reflects a cascade of environmental pressures: prolonged drought has decimated natural food sources in Colorado's forests, while wildfire has destroyed habitat across the state. Bears, starving and desperate, are venturing into towns and suburbs in record numbers, triggering dangerous encounters with residents and forcing CPW's hand.

Conservatives and property owners argue the state must balance wildlife management with public safety and private land rights. While environmentalists often resist culling programs, the math here is stark: more bears in towns means more dead bears. The real solution, critics say, lies upstream—forest management, controlled burns to reduce catastrophic wildfire risk, and water policy that sustains both ecosystems and ranching communities.

Colorado taxpayers are footing the bill for this crisis response while facing questions about whether state land-use and environmental policies have adequately prepared for climate volatility. Watch for CPW's next move on bear corridors and whether the state will fund aggressive forest thinning and prescribed burns to restore habitat and reduce human-bear conflict long-term.
