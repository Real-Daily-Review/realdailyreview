# Colorado Voters Face Clear Choice on Girls' Sports Fairness

Colorado's Proposition 134 puts a straightforward question before voters: should biological males identifying as transgender be allowed to compete on girls' school sports teams? The measure directly challenges the state's current policy permitting athletes to compete according to gender identity rather than sex.

Republicans and conservatives argue the ballot initiative protects fairness and safety for female athletes. They contend that biological differences in strength, speed, and endurance create an inherent competitive advantage—a concern backed by sports scientists and women's athletic organizations nationwide. Supporters say girls deserve equal opportunity to compete on level ground and that schools should prioritize the rights of the majority of female students.

Democrats and progressive groups counter that the ban discriminates against transgender youth and violates their dignity. They argue such restrictions marginalize already vulnerable students and that existing athletic policies adequately address legitimate concerns.

The Colorado angle is significant: this ballot measure reflects a nationwide pushback against progressive gender ideology in schools, following similar efforts in other states. New York's Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman has pledged comparable restrictions on bathroom and locker-room access (New York Post), signaling momentum for this issue across the country.

Conservatives should monitor how Colorado voters respond—a decisive margin either way will signal whether the state's electorate supports protecting women's sports or embraces identity-based policy in athletics.
