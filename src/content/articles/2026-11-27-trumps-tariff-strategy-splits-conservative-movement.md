# Trump's Tariff Gamble Splits Right; Colorado Manufacturers Face Uncertainty

President Trump's aggressive tariff strategy is drawing fire from conservative economists and foreign-policy hawks who warn the approach mirrors failed protectionist experiments rather than Reagan-era strength-through-strength diplomacy.

The "Liberation Day" tariffs announced in April 2025 triggered immediate skepticism on the right. *Washington Examiner* opinion writers argue Trump's tariff justifications are "absurd" and note his approval ratings began sliding when the policy rolled out—a sign, they suggest, that voters and markets see the move as economically risky rather than strategically sound.

*National Review* counters that Trump should adopt Reagan's Cold War playbook: build military and economic capacity first, then negotiate from overwhelming strength. The distinction matters: Reagan used demonstrated capability to force Soviet concessions. Critics worry Trump is using tariffs as a blunt instrument without the underlying economic foundation.

**Colorado angle:** Manufacturing-dependent businesses along the Front Range and in western slope industrial corridors face immediate cost pressures if tariffs raise input prices. Agricultural exporters—critical to Colorado's rural economy—could face retaliatory tariffs on beef and crops.

Republicans remain divided on whether tariffs serve legitimate national-interest goals or undermine free-market principles conservatives have championed for decades. Conservatives should watch whether Trump's approval stabilizes or continues eroding as tariff impacts ripple through supply chains and consumer prices.
