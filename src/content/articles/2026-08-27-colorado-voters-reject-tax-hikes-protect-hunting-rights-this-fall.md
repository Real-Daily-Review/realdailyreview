# Colorado Voters Set to Decide Tax Cap and Hunting Rights This Fall

Colorado voters are poised to reject another progressive income tax scheme while simultaneously protecting constitutional hunting and fishing rights—a one-two punch against left-wing overreach.

Coloradans will decide whether to cap the state income tax rate, continuing a recent pattern of rejecting higher levies (Complete Colorado). The measure arrives as taxpayers discover they've been footing the bill for progressive tax advocacy: the Colorado Fiscal Institute has received nearly $2 million in state tax-funded grants and programs while pushing for higher earner taxes (Complete Colorado). That's government subsidizing its own expansion—a hallmark of progressive politics that conservatives say must stop.

Separately, voters will decide a constitutional amendment protecting the right to hunt and fish, requiring a 55 percent supermajority for passage (Complete Colorado). The measure reflects growing concern among rural Coloradans and sportsmen that environmental regulations and land-use restrictions threaten traditional outdoor heritage.

Republicans have also narrowed their gubernatorial field through assembly voting, with only two candidates advancing from 18 initial contenders (Complete Colorado).

The tax-cap vote signals Colorado's electorate remains skeptical of progressive schemes to soak high earners—even when the left uses taxpayer money to lobby for them. Watch whether the hunting amendment clears its supermajority threshold; if it does, expect similar constitutional protections for other individual liberties to follow.
