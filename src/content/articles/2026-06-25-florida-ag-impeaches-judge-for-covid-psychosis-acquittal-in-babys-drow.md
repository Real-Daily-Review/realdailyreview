# Florida AG Moves to Impeach Judge Over 'COVID Psychosis' Acquittal in Infant Drowning

A Florida judge's decision to acquit a mother of drowning her 15-month-old daughter on grounds of COVID-induced insanity has triggered an impeachment push from the state's attorney general—a stark reminder of how progressive judicial activism can undermine public safety and accountability.

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier announced Thursday he will file articles of impeachment against Judge Miguel de la O after the judge found 43-year-old Precious Bland not guilty by reason of insanity. Bland drowned her daughter Emii, but de la O ruled she was experiencing a "psychotic episode" caused by COVID-19, effectively freeing her from criminal responsibility.

Uthmeier's move reflects conservative concerns about judicial overreach and the weaponization of mental-health defenses to excuse serious crimes. Republicans argue that accepting COVID-related psychosis as grounds for acquittal sets a dangerous precedent—one that could embolden defense attorneys to invoke pandemic-related mental states in future cases, potentially letting dangerous individuals walk free.

While Colorado has no direct stake in this Florida case, state lawmakers and judges should note the broader pattern: progressive courts increasingly favor therapeutic jurisprudence over accountability. Colorado conservatives should watch whether similar "insanity" defenses gain traction in state courts, particularly as left-leaning judges expand mental-health exceptions to criminal liability.
