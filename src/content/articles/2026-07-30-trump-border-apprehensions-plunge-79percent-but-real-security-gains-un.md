# Trump Border Enforcement Shows Sharp Drop, But Questions Remain on Actual Security Gains

Border enforcement numbers under Trump have plummeted—but whether that translates to genuine security is a different question. Southwest Border Patrol apprehensions in January 2026 were 79% lower than the same month a year prior, while total encounters at and between ports of entry fell 84%, according to the Washington Examiner. By April, Border Patrol was averaging just 298 apprehensions daily.

Those figures represent a dramatic shift from the Biden-era surge. Conservatives argue the decline reflects Trump's tougher deterrence posture—stricter enforcement messaging, expedited deportations, and a credible threat of consequences. The administration points to these numbers as proof the strategy works.

Yet the Examiner cautions that apprehension counts are "enforcement outputs rather than complete measures" of border security. Critics worry the statistics mask whether actual illegal crossings have dropped or simply shifted to harder-to-detect methods. Conservatives counter that enforcement *is* deterrence: when word spreads that crossing means immediate deportation, fewer attempt it.

For Colorado, border security directly affects illegal immigration flows into the state, strain on local services, and labor market dynamics in agriculture and construction. Taxpayers should watch whether these enforcement numbers hold through 2026 and whether actual deportations match the apprehension claims.
