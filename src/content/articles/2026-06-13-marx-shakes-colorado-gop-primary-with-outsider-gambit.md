# Colorado GOP Primary Heats Up as Marx Challenges Establishment in Governor's Race

Victor Marx is shaking up Colorado's Republican primary for governor with an unconventional campaign and a backstory so dramatic it's drawn skepticism from national media—but Marx says he welcomes the scrutiny (Denver Post). The Colorado businessman is positioning himself as an outsider willing to challenge the GOP establishment, a strategy that resonates with primary voters tired of business-as-usual politics in Denver.

Marx's willingness to embrace controversy rather than run from it marks a sharp departure from typical candidate playbooks. While establishment Republicans worry about electability in a general election, conservatives backing Marx argue the state needs a fighter unafraid to take on progressive overreach on taxes, regulation, and energy policy—areas where Colorado's Democrat-controlled legislature has aggressively pushed left.

The race comes as national Republicans face similar primary dynamics: Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) is running hard against scandal-plagued Democratic nominee Graham Platner, with GOP groups launching ads highlighting his controversial past statements (Washington Examiner). Collins's strategy—attacking opponent baggage rather than defending her own record—offers a preview of how Republicans may campaign in competitive 2026 races.

For Colorado conservatives, the Marx primary challenge tests whether GOP voters prioritize outsider credentials and combative messaging over traditional resume-building and donor backing.
