# Trump Rolls Out Red Carpet for Xi as U.S.-China Talks Begin

President Trump is betting personal diplomacy can reset the U.S.-China relationship, greeting Xi Jinping on the tarmac at Joint Base Andrews Wednesday evening in an unusually warm welcome for a visiting adversary (Breitbart). The Chinese leader will remain in Washington through Friday, with a formal White House arrival ceremony and state dinner scheduled for Thursday, followed by bilateral talks on trade, technology, and geopolitical tensions.

The optics matter. Trump's tarmac greeting—a break from protocol for most state visits—signals his willingness to engage directly with Beijing at a moment when U.S.-China competition spans semiconductors, rare earth minerals, and military posturing in the Pacific. For Colorado, the stakes are real: Chinese investment in U.S. energy and agriculture affects state ranchers and oil-and-gas workers, while tech competition shapes defense contracts tied to Colorado Springs and the aerospace sector.

Republicans argue direct engagement with Xi can yield concrete concessions on trade imbalances and intellectual property theft. Critics worry the pageantry masks the fundamental challenge of negotiating with an authoritarian regime that doesn't play by Western rules.

Watch whether Trump emerges from these talks with enforceable commitments on tariffs, supply chains, and critical minerals—or whether this becomes another photo op with no teeth.
