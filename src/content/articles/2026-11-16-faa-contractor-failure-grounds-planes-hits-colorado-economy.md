# FAA Meltdown Exposes Government Contractor Failures—Colorado Travelers Left Stranded

A catastrophic FAA system failure Friday—the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years—paralyzed air travel across America, leaving Colorado travelers and businesses scrambling through the weekend. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy pinned blame squarely on contractor L3Harris, whose maintenance work on overloaded routers triggered the near-total shutdown (Fox News).

Denver International Airport bore the brunt. The nearly 4.5-hour halt cascaded into weekend cancellations and delays that rippled through Colorado's economy, disrupting travel plans and stranding passengers (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

**The conservative takeaway:** This is government incompetence on full display. The FAA outsourced critical infrastructure maintenance to a private contractor, and the result was a preventable disaster affecting millions. Republicans will rightfully demand accountability—not just from L3Harris, but from the federal agency that failed to oversee the work properly. Colorado's tourism, business travel, and convention economy took a direct hit, costing local hotels, restaurants, and service workers real money.

The broader lesson: when government monopolizes essential services like air traffic control, there's no competition to drive reliability or innovation. Failures cascade nationwide with no alternatives.

Taxpayers should watch whether Congress holds the FAA accountable or simply throws more money at a broken system.
