# Trump's Canada Trade Brink: Colorado Energy, Agriculture at Risk

Trump's threatened tariffs on Canada could hammer Colorado's economy before either side backs down. The U.S. and Canada remain locked in trade-war brinksmanship, with 9News reporting that a last-minute deal would require American concessions—but neither side has signaled willingness to move.

Colorado stands to lose big. Canadian energy imports fuel refineries along the Front Range; agricultural exports to Canada support ranchers and farmers across the state. Tariffs would ripple through supply chains, raising costs for manufacturers, construction firms, and consumers.

National Review warns the standoff risks long-term damage to a critical alliance, calling the dispute "pointless" and noting the relationship will take years to repair. Conservatives are split: some back Trump's hardball tactics as necessary leverage against unfair trade practices, while others—including free-market advocates—argue tariffs are self-inflicted wounds that punish American workers and businesses more than foreign competitors.

Democrats predictably blame Trump for reckless brinkmanship; Republicans counter that previous administrations failed to hold Canada accountable on trade imbalances and energy policy.
