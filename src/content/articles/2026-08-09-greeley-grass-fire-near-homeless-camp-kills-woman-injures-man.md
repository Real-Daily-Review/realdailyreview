# Deadly Greeley Grass Fire Near Homeless Encampment Kills Woman, Critically Injures Man

A preventable tragedy unfolded Saturday in Greeley when a massive grass fire near homeless encampments claimed one life and left another person in critical condition—raising hard questions about how Colorado communities manage sprawling encampments and fire risk.

The blaze erupted around 1:40 p.m. near the Highway 34 and Highway 85 interchange (9News Denver), spreading rapidly through dry grassland adjacent to where homeless individuals were camped. A woman died in the fire; a man remains hospitalized in critical condition, according to KDVR Fox31 Denver.

The incident highlights a growing tension in Colorado towns: as progressive policies have expanded tolerance for unregulated encampments, fire hazards—from cooking fires, discarded cigarettes, and debris—have multiplied. Greeley, like Denver and other Front Range cities, has struggled to balance compassion with public safety and property protection.

Conservatives argue that enabling large, unsupervised encampments without adequate oversight or services creates dangerous conditions for both homeless individuals and surrounding communities. They point to incidents like this as evidence that catch-and-release approaches to homelessness fail vulnerable people and burden taxpayers.

Local officials have not yet released details on the fire's cause or whether encampment operations contributed to the spread.
