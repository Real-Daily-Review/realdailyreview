# Michigan Dem Primary Winner Signals Radical Shift That Could Reshape National Politics

Abdul el Sayed's Michigan Democratic Senate primary victory Wednesday is a warning sign for Colorado Democrats and moderate voters nationwide: the party's base is lurching hard left, and establishment figures can't stop it.

El Sayed, who does not distance himself from Democratic Socialists of America politics and has called for abolishing multiple institutions, defeated Rep. Haley Stevens in a stunning upset (National Review). President Trump immediately seized on the result, attacking el Sayed in Las Vegas as "full of s***" and repeating claims that el Sayed "hates Jews" (Washington Examiner).

Republicans are already weaponizing the win. The GOP is coordinating a unified messaging campaign across campaigns, party committees, and super PACs to paint el Sayed as the true face of the Democratic Party's leftward drift (Washington Examiner). That strategy could reshape the 2026 midterm narrative nationally—and in Colorado, where Democrats hold the Senate seat and governor's office.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters: if the Democratic Party nationally embraces el Sayed's radical platform—abolishing institutions, DSA-aligned economics—it validates Republican warnings about progressive overreach on taxes, regulation, and government expansion. Colorado's moderate Democrats will face pressure to either embrace or repudiate the national party's direction.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic establishment distances itself from el Sayed or stays silent, signaling tacit acceptance of the party's ideological shift.
