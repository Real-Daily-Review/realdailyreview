# Colorado Springs Sued Over April Municipal Elections; Aurora Voters Eye Election Timing Shift

Colorado Springs is facing a lawsuit over its decision to hold municipal elections in April—the first enforcement action since a 2025 voting law took effect, according to Complete Colorado. The legal challenge signals growing tension over when Colorado cities schedule local races.

Meanwhile, Aurora voters will decide this November whether to move municipal elections from odd-numbered years to even-numbered years via Ballot Question 3D, the Denver Post reports.

**The conservative concern:** Moving municipal elections to even-numbered years—when federal and state races dominate—risks drowning out local accountability. Odd-year elections force voters to focus on city spending, zoning, and local governance without national distractions. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that bundling municipal races with presidential or midterm cycles inflates turnout among voters motivated by partisan national issues, not local fiscal discipline. This can empower big-spending coalitions and union-backed candidates who benefit from higher, more ideologically driven electorates.

The Colorado Springs lawsuit raises questions about what the 2025 voting law actually requires and whether cities have flexibility in scheduling. Conservatives should watch whether courts side with election-timing restrictions or preserve local control—and whether Aurora's ballot measure passes, signaling whether Colorado cities are drifting toward consolidated election calendars that weaken local fiscal oversight.
