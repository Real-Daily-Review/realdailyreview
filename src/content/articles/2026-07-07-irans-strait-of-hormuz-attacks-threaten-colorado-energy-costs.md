# Iranian Aggression in Strait of Hormuz Threatens U.S. Energy Security and Colorado Wallets

Iran's escalating attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz—including a tanker struck by projectile fire and missile strikes on vessels transiting the critical waterway—underscore the folly of the Biden-Harris administration's failed Middle East policy and threaten Colorado's energy costs and economic stability.

According to 9News Denver, a tanker was set ablaze after being struck by a projectile off Oman's coast, marking the latest in a pattern of attacks targeting vessels in the narrow Persian Gulf passage. The New York Post reported that Iran's Revolutionary Guards fired at least two missiles at commercial ships Monday night, citing U.S. officials.

These Iranian provocations directly threaten American interests. The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly one-third of global seaborne oil trade—disruptions ripple through U.S. energy markets, raising gas prices at Colorado pumps and heating costs for families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation.

Republicans argue the administration's appeasement posture toward Tehran—including the failed Iran nuclear deal framework—has emboldened Iranian aggression. Conservatives say strength and deterrence, not diplomacy with hostile regimes, protect American energy security and economic interests.
