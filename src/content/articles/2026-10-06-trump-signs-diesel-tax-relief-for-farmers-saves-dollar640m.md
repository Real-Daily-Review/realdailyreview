# Trump Signs Diesel Tax Relief Order at Nebraska Rally

President Trump is putting money back in the pockets of American farmers and construction workers—exactly the kind of targeted relief Colorado's agricultural and energy sectors need.

At a campaign rally in Grand Island, Nebraska on Monday, Trump signed an executive order waiving the off-road requirement for tax-exempt red-dyed diesel and deferring federal diesel excise tax payments through the end of 2026 (Breitbart, Washington Examiner). The move temporarily allows farmers and construction crews to use cheaper red-dye diesel on highways without penalty, addressing soaring fuel costs that have squeezed rural economies nationwide.

Secretary Rollins projects the order will save farmers $640 million, according to Breitbart. For Colorado ranchers, hay producers, and construction firms already battered by inflation and regulatory costs, this is meaningful relief—diesel is a non-negotiable input cost that directly hits their bottom line.

The order sidesteps the typical bureaucratic process: Trump signed it on stage, signaling executive action over legislative gridlock. Democrats will likely argue the move reduces federal revenue, but conservatives counter that lower fuel costs stimulate economic activity and help working families and small businesses survive Biden-era inflation.

Trump is actively campaigning ahead of the 2026 midterms, hitting rallies across the country to support Republican candidates in tight races (Just The News).
