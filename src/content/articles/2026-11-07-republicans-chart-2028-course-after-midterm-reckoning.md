# Colorado Republicans Eye 2028 as National Party Resets After Midterm Losses

Republicans are already positioning for 2028 after midterm defeats reshaped the presidential landscape, with party insiders viewing the results as both a closing chapter on one race and the opening of another (Washington Examiner). The scale of GOP losses has forced a reckoning within conservative ranks about who can carry the party forward.

Meanwhile, Democrats are floating potential 2028 candidates, though progressive darling Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez faces significant structural headwinds despite the left's electoral gains this cycle (National Review). The congresswoman's path to a national ticket remains uncertain, even as her profile dominates progressive circles.

For Colorado Republicans, the 2028 reset matters enormously. The state has trended blue in recent cycles, and the GOP needs a candidate who can appeal to independent voters in the Front Range suburbs while energizing the conservative base in rural counties and the Western Slope. Colorado's electoral math—purple but leaning Democratic—will be a proving ground for whoever emerges as the Republican standard-bearer.

Conservatives should watch whether the GOP coalesces around a clear frontrunner or splinters into competing factions, as that infighting could hand Colorado to Democrats in 2028.
