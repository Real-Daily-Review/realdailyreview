# Colorado's Top Races Pit Election Security Against Progressive Activism

Colorado voters face a stark choice in November between candidates who want to restore the secretary of state's office to a neutral election administrator and those who view it as a platform for progressive causes.

The secretary of state race has become ground zero for that divide. According to the Denver Post, the office "has taken on a more prominent role in recent years amid increased politicization over the voting process." Republicans argue the position should focus narrowly on administering fair elections and securing ballot integrity—not advancing a partisan agenda.

The Colorado Sun's candidate interviews reveal the philosophical gulf. Conservatives backing Republican James Wiley emphasize election security and restoring public confidence in mail ballots, while Democrat Amanda Gonzalez's positions reflect the left's push to expand voting access and combat what progressives call "disinformation."

Similar contrasts appear in Colorado's 5th Congressional District race between Republican Jeff Crank and Democrat Jessica Killin, where the economy, healthcare, and immigration dominate debate. In the attorney general's race, Republican Michael Allen and Democrat Jena Griswold clash over immigration enforcement and the Trump administration's policies.
