# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Attack Progress

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are the invisible backbone of modern commerce, yet environmental activists and progressive regulators threaten to strangle the industry that keeps the digital world running. Complete Colorado reports that these facilities are essential infrastructure—not the enemy—powering everything from cloud computing to AI. Yet Colorado faces mounting pressure from the left to impose stricter energy regulations and water restrictions that would cripple data center expansion and drive jobs and investment to neighboring states.

Republicans and business leaders argue that Colorado's data center sector represents high-value, low-footprint economic growth: good-paying jobs, tax revenue, and technological leadership without the sprawl of traditional manufacturing. Conservatives counter that progressive climate mandates and regulatory overreach threaten to export this prosperity elsewhere.

The stakes are real for Colorado taxpayers. Data centers generate substantial tax revenue while requiring relatively modest land use compared to other industries. Yet if Colorado's regulatory environment becomes hostile—through aggressive energy mandates or water-use restrictions—companies will simply relocate operations to Texas, Arizona, or other business-friendly states.

Watch for whether Colorado's legislature and governor prioritize economic competitiveness or bow to environmental activist pressure on data center permitting and energy policy.
