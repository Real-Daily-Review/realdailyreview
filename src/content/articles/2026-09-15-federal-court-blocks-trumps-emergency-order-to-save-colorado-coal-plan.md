# Federal Court Ruling Threatens Trump's Bid to Save Colorado Coal Jobs

A federal appeals court has gutted the Trump administration's legal strategy to keep Colorado's Craig Unit 1 coal plant operating, ruling that an "emergency" order to keep a similar Michigan facility online exceeded executive authority (CPR News). Environmental groups challenging Craig Unit 1's closure say the Michigan decision—which found the administration acted without proper statutory authority—applies directly to Colorado's situation.

The ruling complicates efforts to preserve one of Colorado's largest coal employers and a major source of tax revenue for Northwest Colorado communities that depend on coal-fired generation. Republicans and energy advocates argue that keeping reliable baseload power online protects grid stability and Colorado jobs; the Biden-Harris administration and green groups counter that coal plants are economically obsolete and environmentally harmful.

The Trump administration had sought to invoke emergency powers to mandate coal plant operations, but the court rejected that approach as overreach. Colorado Sun reports that organizations opposing Craig Unit 1's planned closure believe the Michigan precedent strengthens their legal position.

The decision underscores a broader conservative concern: unelected judges and regulators are dismantling Colorado's energy sector without regard for workers, ratepayers, or rural economies.

**Watch whether Colorado Republicans and energy companies appeal this ruling or pursue legislative remedies to protect the state's remaining coal infrastructure.**
