# Denver Police Horse Retires; Separate Officer-Involved Shooting Raises Questions

A Denver Police mustang named Maverick is heading to retirement after years of service in the city's mounted unit—a feel-good story in a department increasingly under scrutiny over use-of-force decisions.

Meanwhile, bodycam footage obtained by FOX31 shows Denver officers firing shots at an alleged auto theft suspect inside a gas station, raising fresh questions about officer training and decision-making in confined spaces (KDVR). The incident underscores ongoing tensions between law enforcement and community safety advocates over how Denver police respond to property crimes and whether lethal force protocols are being applied appropriately.

Denver Police described Maverick, a previously wild mustang, as bringing "steady, gentle presence" to the department (9News). The horse's retirement marks the end of an era for the mounted unit, which has long served a community-relations function downtown.

The contrast is stark: while Maverick's departure draws warm farewells, the gas station shooting footage reignites debate over police accountability in Colorado's capital. Conservatives and law-and-order advocates generally support officer safety and discretion; critics argue Denver needs clearer use-of-force guidelines, especially in civilian-populated areas.

**Watch for:** Whether Denver's police commission reviews the gas station incident and what policy changes, if any, emerge from the bodycam evidence.
