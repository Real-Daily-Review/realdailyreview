# Tragic I-25 Incident Highlights Colorado's Highway Safety Crisis

Colorado's highways claimed another life this weekend when a FedEx truck struck a 68-year-old Fort Collins man walking in southbound lanes on I-25 near Mead Saturday morning, according to Colorado State Patrol. The fatality underscores a troubling pattern: just days earlier, a 35-year-old was killed after being struck by two vehicles in Aurora on Friday night.

These incidents raise hard questions about highway design, enforcement, and driver accountability that Colorado officials must address. While pedestrians walking on interstate highways face obvious risks, the frequency of such collisions demands scrutiny of whether current road conditions, signage, and traffic management are adequate.

Conservatives emphasize personal responsibility and question whether throwing more taxpayer money at vague "safety initiatives" will solve the problem—or whether Colorado needs smarter enforcement of existing traffic laws and better driver education. The state should focus on practical solutions: improved lighting, clearer hazard warnings, and holding reckless drivers accountable rather than expanding bureaucratic oversight.

Taxpayers should watch whether state transportation officials use these tragedies to justify new spending or regulations, or whether they'll pursue cost-effective, common-sense improvements to highway safety.
