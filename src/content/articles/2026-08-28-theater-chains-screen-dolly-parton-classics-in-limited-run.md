# Movie Theaters Honor Dolly Parton With Limited Film Screenings

Major theater chains are bringing Dolly Parton back to the big screen—a rare bright spot for an industry that's been hammered by streaming competition and changing consumer habits.

AMC Theaters and Regal will screen "Steel Magnolias" (1989) and "The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas" (1982) at approximately 200 locations nationwide in tribute to the country music legend, who died Tuesday at 80, according to *The Daily Wire*. AMC Entertainment CEO Adam Aron announced the limited run Thursday.

The move reflects Hollywood's ongoing struggle to draw audiences into theaters. While nostalgia-driven screenings of classic films offer a modest revenue opportunity, they underscore a harder truth: theatrical exhibition continues its long decline as streaming platforms dominate entertainment consumption. Colorado moviegoers will have access to these showtimes, though specifics on local theater participation remain unclear.

For conservatives who value free-market solutions, this is a straightforward case: private companies responding to consumer demand and cultural moments without government intervention. No subsidies, no mandates—just business adapting to opportunity.

The real test for theater chains remains whether limited, event-driven screenings can reverse the structural decline in theatrical attendance, or if they're merely temporary nostalgia plays masking a permanent shift in how Americans consume entertainment.

Watch whether Colorado's remaining independent and chain theaters participate, and whether similar tribute screenings become a regular survival strategy for the industry.
