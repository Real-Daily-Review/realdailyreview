# Trump Holds First Televised Cabinet Meeting at Camp David

President Trump broke with presidential tradition Friday by holding the first live, televised Cabinet meeting at Camp David, the secluded Maryland retreat typically reserved for diplomatic talks and foreign dignitaries. White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt confirmed the historic nature of the gathering, which marked Trump's 13th Cabinet meeting of his second term.

The move signals Trump's willingness to operate outside conventional Washington protocols—a hallmark of his presidency that appeals to supporters who view traditional executive practices as unnecessarily secretive. By opening the Cabinet session to live coverage, Trump continues his pattern of direct communication with the American people, bypassing traditional media filters.

The meeting comes as Trump's Cabinet tackles major policy priorities, including regulatory rollback, border enforcement, and energy development. For Colorado, Cabinet decisions on energy permitting, water rights, and federal land management will carry direct consequences for the state's economy and resource industries.

Conservatives have long criticized the opacity of executive branch operations under previous administrations. This transparency move aligns with Trump's "America First" agenda of making government more accountable to voters rather than entrenched bureaucracies.

Watch for whether this televised format becomes standard practice and what specific policy directives emerge from the Camp David session regarding energy, border security, and regulatory reform.
