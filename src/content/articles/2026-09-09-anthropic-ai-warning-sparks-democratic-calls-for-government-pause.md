# AI Existential Risk Sparks Calls for Government Pause—But Conservatives Warn Against Rushed Regulation

Three researchers from AI firm Anthropic—including departing researcher Jacob Coxen—warned this week that artificial intelligence could pose an existential threat to humanity within a decade, citing risks from "superhuman systems that can hack anything" and acquire "real power and resources" (Just The News).

The warning has already triggered predictable calls for government intervention. Michigan Democratic Senate nominee Abdul el Sayed seized on the Anthropic researchers' concerns to demand a federal AI development pause, citing the possibility that technology could "go rogue and erase humanity" (Washington Examiner).

**Here's what conservatives should know:** While legitimate safety concerns merit serious industry discussion, Democratic demands for government AI "pauses" are a Trojan horse for regulatory overreach. History shows that once bureaucrats seize control of emerging technology, innovation stalls and American competitiveness suffers—handing leadership to China and Europe.

Colorado tech workers and entrepreneurs should be wary. Heavy-handed federal AI regulation would chill investment in Colorado's growing tech sector and hand compliance costs to startups that can't afford armies of lawyers. The better path: let industry self-regulate, fund independent safety research, and let market competition drive responsible development.

Watch whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democratic members—echoes el Sayed's call for federal AI restrictions that could hobble American innovation.
