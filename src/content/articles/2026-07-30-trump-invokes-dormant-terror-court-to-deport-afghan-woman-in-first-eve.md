# Trump Admin Dusts Off 30-Year-Old Terror Court to Deport Afghan Woman Tied to Election Day Plot

The Trump administration is invoking the Alien Terrorist Removal Court for the first time since its 1996 creation, seeking to deport an Afghan woman allegedly connected to a terrorist organization and an Election Day threat. The court, signed into law by President Bill Clinton but never used in nearly three decades, offers a streamlined deportation process that bypasses standard legal protections.

According to Fox News, this marks the first deportation case processed through the specialized tribunal. Just The News reports that proceedings in the Alien Terrorist Removal Court prohibit suspects from reviewing classified evidence against them and bar legal challenges to how evidence was collected—a significant departure from ordinary due process.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the court's activation reflects legitimate national security concerns and the administration's commitment to protecting Americans from terrorist threats. The legal framework, created by a Democratic president, exists precisely for situations like this.

**What critics worry about:** Civil liberties advocates and some legal scholars contend the court's opacity and evidentiary restrictions raise due process concerns, though those objections carry less weight in conservative circles focused on border security and counterterrorism.

For Colorado, this signals the Trump administration's aggressive stance on immigration enforcement and national security—policies that resonate with voters in conservative counties but face resistance in Denver and Boulder.

Watch whether Congress moves to modernize or expand the court's authority, or if civil rights groups mount legal challenges.
