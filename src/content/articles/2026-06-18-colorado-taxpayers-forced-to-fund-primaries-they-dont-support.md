# Colorado Taxpayers Forced to Fund Primaries for Parties They Don't Support

Colorado forces taxpayers to finance primary elections for political parties—even when those voters belong to no party or a different one. That's the complaint from conservatives and reform advocates who argue the practice amounts to compelled political speech and wasteful government spending (Complete Colorado).

The issue cuts to a core conservative principle: why should a libertarian voter or Republican fund a Democratic primary, or vice versa? Taxpayer-financed primaries benefit the two major parties at public expense, critics say, while independent and third-party voters get no equivalent benefit.

Meanwhile, Colorado political ads are raising separate concerns about deceptive messaging. Campaigns are using video and audio clips from Pope Francis, Barack Obama, and Ruth Bader Ginsburg in ways that imply endorsements—without those figures' actual consent (9News Denver). The ads exploit recognizable figures to create false impressions of support, a tactic that blurs the line between legitimate campaigning and voter manipulation.

Republicans and conservatives argue both issues reflect government overreach and erosion of electoral integrity. The primary-funding scheme subsidizes partisan machinery; the fake-endorsement ads exploit voter confusion.

Watch whether Colorado legislators move to reform taxpayer-funded primaries or tighten rules on misleading political advertising before 2026.
