# Teen Killed in Lightning Strike on Mount Blue Sky

Colorado's high-altitude hiking season claimed another life Sunday when a lightning strike on Mount Blue Sky's 14,000-foot summit killed a teenage boy and injured four others, including a second minor airlifted to Denver-area hospitals with serious injuries.

The group of five hikers was struck during afternoon thunderstorms typical of Colorado's summer monsoon season. Three adults sustained minor injuries. Clear Creek County authorities responded to the scene on the popular fourteener near Idaho Springs.

The incident underscores a persistent danger for Colorado outdoor enthusiasts: sudden alpine weather. Mount Blue Sky, one of the state's most accessible 14,000-foot peaks, draws thousands annually—many unprepared for rapid weather deterioration and electrical storm risk at elevation. The Colorado Fourteeners Initiative and local hiking guides consistently warn that afternoon thunderstorms can develop with little warning above treeline, where hikers are exposed.

Conservatives and outdoor advocates emphasize personal responsibility and risk awareness over regulatory solutions. Rather than government restrictions on summit access, they argue education—about weather patterns, descent timing, and lightning safety—remains the most effective tool.

Colorado taxpayers should monitor whether this tragedy prompts calls for new regulations or summit-access restrictions that could burden outdoor recreation on public lands.
