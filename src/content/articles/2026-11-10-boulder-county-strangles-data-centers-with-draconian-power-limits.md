# Boulder County's Data Center Power Grab Will Strangle Tech Growth and Colorado Jobs

Boulder County is about to cripple its own economy with a regulatory sledgehammer disguised as environmental caution. County commissioners will vote Tuesday on draft rules that would cap new data centers at 75 kilowatts of power—essentially the same threshold as a marijuana grow operation—effectively banning meaningful tech investment in the region (Colorado Sun).

This is classic progressive NIMBYism: Boulder wants the tax revenue and job creation that come with data centers, but not the actual infrastructure. Data centers are clean, high-paying employers that don't require sprawl or resource extraction. They're exactly the kind of industry Colorado should be courting.

Republicans and free-market advocates argue these arbitrary power limits ignore market demand and punish businesses for investing in Colorado. The restrictions will simply push data center development to friendlier counties—and states—costing Boulder workers and taxpayers economic opportunity.

Meanwhile, Boulder and Douglas County voters approved expanding their boards to five commissioners each (Complete Colorado), a move that could reshape local governance. Proponents on both sides reportedly fell short of their ideal outcomes, suggesting compromise rather than clear ideological victory.

**Watch whether other Colorado counties follow Boulder's anti-growth playbook, or whether business groups push back hard enough to preserve the state's competitive edge.**
