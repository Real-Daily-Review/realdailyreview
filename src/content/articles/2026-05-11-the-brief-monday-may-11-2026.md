Global tensions, a fatal runway incident, and a Nobel laureate's health crisis shape the morning's agenda.

## U.S. Soldier's Remains Recovered Off Morocco Coast

The U.S. Army confirmed Saturday that the remains recovered from the Atlantic Ocean belong to 1st Lt. Kendrick Lamont Key Jr. of Richmond, Virginia, who went missing during military exercises in Morocco. The Army Europe and Africa command made the identification official. Key's disappearance had drawn attention to the risks of multinational training operations along the North African coast, and his family and unit now face the formal process of repatriation.

## Iran Releases Ailing Nobel Laureate on Bail

Iranian authorities granted bail to imprisoned human rights activist Narges Mohammadi and transferred her to a hospital amid mounting concern over her deteriorating health. Mohammadi, a Nobel Peace Prize laureate, had been held in Evin Prison. Her supporters had raised repeated alarms about her condition in recent months. The move stops short of a full release, and rights groups are watching closely to see whether Iranian authorities will allow her adequate medical care or return her to custody.

## Moscow's Victory Day Parade Stripped of Military Hardware

BBC Russia editor Steve Rosenberg reports that this year's Victory Day parade on Red Square was notably different from prior years — conspicuously absent was the military hardware the Kremlin typically uses to project power. The change drew immediate attention given Russia's ongoing war in Ukraine, with analysts noting that equipment losses and operational demands may be constraining what Moscow can put on public display. The parade remains one of Russia's most symbolically loaded annual events.

## Pedestrian Killed by Frontier Airlines Plane at Denver Airport

A pedestrian was struck and killed by a departing Frontier Airlines aircraft at Denver International Airport late Friday night, the airport and airline both confirmed. The plane was en route to Los Angeles at the time of the incident. Authorities have not yet released details on how the individual came to be in the aircraft's path. Federal aviation investigators are expected to examine the circumstances, and Frontier Airlines has not yet issued a detailed public statement on the sequence of events.

## Amazon Opens Door to Quebec Talks After Shutdown Announcement

Amazon said it is willing to discuss its decision to shut down operations in Quebec with Canadian and provincial government officials. The closure would result in approximately 1,700 job losses in the French-speaking province. The announcement drew swift political reaction from both Ottawa and Quebec City. Amazon has not indicated it will reverse the decision, but its openness to dialogue gives officials a narrow window to negotiate terms or seek alternatives before the shutdown takes effect.

## India Urges Austerity as Middle East Conflict Strains Energy Supply

Prime Minister Narendra Modi called on Indians to reduce fuel consumption, limit overseas travel, and cut back on gold purchases as the ongoing Middle East conflict exposes India's heavy dependence on imported energy. The appeal underscores the economic pressure building on one of the world's largest energy importers as regional instability disrupts supply chains. No emergency measures were announced alongside the appeal.
