# Denver's Foie Gras Ban: Another Day, Another Nanny-State Ballot Measure

Denver voters face yet another micromanagement mandate: Initiated Ordinance 311, which would ban the sale of foie gras and criminalize the production method used to make it (Complete Colorado). The measure exemplifies progressive overreach—government dictating what consenting adults can buy and sell based on animal-welfare ideology rather than public health or safety.

Conservatives argue this is exactly the kind of paternalistic regulation that stifles business freedom and wastes city resources on enforcement. Denver already struggles with homelessness, crime, and crumbling infrastructure; instead, city leadership entertains bans on luxury food products. The ordinance would affect restaurants and specialty food vendors, adding compliance costs and legal liability for what amounts to a lifestyle choice.

Meanwhile, Denver held a "ballot block party" in the Five Points neighborhood Friday to help voters navigate the crowded ballot (KDVR Fox31). Voter education is fine—but the real question is whether Denver's electorate will reject this nanny-state impulse or double down on it.

This matters to Colorado because Denver sets the tone for progressive policy statewide. If the foie gras ban passes, expect similar bans on other foods, production methods, and consumer choices to follow.
