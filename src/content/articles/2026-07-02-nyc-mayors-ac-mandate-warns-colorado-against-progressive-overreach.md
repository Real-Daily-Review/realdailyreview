# NYC Mayor's Thermostat Decree Shows Why Colorado Must Guard Against Progressive Overreach

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's directive ordering residents to set air conditioners to 78 degrees during a heat wave has become a cautionary tale for Coloradans watching progressive governance run amok.

Mamdani, a self-described socialist, framed the thermostat mandate as collective sacrifice to ease grid strain—a rationale that sparked immediate backlash. Critics on the right (Fox News, The Daily Wire) called it textbook command-economy thinking, with one observer quipping "Welcome to Communism!" The mayor's broader agenda compounds the concern: he's also championed a rent freeze that the Washington Examiner notes merely shifts housing costs onto landlords and property owners rather than solving the crisis.

**The Colorado angle:** While our state hasn't adopted such explicit thermostat mandates, progressive activists and some local officials have pushed similar "climate emergency" measures that restrict energy use and raise utility costs for working families. Colorado's robust energy sector—oil, gas, and coal—depends on market-driven policy, not government micromanagement of personal choices.

Republicans argue that when government dictates how citizens cool their homes, it's not climate policy; it's control. Market solutions and voluntary conservation work better than bureaucratic decrees that punish ordinary people during extreme weather.

Coloradans should watch whether similar "emergency" power-grid mandates gain traction in Denver or Boulder—and demand their elected officials reject this model outright.
