# Conservative Case for Market-Based Immigration Gains Traction Against Population-Control Left

A growing number of conservatives are rejecting the anti-growth, population-control ideology creeping into right-wing immigration debate—and the case for market-driven immigration policy is gaining ground.

The Left has long framed population growth as an environmental burden, viewing additional people as inherent polluters (Washington Examiner). But some self-described conservatives have adopted the same anti-growth conclusion through a different rhetorical path, effectively aligning with progressive neo-Malthusianism on limiting immigration and population.

This represents a fundamental split within conservative ranks. Market-based immigration advocates argue that population growth drives economic dynamism, labor-force expansion, and entrepreneurship—core free-market principles. They contend that immigration policy should reflect labor-market demand and fiscal capacity, not arbitrary caps rooted in environmental pessimism or cultural anxiety.

For Colorado specifically, this debate matters: the state has experienced significant population growth tied to both domestic migration and immigration. A market-based approach would allow Colorado businesses—particularly in energy, agriculture, and tech—to compete for talent without federal restrictions that ignore regional labor needs.

Conservatives should watch whether this intellectual shift influences 2024 immigration policy platforms and whether Colorado Republicans embrace market principles or lean into population-control rhetoric.
