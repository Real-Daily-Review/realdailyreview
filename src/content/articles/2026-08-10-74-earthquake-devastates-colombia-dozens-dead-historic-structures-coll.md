# Powerful 7.4 Earthquake Devastates Colombia; Dozens Dead

A devastating 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia on Monday morning, collapsing buildings and a nearly century-old Catholic church, killing at least 47 people according to 9News Denver, with initial reports from the New York Post citing at least 22 fatalities. The Geological Service of Colombia confirmed the quake hit around 7:34 a.m. local time in the Chocó region, triggering widespread evacuations across Colombia and neighboring Ecuador.

Footage captured the moment historic structures crumbled under the seismic force, underscoring the vulnerability of older infrastructure in earthquake-prone regions. Rescue operations are underway as authorities assess the full scope of damage and search for survivors in the rubble.

While this tragedy unfolds thousands of miles away, it serves as a stark reminder of natural disaster preparedness—a lesson Colorado communities should heed as the state faces its own seismic risks and aging infrastructure challenges. Coloradans should monitor how federal disaster response unfolds and whether Washington uses this moment to push unnecessary regulations on state energy development or water management under the guise of "resilience."
