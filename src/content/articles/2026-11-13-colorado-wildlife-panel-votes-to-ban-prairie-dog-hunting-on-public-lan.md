# Colorado Wildlife Commission Advances Prairie Dog Ban—Hunters Cry Foul

Colorado Parks and Wildlife Commission voted 6-5 to advance a petition that would ban recreational shooting of prairie dogs on public land, a move that has sparked backlash from hunters and property rights advocates who view the rodents as agricultural pests.

The narrow vote reveals deep division over the so-called "keystone species" designation. **Hunters and ranchers argue** prairie dogs destroy grazing land, damage irrigation systems, and compete with livestock for forage—making recreational shooting a legitimate management tool. They contend the commission is prioritizing environmental ideology over the economic interests of Colorado's agricultural communities.

**Wildlife advocates counter** that prairie dogs play a crucial ecological role and that their populations have declined significantly. They support the ban as conservation policy.

The real-world impact cuts across Colorado's rural economy. Ranchers in eastern Colorado and the Western Slope depend on prairie dog control to protect their operations. A statewide ban would effectively criminalize a long-standing practice and further restrict property owners' ability to manage their land.

The commission's 6-5 margin suggests this fight isn't over—expect fierce testimony at the next hearing and potential legislative pushback from rural Republicans.

**Watch:** Whether the full commission adopts this petition and whether the legislature moves to block or modify any final rule.
