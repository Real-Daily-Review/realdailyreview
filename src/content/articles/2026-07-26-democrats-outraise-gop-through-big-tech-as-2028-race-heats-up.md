# Democrats Flush With Cash as 2028 Race Takes Shape—But Colorado Republicans Must Mobilize Now

With Vice President JD Vance emerging as the likely Republican standard-bearer for 2028, Democrats are already outpacing GOP fundraising through their Silicon Valley allies. OpenAI employees alone donated more than $296,000 to Democratic candidates while Republicans received nothing, according to FEC filings reported by the Daily Caller—a stark reminder that Big Tech's political wallet remains firmly blue.

Meanwhile, the 100-day sprint to the midterms is underway, with Democrats needing just six House seats to reclaim control of the chamber (Fox News). Generic ballot polling shows Democrats hitting their highest numbers since 1996, signaling potential momentum heading into the cycle.

But not all Democrats are marching in lockstep. Some crossed party lines to vote for the NDAA and SAVE Act, while Republicans like Thomas Massie and Andy Burchett opposed them, citing concerns over endless military commitments and government overreach (Daily Caller). These fractures matter—they expose the left's internal contradictions on defense and spending.

For Colorado conservatives, the takeaway is clear: Republicans must match Democratic fundraising intensity and message discipline in statewide races, or risk ceding ground to a well-funded progressive machine bankrolled by coastal tech oligarchs.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans can build a credible primary challenger to Vance or unite behind him early.
