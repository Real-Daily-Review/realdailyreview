# Border Security Tightens as Military Laser Weapons Cut Cartel Drone Activity 75%

The U.S. military's deployment of laser weapons at the Mexico border is delivering real results—cartel drone operations have plummeted 75 percent, according to military reports cited by *Just The News*. The shift from jamming tactics to direct elimination represents a harder line on border security that conservatives have long demanded.

The timing underscores mounting tensions along the southwest border. The U.S. Consulate in Nogales, Sonora, recently issued a travel warning citing unspecified threats—a move Mexico's government flatly denied, claiming no security issues exist in the region (*Breitbart*). The disconnect highlights how cartels operate across borders while Mexican authorities downplay the threat.

For Colorado, this matters. Cartel-controlled drug trafficking networks funnel fentanyl and other narcotics through southwestern corridors into mountain communities. Tighter border security and degraded cartel logistics could reduce the flow of deadly drugs reaching Colorado streets and schools.

Conservatives argue that eliminating the cartel drone threat—rather than merely disrupting it—demonstrates the effectiveness of decisive military action. The laser-weapon success vindicates calls for robust border enforcement over diplomatic hand-wringing.
