# Summer EBT Program Hemorrhages Taxpayer Dollars as Colorado Families Leave Benefits on the Table

Colorado's Summer EBT program is burning through state and federal money with minimal accountability—35% of allocated grocery assistance went unspent by eligible families last year, according to state data reviewed by CPR News. The program distributes $120 per child when school is out, yet more than one-third of those funds sat unclaimed.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue the program exemplifies government waste: if families aren't using the benefits, why expand it? The unspent money represents taxpayer dollars that could have funded tax relief or infrastructure instead. Colorado families struggling with genuine hardship deserve help, but a program losing over a third of its budget to non-redemption raises hard questions about design, outreach, and whether the state is throwing money at a problem rather than solving it.

Democrats counter that low redemption rates reflect awareness gaps and logistical barriers—not lack of need—and argue better outreach could improve participation. They contend the program still reaches vulnerable children.

The real issue: Colorado taxpayers are funding a program with a 35% failure rate. Conservatives should demand the state audit why families aren't using these benefits before requesting additional funding.

Watch for legislative pushback on Summer EBT expansion in the next budget cycle.
