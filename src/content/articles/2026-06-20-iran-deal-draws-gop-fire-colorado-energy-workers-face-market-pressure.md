# Biden's Iran Deal Gamble Leaves Republicans Cold—And Colorado Energy Workers Watching

The Biden administration's Memorandum of Understanding with Iran is drawing sharp criticism from Republicans who argue it hands strategic leverage to a hostile regime while doing little to advance American interests. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has notably remained silent on the deal's merits, signaling internal skepticism within the Trump administration about its framework (Washington Examiner).

National Review argues there's "not much for Americans to like" about the arrangement, pointing to concerns that the MOU tilts momentum toward Tehran rather than constraining its nuclear ambitions or regional aggression.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy markets and sanctions policy directly affect Colorado's oil and gas sector. Weakened Iran sanctions could flood global markets with cheap crude, pressuring domestic producers and the workers who depend on them. Colorado's energy independence agenda—central to the state's economy and tax base—relies on strong deterrence against hostile regimes, not diplomatic accommodation.

Republicans contend the deal repeats the failures of the 2015 JCPOA, which Iran systematically violated while the West looked on. They argue genuine security requires tougher conditions, not softer ones.

Conservatives and Colorado taxpayers should monitor whether this MOU leads to broader sanctions relief that undercuts American energy producers and strengthens a regime hostile to U.S. interests.
