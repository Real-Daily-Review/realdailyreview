# Colorado Communities Mark Juneteenth With Celebrations, Parades

Colorado cities are hosting Juneteenth celebrations this weekend, with Grand Junction and Denver leading the way. Grand Junction's Lincoln Park event will feature games, historical programming, and food, while Denver's Five Points neighborhood on Welton Street is preparing for a weekend-long celebration, according to CPR News and 9News Denver.

Juneteenth, commemorating June 19, 1865—when enslaved people in Galveston, Texas learned of their emancipation—has become an official federal holiday. Colorado communities are using the occasion to highlight local history and bring residents together.

These grassroots celebrations reflect how Coloradans across different communities choose to mark significant historical moments. The events are community-driven and locally organized, allowing neighborhoods to define their own commemorations without top-down mandates.

Conservative Coloradans generally support communities organizing their own civic events and celebrations, viewing them as expressions of local autonomy rather than government-imposed observances. The decentralized nature of these celebrations—organized by neighborhoods and local groups rather than state mandate—aligns with principles of local control and voluntary association.
