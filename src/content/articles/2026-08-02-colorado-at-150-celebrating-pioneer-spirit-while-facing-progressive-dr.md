# Colorado at 150: A State Built on Freedom and Enterprise

Colorado's sesquicentennial marks a century and a half of pioneering spirit—from gold rushes to energy independence—that built the Centennial State into an economic powerhouse. Yet today's celebrations at the State Capitol reveal a state at a crossroads between its libertarian roots and progressive overreach.

The Colorado Sun and CPR News both covered statewide festivities honoring the state's August 1st founding, with leaders reflecting on Colorado's past achievements and future direction. The state's early identity was forged by individuals seeking opportunity, minimal government interference, and the freedom to develop natural resources and build wealth.

That ethos faces pressure. Modern Colorado has embraced aggressive climate regulations, gun restrictions, and land-use controls that would baffle the miners and ranchers who built this state. Energy workers in the San Juan Basin watch drilling permits shrink. Rural counties struggle under federal land mandates. Denver progressives push policies that price out working families.

Conservatives argue Colorado's strength came from economic liberty and resource development—not government planning. The question for Colorado's next 150 years: Will policymakers return to the principles that made the state great, or double down on the regulatory state that threatens its competitive edge?

Watch whether state lawmakers use the sesquicentennial moment to revisit energy policy and land-use rules that hamstring Colorado's traditional economic engines.
