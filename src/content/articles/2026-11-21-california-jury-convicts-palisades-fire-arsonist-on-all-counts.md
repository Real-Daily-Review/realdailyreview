# Palisades Fire Arsonist Convicted on All Counts

A federal jury in California has convicted Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, on all counts for deliberately starting the deadly 2025 Palisades Fire that killed 12 people, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Rinderknecht faced two federal charges—destruction of property by means of fire and arson affecting property used in interstate commerce—following a mistrial in June.

The conviction comes as California continues grappling with catastrophic wildfire seasons that have devastated communities and strained resources across the West. While this case involves criminal arson rather than policy failure, it underscores a broader concern conservatives raise about California's management of public lands and fire prevention: decades of misguided environmental regulations have restricted controlled burns and forest management, leaving tinderbox conditions that enable both natural ignition and deliberate acts of destruction.

Colorado faces similar wildfire risks, with state forests and private lands vulnerable to both negligence and deliberate arson. The conviction demonstrates that federal prosecutors can hold individuals accountable for fire-related crimes, but conservatives argue the real prevention work lies in aggressive forest management—thinning overgrown timber, removing dead wood, and conducting regular controlled burns—policies often blocked by environmental litigation and regulatory overreach.
