# Atlantic Hurricane Drought Reaches Historic Levels—But Don't Expect Climate Alarmists to Acknowledge It

The Atlantic basin has now gone without a hurricane longer than any period in the satellite era, a remarkable weather phenomenon that undercuts the relentless doomsaying from climate catastrophists who've spent years predicting ever-intensifying storm seasons.

According to *Just The News*, the current drought matches conditions last seen in 1907 and 1914—before weather satellites existed. The *New York Post* reports the Atlantic has now broken a 60-year record for the longest hurricane dry spell, with forecasters attributing the phenomenon partly to a strengthening El Niño pattern.

This matters for Colorado and the broader energy debate. Climate alarmists have weaponized hurricane forecasts for years to justify aggressive climate regulations, renewable mandates, and attacks on fossil fuel development—policies that have driven up energy costs for Colorado families and businesses. When actual weather data contradicts their apocalyptic predictions, the media largely ignores it.

The current hurricane drought doesn't prove climate change isn't real or that prudent energy policy doesn't matter. But it does expose the intellectual dishonesty of activists who cherry-pick data to support predetermined conclusions while ignoring inconvenient facts.

Conservatives should watch whether mainstream outlets and Colorado's progressive politicians acknowledge this historic calm—or simply move the goalposts to the next climate scare.
