# Fauci Texts Reveal Private Vaccine Safety Concerns Officials Publicly Dismissed

Newly released text messages show Dr. Anthony Fauci and other top federal health officials privately discussed theoretical miscarriage risks after COVID-19 vaccine doses in 2021—concerns they downplayed or denied in public statements, according to Fox News and Just The News.

Fauci reportedly warned of "significant cytokines storm and fever" following second doses with potential pregnancy implications. Yet Louisiana's attorney general notes that citizens raising identical concerns were publicly branded "anti-vaxxers and liars" by the same officials.

Republicans argue the discrepancy reveals a credibility gap at the heart of the federal pandemic response. If Fauci and CDC leadership harbored private doubts about vaccine safety in pregnancy, they should have communicated that uncertainty transparently rather than dismissing legitimate questions as misinformation.

The documents underscore a broader conservative complaint: federal health bureaucrats operated with a "trust us, don't ask questions" posture while privately entertaining the very safety discussions they publicly ridiculed. Colorado taxpayers funded these agencies and trusted their guidance; the gap between private caution and public certainty demands explanation.

Democrats have not yet responded substantively to the text releases.

Conservatives and Colorado families should watch whether Congress demands full transparency on what other safety concerns were privately discussed but publicly suppressed.
