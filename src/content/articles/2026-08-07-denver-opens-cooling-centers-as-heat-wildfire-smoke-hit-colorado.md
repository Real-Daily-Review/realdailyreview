# Denver Opens Cooling Centers as Heat and Smoke Blanket Colorado

Denver is opening cooling centers this weekend as temperatures near 100 degrees and wildfire smoke chokes the Front Range—a reminder that Colorado's summers are getting hotter and our air quality increasingly depends on forest management decisions made (or avoided) across the West.

The city is activating cooling shelters to help residents escape the heat, according to KDVR. Meanwhile, 9News reports that Air Quality Alerts cover most of Colorado due to wildfire smoke and haze, with forecasts calling for highs in the upper 90s through Friday and near 100 degrees this weekend.

The convergence of extreme heat and poor air quality underscores a conservative concern: Colorado's environmental challenges stem partly from decades of federal forest mismanagement and overly restrictive environmental regulations that have prevented active timber harvesting and fuel reduction. Conservatives argue that aggressive forest thinning and controlled burns—not just cooling centers—are the real solution to reducing catastrophic wildfires that blanket the state in smoke each summer.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that climate change is the primary driver, though they've offered limited support for aggressive logging operations on public lands.
