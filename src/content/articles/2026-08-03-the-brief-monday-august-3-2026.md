# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Monday, August 3, 2026

Colorado turns 150 today — and while the Centennial State has plenty to celebrate, there's no shortage of reminders that the fights over government spending, cultural sanity, and individual liberty are as live as ever.

---

## Colorado Turns 150: Happy Sesquicentennial, Centennial State

Today marks 150 years since Colorado achieved statehood, and it's worth pausing to appreciate what makes this state worth fighting for — the mountains, the ranches, the energy fields, the independent spirit. But the best birthday gift Coloradans could give themselves is electing leaders who actually respect the freedoms that made this state great in the first place. Here's to 150 more years — if we can keep it.

---

## Colorado Taxpayers Eating the Cost of SNAP Mismanagement

Colorado's food stamp program is hemorrhaging money due to processing errors, and taxpayers are on the hook for tens of millions of dollars that could be recovered before the fiscal year closes — if state administrators would simply do their jobs correctly. This is exactly the kind of bureaucratic incompetence conservatives have warned about for years: government expands a program, manages it sloppily, and ordinary Coloradans foot the bill. The Polis administration owes taxpayers a full accounting and a concrete fix.

---

## Grass Fire Near Commerce City Contained Quickly

Crews moved fast Sunday to contain a grass fire near Tower Road in Commerce City, lifting an evacuation order within roughly 30 minutes. Colorado's fire season is a reminder of why responsible land management, reliable water infrastructure, and well-funded local emergency services matter — and why ranchers and rural communities can't afford the kind of regulatory gridlock that ties the hands of the people who actually know this land.

---

## Senate Leaders Strike Short-Term Spending Deal

Senate leaders are pushing a continuing resolution to fund the federal government through early December, punting the hard budget decisions past the midterm elections. Republicans should be clear-eyed: a short-term patch is not fiscal discipline. Kicking the can down the road while federal spending continues to balloon is not a win for Colorado taxpayers or anyone else who believes Washington has a spending problem — not a revenue problem.

---

## WNBA Coach Wears Pro-Trans Shirt to Protest Sophie Cunningham

Minnesota Lynx head coach Cheryl Reeve reportedly showed up wearing a "Trans Kids Belong" shirt, widely interpreted as a direct protest of Indiana Fever guard Sophie Cunningham, who has been outspoken in defense of women's sports. Conservatives say this is exactly the kind of institutional pressure designed to silence athletes who dare to stand up for biological reality and fair competition. Cunningham deserves credit for not backing down — and Colorado parents watching their daughters compete should be paying attention to where this cultural fight is headed.

---

## In Memoriam: Rep. Kay Granger, 83

Former Texas Republican Rep. Kay Granger — Fort Worth's first female mayor and a nearly three-decade veteran of Congress — has died at 83. Granger broke real barriers the old-fashioned way: through hard work, constituent service, and principled conservative governance. She was a reminder that the Republican Party has always made room for strong women who lead with results, not grievance.

## What to Watch

Keep your eye on how Colorado's Democratic-controlled legislature responds to the SNAP error-rate story — if they ignore it, that tells you everything about their priorities with your money. On the federal level, watch whether the continuing resolution deal holds or collapses under pressure from either flank; Colorado's federal contractors and defense workers have skin in that game. And as the WNBA culture war heats up, watch for whether Colorado's own sports institutions feel pressure to take sides.
