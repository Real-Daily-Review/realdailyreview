# Former U.S. Ambassador Accused of Profiting From Taliban Deal

A former senior U.S. diplomat appears to be cashing in on America's chaotic withdrawal from Afghanistan—a move that has drawn sharp criticism from conservatives as the nation marks the 23rd anniversary of 9/11.

Zalmay Khalilzad, who served as the most senior Afghan American in the Bush, Obama, Trump, and Biden administrations, was in the White House Situation Room when the first plane hit the World Trade Center on September 11, 2001. According to the Washington Examiner, Khalilzad now appears positioned to profit from dealings tied to the Taliban following the 2021 U.S. withdrawal.

Conservatives argue the arrangement raises serious ethical and national security concerns. The optics are particularly damaging: a high-ranking official who witnessed 9/11 firsthand now potentially benefiting from a Taliban-connected deal—exactly the kind of government insider profiteering that fuels public distrust.

The story underscores broader Republican concerns about how the Biden administration's Afghanistan exit was handled and who benefited from it. Colorado taxpayers and veterans who served in Afghanistan have legitimate questions about whether their sacrifice is being dishonored by lucrative post-government arrangements with hostile regimes.

Watch for congressional Republicans to demand full transparency on Khalilzad's financial interests and any government contracts or approvals tied to Taliban-related business dealings.
