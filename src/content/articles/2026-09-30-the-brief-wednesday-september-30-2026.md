# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Wednesday, September 30, 2026

Colorado heads into the final day of September watching storm systems roll in from two directions — one meteorological, one political — and neither is entirely welcome.

---

## 🌧️ Hurricane Remnants and Heavy Rain Threaten Eastern Colorado

The remnants of Hurricane Polo are tracking toward Colorado this week, with eastern and southeastern portions of the state expected to take the hardest hit. Separate forecasts also warn of heavy rain and possible flooding in the Denver metro area midweek. For ranchers, farmers, and rural communities on the Eastern Plains already navigating a tough year, this is not abstract weather news — it's a direct threat to livestock, crops, and infrastructure. Colorado's rural counties deserve the same emergency preparedness attention that gets lavished on the Front Range.

---

## 💊 Eric Cantor Lands at PhRMA — The Swamp Runs Deep

Former House Majority Leader Eric Cantor has taken the helm of PhRMA, the pharmaceutical industry's premier lobbying organization — and one of Washington's most powerful special-interest machines. Cantor, who famously cashed out of Congress and into Wall Street after his 2014 primary loss, now leads the group that has spent decades fighting drug pricing transparency and market competition. Republicans who campaigned on draining the swamp should take note: this is exactly the revolving-door culture that fuels voter cynicism. Colorado patients paying sky-high drug prices deserve real market competition, not a better-connected lobby.

---

## 🗳️ Vance: Trump Team War-Gamed a Senate Floor Takeover to Pass SAVE America Act

Vice President JD Vance revealed that he and President Trump actively strategized a Senate floor takeover to force passage of the SAVE America Act, declaring "there can be no more excuses" for congressional inaction. The disclosure underscores the administration's frustration with Senate Republicans who have slow-walked key legislative priorities. For Colorado conservatives who sent their representatives to Washington to deliver results — not procedural excuses — this is a signal that the White House is done playing nice. The question now is whether Senate Republicans will get in line or get out of the way.

---

## 🎙️ Schmitt's Six-Hour VP Audition: A Cautionary Tale

Sen. Eric Schmitt's (R-MO) not-so-subtle campaign for the 2028 vice presidency reportedly launched and collapsed within six hours Tuesday — killed partly by a press corps eager to make it a story and partly by Schmitt's own hubris. It's a reminder that in the current Republican environment, loyalty and discipline matter more than ambition. Colorado conservatives watching the 2028 field take shape should pay attention to which figures are building real records and which are simply auditioning. The base has little patience for politicians who put their own advancement ahead of the agenda.

---

## 💪 Will Reeve's Cancer Diagnosis: A Reminder to Take Your Health Seriously

Will Reeve, son of the late Christopher Reeve, revealed Tuesday that he nearly dismissed the first sign of his testicular cancer as nothing. His candid social media disclosure is a straightforward public health reminder — particularly for men who tend to brush off warning signs. This one transcends politics: get checked, take symptoms seriously, and don't wait.

## 📍 What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the storm track through the end of the week — eastern Colorado communities should be monitoring county emergency management updates closely as Polo's remnants push north. On the political front, watch for Senate Republican leadership's response to Vance's SAVE America Act pressure; Colorado's own congressional delegation will face questions about where they stand. And as the 2028 jockeying quietly begins, Colorado conservatives should be asking every potential candidate the same question: what have you actually delivered?
