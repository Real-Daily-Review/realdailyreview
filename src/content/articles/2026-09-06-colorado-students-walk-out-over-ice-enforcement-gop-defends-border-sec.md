# Colorado Students Stage Walkouts Over Immigration Enforcement

Colorado schools saw coordinated student walkouts this week protesting federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement operations, according to CPR News. Organizers claimed students felt their voices weren't being heard on immigration policy.

Republicans and conservatives argue that walkouts conflate legitimate border-security concerns with anti-enforcement activism. They contend that ICE operations target individuals who entered the country illegally or violated immigration law—a distinction progressives often blur. GOP lawmakers have consistently supported enforcement as essential to rule of law and sovereignty.

Democrats and immigration advocates counter that ICE actions disproportionately affect vulnerable communities and families, and that student activism represents legitimate civic engagement on a moral issue.

The walkouts reflect Colorado's broader immigration divide. The state has seen competing pressures: Denver and Boulder lean heavily progressive on sanctuary policies, while rural and suburban areas—particularly those affected by illegal immigration's costs—favor stricter enforcement. Colorado's business community remains split between those seeking labor flexibility and those prioritizing wage protection for citizens.

The Colorado Sun separately reported on the state's growing gravel-racing scene, a different kind of endurance test drawing adventurers across 643 miles of terrain.

Conservatives should monitor whether school districts face pressure to restrict student political speech or whether these walkouts escalate into demands for formal sanctuary policies that could strain state-federal relations.
