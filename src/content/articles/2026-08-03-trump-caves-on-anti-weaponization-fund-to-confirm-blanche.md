# GOP Senators Force Trump to Scrap 'Anti-Weaponization' Fund to Confirm Blanche

Trump's push to establish an $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund hit a wall when Republican senators John Cornyn and Thom Tillis refused to back Todd Blanche's nomination as attorney general without guarantees the fund would be killed (Washington Examiner). Rather than lose the confirmation fight, Trump capitulated: Cornyn announced a deal Sunday rescinding the fund in writing and restating Blanche's testimony that the DOJ won't be weaponized (Washington Examiner).

Trump had vowed to fight for the anti-weaponization initiative, framing it as essential to preventing future political persecution of conservatives—a core concern after the prosecutions of Trump himself and allies (Fox News). But Cornyn and Tillis apparently feared the fund could be misused or set a dangerous precedent for executive power.

The episode reveals fractures within the GOP over executive authority and DOJ independence. Trump wanted institutional safeguards against selective prosecution; skeptical Republicans prioritized traditional oversight. Blanche's confirmation now appears back on track before the August recess.

**Colorado conservatives should watch whether this capitulation signals weakness on Trump's broader agenda to reform a Justice Department many believe has been weaponized against the right.**
