# Federal Reserve Holds Rates Steady as Inflation Pressures Persist

The Federal Reserve under new Chair Kevin Warsh has signaled an end to rate-cut expectations, removing dovish language from its policy statement after a two-day meeting, according to 9News Denver. The move reflects persistent inflation headwinds: wholesale business inflation exceeded 6% in May, with consumer inflation above 4%, driven partly by geopolitical tensions including the war in Iran (Just The News).

**The Colorado angle:** Higher interest rates ripple through Colorado's economy—making mortgages costlier for homebuyers, pressuring small businesses seeking credit, and complicating expansion plans for energy producers already navigating regulatory uncertainty. Colorado's real estate market, already strained by affordability crises in Denver and ski towns, faces headwinds if rates remain elevated.

**What conservatives note:** The Fed's pivot away from rate cuts signals acknowledgment that inflation remains a real problem, not transitory. Republicans have long argued that excessive government spending—not just monetary policy—fuels price pressures. Warsh's tighter stance may provide some relief, but only if Congress restrains its own fiscal appetite. Colorado taxpayers should watch whether the state legislature uses any economic slowdown as cover for new taxes or whether it finally embraces spending discipline.
