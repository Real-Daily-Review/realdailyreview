# Fourth of July Surveillance Drones: Government Overreach Takes Flight

President Trump hosted America's 250th birthday celebration on the National Mall Saturday, but while fireworks lit the sky, federal regulators quietly expanded their reach into the clouds above.

Public safety agencies nationwide are deploying drones to monitor Fourth of July celebrations and enforce illegal fireworks bans, enabled by new FAA rules that fast-tracked "Drone as First Responder" (DFR) programs by slashing bureaucratic review processes (The Daily Wire). The regulatory loosening allows cities and counties to launch surveillance operations with minimal oversight.

Conservatives should view this expansion with alarm. While framed as public safety, the accelerated drone deployment represents exactly the kind of government surveillance creep that threatens individual liberty. Fewer regulatory hurdles mean less transparency and fewer checks on how agencies use this technology—and where it goes next.

Colorado communities hosting Independence Day events should ask hard questions: Are local police deploying these drones? Under what authority? What footage is collected, stored, and shared? The federal government's eagerness to remove red tape for surveillance tools should concern every taxpayer who values privacy and limited government.

Watch whether Colorado cities adopt DFR programs without public debate—and demand accountability if they do.
