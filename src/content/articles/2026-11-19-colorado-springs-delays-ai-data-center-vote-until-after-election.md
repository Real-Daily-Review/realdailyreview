# Colorado Springs Delays AI Data Center Decision Until After April Election

Colorado Springs City Council is punting on Project Taurus—a major AI data center proposal—by postponing its vote until after April's municipal election, citing the need for a required study that hasn't even been commissioned yet (CPR News). The delay is a classic bureaucratic stall tactic: demand a study, don't order it, then use its absence as cover to avoid a decision before voters head to the polls.

The timing is suspicious. Council members are essentially telling taxpayers and businesses: "We can't decide until we study this," while doing nothing to actually start that study. This leaves Project Taurus—which could bring jobs and tax revenue to the Springs—in limbo while politicians protect themselves from election-year controversy.

The delay also intersects with Colorado's contentious voting law. Colorado Springs is already facing a lawsuit over holding municipal elections in April rather than November, marking the first enforcement action since the 2025 voting law took effect (Complete Colorado). Whether that legal battle influences council's appetite for controversial decisions before April remains unclear.

**What conservatives should watch:** whether the study ever materializes, and whether Project Taurus survives the political theater or gets killed by regulatory delay—a favorite progressive tactic for blocking development.
