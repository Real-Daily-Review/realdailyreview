# College Degree Value Collapsing—Colorado Families Should Rethink the Four-Year Script

The promise that every high school graduate needs a bachelor's degree to succeed is crumbling, and Colorado families are right to question whether tuition debt makes sense anymore.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, a growing share of high school graduates are enrolling in college under the assumption they're making a sound financial investment in their futures. But the underlying economics no longer support that narrative. As degree holders flood the job market, the wage premium that once justified four years of tuition and lost wages has eroded significantly.

For Colorado, this matters. State universities have raised tuition repeatedly, and families are saddled with student debt while employers increasingly value skills and experience over credentials. Trade schools, apprenticeships, and direct workforce entry often deliver faster returns and less financial risk.

Conservatives argue the higher-education establishment has oversold college as the only path to prosperity, inflating enrollment and costs while devaluing alternatives that build real skills. Meanwhile, universities have expanded administrative bloat and ideological programming rather than controlling expenses or improving job outcomes.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether state legislators push back on university spending and tuition hikes, or continue subsidizing an industry that's failing to deliver value to students.
