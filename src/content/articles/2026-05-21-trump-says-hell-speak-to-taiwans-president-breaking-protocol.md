# Trump Says He'll Speak to Taiwan's President, Breaking Diplomatic Protocol

Donald Trump said he will speak directly with Taiwan's president, a departure from decades of U.S. diplomatic protocol that has avoided high-level direct contact to prevent antagonizing China, according to BBC World.

The statement comes as the U.S. considers a $14 billion arms sale to Taiwan, which China claims as its own territory. Trump's willingness to engage with Taiwan's leadership marks a shift from the carefully managed diplomatic distance the U.S. has historically maintained.

Analysts caution against reading the move as a sign of deepening U.S.-Taiwan relations. According to Al Jazeera English, observers view the remarks more as evidence of Trump's unpredictability than as a substantive policy shift toward the island.

The remarks highlight the delicate balance the U.S. maintains between supporting Taiwan and managing its relationship with China. Taiwan's status remains one of the most sensitive geopolitical issues in the region, with Beijing viewing any elevation of Taiwan's diplomatic standing as a challenge to its sovereignty claims.

Watch for whether Trump follows through on the call and how China responds to any direct presidential engagement with Taiwan's leadership.
