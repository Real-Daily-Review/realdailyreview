# Mistrial Mob: How Internet Vigilantism Is Weaponizing the Clancy Case

A lone juror's refusal to convict in the Lindsay Clancy triple-murder trial has exposed a dangerous collapse of judicial process—replaced by social-media mob justice and death threats against the holdout juror himself.

Michael P. Desronvil, 48, the sole juror who forced a mistrial, has retained attorney Edward Paltzik (who has represented President Trump) as he faces what his lawyer calls a "lunatic mob" hurling death threats (Breitbart). The Daily Wire reports Desronvil is now defending himself against scorching attacks from fellow jurors and media outlets determined to overturn his verdict.

The deeper problem: internet culture has inverted basic moral clarity. As the Washington Examiner argues, we've entered an era where "toxic empathy" and online pressure campaigns are overriding the presumption of innocence and jury deliberation itself. The case—involving a Massachusetts mother accused of killing her three young children—has become a flashpoint where progressive narratives about mental health and victimhood are being weaponized to delegitimize a juror's conscience.

This matters for Colorado: it's a stark reminder that jury nullification, jury intimidation, and social-media lynch mobs threaten the rule of law everywhere. When jurors face death threats for exercising their constitutional duty, the entire system corrodes.

Watch whether Massachusetts prosecutors pursue a retrial—and whether courts finally address the weaponization of jurors by online mobs.
