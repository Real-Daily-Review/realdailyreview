# Trump-Backed Daylight Saving Time Bill Advances—Colorado Should Decide for Itself

The Sunshine Protection Act cleared a House Rules Committee hurdle this week, moving President Trump's push for permanent Daylight Saving Time closer to floor consideration. The measure would allow states to adopt year-round DST without federal restriction—a federalism win that lets Colorado and other states chart their own course rather than submit to a one-size-fits-all mandate from Washington.

Republicans backing the bill argue it reduces regulatory burden and respects state sovereignty. Trump supports the effort, which passed the Rules Committee 6-4 (Fox News, New York Post).

Opponents warn that permanent DST could disrupt natural circadian rhythms and sleep patterns, raising health concerns. Critics also note that Colorado's high altitude and northern latitude create unique daylight patterns—meaning permanent DST may affect Coloradans differently than residents of lower, southern states.

The real conservative principle here: let Colorado voters and lawmakers decide whether permanent DST serves their interests, rather than having Congress impose it nationwide. If the bill passes, expect Colorado's legislature to weigh in on whether the state should opt in or stick with standard time.

Watch whether Colorado's Republican-controlled legislature moves to adopt permanent DST or pushes back on the grounds of local control and public health data specific to the state.
