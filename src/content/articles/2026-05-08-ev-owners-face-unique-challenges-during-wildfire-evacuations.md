# EV Owners Face Range Anxiety During Wildfire Evacuations

Electric vehicle owners fleeing wildfires confront a distinct set of challenges that gas-powered cars don't, according to the Wall Street Journal.

The primary concern is battery range. During evacuations, drivers may lack time to charge fully before departing, and charging infrastructure along evacuation routes may be unavailable or overwhelmed. Additionally, wildfires can damage power grids, limiting access to public charging stations even if roads remain passable.

However, EVs offer some advantages in emergency scenarios. They can operate without fuel supply chains, which may be disrupted during disasters. Some models allow owners to use their batteries as backup power sources for essential devices.

The WSJ report examines practical guidance for EV owners in fire-prone regions, including pre-evacuation charging strategies and route planning that accounts for battery capacity.

As wildfire seasons intensify across the western United States, EV adoption continues to rise, making evacuation preparedness an increasingly relevant concern for electric vehicle owners and emergency management officials.

Watch for guidance from automakers and state emergency agencies on EV-specific evacuation protocols.
