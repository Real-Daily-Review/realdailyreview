# Colorado Healthcare System Must Learn From Lindsay Clancy Tragedy—Before Another Mother Falls Through Cracks

A Denver social work professor is pushing Colorado's healthcare industry to strengthen postpartum psychosis screening and intervention following the high-profile Lindsay Clancy case, according to KDVR Fox31. The case—which drew national attention—has sparked legitimate questions about whether hospitals and mental health providers adequately identify and treat severe postpartum psychiatric episodes before tragedy strikes.

Conservatives and healthcare professionals argue the focus should be on systemic gaps: better provider training, faster psychiatric evaluation protocols, and clearer communication between obstetrics and mental health departments. The question isn't whether government should micromanage medicine, but whether Colorado's existing healthcare infrastructure is equipped to catch warning signs.

Troublingly, the case has also exposed how social media platforms amplify dangerous narratives. The Washington Examiner notes that TikTok has become a vehicle for sympathy campaigns that obscure accountability—a reminder that Chinese-controlled apps shape American discourse in ways hostile to public safety and truth.

The mistrial itself raised procedural concerns: Breitbart reports jurors admitted using their own medical knowledge to interpret toxicology evidence, potentially compromising the trial's integrity.
