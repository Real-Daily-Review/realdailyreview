# Trump Shrinks Utah Monuments, Opening Path for Energy Development

President Trump has signed an executive order reducing two Utah national monuments by roughly 90 percent, a move that could unlock energy development across millions of acres while reigniting the land-use battle between Republicans and environmental groups.

Grand Staircase-Escalante and Bears Ears national monuments have been reduced from 3 million acres to 300,000, according to Fox News. The action removes federal drilling protections that had blocked coal and uranium extraction—resources Republicans argue are essential to American energy independence and economic growth.

Conservatives view the move as a necessary correction to what they see as federal overreach. The Obama and Biden administrations used the Antiquities Act to lock up vast Western lands, they argue, preventing responsible resource development and harming rural economies. Republicans contend that energy independence, not monument preservation, should drive land policy.

Environmental groups and Democrats will almost certainly challenge the order in court, claiming the reductions violate the Antiquities Act and threaten irreplaceable public lands. The Left frames monument protection as essential to climate and conservation goals.

For Colorado, the decision matters: Utah's energy development affects regional markets, water resources, and the broader fight over federal land control that directly impacts Colorado's own mineral and water interests.
