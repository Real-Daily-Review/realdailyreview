# Late-Night Host Admits Democratic Interview Too Risky for Broadcast TV

Jimmy Kimmel is sidestepping FCC equal-time rules by moving his interview with Texas Democratic Senate nominee James Talarico to YouTube instead of airing it on ABC—a move that exposes how broadcast regulations can handcuff networks when they favor one party.

Kimmel announced Wednesday that the Talarico sit-down would air "under unusual circumstances" on the streaming platform, citing concerns about triggering the FCC's equal-time doctrine, which requires broadcasters to offer comparable airtime to opposing candidates (The Daily Wire). The late-night host claimed legal standoffs with the FCC forced the decision, though he noted he's previously interviewed major figures "from Hillary Clinton ... to Donald Trump" (Just The News).

Conservative outlets are skeptical. Breitbart argues Kimmel is "peddling the same lies" Stephen Colbert used seven months ago when claiming censorship over a similar Talarico interview—suggesting the real issue is avoiding the regulatory burden of balancing Democratic airtime with Republican access, not actual FCC threats.

The incident highlights a broader conservative concern: broadcast regulations designed to ensure fairness can paradoxically discourage networks from covering Democratic candidates at all, rather than extending equal coverage to Republicans. For Colorado voters, it's a reminder that media gatekeepers—even when constrained by law—still shape which candidates get prime-time exposure.
