# Early Voting Opens as GOP Faces Midterm Headwinds Over Trump Tariffs

Americans have just over six weeks to decide whether Republicans keep control of Congress or hand it to Democrats, with early voting now underway across the nation (9News Denver). The stakes are high for Colorado voters, who will determine whether the state's congressional delegation leans toward limited government and pro-energy policies or doubles down on progressive regulation.

But Republicans face a self-inflicted wound: President Trump's tariff regime is weighing down GOP candidates heading into the midterms. The Washington Examiner reports that while Trump claims his tariffs delivered "the most successful two years financially … in the history of the presidency," conservatives argue the trade war is inflating costs for Colorado families and businesses—from manufacturers to ranchers dependent on equipment imports. Higher tariffs mean higher prices at the pump and grocery store, a drag on household budgets when inflation already stings.

Republicans counter that strategic tariffs protect American workers and manufacturing. But the political math is brutal: voters care about their wallets first, and tariff-driven price hikes undercut the GOP message on the economy.

Colorado conservatives should watch whether Republican candidates distance themselves from Trump's tariff policy or double down—a choice that could determine whether the state stays red or flips further blue.
