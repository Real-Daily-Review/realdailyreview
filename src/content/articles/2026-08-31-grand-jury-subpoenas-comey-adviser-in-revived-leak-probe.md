# Federal Grand Jury Revives Comey Leak Probe—Trump Administration Reopens Investigation

A federal grand jury has subpoenaed Daniel Richman, a longtime adviser to former FBI Director James Comey, as part of a revived classified leak investigation (Fox News, Just The News). The probe, which Trump administration officials pursued during the first term before stalling under the Biden administration in 2021, is now back under active investigation.

The subpoena marks a significant escalation in scrutiny of Comey's inner circle and raises questions about potential mishandling of classified information at the highest levels of the FBI. Republicans have long argued that the bureau's leadership operated with a two-tiered system of justice—aggressively pursuing leaks and security violations against Trump associates while overlooking similar conduct by Clinton-era officials and their allies.

Conservatives view the renewed investigation as overdue accountability for what they characterize as institutional corruption and selective enforcement. Democrats are likely to frame the probe as politically motivated retaliation, though they have not yet issued formal statements on the subpoena.

The investigation carries no direct Colorado angle but underscores broader conservative concerns about federal law enforcement credibility and the need for equal application of the law regardless of political affiliation.

**Watch for:** Whether the grand jury issues additional subpoenas to other Comey associates or produces indictments that test the statute of limitations on the original leak allegations.
