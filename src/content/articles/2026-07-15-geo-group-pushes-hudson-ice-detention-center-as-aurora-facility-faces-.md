# GEO Group's Hudson Detention Center Plan Tests Colorado's Immigration Enforcement Appetite

Private prison contractor GEO Group is moving ahead with plans to convert the shuttered Big Horn facility in Hudson into a 1,200-bed ICE detention center—a move that underscores Colorado's role in federal immigration enforcement even as health and safety questions dog the company's existing Aurora facility.

The Hudson project arrives amid fresh scrutiny of GEO Group's operations. Colorado Public Radio and 9News Denver report at least one confirmed tuberculosis case at the company's Aurora ICE detention center, with the Adams County Health Department alleging the facility is obstructing investigation into whether additional cases exist. ICE denies active TB cases at the Aurora site (CPR News).

**The conservative case:** Securing the border and processing detained immigrants efficiently requires operational facilities. GEO Group operates under federal contract, and detention centers are necessary infrastructure for immigration enforcement—a core federal responsibility.

**Progressive critics** (Colorado Sun) raise transparency, emergency access, and due process concerns, though these objections often reflect broader opposition to immigration enforcement itself rather than facility-specific safety failures.

The health department's access dispute is the real red flag. If GEO Group is genuinely obstructing public health investigations, that's a legitimate accountability issue regardless of one's immigration stance.
