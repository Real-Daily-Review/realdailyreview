# Trump's Press Ban Draws Rare Rebuke From Right; Senate GOP Targets Chinese Vape Smuggling

President Trump's war on the press is backfiring—and not just with the left. National Review editorial board argues Trump should rescind his press ban, calling it "a violation of the spirit of a free press" and urging him to confront reporters directly instead (National Review). Colorado Sun columnist Mike Littwin compares the move to Trump's Iran policy, calling it a disaster.

The rare conservative split reflects deeper concerns: even Trump allies recognize that banning press access weakens rather than strengthens his position. Republicans argue direct confrontation and message discipline work better than exclusion.

Meanwhile, Senate Republicans are pressing Trump on a different front: Chinese vape smuggling. Sen. Ted Budd (R-NC) and 10 GOP colleagues urged Trump to demand Xi Jinping crack down on unauthorized Chinese-made vapes flooding U.S. markets (Washington Examiner). The letter, released ahead of Trump's Xi meeting in Washington, signals GOP concern over illicit nicotine products reaching American youth and undercutting legitimate U.S. manufacturers.

For Colorado—home to a robust vaping regulation debate and youth health concerns—the federal pressure on Beijing could ease state-level enforcement burdens if effective.

Conservatives should watch whether Trump actually follows National Review's advice on press access, and whether the Xi meeting yields concrete commitments on vape smuggling enforcement.
