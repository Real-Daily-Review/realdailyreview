# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has dealt a serious blow to water utilities serving Beulah, a small Colorado community of roughly 800 residents, forcing water managers into emergency mode to restore service to evacuated families (Colorado Sun). Unlike most active fires across the state, this one directly damaged critical water systems—a worst-case scenario for rural Colorado towns already stretched thin on resources.

CPR News reports that residents mounted a fierce defense, but many homes were lost in the blaze. Now comes the harder part: water managers are taking first steps toward supplying clean water while assessing the full scope of infrastructure damage (Colorado Sun).

For conservatives and taxpayers, this underscores a hard reality: Colorado's rural communities depend on aging water infrastructure that often lacks redundancy or federal backup. Rebuilding will likely trigger demands for state and federal disaster spending—and questions about whether Colorado's regulatory environment and permitting processes allow communities to harden their systems fast enough before the next fire season.

The real test: whether state and local officials prioritize rapid, cost-effective infrastructure repair over bureaucratic delays and new environmental mandates that could slow recovery.
