# Trump Deregulates Farm Meat Processing—Colorado Ranchers Could Break Free From Big Ag Monopoly

President Trump is moving to let farmers and ranchers process their own meat, a direct challenge to the stranglehold four massive corporations hold over U.S. food production. After conservative media personality Glenn Beck raised the issue during a live interview, Trump announced Friday on Truth Social that deregulation was coming, calling ranchers and farmers "a number one priority."

The move targets a genuine problem: Tysons, Cargill, JBS USA, and National Beef Packing Co. control the vast majority of meat processing in America, leaving independent producers with few options and thin margins. Colorado's ranching community—a backbone of the state's rural economy—has long chafed under federal processing rules that favor industrial-scale operations and squeeze family operations.

Conservatives argue this is textbook regulatory overreach: Washington bureaucrats have created barriers to entry that protect corporate giants while crushing competition and innovation. Allowing on-farm processing restores property rights and lets producers serve local markets without navigating a maze of federal red tape.

Democrats and progressive food-safety advocates typically oppose such deregulation, citing food safety concerns—though the specifics of Trump's proposal remain unclear.
