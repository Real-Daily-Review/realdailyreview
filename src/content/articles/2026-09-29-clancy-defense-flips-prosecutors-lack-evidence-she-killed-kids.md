# Defense Shifts Strategy in Clancy Case as Mistrial Raises Questions About Evidence

A Colorado mother's defense attorney is now arguing prosecutors lack sufficient evidence to prove she killed her three children—a dramatic reversal from the first trial that ended in mistrial.

Lindsay Clancy's lawyer, Kevin Reddington, told the court Tuesday that the state failed to meet its burden of proof, repeatedly asking "Where is the evidence?" (Just The News, Washington Examiner). This marks a notable shift from Clancy's initial defense, which did not dispute that she caused her children's deaths but focused instead on her mental state.

The mistrial was declared September 4 after a deadlocked jury, reigniting scrutiny over the case and maternal mental health issues (9News Denver). Reddington's new tack suggests the defense believes the prosecution's case may not withstand judicial scrutiny on evidentiary grounds—a significant development that could undermine the state's ability to secure a conviction on retrial.

The case has drawn attention to how Colorado courts handle complex criminal matters involving mental health defenses and the sufficiency of forensic evidence. If prosecutors cannot overcome these evidentiary challenges, the outcome could affect how similar cases are prosecuted statewide.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether the court rules on the sufficiency of evidence and whether the state pursues a costly retrial or seeks alternative resolution.
