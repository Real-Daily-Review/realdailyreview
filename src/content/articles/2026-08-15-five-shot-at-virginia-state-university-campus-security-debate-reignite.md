# Virginia State University Shooting Leaves Five Wounded; Campus Security Questions Mount

A shooting at Virginia State University near Richmond early Saturday left five people injured, with at least one gunman opening fire near dormitories around 1:30 a.m. ET, according to the Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire. One victim remained in critical condition after police discovered the wounded outside residence halls. The incident occurred just two days before the semester's start, forcing the campus into lockdown as law enforcement investigated.

The shooting raises familiar questions about campus safety protocols and whether universities adequately protect students in their care. While details remain limited, the incident underscores a broader concern: how institutions balance open campuses with security measures that actually deter criminals.

Colorado universities face similar pressures. State schools must balance progressive demands for "gun-free zones" with the reality that such policies disarm law-abiding students and staff while criminals ignore them entirely. Conservatives argue that allowing trained, armed security personnel and permit-holders on campus—rather than creating soft targets—offers genuine protection.

Watch for how Virginia State and other universities respond: Will they double down on ineffective gun-free policies, or will they acknowledge that armed deterrence works?
