# U.S. Escalates Military Strikes on Iran as Energy Markets Brace for Disruption

Renewed American military strikes on Iran are raising the specter of global energy disruption—a direct threat to Colorado's economy and gas prices at the pump.

The U.S. military launched a new wave of strikes Wednesday morning, according to the New York Post, as Iranian officials claim more than 30 people have been killed in recent days. Iran has threatened to halt energy exports and potentially blockade the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments.

**The Colorado angle:** Energy price volatility ripples through Colorado's economy. Higher fuel costs hit consumers and businesses; instability in Middle Eastern oil markets can pressure domestic energy development and job growth in the state's oil and gas sector. Colorado taxpayers and workers depend on stable, affordable energy markets.

**What conservatives argue:** Military strength and clear deterrence prevent escalation. Weakness invites aggression. However, Republicans also emphasize that energy independence—achieved through responsible domestic production, not foreign entanglement—shields America from Middle Eastern volatility. Colorado's energy workers and consumers benefit from expanding U.S. oil and gas output, not reliance on unstable global markets.

Democrats have largely avoided direct comment, though some progressives oppose military action and call for diplomatic resolution.

**Watch next:** Whether Iran follows through on blockade threats and how global oil markets respond—and whether Colorado's energy sector sees renewed investment or faces regulatory headwinds from the Biden-Harris administration.
