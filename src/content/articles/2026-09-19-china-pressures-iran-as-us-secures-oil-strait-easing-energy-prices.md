# China Pressures Iran as U.S. Military Secures Critical Oil Chokepoint

China is reportedly leaning on Iran to rein in Houthi proxies and reopen the Strait of Hormuz—a telling sign that even Beijing recognizes the economic damage from Middle Eastern chaos. According to Breitbart, after Iran's failed diplomatic outreach to BRICS this week, Chinese officials privately urged Tehran to control the Yemeni insurgents who've repeatedly attacked shipping and blocked a critical energy corridor.

The pressure appears to be working. The Washington Examiner reports that oil and gas shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have hit their highest level in six months, with U.S. Central Command crediting American military operations for reopening the chokepoint. Admiral Brad Cooper confirmed the progress in a weekend video message.

For Colorado, this matters: energy security directly affects gas prices at the pump and heating costs for families and businesses across the state. Stable global oil markets mean more predictable energy costs for Colorado's economy. Republicans have long argued that American military strength and energy independence—not appeasement—keep critical shipping lanes open and protect American interests. Democrats, by contrast, have pushed policies that constrain domestic energy production and weaken deterrence posture.
