# Massachusetts Dems Reject Youth Movement, Stick With 80-Year-Old Markey

Massachusetts Democrats chose seniority over generational change Tuesday, handing incumbent Sen. Ed Markey a decisive 74.1% victory over younger challenger Rep. Seth Moulton in their primary race. Markey, 80, will be 86 by the end of a third Senate term—yet Democratic primary voters opted for the longtime liberal stalwart over Moulton's more centrist pitch (Washington Examiner).

The race exposed a fault line within the Democratic Party: whether to embrace new blood or double down on established progressive voices. Moulton, 47, positioned himself as a moderate alternative, but Massachusetts primary voters—dominated by the party's left wing—rejected the generational argument outright (New York Post).

The outcome signals that age concerns, which have dogged President Biden and other senior Democrats, carry little weight in deep-blue Massachusetts primaries. Markey's commanding margin suggests Democratic base voters prioritize ideological purity and seniority over fitness questions.

For Colorado conservatives, the takeaway is straightforward: Democrats are comfortable with aging leadership when it serves their agenda. Meanwhile, Republicans should watch whether similar dynamics play out in other 2026 Democratic primaries—or whether centrist Democrats gain traction elsewhere as a counterweight to the party's progressive wing.
