# Minnesota Noncitizen Voting Charges Signal Trump Administration's Election Integrity Push

Attorney General Todd Blanche has indicted 10 lawful permanent residents accused of illegally voting in Minnesota elections since 2022, marking a direct enforcement action on election integrity that conservatives have long demanded (Fox News, Just The News).

The charged foreign nationals hail from Brazil, Ghana, and Liberia and allegedly cast ballots in 2022, 2024, and 2026 primary elections. Blanche told Breitbart News the Trump administration is "fighting hard" against fraud—a sharp contrast to the Biden-Harris years, when conservatives argue the Justice Department largely ignored noncitizen voting concerns.

**The Colorado angle:** While Minnesota-focused, this enforcement signals how the Trump administration plans to handle election integrity nationwide. Colorado Republicans have long raised concerns about voter roll accuracy and noncitizen registration, though the state's Democratic leadership has resisted stricter verification measures.

Democrats and the left typically frame such prosecutions as rare or overblown, arguing noncitizen voting is statistically minimal. Republicans counter that even small numbers undermine election legitimacy and that lax verification invites fraud.

Separately, the DOJ filed misconduct complaints against Minnesota judges who made allegedly partisan remarks to the New York Times—signaling the administration's willingness to challenge judicial overreach (Just The News).
