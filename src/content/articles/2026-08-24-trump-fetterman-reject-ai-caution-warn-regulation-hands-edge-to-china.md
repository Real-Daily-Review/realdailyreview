# Trump, Fetterman Push AI Development Over Regulatory Caution

President Trump and Sen. John Fetterman (D-Pa.) are rejecting what they call "AI doomsdaying," arguing that aggressive American development—not regulatory restraint—is the path to staying ahead of China in artificial intelligence.

Trump told Michael Cohen in a recent interview that the U.S. leads the AI race "by a lot" and framed current efforts as "a renaissance" (Washington Examiner). Fetterman echoed the sentiment, warning that American "overreaction" to AI risks could hand competitive advantage to Beijing (Fox News).

The alignment reflects a rare bipartisan skepticism toward the kind of heavy-handed regulation progressives have championed. Democrats and tech critics have pushed for strict AI guardrails, arguing the technology poses existential risks without proper oversight. Republicans counter that such caution stifles innovation and cedes ground to authoritarian competitors unconstrained by Western scruples.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts growing AI and tech clusters in Denver and Boulder; regulatory overreach could slow job creation and investment in those sectors. Colorado's energy sector also stands to benefit from AI-driven efficiency gains in power generation and grid management—gains that require freedom to experiment, not bureaucratic friction.

Watch whether Colorado's Democratic delegation—particularly Sens. Michael Bennet and John Hickenlooper—align with Fetterman's pragmatism or double down on regulation.
