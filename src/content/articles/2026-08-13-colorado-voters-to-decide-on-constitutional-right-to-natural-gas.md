# Colorado Voters to Decide on Constitutional 'Right to Natural Gas'

Colorado's energy independence just got a ballot box moment. Initiative 177, backed by Advance Colorado, qualified for November's election and would enshrine a "right to natural gas" directly into the state Constitution, according to the Colorado Secretary of State's office (Denver Post).

The measure represents a direct conservative pushback against years of progressive efforts to phase out natural gas in Colorado homes and businesses. Republicans and energy advocates argue the amendment protects Coloradans' freedom to choose their energy source and shields the state's robust natural gas industry—a major employer and tax revenue generator—from regulatory overreach by Democratic lawmakers and local governments pursuing aggressive climate mandates.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that such a constitutional guarantee would lock in fossil fuel dependence and hamper Colorado's transition to cleaner energy sources. They argue it's an unnecessary protection for an industry already well-established in state law.

The ballot measure arrives as Colorado faces mounting pressure from progressive majorities in Denver to restrict natural gas hookups in new construction and phase out existing infrastructure. For Colorado's energy workers, rural communities dependent on gas revenue, and families who prefer natural gas heating and cooking, this November vote could determine whether their choices remain protected or become collateral damage in the state's climate agenda.

Watch whether environmental groups mount a well-funded campaign to defeat Initiative 177 before November.
