# Colorado's Reading Crisis: Pandemic Damage Lingers While Younger Kids Recover

Colorado's education system faces a stubborn problem: while younger students have bounced back from pandemic learning losses, older kids remain stuck below pre-pandemic reading and math levels, according to new data cited by 9News Denver.

Third-graders and younger have shown improvement since 2022, but 13-year-olds demonstrate no significant gains—a troubling sign that Colorado's schools failed to adequately address learning deficits for older students during the recovery period. The Colorado Sun's editorial board argues the next governor must prioritize literacy intervention, warning that thousands of students will suffer without action.

**The conservative concern:** While Democrats typically respond to education shortfalls by demanding more taxpayer funding, Republicans should demand accountability first. Colorado already spends substantially on K-12 education; the issue isn't always money—it's how schools deploy resources and whether classroom instruction prioritizes phonics-based reading instruction over progressive pedagogical fads. Before Coloradans open their wallets wider, policymakers must answer why recovery efforts succeeded for younger students but failed for older ones.

The state should examine which schools and districts closed longest during COVID lockdowns and which teaching methods actually work, rather than reflexively increasing budgets.

Watch whether the next governor ties education funding increases to measurable literacy benchmarks and classroom-level accountability.
