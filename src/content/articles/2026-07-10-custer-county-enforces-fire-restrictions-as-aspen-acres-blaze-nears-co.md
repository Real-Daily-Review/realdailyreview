# Custer County Tightens Fire Rules as Aspen Acres Blaze Nears Containment

Custer County is enforcing strict fire restrictions even as firefighters gain ground on the massive Aspen Acres fire, which has burned nearly 97,000 acres in Southern Colorado with nearly 2,000 personnel engaged in suppression efforts (CPR News).

The county's move reflects the reality of Colorado's fire season: even as crews make progress—doubling containment on the Aspen Acres blaze—officials anticipate firefighting operations will continue for weeks (Colorado Sun). The restrictions aim to prevent new ignitions while resources remain stretched across multiple active fires statewide.

For Colorado property owners and rural communities, the message is clear: seasonal fire restrictions are here to stay. Conservatives and landowners have long argued that aggressive forest management—thinning overgrown timber, clearing deadfall, and reducing fuel loads—offers better long-term protection than reactive restrictions that limit land use. The state's failure to maintain forests proactively has left Colorado vulnerable to catastrophic burns that dwarf any single fire season.

Residents in evacuation zones are beginning to return home as containment improves, a welcome sign for families displaced by the blaze.

Watch for whether state and county officials commit to year-round forest management funding or continue relying primarily on seasonal use restrictions to manage fire risk.
