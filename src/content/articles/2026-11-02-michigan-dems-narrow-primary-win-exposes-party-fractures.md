# Michigan Democrat's Narrow Primary Win Signals Deep Party Fractures

Michigan Democrats' vaunted unity cracked wide open this week, and the fault lines reveal a party increasingly at war with itself over identity politics and radical positioning.

Abdul El-Sayed, the Muslim Democrat running for U.S. Senate, was supposed to cruise to a primary victory by double digits. Instead, he barely scraped by with roughly a 1-point win—a stunning underperformance that *Washington Examiner* analysis attributes to a coalition of moderate Democrats and Jewish voters defecting from his campaign amid mounting controversies (Washington Examiner).

Rather than address the underlying fractures, El-Sayed's team doubled down, bringing in left-wing celebrity D.L. Hughley for a photo op—a move *Breitbart* characterized as a sign his campaign is "swamped with controversies" and hemorrhaging Democratic support (Breitbart).

**The Colorado angle:** While this Michigan primary drama plays out 1,500 miles away, it's a cautionary tale for Colorado Democrats. The state's own progressive wing has pushed similar identity-focused messaging that alienates working-class and moderate voters. Colorado Republicans should note how quickly Democratic unity evaporates when the party's far-left base collides with mainstream voters.
