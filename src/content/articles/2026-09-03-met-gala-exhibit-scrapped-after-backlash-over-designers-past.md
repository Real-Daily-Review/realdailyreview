# Met Gala Exhibit Scrapped After Backlash Over Designer's Past

New York's cultural elite just got a lesson in consequences: the Metropolitan Museum of Art canceled a planned exhibit honoring designer John Galliano after public pressure over his controversial statements, leaving Anna Wintour and Vogue scrambling to defend their judgment.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, columnist Joe Concha didn't mince words, telling Wintour—who has reigned over liberal Manhattan's fashion establishment for 37 years—to "take the L" for backing the exhibit in the first place. The decision to scrap the show marks a rare public defeat for the influential editor, who had championed the retrospective despite Galliano's documented history of offensive remarks.

The *New York Post* reports the Met is now weighing alternatives, including pivoting to an exhibit built around the Costume Institute's permanent collection instead. That pivot amounts to a full retreat from the original vision.

The broader lesson here: even progressive institutions answer to public opinion when the heat turns up. Wintour's 37-year grip on cultural gatekeeping couldn't insulate her from accountability—a reminder that no one, no matter how entrenched, is immune to backlash.
