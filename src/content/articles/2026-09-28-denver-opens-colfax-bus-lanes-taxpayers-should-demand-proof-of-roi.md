# Denver's Bus-Only Lanes Open—But Questions Remain on ROI for Taxpayers

Denver's new bus-only lanes on East Colfax opened Sunday after two years of disruptive construction, with RTD and city officials touting the East Colfax Bus Rapid Transit project as a transit modernization win. Businesses along the corridor, which endured years of gridlock and lost foot traffic, are hopeful the dedicated lanes will finally drive customer recovery (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

Here's the conservative concern: Denver and RTD have spent heavily on transit infrastructure while ridership trends remain mixed across the region. Bus-only lanes reduce road capacity for private vehicles—a trade-off that benefits a subset of commuters while imposing costs on drivers and delivery services. The project's success hinges on whether RTD can actually attract and retain riders to justify the public investment and the congestion costs imposed on everyone else.

Coloradans should demand transparency on ridership projections versus actual performance, operating costs, and whether this model delivers better outcomes than market-driven transportation solutions. RTD's track record of cost overruns and service gaps makes skepticism warranted.

Watch whether RTD releases honest ridership data and whether Denver considers similar lane restrictions on other corridors—a sign of ideological commitment to transit-first planning over practical results.
