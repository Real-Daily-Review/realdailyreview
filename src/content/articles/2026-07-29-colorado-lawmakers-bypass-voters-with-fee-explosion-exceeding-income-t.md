# Colorado Lawmakers Quietly Rake In Billions Via Fee Hikes While Voters Block Tax Increases

Colorado legislators have engineered a backdoor revenue explosion, stacking fees that now exceed three times the state income tax rate—all while voters repeatedly reject direct tax hikes at the ballot box. According to Complete Colorado, state lawmakers have layered on fees over recent years that dwarf current income tax collections, effectively circumventing voter resistance to traditional taxation.

This is government by workaround. Conservatives argue that fees function as taxes by another name, allowing politicians to expand spending without facing voter accountability. When Coloradans vote down tax measures, lawmakers simply reclassify revenue streams as "fees" on licenses, permits, and services—shifting the burden onto businesses, workers, and families without their consent.

The fee explosion matters for Colorado's economy and competitiveness. Higher compliance costs burden small businesses and ranchers already squeezed by regulation. Energy companies, water users, and gun owners face mounting fee structures that effectively function as hidden taxes.

Meanwhile, Colorado Springs' congressional race tightens: Democrat Jessica Killin trails Republican Jeff Crank by just one point in a new poll (Colorado Sun), signaling potential Democratic gains in a traditionally Republican district. And across the state, voters express deep skepticism about AI regulation, with majorities agreeing that tech companies alone cannot be trusted to police the technology (Denver Post).

Taxpayers should watch whether the legislature attempts to formalize these fee structures before 2028, locking in revenue without voter approval.
