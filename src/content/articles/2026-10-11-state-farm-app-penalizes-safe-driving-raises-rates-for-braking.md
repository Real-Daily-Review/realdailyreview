# Insurance Tracking Apps Punish Safe Driving—and Your Privacy

State Farm's usage-based insurance app promises lower premiums for safe drivers, but Colorado motorists are discovering the fine print cuts the other way: defensive driving—like hard braking to avoid a crash—gets flagged as "risky behavior" and jacks up your rates instead.

The pitch sounds consumer-friendly in an inflation-squeezed economy: download an app, let the insurer monitor your driving, pocket savings. Major carriers market usage-based insurance as a fair system that rewards genuinely safe drivers and lets them escape bloated, one-size-fits-all risk pools.

Reality tells a different story, according to the *Washington Examiner*. State Farm's Drive Safe app penalizes sudden braking—the kind of defensive maneuver that actually *prevents* accidents. Drivers who tap the brakes to avoid a collision find themselves dinged by the algorithm, not rewarded.

Conservatives and privacy advocates raise two concerns: First, insurers are collecting granular location and driving data with minimal transparency about how algorithms actually score behavior. Second, the system inverts incentives—punishing the very defensive moves that keep roads safer.

Colorado drivers should demand clarity from their carriers on exactly what triggers rate hikes and insist on opting out without penalty. Watch whether state regulators demand transparency from insurers on their algorithmic scoring before more Coloradans get burned by apps designed to look fair but built to extract more premium dollars.
