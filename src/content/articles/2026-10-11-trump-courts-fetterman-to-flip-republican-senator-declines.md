# Trump Recruits Pennsylvania Democrat, But Fetterman Stays Put

President Trump is openly courting Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) to switch parties, calling the freshman senator a "great guy" and saying he'd "love to see" him join Republican ranks (Fox News, Washington Examiner). Trump argues Fetterman "does better as a Republican than he does as a Democrat" (Just The News).

The pitch reflects Republican confidence that moderate Democrats in swing states face growing pressure from their party's left wing. Fetterman, elected in 2022, has occasionally broken with Democratic orthodoxy—most notably on public safety and law-and-order issues—fueling speculation about his long-term party loyalty.

But Fetterman isn't biting. The Pennsylvania senator has repeatedly insisted he intends to remain a Democrat, even as he maintains independence on certain votes.

**The Colorado angle:** While Pennsylvania's Senate seat isn't directly Colorado's concern, the broader dynamic matters. If moderate Democrats feel squeezed out by progressive activists, similar pressure could affect Colorado's own political realignment—particularly in rural and energy-producing counties where the state party has drifted sharply left on oil, gas, and water policy.

**Watch next:** Whether Fetterman's resistance holds, or whether other vulnerable Democrats in red-leaning states begin calculating the cost of staying in a party increasingly hostile to their constituents' values.
