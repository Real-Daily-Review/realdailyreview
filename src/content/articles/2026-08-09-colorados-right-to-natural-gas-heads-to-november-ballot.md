# Colorado's Natural Gas Fight Heads to November Ballot

Colorado voters will decide this fall whether to enshrine a constitutional "right to natural gas"—a direct pushback against progressive efforts to phase out the fuel that heats homes and powers businesses across the state.

Initiative 177, brought forward by Advance Colorado, qualified for the November ballot after gathering sufficient signatures, according to the Colorado Secretary of State's office (Denver Post). The measure seeks to prevent state or local bans on natural gas sales and use, protecting both consumers and the energy workers whose livelihoods depend on Colorado's gas industry.

The ballot fight reflects a deeper divide: progressives have pursued aggressive electrification mandates and restrictions on natural gas hookups in new construction, arguing climate concerns justify the transition. Republicans and energy advocates counter that such bans drive up costs for working families, eliminate consumer choice, and threaten Colorado's energy independence and economy.

Colorado Sun reports the initiative explicitly aims to block future prohibitions on natural gas. For a state where natural gas remains critical to heating, manufacturing, and agriculture—particularly in rural Colorado—this constitutional protection could prove decisive in limiting regulatory overreach.

Conservatives should watch whether the measure gains traction with suburban and rural voters who've grown weary of one-size-fits-all energy mandates from Denver.
