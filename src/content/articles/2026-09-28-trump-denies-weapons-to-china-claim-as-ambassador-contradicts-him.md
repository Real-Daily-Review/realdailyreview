# Trump Denies Offering Weapons Sales to China; Ambassador Contradicts

President Trump flatly rejected claims he discussed selling U.S. weapons to China during Xi Jinping's recent White House visit, but his own ambassador to Beijing says otherwise—a rare public split that raises questions about Trump's negotiating approach with America's chief strategic competitor.

Ambassador David Perdue told Fox News that Trump asked Xi whether China would be interested in purchasing American weapons, framing it as consistent with U.S. arms sales to allied nations worldwide. Trump, however, told reporters Monday he'd "never heard of that one" and said he'd need to ask Perdue directly (The Daily Wire, Washington Examiner).

**The conservative concern:** Republicans and defense hawks worry that weaponizing commerce with Beijing—even rhetorically—undermines U.S. strategic leverage and muddles messaging about containing Chinese military expansion. Selling advanced arms to an authoritarian rival contradicts decades of bipartisan policy restricting sensitive military technology transfers to Beijing.

For Colorado, this matters: the state hosts major defense contractors and aerospace firms dependent on clear, consistent U.S. security doctrine. Confusion about Trump's China strategy could ripple through defense procurement and employment.

The contradiction between Trump and Perdue demands clarification—especially if the White House is improvising foreign policy on the fly rather than executing a coherent strategy.
