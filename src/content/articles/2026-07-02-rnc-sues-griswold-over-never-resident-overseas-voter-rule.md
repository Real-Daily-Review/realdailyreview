# RNC Challenges Griswold's Overseas Voter Residency Rule

The Republican National Committee is suing Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold over a voting residency policy that conservatives say opens the door to ballot fraud by allowing people who've never lived in Colorado to cast votes here.

According to the RNC's complaint, Griswold's interpretation permits residency for voting purposes to be established through a parent or spouse—even if the voter themselves has never actually lived in the state. Republicans argue this arrangement is unconstitutional and creates a pathway for ineligible voters to participate in Colorado elections.

The lawsuit strikes at a core conservative concern: election integrity. If residency can be claimed by proxy rather than demonstrated through actual domicile, Colorado's voter rolls become vulnerable to manipulation, Republicans contend. The state's election administration has already faced scrutiny over mail-ballot security and voter verification procedures.

This case matters directly to Colorado taxpayers and election officials. A ruling against Griswold could force the state to tighten residency verification standards—potentially affecting thousands of overseas voters, military families, and others claiming Colorado residency remotely. Conversely, if the courts side with the secretary of state, Colorado's voting eligibility rules remain among the nation's most permissive.

Watch for the court's timeline on the RNC's motion for preliminary injunction—it could reshape Colorado voting rules before the next election cycle.
