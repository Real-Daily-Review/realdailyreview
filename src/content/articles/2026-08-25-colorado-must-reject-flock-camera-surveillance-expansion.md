# Surveillance Creep: Colorado Should Reject Flock Camera Expansion

San Diego's embrace of Flock license plate reader cameras—complete with a Darth Vader costume endorsement (9News Denver)—signals a troubling expansion of government surveillance infrastructure that Colorado must resist. While National Review argues Flock cameras give law enforcement "a valuable asset," conservatives should recognize this technology as precisely the kind of mass surveillance that erodes privacy without meaningful due process safeguards.

Flock cameras automatically scan and log every vehicle's license plate and location, creating a permanent digital dragnet. The technology raises serious Fourth Amendment concerns: drivers have no notice they're being tracked, no opportunity to challenge the data collection, and no guarantee their information won't be misused by government agencies or shared with federal authorities.

Colorado's law enforcement agencies have already begun deploying these systems. Republicans and privacy advocates argue that warrantless, mass surveillance contradicts conservative principles of limited government and individual liberty. The Left claims the cameras improve public safety and help solve crimes—but at what cost to freedom?

**Watch for:** Colorado legislators' response if local police departments push for statewide Flock expansion; any bills that would require warrants or impose strict data-retention limits should be supported by taxpayers concerned about government overreach.
