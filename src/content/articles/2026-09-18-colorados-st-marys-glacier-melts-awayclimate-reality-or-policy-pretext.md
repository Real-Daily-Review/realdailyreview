# St. Mary's Glacier Melts Away in Record Heat—What It Means for Colorado

Colorado's iconic St. Mary's Glacier near Idaho Springs has vanished, melted entirely during this summer's record temperatures, leaving hikers and tourists facing an empty basin where a perennial snowfield once stood (Colorado Sun, KDVR).

The glacier's disappearance marks a stark visual reminder of Colorado's warming climate. According to the Colorado Sun, while future snowfalls could theoretically replenish the ice, snow and melt are now outpacing accumulation—a pattern that threatens other alpine features across the state.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, this raises hard questions about climate policy priorities. While progressives will cite this as evidence demanding aggressive carbon restrictions and energy regulations, conservatives should ask: Do proposed climate mandates—like Colorado's aggressive renewable energy targets and vehicle emission rules—actually prevent natural climate cycles? Or do they simply transfer wealth to green-energy companies while hiking energy costs for working families?

The glacier's loss also underscores Colorado's water vulnerability. Snowpack feeds reservoirs and rivers that supply farms, cities, and hydropower. That's a legitimate infrastructure concern separate from climate ideology.

Visitors to Clear Creek County should expect disappointment this season—and taxpayers should watch whether state Democrats use this natural event to justify further energy regulations that drive up costs without measurable climate benefit.
