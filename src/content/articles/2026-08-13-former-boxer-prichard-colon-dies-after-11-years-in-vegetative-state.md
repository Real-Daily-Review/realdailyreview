# Former Boxer Prichard Colon Dies After 11 Years in Vegetative State

Prichard Colon, a professional boxer who suffered catastrophic brain trauma during a 2015 fight, has died at 33 after spending more than a decade in a persistent vegetative state requiring round-the-clock care.

Colon was pronounced dead Thursday afternoon, according to his father's Facebook post (9News Denver). The fighter's career ended instantly during the brutal 2015 match that left him unable to recover consciousness or independent function (The Daily Wire).

The tragedy underscores the severe, often irreversible risks inherent in professional combat sports—a reality that should inform both athlete decision-making and regulatory oversight of boxing and mixed martial arts. While individual liberty includes the right to accept such risks, the long-term medical and caregiving burden Colon's family bore raises legitimate questions about whether current safety protocols and fighter protections are adequate.

Colon's death marks a somber reminder that some injuries transcend recovery. His family's decade-long vigil reflects both their devotion and the harsh limits of modern medicine when catastrophic head trauma occurs.

Coloradans should monitor whether state athletic commissions strengthen concussion protocols and fighter safety standards in the wake of high-profile cases like Colon's.
