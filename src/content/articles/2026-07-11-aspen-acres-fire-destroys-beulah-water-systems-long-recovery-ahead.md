# Wildfire Destroys Critical Water Infrastructure in Beulah; Rebuilding Will Test State Resources

The Aspen Acres fire has gutted water utilities serving Beulah's roughly 800 residents—a stark reminder that Colorado's wildfire crisis now threatens the infrastructure that keeps communities alive. Water managers are scrambling to restore service after the blaze torched critical systems, according to the Colorado Sun, with officials warning recovery will be lengthy.

While most active Colorado fires have spared water infrastructure, this one didn't. The small community lost homes and now faces the compounded burden of rebuilding both housing and the utilities that serve it—a dual crisis that will strain local budgets and likely demand state or federal aid.

Conservatives should note the broader lesson: Colorado's water security depends on preventing catastrophic wildfires through aggressive forest management, not restricting development or imposing new environmental regulations that slow fuel reduction. The state's regulatory approach to land use and water policy will determine whether communities can rebuild quickly or face years of bureaucratic delays.

Taxpayers should watch whether state officials prioritize rapid infrastructure restoration or use this crisis to expand water regulations and spending.
