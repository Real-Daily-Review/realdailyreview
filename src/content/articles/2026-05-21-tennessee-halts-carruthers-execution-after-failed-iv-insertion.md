# Tennessee Halts Execution After Failed IV Insertion

Tennessee called off the execution of Tony Carruthers on Thursday after executioners failed to find a vein to insert the required IV line for lethal injection, according to CBS News. Gov. Bill Lee (R-TN) delayed the execution by one year following the unsuccessful procedure, the Washington Examiner reported.

Carruthers had been convicted of a triple murder in Memphis in 1994. Under Tennessee's lethal injection protocol, a backup IV line is mandatory, and the execution cannot proceed without it.

The failed attempt marks another instance of complications during capital punishment in the state. Execution difficulties—including problems establishing IV access—have drawn scrutiny from death penalty opponents and raised questions about the reliability of lethal injection procedures nationwide.

Tennessee has not yet announced a new execution date for Carruthers. The state's next steps regarding the inmate and its lethal injection protocols remain unclear.
