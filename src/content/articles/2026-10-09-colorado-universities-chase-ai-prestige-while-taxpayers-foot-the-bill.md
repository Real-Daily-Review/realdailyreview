# Colorado Universities Chase AI Prestige While Taxpayers Foot the Bill

Colorado's higher-education establishment is embracing artificial intelligence with state-funded enthusiasm. Six Colorado colleges and universities have earned a new "AI Engaged Campus Designation," according to CPR News, signaling institutional commitment to integrating AI into teaching and research.

On its face, technological adoption sounds forward-thinking. But conservatives should ask hard questions: What's the actual return on investment for Colorado taxpayers? Are these institutions using AI to cut costs and improve efficiency, or simply chasing prestige while expanding administrative overhead?

The designation itself—a credential with no clear market value—exemplifies how higher education often prioritizes buzzwords over fiscal discipline. Colorado's universities already consume substantial state appropriations. Adding AI initiatives without demonstrating measurable outcomes in student outcomes, graduation rates, or workforce readiness is a classic case of mission creep dressed up as innovation.

Republicans have long warned that Colorado's public universities operate with insufficient accountability to taxpayers. Absent transparent metrics showing how AI investments translate to better education at lower cost, this initiative risks becoming another example of higher-ed spending that benefits administrators more than students or the state's bottom line.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers demand accountability reports showing concrete cost savings or educational improvements from these AI programs—or whether they simply rubber-stamp the designation as another feel-good credential.
