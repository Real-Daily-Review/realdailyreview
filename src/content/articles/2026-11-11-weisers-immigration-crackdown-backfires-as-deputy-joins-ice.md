# Colorado Deputy's ICE Move Highlights AG Weiser's Immigration Enforcement Clash

A former Mesa County Sheriff's deputy disciplined for working with federal immigration authorities has landed a job at Immigration and Customs Enforcement—a move that underscores the ideological divide over border security in Colorado.

Alexander Zwinck resigned last year after facing disciplinary action and a lawsuit from Attorney General Phil Weiser (Colorado Sun). Weiser's office had targeted Zwinck's collaboration with ICE, reflecting the Democratic AG's resistance to local law enforcement cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.

**Republicans and conservatives argue** that Weiser's hostility toward ICE cooperation hamstrings legitimate border security efforts and ties the hands of local sheriffs trying to protect their communities. They contend that sanctuary-state policies—which Weiser's actions reinforce—create dangerous gaps in law enforcement coordination.

**Democrats counter** that such cooperation can lead to civil rights violations and erodes trust between immigrant communities and police, ultimately harming public safety.

Zwinck's departure to ICE reflects a broader pattern: Colorado law enforcement officers who prioritize immigration enforcement face political and legal pressure from progressive state leadership. Meanwhile, law-and-order conservatives question whether Weiser's office should be suing deputies for working with federal authorities at all.

Watch whether other Colorado sheriffs face similar pressure—or whether the state's immigration enforcement posture becomes a 2026 campaign issue.
