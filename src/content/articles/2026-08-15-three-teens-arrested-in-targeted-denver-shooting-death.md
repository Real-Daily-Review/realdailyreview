# Three Teens Arrested in Denver Shooting Death

Denver's violent crime problem claimed another young life this week, with police arresting three teenagers in connection with the fatal shooting of 19-year-old Maikel Garresquero Mavo on August 6.

According to Denver Police, investigators believe Mavo was lured to the scene before being shot around 11 p.m. The three juvenile suspects now face charges in what appears to be a targeted attack rather than random violence—a distinction that underscores the gang and organized crime elements plaguing Denver neighborhoods.

This incident reflects a broader pattern: Colorado's capital has struggled with rising violent crime despite years of progressive criminal justice policies, including bail reform and reduced prosecution standards. Republicans and law-and-order conservatives argue that soft-on-crime approaches—championed by Denver's progressive district attorney and city leadership—have emboldened criminals and failed to protect residents.

Democrats counter that root causes like poverty and lack of opportunity drive youth violence, requiring social spending rather than tougher enforcement. Yet Denver has poured resources into both directions with limited success.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, the message is clear: Denver's experiment in progressive policing and criminal justice hasn't made neighborhoods safer. Watch whether the incoming police leadership and district attorney will reverse course toward accountability and enforcement.
