# Record Gas Prices Hammer Colorado Drivers as Energy Crisis Deepens

Colorado drivers are getting squeezed at the pump as national gas prices hit a record $4.15 per gallon over Labor Day weekend, with diesel shortages expected to persist through winter, according to top oil traders cited by the Daily Caller.

The spike poses a direct threat to Colorado's economy. Higher fuel costs ripple through transportation, agriculture, and tourism—three pillars of Colorado's economy. Ranchers and trucking operations face mounting expenses, while consumers heading into fall and winter face sustained pain at the pump.

Republicans argue the crisis stems from failed energy policy and underinvestment in domestic production. The Daily Caller reports oil traders warn U.S. stockpiles are "pretty much at the bottom," signaling supply constraints that could worsen as heating season arrives.

Democrats typically blame market volatility and geopolitical factors beyond Washington's control, though they face political headwinds as midterm elections approach (per Fox News Politics).

For Colorado specifically, the energy crunch underscores why conservatives oppose restrictions on oil and gas development. State policies that hamper domestic energy production—whether through permitting delays or regulatory barriers—directly harm Colorado workers and families already battered by inflation.
