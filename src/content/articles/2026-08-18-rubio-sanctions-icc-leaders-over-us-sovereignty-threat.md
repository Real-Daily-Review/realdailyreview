# Trump Administration Sanctions ICC Over Sovereignty Concerns

Secretary of State Marco Rubio is taking direct aim at what conservatives see as an overreaching international court threatening American sovereignty. According to the Washington Examiner, Rubio announced sanctions Tuesday against ICC President Tomoko Akane and a senior trial lawyer, accusing the tribunal of pursuing "pretend offenses" and operating as a "sham court."

The core conservative argument is straightforward: the United States never ratified the 2002 Rome Statute establishing the ICC, so the court has no jurisdiction over American citizens or interests (Just The News). Rubio's position reflects the Trump administration's view that the ICC is stepping outside its legal authority and attempting to undermine U.S. sovereignty.

This matters for Colorado because it signals the administration's willingness to confront international bodies that claim power over Americans—a principle conservatives have long championed. The move aligns with Republican skepticism of multilateral institutions that operate without direct American accountability.

Meanwhile, the DOJ is separately asking an appeals court to reinstate criminal charges against a deportation challenger, showing the administration's aggressive posture on immigration enforcement (Just The News).

Watch whether Congress moves to formally defund or withdraw U.S. participation in ICC-related activities—a step conservatives have long demanded.
