# China's Missile Test Exposes NATO's Spending Gaps—and Trump's 2% Demand

China's ballistic missile test in the Pacific is a stark reminder that America's NATO allies aren't pulling their weight on defense—and President Trump isn't letting them off easy.

The submarine-launched missile test, which drew rebukes from Australia and Japan ahead of NATO's Ankara summit, underscores the real military threats facing the alliance. Yet many of NATO's heaviest hitters are still struggling to meet Trump's 2030 defense spending target, according to the New York Post. Trump has warned allies against using "funny math" to artificially inflate their defense commitments—a direct shot at countries that cook the books rather than genuinely increase military budgets.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** American taxpayers have subsidized European defense for 75 years while those nations underfund their own militaries. If China is testing advanced weapons systems, NATO members need to stop gaming the numbers and actually spend real money on real capabilities. Trump's demand for accountability isn't isolationism; it's fiscal sanity.

For Colorado, this matters: defense contractors and military installations across the state depend on a credible NATO alliance and sustained U.S. military readiness. Weak allied commitments force America to shoulder more of the burden—and the bill.

**Watch whether Trump follows through with concrete consequences if NATO members continue the accounting charades.**
