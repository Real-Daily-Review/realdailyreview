# Cost of Living Crisis Hits Home for Colorado Families

While Washington celebrates economic growth, Coloradans are feeling the squeeze at the grocery store and gas pump—and it's not getting better. According to the *Washington Examiner*, the disconnect between headline GDP numbers and household reality has become impossible to ignore across the country, and Colorado families know it firsthand.

The core problem: wages aren't keeping pace with inflation. Whether you're buying groceries in Denver or filling up in rural Colorado, the "heavy weight" of higher prices is real. Conservatives argue that government spending and regulatory overreach—not market forces—have driven this cost spiral. Republicans point to excessive federal stimulus, supply-chain disruptions from lockdown policies, and energy regulations that have constrained domestic production as culprits.

Democrats counter that inflation is a global phenomenon tied to pandemic recovery and geopolitical shocks. But for Colorado taxpayers already burdened by state income taxes and rising property costs, the debate feels academic. Working families need relief now, not explanations later.

The real question: Will Colorado policymakers acknowledge that government-imposed constraints on energy development, housing supply, and business growth are making life unaffordable for ordinary Coloradans?
