## SCOTUS Puts the "Spirit of Aloha" Back in Its Lane

Justice Samuel Alito and the Supreme Court struck down Hawaii's sweeping firearm restrictions Thursday, rejecting the state's argument that its cultural "spirit of aloha" could override the Second Amendment. The ruling is a clean, unambiguous win for gun owners nationwide. For Colorado conservatives — who have watched Denver and the legislature chip away at firearms rights — this is a reminder that the Constitution still has teeth, and activist state governments can't invent cultural carve-outs to nullify it.

## Colorado Case Could Rein In Federal Overreach

A Colorado case is working its way toward a potential Supreme Court showdown over the Commerce Clause — the constitutional provision the federal government has stretched for decades to justify nearly unlimited regulatory power. Conservatives and constitutional scholars argue it's long past time for SCOTUS to reassert meaningful limits on Washington's appetite for control. Colorado ranchers, energy producers, and small businesses that have lived under the boot of federal overreach should be paying close attention to how this one develops.

## Aurora Officer Cleared in 2025 Shooting

The 17th Judicial District Attorney's Office has declined to charge an Aurora police officer in the May 2025 shooting of Rashaud Johnson, ruling the use of force was justified. This is how the system is supposed to work — a thorough review followed by a clear-eyed legal conclusion. Republicans argue that anti-police rhetoric from the left creates a chilling effect on law enforcement and emboldens criminals. Aurora has been ground zero for that debate, and this decision deserves to be reported plainly, without the activist spin that typically surrounds these cases.

## Soil Moisture Research Could Sharpen Colorado Wildfire Predictions

New research suggests soil moisture levels beneath Western forests may be a strong predictor of where wildfires ignite and spread — a finding with direct implications for Colorado's fire-prone Front Range and mountain communities. Conservatives note that responsible land management, not climate alarmism and regulatory paralysis, is what actually protects communities from catastrophic fire. If this science can sharpen early warning systems and inform smarter forest management, that's a practical win worth watching.
