# AI Infrastructure Needs Clear Rules, Not State Blockades

Colorado and other states risk ceding America's AI advantage to China if they impose moratoriums on data centers rather than establish sensible permitting standards.

New York's recent moves to restrict AI facility development (Washington Examiner) highlight a growing problem: states are responding to vague environmental and power-grid concerns with blanket bans instead of clear rules. Republicans and conservatives argue this approach is backward. While Washington has rightly protected semiconductor manufacturing from Chinese competition, states shouldn't weaponize permitting to kill domestic AI infrastructure.

For Colorado—home to tech corridors in Denver and Boulder, plus abundant hydroelectric and natural gas resources—this matters enormously. Data centers represent high-wage jobs and tax revenue. Yet progressive regulators could easily use water concerns or grid-capacity arguments to block projects that would strengthen American technological independence.

The conservative case is straightforward: establish transparent, predictable standards for power consumption, cooling systems, and environmental impact. Let companies and communities negotiate. Don't ban the future because you haven't thought through the rules.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that AI's power demands threaten grid stability and water supplies—legitimate concerns in drought-prone states. But moratoriums solve nothing; clear frameworks do.

**Watch whether Colorado's Public Utilities Commission and state legislature move to clarify AI facility standards before the next major data-center proposal lands.**
