# Trump Official Calls for Fox News Boycott Over Pool Participation

The Trump administration is turning on its own allies. White House trade adviser Peter Navarro is urging fellow officials to shun Fox News after the network joined a TV pool boycott supporting three outlets—CNN, MSNBC, and Politico—that were banned from White House access this week (Washington Examiner).

The administration is defending those bans in court filings, arguing that "access to the White House is a privilege — not a right" (9News Denver). Navarro's op-ed escalates internal tensions over press access policy, signaling frustration that Fox News prioritized solidarity with competitors over loyalty to the administration.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the White House has broad authority to manage its own premises and control which outlets receive credentials. Press access, they contend, isn't a constitutional entitlement—it's a privilege the executive branch can condition or revoke.

**The counterargument:** Media freedom advocates and Democrats warn that banning outlets sets a dangerous precedent for government censorship and undermines First Amendment principles, regardless of which party controls the White House.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado voters and taxpayers should watch how federal courts rule on executive press access—the precedent will affect transparency and accountability reporting on federal land policy, water rights, and energy regulation that directly impact the state.

Watch for the court's decision on whether the administration's ban survives legal challenge.
