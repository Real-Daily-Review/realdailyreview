# Trump's AG Pick Moves Forward After Killing $1.8B IRS Slush Fund

Todd Blanche, Trump's nominee for Attorney General, cleared a major confirmation hurdle by rescinding a controversial $1.8 billion settlement fund tied to an IRS audit immunity deal that benefited the Trump family. According to Fox News and the New York Post, Blanche met demands to narrow the scope of the arrangement, removing a key obstacle to his Senate confirmation.

The move signals the incoming administration's intent to wind down what conservatives view as weaponized federal agencies. Republicans have long argued the IRS became a tool of Democratic administrations to target political opponents—a concern that gained traction after the agency's targeting of Tea Party groups during the Obama era.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, Blanche's confirmation matters: a more restrained IRS could mean fewer aggressive audits and less regulatory harassment of conservative-leaning enterprises. The settlement's rescission also suggests the Trump administration will scrutinize how federal agencies deploy settlement funds, potentially curbing what critics call slush-fund spending that bypasses Congress.

Democrats will likely argue the move protects Trump personally, but Republicans counter that reining in IRS overreach protects all Americans from government abuse.
