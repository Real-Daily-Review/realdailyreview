# Trump Open to Putin at Miami G20—But Diplomatic Gamble Raises Security Questions

President Trump said Thursday he'd welcome Russian President Vladimir Putin to the G20 summit in Miami next month, following their recent trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping. "If he wanted to come, he certainly can," Trump told reporters, adding "I'd like to have him" (Washington Examiner, New York Post).

The move reflects Trump's broader engagement strategy with Russia and China, but it carries diplomatic and security risks. Hosting Putin at a major international economic summit—especially in Florida, a state with significant national security infrastructure—signals a willingness to normalize relations that many Republicans view cautiously. The optics matter: a Putin appearance could overshadow substantive G20 discussions on trade, economic policy, and energy markets where American interests diverge sharply from Moscow's.

For Colorado, the calculus is straightforward. Energy markets, agricultural exports, and supply-chain stability all hinge on U.S.-Russia relations. If Trump's engagement strategy yields better terms on energy competition or reduces geopolitical friction affecting commodity prices, Colorado energy workers and farmers could benefit. Conversely, if the summit becomes a photo-op divorced from hard negotiating outcomes, taxpayers foot the security bill for theater.

**Watch whether Congress or the State Department formally objects—and whether Putin actually accepts.**
