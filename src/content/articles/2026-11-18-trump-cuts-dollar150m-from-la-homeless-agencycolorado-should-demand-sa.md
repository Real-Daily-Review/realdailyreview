# Trump Administration Defunds LA Homeless Agency—A Warning for Colorado's Own Spending Failures

The Trump administration has permanently pulled $150 million in federal funding from Los Angeles's notoriously dysfunctional homeless agency, forcing LA County to take direct control of the money. This is what accountability looks like—and Colorado should take note.

The Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority burned through billions in taxpayer dollars while homelessness actually worsened, a cautionary tale for any state or city throwing money at progressive social programs without measurable results. According to the New York Post, the agency's collapse underscores a fundamental truth: spending alone doesn't solve homelessness. Real solutions require clearing encampments and moving people into mental health and sobriety programs—not endless subsidies to bloated bureaucracies.

Colorado's own homeless initiatives deserve the same scrutiny. Denver and other Front Range cities have poured hundreds of millions into housing-first programs and supportive services with mixed results at best. If federal auditors are willing to yank funding from failed agencies, state legislators and local officials should demand the same rigor from Colorado's homeless-services contractors and nonprofits.

The message is clear: waste federal dollars, lose federal dollars. Colorado taxpayers should demand their elected officials apply the same standard to every state and local program claiming to help the homeless.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's Democratic-controlled legislature will adopt similar accountability measures—or double down on failed spending models.
