# Michigan Democrat's DSA-Aligned Senate Win Signals Radical Shift in Party

A Michigan Democrat's primary victory this week—powered by Democratic Socialists of America backing—reveals how far left the party has drifted, and Colorado voters should take note as similar forces reshape Democratic politics nationwide.

Dr. Abdul El-Sayed, who won Michigan's Democratic Senate primary, has not distanced himself from DSA's radical platform and openly supports abolishing multiple institutions, according to *National Review*. The *Washington Examiner* calls him "an American tragedy," underscoring conservative alarm at his nomination. Even his Democratic primary opponent, State Sen. Mallory McMorrow, reportedly advised him not to "turn everybody into an enemy"—a tacit admission that his politics alienate mainstream voters (Breitbart).

Republicans argue El-Sayed's nomination exemplifies how progressives have captured Democratic primary machinery, pushing moderate candidates aside. His institutional abolitionism and DSA alignment represent exactly the kind of radical governance conservatives warn threatens economic freedom and limited government.

For Colorado, this matters: Democratic primary voters here have similarly nominated candidates backed by activist wings of the party. If Michigan's trend holds, expect Colorado Democrats to face similar pressure from their left flank in 2026 and beyond.

Watch whether El-Sayed's general-election positioning softens his radical rhetoric—or whether he doubles down and loses winnable swing voters.
