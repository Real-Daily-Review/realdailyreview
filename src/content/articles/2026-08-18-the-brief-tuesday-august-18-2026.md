## Hickenlooper Stonewalled on TB Outbreak at Aurora Detention Center

Sen. John Hickenlooper visited the GEO Group's Aurora immigrant detention facility on the same day state health officials had set as a deadline for the company to hand over information about an ongoing tuberculosis investigation — and he left without answers. Coloradans deserve to know what's happening inside a facility operating in their backyard. Whether you support or oppose the detention center, a communicable disease outbreak with no transparency from the operator is a public health problem that demands accountability, not stonewalling.

## Businesses Are Leaving Colorado — and Polis Owns It

Colorado's regulatory burden and cost of living are driving a business exodus — not failures, not mergers, but companies actively relocating to other states. Republicans have warned for years that Denver's progressive regulatory agenda and rising costs would make Colorado inhospitable to employers. They were right. Every business that packs up takes jobs, tax revenue, and economic opportunity with it. Colorado ranchers, small business owners, and working families pay the price while the governor's office keeps patting itself on the back.

## Fort Morgan Cargill Workers Return After 89-Day Lockout

After 89 days without work, roughly 1,700 meatpackers at Cargill's Fort Morgan plant voted to accept a new contract and end a labor lockout. Union officials acknowledged gains but conceded it was simply "time to go back to work." For rural Colorado and the agricultural economy that depends on processing capacity, getting workers back on the floor is the right outcome. Prolonged labor disruptions hit ranchers and supply chains hard — the people who grow and raise Colorado's food can't afford indefinite shutdowns.
