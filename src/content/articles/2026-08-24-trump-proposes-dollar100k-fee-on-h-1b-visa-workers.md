# Trump Administration Targets H-1B Visa Program With Six-Figure Fee

The Trump administration is moving to impose a roughly $100,000 fee on employers hiring skilled foreign workers through the H-1B visa program—a direct challenge to tech industry lobbying and a signal that immigration restriction remains central to this administration's economic agenda.

According to the draft rule published Monday, the fee would apply to all H-1B petitions for workers hired within the U.S. (Washington Examiner). The H-1B program, ostensibly designed to fill positions when American workers are unavailable, has long drawn fire from conservatives who argue it suppresses wages for American tech workers and allows companies to dodge hiring domestic talent.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** If American workers can fill these jobs, why import foreign labor at depressed wages? The fee creates a financial incentive for employers to hire American first—or face six-figure costs per visa petition.

Tech industry groups will almost certainly challenge the rule in court, as they did with a similar Trump-era fee proposal last year that courts rejected. The administration is betting this iteration survives legal scrutiny.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado's tech sector, concentrated in Denver and Boulder, relies heavily on H-1B workers. This fee could raise hiring costs for local software firms and startups, potentially slowing growth in one of the state's strongest economic engines.

Watch whether Colorado's business community—typically Republican-aligned on taxes and regulation—breaks ranks to oppose the fee or backs the administration's immigration-restriction stance.
