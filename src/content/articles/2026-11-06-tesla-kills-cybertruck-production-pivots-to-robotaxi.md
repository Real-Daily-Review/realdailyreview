# Tesla Winding Down Cybertruck Production in Texas by Mid-2027

Elon Musk's pivot away from the Cybertruck signals a hard reality check for electric vehicle manufacturing: consumer demand doesn't always match Silicon Valley hype.

Tesla will halt Cybertruck production at its Texas Gigafactory around mid-2027 to shift capacity toward Cybercab autonomous vehicle development, Musk announced. The move represents a strategic retreat from the polarizing stainless-steel pickup that generated massive buzz but struggled to find sustained market traction.

For Colorado, this matters. Tesla operates significant operations in the state, and EV manufacturing remains a linchpin of the state's economic diversification strategy. The Cybertruck's underperformance raises questions about whether the EV market can deliver the jobs and tax revenue progressives promised when they championed electric vehicle subsidies and mandates.

Conservatives have long warned that government-backed EV incentives distort markets and prop up vehicles consumers don't actually want at scale. Musk's decision—driven by profit motive, not politics—proves the market ultimately decides winners and losers better than bureaucrats do.

The real test: whether Colorado's EV-heavy economic bets hold up when companies follow profit, not green ideology.
