# Colorado Parks and Wildlife Halts Wolf Killings, Leaving Ranchers Exposed

Colorado Parks and Wildlife is suspending lethal removal of wolves that attack livestock, citing low adult survival rates and falling pup counts—a decision that leaves ranchers vulnerable to predation while the state prioritizes wolf population recovery.

CPW announced the temporary halt Wednesday after evaluating survival data and translocation efforts (9News Denver). The agency says it will "continue to work with livestock producers to mitigate wolf attacks," but Colorado Sun reports the state is now focused on addressing lower-than-normal adult wolf survival and pup survival rates expected to decline further.

**The ranching problem:** Conservatives and agricultural groups argue this policy shift abandons rural Coloradans who bear the real cost of wolf reintroduction. While CPW promises "mitigation," ranchers face mounting losses with no lethal recourse—the traditional tool for protecting herds. The state is essentially choosing wolf recovery over livestock protection.

**What's next:** Watch whether CPW's promised "mitigation" measures—fencing, hazing, compensation—actually materialize and whether ranchers will demand legislative action to restore their ability to defend their herds when wolves attack.
