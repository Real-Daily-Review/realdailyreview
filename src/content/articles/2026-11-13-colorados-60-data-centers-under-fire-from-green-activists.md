# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Wage War on Progress

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are the invisible backbone of modern life, yet they face mounting regulatory pressure from environmental activists and progressive policymakers who view them as energy-hungry villains rather than economic engines.

Complete Colorado reports that these facilities are essential infrastructure—the servers that power everything from cloud computing to AI to streaming services that millions depend on daily. Yet Colorado Democrats and green groups have increasingly targeted data centers over electricity consumption and water use, treating them as obstacles to climate ideology rather than recognizing their role in economic growth and job creation.

Republicans and industry advocates argue that Colorado's data center sector represents a genuine competitive advantage: high-speed internet infrastructure, skilled workforce, and reasonable operating costs that attract major investment. Shutting down or heavily regulating these facilities would cede economic opportunity to other states while raising costs for consumers nationwide.

The left's counterargument centers on environmental impact—claiming data centers strain Colorado's water resources and grid capacity. But conservatives note that the state's energy mix is already diversifying, and data centers drive demand for reliable power that incentivizes investment in all energy sources, including natural gas and renewables.

**Watch whether Colorado Democrats push new data center regulations that could drive this sector—and its six-figure jobs—to Texas or other business-friendly states.**
