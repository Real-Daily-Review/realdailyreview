# Colorado Universities Pivot to Social Engineering While Core Academics Fade

Colorado's universities are doubling down on feel-good programming instead of rigorous education. The University of Denver is pairing student volunteers with isolated seniors through its "Perfect Pair" initiative, while another Colorado institution now offers coursework on becoming a social media influencer—priorities that raise hard questions about what higher education should actually teach.

The senior-isolation angle has merit: Colorado's aging population faces real loneliness, and intergenerational connection matters. But conservatives should ask whether universities funded by Colorado taxpayers should be running social services that nonprofits and community organizations already handle. Meanwhile, teaching students "influencer" skills—in an era when teenagers already spend hours chasing social media validation—suggests universities have abandoned their traditional mission: critical thinking, technical competency, and intellectual rigor.

Republicans argue that higher education has strayed from preparing students for productive careers and citizenship into lifestyle coaching and social activism. The Left counters that universities have a civic duty to address community problems holistically.

Colorado families paying tuition and state education dollars deserve clarity: Are these programs core to a degree, or expensive distractions from math, science, writing, and history? Watch whether Colorado's Board of Education or legislature demands accountability on how universities spend public funds and what graduates actually know.
