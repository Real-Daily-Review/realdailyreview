# Supreme Court Closes Door on Carter Page's FBI Surveillance Case

The Supreme Court has refused to revive Carter Page's lawsuit against former FBI Director James Comey and other federal officials over the bureau's warrantless surveillance during the Trump-Russia probe—a decision that leaves Page without legal recourse despite documented abuses.

Page, a former Trump campaign aide, argued that public revelations of the FBI's secret monitoring had damaged his reputation and business prospects. Lower courts had already rejected his claims, and the Supreme Court declined to reconsider those rulings without explanation (Fox News, Just The News).

**The Conservative Take:** Republicans have long argued the FBI's surveillance of Page—later found to rest on a flawed FISA warrant application—exemplified deep-state overreach and political weaponization of law enforcement against Trump associates. The Justice Department's own inspector general concluded the FBI made significant errors in its application process. Yet Page's inability to hold officials accountable in court underscores a broader conservative concern: federal employees rarely face personal liability for constitutional violations, even when misconduct is proven.

The decision also highlights the judiciary's reluctance to second-guess intelligence agencies, a pattern conservatives say enables future abuses. Without meaningful consequences for officials who abuse surveillance powers, taxpayers foot the bill for settlements while perpetrators escape accountability.

**Watch:** Whether Congress will finally pass legislation holding federal law enforcement accountable for FISA violations—or if this remains another cautionary tale of unchecked government power.
