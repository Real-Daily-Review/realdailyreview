# Colorado Cyclists Pedal Past Gas Prices, But Safety Concerns Linger

Colorado commuters are increasingly ditching their cars for bicycles, driven by rising gas prices, health concerns, and quality-of-life considerations highlighted during this year's Bike to Work Day, according to CPR News. The trend reflects genuine economic pressure on households—fuel costs remain a real burden for working Coloradans trying to stretch paychecks.

However, safety remains a legitimate worry. Expanding bike infrastructure without addressing traffic safety comprehensively could create new risks for cyclists and drivers alike, conservatives argue. Before cities invest heavily in bike-lane expansion, policymakers should ensure these projects don't waste taxpayer dollars on feel-good initiatives that don't meaningfully reduce congestion or improve safety.

The shift also highlights a broader reality: when government policies and inflation make driving expensive, people find alternatives—not always by choice. Rather than celebrating reduced vehicle use, conservatives say the focus should be on addressing the root causes: energy policy that keeps fuel affordable and inflation under control.

Coloradans choosing bikes for commuting is fine; forcing them into it through policy is another matter entirely.
