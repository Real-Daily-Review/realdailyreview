# Writer Sues Over 'Yellowstone' Origins—But Colorado's Real Story Is the Show's Impact on the State

A Los Angeles writer, Lauren Salkin, is suing Taylor Sheridan, Paramount, and other studios, claiming her 2016 series pitch was stolen and became the hit show "Yellowstone" (9News Denver). Sheridan denies the allegations, and actor Billy Bob Thornton recently defended him, noting Sheridan's own hardscrabble path to success—including periods of homelessness (Daily Caller).

The lawsuit itself is a Hollywood IP dispute, but Coloradans should care about the bigger picture: "Yellowstone" has become a cultural juggernaut that shapes national perceptions of ranching, land use, and Western life. The show's anti-government, pro-property-rights messaging resonates with Colorado's conservative base and has elevated conversations about federal overreach on private lands—themes that directly parallel debates over water rights, energy development, and state sovereignty.

Whether Sheridan's creation originated from Salkin's pitch or elsewhere, the show's success has given voice to conservative Western values when mainstream media typically marginalizes them. That cultural win matters more than courtroom drama.

Watch whether this lawsuit gains traction or settles quietly—and whether "Yellowstone's" successor projects maintain the same unapologetic defense of landowner rights and skepticism of government intrusion.
