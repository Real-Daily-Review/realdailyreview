# Jeffco Schools' $73M Tax Hike Fails by Razor Margin—Recount Looms

Colorado taxpayers in Jefferson County narrowly rejected a $73 million mill levy override Tuesday, with Ballot Issue 5A failing by just five votes out of 301,789 cast, according to the *Denver Post*. A recount is expected as the measure teeters on the edge.

The mill levy override—one of two tax-increase proposals Jeffco schools placed before voters—would have raised property taxes to fund the district's operations. The companion measure, 5B, failed by a wider margin, *CPR News* reports.

**The conservative case:** Taxpayers are already shouldering heavy property tax burdens in Colorado's Front Range. School districts routinely argue they need more money, yet per-pupil spending has climbed for decades while academic outcomes stagnate. Republicans and fiscal conservatives say the answer isn't perpetual tax hikes—it's efficiency, accountability, and reallocation of existing resources. Voters in Colorado's second-largest school district sent a clear message: *no*.

Democrats and school advocates counter that aging facilities, rising costs, and competitive teacher salaries require new revenue. They argue underfunding education harms long-term competitiveness.

The recount will determine whether this narrow rejection holds—and whether Jeffco's school board tries again with voters or finally reckons with spending discipline.
