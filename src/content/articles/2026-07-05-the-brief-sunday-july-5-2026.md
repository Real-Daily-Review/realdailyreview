# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Sunday, July 5, 2026

America turned 250 yesterday — and from the National Mall to the Colorado Rockies, the country showed it still knows how to celebrate. But as the smoke clears, there's plenty for Colorado conservatives to keep their eyes on.

---

## Trump Puts America Back on the Frontier

President Trump used his Fourth of July address to recognize the Artemis II astronauts and deliver a characteristically bold promise: "We're going to go to Mars, and we're going to continue to be way ahead." It was a fitting message for the 250th — a reminder that American ambition, driven by competition and national pride rather than bureaucratic timidity, is what built this country. Colorado's aerospace corridor, from Lockheed Martin in Littleton to the Space Command hub in Colorado Springs, stands to benefit directly from a White House that treats space as a strategic priority.

---

## Brooklyn Shooting Darkens Fourth of July Celebrations

Five people — including a 7-year-old and a 5-year-old — were shot while watching fireworks in Brooklyn late Friday night. This is what soft-on-crime governance looks like in practice. New York's progressive prosecutors and defund-the-police movement have made Independence Day dangerous for families in America's largest city. Colorado conservatives should take note: Denver has been drifting in the same direction, and the results of gutting law enforcement accountability don't stay contained to the coasts.

---

## America at 250: Stewards, Not Owners, of Freedom

As the nation marks its semiseptennial, the conversation conservatives need to be having isn't just celebratory — it's about obligation. The framing of Americans as stewards, not owners, of freedom is exactly right. The Founders pledged their lives, fortunes, and sacred honor. Colorado's ranchers, veterans, small business owners, and energy workers carry that tradition forward every day against a regulatory state that keeps expanding its reach. The 250th is a good moment to remember what we're actually defending — and who keeps threatening it.

---

## Drones Watching the Skies on the Fourth

Public safety agencies across the country deployed drones to spot illegal fireworks this holiday weekend, enabled by new federal rules that accelerated drone-based enforcement — rules the FAA fast-tracked last year. Conservatives should ask a straightforward question: at what point does public safety become a pretext for routine aerial surveillance of American citizens on their own property? Colorado's rural counties and Front Range suburbs alike deserve a clear answer about how this data is collected, stored, and used before the program quietly expands year over year.

---

## Swift and Kelce Make It Official

Taylor Swift and Travis Kelce were married Friday at Madison Square Garden in front of roughly 1,000 guests, ending weeks of speculation. It's a cultural moment, not a political one — and that's worth noting. Two people building a life together, in public, without apology, is still something most Americans celebrate regardless of party. Colorado Chiefs fans — and there are plenty along the Front Range — can tip their hats and move on.

---

## What to Watch

Keep an eye on how Colorado's Democratic-controlled legislature responds to the federal drone enforcement expansion — if they embrace it as a model for state-level surveillance programs, taxpayers and property rights advocates will need to push back hard. Watch also for any post-holiday spending announcements out of Washington or Denver dressed up as patriotic "infrastructure" or "community investment" — that's the season for it. And as the America 250 commemorations wind down, the real question is whether Colorado's elected leaders govern like they believe the Founders' principles still apply — or just say so at ribbon-cuttings.
