# Heat Wave Headed East; Colorado Spared—For Now

A dangerous heat wave will pummel the central and eastern United States this week ahead of July Fourth, with the National Weather Service warning that high humidity will make temperatures feel even worse than the thermometer reads (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31).

The brunt of the heat targets the eastern half of the country, sparing Colorado from the worst of it—though Coloradans should note that western water supplies remain strained from years of drought and reduced snowpack. Energy demand in heat-stressed states will spike, underscoring why reliable baseload power from natural gas and coal remains critical infrastructure, not a relic to be phased out by renewable mandates.

Parts of the Southwest, including Phoenix, are already experiencing dangerous conditions. The National Weather Service is urging Americans to take precautions: stay hydrated, check on vulnerable neighbors, and avoid prolonged sun exposure.

For Colorado, the silver lining is temporary relief—but the long-term lesson is clear: climate variability demands resilient energy grids and water management, not ideological bans on proven energy sources that keep the lights on during peak demand.

Watch whether progressive policymakers use this heat event to justify accelerated coal plant closures or grid-destabilizing renewable mandates that could leave Colorado vulnerable when the next extreme weather hits.
