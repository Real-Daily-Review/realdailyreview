# Denver Braces for Cold Snap as City Opens Emergency Shelters

Denver is opening additional cold weather shelters as temperatures plunge into the 20s, with light snow already falling across the metro area and foothills (KDVR). The forecast shows a chilly Monday ahead, followed by a quieter midweek stretch with no major travel disruptions expected for Thanksgiving week (9News, KDVR).

Light mountain snowfall is anticipated, but conditions should remain dry enough for holiday travel plans. Overnight lows in the 20s mean residents should prepare for typical Colorado winter weather—bundle up, check vehicle fluids, and ensure heating systems are ready.

The city's decision to expand shelter capacity reflects standard cold-weather protocol, though it underscores the ongoing costs of homelessness management in Denver. Conservative critics have long argued that throwing more taxpayer money at shelters without addressing root causes—addiction, mental health, and work disincentives—merely perpetuates dependency rather than solving the problem. Republicans contend that sustainable solutions require personal responsibility, job training, and private-sector partnerships, not endless municipal spending.

Coloradans should monitor whether the city's shelter expansion includes accountability measures or simply becomes another open-ended budget line item.
