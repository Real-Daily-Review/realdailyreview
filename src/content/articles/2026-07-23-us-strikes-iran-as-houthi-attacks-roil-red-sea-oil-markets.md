# U.S. Strikes Iran as Houthi Attacks Threaten Global Oil Markets—Colorado Energy Workers Should Pay Attention

The U.S. military is conducting sustained strikes on Iranian targets as Iran-backed Houthis escalate attacks on commercial shipping in the Red Sea, threatening to destabilize global energy supplies and spike prices at Colorado pumps.

U.S. Central Command launched its 13th night of strikes against Iranian infrastructure (Just The News), responding to Houthi assaults on oil tankers transiting critical shipping routes. The Iran-backed rebels claimed dual attacks on Saudi tankers early Thursday as American forces targeted both military and civil infrastructure (Breitbart).

**The Colorado angle:** Disruptions to global oil markets directly hit Colorado consumers and energy workers. Higher crude prices ripple through gasoline costs and threaten the state's oil and gas sector—already under pressure from Biden-era regulatory hostility. Colorado's energy independence depends on stable global markets; Middle East instability undermines both.

Conservatives argue the Biden administration's weak posture toward Iran emboldened Tehran to sponsor proxy attacks. Republicans contend that strength and deterrence—not appeasement—prevent regional wars from metastasizing into economic crises at home.

Democrats frame the strikes as necessary defense of international commerce, though they've offered no coherent strategy to end the cycle.
