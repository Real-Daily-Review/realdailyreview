# Colorado Should Reject the Assisted-Suicide Agenda Gaining Ground in Blue States

Medical assistance in dying—euphemistically called "death with dignity"—is spreading across America, and Colorado must resist the pressure to follow.

The movement gained fresh momentum after Chicago radio personality Judy Markey, 82, traveled to Switzerland for assisted suicide last month. While sympathetic cases dominate headlines, conservatives warn the underlying philosophy is deeply troubling: the notion that individuals become "burdensome" and have a duty to exit rather than burden others (National Review).

Colorado already permits medical aid in dying under narrow circumstances, but progressive activists are pushing to expand eligibility and remove safeguards. Republicans and pro-life advocates argue these laws inevitably slide toward coercion—particularly targeting elderly, disabled, and economically vulnerable Coloradans who may feel pressured to choose death over being a "burden" on family or state resources.

The real danger: once society accepts that some lives aren't worth living, cost-cutting bureaucrats and guilt-laden families become enablers. Colorado's healthcare system already struggles with rationing; loosening assisted-suicide rules invites the state to subtly discourage expensive treatments for seniors and the disabled.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats introduce bills to expand the state's current law—and whether they'll be honest about the philosophical shift from "choice" to "duty to die."
