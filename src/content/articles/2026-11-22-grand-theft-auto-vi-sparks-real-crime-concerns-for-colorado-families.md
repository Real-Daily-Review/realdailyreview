# Grand Theft Auto VI Sparks Real-World Crime Concerns—Colorado Parents Should Pay Attention

Colorado families should think twice before handing over the latest *Grand Theft Auto* installment to their kids: two Italian minors allegedly stole a bus and vandalized others while imitating the game, according to Breitbart.

The incident underscores a growing conservative concern about the cultural messaging embedded in hyper-realistic video games. National Review has raised pointed questions about *GTA VI*'s glorification of criminality and whether spending hours simulating theft, violence, and mayhem shapes young minds—particularly those still developing impulse control and moral reasoning.

Game publishers and the entertainment industry argue that millions play these titles without committing crimes, and that parental controls and age ratings exist for a reason. They contend that blaming games for criminal behavior ignores deeper social and family factors.

But conservatives counter that the line between entertainment and influence is real, especially for minors. When a game's entire premise rewards criminal behavior with no meaningful consequences, it normalizes lawlessness in ways previous generations never experienced.

Colorado parents and educators should demand transparency from retailers about who's buying these titles and consider whether the "M for Mature" rating is sufficient protection—or whether family conversations about media consumption need to happen first.

Watch for whether Colorado retailers face pressure to restrict *GTA VI* sales to minors, and whether parental advocacy groups push back against the game's marketing to younger audiences.
