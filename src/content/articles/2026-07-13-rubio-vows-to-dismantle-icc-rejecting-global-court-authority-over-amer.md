# Trump Administration Moves to Defang Unaccountable International Court

Secretary of State Marco Rubio is escalating the Trump administration's pushback against the International Criminal Court, vowing to "dismantle" the institution that claims authority to prosecute American officials and military personnel. Rubio characterized the ICC as an "unaccountable arbiter of a new global law"—a direct challenge to what conservatives view as creeping supranational power over U.S. sovereignty (The Daily Wire).

The move reflects a core Trump-era principle: rejecting international bodies that constrain American decision-making without democratic accountability. The ICC, based in The Hague, has asserted jurisdiction over U.S. servicemembers and policymakers, a claim that Republicans argue undermines American independence and exposes U.S. personnel to politically motivated prosecution.

For Colorado, the broader implication is clear: the Trump administration is signaling resistance to global governance frameworks that could eventually constrain domestic policy—from energy development to Second Amendment protections—through international legal pressure. Coloradans have long resisted federal overreach; this stance extends that skepticism to unelected foreign tribunals.

Democrats typically defend multilateral institutions as necessary for global stability, but conservatives counter that American citizens should answer only to American courts, not international bodies beyond voter reach.

Watch whether Congress moves to formally withdraw U.S. cooperation with the ICC or strip its funding.
