# Colorado's 4th District Hangs in Balance as Boebert Race Remains Uncalled

Thousands of uncounted ballots in Colorado's 4th Congressional District could still determine whether Rep. Lauren Boebert keeps her seat or loses to Democrat Eileen Laubacher.

As of mid-week, Laubacher held a lead of 3,352 votes, but the Colorado Sun reports that "possibly thousands" of remaining ballots won't be counted until the end of the week. The Associated Press has not yet called the race, leaving the outcome genuinely uncertain—a rarity in modern elections where media outlets typically project winners well before final tallies.

Boebert has refused to concede, according to Breitbart, a position consistent with her stated commitment to wait for all votes to be counted. Her campaign's caution reflects legitimate concerns about mail-in ballot processing timelines and the genuine possibility that outstanding votes could shift the result.

The 4th District race carries national significance: Boebert is a vocal conservative voice on Second Amendment rights, energy development, and federal overreach—issues that resonate strongly with rural Colorado voters. A Laubacher victory would hand Democrats another Colorado House seat and further entrench the state's leftward drift in federal representation.

Conservatives and Republicans should monitor the final ballot count closely and demand transparency in how remaining votes are processed and tabulated.
