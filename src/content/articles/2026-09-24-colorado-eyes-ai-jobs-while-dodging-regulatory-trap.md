# Colorado Should Embrace AI Jobs, Not Regulate Them Into Oblivion

Colorado has a chance to lead on artificial intelligence workforce development—if lawmakers resist the urge to over-regulate a booming sector.

The debate is heating up. The Colorado Sun's opinion section argues the state should create AI-enabled apprenticeships, positioning young workers in supervised roles with clear standards. That's a market-friendly approach worth considering.

But conservatives worry about the regulatory creep that typically follows. Complete Colorado notes that a Magellan Strategies survey shows Coloradans are ready to embrace AI opportunity rather than panic. The state's data center and tech corridor could become a genuine economic engine—if government stays out of the way.

Here's the conservative case: Colorado's workforce needs good-paying jobs. AI and data centers deliver them. Apprenticeships make sense when they're voluntary partnerships between employers and workers, not government-mandated programs that add compliance costs. The risk is that well-intentioned "standards" become red tape that kills the very jobs progressives claim to want.

Coloradans should watch whether the legislature treats AI as an opportunity for economic growth or a threat requiring new regulations and restrictions. The difference will determine whether Colorado leads or lags in the next decade of job creation.
