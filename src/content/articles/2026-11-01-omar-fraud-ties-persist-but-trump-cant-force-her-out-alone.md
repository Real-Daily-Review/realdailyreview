# Omar Scandal Won't Die—But Constitutional Reality Limits Trump's Options

Minnesota Democrat Ilhan Omar faces mounting scrutiny over alleged ties to individuals involved in fraud schemes, but President Trump's calls for her resignation bump against a constitutional wall: only the House can remove her.

Fox News reports that Trump has demanded Omar resign over alleged immigration fraud allegations, yet the Constitution vests expulsion power exclusively in Congress. Republicans, meanwhile, are keeping pressure on. Minnesota State Rep. Kristin Robbins told The Daily Wire that Omar was "thick as thieves" with people caught up in a major fraud scandal, describing her as "one fed-up Republican" over unanswered questions about those connections.

The New York Times recently exposed another twist in the saga, reigniting conservative calls for accountability. Republicans argue Omar's associations demand serious investigation; Democrats have largely remained silent on the specifics, instead dismissing GOP criticism as politically motivated.

**Colorado angle:** While Omar represents Minnesota, her conduct reflects broader concerns conservatives raise about Democratic leadership accountability—a message that resonates with Colorado Republicans skeptical of progressive politicians' judgment on spending, regulation, and governance.
