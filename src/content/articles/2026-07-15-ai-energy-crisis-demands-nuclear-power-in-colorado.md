# AI Data Centers Demand Nuclear Power—Colorado Must Act Now

Uncontrolled AI data center sprawl is creating an energy crisis that threatens Colorado's grid and economy unless policymakers embrace nuclear power as the only viable solution.

Data centers powering artificial intelligence are consuming electricity at unprecedented rates, straining grids across the country. The *Washington Examiner* reports that Virginia—hosting roughly a quarter of U.S. data centers—faces residential neighborhoods plagued by diesel backup generators and air quality problems from inadequate zoning oversight. Colorado faces similar risks as tech companies eye our state's lower costs and available land.

Conservatives argue that nuclear power is the only realistic answer to meet AI's massive energy demands without crippling the grid or forcing rolling blackouts. Unlike wind and solar—which depend on weather and require massive battery storage—nuclear provides reliable, 24/7 baseload power with minimal land footprint. It's also carbon-free, undercutting the left's climate arguments against it.

The problem: Colorado's regulatory environment and anti-nuclear sentiment among progressive policymakers have stalled serious nuclear development. Meanwhile, data centers will either locate here with proper infrastructure or move elsewhere, taking jobs and tax revenue with them.

Coloradans should watch whether state legislators will streamline nuclear permitting and zoning rules to attract data centers responsibly—or cede the opportunity to states with smarter energy policies.
