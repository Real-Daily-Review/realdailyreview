# Supreme Court Backs Trump on Green Card Immigration Powers

The Supreme Court has sided with the Trump administration in a significant immigration case involving the government's authority over green card holders—a win for border security advocates who argue Washington needs stronger enforcement tools.

The decision arrives as the Court works through multiple immigration disputes amid the administration's aggressive crackdown on illegal entry and deportations. Conservatives have long argued that federal immigration law grants the executive branch broad discretion to manage who enters and remains in the country, and that courts should defer to executive judgment on national security grounds.

The ruling underscores a broader judicial shift toward upholding executive immigration authority after years of progressive litigation blocking Trump-era policies. For Colorado, where Denver and other cities have clashed with federal immigration enforcement, the decision could embolden ICE operations and complicate sanctuary city policies that local Democrats have championed.

Republicans argue the Court's decision restores proper constitutional balance—giving the president tools to enforce immigration law without endless judicial obstruction. Democrats and immigrant-rights groups counter that unchecked executive power over green card holders threatens due process and legal permanent residents.
