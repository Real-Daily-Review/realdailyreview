# Justice Department Settles TikTok Privacy Case; Conservatives Question Selective Enforcement

The Justice Department has reached a $400 million settlement with TikTok over violations of children's privacy laws, capping a 2024 lawsuit that forced major changes to the platform's U.S. ownership structure, according to 9News Denver.

The settlement marks a rare enforcement action against Big Tech on data protection—but conservatives are raising questions about the DOJ's selective priorities. While the agency pursues TikTok aggressively, Republicans argue the same Justice Department has greenlit corporate discrimination against American workers in hiring practices. Texas Gov. Greg Abbott recently asked the DOJ to investigate "discriminatory" Islamic washing stations at Dallas-Fort Worth and Houston airports, citing potential civil rights violations—a request that underscores conservative frustration with what they see as inconsistent enforcement of federal law (Fox News Politics).

Meanwhile, immigration attorney John Miano warns that the DOJ has effectively permitted companies to discriminate against American job applicants in favor of H-1B visa holders, according to Breitbart. Conservatives argue this represents a troubling double standard: aggressive action against foreign tech platforms, but permissive treatment of corporate hiring bias against U.S. citizens.

**Watch:** Whether the DOJ applies the same enforcement vigor to alleged discrimination in federal contractor hiring and airport accommodations as it does to TikTok's data practices.
