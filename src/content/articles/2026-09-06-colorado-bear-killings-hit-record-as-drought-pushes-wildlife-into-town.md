# Colorado Bear Killings Hit Record as Drought, Wildfire Drive Wildlife Into Towns

Colorado Parks and Wildlife has euthanized 294 bears this year—more than triple last year's toll—as drought and wildfire push starving wildlife into residential areas at unprecedented rates. The surge reflects a hard reality: when government land management fails to prevent catastrophic fire and water scarcity, Coloradans and wildlife both pay the price.

According to the Colorado Sun, the spike stems from record human-bear encounters driven by environmental stress. Bears desperate for food are ransacking garbage, entering homes, and threatening families across the state. Rather than address root causes—forest management, water policy, and land stewardship—progressive environmental policies have often restricted controlled burns and water development, leaving Colorado's landscape tinderbox-dry.

Republicans and conservatives argue that sound forest management and responsible water infrastructure investment could reduce both wildfire severity and wildlife desperation. Instead, Colorado has seen years of regulatory gridlock and environmental litigation that delay necessary forest thinning and water projects.

The human cost is clear: more dead bears, more frightened families, more taxpayer resources spent on crisis management rather than prevention. Wildlife officials face an impossible choice between public safety and animal welfare—a choice that wouldn't be necessary with proactive land stewardship.

Watch whether Colorado's next legislative session prioritizes forest management funding and water infrastructure, or doubles down on the policies that created this crisis.
