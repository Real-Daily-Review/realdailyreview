# Army Moves Forward With Hazardous Chemical Detonation in Iowa

The U.S. Army is proceeding with a Wednesday detonation of unstable lead azide—a bomb-making chemical—in Iowa after securing approval from the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, Gov. Kim Reynolds (R-IA), and Gov.-elect Rob Sand (D-IA), according to the Washington Examiner and 9News Denver.

Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle announced the decision Monday, clearing the way for the controlled demolition of the volatile material that has been stored at an Army ammunition plant. The chemical's instability has made its continued storage a growing safety and environmental concern.

The court's green light came after legal challenges to the detonation plan, which raised questions about air quality and community safety in the surrounding region. Both Iowa's outgoing Republican governor and incoming Democratic governor backed the Army's approach, suggesting broad agreement that removal of the hazardous material outweighs risks from the controlled blast.

While the detonation is localized to Iowa, Colorado taxpayers should note that the Army manages similar munitions and chemical stockpiles at installations across the West. The precedent set here—balancing environmental caution against the practical necessity of disposing hazardous military materials—could affect how the Defense Department handles similar situations at Colorado bases.
