# Connecticut Democrats Purge Moderates, Lurch Further Left in Primary Rout

Connecticut Democrats are eating their own—and it's a preview of how far the party's activist base has drifted from the center. Fourteen-term Rep. John Larson, 78, lost his primary Tuesday to Hartford Mayor Luke Bronin, a younger progressive who hammered the incumbent for being insufficiently radical on generational issues (Fox News, Breitbart). Meanwhile, Gov. Ned Lamont, already a reliably progressive executive, faces a primary challenge from Josh Elliott, who attacks Lamont as too "nice" to Trump and therefore unfit to combat what Elliott calls "fascism" (Fox News).

The pattern is unmistakable: Democratic primary voters are rejecting even their own establishment figures as too moderate. Larson's ouster after nearly 30 years in Congress signals that seniority and legislative experience count for nothing when the activist left demands ideological purity.

**The Colorado angle:** While Connecticut's internal Democratic drama may seem distant, it mirrors pressure on Colorado's own Democratic establishment. As the state's progressive wing grows bolder on taxes, gun control, and energy regulation, moderate Democrats face similar primary threats. Colorado Republicans should watch whether the state's Dem-controlled legislature accelerates its spending and regulatory agenda—or whether primary challenges from the left force even bolder moves.
