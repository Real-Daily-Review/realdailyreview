# Denver Violence Spike Raises Questions About Public Safety Strategy

Denver's streets saw multiple violent incidents over the weekend—including officer-involved shootings and stabbings—renewing conservative concerns about whether the city's approach to crime and mental health is working.

On Sunday, Denver police shot a man reported to be harassing people with a knife in north Denver (KDVR Fox31). The same day, officers responded to another shooting in the Globeville neighborhood at 50th and Washington (9News). Separately, a 44-year-old man was killed early Sunday outside a home on East 91st Avenue in Thornton where a party was being held (9News). On Saturday, a stabbing incident left one woman dead and multiple others hospitalized (KDVR).

**The conservative angle:** Republicans and public safety advocates argue Denver's progressive approach—emphasizing de-escalation, reducing police funding, and relying on social workers for mental health calls—has failed to stem violence. They point to these incidents as evidence that criminals exploit a permissive environment while law-abiding Coloradans bear the cost.

Democrats counter that isolated incidents don't reflect systemic failure and that addressing root causes requires sustained investment in mental health and social services.

**Watch next:** Whether Denver's city council will reverse course on public safety funding or double down on the current strategy heading into budget season.
