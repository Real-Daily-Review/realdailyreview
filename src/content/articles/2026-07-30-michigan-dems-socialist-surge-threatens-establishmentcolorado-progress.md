# Michigan Democrats' Socialist Surge Exposes Party Fracture—Colorado Progressives Should Take Note

Michigan's Democratic primary is imploding as socialist candidate Abdul El-Sayed crushes establishment moderate Rep. Haley Stevens by 15 points in the U.S. Senate race, according to Breitbart, signaling a dramatic leftward lurch that mirrors tensions within Colorado's own Democratic establishment.

El-Sayed, a former public health official, is running hard left on healthcare, immigration, and corporate influence—positions that resonate with the party's activist base but alienate moderates and general-election voters. Stevens and other establishment Democrats are mounting a last stand, but the numbers tell the story: El-Sayed leads the Republican general-election opponent, Mike Rogers, by only 10 points despite his primary dominance (Breitbart).

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado Democrats face similar internal warfare between progressive activists demanding Medicare-for-All, open borders rhetoric, and corporate-accountability crusades versus moderates trying to hold purple districts. If Michigan's socialist surge translates to a general-election loss—or a weakened nominee—it's a cautionary tale for Democrats banking on ideological purity over electability.

Republicans should watch whether El-Sayed's nomination energizes GOP turnout and whether Colorado's Democratic primary season mirrors Michigan's fracture.
