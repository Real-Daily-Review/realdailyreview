# Colorado Sheriff Eyes Takeover of Volunteer-Run Search and Rescue

Larimer County Sheriff John Feyen is pushing to centralize Colorado's backcountry search and rescue operations under law enforcement control—a move that would upend a half-century of volunteer-led coordination that has worked without government bureaucracy.

The proposal would shift coordination of multiagency SAR missions from Colorado's established volunteer network to the sheriff's office. Conservatives should ask hard questions: Why fix what isn't broken? Colorado's volunteer SAR system has operated effectively for over 50 years without taxpayer-funded overhead, administrative bloat, or government red tape. Centralizing under a sheriff's department means new costs, potential inefficiency, and another layer of government control over a community service that thrives on local initiative and voluntary cooperation.

The timing is notable: a recent Colorado National Guard helicopter sustained a blade strike during a rescue mission in Boulder County (9News), though no injuries were reported. Whether this incident is driving the proposal remains unclear, but it shouldn't justify dismantling a proven volunteer model.

Coloradans should watch whether this proposal gains traction in the legislature or among county commissioners—and demand clear evidence that government takeover improves outcomes rather than simply expanding the bureaucracy.
