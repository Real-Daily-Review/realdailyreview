# Employer Health Benefits Crumbling: Colorado Workers Face Shift to Individual Coverage

Major employers are abandoning traditional health benefits, signaling a fundamental break in the postwar employer-insurance model that has left workers vulnerable and dependent on government solutions. Bloomberg announced it will end employee health coverage in 2027, forcing workers to buy their own policies—a move the *Washington Examiner* reports reflects a broader trend: 51% of employers are "likely or" considering similar cuts.

The shift exposes a core conservative argument: employer-based healthcare locks workers into corporate dependency and leaves them exposed when companies exit the market. Republicans have long argued that portable, individually-owned health insurance—decoupled from employment—would increase competition, lower costs, and give workers real choice. This trend, however, risks pushing Colorado workers toward government-run alternatives if the private market doesn't fill the gap quickly.

The danger: without a robust private insurance marketplace ready to absorb millions of newly uninsured workers, Democrats will seize the opening to expand Medicaid, push a public option, or resurrect single-payer schemes—exactly what progressives have wanted for years.

Colorado policymakers and business leaders should watch whether the state's insurance market can adapt fast enough to offer affordable, portable plans before the political left weaponizes the coverage gap.
