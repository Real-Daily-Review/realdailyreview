# Colorado School of Mines Sweetens Admissions Deal While Districts Push Tax Hikes

Colorado School of Mines is guaranteeing admission and scholarships to qualified in-state high school graduates—a smart move to lock in talent and reduce barriers for Colorado kids pursuing engineering and STEM careers (9News Denver). The initiative rewards academic achievement without adding state burden.

Meanwhile, school districts across Colorado are lining up hundreds of millions in property tax hike ballot measures for November (Complete Colorado), betting voters will approve new obligations on homeowners and businesses already squeezed by inflation and rising costs. Conservatives argue these districts should prioritize spending efficiency and fiscal restraint before asking taxpayers for more.

On the elections front, Colorado voters will decide Initiative 362 in November, which would add voter ID requirements to the state's all-mail voting system (Complete Colorado)—a measure Republicans say strengthens election integrity, while Democrats typically oppose tighter authentication rules.

The political landscape is shifting: as Colorado Republicans lose ground in statewide races, conservative groups are winning ballot fights on policy (Denver Post), proving ideas can advance even when candidates falter. A Denver judge also ordered a new Libertarian secretary of state candidate onto the ballot after the previous nominee's sudden withdrawal (Denver Post).

**Watch closely:** whether November's school tax measures pass and how Initiative 362 performs—both will signal Colorado voters' appetite for fiscal restraint and election security.
