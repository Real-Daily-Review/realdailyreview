# New Mexico County Blocks Data Centers—Colorado Should Watch the Regulatory Backlash

Colfax County in northern New Mexico just slapped a moratorium on data centers, signaling that even Republican-leaning rural counties are souring on the infrastructure boom that could reshape the Mountain West. The move leaves incorporated towns like Raton free to approve projects independently, but the message is clear: local resistance to data-center sprawl is hardening.

The timing matters for Colorado. As *National Review* notes, the Trump administration is bullish on data-center development as a path to AI dominance and energy independence. Trump himself has said "let data reign" in response to pushback (per *Just The News*), and even some Democrats—notably Sen. John Fetterman—have sided with the pro-development camp.

But Colfax County's move reflects a real tension: data centers demand massive power and water resources, two commodities Colorado guards jealously. Rural counties worry about land use, grid strain, and environmental impact. Republicans and conservatives are split. Some see data centers as free-market opportunity and energy-sector jobs; others fear unchecked industrial development trampling local control and water rights.

Colorado's Front Range and Eastern Plains could face similar pressure. Watch whether state lawmakers move to preempt local moratoria or strengthen county authority over large industrial projects—a classic conservative battleground between property rights and community sovereignty.
