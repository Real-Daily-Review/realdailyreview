# Teens' Red Rocks Hike Ends in Rescue—A Reminder of Colorado's Backcountry Risks

Two teenagers learned an expensive lesson in Colorado outdoor planning after deciding to hike back to Morrison from a Red Rocks concert Wednesday night. West Metro Fire Rescue deployed resources to locate and extract the lost hikers, who had underestimated both the terrain and darkness on trails they didn't know.

The incident underscores a growing problem: unprepared visitors treating Colorado's backcountry like a theme park. Red Rocks sits in Morrison, just west of Denver, and while the venue itself is accessible, the surrounding terrain demands respect and planning. Hikers who skip basic precautions—maps, headlamps, knowledge of exit routes—routinely trigger rescue operations that tie up emergency personnel and drain public resources.

West Metro Fire Rescue's Facebook post noted the obvious: "It seemed like a great idea – until it was time to find their way back." That's the Colorado reality. Our mountains don't care about your concert schedule.

This isn't a knock on the teens personally—it's a reminder that Colorado's natural beauty demands preparation. Too many visitors arrive expecting wilderness to behave like a parking lot. Rescue operations cost taxpayer money and put first responders at risk for preventable mistakes.

**Watch for:** Whether local authorities increase public education campaigns on backcountry safety, or whether rescue costs prompt policy discussions about liability for unprepared hikers.
