# Front Range Rail District Pushes $295M Annual Tax Hike on Colorado Voters

Colorado's Front Range Passenger Rail District is asking voters to approve a 0.333% sales tax increase—projected to raise $295 million annually—to fund the Colorado Connector passenger rail line from Denver to Pueblo, according to Complete Colorado.

The ballot measure would authorize both the new sales tax and additional debt to finance the southern route of the proposed CoCo rail project (CPR News). Supporters frame it as essential infrastructure for regional connectivity.

**Conservatives argue** this represents another tax grab on Colorado families and businesses already burdened by state and local levies. The permanent sales tax increase would hit every purchase across the district, disproportionately affecting lower-income households and small retailers. Republicans question whether government-subsidized passenger rail—historically dependent on ongoing federal and state support—represents sound fiscal policy or another unfunded liability dressed up as progress.

The rail district's reliance on a new tax rather than private investment or existing revenue sources underscores the progressive model: expand government services first, then extract the taxes to pay for them. Colorado taxpayers should scrutinize whether this permanent tax delivers the promised ridership and fiscal returns, or becomes another money-losing government operation.

**Watch whether the rail district releases independent ridership projections and long-term operating cost estimates before the ballot measure reaches voters.**
