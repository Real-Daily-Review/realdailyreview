# Democratic Candidates Face Serious Allegations as Election Nears

Two Democratic House candidates are dealing with damaging allegations that underscore security and vetting concerns plaguing their party's 2024 campaign infrastructure.

In Pennsylvania's 10th District, the campaign of Democrat Janelle Stelson denied any connection to Chimaobim Adedayo Igbojekwe, a 35-year-old Nigerian national arrested by ICE on September 27 after pleading guilty to disorderly conduct for allegedly exposing himself in public (Washington Examiner). The denial came after questions about the individual's association with her campaign challenging Rep. Scott Perry (R-PA).

Meanwhile, Arizona Democrat Bernadette Greene-Placentia, running against Rep. Abraham Hamadeh (R-AZ) in the state's 8th District, reported being raped in August while repairing campaign signs at 2 a.m. in a parking lot. The Maricopa County Sheriff's Office is investigating the allegation (The Daily Wire, Washington Examiner).

**The conservative takeaway:** These incidents raise hard questions about Democratic campaign vetting, worker safety protocols, and judgment in field operations. Republicans argue the left's open-borders rhetoric and lax hiring standards create vulnerabilities—both to criminal elements and to operational chaos that puts volunteers at risk.
