# Colorado School Districts Push $177M in New Tax Asks While Dodging Accountability

Jefferson County Schools is demanding $133 million more from already-burdened taxpayers—even as the district battles federal litigation over its controversial transgender-student policies (Complete Colorado). Meanwhile, Denver Public Schools is seeking a $44 million mill levy override, with $25.8 million earmarked for employee raises (Denver Post).

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue these districts are asking for blank checks without addressing core problems. Jeffco's push comes amid ongoing federal court fights that have divided the community and distracted from educational outcomes. Denver's proposal prioritizes staff compensation over classroom resources—a familiar pattern in Colorado's education-spending debates.

Neither district has made a compelling case that throwing more money at bloated bureaucracies will improve student achievement. Colorado taxpayers already shoulder heavy education funding burdens; these asks ignore the real question: where did the last dollar go?

School boards across the state are banking on voter fatigue and the assumption that "it's for the kids" will carry the day. Watch whether Colorado voters reject these overrides or continue rubber-stamping district spending regardless of performance metrics.
