# Healthcare Bureaucracy Is Killing the Doctor-Patient Relationship—and Colorado Patients Are Paying the Price

Modern healthcare policy has become a cautionary tale in government overreach: policymakers obsess over top-down metrics—insurance premium caps, digital health mandates, electronic health record requirements—while systematically destroying the human connection that makes medicine work (Washington Examiner).

The result is physician burnout and a system that makes patients feel irrelevant. Colorado doctors, already stretched thin by regulatory compliance costs and insurance company red tape, are caught between corporate mandates and the care their patients actually need. When bureaucrats prioritize administrative efficiency over the doctor-patient relationship, medicine stops being about healing and starts being about checking boxes.

Conservatives argue that healthcare reform should restore individual choice and physician autonomy, not expand government mandates and corporate control. The left's solution—more regulation, more digital infrastructure requirements, more top-down oversight—has demonstrably failed to improve outcomes while driving up costs and driving out doctors.

Colorado's healthcare workforce is already strained. Adding more regulatory burden won't fix access or quality; it will only accelerate physician shortages and force more Coloradans into impersonal, algorithm-driven care.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado legislators push back against federal healthcare mandates or double down on state-level regulatory expansion in the next session.
