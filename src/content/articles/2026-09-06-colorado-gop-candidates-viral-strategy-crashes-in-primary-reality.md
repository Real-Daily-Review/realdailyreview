# Colorado Republican's Online Fame Didn't Translate to Primary Votes

Casey Putsch's viral social-media strategy collapsed when Colorado Republicans actually voted. The Colorado gubernatorial candidate built a national following through provocative online content—including AI-generated Hitler videos—but lost his GOP primary by a wide margin, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Putsch and fellow Republican James Fishback (who lost his own gubernatorial primary) both operated under the same flawed assumption: digital attention converts into electoral support. It doesn't.

Republicans argue this cautionary tale exposes a dangerous disconnect in modern politics: influencer campaigns that generate clicks and national media coverage can mask weakness with actual voters. Putsch's online notoriety never translated to ground game, voter contact, or genuine grassroots support in Colorado—the fundamentals that win primaries.

The lesson for Colorado conservatives: viral moments and national attention are not substitutes for old-fashioned campaigning, local relationships, and clear policy platforms. Candidates who chase online clout over substance will lose to disciplined opponents who focus on Colorado voters' real concerns: taxes, energy policy, water rights, and Second Amendment protections.

Watch whether future Colorado Republican candidates learn this lesson or continue chasing TikTok fame at the expense of serious primary competition.
