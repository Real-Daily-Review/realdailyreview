# Permanent Daylight Saving Time Stalls in Senate—Colorado Winters Would Stay Dark

Permanent daylight saving time faces an uphill battle in Congress, with Senator Tom Cotton (R-Ark.) telling Breitbart he doesn't expect the measure to pass the Senate. The proposal would keep clocks permanently one hour ahead year-round, eliminating the twice-yearly time changes that frustrate millions of Americans.

For Colorado and other western states, the trade-off is stark: while summer evenings would stay lighter longer, winter mornings would shift dramatically. Under permanent daylight saving time, the sun wouldn't rise until after 8 a.m. on many winter days—and after 9 a.m. for at least a few days—according to KDVR Fox31 Denver analysis.

Conservatives and Republicans have long opposed permanent daylight saving time, arguing that standard time better aligns with natural circadian rhythms and school schedules. The disruption to Colorado's already-short winter daylight hours would affect ranchers, outdoor workers, and families managing early morning routines.

The Senate's resistance suggests the measure lacks the political momentum to advance, despite periodic pushes from proponents who claim the change would boost economic activity and reduce energy consumption—claims conservatives dispute.

Watch whether Congress revisits permanent standard time instead, which would eliminate the spring-forward change without darkening Colorado winters.
