# Thanksgiving Affordability Crisis Hits Colorado Families' Wallets

Coloradans are facing a harsh reality this holiday season: putting a traditional Thanksgiving dinner on the table has become a genuine financial strain for working families across the state.

According to the Daily Caller, even basic holiday meals now represent a significant bite out of household budgets, with food and travel costs climbing beyond what many middle-class Coloradans can comfortably afford. The affordability crisis reflects broader economic pressures—inflation, energy costs, and grocery prices—that have squeezed household purchasing power over the past two years.

Republicans and conservatives argue that Biden-era spending policies and the Federal Reserve's delayed interest-rate response fueled the inflation spike that's now pricing ordinary Americans out of holiday traditions. They point to energy policy uncertainty and regulatory overreach as contributors to sustained cost-of-living increases that disproportionately harm working Coloradans and fixed-income seniors.

Democrats counter that supply-chain disruptions and global factors drove inflation, not domestic policy alone, and that wage growth has partially offset price increases.

The real story for Colorado taxpayers: middle-class families are making hard choices about holiday gatherings. Watch whether state and federal policymakers propose targeted relief or continue pursuing policies that keep upward pressure on everyday costs.
