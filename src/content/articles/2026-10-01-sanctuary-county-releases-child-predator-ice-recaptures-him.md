# Border Chaos Enables Child Predators While Local Governments Look Away

A Honduran illegal immigrant accused of sexually assaulting children was arrested by ICE after Fairfax County, Virginia, released him despite a federal detainer—a stark reminder that sanctuary policies endanger American kids.

Oscar Evaristo Nunez faced child sex crime charges when local authorities let him walk free last month, according to the Department of Homeland Security. ICE recaptured him, but the damage was done: a predator roamed free because one county prioritized ideology over public safety.

**Republicans argue** that sanctuary jurisdictions that ignore ICE detainers are complicit in preventable crimes. They point to cases like Nunez's as evidence that catch-and-release policies and refusal to cooperate with federal immigration enforcement create dangerous gaps—especially for vulnerable children.

**Democrats counter** that local law enforcement shouldn't be deputized as immigration agents, claiming it erodes community trust. But conservatives say that's a false choice: protecting children from violent criminals isn't "deputization"—it's basic governance.

Colorado has its own sanctuary-adjacent policies in some jurisdictions. While the state isn't as restrictive as California or New York, Denver and other cities have limited ICE cooperation, raising questions about how many dangerous criminals slip through local hands.

Coloradans should demand their local sheriffs and police chiefs clarify: will they honor ICE detainers for violent offenders, or will ideology trump child safety?
