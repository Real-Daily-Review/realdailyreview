# Former Colorado Supreme Court Justice Melissa Hart Dies

Colorado loses a judicial figure whose tenure shaped state law during a period of significant ideological shifts on the bench.

Melissa Hart, who served on the Colorado Supreme Court from 2017 to 2025, has died. Hart was appointed by then-Gov. John Hickenlooper in 2017 and resigned earlier this year following a health-related leave of absence, according to CPR News and the Colorado Sun.

Hart's decade-long tenure coincided with major decisions affecting Colorado's regulatory landscape, energy policy, and constitutional interpretation. Her departure from the bench in 2025 marked another transition in Colorado's judicial composition—a court that has increasingly reflected the state's demographic and political shifts.

The vacancy Hart's resignation created will be filled through Colorado's merit selection process, which allows the governor to appoint justices from a list vetted by a judicial commission. Conservatives have long scrutinized this system, arguing it can insulate judicial appointments from direct voter accountability and allow progressive legal philosophies to entrench themselves on the bench without electoral oversight.

Hart's passing removes a sitting justice whose judicial philosophy and opinions will now be part of Colorado's legal record. The state's Supreme Court will continue operating with the current composition pending any new appointments or retirements.

Watch whether Gov. Jared Polis uses Hart's vacancy to shift the court's ideological balance further left—or whether Republicans push for greater transparency in the merit selection process.
