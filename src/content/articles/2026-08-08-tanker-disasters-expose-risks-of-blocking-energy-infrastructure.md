# Oil Tanker Disasters Underscore Need for Energy Infrastructure Modernization

A sanctioned tanker carrying nearly 1 million barrels of crude is sinking off Oman's coast, while a separate incident saw a California gas tanker explode on Highway 99, killing energy infrastructure vulnerabilities that demand attention—not more environmental restrictions.

The Oman vessel, visible in satellite imagery, is increasingly submerged with a growing oil spill threatening wildlife (9News Denver). Meanwhile, the California explosion involving 8,600 gallons of gasoline forced highway closures and required emergency response crews (New York Post).

**The conservative case:** These disasters highlight why America needs *reliable* energy infrastructure and domestic production capacity. Environmental activists who oppose pipeline modernization and new drilling on federal lands—including Colorado acreage—push energy transport onto aging tanker fleets and overloaded highways. The solution isn't less energy; it's safer, more efficient transport and production systems.

Democrats typically respond that such incidents justify stricter environmental regulations and accelerated transition away from fossil fuels. Conservatives counter that well-maintained pipelines and domestic infrastructure are far safer than the alternatives progressives force upon us.

For Colorado: watch whether state regulators use these incidents to further restrict energy development or pipeline projects—moves that would harm our economy and energy independence while solving nothing.
