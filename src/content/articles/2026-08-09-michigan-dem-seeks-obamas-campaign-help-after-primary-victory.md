# Michigan Democrat Seeks Obama Boost After Primary Win

Michigan's progressive Democratic Senate nominee Abdul el-Sayed is banking on star power from the Obama wing of the party to carry him through the general election. After defeating Rep. Haley Stevens in the Democratic primary last week, el-Sayed announced he'd spoken with former President Barack Obama, calling the conversation "warm and inspiring" and expressing hope that Obama will hit the campaign trail on his behalf (Washington Examiner).

The move signals el-Sayed's strategy: lean on national Democratic celebrity to energize the base and offset any enthusiasm gap heading into November's matchup against Republican Mike Rogers (Just The News).

Republicans argue that el-Sayed's reliance on Obama-era progressivism—rather than focusing on kitchen-table issues like inflation, border security, and energy costs—reveals how out of touch Michigan Democrats have become. Conservatives note that invoking Obama's legacy, while popular with the activist left, may alienate working-class voters in a state hit hard by economic headwinds.

For Colorado conservatives watching national trends: this is a bellwether race. If Democrats must resurrect Obama nostalgia to motivate voters in a swing state, it suggests their current agenda isn't selling on its own merits—a pattern worth monitoring as we head into November.

Watch whether Obama actually campaigns for el-Sayed and whether that move moves the needle in Michigan.
