# Lopez Qualifies for Governor's Race as Unaffiliated—Colorado's Third-Party Gamble Heats Up

Greg Lopez, the former Republican congressman turned perennial candidate, has officially qualified for Colorado's November gubernatorial ballot as an unaffiliated candidate—his third consecutive run for the office. The move fractures the anti-Democrat vote in a state where Republicans are already fighting uphill against Democratic registration advantages.

Lopez's unaffiliated status reflects broader frustration among Colorado conservatives with the GOP establishment, but it also raises hard questions about spoiler dynamics. In a tight race, splitting the center-right vote could hand the governorship to Democrat Phil Weiser or another progressive candidate—exactly what happened in 2022 when Jared Polis cruised to reelection while Republicans remained divided.

Meanwhile, the Democratic primary field includes socialist candidate Francesca Hong, whose campaign has already faced scrutiny over false claims about her policy positions (Daily Caller). Hong's presence signals how far left Colorado Democrats have drifted on issues like energy policy, gun control, and government spending—positions that will matter enormously for Colorado's energy workers, gun owners, and taxpayers.

Republicans and conservatives should watch whether Lopez's candidacy becomes a liability or a genuine alternative. Either way, Colorado voters deserve clarity: will the center-right coalesce behind one candidate, or will November's ballot become a three-way free-for-all that benefits the left?
