# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Thursday, September 24, 2026

Colorado's courts, its hospitals, and Washington's press rooms are all flashpoints today — and in every case, the fight is over who holds power and who gets to say so. Here's what you need to know.

---

## Colorado Springs in the Crosshairs Over April Elections

Colorado Springs is facing a lawsuit over its practice of holding municipal elections in April — a lower-turnout window that critics argue dilutes voter participation. Three outlets are tracking the case, and conservatives should watch it closely: election timing and structure matter enormously for local governance, and any court-ordered changes to how Colorado's second-largest city runs its elections could set a precedent that reaches every municipality in the state. Republicans argue election integrity means more than just ballot security — it means transparent, accountable processes that voters actually trust.

---

## Proposition 137: The Left Wants to Lock In Activist Judges

A guest editorial is making the rounds arguing against Colorado's Proposition 137, which would give voters more say over retaining state Supreme Court and Court of Appeals judges. Opponents of the measure — leaning left — want to keep judicial retention insulated from the public. Conservatives counter that unelected judges wielding unchecked power is exactly the kind of government overreach the Founders warned against. Colorado taxpayers and gun owners who've watched activist courts rewrite policy from the bench have every reason to support giving voters a stronger voice.

---

## Trump Wins Round One in White House Press Access Fight

A federal judge declined Wednesday to immediately restore access for outlets banned from White House press pools, siding — at least temporarily — with the Trump administration's argument that press access is a privilege, not a constitutional right. The ruling is a meaningful check on the media's sense of entitlement to government access. Conservatives say the press corps has operated for decades as an unelected fourth branch of government, and the administration is right to assert that proximity to the president is earned, not guaranteed.

---

## Denver's Children's Hospital Cleared in Gender Care Case

A Denver judge ruled that Children's Hospital Colorado is not in contempt of court after a prior ruling prohibited it from refusing to provide gender-affirming care to transgender youth — meaning the hospital complied by continuing to offer those procedures. Republicans and parental-rights advocates argue that Colorado's courts and medical institutions have become enforcers of a progressive gender ideology agenda, and that parents — not hospitals or judges — should have final authority over their children's medical decisions. This case is far from over.

---

## Former IRS Employee in Denver Charged With Tax Fraud for Drug Dealers

A former IRS employee and Denver tax preparer is facing a dozen charges including money laundering and filing false tax returns — allegedly on behalf of drug dealers. The case is a reminder that the agency entrusted with collecting taxes from every hardworking Colorado family is not immune to corruption from within. Conservatives who have long warned about expanding IRS power and headcount will note the irony: the same bureaucracy Democrats wanted to supersize is the one producing defendants like this.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Colorado Springs election lawsuit — a ruling there could reshape how dozens of Colorado cities structure their elections, with major implications for conservative turnout and local control. On Proposition 137, expect the left to pour money into defeating it; the fight over who controls Colorado's judiciary is one of the most consequential battles on the fall ballot. And in Washington, the press access ruling is only round one — expect an appeal, and expect the media to make it a cause célèbre heading into the weekend.
