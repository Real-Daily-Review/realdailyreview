# Trump Signals North Korea Engagement as Beijing and Moscow Tighten Grip on Pyongyang

The Trump administration is moving toward direct talks with Kim Jong-un even as North Korea remains a firm proxy of China and Russia—a strategic miscalculation that could undermine American interests in the Pacific and embolden adversaries without securing meaningful concessions.

According to *Just The News*, Trump has signaled willingness to meet with Kim, following administration decisions to downscale military exercises with South Korea. The president attributed the pullback partly to Seoul's reluctance to support U.S. operations in Iran—a troubling conflation of separate strategic theaters.

*National Review* warns bluntly: Kim Jong-un is not America's friend. North Korea remains "firmly within the China and Russia axis," meaning any bilateral talks risk legitimizing a regime while strengthening Beijing and Moscow's regional position without tangible American gains.

The *Washington Examiner* notes South Korea has renewed peace overtures, but North Korea's rejection of dialogue suggests Kim fears genuine reconciliation would threaten regime control—not that engagement will unlock cooperation.

**The conservative concern**: Direct talks without preconditions risk rewarding bad behavior, weakening the U.S.-South Korea alliance, and handing propaganda victories to authoritarian powers. Trump should demand verifiable denuclearization and human-rights improvements before any summit.

Watch whether the administration secures concrete North Korean commitments or simply grants diplomatic legitimacy in exchange for vague promises.
