# One Year After Charlie Kirk's Assassination, Turning Point USA Surges—and Conservatives Vow to Continue His Fight

A year after conservative activist Charlie Kirk was assassinated on September 10, 2025, his organization Turning Point USA has expanded dramatically, signaling that the left's attempt to silence a rising voice has backfired. The group now operates roughly 1,500 college chapters—a 64% increase—along with nearly 3,500 high-school clubs, over 1,200 staff members, and more than a million students engaged in conservative activism.

Kirk's killing exposed what conservatives argue is the radical left's willingness to resort to political violence when losing the argument in the public square. President Trump honored Kirk at the Republican Midterm Convention, declaring "We Will Not Let Them Win" and framing the assassination as evidence of progressive extremism (The Daily Wire).

The Washington Examiner's analysis captured the conservative view: Kirk, despite lacking a college degree or institutional authority, became one of his generation's most prominent pro-American voices precisely because he engaged relentlessly in persuasion and campus activism—a model National Review urged young conservatives to emulate.

Turning Point USA's growth under new leadership demonstrates that Kirk's legacy and mission endure. Conservatives should watch whether this momentum translates into sustained voter mobilization and whether Colorado's college campuses—particularly CU Boulder and CSU—see similar expansion of conservative student organizing.
