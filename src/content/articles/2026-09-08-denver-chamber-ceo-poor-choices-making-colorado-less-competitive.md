# Colorado Business Leader Warns: 'Poor Choices' Driving Out Talent and Investment

Denver Metro Chamber CEO J.J. Ament is sounding the alarm: Colorado's business environment is deteriorating, and the state's competitive edge is slipping away. Speaking after the Chamber's annual meeting on *Colorado Matters* (CPR News), Ament pointed to "poor choices" that have made the state less desirable for companies and workers alike—a blunt assessment from one of the region's most influential business voices.

The warning reflects growing frustration among Colorado's private sector over regulatory overreach, tax policy, and downtown deterioration. Downtown Denver's office vacancy crisis and talent flight aren't accidents; they're consequences of progressive policies that have made Colorado less business-friendly than neighboring states. When companies can relocate to Texas, Arizona, or Utah and find lower taxes, lighter regulation, and safer streets, Colorado loses.

Ament's critique aligns with conservative arguments that Colorado's Democrat-controlled legislature and progressive local governments have prioritized ideology over economic competitiveness. Higher taxes, aggressive environmental mandates, and permitting delays drive businesses elsewhere. Meanwhile, downtown cores languish as remote work and urban crime accelerate the exodus.

The state's energy sector—historically a Colorado economic engine—faces particular headwinds from anti-fossil-fuel policies that threaten thousands of jobs.
