# Colorado's One-Party Rule Faces Internal Cracks as Moderates Push Back Against Progressive Wing

Colorado's Democratic supermajority is fracturing—and it's exposing the limits of the progressive movement's grip on state politics.

Recent primary losses by Democratic Socialist candidates suggest moderate Democrats are reasserting control, signaling that far-left ideology may alienate swing voters and rural Coloradans beyond urban strongholds (Just The News). The defeats indicate growing tension within the party as moderates attempt to reclaim messaging and direction ahead of general elections.

**The conservative angle:** Colorado Republicans have long argued that one-party Democratic rule has enabled unchecked spending, regulatory overreach, and policies hostile to energy development, gun rights, and property owners. With Democrats now battling themselves, conservatives see an opening—but only if they can capitalize on voter frustration with progressive excess.

**What's at stake:** Complete Colorado notes that vast swaths of the state lack meaningful political competition or accountability for those "who actually pull the levers of power." Without a viable Republican alternative, even moderate Democratic victories leave conservative Coloradans without genuine representation on taxes, energy policy, and land use.

The real test: Can Colorado Republicans build a credible statewide message before moderates stabilize Democratic control, or will one-party rule simply shift from far-left to center-left?
