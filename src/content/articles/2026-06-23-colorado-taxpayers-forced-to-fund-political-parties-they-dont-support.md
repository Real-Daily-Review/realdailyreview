# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado taxpayers are bankrolling primary elections for political parties whether they belong to them or not—a wasteful government entanglement that conservatives say has no place in a free state.

Complete Colorado argues the practice amounts to unconstitutional "party privilege," forcing citizens to subsidize Democratic and Republican primaries regardless of their own affiliation or beliefs. The outlet frames ending this arrangement as a "courageous" move toward separating state from party machinery.

The core conservative complaint is straightforward: Why should an independent voter, Libertarian, or anyone outside the two-party duopoly pay taxes to run primaries for organizations they've rejected? It's government picking winners, using public dollars to entrench the political establishment.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives contend Colorado should either require parties to fund their own elections or shift to a truly neutral, taxpayer-neutral primary system. The current setup treats major parties as quasi-governmental entities deserving public subsidy—precisely the kind of crony capitalism conservatives oppose.

Democrats haven't articulated a public counter-argument in these sources, though they likely defend the current system as necessary infrastructure for orderly elections.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado legislators introduce bills to eliminate party-funded primary subsidies and how the state's major parties respond to losing public money.
