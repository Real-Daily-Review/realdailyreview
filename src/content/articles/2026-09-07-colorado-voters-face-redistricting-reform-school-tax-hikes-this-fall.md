# Colorado Voters Face Redistricting Reform, School Tax Hikes, and Election Security Questions This Fall

Colorado voters will decide whether to handcuff partisan gerrymandering with Initiative #256, which would ban congressional redistricting designed to favor either political party—a direct challenge to the map-drawing power Democrats have wielded since 2021 (Complete Colorado). Meanwhile, school districts across the state are asking voters to approve millions in mill levy overrides, forcing taxpayers to choose between crowded ballots and tax increases during a cost-of-living crisis (Denver Post).

The redistricting push reflects conservative frustration with Democratic gerrymandering. Republicans argue that Initiative #256 would restore fairness to Colorado's congressional maps after Democrats redrew them to entrench their advantage. Supporters say the measure creates genuine guardrails against partisan abuse.

On the education front, school districts face an uphill battle. Taxpayers are already stretched thin, and asking for more money amid inflation and stagnant wages feels tone-deaf to many Colorado families. The Denver Post notes districts must "make their case for tax hikes during a cost-of-living crisis"—a tall order when government spending, not revenue shortfalls, is the real problem.

Adding to election concerns, the Colorado Secretary of State's Office accidentally exposed confidential voter information for over 130,000 residents after a website update error (Denver Post), raising questions about election administration competence.
