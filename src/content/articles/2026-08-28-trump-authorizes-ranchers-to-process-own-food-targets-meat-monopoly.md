# Trump Moves to Break Meat-Processing Monopoly for Western Ranchers

Trump is taking direct aim at the stranglehold four mega-corporations hold over America's ranchers and farmers. On Friday, President Trump announced he would authorize ranchers and farmers to process their own food, directly challenging what he called a "nasty monopoly" controlled by Tyson, Cargill, JBS USA, and National Beef Packing Co. (The Daily Wire, Just The News).

For Colorado's ranching community—a cornerstone of the state's rural economy and way of life—this move could prove significant. The big four processors have long dictated terms to independent producers, squeezing margins and limiting options. Trump's directive to break that stranglehold aligns with conservative principles: reduce corporate consolidation, restore market competition, and empower independent producers over entrenched monopolies.

The president framed the action squarely: "Ranchers and Farmers have always been a number one priority for me. They work very hard" (The Daily Wire). By allowing on-farm processing, producers gain leverage to negotiate better prices and retain more profit—a direct blow to corporate gatekeepers.

No significant Democratic pushback has emerged yet, though the left typically favors centralized food-safety oversight that smaller operations struggle to meet.

Watch whether Colorado's agricultural leaders embrace this deregulatory move and how quickly the big processors respond to defend their market position.
