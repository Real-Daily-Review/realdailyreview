# Senate Republicans Demand Answers on Verizon's Secret Subpoena Handling

Sen. Chuck Grassley and 15 Republican senators are pressing Verizon to explain why the telecom giant failed to disclose that Special Counsel Jack Smith's team had secretly subpoenaed congressional phone records under the code name "Arctic Frost."

According to Fox News and Just The News, Grassley's letter follows his 2023 request asking Verizon to identify all Justice Department and federal law enforcement requests for Senate devices dating back to January 2016—the period covering the FBI's Crossfire Hurricane investigation into the Trump campaign. Verizon apparently complied with Smith's subpoena without alerting Congress, raising serious questions about whether the special counsel's office was conducting surveillance of lawmakers without their knowledge.

Republicans argue this represents a troubling overreach of prosecutorial power and a potential breach of congressional privilege. The silence from Verizon—a major telecom contractor—suggests either inadequate internal controls or a willingness to cooperate with federal investigators without proper transparency safeguards.

The incident underscores conservative concerns about unchecked federal law enforcement authority and the weaponization of government agencies against political opponents. Colorado voters should note how this pattern of secrecy erodes trust in institutions meant to serve the public, not target it.

Watch whether Verizon's response reveals additional undisclosed subpoenas or whether Democrats in Congress demand accountability from Smith's office.
