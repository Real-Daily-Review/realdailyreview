# Trump Endorsement Can't Save Scandal-Plagued Florida GOP Rep. Mills

Even a Trump endorsement wasn't enough to save Rep. Cory Mills in Florida's 7th Congressional District primary, where challenger Ryan Elijah pulled off an upset victory (Fox News, Daily Caller).

Mills, who had secured former President Trump's backing, lost his reelection bid amid ongoing investigations into his finances and allegations of sexual misconduct. The result signals that Republican primary voters—even Trump-friendly ones—won't automatically rubber-stamp candidates facing serious ethical clouds.

For Colorado Republicans watching national GOP dynamics, the lesson is straightforward: endorsements matter, but they don't override voter concerns about a candidate's personal conduct and legal exposure. Colorado's own primary races have shown similar patterns, where grassroots conservatives demand accountability from their representatives regardless of party establishment backing.

The Mills loss also underscores a broader reality: the GOP base is willing to move on from damaged goods, even when national figures intervene. That's healthy for party discipline and candidate quality—but it means Colorado Republicans eyeing higher office need clean records and solid constituent service, not just proximity to national figures.

Watch whether Elijah's victory emboldens primary challengers to scandal-plagued incumbents in other Republican strongholds heading into 2026.
