# Trump Opts for Old Air Force One Over New $400M Aircraft—Here's Why

President Trump is flying the aging Air Force One back to Washington instead of the newer presidential jet, citing a practical reason: he wants U.S. troops stationed abroad to tour the new aircraft at a NATO base in the United Kingdom (Fox News Politics).

The decision to bypass the newer plane—which cost taxpayers $400 million—has raised eyebrows. Trump announced the switch after the NATO summit in Turkey, saying the newer aircraft would head to Mildenhall Air Force Base so service members could see the upgraded ride (Washington Examiner).

The move underscores a recurring tension in Trump's messaging: he campaigned on fiscal restraint and cutting wasteful government spending, yet presides over massive defense expenditures. While the new Air Force One represents a genuine modernization of aging Cold War-era aircraft, the $400 million price tag remains a lightning rod for conservatives skeptical of Pentagon budget bloat.

Trump's explanation—prioritizing troop morale over presidential convenience—aligns with his populist brand. But it also highlights questions about whether such lavish upgrades represent necessary defense investment or the kind of government excess that fuels taxpayer frustration.

Coloradans should watch whether Trump's second term actually delivers on promises to audit and trim federal waste, or whether defense spending continues its upward trajectory.
