# Iran Peace Deal Collapses as Hezbollah Defies U.S.-Brokered Agreement Within Days

The Biden administration's much-touted memorandum of understanding with Iran is already unraveling—Iran's delegation didn't even show up to sign the deal in Switzerland on Friday, citing Israel's military response to Hezbollah attacks as cover for backing out (Breitbart).

The collapse came swiftly. Just two days after the U.S. and Iran signed the initial agreement, Hezbollah—the Iranian-backed terrorist proxy—killed four Israeli soldiers, including a battalion commander, in a strike on an Israeli tank in southern Lebanon (The Daily Wire). Israel responded with intense strikes against Hezbollah positions, prompting Tehran to use the fighting as an excuse to abandon the negotiating table.

Republicans have long warned that Iran cannot be trusted to honor agreements. This debacle vindicates those concerns. The U.N. had noted a temporary decline in violence after the MOU was announced, but that ceasefire lasted barely 48 hours—revealing the deal's fundamental weakness and Iran's bad faith (Washington Examiner).

For Colorado, this matters: a destabilized Middle East drives energy prices higher, hitting working families at the pump and raising heating costs this winter. Coloradans who depend on affordable energy should expect their utility bills to reflect this geopolitical failure.

Watch whether the Biden team attempts to resurrect talks or finally acknowledges that appeasing Iran doesn't work.
