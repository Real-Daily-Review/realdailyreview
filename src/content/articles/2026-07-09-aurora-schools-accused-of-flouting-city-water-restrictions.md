# Aurora Schools Face Water Violation Accusation as Mayor Cracks Down on Drought Rules

Aurora's school district is under fire for allegedly flouting the city's water restrictions—a reminder that government agencies often exempt themselves from rules ordinary Coloradans must follow.

Mayor Mike Coffman accused the district of maintaining suspiciously lush lawns in violation of current watering limits, posting concerns on Facebook that some school grounds appear "too green" to comply with drought protocols (KDVR). The accusation underscores a persistent frustration among Colorado taxpayers: while residents face strict outdoor watering bans and fines, public institutions operate under different standards.

Water scarcity is no joke in Colorado. The state's chronic drought has forced municipalities to impose increasingly aggressive conservation measures. When school districts—funded by local tax dollars—appear to skirt those same restrictions, it breeds justified skepticism about government accountability and equal application of the law.

The district has not yet publicly responded to the mayor's allegations. Conservatives argue that if water restrictions are necessary for the public good, they must apply uniformly—no exceptions for government entities. Selective enforcement erodes public trust and suggests bureaucratic privilege trumps shared sacrifice.

Watch whether Aurora's city council pursues formal enforcement action or whether the district receives a quiet pass.
