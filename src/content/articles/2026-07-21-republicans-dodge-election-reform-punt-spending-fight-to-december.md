# Republicans Punt on Election Reform, Spending Cuts Ahead of Midterms

House Republicans are kicking the can down the road again. Rather than use their leverage to pass the SAVE America Act—legislation designed to strengthen election integrity—GOP leaders are instead rushing a short-term continuing resolution through December to avoid a government shutdown before the midterm elections, according to the Washington Examiner.

The move reveals a familiar pattern: Republicans control the House but lack the political spine to force real spending restraint or advance core conservative priorities when it matters. The election integrity movement has spent months pushing the SAVE Act as a must-pass priority, even persuading President Trump to withhold his signature from other major bills until Congress acted. Yet House leadership is choosing the path of least resistance instead.

Conservatives argue that a pre-election shutdown would be politically damaging—fair enough. But so is caving to the status quo. By punting spending fights to December, Republicans are essentially guaranteeing another chaotic funding battle after voters have already cast ballots, when leverage evaporates and Democrats regain negotiating power.

Colorado taxpayers and election-integrity advocates should watch whether Republicans use the December deadline to actually fight for the SAVE Act or whether they'll simply extend the continuing resolution into 2023, abandoning election reform altogether.
