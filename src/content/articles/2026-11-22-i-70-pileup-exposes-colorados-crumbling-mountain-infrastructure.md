# I-70 Reopens After Multi-Vehicle Pileup in Winter Weather

Colorado's critical mountain corridor shut down again Wednesday as winter weather turned I-70 into a parking lot—the latest reminder that our state's infrastructure remains vulnerable to seasonal chaos.

Colorado State Patrol reported a 12-car pileup near Floyd Hill, with multiple spin-offs and slide-outs scattered across the westbound lanes at Vail Pass (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31 Denver). The highway eventually reopened, but the incident underscores a persistent problem: I-70 remains a bottleneck that gridlocks during every significant snowstorm, stranding commuters, truckers, and tourists for hours.

Conservatives have long argued that Colorado needs smarter transportation solutions—not endless environmental reviews and progressive "transit-first" policies that ignore the reality of mountain driving. The state's fixation on rail projects and bike lanes does nothing for families stuck on I-70 in a whiteout. Meanwhile, CDOT's budget priorities often favor urban sprawl mitigation over the basic maintenance and capacity improvements that would actually move traffic safely.

Taxpayers foot the bill for closures, emergency response, and accident cleanup—costs that balloon when infrastructure planning prioritizes ideology over engineering.

Watch whether CDOT proposes real solutions (additional lanes, better snow removal) or retreats into the same regulatory delays that have stalled I-70 improvements for a decade.
