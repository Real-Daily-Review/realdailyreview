# Trump's Tariff Gamble Risks Colorado Economy

President Trump's "Liberation Day" tariffs are drawing fire from the right, with conservatives warning that protectionist policies could backfire on Colorado businesses and consumers already facing inflation headwinds.

The Washington Examiner reports that Trump's tariff announcement in April 2025 was "chaotic" with "absurd" justifications, and notes his approval ratings began sliding following the rollout. The criticism stings because it comes from Trump's own ideological flank—conservatives who typically back the president but see tariffs as economically counterproductive.

Colorado's economy depends on free trade. The state's agriculture sector, tech companies, and manufacturers rely on open markets and predictable trade rules. Tariffs raise input costs for Colorado businesses, increase consumer prices at the pump and grocery store, and invite retaliatory tariffs that could hammer Colorado exporters.

National Review suggests Trump should follow Reagan's Cold War playbook on nuclear policy—implying a more disciplined, strategic approach to major economic and foreign policy moves rather than chaotic announcements.

Republicans and conservatives are split: some back Trump's nationalist instincts, while others argue tariffs contradict free-market principles and will ultimately harm working Coloradans through higher prices and job losses.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's business community and GOP lawmakers push back publicly on tariff expansion.
