# FAA Halts Router Rollout After Bungled Ground Stop Strands Thousands

The Federal Aviation Administration has paused installation of the faulty routers that triggered last week's nationwide aviation shutdown, grounding flights and leaving travelers—including Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's daughter and newborn—stranded across the country (9News Denver).

Duffy acknowledged the disruption hit his family personally but defended the decision to halt all flights, saying he'd make the same call again prioritizing safety over convenience (The Daily Wire). The move underscores a critical question for Colorado travelers and businesses: how did the FAA's infrastructure fail so spectacularly, and what's the real cost of government mismanagement?

The ground stop exposed vulnerabilities in America's air traffic control system at precisely the moment when Colorado's economy—dependent on tourism, business travel, and cargo operations—can least afford disruption. Denver International Airport, a major hub, felt the ripple effects acutely.

Republicans and conservatives have seized on the incident as evidence of federal bureaucratic incompetence. The FAA's inability to properly test and deploy critical infrastructure before rollout raises hard questions about government oversight and the agencies tasked with protecting public safety.

Coloradans should watch whether the FAA implements genuine accountability measures or simply delays the problem while bureaucrats shuffle responsibility.
