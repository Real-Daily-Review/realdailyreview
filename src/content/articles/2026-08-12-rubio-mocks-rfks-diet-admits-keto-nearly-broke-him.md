# Rubio's Diet Confession: Secretary of State Admits Keto Left Him Irritable, Unfit for Office

Secretary of State Marco Rubio isn't buying into trendy diets—and he's not shy about saying so. During a Tuesday podcast appearance with his wife on "The Katie Miller Podcast," Rubio dismissed Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s sauerkraut-heavy eating regimen as "ridiculous" and revealed his own dietary experiment gone wrong (The Daily Wire).

Rubio's keto adventure from roughly five years ago was no success story. The diet left him so irritable he joked he would have been "ready to declare war" had he held his current position at the time, calling the experience a "near psychotic event" (Washington Examiner). The takeaway: Rubio prefers Pop-Tarts to ketones.

While the confession has gone viral for its humor value, it underscores a broader skepticism among some conservatives toward the wellness-obsessed messaging that often accompanies government health initiatives. Kennedy's "Make America Healthy Again" platform, despite its appeal to some on the right, clearly doesn't resonate with everyone in the Trump administration—even if the dietary philosophy behind it sounds reasonable in theory.

The real question for Colorado conservatives: as MAHA gains traction in federal health policy, will Washington push one-size-fits-all dietary guidance on states, or will individual choice remain paramount?
