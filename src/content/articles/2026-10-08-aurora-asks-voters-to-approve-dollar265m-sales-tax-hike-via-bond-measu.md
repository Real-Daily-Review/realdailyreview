# Aurora Pushes $265M Tax Hike on Voters—Dressed as "Bond Measures"

Aurora's city council is asking voters to approve three ballot measures that would hike the sales tax to fund $265 million in municipal projects, according to the Denver Post. Measures 3A, 3B, and 3C would authorize bond sales backed by higher sales taxes on Aurora residents and businesses.

The pitch: infrastructure, public amenities, and city services. The reality: another tax burden on households already squeezed by inflation and rising costs of living. Aurora's approach mirrors the progressive playbook—wrap spending in bond language and let voters decide whether to pay more at the register.

Conservatives argue that cities should prioritize existing revenue streams and cut wasteful spending before asking taxpayers for more. Every sales tax hike hits working families hardest, since they spend a larger share of income on taxable goods. Aurora's three-measure package gives voters little granular control—it's an all-or-nothing proposition on a bloated wish list.

The measures face a November vote. If approved, Aurora residents will see their sales tax climb, making the metro area less competitive for businesses and families considering relocation.

Watch whether Colorado Springs, Boulder, or other Front Range cities follow Aurora's lead with their own tax-hike ballot measures this cycle.
