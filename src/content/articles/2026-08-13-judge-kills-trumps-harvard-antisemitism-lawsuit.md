# Federal Judge Dismisses Trump's Harvard Antisemitism Lawsuit

A federal judge has thrown out the Trump administration's civil rights case against Harvard University over alleged antisemitism on campus, dealing a setback to the DOJ's effort to hold elite universities accountable for discrimination complaints.

The judge ruled that the Trump administration failed to prove an ongoing violation of federal civil rights laws (9News Denver), effectively ending the legal challenge before trial. The lawsuit had targeted Harvard's handling of antisemitic incidents and alleged hostile environment for Jewish students—a concern that gained urgency following October 2023 campus protests.

**The conservative argument:** Republicans and Trump officials contended that Ivy League schools have systematically ignored antisemitism while aggressively policing other forms of bias, reflecting ideological double standards in higher education. The DOJ action represented an attempt to enforce Title VI protections equally across all protected classes.

**The counterargument:** Harvard and its defenders argued the university has robust policies addressing all forms of discrimination and that the lawsuit mischaracterized campus dynamics.

The dismissal underscores the legal hurdles facing federal enforcement actions against universities—even when backed by executive authority. While Colorado has no direct stake in Harvard's policies, the ruling signals how courts may treat future DOJ challenges to progressive institutional practices, including those at CU Boulder and other state schools.

Conservatives should monitor whether the Trump administration appeals or pursues similar cases at other universities with documented antisemitism problems.
