# AARP's Credibility Crisis: The Organization Cashing In on Seniors While Pushing Progressive Agendas

AARP claims to speak for America's retirees, but the organization has become a cash machine that profits from the very seniors it purports to represent—while lobbying for policies that hurt affordability.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, AARP generates substantial revenue through membership dues and insurance partnerships, particularly with UnitedHealth Group, creating a fundamental conflict of interest. Rather than prioritizing senior affordability during years of persistent inflation, AARP has aligned itself with progressive policy positions that often contradict the financial interests of its own members.

Conservatives argue AARP's business model—charging seniors for membership while profiting from health insurance commissions—undermines its credibility as an independent advocate. The organization's lobbying efforts frequently support government expansion and higher taxes, positions that strain fixed-income retirees already squeezed by inflation and rising healthcare costs.

For Colorado seniors on fixed incomes, this matters directly. AARP's influence on state and federal policy affects Medicare, prescription drug pricing, and tax policy that impacts retirement security. Yet the organization's revenue streams suggest it answers more to corporate partners and progressive donors than to the retirees paying membership dues.

Watch whether Colorado lawmakers and conservative groups begin challenging AARP's legislative positions and demanding transparency on how the organization spends senior dollars.
