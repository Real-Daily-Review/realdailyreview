# Colorado Democratic Socialists Face Backlash as Radical Left Stumbles Nationally

Colorado's Democratic Socialist Alliance is doubling down on an ideology that voters are beginning to reject—even in blue states. While national Democratic Socialists suffered a stunning primary upset in Wisconsin, where radical candidate Francesca Hong lost to moderate David Crowley (National Review), Colorado's DSA contingent continues channeling what critics describe as Maoist dogma, according to Complete Colorado.

Republicans and conservatives argue the DSA's "vengeful" narrative—echoing Bolshevik rhetoric from the Russian Revolution—represents the dangerous fringe of Colorado's Democratic Party. The movement's push for wealth redistribution, expanded government control, and anti-market policies threatens Colorado's business climate, energy sector, and property rights.

The Wisconsin loss signals voters are tiring of far-left experiments. Yet Colorado's DSA remains embedded in state Democratic leadership, influencing policy on taxes, regulation, and land use that directly impact Colorado ranchers, energy workers, and small-business owners.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado Democrats distance themselves from DSA-backed candidates in 2026—or double down and risk the same electoral backlash Wisconsin just delivered.
