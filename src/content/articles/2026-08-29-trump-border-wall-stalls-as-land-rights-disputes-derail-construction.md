# Trump Border Wall Construction Stalls Amid Land-Use Clashes

The Trump administration's border wall push is hitting unexpected friction—not from Democrats, but from property owners and Native American tribes claiming sacred land rights. While construction kicked off in Arizona, a federal pause in Big Bend National Park (Texas) has now been extended through September 15, signaling that even conservative priorities face real legal and logistical hurdles when they collide with private property and tribal sovereignty.

The Tohono O'odham Nation alleges that masked CBP agents blocked tribal police as contractors drilled on what the tribe considers sacred ground (Fox News). Meanwhile, the Big Bend halt—originally imposed by CBP Commissioner Rodney Scott—persists after bipartisan controversy erupted over construction in a national park (Washington Examiner).

**The conservative tension here is real:** Republicans support border security and wall construction as essential to immigration enforcement and national defense. But conservatives also champion property rights, limited federal overreach, and respect for lawful tribal sovereignty. When federal agencies steamroll landowners—tribal or otherwise—to build infrastructure, that contradicts core conservative principles.

For Colorado, this matters: Western water rights, energy development, and land-use disputes often pit federal agencies against state interests and private landowners. If the Trump administration can't navigate these conflicts smoothly on the border wall, Colorado should watch how federal agencies handle similar land disputes in the West.
