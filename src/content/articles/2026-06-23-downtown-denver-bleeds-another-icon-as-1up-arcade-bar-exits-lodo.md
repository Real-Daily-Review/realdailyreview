# Denver's Downtown Slump: Another Iconic Venue Exits LoDo After 15 Years

Colorado's downtown Denver core continues to hemorrhage businesses as 1Up Arcade Bar—a pioneering venue that opened 15 years ago—shuttered its original Lower Downtown location this week (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The closure marks another casualty in Denver's struggling urban center, where foot traffic, tourism, and consumer confidence have cratered since the pandemic and progressive policies reshaped the city's character.

1Up plans to relocate to a larger space in Lakewood's Belmar development, signaling what many business owners already know: suburban locations now offer better economics than downtown Denver. The arcade bar joins Church and Union, which closed after less than two years in the Union Station neighborhood (KDVR Fox31), underscoring a broader trend of retail and hospitality flight.

Downtown Denver has been hammered by homelessness, crime concerns, remote work adoption, and heavy-handed regulations that have made operations costlier and less profitable. While city officials tout "revitalization" efforts, the market is voting with its feet—and wallets.

Conservatives point to this exodus as evidence that progressive governance and regulatory overreach drive away the very businesses and tax base that fund city services. As more venues relocate to the suburbs, watch whether Denver's city council acknowledges the cost of its policies or doubles down on the same approach.
