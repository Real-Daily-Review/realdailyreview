# Trump Secures Greenland Military Agreement—But at What Cost to U.S. Credibility?

President Trump is moving forward with a historic Arctic security deal that grants the U.S. permanent military rights in Greenland, set for signing at the U.N. with Denmark and Greenland officials. The agreement represents a strategic win in the Arctic—a region of growing geopolitical importance as China and Russia expand their influence in the far north.

But the path to this deal has left conservative observers divided. While Trump was right to prioritize Arctic security and U.S. interests in Greenland, *National Review* argues the monthslong public fight over potential U.S. control "damaged our reputation as a reliable ally." The aggressive pursuit of the deal, including earlier talk of purchasing the island outright, raised questions about diplomatic judgment.

For Colorado, the Arctic security angle matters: energy development, mineral access, and strategic positioning in the Arctic could affect U.S. energy independence and domestic resource availability—priorities conservatives support.

Republicans largely back the strategic outcome, though some worry the bruising negotiations may have strained the Denmark alliance unnecessarily. The left has criticized the entire approach as reckless.
