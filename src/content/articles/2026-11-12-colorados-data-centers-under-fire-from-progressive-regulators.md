# Colorado's Data Centers Power the Digital Economy—While Activists Push Back

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers are critical infrastructure powering the modern internet, yet they face mounting pressure from environmental activists and progressive regulators who view them as energy hogs, according to *Complete Colorado*. These facilities—which process everything from cloud computing to AI workloads—are economic engines for the state, generating jobs and tax revenue while supporting businesses nationwide.

Conservatives argue data centers represent responsible economic development and technological leadership. The facilities operate efficiently and attract high-wage employment to Colorado communities. However, the left increasingly targets data centers over energy consumption concerns, pushing for stricter environmental regulations that could drive this vital industry elsewhere.

Colorado taxpayers and businesses depend on reliable, affordable data infrastructure. If progressive policymakers impose costly regulations—whether through water-use restrictions, energy mandates, or zoning barriers—Colorado risks losing competitive advantage to states with friendlier business climates. Texas, Virginia, and other states are actively courting data center investment with tax incentives and streamlined permitting.

The stakes are clear: Colorado can either embrace data centers as engines of prosperity and innovation, or cede this high-value sector to competitors. Watch whether the state legislature attempts new regulations on data center operations in the 2027 session—and whether Colorado's business community pushes back.
