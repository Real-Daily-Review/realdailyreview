# Colorado Marks 50 Years of River Otter Reintroduction—But Questions Linger on Ecosystem Management

Colorado is celebrating a half-century since state wildlife officials reintroduced river otters to the state's waterways, with Governor Polis marking the milestone as a conservation win. The July 9 anniversary highlights what state officials call the animals' role in "strengthening the health of our rivers" (KDVR Fox31).

The reintroduction program, launched in 1974, represents one of Colorado's longest-running wildlife restoration efforts. Otters, once trapped to near-extinction in the state, have since established viable populations across multiple river systems.

**The conservative angle:** While wildlife restoration can reflect sound stewardship of Colorado's natural resources, taxpayers should ask hard questions about the long-term costs of such programs and whether regulatory restrictions tied to otter protection limit water access for agriculture, energy development, or municipal use. Environmental mandates often expand quietly once species are reestablished, constraining Colorado's economy and property rights.

State wildlife management decisions deserve scrutiny—particularly when they affect ranchers, farmers, and water users already squeezed by competing demands on Colorado's finite water supply. The otter story is heartwarming, but conservatives should watch whether Polis uses this anniversary to justify new environmental regulations that burden Colorado's working landscape.
