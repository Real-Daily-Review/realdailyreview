# Colorado's 150th: A Milestone Worth Remembering—and Learning From

Colorado's sesquicentennial arrives amid a state transformed by a century and a half of enterprise, resource development, and individual initiative—qualities now under pressure from progressive overreach.

The state marked its 150th birthday with celebrations at the Capitol, where leaders reflected on Colorado's past while charting its future (CPR News). The Colorado Sun highlighted 150 things that exemplify the state's character, from mining and ranching heritage to outdoor recreation and entrepreneurship.

But here's what conservatives should note: Colorado's prosperity was built on **limited government, private enterprise, and resource freedom**—not regulatory mandates. The state's mining boom, agricultural dominance, and energy sector created the wealth that built schools, towns, and opportunity. Today's progressive legislature has moved sharply away from that model, imposing strict environmental regulations, energy restrictions, and tax increases that threaten the very industries that made Colorado great.

As the state looks forward, the question isn't whether Colorado can celebrate its past—it's whether policymakers will protect the free-market principles and individual liberty that created it. Watch for whether the legislature uses this milestone moment to recommit to Colorado's entrepreneurial roots or doubles down on the regulatory agenda that's already driving businesses and workers to friendlier states.
