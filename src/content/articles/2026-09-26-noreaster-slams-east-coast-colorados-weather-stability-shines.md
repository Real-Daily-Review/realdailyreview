# Nor'easter Hammers East Coast; Colorado Spared Winter's Worst

A powerful nor'easter is battering the East Coast with destructive winds, heavy rain, and widespread flooding as tens of millions hunker down for the storm's peak impact. According to 9News Denver, the system is already lashing the region with strong winds and rain, with much worse conditions expected. Just The News reports that about 30,000 customers in New Jersey lost power as of Saturday morning, with outages likely to spread as the system intensifies.

The storm underscores why Colorado's geographic position remains an economic advantage: while Eastern states face recurring nor'easters, ice storms, and hurricanes that disrupt power grids and commerce, Colorado's Front Range enjoys a more stable climate for business and energy infrastructure. The state's diverse economy—agriculture, energy, manufacturing, and tech—doesn't face the same seasonal weather disruptions that plague coastal and Northeast regions.

For Colorado conservatives and taxpayers, this is a reminder of why energy independence and reliable power generation matter. Eastern states' reliance on aging grid infrastructure and renewable-only mandates leaves them vulnerable when major storms hit. Colorado's balanced energy portfolio—including natural gas and coal alongside renewables—provides resilience.

Watch whether federal disaster spending follows this nor'easter, and whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes back on unfunded mandates tied to climate-related relief packages.
