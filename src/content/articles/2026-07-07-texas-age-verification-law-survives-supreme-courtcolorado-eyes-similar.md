# Texas Age Verification Law Clears Supreme Court Hurdle—Colorado May Follow

The Supreme Court declined Monday to block Texas's age verification law for app store downloads, handing a win to parents and child safety advocates while the case proceeds through litigation. The decision allows the law to take effect, potentially creating a model for other states—including Colorado—to restrict minors' access to social media and adult content.

Tech industry groups challenged the law, arguing it violates free speech and creates privacy concerns. But conservatives counter that protecting children from algorithmic addiction and explicit material is a legitimate government interest that outweighs corporate objections. The Washington Examiner notes the ruling could set important precedent for states seeking stronger online protections.

Colorado lawmakers have shown interest in similar measures. If Texas's law survives appellate review, expect pressure on the Colorado legislature to pass comparable age verification requirements—a move that would pit parental rights advocates against Big Tech's lobbying machine and progressive free-speech absolutists.

Conservatives argue government has a duty to protect minors from predatory platforms designed to maximize engagement regardless of harm. The tech industry counters that such laws are unworkable and infringe on adult rights.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans introduce age verification legislation in the 2025 session and whether Gov. Jared Polis signals support or resistance.
