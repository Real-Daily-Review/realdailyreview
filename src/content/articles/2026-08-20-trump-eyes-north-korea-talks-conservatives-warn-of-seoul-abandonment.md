# Trump Signals North Korea Talks as Allies Question Strategic Shift

The Trump administration is moving toward direct engagement with Kim Jong Un, but conservatives warn the diplomatic pivot risks abandoning South Korea and strengthening Beijing's grip on the peninsula.

According to *Just The News*, Trump has signaled willingness to meet with the North Korean dictator, following administration decisions to downscale joint military exercises with Seoul. Trump reportedly tied the pullback to South Korea's reluctance to support U.S. operations in Iran—a linkage that has alarmed regional security hawks.

*National Review* flatly states Kim Jong Un "is not our friend," noting North Korea remains "firmly within the China and Russia axis." The concern: any U.S.-North Korea rapprochement without ironclad security guarantees could isolate South Korea and embolden Beijing's regional ambitions.

The *Washington Examiner* argues that Kim Jong Un actually fears genuine peace with the South, since the regime's survival depends on perpetual tension and Chinese patronage. South Korea's current overtures—respecting the North's regime, renouncing unification, and pledging no hostile acts—may simply reward North Korean intransigence without yielding real denuclearization.

**For Colorado conservatives:** This matters. Weakening the U.S.-South Korea alliance destabilizes the Pacific and strengthens authoritarian competitors. Watch whether Trump's talks produce verifiable nuclear concessions or merely hand Beijing a freer hand in Asia.
