# Trump Admin Pushes Iran Deal Despite GOP Skepticism—Colorado Should Demand Answers on Energy Impact

Vice President JD Vance is defending the Trump administration's Iran nuclear agreement against Republican pushback, telling Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) not to "believe hard-liner propaganda" and instead trust the deal's terms (Washington Examiner). Vance disclosed that Israeli strikes on Beirut nearly torpedoed negotiations, with intelligence suggesting Iran would launch massive retaliation against Israel (Just The News).

The problem: Colorado's energy sector and national security interests deserve clarity. An Iran deal that lifts sanctions could flood global oil markets, undercutting domestic energy production and Colorado's oil and gas workers. Republicans argue the administration should be transparent about whether the agreement constrains Iran's nuclear weapons program or simply delays it—and what concessions were made to keep Tehran at the negotiating table.

Graham's skepticism reflects legitimate conservative concerns: Iran has a decades-long record of deception and sponsoring terrorism. Vance's dismissal of "hard-liner propaganda" sidesteps the real question: what verification mechanisms exist, and how does this serve American interests?

**Watch next:** Demand that Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Sen. John Hickenlooper—publicly state whether this deal protects U.S. energy independence and national security, or sacrifices both for a fragile agreement.
