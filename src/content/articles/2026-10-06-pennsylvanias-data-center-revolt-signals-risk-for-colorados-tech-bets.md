# Data Center Backlash Signals Warning for Colorado's Tech Economy

Pennsylvania's sudden turn against data centers—once hailed as economic saviors—should alarm Colorado policymakers betting big on AI infrastructure and server farms to diversify away from energy dependence.

According to the Washington Examiner, data centers that were celebrated as job creators just months ago have become "public enemy No. 1" in Pennsylvania, with local opposition mounting over land use, power consumption, and environmental concerns. The shift reflects a broader pattern: communities embrace development promises, then recoil when infrastructure arrives.

Colorado faces similar pressures. State and local officials have courted data center investment as a hedge against oil-and-gas volatility, but the Pennsylvania experience warns that public sentiment can flip fast. Conservatives argue that consistent, predictable permitting and property-rights protections—not reactive regulation—are what attract and retain such investment. When governments cave to NIMBY opposition or impose surprise environmental restrictions mid-project, businesses simply relocate to friendlier states.

The Colorado angle: if Boulderites, Front Range environmentalists, or local activists begin organizing against data centers the way Pennsylvania residents have, state leaders will face pressure to impose new restrictions that could kill deals already in negotiation.

Watch whether Colorado's legislature moves to preempt local data-center bans or imposes state-level environmental mandates that make projects uneconomical.
