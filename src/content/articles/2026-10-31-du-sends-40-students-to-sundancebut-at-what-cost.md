# Sundance Brings Film Classes to DU, But Questions Linger on Public University Priorities

Denver University is jumping into the Sundance Film Festival with both feet, offering a new class that will haul about 40 students to Utah for the annual event (9News). Meanwhile, the Sundance Institute itself is rolling out a Legacy program featuring classic films at the festival (KDVR).

The move reflects how Colorado's universities are positioning themselves around high-profile cultural events. But conservatives should ask a harder question: Is this the best use of DU's resources and tuition dollars?

Universities nationwide face pressure to justify bloated administrative costs and mission creep. When a private institution like DU dedicates faculty time and institutional resources to film festival field trips, it's worth examining whether that's core to education or luxury programming that drives up costs for students already burdened by debt.

The Sundance connection itself carries baggage. The festival has long been a launchpad for progressive Hollywood narratives and left-leaning filmmakers—hardly a neutral cultural venue. DU's investment in this particular ecosystem deserves scrutiny from parents and donors concerned about ideological capture in higher education.

The Sundance Legacy program and DU's festival class may produce worthwhile art. But Colorado taxpayers and families should watch whether universities continue expanding non-essential programming while tuition climbs and core academics suffer.
