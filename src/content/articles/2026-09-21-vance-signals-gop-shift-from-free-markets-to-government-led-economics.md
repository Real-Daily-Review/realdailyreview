# GOP's Hamiltonian Turn: Vance Signals Shift Away From Free-Market Conservatism

Vice President JD Vance is openly abandoning the free-market principles that once defined Republican economic policy, declaring that GOP doctrine is now "much more Alexander Hamilton than it is Milton Friedman"—and calling that shift "obviously a good thing," according to the *Washington Examiner*.

The distinction matters. Friedman's tradition treated markets as the default and government intervention as the exception requiring justification. Hamilton's framework treats a strong central government as the engine of economic policy, opening the door to industrial policy, targeted subsidies, and strategic state intervention.

**The conservative concern:** This represents a fundamental break from limited-government doctrine. Free-market conservatives argue that government-directed economic picking and choosing—even with patriotic intent—inevitably breeds cronyism, misallocates capital, and punishes taxpayers footing the bill. Colorado businesses and energy producers should note this shift: Hamiltonian policy often means federal mandates and subsidies favoring politically connected industries over competitive markets.

Republicans backing this turn argue it reflects pragmatism in competing with China and rebuilding domestic manufacturing. But traditionalists warn it abandons the principle that individuals and markets, not bureaucrats, should drive economic decisions.
