# Boulder Police Eye New DNA Tech in JonBenét Case—But Say It's Not Ready Yet

Thirty years after six-year-old JonBenét Ramsey was murdered in her Boulder home, investigators say emerging DNA technology could eventually crack Colorado's most infamous cold case—but they're pumping the brakes on deploying it now.

According to the Colorado Sun, Boulder police acknowledge that cutting-edge DNA techniques show promise for the investigation. However, KDVR reports that law enforcement in charge of the case says the technology isn't sufficiently developed or validated for deployment in an active murder investigation.

The hesitation reflects a legitimate concern: premature use of unproven forensic methods could contaminate evidence, create legal vulnerabilities, or lead investigators down false trails. Conservatives and law-and-order advocates generally support rigorous, science-based investigative standards—even when they slow progress on high-profile cases.

The Ramsey case has haunted Boulder for three decades, generating countless theories and consuming significant public resources. Taxpayers have a legitimate interest in seeing the case solved, but not at the cost of sloppy forensics that could compromise prosecution or let a killer walk free on a technicality.

Watch whether Boulder police establish clear benchmarks for when this DNA technology will be considered trial-ready—and whether they commit to deploying it once validated.
