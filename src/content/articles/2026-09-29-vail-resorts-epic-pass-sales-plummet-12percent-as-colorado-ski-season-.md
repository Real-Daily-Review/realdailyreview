# Vail Resorts Faces Sharp Pass Sales Collapse as Colorado Ski Season Uncertainty Looms

Colorado's tourism economy is taking a hit as Vail Resorts reports a stunning 12% drop in Epic Pass sales—the steepest decline in recent memory—following last winter's dismal snow conditions (9News Denver). The Broomfield-based resort giant is now making conservative forecasts for the coming season, hoping Mother Nature cooperates where she failed last year (CPR News).

The collapse matters for Colorado. Ski resorts drive billions in annual spending across the state, supporting lodging, restaurants, retail, and local tax bases in mountain communities. When pass sales crater, it signals weakness in consumer confidence and discretionary spending—a canary in the coal mine for Colorado's broader economy heading into 2027.

Vail's cautious outlook reflects real risk: one bad winter can devastate seasonal workers, small businesses dependent on ski traffic, and county revenues. The company is essentially betting conditions improve; if they don't, expect further contraction in mountain-town employment and tax collections.

The question for Colorado taxpayers: Will state and local governments use this downturn as an excuse to raise taxes on already-struggling tourism businesses, or will they cut spending to match lower revenues?
