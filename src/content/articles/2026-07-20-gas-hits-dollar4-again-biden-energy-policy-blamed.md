# Gas Prices Surge Past $4 as Biden Energy Policy Fails Colorado Drivers

National average gasoline prices have climbed back above $4 per gallon, hammering Colorado families and small businesses already squeezed by inflation. According to AAA data cited by 9News Denver, the national average hit $4.003 Monday, with regional variation stark: Indiana drivers paid as little as $3.35 while California motorists faced $5.50 per gallon (Just The News).

The price spike coincides with renewed Middle East tensions, but conservatives argue the root cause lies in Biden administration energy policy—not geopolitics alone. Restrictions on domestic oil production, delayed permitting for new wells, and the regulatory assault on fossil fuels have left America dependent on volatile global markets. Colorado's oil and gas sector, already battered by years of anti-energy regulation, faces renewed pressure as prices climb and investment dries up.

Democrats blame external factors beyond Washington's control. Republicans counter that energy independence—achievable through expanded domestic drilling and streamlined permitting—would insulate Americans from foreign shocks and lower prices at the pump.

For Colorado taxpayers and workers in energy-dependent communities, the message is clear: progressive climate ideology has real costs. Watch whether state regulators accelerate new restrictions on oil and gas development, further undermining Colorado's energy economy and tax base.
