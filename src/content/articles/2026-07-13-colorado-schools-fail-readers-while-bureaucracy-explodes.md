# Schools, Not Smartphones, Are Failing Colorado Readers

Colorado's reading crisis isn't a tech problem—it's a government school management failure. While education bureaucracies have ballooned over three decades, student reading proficiency has stalled, according to conservative critics examining the disconnect between spending and outcomes.

The Daily Wire argues that smartphones didn't kill reading; **schools did**. Over 30 years, most children have learned to hate reading under current educational models, the outlet reports. Republicans point to a stark reality: the education bureaucracy has exploded 702% while reading scores haven't budged, per the Washington Examiner's analysis of congressional testimony.

House GOP Conference Chairwoman Lisa McClain confronted school leadership directly, calling out administrative failures and misplaced priorities. The pattern is clear: Colorado taxpayers pour billions into K-12 education while administrators expand overhead instead of fixing fundamentals.

Conservatives argue the problem isn't devices or culture—it's that bloated school districts prioritize bureaucratic growth over literacy instruction. Colorado parents should demand accountability: where is the money going, and why aren't kids reading?

Watch for Colorado school board races this cycle and push candidates to commit to reading proficiency metrics tied directly to classroom instruction, not administrative expansion.
