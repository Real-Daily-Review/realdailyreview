# Michigan Democrat's GOP Endorsement Exposes Left's Radical Drift

A sitting Democratic congresswoman just endorsed a Republican for Senate—and her own party erupted in fury, proving conservatives' point about progressive extremism.

Rep. Haley Stevens (D-MI) backed former Rep. Mike Rogers (R-MI) over Democrat Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan's U.S. Senate race, triggering backlash from Chuck Schumer, Hakeem Jeffries, and radical left-wing voices (Fox News). The timing was explosive: Stevens told fellow Democrat Debbie Dingell at midnight that the endorsement rumor was false, then stood beside Rogers at a Detroit church five hours later.

Stevens defended the switch to Anderson Cooper, saying El-Sayed has been "getting angrier and angrier" recently (The Daily Wire). Even Dingell, a fellow Michigan Democrat, called the defense "painful to watch" and "sad" (Breitbart).

**The conservative takeaway:** When a Democrat would rather back a Republican than her own party's nominee, it signals the left has abandoned the center. El-Sayed's radicalism—apparently too extreme even for a sitting Democratic congresswoman—reflects the progressive movement's lurch toward the fringe. President Trump invited Stevens to join the GOP, calling her "very brave" (Breitbart).

Colorado conservatives should watch whether this Michigan moment signals a broader realignment, as moderate Democrats distance themselves from the party's activist base.
