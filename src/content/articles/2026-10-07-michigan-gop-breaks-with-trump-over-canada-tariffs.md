# Michigan Republican Breaks With Trump Over Tariffs—Colorado Should Take Note

Michigan Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers is publicly distancing himself from President Trump's tariff agenda, arguing that trade barriers with Canada harm his state's economy. In a new campaign ad (Washington Examiner), Rogers declares "Canada is not our enemy" and calls for an end to the U.S.-Canada trade war, pointedly avoiding Trump's name while signaling disagreement on one of the president's signature economic policies.

**The conservative split:** Republicans backing Rogers argue that blanket tariffs, even on allies, risk retaliatory measures that hurt American workers and businesses—particularly in manufacturing-heavy states. Trump's tariff supporters counter that trade leverage is essential to renegotiating deals and protecting domestic industry from unfair competition.

**Why Colorado cares:** Colorado's agricultural, energy, and manufacturing sectors depend heavily on cross-border trade with Canada. Tariff escalation could squeeze Colorado ranchers, oil and gas operators, and exporters already navigating thin margins. If Rogers's position gains traction among GOP candidates, it signals a potential fracture in Republican economic messaging heading into 2026.

Conservatives and taxpayers should watch whether other Republican candidates follow Rogers's lead—or double down on Trump's protectionist stance—as the party clarifies its trade philosophy.
