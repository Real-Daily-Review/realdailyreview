# A Generation Born After 9/11 Faces a Nation Still Fighting Ideological Enemies

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students who never knew the pre-9/11 world are grappling with a national trauma that shaped American security policy, military spending, and counterterrorism doctrine—yet many lack the lived memory that forged national resolve.

CPR News reports that Denver-area educators are using the anniversary to teach younger generations about the attacks' historical significance. But conservatives raise a harder question: as institutional memory fades, is America losing sight of the ideological threats that persist?

National Review argues that while the immediate terror threat has evolved, "Sharia supremacism is on the march, still"—a reminder that the underlying conflict hasn't ended, merely transformed. Republicans and defense hawks worry that a generation without direct memory of 9/11's consequences may underestimate the need for robust counterterrorism capabilities, strong border security, and vigilant vetting of foreign actors.

For Colorado specifically, this matters: the state hosts military installations, defense contractors, and communities with deep ties to post-9/11 veterans and intelligence work. Weakening public understanding of persistent threats could undermine support for the defense spending and security measures that protect American lives.

**Watch whether Colorado schools teach 9/11 as historical tragedy alone—or as a warning about ongoing ideological warfare.**
