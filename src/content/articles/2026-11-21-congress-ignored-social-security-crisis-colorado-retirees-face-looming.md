# Congress's Social Security Negligence Could Cost Colorado Retirees Millions

Congress has ignored decades of warnings about Social Security's structural collapse, and millions of Americans—including Colorado's 800,000-plus retirees—face automatic benefit cuts within years if lawmakers don't act.

The culprit, according to experts cited by Fox News and the New York Post, traces to a 1977 law tying benefit growth to wages instead of inflation. Sen. Ron Johnson's recent report blasts the program as a "Ponzi scheme," highlighting how this decision locked in long-term insolvency. The Social Security Trust Fund is projected to deplete reserves, triggering mandatory across-the-board cuts unless Congress reforms the system.

Republicans argue the solution requires tough choices: means-testing benefits for wealthy retirees, raising the retirement age, or adjusting the wage-indexing formula. Democrats typically resist these reforms, preferring tax increases on high earners—a position that would burden Colorado's middle-class workers and small-business owners already squeezed by state and federal taxes.

Meanwhile, the Washington Examiner reports the third round of November payments (capped at $5,181) went out Nov. 25 to those born after the 21st. For Colorado seniors living on fixed incomes, even modest cuts would sting.

**Watch for: Whether Colorado's congressional delegation—particularly Democratic members—will support genuine Social Security reform or punt the crisis to the next generation.**
