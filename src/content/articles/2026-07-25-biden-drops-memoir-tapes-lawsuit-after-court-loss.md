# Biden Surrenders Memoir Tapes Fight—But Trump DOJ's Selective Transparency Raises Questions

Former President Joe Biden has dropped his lawsuit blocking release of his memoir tapes after losing in federal appeals court, according to the Washington Examiner. The move marks a rare defeat for Biden's legal team, which had sought to keep the recordings private.

Yet conservatives are raising an eyebrow at the Trump Justice Department's own transparency record. National Review notes that while Trump's DOJ urged the appeals court to disclose Biden's tapes, it has simultaneously suppressed special counsel Jack Smith's report on the Mar-a-Lago investigation—a move that cuts both ways on the principle of government openness.

Republicans argue the Biden tapes release is justified accountability; the former president should not hide records from public scrutiny. But the inconsistency matters: if transparency is the principle, it must apply uniformly, not selectively when politically convenient.

For Colorado taxpayers and voters, the broader lesson is clear—both parties invoke "the rule of law" when it suits them. Coloradans should demand consistent, principled transparency from any administration, regardless of party affiliation.

Watch whether Trump's DOJ faces sustained pressure to release the Smith report with the same vigor it applied to Biden's tapes.
