# Colorado Fire Crews Warn: Deep-Fried Turkey Disasters Spike During Holiday Season

North Metro Fire Rescue District demonstrated the real dangers of backyard turkey frying this week—and it wasn't pretty. Crews showed both the safe and catastrophically unsafe ways to deep-fry a turkey, with the wrong-way demo erupting in flames to drive home the message: one mistake turns dinner into a house fire.

Turkey-frying accidents spike during Thanksgiving and Christmas, sending dozens to emergency rooms annually with severe burns. The culprit is usually operator error—overfilled oil, wet birds, or unattended fryers. Fire officials stress keeping fryers outdoors, away from structures and propane tanks, and never leaving them unattended.

Meanwhile, Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. promoted his "Make America Healthy Again" initiative by joining Indigenous chef Ray Naranjo to demonstrate turning Thanksgiving leftovers into traditional turkey tamales—a pivot toward whole-food cooking that aligns with the administration's skepticism of ultra-processed foods.

For Colorado families planning holiday meals, the takeaway is straightforward: if you're frying turkey, follow the safety protocols fire crews outlined, or stick to conventional cooking methods that won't land you in the burn unit.
