# Federal Colorado River Deal Leaves Upper Basin Bracing for Next Fight

Colorado dodged a water bullet—for now. The federal government's "final" decision on Colorado River allocations spares Upper Basin states, including Colorado, from immediate cuts, but don't mistake temporary relief for long-term security (Complete Colorado).

The reprieve masks a deeper problem: the river's structural deficit remains unsolved. As demand outpaces supply across the Southwest, Colorado's agricultural and municipal water users face mounting pressure to accept future reductions. Farmers and ranchers—the backbone of Colorado's rural economy—should expect Washington to return with new demands.

Meanwhile, Colorado's energy sector faces its own regulatory headwind. The Colorado Supreme Court rebuffed Xcel Energy's attempt to fast-track a power line condemnation through Cheyenne County ranchland (Colorado Sun). Xcel sought to condemn a 550-foot strip to connect two wind farms to a substation, but the court sided with the landowner's right to contest the route. The decision underscores a conservative principle: property rights matter, even when they complicate green-energy infrastructure.

Both stories reveal the same tension: government and corporate interests pushing past landowner objections in the name of resource management and climate policy.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's congressional delegation pushes back on federal water cuts or capitulates to environmental lobby pressure.
