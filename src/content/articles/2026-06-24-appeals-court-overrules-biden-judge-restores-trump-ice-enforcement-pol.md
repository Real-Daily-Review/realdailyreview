# Federal Court Blocks Trump ICE Arrests at Immigration Courts—Then Gets Overruled

A Biden-appointed federal judge tried to block Trump administration immigration enforcement at courthouses nationwide, but a federal appeals court quickly reversed course and restored the policy.

U.S. District Judge P. Casey Pitts issued a nationwide injunction blocking ICE arrests at immigration courthouses and limiting detention time for illegal immigrants. The move drew sharp criticism from conservatives, who argue such judicial overreach hamstrings law enforcement and protects illegal aliens from accountability (Washington Examiner).

Within days, a federal appeals court revived Trump's expedited removal policy, allowing fast-track deportations of eligible illegal immigrants (Fox News). Republicans argue this represents common-sense border enforcement: processing deportations efficiently without endless litigation delays that tie up immigration courts and drain resources.

**The Colorado angle:** Colorado's immigration enforcement capacity depends partly on federal policy clarity. Repeated judicial blocks on deportation procedures create uncertainty for state and local law enforcement coordinating with ICE, and add costs to the criminal justice system when illegal immigrants cycle through courts repeatedly.

Conservatives say the back-and-forth illustrates the problem: activist judges appointed by the Biden administration weaponizing the bench to block legitimate executive enforcement. The appeals court's swift reversal suggests the judiciary may finally be checking judicial overreach on immigration.

Watch whether additional Biden-appointed judges attempt similar nationwide injunctions—and how quickly appellate courts dispose of them.
