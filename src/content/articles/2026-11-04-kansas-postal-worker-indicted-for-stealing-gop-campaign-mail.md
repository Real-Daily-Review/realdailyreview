# Mail Theft, Election Integrity, and Double Standards

A Kansas postal worker's indictment for stealing over 4,400 pieces of mail—including nearly 400 Republican campaign mailers—raises serious questions about election security that Colorado voters should care about.

Kelly R. Cram allegedly intercepted and failed to deliver Republican political flyers, according to Fox News and Breitbart. The selective targeting of GOP materials suggests this wasn't random negligence; it was deliberate suppression of campaign speech during a critical election period.

The case underscores a hard truth: mail-in voting and campaign mail delivery depend entirely on the integrity of federal postal workers. Colorado, which relies heavily on mail ballots, has no guarantee that every piece of campaign literature—or ballot information—reaches voters safely. If a single postal employee can intercept hundreds of mailers without immediate detection, what's stopping similar abuses elsewhere?

Republicans argue this incident demands urgent scrutiny of USPS security protocols and employee vetting. Democrats have largely ignored the story, preferring to focus on other election-integrity concerns that align with their narrative.

The contrast is stark: while a Kansas postal worker faces indictment for mail theft, an indicted New Jersey Democrat (LaMonica McIver) won re-election despite an ongoing ICE case, per the New York Post. One side faces consequences; the other doesn't.

**Watch whether Colorado election officials strengthen mail-handling audits and whether the USPS implements real accountability measures before 2026.**
