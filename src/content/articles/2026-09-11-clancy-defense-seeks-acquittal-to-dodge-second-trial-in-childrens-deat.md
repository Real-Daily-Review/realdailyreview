# Colorado Mom's Murder Case Heads to Judicial Crossroads After Hung Jury

Lindsay Clancy's legal team is pulling out all stops to avoid a second trial for the deaths of her three young children in Duxbury, Massachusetts—filing a motion asking the judge to acquit her outright rather than face a jury retrial (9News Denver). Her attorney, Kevin Reddington, has also filed a renewed motion for a "not responsible" verdict based on an insanity defense (The Daily Wire).

The first trial ended in a mistrial after jurors deadlocked, leaving prosecutors to decide whether to retry the case. Clancy's defense strategy now hinges on convincing the judge that acquittal or an insanity ruling is warranted without another jury hearing.

The Daily Wire notes that jury members and the defense have already begun what amounts to a "shadow trial" in public commentary, potentially influencing any future jury pool before formal proceedings resume.

While this case originates in Massachusetts, it underscores broader questions about the criminal justice system's handling of high-profile cases involving mental health claims—issues that resonate across state lines, including in Colorado courtrooms where similar insanity defenses and jury dynamics play out.

**Watch for:** whether the judge grants the motion to acquit or forces prosecutors into a costly second trial.
