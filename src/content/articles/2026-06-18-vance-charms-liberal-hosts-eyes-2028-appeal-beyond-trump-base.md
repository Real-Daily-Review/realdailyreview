# VP Vance Wins Over Liberal Hosts, Signals Broader Appeal Beyond Trump Base

Vice President JD Vance's media blitz this week—including an appearance on ABC's *The View*—is reshaping perceptions of him among centrist and even left-leaning audiences. Joy Behar, the daytime talk show's co-host, called Vance "intelligent" and urged him to consider a 2028 presidential run, according to the Washington Examiner. Behind the scenes on *The View's* podcast, Behar admitted Vance "isn't a bad guy," noting he differed sharply from her expectations.

The appearance comes as Vance promotes his new book, *Communion: Finding My Way Back to Faith*. Rather than the hostile reception many anticipated from the liberal hosts, Vance's calm demeanor and willingness to engage substantively appears to have softened some resistance (Washington Examiner opinion).

For conservatives, the takeaway is mixed. Vance's ability to communicate across ideological lines without compromising core positions demonstrates political skill—a trait Republicans will need heading into 2028. However, some on the right worry that outreach to centrists signals a softening on Trump-era policies or a pivot away from the America First agenda that energized the base.

The real test: whether Vance can maintain credibility with grassroots conservatives while building the broader coalition necessary to win a general election.
