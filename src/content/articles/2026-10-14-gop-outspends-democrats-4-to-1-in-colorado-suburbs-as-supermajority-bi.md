# Colorado Republicans Outspend Democrats in Suburban Races as Supermajority Bid Falters

Colorado Democrats' dream of a legislative supermajority is slipping away—and Republicans are making them pay for it in the suburbs.

GOP groups are outspending Democrats by more than four to one in key battlegrounds like Highlands Ranch, Pueblo, and Loveland, according to the Colorado Sun. The spending disparity underscores a broader Democratic vulnerability heading into November, even as state party officials attempt a county-level fundraising and organizing push (Complete Colorado).

Meanwhile, Colorado voters are getting a clearer picture of what Democratic priorities actually cost. An opinion piece in the Colorado Sun warns that Proposition NN—a tax measure Democrats are backing—would "disrupt" constitutional taxpayer protections and force families to redirect money earmarked for their own needs. The piece notes that voters should be "skeptical whether today's good causes justify damaging" existing safeguards against unchecked government spending.

Republicans aren't just winning on messaging. Initiative 362, which adds voter ID requirements to Colorado's all-mail system, is also on the November ballot, giving conservatives a concrete election-integrity win to campaign on.

**Watch for:** Whether Republican spending advantages in suburban districts translate into actual seat gains that prevent Democrats from consolidating power in the state legislature.
