# Colorado's Wildfire Season Tests State's Preparedness as Aspen Acres Fire Spreads

Colorado's largest active wildfire is forcing evacuations and testing whether state and local officials have adequately prepared for the fire season ahead. The Aspen Acres fire in Custer County prompted mandatory evacuation orders this week as hot, dry conditions overwhelmed initial defenses, though heavy rains have since provided temporary relief (Colorado Sun).

Crews are now working to shore up protections around homes in southern Colorado as moderate fire activity continues. The fire represents the kind of large-scale threat that has become routine in Colorado—a state where forest management, water availability, and land-use decisions directly affect wildfire risk.

Republicans and conservatives have long argued that Colorado's forests are overgrown due to decades of fire-suppression policies and insufficient timber harvesting, making them tinderboxes. They contend that aggressive forest management—thinning dense stands and removing dead wood—is essential to reducing catastrophic fire risk. Democrats and environmental groups counter that climate change is the primary driver and that logging operations can damage ecosystems.

The question for Colorado taxpayers: Are state officials investing adequately in preventive forest management, or are they waiting until fires force expensive emergency responses? Watch whether the legislature funds forest-thinning initiatives or continues prioritizing environmental restrictions over fuel reduction.
