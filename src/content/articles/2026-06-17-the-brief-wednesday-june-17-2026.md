# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Wednesday, June 17, 2026

Colorado conservatives are watching Washington, Denver City Hall, and the hospital system all at once today — and in every case, the story is about who holds power and who's being forced to pay for someone else's decisions.

---

## PERA Bonus Scandal Puts Public Pension Accountability Back on the Table

Colorado lawmakers are moving to cap performance bonuses at the Public Employees' Retirement Association after a Colorado Sun investigation revealed PERA has been handing its investment staff millions in bonuses in recent years — while Colorado taxpayers and public employees remain on the hook for the fund's long-term obligations. Republicans and fiscal conservatives have long argued that PERA's governance lacks the accountability demanded of private-sector fund managers. This investigation gives the legislature an opening to rein in a system that has operated with too little scrutiny for too long.

---

## Children's Hospital Doctors Defy Court Order on Gender Procedures

Children's Hospital Colorado was ordered by a court to resume gender-affirming care for minors — but the doctors who work there are refusing to perform the procedures, citing fear of criminal charges under state or federal law. Conservatives say this is exactly the right instinct: no physician should be compelled by a court order to perform controversial, irreversible procedures on children. The standoff exposes the absurdity of using the judiciary to mandate medical care, and Colorado parents watching this case should understand that the fight over who controls their children's healthcare is far from over.

---

## Denver Post Owner Settles for $13.5 Million, Vacates Lease Early

The owner of the Denver Post has agreed to a $13.5 million settlement with the City of Denver and will pull out of its lease — which was supposed to run through 2029 — by June 30. Whatever one thinks of the Post's editorial direction, the episode is a reminder that Denver's city government has become a difficult landlord and an even more difficult partner for private business. Colorado's business community should take note of how the city handles contractual obligations when it suits its interests.

---

## Hollywood's Jan. 6 Project: Sean Penn, Bradley Cooper, and an Agenda

Sean Penn — one of Hollywood's most reliably left-wing voices — will direct the first major studio film about the January 6, 2021, Capitol riot, with Bradley Cooper reportedly in talks to star as a Capitol police officer. Conservatives should have no illusions about what this film will be: a two-hour campaign ad dressed up as drama, timed and targeted for maximum political effect. The right's response shouldn't be outrage — it should be building the alternative media and entertainment infrastructure to tell its own stories.

---

## GOP Fault Lines on Iran: Vance Faces Hard Questions

Conservative commentator Megyn Kelly pressed Vice President JD Vance directly on the growing divide within the Republican Party over the Trump administration's military operation in Iran — describing internal party relationships as strained. The split between hawks and restraint-minded conservatives is real, and it matters for Colorado, home to a significant military and veteran community at installations like Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, and Buckley. How the administration manages this debate will define the GOP's foreign policy identity heading into the midterm cycle.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eye on the PERA bonus legislation — the details will determine whether this is genuine reform or a political head-fake that leaves the underlying governance problems intact. On the Children's Hospital standoff, watch for whether Colorado's Democratic-controlled legislature attempts to add new legal pressure on physicians, which would mark a serious escalation of government coercion in medicine. And as the Iran debate sharpens inside the GOP, Colorado's congressional delegation will face increasing pressure to take a clear public position.
