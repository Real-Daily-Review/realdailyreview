# Immigration Judge Rules Mosque Leader Can Be Deported on National Security Grounds

An immigration judge has cleared the way for the Trump administration to deport Salah Sasour, president of the Islamic Society of Milwaukee, on national security grounds (Just The News). Sasour, who has lived in the U.S. for over three decades, was convicted in Israel as a teenager in 1988 after confessing to throwing Molotov cocktails and stones at Israeli military personnel, and was later convicted in 1995 of attempting to possess weapons (The Daily Wire).

The ruling represents a significant win for the Trump administration's immigration enforcement agenda. Conservatives argue that vetting and removing individuals with ties to extremism or hostile foreign regimes is essential to national security—a position that aligns with stricter border and immigration policies favored by Republicans.

The case underscores broader tensions over how the U.S. handles immigration cases involving national security concerns. While the Trump administration has moved aggressively on deportations tied to security risks, critics on the left typically argue such cases require careful due-process protections and context about rehabilitation over decades of residence.

For Colorado, this ruling reinforces the administration's commitment to enforcement priorities that conservatives support: vetting immigrants thoroughly and removing those with security red flags.
