# Colorado Primary Turnout Surges, But Election System Flaws Remain Unresolved

Colorado voters showed up in force for the 2026 primary election, posting the second-highest turnout since 2010 (CPR News). Yet the strong participation masks a deeper problem: Colorado's primary system allows candidates to win with a small plurality in crowded fields, even when voters would prefer someone else.

The so-called "Griswold fiasco" underscored this vulnerability. Conservatives argue the state's current primary structure fails to reflect genuine voter preference and invites outcomes that don't represent the will of the majority (Complete Colorado). When a single candidate can capture a nomination with 30 percent of the vote in a five-way race, the system rewards narrow bases over broad appeal—a particular concern for Republicans trying to field competitive general-election candidates.

Election reform advocates on the right have pushed for ranked-choice voting or runoff mechanisms to ensure nominees command genuine majority support. Democrats, by contrast, have largely resisted such changes, preferring the status quo.

Colorado taxpayers and conservative activists should watch whether the state legislature takes up primary reform in the 2027 session—and whether Democrats will finally acknowledge that the current system produces outcomes that even their own voters find problematic.
