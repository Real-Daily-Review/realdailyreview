# Anthropic's $81B IPO Creates Windfall for Bay Area Tech Elite—Colorado Left Behind

San Francisco-based AI firm Anthropic just raised a record $81 billion going public, minting instant millionaires across its 5,300-person workforce while Colorado's tech sector watches from the sidelines. The Daily Wire reports the average employee's net worth jumped above $40 million Thursday, with at least 400 workers now worth over $100 million each after the company granted options on 1.77 billion shares (Daily Caller).

**The Colorado angle:** While California's tech corridor captures transformational wealth creation, Colorado's economy remains dependent on energy, agriculture, and tourism. The state has struggled to build a homegrown AI or deep-tech ecosystem competitive with Silicon Valley—a gap that costs Colorado workers, investors, and tax revenue.

Republicans and conservatives argue that California's regulatory burden and high taxes should have driven companies like Anthropic to business-friendly states. Colorado offers lower costs and less red tape than the Bay Area, yet lacks the venture capital density and talent pipeline to compete. Meanwhile, progressive policies in California—from labor mandates to environmental rules—haven't stopped mega-cap tech firms from thriving there, undercutting the left's argument that such regulations don't harm competitiveness.

**Watch next:** Whether Colorado's legislature pursues targeted tax incentives or regulatory streamlining to attract AI and advanced-tech firms before the next funding cycle.
