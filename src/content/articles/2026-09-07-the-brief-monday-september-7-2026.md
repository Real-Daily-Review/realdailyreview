## Trump Backs Paxton — And Puts Colorado On Notice

President Trump is opening his war chest to rescue Ken Paxton's Senate campaign, but the bigger story for Coloradans is the administration's broader posture: states that resist federal pressure risk being made an example of. Colorado's Democrat-controlled government has positioned itself as a sanctuary from federal enforcement on everything from immigration to energy policy. Republicans argue that's not constitutional courage — it's defiance that invites consequences Colorado taxpayers will ultimately pay for.

## Biden Judge Cuts MS-13 Killer Loose in California

A Biden-appointed federal judge released a convicted MS-13 gangster — known as "La Bruja," wanted for murder — after ruling ICE held him without a required bond hearing. DHS confirmed the machete-wielding killer is now free in California. Conservatives say this is exactly what happens when activist judges prioritize procedural technicalities over public safety. Colorado, which has its own sanctuary-adjacent policies and a Democratic attorney general hostile to ICE cooperation, should take note: this is what soft enforcement looks like in practice.

## Trump's Approval Numbers Spell Midterm Trouble

Washington Examiner chief political correspondent Byron York isn't sugarcoating it — Trump's job approval rating is "not good," and historically, a president's standing heading into midterms directly shapes congressional outcomes. For Colorado Republicans, that's a double-edged warning. The party needs to hold seats in competitive districts while making the affirmative case for conservative governance on the economy, border security, and energy. Coasting on Trump's brand alone won't cut it if the numbers don't improve between now and November.

## Federal Judge Activism Lets Criminal Walk — A Pattern, Not an Anomaly

The MS-13 release isn't an isolated incident — it reflects a coordinated legal strategy by left-aligned judges to obstruct immigration enforcement at every turn. Republicans argue the judiciary has become a political weapon deployed against lawful executive action. For Colorado, where federal immigration enforcement has faced local resistance and courtroom challenges, the message is clear: until Congress acts to limit judicial overreach on enforcement matters, bad actors will keep walking free on procedural grounds while communities bear the cost.

## Denver's Nob Hill Inn Fights City Hall — Literally

The Nob Hill Inn, a beloved Colfax Avenue dive bar and piece of Denver's working-class history, is fighting for survival after city construction threatened its viability. The community is rallying, and the bar remains hopeful. It's a small story with a big lesson: government projects — however well-intentioned — have real costs that fall on small business owners and the neighborhoods that depend on them. Denver conservatives know this playbook well. The city builds; locals pay.
