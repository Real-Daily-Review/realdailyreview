# Fauci's Fifth Amendment Dodge Raises Questions About Biden's Pardon

Dr. Anthony Fauci invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination more than 100 times during Senate testimony on his COVID-19 pandemic response, prompting Republicans to question the legitimacy of President Biden's autopen pardon issued beforehand (Washington Examiner).

House Oversight Chair James Comer called the pardon "legally questionable," arguing that Fauci's refusal to answer questions undermines the administration's claim that the former health official did nothing wrong (Fox News). Sen. Josh Hawley went further, suggesting Fauci's silence indicates he may have enriched himself during the pandemic (Just The News).

While the Washington Examiner's opinion section notes that invoking the Fifth doesn't inherently prove guilt—innocent people sometimes fear intimidation or misspeaking under oath—the optics remain damaging. A federal health bureaucrat who shaped pandemic policy nationwide, then refuses to defend his record under oath, then receives a presidential pardon, presents a troubling picture of unaccountable government power.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses that suffered under lockdown policies, Fauci's silence feels like a final insult: no accountability, no answers, just a pardon and a closed door.

Watch whether Republican senators pursue contempt charges or demand the pardon's legal basis be tested in court.
