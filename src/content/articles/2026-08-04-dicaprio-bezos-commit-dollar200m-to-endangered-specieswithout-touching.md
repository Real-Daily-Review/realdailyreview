# DiCaprio, Bezos Back $200M Species-Rescue Initiative—But Don't Expect Colorado Taxpayers to Foot the Bill

Hollywood and Big Tech are putting their money where their mouths are: Leonardo DiCaprio and Jeff Bezos have launched a $200 million private initiative to save 100 endangered species, including lemurs, salamanders, pangolins, and echidnas (9News Denver, Breitbart).

The foundations backing the effort frame the project around climate-change concerns, though the sources don't detail specific Colorado impacts or how the funds will be deployed domestically.

**The conservative take:** When billionaires voluntarily spend their own fortunes on environmental causes, that's capitalism working as intended—no government mandates, no taxpayer extraction, no regulatory expansion required. If DiCaprio and Bezos believe species conservation is worth $200 million, they're free to write the check without forcing Colorado ranchers, energy workers, or small-business owners to subsidize their vision through higher taxes or burdensome regulations.

The contrast is sharp: private philanthropy versus the progressive playbook of government-mandated conservation schemes that often restrict land use, water rights, and resource development in Colorado.

Conservatives should watch whether this initiative becomes a template for federal or state officials to justify new environmental regulations or spending programs—using private-sector action as cover for government overreach.
