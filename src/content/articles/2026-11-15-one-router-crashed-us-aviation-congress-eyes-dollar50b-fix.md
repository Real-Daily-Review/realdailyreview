# One Router Crashed U.S. Aviation—Now Congress Faces $50B Modernization Demand

A single faulty router at an FAA facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, paralyzed American air travel Friday, exposing the fragility of aging federal infrastructure and sparking calls for massive taxpayer-funded upgrades.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy is now pushing Congress for $50 billion to modernize the nation's air traffic control systems (Daily Caller). The pitch: without modernization, catastrophic outages will repeat.

**The conservative concern:** Before writing a $50 billion check, Congress should demand accountability for how the FAA allowed a single point of failure to cripple the entire system. Republicans argue the real problem isn't insufficient funding—it's government mismanagement and lack of redundancy in critical systems. Why should taxpayers foot a massive bill when the FAA failed to implement basic backup protocols?

Colorado's aviation industry, centered around Denver International Airport, felt the disruption acutely. DIA is a major hub; any extended shutdown hammers Colorado's tourism economy and strands business travelers.

**The Republican counterargument:** Rather than blank-check spending, Congress should demand the FAA conduct a full audit of infrastructure vulnerabilities, implement mandatory redundancy requirements, and prove existing funds are being spent efficiently before approving new appropriations.

Watch whether Congress demands performance metrics and accountability measures—or simply opens the federal wallet.
