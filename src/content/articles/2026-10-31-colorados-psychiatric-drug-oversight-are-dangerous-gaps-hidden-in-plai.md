# Psychiatric Oversight Failure: How Unmonitored Drug Cocktails Slip Through the Cracks

A Massachusetts tragedy reveals how government prescription monitoring systems leave dangerous gaps—and Colorado's oversight may have similar blind spots.

The case of Lindsay Clancy, who killed her three children after being prescribed 13 different psychiatric medications in four months, exposes a critical failure in state drug-tracking infrastructure, according to the *Washington Examiner*. Of those 13 medications, only four fell within Massachusetts's prescription monitoring program; the remaining nine—including antidepressants, antipsychotics, and mood stabilizers—operated outside routine reporting channels.

This matters for Colorado. State prescription monitoring programs are supposed to flag dangerous drug interactions and polypharmacy (prescribing multiple psychiatric drugs simultaneously without adequate oversight). If Colorado's system has similar gaps, patients could face untracked medication cocktails that increase risks of adverse reactions, overdose, or behavioral crises.

Conservatives argue this case demonstrates why government agencies must actually *enforce* existing safeguards rather than expand regulations. The problem wasn't too few rules—it was that existing monitoring systems failed to catch a dangerous prescribing pattern. Better coordination between prescribers and state oversight, not more bureaucracy, could prevent tragedies.

Colorado taxpayers and families should demand transparency: Does Colorado's prescription monitoring program track *all* psychiatric medications, or do gaps exist here too?
