# Colorado Should Stop Forcing Taxpayers to Fund Political Parties They Don't Support

Colorado government is using taxpayer dollars to finance primary elections for political parties—a practice that forces citizens to subsidize organizations they may oppose or reject entirely. According to Complete Colorado, the state has "no proper business granting special recognition or privileges to political parties," yet continues to do exactly that through primary funding mechanisms.

The conservative case is straightforward: Why should a libertarian, independent, or Republican voter in a Democratic stronghold—or vice versa—be forced to pay for another party's nominating process? Complete Colorado argues this represents an unjustifiable transfer of public money to private political organizations. Taxpayers who belong to no party, or who actively oppose the party in power, have no say in how their contributions are spent.

Democrats and party establishment figures defend the current system as necessary infrastructure for orderly elections and voter participation. They argue that primary elections serve a public function and warrant public funding.

But conservatives see it differently: if political parties want to hold primaries, they should fund them through member dues and donations—not raid the state treasury. This is a matter of basic fairness and limited government.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado lawmakers move to end taxpayer-financed primaries and require parties to self-fund their nominating processes.
