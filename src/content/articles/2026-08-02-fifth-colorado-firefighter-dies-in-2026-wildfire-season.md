# Colorado Loses Fifth Firefighter to Wildfires in 2026

Colorado's wildfire season has claimed another life—the fifth firefighter to die battling blazes across the state this year. Nathan Matthews, a member of the Grand Junction fire service, will be honored at a memorial service Saturday as the state grapples with what appears to be an exceptionally deadly fire season.

The rising death toll underscores a hard reality conservatives and taxpayers must confront: Colorado's forest management policies have left vast stretches of state and federal land tinder-dry and prone to catastrophic burns. For years, environmental groups have blocked or delayed timber harvesting and fuel-reduction projects on public lands, citing spotted owls and other regulatory concerns. Meanwhile, firefighters pay the price in blood.

Republicans have long argued that active forest management—thinning overcrowded stands, removing dead timber, and allowing controlled burns—reduces wildfire intensity and protects both lives and property. The Biden administration's environmental restrictions on logging and land-use decisions have only worsened conditions across the West, conservatives say.

Beyond the human tragedy, these fires devastate Colorado's economy, tourism, and air quality. Taxpayers foot massive suppression bills while watching preventable disasters unfold.

Watch for whether state lawmakers push back against federal land-use restrictions and demand faster approval for forest-thinning projects on Colorado's public lands.
