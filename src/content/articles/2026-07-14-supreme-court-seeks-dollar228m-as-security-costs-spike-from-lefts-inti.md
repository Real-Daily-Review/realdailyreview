# Supreme Court Seeks $228M Budget Boost as Security Costs Soar

The Supreme Court is asking Congress for a hefty $228 million in next year's budget, with a significant chunk earmarked for protecting justices against threats—a direct consequence of the left's campaign to intimidate the bench over controversial rulings.

Justices Elena Kagan and Amy Coney Barrett testified before the House appropriations subcommittee on Tuesday to defend the spending request. The security increase reflects real dangers: justices have faced sustained harassment and threats following high-profile decisions, including the Dobbs ruling overturning Roe v. Wade.

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the Court shouldn't need fortress-level security because of political intimidation. The real problem, they contend, is the left's refusal to accept unfavorable rulings and its encouragement of protests outside justices' homes—tactics that crossed ethical and legal lines. Conservatives say Democrats should condemn the harassment rather than expect taxpayers to fund ever-expanding security details.

**Colorado angle:** Colorado taxpayers foot their share of federal spending. As the Court faces mounting costs tied to ideological pressure campaigns, fiscal conservatives question whether Congress will rubber-stamp the request without demanding accountability for why judicial security has become a crisis.

Watch whether House Republicans push back on the full amount or demand the Court and Justice Department crack down on the groups fueling the threats in the first place.
