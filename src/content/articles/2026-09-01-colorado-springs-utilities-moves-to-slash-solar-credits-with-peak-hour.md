# Colorado Springs Utilities Targets Solar Owners With Peak-Hour Rate Hike

Colorado Springs Utilities is moving to gut the economics of rooftop solar through a new billing scheme that charges premium rates during peak evening hours—precisely when solar panels stop producing. The utility wants to shift how it credits solar customers, effectively punishing those who invested in energy independence based on previous rate structures.

Solar advocates argue the new program, which charges higher prices during peak usage hours on weekdays from 5-9 p.m. (CPR News), will render existing solar installations economically unviable and discourage future adoption. Conservatives should recognize this as classic regulatory bait-and-switch: government-favored utilities changing the rules after customers make capital investments based on stable incentives.

The broader Colorado angle: this hits homeowners and small businesses statewide who bet on solar as a hedge against rising electricity costs and government overreach. If utilities can retroactively rewrite rate structures, what investment is safe?

Republicans and free-market advocates argue utilities should honor existing agreements and compete fairly rather than use regulatory power to protect their monopoly position. The utility counters that peak-hour pricing reflects actual grid costs—a claim worth scrutinizing given utilities' track record of rate-hiking.

Watch whether Colorado's Public Utilities Commission allows this precedent-setting rate restructuring to proceed.
