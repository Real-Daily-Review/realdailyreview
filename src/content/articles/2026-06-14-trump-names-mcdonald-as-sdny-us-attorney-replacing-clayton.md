# Trump Taps McDonald for SDNY U.S. Attorney Post

President Trump has announced his intent to appoint James McDonald as U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York, one of the nation's most powerful federal prosecutor positions, replacing Jay Clayton who is moving to the Director of National Intelligence role.

McDonald, a senior partner at Sullivan &amp; Cromwell, brings heavyweight credentials: he clerked for Chief Justice John G. Roberts Jr. and holds degrees from Harvard College and the University of Virginia School of Law (Just The News). The SDNY post oversees federal prosecutions across Manhattan, the Bronx, and surrounding counties—a jurisdiction that has historically pursued high-profile cases against financial crimes, organized crime, and public corruption.

For Colorado conservatives, the appointment matters as part of Trump's broader effort to staff the Justice Department with figures seen as committed to restoring institutional credibility after years of perceived politicization. Republicans argue the DOJ under Biden weaponized federal power against Trump and his allies; Trump's personnel picks signal a reset toward impartial law enforcement.

McDonald's legal pedigree and private-sector background suggest a prosecutor focused on substantive criminal law rather than political theater—a contrast conservatives say characterized recent DOJ leadership.

Watch whether Senate Republicans move swiftly to confirm McDonald and whether the SDNY office begins investigating alleged abuses of power during the previous administration.
