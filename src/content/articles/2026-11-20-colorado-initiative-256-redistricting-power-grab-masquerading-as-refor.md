# Colorado Voters Face Redistricting Power Grab Disguised as Reform

Colorado voters will decide this fall whether to strip the state legislature of its constitutional authority over congressional redistricting—handing the power to an unelected commission instead. Initiative #256, backed by good-government advocates, would ban partisan gerrymandering by removing politicians from the line-drawing process entirely (Complete Colorado).

Supporters frame this as anti-corruption reform. But conservatives should recognize what's really at stake: the initiative transfers redistricting authority from elected representatives accountable to voters to an appointed body insulated from democratic pressure. While gerrymandering is a legitimate concern, the cure here is worse than the disease—it's bureaucratic overreach dressed up as fairness.

Republicans argue that voters already have recourse: they can elect different legislators who redraw lines differently. Handing redistricting to a commission removes that accountability lever and concentrates power in the hands of unelected technocrats who answer to no one.

The timing matters. Colorado just sent four new Democrats to Congress (CPR News), and Democrats control the state legislature. Republicans should ask themselves whether they'd support this same commission if the shoe were on the other foot—or whether this is simply a power play by the party currently in charge.

Watch whether Republican leadership in Colorado makes a clear case against surrendering legislative authority to bureaucrats, or whether they let this slide through voter indifference.
