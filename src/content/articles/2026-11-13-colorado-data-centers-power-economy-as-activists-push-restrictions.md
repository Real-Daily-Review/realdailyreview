# Colorado's Data Center Boom Powers the Digital Economy—While Activists Want to Shut It Down

Colorado's roughly 60 data centers aren't luxuries—they're the backbone of modern life, from cloud computing to AI infrastructure that powers everything Americans rely on daily. Yet progressive activists and some local governments treat them as environmental villains, pushing restrictions that threaten Colorado's competitive advantage in a critical 21st-century industry.

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Data centers create high-wage jobs, generate substantial tax revenue for Colorado counties, and position the state as a tech hub. They're essential infrastructure, not frivolous consumption. The facilities operate with minimal environmental footprint compared to their economic output—and Colorado's abundant, affordable hydroelectric power makes the state an ideal location.

**The left's objection:** Environmental groups claim data centers consume excessive water and electricity, framing them as climate threats. Some local officials have imposed moratoriums or restrictive zoning, citing vague sustainability concerns.

Republicans and business advocates counter that these restrictions are economically destructive. Colorado can't afford to cede data center development to Texas, Arizona, or other states hungry for the jobs and tax base. Killing this industry through regulation means losing opportunity to competitors.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado's legislature moves to clarify data center permitting rules or whether local anti-growth activists succeed in strangling the sector through piecemeal restrictions.
