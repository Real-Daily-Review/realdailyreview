# Trump's Nvidia Move Sparks Conservative Backlash Over Executive Power

President Trump's direct intervention to reinstate Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has triggered rare criticism from the right, with conservatives warning that presidential meddling in corporate governance threatens free-market principles and sets a troubling precedent for government overreach.

GOP Senator Dave McCormick (Daily Caller) argues the move "sets a dangerous precedent" that could "erode the country's competitive edge" by signaling that boardroom decisions now flow from the White House rather than shareholder interests. The Washington Examiner editorial board echoed the concern: "When the president of the United States can call a company's board and get its CEO reinstated, something has gone wrong with American capitalism."

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** Companies must answer to owners and markets, not political pressure from Washington. If Trump can pick Nvidia's leadership, what stops future presidents—Democratic or Republican—from dictating personnel at energy firms, banks, or manufacturers? That's crony capitalism dressed up as patriotism.

For Colorado, this matters. The state hosts tech talent, venture capital, and semiconductor-adjacent industries. When federal power replaces market discipline, Colorado entrepreneurs and investors lose confidence in property rights and contract law.

**Watch whether Trump doubles down on corporate intervention or backs away from the precedent his own party is warning against.**
