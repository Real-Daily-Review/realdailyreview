# Colorado Funeral Home Collapse Exposes Regulatory Failure

A Pueblo mortuary's catastrophic mismanagement—with decomposing bodies left unattended and families defrauded—reveals how Colorado's regulatory oversight failed vulnerable Coloradans when it mattered most.

Two owners of Davis Mortuary, the Cotter brothers, were arrested and charged with 161 counts of abuse of a corpse, forgery, and theft, according to the Colorado Bureau of Investigation and the 10th Judicial District Attorney's Office (CPR News). Inspectors discovered more than 125 decomposing bodies during a facility inspection, with five remaining unidentified (9News Denver).

The case raises hard questions about state licensing and inspection protocols. Conservatives argue that when government agencies fail at their core duty—protecting citizens from fraud and abuse—it underscores why regulatory bodies must be held accountable and why families deserve transparent, enforceable standards. The mortuary industry requires baseline oversight; this disaster suggests Colorado's current system didn't deliver it.

Democrats and regulators will likely call for *more* regulation and funding. Republicans should demand answers: Why did inspections not catch this sooner? What specific failures occurred, and which officials are responsible? Coloradans entrusting loved ones to funeral homes deserve competence, not bureaucratic theater.

Watch for the state's response: Will Colorado strengthen enforcement without expanding red tape, or use this tragedy to justify broader regulatory expansion?
