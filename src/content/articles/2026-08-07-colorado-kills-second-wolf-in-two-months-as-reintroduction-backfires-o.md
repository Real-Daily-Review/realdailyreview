# Colorado's Wolf Experiment Keeps Killing Livestock—and Now Wolves

Colorado's taxpayer-funded wolf reintroduction is backfiring fast: state wildlife officials have now killed a second yearling wolf in under two months after it preyed on calves in Jackson and Grand counties (Colorado Sun). The dead wolf belonged to the One Ear Pack, which has grown to at least 17 members across two generations—a sign the reintroduced population is thriving while Colorado ranchers absorb the losses.

This marks the 17th wolf death overall since the state imported 25 wolves in two rounds: 10 from Oregon in December 2023 and 15 from British Columbia in January 2025 (9News Denver). The pattern is clear: wolves hunt livestock. Colorado Parks and Wildlife responds by killing the offending animals—a cycle that exposes the core problem with the reintroduction scheme.

**The conservative case:** Ranchers and Republicans warned this would happen. Wolves don't distinguish between elk and cattle. Killing problem wolves after the fact doesn't compensate ranchers for dead calves or the stress of constant vigilance. The state should have listened to rural Colorado before importing predators into working ranch country.

**Democrats counter** that wolves are essential to ecosystem health and that compensation programs and non-lethal deterrents can coexist with the program.

Watch whether Colorado expands kill authority or doubles down on the reintroduction despite mounting livestock losses.
