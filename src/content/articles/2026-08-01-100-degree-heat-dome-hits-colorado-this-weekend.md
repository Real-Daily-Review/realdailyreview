# Colorado Braces for Triple-Digit Heat as Dome Settles Over State

Denver and the Front Range are about to get a brutal reminder that Colorado summers can turn punishing fast. A heat dome is moving across the state this weekend, with temperatures expected to hit 100 degrees both Sunday and Monday, prompting the National Weather Service to issue a Weather Impact Alert for the region (9News Denver).

CPR News reports the extreme heat will blanket much of Colorado as the dome settles in, bringing triple-digit conditions that will test power grids, strain water resources already under pressure from drought, and spike energy demand across the state.

For Colorado taxpayers and businesses, the timing matters. Peak summer heat drives up electricity costs and strains the grid—a particular concern as the state pushes aggressive renewable mandates that critics argue leave the system vulnerable during peak demand. Ranchers face water stress during critical irrigation season, and outdoor workers in construction, agriculture, and energy development will face dangerous conditions.

Coloradans should prepare: stock water, check on elderly neighbors, and keep generators fueled. Conservatives should watch whether state regulators use this heat event to justify further grid interventions or renewable mandates—or whether market-driven energy solutions and responsible natural gas capacity prove their worth.
