# Ex-ICE Guard Faces Prosecution; Colorado Voters Eye Land-Grab Ballot Measure

A Colorado judge has ruled sufficient evidence exists to prosecute Brandon Booth, a former detention center guard accused of shooting toward protesters who made inflammatory remarks about "race traitors" and threatened to locate guards' children, according to the Colorado Sun. The case underscores tensions around immigration enforcement facilities in the state.

Meanwhile, Colorado conservatives are mobilizing against Proposition 137, a ballot measure that would funnel taxpayer dollars to environmental nonprofits—chiefly The Nature Conservancy, the initiative's primary backer. Complete Colorado warns the measure amounts to corporate welfare disguised as conservation, forcing Coloradans to finance wealthy NGOs' land acquisitions and policy agendas without meaningful public oversight or accountability.

Republicans argue Prop 137 represents exactly the kind of government-enabled cronyism that inflates costs, reduces transparency, and diverts resources from legitimate state priorities. Conservatives say Colorado voters should demand answers: Why should taxpayers subsidize private organizations' real estate deals? What guarantees exist that land purchases serve Colorado families rather than progressive environmental ideology?

The Booth prosecution and Prop 137 vote both land in November—one a criminal matter, the other a direct test of whether Coloradans will endorse another round of taxpayer-funded environmental activism.

Watch whether conservative groups successfully message Prop 137's corporate-welfare angle before Election Day.
