# Democrats' Antisemitism Problem Won't Go Away With Apologies

Michigan Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abdul El-Sayed met with the state's Democratic Jewish Caucus to apologize for past associations, but Republicans say the gesture rings hollow. According to Fox News, El-Sayed drew the line at distancing himself from Streamer Hasan Piker, whose recent remarks blamed Jews for potential violence against them—a comment that sparked outrage across the political spectrum.

The Washington Examiner's analysis cuts deeper: despite media claims that Democrats have "distanced themselves" from Piker's antisemitic rhetoric, only six Democrats have explicitly condemned his latest statements. That's a damning indictment of party-wide silence.

Republicans argue this exposes a deeper problem within progressive politics—a willingness to tolerate extremism when it aligns with anti-Israel sentiment. Mike Rogers, El-Sayed's opponent, called the apology a "sham," suggesting voters shouldn't be fooled by performative gestures that don't address the underlying issue.

For Colorado Democrats, this national story matters. The state's progressive wing has increasingly aligned with anti-Israel activism on college campuses and in party platforms. Coloradans should watch whether state Democratic leaders will clearly condemn antisemitism or follow the national party's pattern of selective outrage.
