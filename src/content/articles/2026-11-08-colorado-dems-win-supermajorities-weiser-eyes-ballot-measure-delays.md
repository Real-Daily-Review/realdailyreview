# Colorado Democrats Win Supermajorities as Ballot Measures Face Legal Scrutiny

Colorado voters approved three ballot measures backed by Protect Kids Colorado, but incoming Democratic Gov. Phil Weiser is already signaling he'll subject them to "a long, hard look" before implementation, according to the Denver Post.

The measures—addressing transgender issues and fentanyl penalties—passed despite Democrats securing supermajorities in both the Colorado House and Senate, giving the party near-total legislative control. Weiser's hesitation suggests the incoming administration may seek to delay, water down, or challenge the voter-approved initiatives through legal or regulatory means.

Conservatives argue this exemplifies progressive resistance to the will of Colorado voters. When ballot measures don't align with Democratic priorities, the left uses the courts and bureaucracy to obstruct implementation—a pattern taxpayers have seen repeatedly. Republicans counter that Weiser's caution reflects legitimate concerns about constitutional compliance.

The real story: Colorado's political landscape has shifted decisively leftward. With supermajorities and a Democratic governor, expect aggressive movement on taxes, gun regulations, energy policy, and social issues. Conservatives should brace for a legislative session that tests the limits of one-party rule.
