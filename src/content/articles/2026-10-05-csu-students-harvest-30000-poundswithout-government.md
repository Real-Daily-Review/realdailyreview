# Colorado State Students Grow Food, But Campus Culture Remains Divided

Colorado State University's Growing Food Security program is harvesting real solutions to student hunger—30,000 pounds of food expected in 2026—without waiting for government handouts. The on-campus farm initiative demonstrates what private enterprise and student initiative can accomplish when they skip the bureaucratic middleman.

It's a refreshing contrast to the broader campus climate. While CSU students are rolling up their sleeves to address food insecurity through voluntary work, college campuses nationwide continue hosting the kind of radical rhetoric that landed three University of South Alabama students in federal custody for threatening President Trump ahead of a rally (Daily Caller). That's the real food-for-thought moment here: Colorado's universities are producing both productive citizens *and* a vocal contingent comfortable with political violence.

The CSU farm model works because it's decentralized, student-driven, and doesn't depend on expanding the welfare state. It's the kind of solution conservatives champion—community-based, sustainable, and rooted in personal responsibility rather than government expansion.

Colorado taxpayers should watch whether CSU's success prompts other state universities to replicate the model, or whether progressive administrators instead push for more state funding to address the same problems the farm is already solving.
