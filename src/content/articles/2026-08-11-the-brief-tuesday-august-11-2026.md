## Trump's Vaccine Executive Order Lands as School Year Begins

President Trump has signed an executive order related to childhood vaccines, drawing immediate national debate just as school bells ring across Colorado and the country grapples with a measles outbreak. Conservatives are divided: some see the order as a legitimate assertion of parental rights and federal oversight reform; others worry about the public health timing. Colorado parents navigating school enrollment requirements deserve straight answers from state health officials about what, if anything, changes — not bureaucratic double-talk.

## Longmont Officers Shoot, Kill Suspect in Hostage Standoff

Three Longmont police officers responded to a situation in which a suspect had taken a victim hostage, declared he was armed, and refused all commands to comply. The confrontation ended with the suspect dead. Colorado law enforcement officers put their lives on the line daily, and when a suspect takes a hostage and announces he has a weapon, officers have every right to protect innocent life. Conservatives stand with the Longmont officers — and against any rush to second-guess their split-second decisions before the facts are fully in.

---

## Colorado Hunter Mistook Reintroduced Wolf for Coyote

A Colorado hunter who shot and killed what turned out to be a reintroduced gray wolf in January believed the animal was a coyote. Colorado Parks and Wildlife declined to press charges after the hunter immediately self-reported the incident. This is exactly the kind of real-world consequence that rural Coloradans and sportsmen warned about when the Polis administration pushed wolf reintroduction over the objections of ranchers and hunters: wolves are now roaming the same terrain as coyotes, and the legal exposure for honest mistakes falls on law-abiding gun owners and hunters — not on the bureaucrats who put the wolves there.

---

## Colorado Trooper Airlifted After I-70 Crash Near Eisenhower Tunnel

A Colorado State Patrol trooper was seriously injured and airlifted after a four-vehicle crash on I-70 near the Eisenhower Tunnel Monday morning. The trooper is expected to recover. I-70 through the mountains remains one of the most dangerous corridors in the state, and the men and women of the Colorado State Patrol run toward those accidents while everyone else is pulling over. Coloradans should keep this trooper and the CSP in their thoughts — and their legislators should be funding public safety, not raiding those budgets for progressive spending priorities.
