# FCC Kills Local TV Ownership Caps—Colorado Broadcasters Face Consolidation Wave

The FCC has voted to eliminate ownership limits on local television stations, a move that will reshape Colorado's media landscape and hand consolidation power to large broadcasters. The FCC chair argues the deregulation will strengthen local news operations, but critics—including some conservatives—worry it opens the door to media monopolies that could squeeze out independent voices and local coverage.

The vote comes as traditional TV news already struggles with cord-cutting and digital competition. Supporters say lifting caps allows stations to merge operations, cut costs, and invest in newsrooms. Opponents counter that consolidation typically means fewer newsrooms, less local accountability reporting, and homogenized content across markets.

For Colorado specifically, the ruling could accelerate deals among Denver, Boulder, and Springs broadcasters. Local station owners may face pressure to sell to larger chains—potentially reducing the number of independently operated newsrooms covering state politics, water rights, energy development, and local elections.

Conservatives are split: free-market advocates see deregulation as pro-growth; others worry that media consolidation undermines the competitive marketplace of ideas and local editorial independence that checks government power.

Watch whether Colorado broadcasters use newfound consolidation flexibility to strengthen or gut local news operations—and whether state legislators consider any guardrails.
