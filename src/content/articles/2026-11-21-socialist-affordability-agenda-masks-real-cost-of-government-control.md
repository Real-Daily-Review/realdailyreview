# Socialist 'Affordability' Push Proves Government Can't Deliver Lower Costs

New York City's socialist Mayor Zohran Mamdani is rebranding government mandates as "affordability"—a rhetorical sleight of hand that masks the real cost of progressive policy, according to conservative critics.

The National Review argues that Mamdani's affordability agenda conflates government control with lower prices, a fundamental contradiction. "Affordability and socialism are incompatible," the outlet notes, pointing to the mayor's pattern of banning businesses, imposing new fees on consumers, and then blaming companies for "demagoguing" the costs.

The Washington Examiner adds that Mamdani's approach—closing jails, dismantling elite police units, and expanding government mandates—ignores the actual trade-offs. While progressives frame these moves as helping ordinary people, conservatives argue they create chaos, undermine public safety, and ultimately make life more expensive and less free.

**The Colorado angle:** This matters for Coloradans watching similar progressive experiments locally. When Denver or state Democrats push "affordability" policies—whether rent control, energy mandates, or regulatory expansion—they're following the same playbook. Conservatives warn that government-imposed "solutions" consistently fail to lower costs; they shift burdens, reduce choice, and expand bureaucracy.
