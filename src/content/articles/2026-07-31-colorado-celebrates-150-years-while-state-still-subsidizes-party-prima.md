# Colorado's 150th Birthday Weekend Draws Crowds—But State Still Subsidizes Party Politics

Colorado celebrates its sesquicentennial this weekend with harvest festivals, rodeos, and concerts across the state, marking Saturday as the official Colorado Day (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). The summer festival season is in full swing, drawing families statewide.

Yet while Coloradans celebrate their heritage, state government continues a practice conservatives say undermines fiscal responsibility: taxpayer-funded primary elections for political parties. Complete Colorado argues that forcing Colorado taxpayers to finance primaries for parties they don't belong to amounts to government overreach and misuse of public resources.

The conservative case is straightforward: if Democrats, Republicans, and third parties want to nominate candidates, they should fund their own primaries—not ask Colorado taxpayers to foot the bill. This isn't about partisan advantage; it's about the principle that government shouldn't compel citizens to finance political machinery they may oppose. Complete Colorado has called separating state from party a "courageous" move, questioning why Colorado government grants special recognition and privileges to political parties at all.

As Colorado marks 150 years of statehood, taxpayers should watch whether legislators take up primary-funding reform—or continue writing checks for party politics.
