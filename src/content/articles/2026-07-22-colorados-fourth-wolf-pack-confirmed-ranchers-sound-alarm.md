# Colorado Wolf Population Expands Again—Ranchers Brace for Conflict

Colorado Parks and Wildlife confirmed another established wolf pack this week, marking the fourth pack in the state this year, each with an estimated four to six pups, according to the Colorado Sun. The expansion comes as CPW continues managing the reintroduction program that has drawn sharp criticism from rural communities and livestock operators.

Republicans and ranchers argue that accelerating wolf populations threaten cattle herds, sheep operations, and rural livelihoods across Colorado's Western Slope and mountain counties. They contend that CPW's management prioritizes environmental ideology over the economic survival of family farms and ranches—and that taxpayers, not predator advocates, ultimately bear the cost of livestock losses and increased wildlife management spending.

Democrats and environmental groups counter that wolves are essential to ecosystem health and that compensation programs and non-lethal deterrents can coexist with ranching. They point to successful reintroduction efforts in other states.

The real tension: Colorado voters approved wolf reintroduction in 2020, but implementation has sparked genuine conflict between conservation mandates and rural economic interests. CPW's ability to balance both will define whether this experiment succeeds or deepens the urban-rural divide.

Watch whether the state legislature moves to expand or restrict wolf protections—and whether ranchers push for stronger predator-control authority.
