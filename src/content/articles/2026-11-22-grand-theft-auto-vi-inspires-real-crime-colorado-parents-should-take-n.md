# Grand Theft Auto VI: When Virtual Crime Becomes Real Problem

Colorado parents should pay attention to what their kids are playing—because a new video game is already inspiring real-world crime overseas.

Two Italian minors allegedly stole a bus and vandalized others at a depot while attempting to "imitate" *Grand Theft Auto VI*, according to Breitbart. The incident underscores a persistent concern conservatives have raised about violent video games: they don't exist in a vacuum.

National Review has published sharp critiques of the franchise, questioning whether spending hours committing simulated crimes—stealing vehicles, evading police, causing mayhem in a digital environment—is a worthwhile use of finite time on earth. The magazine's writers argue the "fantasy" at the game's core normalizes criminality and desensitizes players to consequences.

The Left typically dismisses such concerns as moral panic, insisting games don't cause real violence and that millions play without incident. That's partly true—but the Italy case shows the risk isn't theoretical. When a game explicitly rewards criminal behavior and makes it entertaining, some young people will test those boundaries in reality.

Colorado law enforcement and parents should remain vigilant about *GTA VI*'s release and monitor whether copycat incidents emerge locally, particularly among minors already prone to poor judgment.
