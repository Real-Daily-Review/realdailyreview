# 'Fortress North America' Trade Scheme Masks Corporate Interests, Conservatives Warn

A resurgent push for "Fortress North America"—a trade framework pitched by cross-border business groups—is drawing fire from conservatives as a backdoor deal that prioritizes corporate interests over American sovereignty and workers.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, trade groups operating on both sides of the Canadian border are repackaging the concept with language designed to appeal to American interests. But conservatives argue the framing obscures what amounts to deeper continental integration that could undermine U.S. manufacturing, energy independence, and labor protections.

The scheme matters for Colorado. Any trade architecture affecting North America directly impacts Colorado's energy sector, agricultural exports, and manufacturing base. Conservatives worry that "Fortress North America" language—suggesting a unified trading bloc—could lock in regulatory harmonization that favors multinational corporations over domestic producers and workers, particularly in energy and agriculture where Colorado has significant stakes.

Republicans argue such arrangements historically benefit coastal elites and multinational firms while hollowing out middle-class jobs in interior states. They contend America should negotiate trade deals that protect domestic industries and workers, not surrender leverage to continental bureaucracies.

Watch for how Colorado's congressional delegation responds to any formal "Fortress North America" proposal—and whether they prioritize Colorado workers and energy independence over corporate trade lobbies.
