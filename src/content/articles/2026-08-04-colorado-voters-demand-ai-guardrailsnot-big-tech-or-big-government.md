# Colorado Voters Skeptical of AI Regulation—and Rightly So

Colorado voters in the competitive 8th Congressional District are expressing deep concerns about artificial intelligence, and they're right to demand answers before Washington and Silicon Valley run roughshod over the issue (Denver Post).

According to polling cited by the Denver Post, voters across party lines worry that tech companies cannot be trusted to self-regulate AI. Steven Kull of the Program for Public Consultation noted that while voters find AI "intriguing," they harbor "a lot of anxieties" about its unchecked development.

Here's the conservative concern: Democrats will use AI anxiety as cover for heavy-handed federal regulation that stifles innovation, kills jobs, and hands bureaucrats power over the tech sector. Republicans should counter with a market-driven approach—transparency requirements and liability rules, yes, but not the kind of government micromanagement that turns Colorado's growing tech workforce into a regulatory minefield.

The 8th District polling matters because it's a bellwether. If Colorado voters—traditionally skeptical of government overreach—are demanding guardrails on AI, national Republicans need a credible alternative to the left's regulatory playbook, not just opposition.

Watch whether Colorado Republicans running in 2026 offer a coherent, pro-innovation AI policy that protects consumers without empowering the federal bureaucracy.
