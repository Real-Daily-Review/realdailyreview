# Colorado Libertarian's Ballot Maneuver Raises Questions About Election Integrity

A Denver judge has ordered the placement of a new Libertarian candidate for Colorado secretary of state on the November ballot, following a legal dispute that highlights how ballot access rules can create unintended consequences (Denver Post). The decision comes after Alex Astley, the original Libertarian nominee, withdrew from the race—then the party attempted to replace him, only to face a court challenge.

Astley denies timing his departure to block the Libertarian Party from fielding a replacement candidate or to benefit Republican James Wiley, according to the Denver Post. But the legal outcome raises conservative concerns about ballot access and whether Colorado's election rules adequately protect party autonomy and voter choice.

The case underscores a broader tension: Colorado's ballot access laws, while designed to ensure voter options, can create perverse incentives where candidate withdrawals trigger litigation rather than smooth party substitutions. For Colorado taxpayers and election observers, the question is whether the state's Secretary of State's office—currently held by Democrat Jena Griswold—is administering these rules consistently and fairly.

Republicans and conservatives should monitor whether Colorado's election bureaucracy continues to create barriers to ballot access for non-establishment candidates, or whether clearer rules emerge from this dispute.
