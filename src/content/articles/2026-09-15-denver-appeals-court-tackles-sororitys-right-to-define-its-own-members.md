# Federal Court in Denver Takes Up Kappa Kappa Gamma Transgender Membership Fight

A Denver federal appeals court is now the battleground for a clash between free association and progressive gender ideology—and Colorado's at the center of it.

The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals heard arguments Monday in a case challenging Kappa Kappa Gamma's membership policies, specifically whether the sorority can set its own standards or must bow to federal pressure to admit transgender members (KDVR Fox31 Denver).

**The conservative case is straightforward:** Private organizations have the right to set their own membership rules without government interference. Forcing a women's sorority to accept members based on gender identity—rather than biological sex—strips the group of its fundamental right to associate freely and define its own purpose. Republicans and civil liberties conservatives argue this is government overreach dressed up as civil rights enforcement.

**The left counters** that anti-discrimination law supersedes private preference, framing exclusion as unlawful discrimination.

This case matters for Colorado because it will likely set precedent affecting how private clubs, organizations, and businesses across the state navigate federal pressure on gender and sex-based policies. The ruling could reshape what "membership" and "association" actually mean in America.

Watch whether the Tenth Circuit upholds organizational autonomy or expands federal power to dictate private group membership—a decision that will ripple through Colorado nonprofits, clubs, and faith communities for years.
