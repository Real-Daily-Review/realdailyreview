# Wildfire Destroys Critical Colorado Water Infrastructure—Beulah Residents Face Long Recovery

The Aspen Acres fire has dealt a direct hit to Beulah's water systems, forcing local managers into damage-control mode while a community of 800 grapples with lost homes and evacuations. Unlike most active Colorado fires this season, this one didn't miss—it struck the utilities that keep a rural mountain town functioning.

Water officials are now scrambling to restore clean water delivery to residents, but recovery will take months, not weeks (Colorado Sun). The fire's assault on critical infrastructure underscores a hard reality: Colorado's water systems remain vulnerable to wildfire damage, and rural communities often lack the resources to rebuild quickly.

For Beulah residents, the immediate crisis is survival and shelter. For Colorado taxpayers statewide, the longer question looms: Who pays for rebuilding water infrastructure destroyed by fire? State and federal disaster relief typically covers some costs, but gaps remain. Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that Colorado needs smarter forest management—thinning overgrown timber and clearing dead wood—to prevent catastrophic fires in the first place, rather than perpetually funding post-disaster cleanup.

The state's water managers will need to coordinate with county and federal agencies on funding and timelines for repairs.

Watch whether state legislators push for increased wildfire prevention funding or rely on federal disaster dollars to fill the gap.
