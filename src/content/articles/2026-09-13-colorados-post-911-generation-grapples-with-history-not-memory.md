# A Generation Born After 9/11 Faces a Nation Still Grappling With Its Lessons

Twenty-five years after the September 11 attacks, Colorado students who never knew the pre-9/11 world are now confronting what was once America's defining national trauma—but without the visceral memory that shaped their parents' worldview.

According to CPR News, younger Coloradans are engaging with 9/11 through education and historical reflection rather than lived experience. The attacks fundamentally altered American counterterrorism strategy, national security policy, and public consciousness in ways that continue to reverberate through schools and civic institutions across the state.

Yet conservatives argue the broader ideological threat persists. National Review contends that while the immediate terror threat has evolved, the underlying ideological challenge—what it calls "Sharia supremacism"—remains a serious long-term concern that Americans cannot afford to forget or minimize.

For Colorado, this generational divide carries real implications: younger voters and citizens may lack the security-minded perspective that shaped post-9/11 policy on defense spending, intelligence operations, and border security—issues that directly affect federal resources flowing to Colorado military installations and defense contractors.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's schools are teaching a clear-eyed, historically accurate account of 9/11's causes and consequences, or whether progressive educators are downplaying the ideological roots of the threat.
