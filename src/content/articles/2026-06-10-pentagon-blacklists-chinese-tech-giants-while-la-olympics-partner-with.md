# Pentagon Blacklists Chinese Tech Giants Over Military Ties—While L.A. Olympics Partner With Them

The Pentagon has added Chinese tech behemoth Alibaba, EV maker BYD, and search engine Baidu to its list of companies with direct links to China's military, barring them from U.S. defense contracts (Breitbart). The move reflects growing national security concerns about Beijing's control over nominally private firms.

Yet the timing exposes a glaring contradiction: Alibaba is a key partner for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics (Just The News), meaning American taxpayers and Olympic organizers are funneling resources to a company the Pentagon itself deems a national security threat.

Republicans and defense hawks argue the blacklist doesn't go far enough if federal agencies and Olympic committees continue doing business with the same entities. The Pentagon's action signals serious concerns about technology transfer and espionage risks—concerns that should apply across all government spending, not just defense procurement.

Colorado's tech and energy sectors should note this precedent: if Chinese military-linked firms are off-limits for Pentagon work, similar scrutiny should apply to state contracts, renewable energy supply chains, and critical infrastructure partnerships. The contradiction between defense policy and Olympic partnerships underscores how fragmented U.S. China policy remains.

Watch whether Congress demands the Olympic Committee sever ties with Alibaba or face federal funding consequences.
