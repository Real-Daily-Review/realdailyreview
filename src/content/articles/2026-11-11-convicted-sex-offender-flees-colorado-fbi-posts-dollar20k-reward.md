# Convicted Colorado Sex Offender Flees Before Sentencing; FBI Posts $20K Reward

A convicted sexual predator has vanished from Colorado's justice system, exposing a dangerous gap in court supervision that should alarm every parent in the state.

Jose Del Carmen Garcia Martinez was convicted in March of sexually assaulting a 15-year-old girl with disabilities—then simply failed to appear for sentencing. The FBI has now placed him on its most wanted list and is offering a $20,000 reward for his arrest (KDVR Fox31).

This isn't a minor administrative slip. A man found guilty of one of the most heinous crimes imaginable walked free before facing consequences. The case raises hard questions about Colorado's bail system, pretrial monitoring, and whether judges are adequately protecting public safety over defendant convenience.

Conservatives have long warned that soft-on-crime policies and inadequate bail conditions endanger communities. This fugitive represents exactly what happens when the system prioritizes process over protection. Colorado taxpayers deserve answers: How did this happen? What safeguards failed? Why wasn't Garcia Martinez held in custody pending sentencing?

The FBI's involvement signals the severity, but locals shouldn't wait for federal agents to do state-level work (9News Denver).

**Watch whether Colorado's judiciary and law enforcement conduct a transparent review of this case and tighten pretrial supervision protocols—or quietly move on.**
