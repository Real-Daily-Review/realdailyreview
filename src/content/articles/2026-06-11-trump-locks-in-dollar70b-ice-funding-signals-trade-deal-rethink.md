# Trump Secures ICE Funding, Eyes Trade Deal Overhaul

President Trump signed a $70 billion homeland security bill this week, locking in funding for ICE and CBP through the remainder of his term—a move that settles uncertainty over potential agency shutdowns and signals his administration's commitment to border enforcement priorities.

The measure narrowly cleared the House before reaching Trump's desk (Fox News). The funding ensures immigration enforcement operations can proceed without the budgetary cliffhangers that plagued previous administrations, giving ICE and Customs and Border Protection the resources to sustain deportation operations and border security initiatives.

Separately, Trump is reconsidering his signature trade deal, with reports suggesting he may pursue renegotiation or termination (Daily Caller). "We don't need anything Canada has," Trump reportedly said, signaling potential friction with North American trade arrangements.

**Colorado angle:** Border enforcement funding directly impacts Colorado's role in immigration enforcement coordination and federal law enforcement partnerships. Trade policy shifts could affect Colorado agriculture, energy exports, and supply chains tied to cross-border commerce.

Republicans argue sustained ICE funding is essential to enforcing immigration law and protecting border security. Democrats typically oppose aggressive deportation operations, framing them as inhumane and economically disruptive.

**Watch next:** Whether Trump follows through on trade deal termination and how renegotiation might reshape Colorado's agricultural and energy export markets.
