# Inflation Stuck at 3.4%—Colorado Voters Should Demand Answers on Spending

Inflation remains stubbornly high at 3.4% year-over-year, unchanged from July, according to August Commerce Department data reported by the *Washington Examiner*. For Colorado families already squeezed by rising grocery, energy, and housing costs, the stalled progress is a reminder that Washington's spending addiction shows no signs of stopping.

Republicans argue that unchecked government spending—particularly on progressive priorities like climate mandates and regulatory expansion—continues to fuel inflation. They contend that until Democrats and the Biden administration rein in federal expenditures and stop weaponizing the regulatory state against energy producers and small businesses, price relief for Colorado workers and retirees will remain out of reach.

The Left counters that inflation is a global phenomenon tied to supply-chain disruptions and energy markets, not domestic policy. They claim their spending addresses long-term economic competitiveness.

For Colorado, this matters acutely. Energy workers face regulatory headwinds that compound inflation's bite. Ranchers and agricultural producers absorb higher input costs. And middle-class Coloradans watching their paychecks lose purchasing power month after month have little patience for Washington's excuses.

**Watch for how Colorado Republicans frame inflation in the midterm messaging—and whether they tie it directly to state-level spending and regulatory overreach.**
