# Colorado Voters Reject Judges, Approve Tax Hikes in Election Night Reckoning

Colorado's 2026 election delivered a mixed verdict: voters ousted three state judges in retention elections while approving what conservatives warn are unsustainable tax increases that will burden Colorado families and businesses.

According to Complete Colorado, three judges failed retention after performance evaluations, with a fourth resigning ahead of the vote—a rare rebuke of the bench. The judicial rejections signal voter frustration with the courts, though specifics on judicial records remain limited.

More troubling to fiscal conservatives: Colorado approved the "Big Three" tax hikes (Complete Colorado). Conservatives argue the new revenue does not substitute for difficult budget choices, meaningful oversight, or fiscal restraint—and that lawmakers will simply spend more rather than address structural spending problems.

Complete Colorado also flagged dysfunction in Colorado's election rules, calling them "substantially undemocratic and extremely stupid" in coverage of the governor's race, suggesting voting procedures created confusion and undermined confidence in outcomes.

The election left Colorado "poorer and bluer," as one outlet put it—a stark reminder that progressive policies continue gaining ground in the state despite Republican warnings about tax burden and government overreach.
