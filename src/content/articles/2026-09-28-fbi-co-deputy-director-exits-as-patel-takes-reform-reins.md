# FBI Co-Deputy Director Exits as Patel Takes Reform Helm

The FBI's Co-Deputy Director Andrew Bailey has resigned after roughly a year in the newly created post, citing family reasons and expressing confidence that incoming FBI Director Kash Patel will continue the agency's reform agenda.

Bailey joined the FBI in September 2025 alongside then-Deputy Director Dan Bongino, who departed earlier this year (Just The News). The dual-leadership structure was designed to accelerate internal reform and restore public trust in an agency conservatives have long criticized for perceived political bias and institutional overreach.

Bailey's exit comes as Patel, a Trump loyalist and vocal FBI critic, assumes the director's chair. Conservatives have pointed to the bureau's handling of January 6th investigations, the Hillary Clinton email probe, and alleged surveillance abuses as evidence of deep institutional problems requiring top-to-bottom reform.

Republicans argue the FBI needs leadership willing to depoliticize the agency and hold career officials accountable for past misconduct. Democrats counter that aggressive leadership changes risk undermining the bureau's independence and morale.

For Colorado, the FBI's direction matters: the agency oversees field offices handling everything from election security to public lands disputes and gun trafficking investigations that affect state law enforcement partnerships.

Watch whether Patel's tenure produces concrete disciplinary action against officials involved in past controversies—or whether institutional resistance stalls reform efforts.
