# Trump Hosts Xi at White House State Dinner—But Where's the Beef?

President Trump rolled out the diplomatic red carpet Thursday for Chinese President Xi Jinping, but the White House menu tells an unexpected story: a pescatarian spread featuring sesame-crusted sea bass and honey dessert—notably absent of American beef, a staple of traditional state dinners (Fox News Politics).

The guest list read like a who's who of Trump allies and media figures, including House Speaker Mike Johnson, Elon Musk, Trump's daughters Ivanka and Tiffany, and Fox News hosts Bret Baier, Jesse Waters, and Laura Ingraham (Just The News). Trump gifted Xi an ornate bald eagle statue, symbolizing American strength during the carefully choreographed evening in the East Room (Washington Examiner).

The dinner's timing matters: Trump and Xi are working to settle trade differences while warming U.S.-China relations. For Colorado, this matters. American beef producers—including Colorado ranchers—have long faced Chinese tariffs and market access barriers. The absence of beef from a high-profile state dinner raises questions about whether trade negotiations are already tilting toward Beijing's preferences, or whether the menu simply reflected diplomatic caution.

Conservatives should watch whether this dinner translates into concrete wins on trade and intellectual property protection—or becomes another example of American concessions dressed up as diplomacy.
