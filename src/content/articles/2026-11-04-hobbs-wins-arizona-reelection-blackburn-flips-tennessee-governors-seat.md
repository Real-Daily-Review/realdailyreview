# Arizona Loses Ground as Democrat Hobbs Cruises to Reelection

Arizona's conservative movement suffered a setback Tuesday as Gov. Katie Hobbs (D-AZ) secured reelection with 56.3% of the vote, defeating Republican challenger Andy Biggs's 42%, according to the Washington Examiner. With 75% of ballots counted, Hobbs clinched a second term in Phoenix—a significant win for Democrats in a state Republicans had hoped to flip.

The result underscores the challenge Republicans face in purple-state gubernatorial races, particularly where Democratic incumbents have consolidated support. Conservatives had viewed Biggs, a hardline Republican, as a strong challenger, but Hobbs's margin suggests Arizona voters—or at least those who turned out—backed her record.

Meanwhile, Republicans notched a win in Tennessee, where Sen. Marsha Blackburn won the governor's race with 45% of the vote against Democrat Jerri Green's 43%, per The Daily Wire. Independent David Hatley drew nearly 10% of ballots. Blackburn will succeed term-limited Gov. Bill Lee.

**The Colorado angle:** These races signal how competitive gubernatorial contests remain nationwide. Colorado Republicans should study both outcomes—Hobbs's strength in Arizona and Blackburn's narrow Tennessee victory—as they prepare for 2026 state races and assess whether conservative messaging on inflation, border security, and government overreach resonates with swing voters.

**Watch:** Whether Republicans can replicate Blackburn's formula in other purple states, or if Hobbs's Arizona victory signals a Democratic surge heading into 2026.
