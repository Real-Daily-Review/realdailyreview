# DOJ Targets California Over Noncitizen Voter Mail Ballots—Colorado Should Demand Same Scrutiny

The Justice Department is threatening legal action against California after discovering noncitizens received mail-in ballots ahead of the November election, according to the Washington Examiner and 9News Denver. The DOJ sent formal letters to California Secretary of State Shirley Weber and Los Angeles County officials demanding they review lists of "likely noncitizens" who were sent ballots.

This is exactly the kind of election integrity crisis conservatives have warned about for years. Democrats have repeatedly dismissed voter roll vulnerabilities as overblown, but the DOJ's action proves the threat is real and documented.

**Republicans argue** that California's lax voter registration standards and mail-ballot practices create obvious opportunities for noncitizens to vote—whether intentionally or through administrative failure. They're demanding similar audits nationwide.

**Democrats counter** that isolated cases don't represent systemic fraud and that aggressive voter roll reviews risk disenfranchising eligible citizens.

For Colorado, this raises urgent questions: Are noncitizens on our voter rolls? Has Colorado's mail-ballot system created similar vulnerabilities? Secretary of State Jena Griswold should immediately audit Colorado's voter registration database and report findings publicly—or explain why she won't.

Watch whether Colorado Democrats demand the same transparency California now faces, or whether they resist voter roll audits in their own state.
