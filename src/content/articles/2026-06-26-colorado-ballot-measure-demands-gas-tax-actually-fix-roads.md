# Colorado Voters Get Shot at Forcing State to Actually Spend Gas Tax on Roads

Colorado's November ballot will include a measure to guarantee that motor vehicle and gas tax revenue stays dedicated to highway repairs—a direct rebuke of state government's habit of raiding transportation funds for other priorities (Denver Post, Complete Colorado).

The "Restore Our Roads" initiative would require state agencies to spend all gas and motor vehicle tax revenue exclusively on road maintenance and construction. Coloradans have watched their gas taxes climb while pothole-ridden highways deteriorate, with lawmakers routinely diverting transportation dollars to general fund spending.

Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue the measure is essential accountability. Voters approved gas taxes with the explicit understanding the money would fix roads—not fund bureaucratic overhead or pet projects. When government breaks that compact, taxpayers lose faith in both the tax system and elected officials.

Democrats haven't mounted a visible public opposition campaign, though some worry the measure could constrain budget flexibility during economic downturns or limit funding for transit initiatives they favor.

The measure reflects broader Colorado frustration: citizens are taxed heavily, yet infrastructure crumbles while state spending balloons elsewhere. It's a test of whether ballot initiatives can actually force government to honor its promises.

Watch whether the state legislature or governor's office launches a late campaign against the measure before November.
