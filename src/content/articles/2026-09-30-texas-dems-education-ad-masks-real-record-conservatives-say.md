# Texas Democrat's Education Ad Masks Real Record, Critics Say

A Texas Democratic Senate candidate is facing conservative scrutiny over misleading campaign messaging on education. James Talarico's Spanish-language education ad features students from a low-income school where he once taught, but *The Daily Wire* reports the spot obscures a "$27,000 detail"—suggesting the portrayal doesn't match his actual teaching record or the school's circumstances.

*National Review* goes further, arguing Talarico wraps progressive Democratic platform positions in Christian language, claiming Jesus conveniently aligns with 2026 Democratic planks on issues like education spending and social programs. The outlet frames this as rhetorical sleight of hand: using faith messaging to obscure left-wing policy.

Republicans argue the ad exemplifies how Democrats use emotional imagery and selective framing to dodge scrutiny of their actual records and spending priorities. Conservatives say voters deserve clarity on whether candidates' stated values match their policy proposals and past performance.

While this race is in Texas, Colorado conservatives should note the playbook: progressive candidates nationwide increasingly blend faith-based messaging with big-government spending proposals. Watch how Colorado Democratic candidates frame education and social spending in 2026—and demand specifics on costs and results rather than feel-good rhetoric.
