# mRNA Flu Vaccine Shows Higher Injury Rate; Conservative Critics Question FDA Oversight

Colorado families should know: the FDA just approved Moderna's first mRNA flu vaccine despite data showing it causes injury at rates significantly higher than traditional flu shots, raising fresh questions about pharmaceutical company influence at the agency.

According to *Just The News*, Moderna's own research indicates the new jab requires roughly 5,000 doses to prevent one hospitalization compared to standard vaccines—a stark efficiency gap. Critics argue the approval signals weakened FDA standards. "Pharma finished recapturing" the agency after key officials departed, one drug industry journalist told *Just The News*, suggesting the revolving door between industry and regulators is spinning again.

*National Review* counters that dismantling the Vaccine Injury Compensation Program—which has compensated patients for decades—would be reckless, arguing the system itself should be strengthened rather than scrapped. But conservatives worry the real issue isn't the compensation program; it's whether the FDA is adequately vetting new vaccines before they reach market.

For Colorado taxpayers and families, the concern cuts deeper: if federal regulators are rubber-stamping pharmaceutical products with questionable safety profiles, who's protecting public health? The state's health bureaucracy often follows federal lead.
