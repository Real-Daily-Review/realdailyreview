# Supreme Court Faces Dueling Overreach in Trump Cases

The Supreme Court is wading into two high-stakes Trump disputes that pit executive authority against judicial overreach—and neither side comes out looking restrained.

In the first case, the Court is examining Trump's mail-in ballot order against a district judge's sweeping countermand, according to *National Review*. Both moves stretch constitutional limits: Trump's directive and the lower court's response each represent the kind of executive and judicial power-grabbing that should concern anyone worried about separation of powers.

More directly, Trump is asking the justices to overturn the $83 million judgment in the E. Jean Carroll case, *Just The News* reports. Trump's argument rests on the Court's 2024 presidential immunity decision, which granted presidents broad protection for official acts. The question now: does that immunity shield him from civil liability in this case?

**The conservative case:** Republicans argue the lower courts have weaponized the judiciary against Trump, while the immunity doctrine—properly applied—shouldn't expose presidents to endless civil suits for actions taken in office. Conservatives worry that without clear boundaries, future presidents face crippling litigation.

**What's at stake for Colorado:** While these are national cases, they set precedent for how courts treat executive power and presidential accountability—issues that ripple through federal land management, energy policy, and regulatory enforcement affecting Colorado's economy.

Watch whether the Court narrows Trump's immunity claim or expands it—either way signals how much judicial restraint remains.
