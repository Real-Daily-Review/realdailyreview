# White House Power Grab on Energy Shipping Could Spike Colorado Gas Prices

The Biden administration's push to impose tolls on oil tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz represents a dangerous overreach of executive authority—and threatens to drive up energy costs for Colorado families and businesses already hammered by inflation.

According to National Review, the President has no constitutional power to unilaterally impose tolls on international shipping lanes. Republicans argue such a move would exceed executive authority and invite retaliation against American commerce. The Strait of Hormuz remains critical infrastructure for global oil markets; any disruption ripples directly to Colorado's pump prices and heating costs.

Meanwhile, 9News Denver reports that oil tankers continue navigating the Strait despite escalating threats from Iran's Revolutionary Guard, with tensions spiking following renewed fighting between Israel and Iranian-backed Hezbollah in Lebanon. The Daily Wire notes Iran has issued fresh warnings, intensifying regional instability.

Conservatives warn that unilateral toll schemes—dressed up as foreign policy—are really backdoor price controls that destabilize energy markets without congressional approval. Colorado's energy sector, already under siege from state and federal regulations, cannot afford Washington's constitutional adventurism.

Watch whether Congress moves to explicitly block any toll authority the White House attempts to claim.
