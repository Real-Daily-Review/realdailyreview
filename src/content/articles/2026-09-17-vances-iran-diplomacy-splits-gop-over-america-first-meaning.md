# Vance's Iran Strategy Sparks Right-Wing Debate Over 'America First' Foreign Policy

Vice President JD Vance's willingness to engage Iran diplomatically is dividing conservatives over what "America First" actually means in practice.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, Vance's approach has drawn fire from GOP critics who worry he's too soft on Tehran and insufficiently committed to Israel. But supporters argue he's not betraying either—he's applying strategic restraint, not weakness. The debate reflects a larger fault line on the American Right: whether prioritizing U.S. interests means maintaining traditional alliances or rethinking them entirely.

For Colorado conservatives, this matters. The state has significant defense and aerospace interests, and foreign policy uncertainty can ripple through defense contracting and energy markets. A coherent "America First" doctrine—whether Vance's version or another—will shape military spending, sanctions policy, and Middle East stability in ways that affect Colorado jobs and national security posture.

Republicans backing Vance argue that endless Middle East entanglements drain American resources better spent at home. Critics counter that abandoning Israel or appearing weak to Iran invites regional chaos and terrorism.
