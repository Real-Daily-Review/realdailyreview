# Data Centers Spark Conservative Divide Over Tax Incentives and Economic Development

Colorado and other states face a growing debate over whether to offer tax breaks for new data center projects—and conservatives are split. While National Review argues that rejecting such developments historically backfires economically, Republican Rep. Rich McCormick (R-GA) contends that data centers shouldn't receive "any tax breaks," even if the facilities themselves are beneficial (Breitbart).

The tension reflects a broader conservative disagreement: free-market advocates see data centers as engines of job creation and tax revenue, while fiscal hawks worry that corporate incentives represent crony capitalism and wasteful government spending. The Daily Wire notes data centers have become "the new culture war provocation," signaling how polarized the issue has become across the political spectrum.

For Colorado, this matters significantly. The state's energy grid and water resources face mounting pressure from data center expansion, while rural communities eye the economic upside. Tech companies seeking locations will shop aggressively for incentive packages—and Colorado lawmakers must decide whether to compete or hold the line on corporate welfare.

Conservatives should watch whether Colorado's legislature prioritizes fiscal discipline over recruitment, or whether economic pragmatism wins out.
