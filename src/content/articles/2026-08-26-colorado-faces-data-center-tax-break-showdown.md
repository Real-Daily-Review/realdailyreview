# Data Centers Spark Conservative Divide Over Tax Breaks and Development

Colorado policymakers face a growing debate over whether to sweeten the pot for data center development—and conservatives are split on the answer.

Data centers are increasingly attractive to states seeking economic growth and jobs, but the question of tax incentives has divided the right. National Review argues that rejecting new development rarely benefits communities historically, suggesting opposition to data centers is self-destructive (National Review). The Daily Wire notes data centers have become a cultural flashpoint, with some communities resisting the facilities despite their economic potential.

However, not all Republicans are on board with giveaways. Rep. Rich McCormick (R-GA) stated on NewsNation that while data centers are beneficial, "if you're negotiating any new data centers, I don't think you should give them any tax breaks" (Breitbart).

For Colorado, the stakes are real: data centers mean jobs, tax revenue, and energy demand—but also questions about water usage, grid strain, and whether taxpayer dollars should subsidize corporate expansion. The state has already seen interest from major tech firms, making this a live issue for local governments weighing incentive packages.

**Watch for:** Whether Colorado counties and municipalities resist or embrace data center tax breaks as the facilities race accelerates across the Mountain West.
