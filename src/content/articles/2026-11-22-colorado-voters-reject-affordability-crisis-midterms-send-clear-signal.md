# Colorado Voters Send Clear Message: Affordability Crisis Demands Action

Coloradans joined millions of Americans in the 2026 midterms to reject an agenda that has left families struggling. According to the *Washington Examiner*, voters across the country cited economic uncertainty, immigration, affordability, and concerns about the nation's direction as primary drivers of their choices at the ballot box.

For Colorado families, the math is brutal: groceries, housing, healthcare, and the cost of raising children have become unaffordable under progressive policies that prioritize government spending and regulatory expansion over economic growth. The state's cost of living has climbed faster than wages, pricing out working families from homeownership and forcing difficult choices between essentials.

Republicans argue that the solution lies in restraining government spending, cutting unnecessary regulations that drive up business costs, and unleashing free-market growth—not more government programs or tax hikes. Conservatives point to inflation driven by federal overspending and supply-chain disruptions caused by overregulation as the root causes of the affordability crisis.

Democrats, by contrast, continue pushing for expanded social programs and higher taxes on businesses and the wealthy, claiming these investments will eventually lower costs—a claim voters have increasingly rejected.

The takeaway for Colorado taxpayers: watch whether state lawmakers in Denver heed the midterm message or double down on the spending and regulatory policies that have made Colorado less affordable.
