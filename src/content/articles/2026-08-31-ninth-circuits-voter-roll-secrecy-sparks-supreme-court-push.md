# Supreme Court Urged to Rein In Ninth Circuit's Voter Roll Secrecy Ruling

The Ninth Circuit's decision to keep voter rolls hidden from public scrutiny is exactly the kind of judicial overreach that erodes election integrity—and conservatives are pushing the Supreme Court to correct it.

Election integrity advocates, including the Honest Elections Project, are urging the nation's highest court to take up a voter roll case after the Ninth Circuit ruled that voter rolls are exempt from public disclosure. According to Jason Snead, the group's executive director, the lower court's logic is backwards: it claimed voter rolls are "not a record related to list maintenance, but the result of list maintenance activity," thereby justifying secrecy (Just The News).

Republicans and conservatives argue that voter roll transparency is essential to detecting fraud, maintaining accurate registration databases, and ensuring election integrity. Keeping those records hidden from public view—and from election observers—undermines accountability and invites questions about why officials would resist scrutiny.

National Review notes that Supreme Court justices have signaled fidelity to their judicial role, suggesting they will respond decisively if executive overreach occurs (National Review). The implication: transparency in voter administration is foundational to that restraint.

Colorado election officials and taxpayers should watch whether the Supreme Court accepts this case and how it rules on public access to voter roll data.
