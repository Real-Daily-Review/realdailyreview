# Maine Democrats' Hasty Replacement Exposes Party Chaos—and Bernie's Judgment Problem

Maine Democrats scrambled to crown Troy Jackson, a fifth-generation logger and former state Senate president, as their replacement nominee after Graham Platner imploded under sexual assault allegations. The problem: Jackson now faces fresh scrutiny of his own, with a GOP group pledging $42 million against him and citing "volatile personality" and "anger issues with women" (Daily Caller). The race against incumbent Susan Collins is now ranked a toss-up (Fox News).

What's particularly damning is Bernie Sanders' refusal to walk back his aggressive support for Platner. When pressed on CBS's *Face the Nation*, Sanders said he didn't regret backing the accused rapist and gave Jackson an "excellent" chance (Washington Examiner, Breitbart). That's not leadership—that's ideological rigidity masquerading as principle.

For Colorado conservatives, this Maine chaos matters: it shows how Democratic vetting has collapsed when ideology trumps basic judgment. The party's willingness to cycle through damaged candidates rather than admit error reveals the rot at the establishment core.
