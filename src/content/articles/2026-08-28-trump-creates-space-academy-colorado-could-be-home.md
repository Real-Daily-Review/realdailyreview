# Trump Signs Space Academy Order; Colorado Eyes Potential Home

President Trump signed an executive order Friday establishing a U.S. Space Academy modeled after West Point to train the next generation of Space Force personnel and aerospace professionals (Fox News Politics, 9News Denver). Trump said the academy will maintain rigorous admission standards and a "beautiful" campus befitting a "top line" school (New York Post).

The location remains undecided—a detail that matters for Colorado, which already hosts the Air Force Academy in Colorado Springs and has emerged as a hub for space industry development and defense contracting. Colorado conservatives should view this as an opportunity: a Space Academy here would anchor the state's aerospace workforce, create high-skill jobs, and reinforce Colorado's role in national security infrastructure without relying on bloated federal bureaucracies.

The executive order signals the Trump administration's commitment to space dominance as a strategic priority, reflecting concerns that America must compete with China and Russia in orbital capabilities. This aligns with conservative support for robust defense spending when directed toward genuine national security needs rather than social programs.

The academy concept draws bipartisan interest in aerospace circles, though fiscal conservatives will want clarity on startup costs and long-term operational budgets before cheering.

**Watch for:** Which states compete hardest for the academy location—and whether Colorado's existing military-aerospace infrastructure gives it the edge.
