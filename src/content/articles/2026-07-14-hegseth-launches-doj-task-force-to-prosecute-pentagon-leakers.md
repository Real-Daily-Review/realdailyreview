# Pentagon Cracks Down on Leaks to Press—Hegseth Launches DOJ Task Force

Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth is finally putting teeth into enforcement against the federal bureaucrats who've spent years undermining elected leadership by leaking classified information to friendly journalists.

Hegseth announced a joint task force with the Department of Justice to identify and prosecute officials who leak sensitive information to the media, vowing to pursue violators "with the full force of the law," according to Fox News and the New York Post.

This move addresses a long-standing conservative complaint: unelected career officials routinely sabotage administration policy by selectively disclosing classified material to sympathetic reporters. The leaks often serve to embarrass leadership, undermine military readiness messaging, or advance a different policy agenda than the one the President has set.

Republicans have argued that such leaks represent a form of insubordination and breach of the oath these officials take to protect national security. The task force signals the administration intends to hold leakers accountable rather than tolerate the status quo of consequence-free disclosure.

For Colorado—home to major military installations including Fort Carson and the Air Force Academy—this enforcement effort could reshape how Pentagon information flows and reinforce chain-of-command discipline across the defense establishment.

Watch whether the task force actually secures convictions or whether bureaucratic resistance and media-sympathetic judges limit its reach.
