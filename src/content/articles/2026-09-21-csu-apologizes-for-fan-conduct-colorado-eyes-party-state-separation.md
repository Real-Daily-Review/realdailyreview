# Colorado State Apologizes for Fan Conduct at BYU Game

Colorado State University issued an apology Saturday night after fans in the stands directed derogatory chants at Brigham Young University during their football matchup, which BYU won 41-23 (9News Denver).

The incident underscores a broader concern about civility and institutional accountability at public universities. Colorado State's swift response—acknowledging the inappropriate behavior and apologizing to BYU—reflects the kind of direct action that should characterize how state institutions handle misconduct by their representatives, whether students or staff.

Meanwhile, Colorado conservatives are watching a separate state-level development with keen interest: the push to separate government from party politics (Complete Colorado). The principle is sound—Colorado government should grant no special recognition or privileges to any political party, regardless of affiliation. This "courageous" move, as some describe it, aligns with conservative values of limited government and equal treatment under law.

Both stories highlight accountability: one at the university level for fan behavior, the other at the state level for institutional neutrality. Colorado taxpayers should monitor whether CSU's apology translates into concrete policies preventing future incidents, and whether state officials genuinely commit to stripping partisan favoritism from government operations.
