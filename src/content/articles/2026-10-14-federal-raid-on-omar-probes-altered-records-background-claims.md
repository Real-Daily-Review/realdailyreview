# Federal Raid on Ilhan Omar Raises Questions About Document Integrity and Official Records

A federal raid on Rep. Ilhan Omar's (D-Minnesota) residence has reignited scrutiny over her official biographical records and potential document irregularities dating back to her 2018 election.

According to The Daily Wire, investigators are examining whether Omar or her staff altered her birthdate on official House records—changing her year of birth from 1981 to 1982 in 2019, shortly after she took office. The outlet also reports that Omar's father, Nur Omar Mohamed, held a high-ranking position in the military apparatus of the Said Barre regime, contrary to her public narrative that he fled the dictatorial government.

Republicans argue these discrepancies raise serious questions about the integrity of official congressional records and whether Omar's office engaged in document falsification. The Daily Caller reports conservatives expect additional consequences to follow the raid, signaling GOP confidence that more details will emerge.

Democrats have largely remained silent on the specifics, with some characterizing the investigation as politically motivated.

While Omar represents Minnesota, not Colorado, the case underscores broader conservative concerns about government accountability and the need for rigorous vetting of official records across all elected offices—a principle Colorado voters should demand from their own representatives.
