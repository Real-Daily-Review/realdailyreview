# Maine Mother's Religious Freedom Case Heads to Supreme Court

A Maine custody order that barred a mother from taking her daughter to church is now before the U.S. Supreme Court—a stark reminder that progressive courts are willing to restrict parental rights and religious liberty in the name of custody disputes.

Emily Bickford is challenging a judicial order that prohibited her from bringing her 13-year-old daughter to her church, arguing the restriction violates her constitutional rights to religious freedom and to direct her child's upbringing (The Daily Wire). The case has already cleared Maine's state supreme court and now seeks federal review.

**The conservative argument is straightforward:** Parents retain fundamental rights to raise their children according to their faith and values—a principle embedded in the First Amendment and centuries of American law. Courts should have an extremely high bar before stripping away parental religious authority, especially in custody matters where one parent's ideology shouldn't override another's constitutional protections.

The broader concern: if Maine courts can prohibit church attendance, what other parental decisions might activist judges second-guess? Home schooling? Dietary choices? Political speech?

This case underscores why judicial restraint and respect for parental rights matter—particularly as progressive jurisdictions increasingly use family law to impose their worldview on religious families.

Watch whether the Supreme Court accepts the petition and, if so, whether it reaffirms robust protections for parental religious liberty or signals deference to state custody orders.
