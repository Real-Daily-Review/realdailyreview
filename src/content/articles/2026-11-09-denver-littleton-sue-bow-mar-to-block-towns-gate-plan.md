# Denver, Littleton Sue Bow Mar Over Gated-Community Plan

Denver and Littleton are using the courts to block a small Colorado town from controlling access to its own entrances—a move that raises questions about property rights and municipal autonomy.

The City and County of Denver and the City of Littleton filed suit Monday against Bow Mar, a town of roughly 1,000 residents in Douglas County, over its plan to install gates at two entrances (9News Denver, KDVR Fox31). Denver and Littleton argue the gates will divert traffic onto side streets "not designed for higher traffic volumes," creating safety hazards in their jurisdictions.

**The conservative angle:** Bow Mar residents and property owners have a legitimate interest in controlling access to their community and managing traffic flow through their own town. Yet Denver and Littleton—larger municipalities with expansive regulatory reach—are using litigation to override local decisions. This exemplifies how bigger government entities often steamroll smaller communities over land-use and infrastructure choices that should remain local.

**What's at stake:** The lawsuit tests whether a town can gate its own entrances without interference from neighboring cities. Conservatives should watch whether courts side with local control or defer to the larger municipalities' traffic-management claims—a bellwether for property rights and municipal independence across Colorado.
