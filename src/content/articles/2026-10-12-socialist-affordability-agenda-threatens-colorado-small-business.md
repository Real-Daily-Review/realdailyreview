# Government-Run Grocery Stores? Colorado Small Businesses Should Fear the "Affordability" Trap

Progressive politicians are dusting off the socialist playbook and rebranding it as "affordability"—and Colorado's small business owners should pay close attention to what's happening in New York City.

According to the *Washington Examiner*, small business owners nationwide face a looming threat of intrusive government involvement and unfair competitive practices. The left's latest experiment: city-run grocery stores, pitched as a solution to high food costs. But conservatives argue this is government overreach dressed up in compassionate language.

Here's the problem for Colorado: when government enters the marketplace as a competitor, it doesn't play by the same rules. Taxpayer-funded operations can undercut private businesses through subsidies and regulatory favoritism. Meanwhile, local grocers—already squeezed by supply chain costs and labor regulations—face an uneven playing field.

Republicans and conservatives warn that this "affordability" agenda masks a fundamental hostility toward free enterprise. Rather than letting markets work and reducing regulatory burdens that drive up costs, progressives choose government control. It's the same failed model that's strangled small businesses in blue cities for decades.

Colorado voters should watch closely whether similar proposals gain traction at the state or local level—and demand that lawmakers choose market freedom over government competition.
