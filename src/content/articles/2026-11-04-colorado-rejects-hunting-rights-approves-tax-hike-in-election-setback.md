# Colorado Voters Reject Hunting Rights Amendment, Approve Tax Hike

Colorado voters dealt a one-two punch to conservative priorities this week: rejecting a constitutional amendment to protect hunting as a fundamental right while approving a higher income tax that critics warn will accelerate the state's economic decline.

Amendment 83, which would have enshrined hunting protections in Colorado's constitution, failed to garner sufficient support Tuesday night. The measure represented a direct response to years of regulatory pressure from environmental groups seeking to restrict hunting seasons and access to public lands—a core concern for Colorado's ranching and outdoor communities.

Meanwhile, Amendment 87 passed, imposing higher income taxes on Colorado residents. According to Complete Colorado, the tax hike "won't fix Colorado" but will "only accelerate its decline." Republicans and fiscal conservatives argue that raising taxes on earners and businesses during an economic slowdown punishes job creators and pushes productive residents and employers toward lower-tax states like Texas and Wyoming.

The dual defeats underscore a troubling trend: Colorado's electorate increasingly favors progressive taxation and environmental restrictions over property rights and economic freedom. Hunting advocates and taxpayers should watch closely whether the legislature moves to tighten hunting regulations further now that constitutional protection has failed—and whether the new tax revenue actually delivers promised services or simply funds government expansion.
