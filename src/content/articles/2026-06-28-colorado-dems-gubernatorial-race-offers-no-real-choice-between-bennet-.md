# Colorado Democrats' Gubernatorial Race Offers Voters Little Real Choice

Colorado Democrats are running what amounts to a single candidate split into two bodies in their 2026 gubernatorial primary, according to *Colorado Sun* cartoonist Jim Morrissey, who notes that U.S. Senator Michael Bennet and Attorney General Phil Weiser hold strikingly similar positions on the major issues facing the state.

The observation underscores a broader problem for Colorado voters: when the dominant party in a state offers no meaningful ideological differentiation, taxpayers and business owners lose the chance to hear genuine debate about spending restraint, regulatory reform, or economic growth strategies. Republicans argue that Democratic consensus on progressive taxation, environmental regulation, and government expansion leaves no room for voices advocating lower taxes, streamlined permitting for energy development, or protection of property rights—issues that directly affect Colorado's working families and energy sector.

Neither Bennet nor Weiser has offered a compelling case for why Colorado needs different leadership on water management, education funding efficiency, or the state's fiscal trajectory. Conservatives say voters deserve a real choice, not a coronation dressed up as a primary.

Watch for whether Colorado Republicans can capitalize on Democratic unity-by-sameness by fielding a candidate who actually challenges the progressive consensus on taxes and regulation.
