# Trump Cabinet Members Face Legal, Security Threats as Left Escalates Pressure

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has relocated to a military base after Iranian officials reportedly escalated assassination threats to an advanced stage, *Fox News* reports—a stark reminder that Trump's team faces genuine security risks even as the administration takes office.

Meanwhile, Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins is battling a Biden-appointed federal judge in California who issued a preliminary injunction Thursday claiming her Easter and Christmas emails to USDA staff violated the Constitution. Judge Trina L. Thompson ruled that Rollins likely breached the Establishment Clause by sending messages including "He is risen" and holiday greetings—a decision conservatives argue represents weaponized judicial overreach against religious expression.

Republicans counter that the ruling is absurd: sending seasonal greetings is not unconstitutional coercion, and the judge's logic would criminalize basic workplace communication. The injunction signals how far progressive judges will go to harass Trump appointees over trivial matters while ignoring genuine constitutional violations from the left.

For Colorado taxpayers and business owners, this pattern matters. If federal judges can sue Cabinet secretaries over holiday emails, what's next for state and local officials? The precedent threatens free speech and religious liberty across the board.

Watch whether this injunction survives appeal—and whether similar lawfare targets other Trump officials in the coming months.
