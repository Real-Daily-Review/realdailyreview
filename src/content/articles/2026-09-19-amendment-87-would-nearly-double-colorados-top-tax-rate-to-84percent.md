# Colorado Faces Tax Hike Disguised as 'Fair Share' on High Earners

Colorado voters are being asked to approve Amendment 87, a progressive income tax that would nearly double the tax rate on high earners—including Denver Broncos players—from 4.4% to 8.4%. Proponents frame it as a "fair share" measure, but conservatives warn it's a dangerous experiment that will drive talent and investment out of state.

Complete Colorado notes the amendment targets anyone earning above a threshold, meaning star athletes and business owners would face an 8.4% state tax—among the nation's highest. Republicans argue Colorado should learn from New Jersey and other states that implemented progressive taxes and watched residents and businesses flee, eroding the tax base and ultimately hurting working families through reduced job growth and services.

The amendment also raises a separate election-integrity question. The Denver Post reported that three fraudulent ballots were counted in 2024 before being caught, though Colorado's signature verification and ballot checks ultimately caught the fraud. Amendment 84, which would require Social Security numbers for voter registration, aims to tighten those safeguards further—a move conservatives support as essential protection against election manipulation.

Conservatives argue Amendment 87 represents exactly the kind of government overreach that drives economic stagnation: punishing success, expanding state spending, and betting that high earners won't simply relocate to Texas or Wyoming.
