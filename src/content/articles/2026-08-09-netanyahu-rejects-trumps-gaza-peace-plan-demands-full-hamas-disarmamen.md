# Israel Rejects Trump's Gaza Peace Plan; Netanyahu Demands Hamas Disarmament First

President Trump's carefully crafted 15-point peace proposal for Gaza hit a wall Sunday when Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu flatly rejected it, declaring Israel will not withdraw from the territory until Hamas is fully disarmed.

Netanyahu made the announcement during a cabinet meeting, signaling that the Trump administration's diplomatic push—aimed at ending the conflict and establishing what the plan calls a "Board of Peace"—faces a major obstacle from Israel itself, a key U.S. ally (9News Denver, Just The News).

The rejection underscores a fundamental gap between Trump's negotiating framework and Israel's security demands. Netanyahu's position is unambiguous: Israeli forces remain until Hamas, designated a terrorist organization by the U.S., is completely eliminated as a military threat. That's a condition unlikely to be met through the diplomatic channels Trump envisions.

For Colorado conservatives watching U.S. foreign policy, this matters: Trump promised a more muscular approach to Middle East stability, yet even his own allies are pushing back on compromise frameworks. The question now is whether Trump will pressure Israel to accept terms it views as inadequate, or whether he'll align U.S. policy with Israeli security requirements.
