# Pentagon's Illegal Blacklist of AI Firm Anthropic Gets Smacked Down by Judge

A federal judge has ruled the Pentagon's attempt to blacklist artificial intelligence company Anthropic was "illegal and baseless," dealing a sharp blow to government overreach in the tech sector. The dispute erupted in February when Pentagon officials accused Anthropic of endangering national security—a charge the court found without merit.

This ruling matters for Colorado's growing tech economy and the broader principle that government agencies cannot simply weaponize their procurement power against companies they dislike. The Pentagon's move was a classic example of bureaucratic retaliation dressed up as national security concern.

**The conservative takeaway:** When federal agencies disagree with a company's policies—in this case, Anthropic's AI safety stance—they cannot resort to blacklisting without legal justification. The judge's decision reinforces that even national security claims require actual evidence, not political theater. This is a win for free markets and against the creeping administrative state that punishes companies for refusing to toe the government line.

Republicans have long warned that unchecked federal agencies treat their authority as absolute. This ruling is a reminder that courts still have a role in checking that power.

**Watch for:** Whether the Pentagon appeals or attempts to manufacture new charges against Anthropic—or whether this precedent emboldens other tech firms to challenge similar government pressure.
