# Real Daily Review — The Brief
### Friday, October 30, 2026

Halloween weekend is here, but Colorado conservatives have bigger scares to worry about: federal election monitors defying state authority, a school tax push colliding with enrollment reality, and Denver's city government deciding what's on your dinner plate. Here's what you need to know.

---

## DOJ Refuses to Follow Colorado's Election Monitor Rules

The Justice Department announced it will station its election monitors *outside* polling places and at least 100 feet away from Adams County voting centers — rather than comply with Colorado's requirements that federal observers stay in designated areas, avoid voter contact, and be accompanied by state-appointed escorts. Republicans argue states have every right to set reasonable ground rules for election observation, and that the DOJ's refusal to comply raises serious questions about federal overreach into Colorado's election administration. Colorado voters deserve to know who is operating in their polling places and under whose authority.

---

## Colorado Firebombing Suspect Pleads Guilty to Federal Hate Crimes

Mohamed Sabry Soliman has pleaded guilty to federal hate crimes in connection with a firebombing attack targeting Jewish residents in Boulder. Under a plea deal, prosecutors agreed to request a life sentence plus 50 years, sparing Soliman from the death penalty. This was a targeted act of antisemitic violence on Colorado soil, and conservatives will rightly scrutinize whether the plea agreement delivers justice proportionate to the crime. Jewish communities across the Front Range are watching the sentencing closely.

---

## Higher Taxes "For the Kids" — But Colorado's Enrollment Is Shrinking

A new report highlights a collision between proposed tax hikes pitched as education funding and Colorado's declining school enrollment numbers. Conservatives say this is exactly the problem with open-ended education spending: the pitch never changes, but the student population does. Colorado taxpayers deserve to ask why they should pay more into a system serving fewer children, and whether the money is actually reaching classrooms or disappearing into administrative overhead and bureaucratic expansion. Fiscal accountability, not blank checks, is what Colorado schools need.

---

## Bodies of 2 Missing College Students Found in Colorado Mountains

Searchers have recovered the bodies of two college students who went missing after leaving Leadville last Friday to hike in Chaffee County. The men did not return as planned Sunday. This is a tragic reminder of the unforgiving nature of Colorado's high-country terrain, especially as late-season weather turns dangerous. Our thoughts are with their families. Coloradans who head into the backcountry this fall should take conditions seriously — the mountains don't offer second chances.

---

## Denver Voters to Decide on Foie Gras Ban

Denver's Initiated Ordinance 311 asks voters whether the city should ban the sale of foie gras and the production process behind it. Conservatives say this is government overreach dressed up as animal welfare — the city of Denver has no business telling restaurants what they can serve or dictating to consumers what they're allowed to eat. If Denver voters approve this, expect the same activist coalition to come back with a longer list. This is how the nanny state expands: one menu item at a time.

---

## What to Watch

Keep your eyes on the Adams County polling place situation as early voting continues — if the DOJ is willing to defy Colorado's own election rules before a single major race is called, that's a precedent worth fighting. On the tax front, watch whether Colorado Democrats push the education spending narrative heading into the legislative session despite the enrollment data staring them in the face. And Denver conservatives: get out and vote on Ordinance 311 — because if the city can ban foie gras today, your burger is not safe tomorrow.
