# Deadly Weld County Hazmat Crash Claims Fourth Victim

A tragic collision in Weld County has claimed its fourth victim, underscoring the dangers of hazardous material transport on Colorado's overtaxed rural infrastructure.

The crash, which occurred early Tuesday morning on Weld County Roads 66 and 43, involved two vehicles and forced closure of both routes, according to Colorado State Patrol. One victim died in the hospital Sunday, joining three others killed in the initial impact (KDVR Fox31).

The incident highlights a persistent problem for Northern Colorado: aging county road systems handling increasing commercial traffic, including hazmat shipments tied to oil and gas operations and agricultural transport. While these industries drive Colorado's economy and employment, the infrastructure supporting them remains underfunded and understaffed.

Rural counties like Weld struggle to maintain roads adequate for both safety and commerce. State and federal transportation dollars often flow to urban corridors, leaving county commissioners scrambling to patch highways that carry critical industrial traffic. Better road maintenance and safety infrastructure aren't luxuries—they're necessities when hazardous materials move through sparsely populated areas.

Colorado taxpayers and policymakers should demand accountability on whether Weld County has adequate resources and emergency response capacity for the industrial activity its roads support.
