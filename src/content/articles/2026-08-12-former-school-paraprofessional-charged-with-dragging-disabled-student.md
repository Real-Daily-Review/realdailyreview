# Former School Paraprofessional Charged After Allegedly Dragging Disabled Student

A former paraprofessional at Ute Meadows Elementary in Jefferson County faces criminal charges after allegedly grabbing and dragging a disabled student across the ground, raising serious questions about school safety oversight and staff accountability in Colorado's public schools.

According to the First Judicial District Attorney's office, Cynthia Monroe—who had worked at the school since 2014—allegedly dragged the student more than ten feet before other staff intervened. The incident underscores a critical gap: how Colorado schools vet, train, and supervise paraprofessionals with direct access to vulnerable children.

Conservatives argue that public schools must implement stricter background checks, mandatory training protocols, and real-time accountability measures for all staff handling students—especially those with disabilities who cannot easily report abuse. The incident also raises questions about whether school administrators properly documented and reported concerns before the alleged assault occurred.

School districts across Colorado employ thousands of paraprofessionals with minimal public transparency about hiring standards or disciplinary records. Parents deserve to know exactly who is working with their children and what safeguards exist.

Taxpayers should watch whether Jefferson County schools conduct a full audit of hiring and supervision practices, and whether the district faces liability claims that could drain education funding.
