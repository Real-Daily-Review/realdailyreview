# Pentagon Revokes Former Air Force Secretary's Clearance Over Air Force One Leak

The Department of Defense has revoked the security clearance of former Air Force Secretary Frank Kendall for disclosing classified information about Air Force One's security capabilities—a stark reminder that even senior officials aren't above the law when they breach national security protocols.

According to Fox News and the Washington Examiner, the Pentagon acted "effective immediately" to strip Kendall of his eligibility to access classified information and hold sensitive positions. Just The News reports that Pentagon spokesman Parnell identified Kendall as the source of the leak, though specific details about which Air Force One disclosure triggered the revocation remain unclear from available accounts.

The move underscores a critical principle: protecting military secrets isn't optional for government officials, regardless of rank or prior service. Kendall's tenure as Air Force Secretary gave him access to some of the nation's most sensitive defense information—and his apparent willingness to discuss Air Force One's security posture publicly demonstrates exactly why clearance revocations exist.

For Colorado, this case carries weight given the state's substantial defense and aerospace footprint, including Peterson Space Force Base and numerous defense contractors. Coloradans depend on rigorous security protocols to protect both national defense and the jobs tied to it.

Watch whether congressional Republicans demand a full accounting of what Kendall disclosed and to whom—and whether similar accountability applies across the Biden administration's security lapses.
