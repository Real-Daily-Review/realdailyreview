# Colorado's Quantum Boom Offers Economic Lifeline—If Denver Fixes Its Office Crisis

Colorado's quantum computing sector is emerging as a genuine economic engine, but the state risks squandering the opportunity if downtown Denver's real estate collapse isn't addressed.

President Trump's recent executive orders on quantum research and cyber defenses signal federal backing for the sector—a recognition that quantum computing poses both national security risks and economic opportunity (Washington Examiner). Colorado, already home to growing quantum innovation clusters, stands to benefit from federal investment and private sector momentum.

But here's the problem: Denver's office market is cratering. Companies expanding quantum operations need talent, infrastructure, and workspace. A hollowed-out downtown—plagued by remote work, crime concerns, and regulatory uncertainty—undermines Colorado's competitive advantage.

Conservatives argue that Colorado's path forward requires three things: (1) removing regulatory barriers to real estate development and office conversion; (2) ensuring tax policy remains competitive against other tech hubs; and (3) letting market forces, not government mandates, drive office-to-residential conversions and downtown revitalization.

The quantum opportunity is real. But if progressive Denver policies continue strangling the downtown core through overregulation and high taxes, companies will take their quantum labs—and Colorado jobs—elsewhere.

Watch whether state and local leaders prioritize business-friendly zoning and tax reform to support downtown recovery, or double down on the regulatory approach that created the office crisis in the first place.
