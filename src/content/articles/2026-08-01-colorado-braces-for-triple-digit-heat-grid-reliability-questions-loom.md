# Colorado Faces Triple-Digit Heat This Weekend—Power Grid and Water Supply in Focus

Another dangerous heat dome is bearing down on Colorado, with temperatures expected to hit 100 degrees or higher across the Front Range through Monday, according to CPR News and 9News Denver. Both outlets have issued Weather Impact Alerts for the weekend.

The timing matters for Colorado's energy and water infrastructure. As demand for air conditioning spikes, the state's power grid—already strained by years of coal plant retirements and intermittent renewable mandates—faces real stress. Meanwhile, Colorado's water supply, already stretched thin by drought and competing claims, will see increased consumption just as summer irrigation demands peak.

Republicans and energy advocates have long warned that Colorado's aggressive push toward renewables without adequate baseload capacity creates vulnerability during exactly these scenarios. The state's 2040 decarbonization targets require phasing out reliable coal and gas generation faster than replacements can reliably deliver power.

State officials recommend residents conserve energy during peak afternoon hours and stay hydrated. The National Weather Service warns of heat-related illness risks, particularly for vulnerable populations.

Coloradans should watch whether this heat event triggers rolling blackouts or emergency grid measures—and whether state policymakers acknowledge the connection between aggressive climate mandates and grid reliability.
